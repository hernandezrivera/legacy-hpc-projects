Project Summary

As part of the proposed action, Secours Islamique France (SIF) will directly contribute to strategies 1 and 2 of the Education Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2021 for Syria through providing non-formal education (NFE) to out-of-school children (OOSC) and children at-risk of dropping out both within and outside public educational institutions to provide a path to enter formal education. This proposed project will be part of an Education, Protection and WASH integrated programme under the 2021 HRP which will include rehabilitation of WASH facilities and classrooms in the targeted 7 public schools and psychosocial support, including case identification and referral to specialized services in public schools and community learning centres. Girls and boys will also be provided with awareness sessions on child protection concerns, including gender- based violence and risk education.
The response proposed by SIF is based on its 4-year experience in implementing UNHCR and UNICEF- funded education projects in Syria. SIF is thus intending to replicate its successes, taking into account the lessons learned, in locations showing high severity of needs: 35% of the targeted locations’ severity scale are 3, 61% are 4 and 4% are 2. The severity level 2 locality is included after the output of SIF’s need assessment conducted in December 2021 in coordination with SARC and local authorities it was observed that in Maliha (the target locality with severity scale 2) has need for education assistance and a high percentage of children out of school. Hence SIF has included this locality with severity scale 2 in the HRP 2021.
Through the proposed intervention, SIF will rehabilitate WAHS facilities and classrooms in 7 schools Rural Damascus, Dara’a, Al- Hassakeh, Dier- Ez-zor, Homs, Hama and Aleppo governorate which will benefit an estimated 2,450 children. SIF will also provide (1) non-formal education (NFE), (2) distribution of furniture in schools, (3) maintenance and operation of learning spaces, (4) distribution of Learning material and (5) installation of solar panels in a total of 26 learning spaces benefiting an estimate of 11,050 students, caregivers, teachers, education personnel and actors. The cost per beneficiary for the programme is estimated to be 123.12 USD.
In addition, 300 teachers and education personnel will benefit from professional development. 100 education actors will be trained on policy, planning, data collection, sector coordination or INEE Minimum Standards. An estimated 5% of all target children live with disabilities. By providing transportation, children with disabilities will benefit from a facilitated access to education facilities in community learning centres. Moreover, girls and boys will be both encouraged to participate in the activities through Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns. These districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and SIF field assessments. SIF ensures participatory approach in all project cycle phases including the planning, project design, implementation and the monitoring phase. The MEAL team will put in place a complaints and feedback mechanism. COVID 19 prevention measures will be strictly followed during the intervention. Personal protection kits will be distributed along with the provision of awareness raising sessions on COVID 19 barrier measures to 8,500 caregivers, parents, students, teachers and education personnel.
In 2020, SIF reached 320,316 beneficiaries across all its programmes, with SYP 6,139,135,982 received as funding. In 2021, the organization has already secured SYP 8,184,575,183 to date. SIF has existing presence in Aleppo, Dara’a, Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama and Quneitra through ongoing projects.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021, there are 6.9 million people in need of education across Syria, with 51% of them being females. Access to education and teaching quality in Syria have significantly deteriorated as a result of the crisis. After SIF’s assessment in December 2020, SIF found that a large percentage of educational facilities were either destroyed or used for purposes other than education, as a result, most of the schools are congested and overcrowded. Most of the families reported high costs of uniforms and school stationeries for children. Coupled with scarcity and unaffordability of transportation, this is further dissuading parents from sending their children to school. Other negative coping mechanisms, such as early marriage, child labour and violence both at school and home, are further limiting children access to education. In this context, girls and female adolescents are particularly subject to gender-based violence. Moreover, children and youth with disabilities face additional challenges to access basic services, including education, and are exposed to higher risks of violence and other protection issues. Access to education is also discriminatory as children with disabilities are facing more difficulties in being integrated in the community while most of the schools are not equipped with disable-friendly facilities or sex-disaggregated WASH facilities. Teaching quality has drastically decreased as most of the teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts which is effecting learning outcomes of students in public schools. The lack of textbooks, teaching and learning materials impairs learning in the classrooms and demotivates children to continue education.

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention will include the rehabilitation of classrooms by contractors selected by SIF as part of a transparent and accountable tender process. SIF will coordinate with MoE and ensure that a period of guarantee is included in the contracts for sustainability. NFE will be directly implemented by SIF. Self-Learning Programme (SLPs) for OOSC will be conducted in public schools during weekends/holidays while SLPs and remedial classes for OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out will be implemented in community centers throughout the week. Children aged 3 to 5 will benefit from Early Childhood Education. Children with disabilities (CwD) or living in hard-to-reach areas will be provided with transportation. Teachers and parents associations will also be supported in the targeted learning spaces. Learning and WASH facilities will also be adapted to CwD and gender-based specific needs. SIF will conduct regular tests of the children to evaluate their initial level and progress. The students will be grouped according to their level and age. The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF through field visits using data collection tools (Child Monitoring System and 4Ws). Through monthly reports and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure that project is on track and resource used properly. COVID 19 Prevention measure will be strictly followed. Children per class will be limited to 10 to respect the physical distance, and awareness will be raised among community. In case of lockdown, NFE activities will be implemented online and internet allowance provided to students family. Close coordination with the sector will be kept on activities, needs and context to ensure alignment with the sector objectives.

Mitigation: 1) establish a code of conduct in learning facilities; training and prevention 2) professional development training on dealing with bullying 3) provide PSS activities in learning spaces. 2. Risk of sexual exploitation and GBV. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: 1) application of Child Safeguarding measures: recruitment of staff according to the safe recruitment process 2) training of all staff and associates 3) signature of code of conduct also posted on walls and notice boards 4) establishment of effective child-friendly complaint mechanism 5) staff training on PSEA, children's rights and GBV 6) sufficient number of adult women working in the learning environment to protect and reassure girls.3. Risk of children and adults’ exposure to physical or verbal harassment and unexploded ordnances on the way to centers and schools.Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigation: 1) safety assessment conducted by SIF team in centers and schools’ surroundings prior to beginning 2) coordinate with other actors in the area for risk assessment 3) ensure that unexploded ordnances’ clearance is conducted4. Risk: Community members or local authorities attempt to exert undue influence or to change the beneficiaries list or the type of support providedLikelihood: medium.Impact: high. Mitigation: 1) Prorgramme designed to be context and conflict effective 2) Community and local authorities involved in the whole cycle of the project and close coordination is kept throughout project life 3) information on the intervention and exit strategy is provided to community.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Not getting approvals from national security to enter the area. Likelihood: Low.Impact: high. Mitigation: target easy-to-reach areas and establish a constant dialogue and follow-up with the authorities. 2. Inflation rate increases prices. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: SIF has already accounted for the increased inflation rate in the budget, in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. 3. Discontinuation of interventions due to the risk of spread of COVID 19 within learning points and/or lockdown enforced at governmental level.Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium.Mitigation: 1) NFE activities will continue to be implemented online 2) rehabilitation activities would continue in a case of a partial lockdown and time schedule adapted in the case of a curfew 3) Covid 19 measures will be in place, PPE kits provided to beneficiaries and staff 4. Security situation deteriorates and effects the implementation of activities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: HighMitigation: 1) regular monitoring of the security situation by SIF Logistics and Security Manager 2) ensure updated contingency plans are in place at field and coordination level 3) ensure the preparedness of SIF team through regular briefings and regular updates on any change in the situation 5. Risk of fraud and misuse of resourcesLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: 1) Ensure that a complaint and feedback mechanism is in place to report any fraud. 2) Random spot checks 3) Ensure procurement procedures are transparent and in line with requirements 4) Ensure that staff is hired in a fairly adhering to SIF procedures 5) Ensure SIF’s Anti-Fraud policy is strictly followed to detect, prevent and address cases.
