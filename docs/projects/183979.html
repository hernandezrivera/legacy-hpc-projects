Project Summary

Gedaref is suffering from multiple shocks which has created a dire humanitarian situation in the state. The sudden influx of Ethiopian refugees from the Tigray region in late 2020, coupled with the ongoing economic crises affecting the country have placed a severe burden on the provision of basic services. Compounding these difficulties has been the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and the devastating floods during the last rainy season. Urgent humanitarian assistance is needed to support local people in Gedaref to meet their basic needs. In response to this crisis, Mercy Corps plans to deliver a multi-sector response comprising of interventions in WASH, FSL, ESNFI, Protection, Health and Nutrition.
The program will strengthen WASH, GBV / protection, shelter provision and health services to enable refugees, host population and vulnerable communities to adapt, withstand, and recover from disasters and emergencies. The project will respond to the needs and priorities of the affected population through an integrated multi-sectoral approach, with gender and protection mainstreamed throughout. Mercy Corps will address WASH needs through rehabilitation of water sources at both community and institutions, formation and training of WASH committees to ensure improved access to clean and safe water. Mercy Corps will then complement the water supply activities through hygiene promotion and train community hygiene promoters who will carry out hygiene promotion awareness raising activities to enhance health and wellbeing of the targeted individuals. During the implementation Mercy Corps will ensure COVID-19 transmission risks are mitigated through behavior change by ensuring adherence preventive practices including social distancing and respiratory hygiene.
MC will deliver a range of nutrition activities targeting children under 5 and pregnant / lactating women (PLW). We will support health facilities with outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) services whilst also training mothers support groups (MSG) to addresses the multi-sectoral causes of malnutrition, such as inadequate diet diversity, poor WASH behaviors, and unsafe food preparation and conservation methods. MC will integrate project activities with respect to nutrition interventions (For example, provision of WASH kits to SAM/MAM children, or referral of SAM/MAM cases to FSL livelihood/GFD response to prevent them from relapsing to malnutrition)
Multiple shocks, including slow and fast onset disasters such as flooding, are continuing to affect a range of vulnerable households in the state. To meet the immediate needs of affected communities, Mercy Corps will provide ES/NFI sectoral interventions including the provision of emergency shelter and NFI kits. A range of vulnerability criteria will be used for inclusion in ES/NFI distributions and the items will be inline with SPHERE standards.
The protracted humanitarian situation within the state has resulted in multiple protection concern. GBV has been identified as a priority concern and MC will ensure the safety, health and dignity of women and girls by provision of quality multi-sectoral GBV response services with a survivor-centred approach and GBV risk mitigation measures. Activities proposed include support to Women and Girls Safe Spaces, protection monitoring visits and assessments, conducting awareness-raising against GBV and PSEA and the provision of dignity kits for Women and Girls.

1 - Needs

In Eastern Sudan, more than 1.4 million people face poverty, drought, famine, and inadequate access to basic services, including health and nutrition. Surveillance reports show that the burden of infectious diseases is compounded by cross-border population movements from Ethiopia, further contributing to disease spread. ). The latest Humanitarian Response Plan estimates that 0.55 million people in the state are in need of humanitarian assistance. Of these PINs, 188,773 residents and are assessed as being in the ‘life threatening’ category. WASH:According to the 2021 HRP, 9.1 million people are in need of access to basic Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) services in Sudan. Sudan's protracted crisis continues to create water, hygiene, and sanitation needs for vulnerable people. Key drivers are deteriorating economy, conflict, food insecurity, malnutrition, floods, and disease, including COVID-19. About three-quarters of the population have access to basic drinking water, 39 per cent have access to limited sanitation services, and 13 per cent have access to basic handwashing facilities Nutrition: The nutrition situation in Sudan has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and under nutrition since the first S3M survey conducted in 2013. Malnutrition in all its forms is a serious public health challenge in Sudan. When nutritional needs are not met, it is impossible for an individual to achieve optimal physical and mental health. This creates limitations on educational and employment opportunities. The rate of malnutrition in Sudan has changed little in the last three decades, with an estimated 52 per cent of cases living in the nine non-conflict states. Drivers of malnutrition include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. The nutrition statistics for Sudan are estimated as follows, GAM 13.6% at national level; SAM is over 600,000 and MAM is 2.2million based on the updated 2022 HNO analysis. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), over 200,000 in Gedaref are in need of nutrition support. GBV:In 2020, communal tension and conflict affected a number of areas in the East of Sudan resulting in GBV risks including gang rape, abduction, physical assault, harassment, and humiliation. Analysis shows that conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) is especially common in IDP camps and in the vicinity of camps. Refugee girls and young women in particular face a high risk of sexual violence. The collapse of traditional community support structures has increased the vulnerability in particular people with specific needs, including those living with disabilities. COVID-19 exacerbated GBV risks for girls and women with anecdotal reports suggesting an increase in domestic violence and sexual abuse. According to the 2021 HNO, there are 2.3 million people in need of GBV prevention, mitigation and response activities ShelterShelter and NFI needs have increased in 2020, despite the decline in armed conflict since 2015. New displacement has occurred in eastern Sudan as a result of inter-communal violence. The economic crisis continues to be a driver of deteriorating humanitarian needs, exacerbated by COVID-19. Containment measures increased the cost and time delivering shelter and NFIs. In 2020, flooding affected close to 900,000 people across the country, with the heavy rains causing damage to houses, livestock, farmland, infrastructure, and services. For the over one million refugees, the need for shelter and NFI remained significant, especially for those living in camps in Gedaref.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will provide vital humanitarian assistance to crisis affected communities, vulnerable residents and Host Communities in Gedaref state. The proposed interventions will meet the needs of thesegroups in terms of WASH, FSL, Protection, ESNFI, Health and Nutrition. Mercy Corps will adopt a multi-sectoral approach to maximize the impact and added value of the proposed activities.The health, nutrition, protection, WASH, and food security sectors are key interventions that are likely to directly and significantly impact morbidity and mortality in emergency contexts. The protection cluster ensures linkages between the psychosocial services provided through the protection cluster partners and mental health services provided by the health cluster partners. Protection interventions promoting safety have clear links to reducing incidence of violence. Increasing access to health services for survivors of gender-based violence and relevant referrals between the protection and health clusters will ensure that the health consequences (both mental and physical) of gender-based violence are addressed. The health consequences of violence are mitigated through multisectoral service provision coordinated across the two sectors. The shelter and NFI clusters will impact health outcomes. Overcrowded settlements and camps can contribute to an increase in disease, while poor lighting and lack of safety considerations can contribute to risks of sexual violence, and poor site planning for hygiene and sanitation can lead to disease. Infrastructure investments in water and sanitation services play a critical role in multi-sectoral food security programming by addressing root causes of many humanitarian crises that stem from droughts, floods, and natural resource-driven conflict. Integrating WASH activities has many benefits in term of reducing the spread of water borne diseases which in turn has health and nutrition benefits.Mercy Corps will lever this multisectoral approach to enhance the outcomes and impacts of the proposed interventions. According to available data, the total population of the 3 target localities is 564,467 (including refugees). Within the target area there are an estimated 156,362 People in Need (PiN) which represents approximately 28% of the population. Ar Rahad locality has the highest concentration, with 90,021 PiN which represents over half the total PIN caseload for the target localities. The number of vulnerable residents is also increasing. In 2018, the number of vulnerable residents was recorded as 83,917 while the current estimate for 2021 is now estimated to be 4156,027.Needs assessments estimate that the target localities have 84,620 people with disabilities and 11,283 elderly people (65+). GBV - the target localities are suffering from a range of GBV issues including child marriage, child labour, IPV, SGBV, rape, FGM and other harmful practices. An estimated 23,858 PiN are in need of GBV support and assistance within the target localities. Ar Rasad has the highest caseload with 12,560 PiN needing GBV assistance. WASH - the targeted communities suffer from a range of WASH problems including the availability of water, water quality and poor hygiene / sanitation conditions. An estimated 153,204 need of WASH assistance. MC needs assessment found that many schools in the target localities did not have access to a functioning water source within or near the school compound. Even where they existed, the water source are often non-functioning and required rehabilitation. Large numbers of the schools surveyed did not have any toilets. In schools with latrines, the majority did not have separate facilities for boys and girls, hand washing facilities or other menstruation management and hygiene facilities near the toilet. Past research has found that girls do not feel safe going to latrines in schools as many lack doors and are not gender-segregated. This impacts therefore on their attendance.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corpswill abide by Do No Harm principles. MC will also strive for gender balance in therecruitment of staff at all levels and will train officers, responsible for deliveringservices and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches.Mercy Corps’ in-house gender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program.Recognizing the key role of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimumof 40 percent of participants will be women. Specifically under WASH interventions, inthe target locations, water collection is typically the responsibility of women and girls.Limited access to water sources forces women and girls to walk long distances insearch of water, often exposing them to additional protection risks. This interventionwill help to reduce these risks through rehabilitating infrastructure which will increasethe availability of water and reduce the distance women and girls are forced to walk tocollect water. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure that women and girls are representedin the community-level WASH committees – minimum 50 percent of WASH committeemembers. Mercy Corps plans to encourage the involvement of women and girls in allelements of the community-based operation and maintenance of the water sources.Ongoing discussions with some of the targeted communities have clearly shown thatwomen are more interested than men in taking key roles in the implementation andmanagement of the proposed user fee. As part of the user fee implementation strategy,Mercy Corps will form an advisory board with women taking key decision-makingpositions regarding rates, exemptions and managing collected money.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Mercy Corps places the issue of accountability at the core of this project design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation processes. Ensuring relevant, quality andtimely programming and interventions reflects this commitment. MC considers thetarget communities as leading the solutions for the humanitarian issues they face, andthus are regarded as key stakeholders in the design, implementation and monitoringand evaluation of the project. MC will work through existing community structurescomprising of a representative of women, youth and community leaders to representcommunities during project implementation. Due to the limitation of gatherings asresult of COVID-19, the information will mainly be shared through public media,posters, call centers, community microphone information broadcasts, and the use of apublic address system in the target areas. Information dissemination will be adaptedto the context of COVID-19 to ensure actions contribute to containment. Rather thangathering large groups, some activities will be done through a variety of approaches,including voice messaging, to cater to the vast majority who are illiterate. The projectwill use participatory, gender- and age-sensitive monitoring approaches to ensure girls,boys, women, and men, as well as CBOs and services providers, are involved centrallyin the process of project follow-up, provide their feedback and concerns on the projectperformance and are informed about decisions made in community meetings anddiscussions. MC will provide timely, relevant, and precise information about theorganization including feedback and complaints mechanisms. Communities andbeneficiaries will be kept up to date in changes in feedback mechanisms and will beprovided with information on how they can feedback remotely, such as via hotline,WhatsApp etc. Information sharing will be via different formats to ensure informationis available promptly while taking into account language barriers and literacy status –such as flyers and posters left in communities, health facilities, hospitals etc. withsimple and clear messages/standards and accountability assigned a number to allproject locations. Mercy Corps’ functional Complaints and Response Mechanism willcollect feedback where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, and a dedicated call-innumber – any meetings held (where still possible) will be in open air locations, inspaces large enough for distancing; and otherwise can be conducted remotely.Complaints/feedback will be captured, categorized, and shared with relevant staff forresolution and logged into the action plan trackers. Depending on the type of feedbackand complaint, some will immediately be closed through sharing the requiredinformation (different methods dependent on scale of response – face to face usingdistancing, telephone etc.). At the same time, for some, an investigation will beundertaken to verify the facts before a concrete decision is made.
