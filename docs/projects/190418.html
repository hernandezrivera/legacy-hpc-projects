Project Summary

The project will respond to the Nutrition needs among vulnerable communities which have been exacerbated because conflict-induced and displacement.
A Nutrition response consisting of Community based management of acute malnutrition (Outpatient therapeutic feeding, Stabilization center, Management of Moderate Acute malnutrition, and Community mobilization), support to strengthening and establishment of OTP and SC at Health facilities, provision of logistical and technical support in conflict affected Woredas of high levels of IDPs and returnees without access to free nutrition care. The project activities will reduce malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable groups through timely capacity building, functioning of OTP and SC at health facilities and strengthening management of acute malnutrition systems by delivering Nutrition and medical supplies and materials for the targeted conflict affected Woredas in Tigray Region.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

GOAL Ethiopia has institutionalized accountability across all levels, ensuring accountability towards our project participants and partners through participation, feedback, transparency, information sharing, appropriate activities that respond to the needs, and appropriate use of resources. GOAL has quality benchmarks in place regarding accountability standards.Engagement with local communities is crucial. GOAL ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. GOAL considers it of paramount importance that beneficiaries have access to information about their entitlements, have access to GOAL’s Code of Conduct and can provide feedback. During any community activities, discussions are held in advance with the affected community, project participants, community leaders and authorities. Information is provided about the project, the Donor, targeting criteria and entitlements, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, and ways to contact the organization.GOAL ensures engagement with local authorities. MoUs will be established with relevant government offices during project set-up. The relevant Woreda and Zonal Health Offices will be key stakeholders to the responses. Information is shared with the relevant authorities as well with the DPPO and Early Warning office, on a regular basis; joint supervision and monitoring activities will take place regularly to the project sites.Representatives from the local population will be engaged as part of the CFM to provide feedback or request clarification on the project from GOAL staff and will be fully engaged throughout the project activities.Monitoring visits will also address accountability questions to establish the level of knowledge of the beneficiaries on the selection criteria, their entitlements, and GOAL’s Code of Conduct. GOAL is committed to having a culture of evidence-based learning and accountability.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

GOAL is a member of Core Humanitarian Standards Alliance. A Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will be established for all locations in order for stakeholders, including beneficiaries, to be able to provide complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries will be informed of this mechanism during activities, entitlements posters will be printed and displayed at relevant sites. The CFM is designed for direct and indirect project participants, community members, local and national government, partners, and GOAL staff. Core principles of GOAL’s CFM are: confidentiality, accessibility, safety, and transparency. GOAL has dedicated third party (independent from operations) in-country resources in place to manage the CFM. All staff are trained on accountability measures, including the CFM, and comprehensive guidelines and SOPs are in place.As part of the CFM, information is shared with communities during interventions about the project, and during information sessions with local authorities. Community members and project participants are provided with clear information on how to contact GOAL to provide feedback, request information, make complaints or express grievances. Contact details are provided for field staff, the Regional Manager and the Country Director. In addition, details of a dedicated “Call me Back” phone number and e-mail address in GOAL’s Head Office in Addis are provided. During distributions, representatives (male and female, and literate if possible) are selected by the community as ‘Community Feedback Members’. The community can also make contact directly. All grievances are dealt with in a serious and confidential manner and shared with GOAL Ethiopia’s Head Office in Addis. The most serious complaints are dealt with directly by the in-country management team.
