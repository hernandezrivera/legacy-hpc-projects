Project Summary

This project is designed to contribute to the increased access to basic services and improved living conditions of vulnerable conﬂict affected populations.
Based on ADRA’s ﬁeld visits and coordination with stakeholders in the ﬁeld, the needs in Homs in areas such as Al Bayada (where ADRA had been present since 2017) remain high, as people continued to return to their homes. Homs governorate has a high number of PiN (2020), more than 120,000 people and ranked at severity level 3 (WoS SNFI PiN 2020). Other areas such as Mahin and Nasra (in Tal Kalakh) are also in high need of rehabilitation of damaged houses. Some returnees from Al Rukban camp moved to Tadmor, but are staying in apartments illegally as they lived in tents before being displaced. These families are in need of emergency response such as shelter kits.
In Deir Ezzor governorate, ADRA identiﬁed a high need for rehabilitation of damaged houses in the neighbourhoods of Al Omal Wasat, Al Arﬁ, Al Jbeila and Al Rojdiya, which were on the frontline during the besiegement. In Deir Ezzor, more than 31,000 individuals are in need of shelter response and the numbers are expected to increase in 2020 as more people return to their areas of origin.
In Aleppo, as per ADRA’s assessments, the needs in Southern Rural Aleppo (such as Al Hajeb and Khanaser) are amongst the highest, due to increased number of returnees and lack of presence of humanitarian partners. Eastern Rural Aleppo is also in a growing need of shelter response. Deir Hafer (in Al Bab) and Al Bab lack adequate living space for the returnees from the Aleppo city and camps in Turkey and Lebanon.
In Rural Damascus, the needs are also among the highest especially in East Ghouta, Arbin (severity level 5), Kafr Batna, Mleha and Shabaa, Moadamiya (Markaz Daraya), Husseiniya (El Eljijeh). Daraya has become a newly assessable area is one of the areas in growing need shelter response, and ADRA begun coordination for access to the area.
ADRA will respond to the needs of 6,030 vulnerable male, female, boys and girls targeting IDPs and returnees through rehabilitation of damaged houses and provision of emergency shelter solutions in the governorates of Aleppo, Homs, Deir Ezzor and Rural Damascus. A total of 800 apartments (approximately 4,000 individuals) will be targeted under the shelter long-term solutions and an additional 406 families will be targeted under emergency response (approximately 2,030 individuals in collective shelters).

4 - Needs

With the conﬂict in Syria reaching a decade and after years of humanitarian response,5.65 million people (draft SNFI HNO 2020) remain in need of shelter assistance; of which4 million are in acute need. During 2019, the number of people returning to their homes increased, registering almost 500,000 spontaneous returns. The highest returns registered by OCHA occurred in Dara’a (more than 170,000 individuals), Aleppo and Deir Ezzor (more than 120,000 individuals in each), Rural Damascus and Homs (more than 65,000 individuals in each). The number of IDPs has also increased, especially in the North West and North East of Syria. In December 2019, alone, 308,200 occurrences of IDP movements were registered in Idleb, Aleppo and Hasakeh. And an additional one million people are expected to be displaced by the end of 2020. IDPs and returnees are unable to meet their basic needs, including shelter, due to already strained resources spent on food and clothes. Meeting life-saving and life-sustaining needs remains crucial and leads families to negative coping mechanisms. In addition, many areas are devoid of safe, digniﬁed and sustainable conditions for returnees, especially women, boys, girls, elderly and disabled, as living is sub-standard houses with no appropriate WASH facilities and not protected from weather and elements affects physical and mental wellbeing, especially for women and children to spend more time at home. The draft HNO 2020 states, ‘shelter severity increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent’. The economic condition in Syria, including ﬂuctuation in the exchange rate, has contributed to the acuteness of the needs, as families are forced to spend more money on their basic needs. In 2019, only 43.4 per cent of the people in need were reached, leaving 56.6 per cent of people underserved (OCHA:Overview of Humanitarian response within Syria Jan–Oct 2019).

5 - Implementation of activities

ADRA’s objective for shelter is to contribute to the increased access to basic services and improved living conditions of vulnerable conﬂict affected populations. This objective responds to the following shelter sector objectives: Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support Activity: 1.4 Rehabilitation of collective centres and transitional shelters Objective 2: Reinforce an enabling protection environment and cohesion of communities by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. Activity: 2.1 Support to sustainability repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities ADRA will coordinate with the relevant governorates, MoLAE and the local municipalities for identiﬁcation of the speciﬁc needs. Underserved communities withhigh number of people in need and families that fall within the shelter prioritization criteria will be selected. Only slightly and moderately damaged houses will be targeted and beneﬁciaries will be consulted during the assessment stage. The response will be tailored to the speciﬁc needs of women, men, girls, boys, elderly and disabled and where required, ramps, western toilets and disability friendly WASH facilities will be installed.Any housing, land and property (HLP) issues will be referred to the local civil cervices. Once the areas and beneﬁciaries are selected, ADRA will conduct the technical assessment, assessing houses against SPHERE standards. The BoQs, will be prepared and shared with the relevant stakeholders. Then, ADRA will conduct the tender process and select the most successful bidder based on the submitted samples, past experience in similar projects, technical and ﬁnancial offers and timeline. ADRA will deliver the shelters to the contractor and the team will monitor the progress on regular basis. The ADRA MEAL team will conduct sporadic visits to the site for monitoring purposes.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA ensures to mainstream protection and adhere to humanitarian standards of ‘Do No Harm’. A close attention will be paid to the protection risks that may occur during all stages of the project. One of the most highly to occur risks is the risk of increase of community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination. The likelihood is high and the impact is medium. As a mitigating measure, ADRA will ensure to adhere to the standardised shelter response as per the shelter sector guidelines and all stakeholders will be informed of the selection criteria. Leaders of the community where ADRA will intervene will be involved in order and the phone number where beneficiaries and other partners can call will be shared to allow raising of any complaints. The MEAL team as well as the ADRA top management will visit the field for an additional monitoring.The risk of GBV, exploitation and abuse may rise due to the increased privacy in houses after rehabilitation. The likelihood is low, but the impact is high. As a mitigating measure, ADRA will ensure each rehabilitated apartment has lockable doors and windows and can provide protection especially for women, boys, girls, elderly, disabled, teenagers and adolescents. The common areas in buildings will also be targeted to provide lighting. ADRA will work closely with the protection and other sectors to understand the issues and the necessary actions together to address them. ADRA will ensure that male and female staff are available during the life of the project to allow men, women, boys and girls to raise issues with the person of the gender they feel most comfortable with. The MEAL team will distribute the complaint cards with a hotline number allowing beneficiaries to raise complaints anonymously. All complaints will be followed up on the sensitive issues will be referred to the ADRA Protection officer, who will then contact appropriate institutions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Inability to transfer funds into Syria due to sanctions. Assumption: transfer of funds into Syria remains manageable with no restrictions and delays. Likelihood is high, impact is also high. Mitigation measures: ensuring more than one pathway of transferring funds into Syria is available, close monitoring of the situation inside and outside of Syria and advocacy with stakeholders. Risk 2: deterioration of security situation in the area of interventionAssumption: security situation in the areas of intervention remains stable and the area remains accessible. Likelihood is low, impact is highMitigation measures: ADRA will ensure to closely monitor the security situation in the areas of interventions. In case the situation deteriorates, remote monitoring will be implemented through local municipalities, local staff, contractors and community leaders. Alternatively, travel and activities in the area might be temporarily suspended.
