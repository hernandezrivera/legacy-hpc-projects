Project Summary

IMC will implement its integrated programme in five states, West, Central and South Darfur, South Kordofan and the Blue Nile covering around 1,947,257 people. The proposed intervention will address gaps in the provision of primary health care and nutrition services. Under the health intervention, International Medical Corps will be providing basic PHC and nutrition services, including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services (antenatal care, delivery service, post-natal care, and family planning), and immunization through 89 health care facilities across the targeted 5 states. In addition to facility care, community-based health interventions including; health and hygiene education, referral of cases, and campaigns will also be provided. In collaboration with SMOH, WHO, and HAC, IMC will respond to seasonal outbreaks such as AWD, measles, and malaria in all targeted areas. Health facilities will be prepared with necessary human resources and equipment (including laboratory equipment and materials) to enable them to respond to outbreaks and related emergencies. IMC will be implementing community-based interventions such as the promotion of child and maternal health, health education, community mobilization for EPI, mass MUAC screening and other activities through local partners.
Under the nutrition intervention, IMC will support SCs, OTPS, and TSFPs, IMC (in conjunction with the Ministry of Health) will provide nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups- children under 5 and PLWs- among the IDPs, returnees and host population and refugees in the targeted localities. The active and passive screening will be done to determine the children that should be enrolled for treatment. Treatment will be provided based on the national guidelines for the management of acute malnutrition (MAM). Children 6-59 months found to have SAM without complications will be treated in the OTPs and those with medical complications or children below 6 months will be transferred to SC where they will receive medical and therapeutic nutrition.
To promote optimal IYCF practices, including exclusive breastfeeding, timely complementary feeding, and dietary diversity, individual counseling and group sessions for caretakers of children 0 to 23 months will be provided. Nutrition, health and hygiene education will also be provided for caregivers of all children admitted in the CMAM program, women attending antenatal and post-natal services, and when immunization services are provided. Key messages will be on recommended feeding and child care practices, improving dietary diversity using locally available foods, and improved water, sanitation, and hygiene practices. Community outreach activities will result in improved timely access to treatment, program coverage, and community engagement and contribute to the prevention of malnutrition.
The health facilities will be made fully functional by the rehabilitation of the structure, provision of drugs, nutritional supplies, medical equipment and capacity building of State Ministry of Health (SMoH) staff on the provision of nutrition-specific and health care services. The aim is to facilitate access to quality health and nutrition services for all, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups.
The WASH intervention will be done at the health facility and community level to ensure adequate access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene in an integrated and comprehensive approach, whilst providing preventative measures to mitigate the risk of other potential outbreaks i.e. cholera, Chikungunya, Malaria, and Dengue. Hence, the focus of WaSH intervention will be the health facilities supported through IMC for health and nutrition interventions. WaSH in health facilities will be including the provision of a sufficient amount of safe water, provision of gender-segregated latrines, medical waste management facilities, and hygiene promotion.

1 - Needs

The health systems in Darfur, South Kordofan, and the Blue Nile remain weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and responds to outbreaks. In Central Darfur, it is reported that26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and NewbornCare (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only 30% in Central Darfur. Only 40% ofClinics in Central Darfur States provide Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI)Services. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of childrenare fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to theprotocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need forstrengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Detection andreferral of pregnant mothers is weak because of low coverage of community outreachactivities. The clinics are not yet fully staffed and still lack the necessary equipment andphysical structure to provide comprehensive health services. Water supply, medical wastemanagement and infection control practices are identified as a gap in the clinics. Thecommunity outreach activities need expansion to more villages. Staff need to be trainedas they are relatively new to the areas for intervention. Dispensaries do not have enoughshelves and storage capacity. Health workers in targeted clinics need training on IMCIwhich is an important skill for better treatment of childhood diseases. According to theMinistry of Health, in Sudan, some 2.2 million children suffer from malnutrition annually(Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over 573,000 suffer from Severe AcuteMalnutrition.. South Kordofan and Blue Nile States have been affected by persistentconflict between government forces and rebel groups since 2011. Two million people areaffected by the conflict since then and more than 250,000 refugees fled to Ethiopia andSouth Sudan. The United Nations estimates that armed movements in South Kordofanand Blue Nile resulted in additional displacement of 545,000 people in areas under theircontrol. The conflict has disrupted traditional livelihood activities and eroded communityresilience to withstand shocks though the areas were known to have good agriculturalproductions before the conflict. Among displaced people, women and children are themost vulnerable and at the greatest risk of being exposed to violence. Healthcare facilitieswere destroyed in both states as a result of the fighting. Lack of basic services and foodscarcity perpetuates displacement and migration of people out of the country, mainly tothe camps in South Sudan. The humanitarian context in South Kordofan is furthercomplicated by arrival of South Sudanese refugees in the state increasing thehumanitarian need and straining the already compromised basic services infrastructure,including health services. There are new arrivals of around 5000 refugees in SouthKordofan in December 2019.According to S3M II 2018 survey result, seven out of the 18 states have malnutrition prevalence rates of above the critical threshold as per the WHO standards (15%).
