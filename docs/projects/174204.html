Project Summary

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Merged Districts (KPMD) are composed of 7 districts, which merged with KP in 2018. Numerous operations by Pakistan’s security forces since 2004 as well as violence by terrorist splinter groups, often religiously motivated, have resulted in a complex crisis leading to the mass displacement of hundreds of thousands of people. Since 2016, the security situation gradually improved and over 98% of the Internally Displaced Population (IDP) have returned. However, returnees faced large-scale devastation of infrastructures, and little or no livelihood resources. Healthcare system, which was already poor prior to conflict, became almost non-existent. In Kurram District, over 40,000 IDPs families have returned since 2010. Whereas there is no confirmation of repatriation of the remaining 12%, it is more likely that majority may have returned. Almost 30% of the facilities have been damaged and there is a lack of skilled staff.
The project aims in providing medical humanitarian relief assistance to crisis-affected population, including IDPs. In that order, it will enhance access to primary healthcare (PHC) by improving the availability and quality of services. As women are in a situation of particular vulnerability, a focus will be given to sexual and reproductive health. To underline these focuses, MdM divided its overall objective in two results:
• Result 1: the target health facilities are strengthened to provide quality PHC to the catchment population.
• Result 2: the targeted catchment population has access to quality Sexual and Reproductive Health including mental health services.
To achieve these objectives and results, MdM will target health facilities that are not or barely functional as per vulnerability criteria (damages of infrastructure, number of staff available, quantity and quality of equipment and material, geographic outreach, etc). The project is designed to fill the significant gaps in financial, material and human resources of three targeted facilities. Therefore, proposed intervention will focus on minor rehabilitation works and provision of essential equipment and medicines to ensure adequate service provision in safe environment. In particular, the project will consider the COVID-19 pandemic, ensuring sufficient quantity of personal protection equipment (PPEs) and that standards of procedure (SOPs) are implemented and followed at all time. In consultation with the health authorities, MdM will advocate for the provision of sanctioned staff and will fill the HR gap, ensuring the availability of female medical staff. Adequate trainings will be provided to ensure (1) the quality of services with gender approach, and (2) the efficient running of the facility (pharmacy management, waste disposal management, hygienic environment, infection prevention and control, information system, etc). By making functional health facilities in remote areas, the aim is to encourage the population to gradually increase the demand and use of services and provide access to health in desert areas. Further, comprehensive information on all available services delivered by MdM and their benefits will be provided to patients during consultations and/or in-situ health education sessions. Although MdM has an extensive experience in KP, it would be the first support to PHC in a merged district. Therefore, this intervention will test the acceptability by the community, to gradually gain trust. The introduction of community-based activities would then be considered for future interventions.

1- Project Activities

Result 1: the target health facilities are strengthened to provide quality PHC to the catchment population.Under this result, MdM will support 3 non-functional or highly under-performing health facilities and will improve the quality of PHC services delivered to the overall catchment population. Activity 1.1. Minor rehabilitation and provision of equipment, including PPEs: the quality of the technical platform will be improved, i.e infrastructure, equipment, material (including PPEs), essential medicines, appointment of skilled staff, especially female doctors and lady health visitors. Activity 1.2. Training for Ministry of Health and MdM staff on Psychological First Aid, pharmaceutical management and Rational Use of Drugs, Infection Control and Prevention for COVID-19, Disease Early Warning System, and Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness: it will allow staff to adequately apply all medical, hygiene and management SOPs. IPC measures will provide safe environment for staff and patients, and contribute to reducing the human transmission of COVID-19.Activity 1.3. Provision of free curative, mental health and psychological consultations: the following services will be provided free of charges for all patients coming to the facilities: consultation will target all age and gender of the catchment population with a focus on children under 5 years old who are amongst the most vulnerable category. Counselling of psychological and mental health issues will be provided by clinical psychologist. Activity 1.4. Provision of general health education sessions (individual or in groups) for the population visiting to targeted health facilities: health workers will conducted the sessions aiming (1) in preventing disease and improving health status by promoting good health and hygiene practices and (2) in promoting the use of the large package of health services available, including SRH services that are not commonly used.Activity 1.5. Assisting DoH in provision of in-clinic vaccination services to visiting children in the target health facilities: MdM will strengthen in-clinic vaccination services by attaching additional human resource to take care of static vaccination services . This will allow DoH vaccination staff to focus on outreach vaccination activities. Result 2: the targeted catchment population has access to quality Sexual and Reproductive Health services.Although SRH is part of PHC, it constitutes a separate result to emphasize the focus on women’s health. Healthcare provision for the general population is inadequate but the situation for pregnant women is alarming, especially in KPMD where it is extremely difficult to appoint trained female staff. This result is important to promote the use of the panel of services available.Activity 2.1. Training of medical staff on SRH rights (SRHR), including Maternal, Neonatal and Child Health (MNCH) and GBV: it will ensure that health services are conducted as per quality criteria. MdM will train the medical staff on Minimum Initial Service Package that comprehensively covers all aspects of SRHR.Activity 2.2. Provision of free access to reproductive care: MdM will provide antenatal, postnatal consultation, FP services, and RH consultation to women of reproductive age (15-49 years). The obstetric children with medical complications will be referred to nearest DHQ for further management. Activity 2.3. Provision of free access to family planning (FP) service: MdM will encourage women in child-bearing age coming to the facilities to gradually access FP services. The access to a minimum of 4 modern FP methods will be guaranteed. Due to high levels of illiteracy and cultural constraints, FP is a very sensitive issue in the project area.Activity 2.4. Identification of GBV survivors and provision of psychological consultations: orientation of staff on the identification of GBV survivors and on referral pathways that include the referral to psychological counselling.

2 - Project Indicators

Overall objective: To provide medical humanitarian relief assistance to crisis affected and host population of merged districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.Specific objective: To contribute in improving access to free comprehensive Primary Health Care services including sexual and reproductive healthcare and integrated maternal, neonatal and child health in Kurram District, KPMD.• Indicator 1: 30% of monthly increase in the use of the target health facilities;• Indicator 2: 80% of patients are satisfied with the services provided at the target health facilities;• Indicator 3: 30% reduction of childhood illnesses through improved IYCF practices Result 1: the target health facilities are strengthened to provide quality PHC to the catchment population.• 1.1 85% of consultation meet the quality criteria (“Complaint-Diagnosis-Prescription” concordance rate for a given protocol);• 1.2 80% of men and women diagnosed in-clinic with mental and psychological health issues will receive psychological counseling;• 1.3 60% of catchment population received primary healthcare services. Result 2: the targeted catchment population has access to quality Sexual and Reproductive Health services.• 2.1 60% of pregnant women of the catchment population coming for antenatal care services• 2.2 30% of post-natal women coming the health facility received FP services (counselling & consultations);• 2.3 40% of delivered women visit for PNC at least once during puerperal period.
