Project Summary

ADRA’s key targets in this intervention will include; 8,000 HHs with an estimated 48,000 individuals from most vulnerable different households with major focus on Women headed households, child headed houses, households with big family sizes, Elderly households, both girls and boys in the community, People living with disabilities (PLWDs), single mothers and the chronically ill persons. The target distribution of households will be; 50% women, 30% youths (girls and boys) and 20% men whereas the other interest groups have been captured among the indicated percentages above. This project aims at enabling Access to SAFE cooking fuel energy to the most vulnerable persons in Damboa IDP camps and Host communities. The project will procure and distribute 8,000 energy saving-cooking stoves to 8,000 HHs in Damboa LGA selecting from both IDPs and HCs. The project will majorly carter for New Arrivals in Damboa camps. ADRA will foster reduced vulnerability of IDPs through increased access to gender-responsive safe fuel energy and livelihood opportunities. The Project will train selected 80 (ToTs) beneficiaries with skills in making locally made energy saving stoves and manufacture of briquettes using organic materials that are environmental friendly which will promote resilience by engaging in locally made energy stoves business who will eventually train other community members. The project integrated the focus on increase of protection and GBV prevention and strengthens referral mechanisms for the IDPs and host communities by forming complaints and feedback mechanisms and issues of Protection and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse prevention will be fostered. The project will carry out training activities in diversified protection and GBV prevention, aspects that would include; prevention mechanisms, avoidance and reporting and referrals that can curb down the prevalence of risks associated with the access to fuel energy and firewood. The project will also target 9,600 indirect beneficiaries in the same locality who are anticipated to gain from the project outcomes as HH members. The project plans to form 80 groups, which will be provided with a start up capital of 450,000 Naira to facilitate them, manufacture local stoves and briquettes. This will lead to establishing micro trade businesses for the trained groups focusing on women households to engage in locally made stoves and briquettes business. The Project will carry out training in nutrition aspects to ensure improved food utilization and consumption. ADRA will incorporate Cash based initiatives (CBI) like cash for work (CFW) in the Access to SAFE energy intervention to the returnees and IDPs as one way of boosting their livelihoods and markets functionalities.

1 - Needs

The assessed needs that this intervention will address shall include the following: (1) Lack of access to safe and sufficient energy which has been identified as a major cause of stiffness between IDPs and host communities’ relations and high levels of protection and GBV issues including abductions, attacks, rape, disappearances of young girls and women and able-bodied men among others. Especially including General Hospital IDP camp, Aburi camp, Hausari IDP camp, and Central Primary School IDP camp in Damboa Central, as well as Damboa host community and Nzuda Wuyaram, Gumsuri, and Azir Multe host communities. (2) Lack of firewood itself within the vicinity of the camps and the communities have often increased the exposure of the women and girls to the risks of abductions, attacks and rapes by the insurgents during their search for firewood.The people of Azir Multe ward are particularly more vulnerable as they are limited by how far they can go to fetch firewood because of the proximity of the insurgents to their communities. Firewood is scarce, and charcoal is very expensive to purchase and even access. (3) Lack of energy which has caused tremendous food insecurity and protection concerns in Damboa LGA; where a massive population living in concentrated camps with regular new arrivals. (4) There is likelihood of having malnutrition due to poor feeding in Damboa especially because people have continued to skip meals, eat half-baked meals, or eat reduced meals to save energy for another day or meal. (5) Long distances being trekked to access firewood in forests. Female IDPs and host community women and girls have to travel more than 8km to and fro in order to access cooking fuel for about 5 times a week. The military in Damboa has also organised the firewood fetchers into coordinated groups who can only go far into the forest to fetch firewood on designated days, and these must be accompanied by armed soldiers. This procedure, because of the risks involved has caused the cost of firewood and charcoal to skyrocket. (6) The military for the security of the population in Damboa LGA, have restricted movements which worsens the situation by not allowing IDPs to move to bushes to look for firewood (7) Deforestation problem and environmental degradation problems because of the regular felling of trees and lack of replacement (8) Lack of cash to purchase firewood and charcoal from vendors in the nearby host communities such as KachallaBulari and NzudaMairi and IDP camp surroundings. An average cost of firewood in Damboa generally ranges between NGN 50 and NGN300, per bundle. (9) Protection especially gender based violence; abductions, rapes are prevalent due to lack of access to SAFE energy.It is very difficult to obtain alternative sources of energy in these communities as it is in most communities in Damboa LGA and no humanitarian agency has distributed or even trained the IDPs and host communities on how to access alternative energy to firewood.(10) Poor feedback and complaints mechanisms and structures in communities. However, there are in place feedback and complaint mechanisms, which in the event of any protection or other issues, beneficiaries may seek to be heard. But the functionality of the structures and justice delivery is still lacking due to the cultures and norms of the society that sidelines the women and girls in the community hence putting them at risk of experiencing GBV. ADRA plans to fulfill its commitment to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse through the following approach: • Ensuring that all ADRA staff and volunteers, as well as beneficiaries and target communities, are aware of what SEA is, the risks and consequences related to SEA.• Ensuring prevention of SEA by ADRA staff and volunteersthrough safe recruitment, safe cooperative arrangements, safe programming and safe communication practices, and by promoting an environment where all ADRA personnel and volunteers work to minimise the risks of SEA.

2 - Activities

The activities be to be carried out to address these needs are as follows: Activity 1.1.1: Recruit personnel with required skills and experiences to implement the activities. Activity 1.1.2: Carry out a baseline survey before selection of beneficiaries and distribution of energy saving stoves and solar lanterns Activity 1.1.3: Carry out Community sensitisation on SAFE fuel energy project in Damboa LGA, Borno State. Activity 1.1.4: Identify, select and register 8000 HHs targeted beneficiaries consisting 65% female and 35% male according to the selection criteria. Activity 1.1.5: Delivery of 8000 SAFE energy kit (stoves and solar lantern) to Damboa LGA. Activity 1.1.6: Distribute 8000 SAFE energy kit to registered beneficiaries, including women and girls, men and boys selected from the most vulnerable populations in Damboa. Activity 1.1.7: Conduct training for beneficiaries on Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) and use of fuel- efficient stove and solar lantern e.g. appropriate filling of combustion chamber, etc. Activity 1.1.8: Carry out post- distribution assessments for 1600 HHs beneficiaries to ascertain adoption rate of the newly introduced technology Activity 1.1.1: Recruit 12 skilled project staff to carry out project implementation. Activity 1.1.2: Train 24 project staff and volunteers at inception on gender equality, SGBV, women’s/human rights, social exclusion and SRHR. Activity 1.1.3: Conduct trainings on SGBV risk, reporting and referral mechanisms and care for survivors for the 12-project staff. Activity 2.1.1: Training of 80 Trainers (52 female, 28 male) on the production of locally made-energy stoves and briquettes. Activity 2.1.2: Conduct post- training tests for trainers to measure the level of skills acquired and capacity to impart knowledge to the beneficiaries Activity 2.1.3: Carry out periodic monitoring of 80 trainers to ensure step- down training to other beneficiaries. Activity 2.2.1: Support the establishment of 80 community- based groups consisting of 20 members each (65% female youth and 35% male youth) from the 5 IDP camps in Damboa Central and 3 host communities of Gumsuri, NzudaWuyaram and AzirMulte of Damboa LGA to engage in locally made stoves manufacture. Activity 2.2.2: Provide start-up capital for the 80 community-based groups at N465, 000 per group composed of women (65%) and male (35%) Activity 2.2.3 Carry out 10 Follow-up assessments on the quality of locally made stoves produced by the 80 CBGs for commercial purposes. Activity 3.1.1: Conduct follow- up trainings on nutrition and Hygiene activities on a regular basis for 8000HHs beneficiaries. Activity 3.1.2: Conduct monthly post- distribution monitoring to ascertain improvement in health and nutritious food consumption of beneficiaries as result of increased access to fuel energy. Activity 3.1.3: Carrying out PDM and house hold visits to 8000HHs in IDPs camps and host community Activity 3.1.4 Conduct household SAFE energy awareness campaigns among the 8000beneficiaries Activity 3.1.5 Identify and establish complaints and feedback mechanisms for reporting any issues related to SAFE Energy technology challenges E.g. Help desk, hotline, complaints and suggestion box, community- based SAFE Energy volunteers etc. Activity 4.1.1: Identify and sensitize 8000 HHs beneficiaries on the need for SGBV and protection training. Activity 4.1.2: Identify and train 50 (30 women, 20 men) community based GBV Prevention Promoters and Anti SGBV Community Volunteers on SGBV and PSEA related issues. Activity 4.1.3: Produce IEC materials for SGBV and protection awareness targeted at 24000IDPs, returnees and HC (women, girls, men and boys). Activity 4.1.4: SGBV and protection awareness training in the community for 24000 IDPs, returnees and women and men plus surrounding host community. Activity 4.1.5: Carry out post- awareness test and demonstration for 8000 HHs beneficiaries.
