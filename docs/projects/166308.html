Project Summary

The project will be conducted using community based network and structures to prevent and control the outbreak of Covid 19, taking into account inclusive community participation and gender equality and to promote sustainable Wash services benefiting 33500 thousand IDPs, in Camp and Host communities, in Ran, Pulka, Monguno, Dikwa and Ngala through risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) to inform decision making related to personal risk, mitigate rumors, share information and advice between experts, communities and officials, and to effectively involve communities in the response to control the outbreak of the Covid 19 through Infection prevention and control (IPC) in Camps, Houses, schools and communities using WASH, RCCE and Education programs.

1 - Needs

North-Eastern Nigeria is highly endemic to diarrheal and other diseases (malaria, measles, Lasa fever, Meningitis, Hep E) and chronic underdevelopment compounded by poverty and years of conflict and displacement. The lack of adequate potable water and sanitation infrastructure coupled with extreme flooding has seen cholera and AWD cases rise every rainy season while the protracted conflict has kept IDPs in overcrowded camps and overstretched the little resources and infrastructure in host community. Repeated displacement, trauma, reduced and/or obliterated coping mechanisms, poor living conditions, exposure to violence and loss and has left much of the population in the Northeast vulnerable. Women, children, persons with disabilities and the elderly are particularly vulnerable. Pre-existing gender inequalities, lack of access to land and basic services, widespread sexual violence and engagement in negative coping mechanisms demonstrate the serious impacts the crises have on the most vulnerable. This vulnerability is prone to attack from the global Coronavirus (Covid-19) Novel coronaviruses, or a new strain of coronavirus not previously identified in humans, pose a potential threat to children and communities worldwide. Coronaviruses cause varying degrees of illness, ranging from the common cold to more severe diseases such as MERS and SARS. The experience of SARS and MERS, both of which were novel coronaviruses at the time of discovery, highlight the risk of high public health impact. In Nigeria 1,932 cases have already been identified, as of May 1st , 2020, and the government is taking measures to slow the continues spread of COVID-19 throughout the country. there is an urgent need to provide lifesaving water, sanitation and hygiene services for the affected population through engaging the community to protect women, children and from exposure to the novel coronavirus, and its impact WASH Sector Emergency preparedness and Response for Covid 19_updated 24/02/2020

2 - Activities

Identify key influence (e.g community leaders, religious leaders, health workers), networks (women’s groups, youth groups, community health workers, social mobilizers for polio, malaria, HIV and trusted channels of communication (local media, radio, social media, hotlines) to strengthen or establish a system for community engagement at all community and Camps level levels To build and develop the capacity of community based level operation Management and hygiene promotion volunteers, Community Base Approach Door to Door Hygiene promotion and Awareness raising using key messaging on Social distancing practiced as much as possible during distribution of WASH NFIs, water collection points, queues at latrines and bathing facilities also on how to prevent Covid-19 infection and stop disease transmission through regular hand washing with soap after sneezing/coughing, before collecting water, covering mouth and nose with flexed elbow or tissue when coughing and sneezing – avoid close contact with anyone who has fever and cough, attention to multi touch surfaces (doors, chairs, tables, phones etc), existing resources will be used to develop/adapt communication materials (infographics, posters, video clips), including for outreach through mass media. Also specific Messaging targeting children, parent/caregivers, pregnant Mothers, Elderly, people with disability, depending on how covid 19 impacted such group of people and their Families, this would include, Key stakeholders and at-risk groups, such as health care providers (e.g. public, private, traditional practices, community health workers – for case detection, management, referral, and infection prevention and control). Strengthen community-based mechanisms (hotlines, surveys, rumor tracking) to inform the response. Involve traditional media and social media monitoring, local communication systems and networks. Setting up of hand washing stations in strategic and public places within the camps and Distribution of hygiene kits, soap and cleaning supplies for latrines, home and health facility disinfection. Provision of WASH services in health facilities to enhanced water supply, sanitation as well as adapted management of medical waste. Providing improved access to safe water supply by daily chlorination and monitoring residual chlorine level at water pointEnsure provision of safe water supply from sources to consumption by promoting community level water safety plan Sensitization to mitigate on open defecation practice in the camps/ Host communities during Covid 19 through participatory community base Approach of latrine maintenance acceptable to the community members. Hygiene promotion to promote and encourage good hygiene practice, gender equality using participatory community base approach Conduct risk information study of camps/ communities identify risk practice
