Project Summary

Cordaid provides health care services in four formers states of Eastern Equatorial, Unity, Upper Nile and Greater Bahr El-Ghazel in South Sudan under the Health Pool Fund (HPF) and UNICEF developmental funding schemes. Due to repeated conflict and flooding in and around these geographical regions, there has been massive displacement and settlement in various parts within the states. The displaced populations are constrained by access to basic and essential health care due to limited health infrastructure, inadequate or lack of trained human resource, drugs and other supplies and bad road network making referrals to other nearby health facility difficult. The facilities supported by existing developmental funds are limited in numbers and in most cases located beyond 5 metres from the displaced population making it impossible to access health services at its dire period.
Following relative security calm in some parts of the Country, returnees from neighbouring Countries, and those from within IDP sites are slowly returning home though a good number of IDPs are still hesitant to return home due to fear of insecurity or returning home with nothing or fear of not able to access basic services like safe water, food, shelter, and basic health care services. Lack of trained health care workers and unavailability of drugs and other supplies also compounded the health situations of the vulnerable populations. As a result of increasing number of returnees in Bentiu Hospital, OPD consultation increased from 12,943 to 28581 between April – June 2019 and July – September 2019 with diarrhoea, pneumonia, malnutrition and malaria among the top ten causes morbidity (DHIS2 2019). Antenatal coverage and facility-based deliveries and immunisation services are equally sub optimal.
Similarly, there has been episodes of disease outbreaks among IDPs, returnees and host population attributed to limited access to health services and low immunization coverage - below 45% across the Country (MOH – 2019). Measles coverage is equally low which explains frequent outbreaks witnessed in different parts of the Country. Since beginning of 2019, measles outbreaks were confirmed in 12 counties and four PoC sites (Juba, Bentiu, Wau and Jur river). Affected counties were Abyei, Juba, Pibor, Gogrial West and East, Mayom, Melut, Aweil South, west and East, Tonj North, Wau and jur river. Cumulative total of 1,214 cases reported in the 12 counties and four PoC sites with 79 laboratory-confirmed cases and seven (CFR=0.59%) deaths (MOH/WHO 2019).
This project is intended to bridge the huge gaps in health care service delivery among the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities by providing access to essential health care services that aims at reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, improvement of access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations and increase access to services for survivors of SGBV in Wau, Jur River, greater Magwi, Mayom, Fashoda and Rubkona. A total of 66,667 vulnerable population is targeted to be reached with essential live serving health care services in two referral hospitals, 5 PHCCs and 10 PHCUs. The project will address health needs of both IDPs, returnees and host community. It is also believed that availing the health services to both IDP & host communities reduces the risk of conflict between them.
Also due to active Ebola outbreak in DRC, there is high risk of EVD spilling into South Sudan through the porous border points with DRS and transit Country bordering DRC. Therefore, this project will scale up EVD preparedness response in the high-risk County of greater Magwi that register huge number of ground border crossing from Uganda. An average of 106,888 persons are screened for EVD per week in Greater Magwi (EWARS – 2019).
Cordaid existing operation in all the above locations will ease swift response in an event of catastrophic surge.

2 - Activities

The project intends to provide health care services in six Counties with focus on two Hospitals, five PHCCS and ten PHCUs. Activities to be undertaken are as described below: The project will embark on emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases and outbreak investigations by prepositioning of emergency commodities, supplies and investigation kits and recruitment of qualified staffs.The project will provide 24 hours case management of morbidities in 2 hospitals and 5 PHCCs in the five Counties. Case management of disease will as well be rendered in 10 PHCUs and in case of health emergency, the project will support referrals to the next level of management. For effective case management, the project will recruit qualified staffs to fill in staffs’ gaps. OPD and in-patient services will be provided.The project will source for cold chain and vaccines that will be delivered to the various supported facilities to provide uninterrupted routine immunization both through fix and outreaches will be provide to the vulnerable community by the trained vaccinators.WASH and IPC in health facilities through procurement and supplies of basic IPCs supplies and provision to the health facilities and training health staffs on IPC protocols. EVD IPC supplies will be sourced from the UNICEF WASH team while the project will purchase and fill the gaps of the missing IPC supplies.Minor rehabilitation will also be carried out during the project period to provide infrastructure upon which essential care services can be provided. In areas that has completely no structure in place, the project will erect tents that can provide health services to the vulnerable community.The project will provide capacity building for health care workers through refresher trainings and on job training to address the knowledge gaps that have been identified in various thematic areas of health services provision.IEC production and dissemination to all health facilities will be undertaken during the project period. The project will also extend sourcing for available IEC materials from the core pipeline partners like UNICEF, WHO and UNFPA.For effective disease surveillance and outbreak response, the project will provide disease surveillance tools, ensure weekly collection, compilation and submission of IDSR reports. The project will as ensure capacity building through offering refresher and on job trainings to health staffs to impact them with knowledge and skills for effective disease surveillance.The project intent to provision uninterrupted essential Health commodities to health facilities by timely sourcing of the health Kits from WHO, UNICEF and UNFPA. Buffer stock supplies and commodities will be swiftly procured and supplied to the different sites serving the affected population.The project will extend services from the static facility to identified affected and hard to reach communities through integrated mobile outreach response. CEMoNC will be provided in the 2 referral hospitals in addition to the BEmONC whereas all other PHCCs will provide BEmONC services to address sexual and reproduction health services. ANC and clean – normal delivery will be provided in PHCUs by trained MCHW.Management of SAM with medical complications will be provided in 2 hospital of Wau and Bentiu by recruited qualified staffs and use of available medication/drugs provided by the project.Using the available drugs, commodities and qualified human resource, the project will provide CMR services in 2 hospitals that will serve as referral points and in the 5 PHCCS and 10 PHCUs in the intended locations. The project will coordinate with other actors providing MHPSS and ensure referral where need be.The project also intends to ensure surveillance of attacks on Health care in 5 Counties by Reports on Vandalization of health care infrastructure and theft of equipment, essential medicine and supplies.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure they abide to Core Humanitarian Standards and Quality (CHS) and SPHERE Standards which provides implementation of project in an open and transparent way to all direct and indirect beneficiaries through integrated social accountability to affected populations and marginalized groups.The project will institute health facility committees to act as liaison between the community and service providers. The community will engage the beneficiaries to identify their health need and priorities at Village, Boma, Payam and County Level.The project will also use IEC material and both social and mainstream media to communicate and share key health messages and get feedback from beneficiaries. This will be followed by periodic meetings with community at Village, Boma, Payam and County level to discuss the health gaps, challenges, priorities and needs at all levels through Boma Health Initiatives.The project will integrate community dialogues and community sensitization forum to provide the affected population with life saving information and come up with community-based action points. This will be done through models of citizen voice action aimed at strengthening meaningful participation of beneficiaries as lobby and advocacy tool at community level.Feedback from beneficiaries will be collected during stakeholder engagement forums and meeting which are organized through administrative leadership at Payam and county level. While at facility level the project will ensure there is suggestion box and enquiry desk where beneficiaries can share their feedback and complaints to be acted upon.The project will work through the elective and administrative leadership and offices so that they can get to know the views and ideas of beneficiaries from their representative. The community level feedback and complaints will be received through local leaders and chiefs.The project will also work through schools to get feedback and complaints from students and teachers
