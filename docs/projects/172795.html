Project Summary

Libya is and will continue to be a destination and transit country for a large number of refugees and asylum-seekers moving in the context of mixed migration flows and who are perceived by the Libyan authorities as illegal migrants who should either be sent back to their countries of their origin, evacuated or resettled to third countries. As a result of this restrictive policy and the negative public attitudes that they are subjected to, refugees and asylum-seekers are exposed to a wide range of protection risks including arbitrary arrest and detention, harassment, abuse and exploitation, in addition to facing serious difficulties in meeting their basic needs and those of their family members. Under these circumstances, UNHCR, together with its partners, will continue to provide a wide range of life-saving protection and assistance services across various sectors of intervention such as protection, shelter and NFI support, food and financial assistance, registration and documentation, healthcare services, PSS, while redoubling efforts aimed at securing the release of refugees and asylum-seekers being held in detention and finding durable solutions out of Libya through humanitarian evacuation and direct resettlement to third countries for the most vulnerable of them who will be carefully identified on the basis of pre-established eligibility criteria both from within the urban refugee population and those kept in detention.
Under this multi-sectoral project, a total of 68,219 persons of concern (POCs) will be targeted with an amount of $24,440,268, broken down as follows:
- 33,903 POCs will benefit from protection activities ($15,000,000);
- 15,400 POCs will benefit from healthcare services ($3,500,000);
- 9,650 POCs with benefit from multi-sectoral cash assistance ($3,530,100);
- 9,266 POCs will benefit from shelter/NFIs assistance ($2,410,168).

1 - Needs

Libya is not party to the 1951 Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees or its Protocol. It has ratified the 1969 Convention Governing the Specific Aspects of Refugee Problems in Africa (OAU Convention) and is also party to the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (“Banjul Charter”). While the right to asylum is provided for in Article10 of Libya’s 2011 interim Constitutional Declaration, there is no asylum legislation or any established asylum procedures. As a result, all non-Libyans, regardless of their status or protection needs, including asylum-seekers and refugees, fall under national immigration laws. Libyan laws criminalize all irregular entry, stay, or exit, for example without the appropriate documentation or through unofficial border posts, without distinguishing between asylum-seekers/refugees, migrants, or victims of trafficking. Over 45,000 refugees and asylum-seekers are registered with UNHCR. Refugees and asylum seekers face discrimination and challenges in access to basic services, while at risk of arrest and arbitrary detention in inhumane conditions. The decreasing resources in an economy damaged by conflict and COVID-19, limited cash liquidity and sharp price increases pose additional challenges for refugees and asylum seekers. Protection challenges are a grave concern, primarily driven by exposure, vulnerability and inability to cope with conflict and violence, human rights violations and abuses, breakdown of rule of law, and impediments to access goods and services. Refugees and asylum-seekers face extreme stress associated with displacement, insecurity, loss of livelihoods and other challenges related to living in a conflict environment which often results in negative coping mechanisms, including early marriage, survival sex, and widespread domestic violence. Lack of awareness and information regarding SGBV services, result further in most survivors of rape and sexual assault not having access to emergency medical assistance, including clinical management of rape. Over 1,300 unaccompanied and separated children are in dire need of interest assessment, specialized services, and alternative care arrangements and support. A long-standing system of arbitrary detention of refugees and asylum seekers continues to persist, with more than 2,000 refugees and migrants held in around a dozen detention centres after arrest upon disembarkation following interception or rescue at sea, or arrest in urban areas for migration-related reasons. Conditions in detention are dire, with refugees including children, women and girls are often held in unsafe environments where they suffer ill-treatment including sexual violence and torture, and where basic needs for safety, sanitation, hygiene, food and support are unmet. Refugees and asylum-seekers in urban areas lack sufficient information about rights and services, require legal advice and assistance. Half of migrants and refugees surveyed for the 2020 MSNA reported being unable to cover at least one of their basic needs for food and shelter. Border closures, disrupted trade, movement restrictions due to COVID-19 and curfews have resulted in significant price hikes of essential goods. JMMI monthly market monitoring has recorded the prices of essential food and other goods were 10 per cent above pre-COVID-19 levels as of September 2020. The loss of livelihoods coupled with price hikes have exacerbated the economic vulnerability of refugees and migrants and therefore these households tend to resort to negative coping mechanism. Public healthcare services in Libya have been heavily impacted by the prolonged conflict and have also become overstretched due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities

1. Monitoring and life-saving assistance in detention in accordance with the Principled Framework and UN HRDDP provisions; identify and assist most vulnerable including victims of trafficking and smuggling, seeking solutions to the most urgent problems through registration and referral to evacuation and resettlement. 2. Capacity building to the authorities on human rights and refugee law, including principle of non-refoulement. 3. Advocacy to end detention and recognize UNHCR documentation. 4. Life-saving interventions at disembarkation points, based on consistent monitoring and emergency response capacity. 5. Community outreach activities, including CwC strategy, Mobile Information Desks in Zawiya and Misrata, mobile registration missions, and pilot access to resettlement for refugees in the East, while increasing legal advice and assistance in the West. 6. Structured PSS activities. 7. Specific services for persons with specific needs and persons with disabilities. 8. Child protection activities including psychosocial support, educational activities, awareness-raising activities, and case management. Through the UNHCR-UNICEF Blueprint Initiative, expand capacity in BIAs/BIDs, strengthen referrals for CP and informal education to UNICEF Bayti Centres and develop partnerships with the MoE, MOSA, Social Registry and local authorities/actors including improvement to education facilities. 9. Provision of Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to the most vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers to meet their basic needs. 10. Provision of humanitarian lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support. 11. Enhance access to healthcare services, including primary healthcare and referrals to secondary healthcare.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNHCR activities are guided through participatory assessments, carried out comprehensively in 2019 for refugees and asylum-seekers in Libya. Facing limitations on direct consultation due to COVID, UNHCR introduced a Protection Needs Assessment mechanism, through which over 4,000 individual refugees have been consulted on needs and concerns, the results of which inform 2021 planning. Video consultations on key issues with community members began in 2020 and will continue. UNHCR supported a Migrant and Refugee-dedicated Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, relied upon by all actors to ensure that programming is based on affected population views and data. In 2020 a joint UNHCR-NRC Call Center was introduced to better facilitate access to information and feedback channels. CFM data indicates that a large percentage of calls provide refugee and migrant related information. Efforts are ongoing in 2020 to establish a new CwC strategy for implementation in 2021, including CwC survey of persons of concern, establishment of Help@UNHCR and KnowledgeBase online tools, and new outreach and feedback materials. UNHCR has an established investigation body, the IGO, and requires all participating partners to have such mechanisms. Complaint boxes are available at all facilities, and regularly checked and communications addressed with feedback to each complainant or communicant.
