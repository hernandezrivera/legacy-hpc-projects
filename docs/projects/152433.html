Project Summary

In response to the Critical conditions (level 4 out of 5) estimated by the latest IPC round for acute malnutrition in Ikwoto for October 2018, the project intends to save the lives of malnourished vulnerable groups -- pregnant and lactating women, and children under five years of age – by increasing access to timely lifesaving management of acute malnutrition and enhancing malnutrition-preventive MIYCN counselling, for both displaced people and the hosting community.
In particular, in addition to continue to support 8 health-facilities-based OTPs and 16 health-facilities-based TSFP AVSI intends to facilitate the establishment of 8 OTPs. Key activities will include trainings (CMAM, MIYCN, nonspecific protection topic and psychosocial support), nutrition/health workers remuneration, rehabilitation of nutrition facilities (including a stabilization center to ensure protection for the vulnerable beneficiaries) promotion of MIYCN counseling, and operation related cost.
A total of 3,120 and 10,475 children (6-59 months) with SAM and MAM respectively will be targeted to access lifesaving treatment of malnutrition. The project will also target 3661 PLW to be treated for moderate acute malnutrition. 9,231/13,538 pregnant and lactating women are targeted for individual/group MIYCN counselling respectively in this project.
Moreover, the intervention will leverage other initiatives put in place in Ikwoto by AVSI such as food security, WASH, education and health interventions. Integration strategies will include: linking malnourished vulnerable households to short-term resilience food security assistance (GFD); the establishment of homestead small scale vegetable production; provision of preventive and curative basic maternal child health care services such as malaria testing for children, immunization, and antenatal services; the provision of WASH NFI for caretakers of malnourished children and drinking water and hand washing facilities for all nutrition; the establishment of health clubs and routine nutrition and health promotion awareness campaigns in primary schools.
The strategic objectives to which this project intend to contribute are: SO1: Increased access to timely management of acute malnutrition among children (boys and girls) less than five years, pregnant and Lactating women SO2: Increased malnutrition preventive MIYCN counselling among vulnerable group (PLW, caretakers of children under five female, male, boys and girls). SO3: Enhanced access to Integrated Nutrition and other relevant sector (FSL, Health, WASH, Protection, and Education) interventions and responses in Ikwoto county SO4: Improved nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response decision making in Ikwoto county.

Needs

The food security and nutrition conditions of Ikwoto continue to be alarming and, after a slight improvement in conjunction with the peak of the harvesting season, they are expected to continue to be so in 2019. Based on the most recent data (FSNMS round 22) the UN-led IPC analysis estimated for October 2018 the food security condition of Ikwoto to be in Crises (IPC Phase 3), and the level of malnutrition to show Critical conditions (level 4 out of 5). The latest SMART survey for Ikwoto, conducted by AVSI on November 2017, indicated a serious nutrition situation as per the WHO malnutrition thresholds. Based on weight-for-height measurements, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 10.3% (7.9-13.3 95% C.I.) and a Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate of 1.6% (0.9-3.1 95% C.I.). Moreover, the SMART survey revealed some marked difference, whereby the communities located in the highlands, with a bimodal rainfall regime, enjoy only a limited incidence of Acute Malnutrition, whereas the communities located in the most arid payams show very alarming level of Acute malnutrition. In particular, AVSI estimated that on November 2017, on the pick of the harvest season the GAM rate in Geria (Hatire and Imotong) was of 8.8% (5.9-13.2 95% C.I.), while in Chorokol and Chahari (an area comprising around 1/3 of the population of Ikwoto county) was of 13.8% (6.9-25.7 95%C.I.), bordering, in spite of the harvesting season, a Critical condition.The SMART studies shows that the main drivers of malnutrition in Ikwoto were: poor infant and Young Child Feeding practices (IYCF), only 15.2% of children 6-23 months was consuming the minimum acceptable diet; exclusive breastfeeding to the age of 6 months (78%) was below the World Health Organization recommended standard of >80%; the proportion of infants reportedly put to the breast within the first hour of birth was also low at 76.2%; introduction of solid, semi-solid or soft foods at the age of 6 months was found to be low at 51.5%. Based on food security indicators, moreover, AVSI’s own multi-sector survey (423 HH Mutli-cluster survey) shows that the limited production and consumption of protein-rich food is among the major reasons for this structural level of malnutrition. These findings are confirmed by the data presented at the September IPC 2018, which reveal that the people of Ikwoto have one of the most limited diet in the country. In particular, pulses, dairy, meat proteins, fruits and sugar are consumed much below average, whereas only the consumption of cereals /grains/tubers is well above the average of the country. Currently Ikwoto has got 27 functional health facilities with only 4 PHCCs capable of providing comprehensive integrate maternal child health care, most of the facilities have got inadequate skilled personnel and basic equipment’s hence poor health care system. Nutrition services implemented currently is targeting treatment of acute malnutrition which is not comprehensive and with minimum integration. The Nutrition sites are not adequate and not easily accessible to majority in hard to reach settlement. In addition, there is only one stabilization center in the county which is not supported and lacks most of the vital required facilities and equipment.

Activities

• Establishment of 10 more OTP/TSFP sites in the existing health facilities to ensure availability of lifesaving management of acute malnutrition in Ikwoto county • Provision of all 26 OTP sites with malaria RDTs kits to test for malaria among SAM and cases and offer appropriate treatment and referral.• Provision of safe potable water, hand washing facilities and soaps to all the 26 OTP/TSFP sites• Rehabilitation of waiting shelters (Temporary/Semi permanent) in 4 OTP/TSFP sites • Training of health Facilities staff on both IYCF package counselling and CMAM according to MoH guideline.• Training of Nutrition sites front health workers on non-specific protection package and how to handle cases gender base violence. • Training of Community Nutrition Volunteers /Home health promoters on Nutrition Education, screening (anthropometric measurements) and referral • Establish and train 16 Mothers Support Groups (MSGs) on MIYCN package• Provision of MIYCN Counselling to targeted vulnerable groups both at facility and community level• Provision of Nutrition MIYCN IEC Material to all the Nutrition sites, and to Community (MSGs & CNVs)• Establishment of 1 Nutrition Mobile team • Conduct quarterly consultation meeting with the stakeholders and community key informants at Payams level to ensure full participation, involvement, and leadership/governance for the project • Conduct monthly Join (AVSI/County Health Department) support supervision to all the Nutrition sites for quality assurance, information sharing, stock monitoring and getting complaints.• Conduct Pre and Post project IYCF (MIYCN) KAP survey• Submission of Weekly and monthly Nutrition reports to the cluster, SMoH, and other relevant UN bodies and Authorities.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Access of Information to beneficiaries will be through Quarterly consultation meeting at community and stakeholders level inform focus group discussion with key informants, community awareness sessions, and coordination meetings at the County Health department. Moreover, Yearly AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and subcounty (payam) officials and traditional leaders (boma and sub-boma chiefs). During these events AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. Last September, for instance, in Ikwoto, along with the results of the 2018 Greater Ikwoto Multi Sector Household Survey, the need to move toward more development-oriented interventions and thus to apply more demanding criteria for the selection of beneficiaries was discussed with the communities. In that occasion AVSI received very positive feedbacks from the community as well as suggestions regarding operative mechanisms to employ for the implementation of projects.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be comprehensive with emphasis on gender sensitive issues especially in regards to differences in nutrition needs for specific gender/age group (women, PLW, adolescence girls/boys and children), and addressing traditional beliefs and myth that hinder access to nutrition requirement especially to women. Moreover, As we work with vulnerable people, the messages, training and guidance provided by AVSI personnel are tailored to the needs and capacities of people with almost no schooling experience and provided in the local language. Most of the field work realized by AVSI is facilitated by the employment of local personnel who speak the local language and have the notions and the experience needed to contextualize guidance and messages.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will have protection mainstreaming through appropriate nutrition response mechanisms that protects beneficiaries from increased insecurity risks in accessing services, Nutrition staff will be trained on non-specialized protection package to identify and refer children with protection concerns for specialized care. Special attention will be given to prevention of neglect among vulnerable groups to accessing nutrition services among care givers and nutrition staff. SEA code of conducts will be displayed at all services access points and awareness sessions, and nutrition services will be considered as potential entry point for GBV victims looking for assistance, with nutrition staff to be trained on GBV cases management and referral pathway

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The number of people consulted was populated from series of consultation meetings conducted between November 2017- March 2018 during community nutrition awareness creation and feedback that involved the community (pregnant and lactating women, adolescence and men) local authorities, community peer groups (mother support groups, HHP, CBDs), and traditional leaders in 5/8 Payams in the county with highest rates of malnutrition Ikotos, chahari, chorokol, losite and Imotong payams. Moreover, Besides the continuous dialogue with state, county and subcounty officials and traditional leaders, the interventions here proposed were crafted on the base of solid multi-sector household survey conducted in Ikwoto and Cueibet (Torit is ongoing), following a two-stage sampling of approximately 400 caretakers whom answered around 400 questions. The main findings of the surveys, and their implications in term of activities recommended, were discussed and validated through community meetings attended by RRC representatives, subcounty authorities (payam administrators) traditional leaders (boma and sub-boma chiefs) and open to the population at large.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Feedback mechanism will be through installation of suggestion box in all nutrition sites, and through focus group discussion with community key informants and beneficiaries. Moreover, Besides the annual surveys, AVSI has a tested system of Detailed Post-Distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community at large. During each field visit AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes for unanimous reporting of abuses or complains.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community will be involved in planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of this project to ensure governance and leadership that will result in ownership and sustainability of the project. Community will participate in ensuring safety of nutrition supplies, maintenance of safe environment and minor road rehabilitation (bush clearing), local resource mobilization for nutrition sites rehabilitation (construction of waiting shelter) and community will be open to access information and give feedback and complaints though out the project life cycle. Moreover, Every other month AVSI conducts community campaigns that allow for open discussion over the quality and progress of the activity conducted. Several actions, for instance Field Farming Schools, have built-in systems of community monitoring. Monthly AVSI reports to the FSL State cluster the result of its activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection analysis was done to ensure all obstacles compromising access to and utilization of services as well as all elements contributing to their higher health and nutrition related vulnerability are to be considered and adequately addressed. Moreover, AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, discapacities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 5 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Nutrition services shall be decentralised (increase coverage) to reduce walking time and reduce the risk of insecurity and gender base violence that might arise to beneficiaries due to inter-tribal differences and conflict that is common in Ikotos. More staffing at the health/nutrition facilities to reduce waiting time for the beneficiaries that might act as a barrier to services. Areas not having nutrition sites due to the nature of the settlement shall be reached with mobile team to offer integrated nutrition services. Moreover, besides the above-mentioned survey, for its largest projects AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind a follow up interview of Key informants, field personnel is conducted to review precisely the barrier that impede a more equitable targeting of the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers. This has led, to cite some examples, at timing the interventions of FSL more in line with the seasonality followed by farmers, and focusing educational support to nurseries and lower primary classes.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project monitoring activities to identify and address protection issues through conducting routine random Nutrition workers interview and beneficiaries’ satisfaction score during the project implementation in all the service access points. Moreover, AVSI’s Foundation shall ensure that the assistance during this project does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the women, men, girls and boys receiving it, and is provided in ways that respect their rights. Equal participation will be ensured through the involvement of women and men equally in decision-making right up from induction meetings, and routine focus group discussions. Likewise, it will be made sure that trainings will target at least an equal number of female and male, that the monitoring will includes sex- and age-disaggregated data, that the project personnel is trained on gender/ protection topics including GBV identification, handing and referral. Moreover, AVSI’s projects include activities of livelihood and capacity building especially tailored to women, individually and as women groups, and is matched by educational projects that always include GBV and peace building sensitization for pupils and training on GBV and referral path for teachers and PTA members. Finally, AVSI operates through numerous permanent bases distributed across the trageted communities and serves an adequate number of service sites equally distributed throughout the counties as a means to avoid crowding of beneficiaries per sites, reduced distance to access services, and thus diminish the gap for remote, inaccessible communities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Access to services will be monitored by carrying out midterm bottle neck analysis to identify key barriers and factors affecting access and continuity of services which shall be measured by analysis of coverage indicators e.g. proportion (%) of beneficiaries met against those in need who were targeted for a given estimated population of Ikotos as provided in the project plan, and other performance indicators such as cure rate, and defaulters rate shall also indicate access and continuity to services. Moreover, for its largest projects AVSI conducts impact analysis and in-depth post- distribution monitoring reviews, which are analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind a follow up interview of key informants and field personnel are conducted to review precisely the barrier that impede a more equitable targeting of the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers. This has led, to cite some examples, at timing the interventions of FSL more in line with the seasonality followed by farmers, and focusing educational support to nurseries and lower primary classes.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews for its projects that include questions about the safety of beneficiaries. For instance, post-distribution monitoring asks beneficiaries about any hazard met on the way to reach the distribution sites. Moreover, annually AVSI conducts multi-sector surveys which include numerous questions aimed at assessing the safety, perceived safety, incidence of hazards, and conflicts and disputes over natural resources

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will put The person (beneficiaries) at the centre with an immediate respect for their dignity and uniqueness and their inclusion in a fabric of family and social relationships. The project will ensure a person is never considered an isolated individual, nor defined by sociological categories or by the circumstances in which they find themselves (poverty, sickness, disability, war). This will be monitored by ensuring that marginalised/handicap beneficiaries (those with disabilities, mental illness etc.) are given high priority and reached equally with the service which should also be reflected in every reports. AVSI has among its core objectives to defend and enhance the dignity of the people that it serves. AVSI surveys include questions such as “Do you think you can bring a special and unique help to your family and community”, which are aimed at monitoring the level of self-esteem and capacity to control one owns life of the beneficiaries of AVSI support, for that is the ultimate objective pursued by AVSI.
