Project Summary

"In 2023 NRC Shelter & Settlement Programme aims to be part of the response to the Sector's two objectives. First, NRC will strive to improve emergency response and provide life-saving emergency Non-food Items (SNFI) support. Secondly, NRC will contribute to the provision of Shelter support, including Energy and HLP, for safe, dignified and improved shelter solutions access to both Rohingya refugees in the camps, and vulnerable crisis-affected populations in the host communities of Ukhiay & Teknaf Upazilla, Cox's Bazar District.
Under the SNFI Sector Objective 1, NRC’s main activity will be the provision of tie-down kits (TDK), accompanied by monsoon preparedness awareness-raising and/or technical support.
Under the SNFI Sector Objective 2, NRC will implement: Reinforcement of existing shelters' technical support, as part of the Shelter Upgrade and Maintenance (SUM) strategy, contributing through technical support as well as porter and construction labour support for EVHHs, including small-scale HH-level site improvement and adaptation of shelter and surroundings for people with disabilities, in Camp 8E in coordination with IOM pipeline partnership. NRC also will continue to explore alternatives that includes the use of recycled plastic materials for construction and raise awareness of repurposing and recycling shelter materials; Repair and maintenance of Mid Term Shelter in Camp 8E, using weather-resistant materials (treated bamboo, alternative materials) in coordination with site planners, including household-level site improvement as well as porter and construction labour support for EVHHs; Awareness sessions and technical support to increase community engagement and participation of women, older people, people with disabilities and gender diverse individuals in shelter and NFI activities in camp 8E; Provision of energy kits, which includes solar lamps in camp and host community; House, Land and Property (HLP) orientation and case management in camp and host community where NRC Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) programme will support the HLP TF with HLP assessments, development of guidance notes and advocacy papers as identified by the HLP TF in its work plan and HLP Strategy 2023-2025. NRC M&E will provide access to an independent and impartial complaints feedback mechanism. NRC will offer energy kits to the most vulnerable members of the host community as well. Host communities will be engaged in each stage of the decision-making to increase the empowerment and ownership of vulnerable individuals. NRC will also provide training and awareness on DRR to increase the host community’s resilience during emergencies.
NRC Shelter & Settlement program will work closely through an integrated approach with NRC’s WASH, ICLA and Education teams and undertake all new construction, repair and maintenance work. Shelter beneficiaries will be engaged through awareness campaigns and capacity building training on shelter upgrade and maintenance, small-scale HHs level site improvement, Shelter strengthening including bracing and proper use of TDK, healthy living environments including the ventilation and alignment with Sectors minimum standards, self-improvement oriented to reduce protection risks for people with special vulnerabilities, safe cooking space improvements and gather knowledge on pre-monsoon. NRC will undertake these activities using a professional training team who has speciality in gender, disability inclusion and PSEA. NRC will conduct all of these interventions following the latest Covid-19 guidelines.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The needs of the Rohingya refugees and host communities have been identified as a result of the Aug 2021 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessments, which identified shelter materials as the critical priority needs in the Rohingya camps and the energy (cooking fuel) fourth most critical need in the host communities; where 36% of Rohingya households reported not having made improvements to their shelter (in the 6 months before data collection). The Rohingya are acutely vulnerable to the effects of climate change as they are living in fragile shelters made of tarpaulin and bamboo. Existing shelter materials provided to the displaced population and improvements in camp infrastructure remain insufficient for protection against adverse weather events. Untreated bamboo has deteriorated due to beetle infestation. In addition, 86% of host community households report using firewood as a source of cooking fuel that creates an adverse effect on the environment. In August 2021, the J-MSNA found that 74% of refugees prioritized their need for shelter materials over food and self-reliance activities – an 11% increase from 2020. In July 2022, 4W data from the Shelter/NFI Sector showed that around 30% of refugee households had not received any non-emergency shelter assistance since 2020 due to COVID-19 restrictions (18 months in 2020 and 2021 when shelter assistance was limited to emergency support). Each year shelters are affected due to heavy monsoons, landslides and floods which require an emergency response within 24 hours for the life-saving of the affected population. Moreover, the Shelter/NFI Sector has established minimum quality standards for bamboo and produced guidance on bamboo selection and storage along with technical advice on how to avoid direct contact between treated bamboo and the ground.It is mandatory for Shelter/NFI partners to use treated bamboo in camp shelter activities. To reduce environmental and market impact from over-reliance on a single shelter material, the Shelter/NFI Sector will advocate the RRRC through the ISCG for approval to use alternative materials that are weather-resistant.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

NRC follows IASC Protection Policy to ensure a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability and d) participation & empowerment. NRC will add questions to the most affected population, in the need assessments, about safety, dignity, and access, including ways to improve this to have accurate information about their specific shelter needs. NRC will assess all Shelter programming to ensure that gender-related considerations are included and align with minimum shelter performance standards. During the beneficiary identification, NRC will assess specific barriers for EVI HHs, including PwD and LGBTIQ+ groups and ensure that shelters are accessible by consultation of specific changes where possible (ramps, larger door size, and larger shelter size when is feasible). All SNFI activities will include porter and construction labour support for EV HHs. All NRC SNFI staff, cash-for-work participants and volunteers should receive training on gender, GBV and PSEA.

3.2 - Gender

NRC always engage and consider the gender sensitivity in all programs and activities and reports will include sex, age and disability disaggregated data (SADDD). Safe and Inclusive (SIP) programming principles and confidentiality will be promoted throughout the project implementation. To mitigate risks of delivering inequitable assistances or reinforce pre-existing inequalities, NRC will ensure intervention is community led with meaningful participation of women in the decision making as well as involve men groups for gender balance. NRC will employ creative ways to ensure community structures have equal gender representatives, while avoiding practices that. Gender equality will be mainstreamed and each of the activities will include sensitization on the abandonment of GBV and promotion of women and girls’ rights.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

NRC operate the program with safeguarding concern and avoid child labours and concern to sex and age disaggregation, gender and disability marker. NRC targeted the individuals with wide range of criteria. These includes the individuals of Adult Females (18-59 years), Adult Males (18-59 years), and Adult Females (above 59 years), Adult Males (above 59 years), Unknown Sex and Age and Differently abled persons. Where possible, age and the law permit, NRC will encourage engagement of young and elderly people in shelter improvement activities through training process in order to achieve valuable skills, confidence, self-esteem and connectedness to the community.

3.4 - Diversity

Vulnerable refuges and host are at risk of protection concern, gender and age discrimination, person with special shelter needs (PWSSN) along with persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in accessing services. NRC will ensure access to equal service and BNF meaningful participations with gender and age sensitivity, separate que in distribution, protection concern and special attention for mostly vulnerable people with quality if service delivery.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

NRC always transparent with its service delivery and accountability also part of inclusive program that NRC will do by BNF orientation on the response and program modality, ensure BNF participation and promote into community decision making process, identify EVI HHs with a NRC technical assessment and community feedback, Provide training on technical how, receiving materials and freedom to choose the materials for shelter strengthen and Maintenance. Community people are open to share their ideas and complaints through CFM hotline number and drop box in camps that are always given a satisfactory answer to them.
