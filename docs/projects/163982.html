Project Summary

Increasing equitable access to health care is imperative to prevent morbidity/mortality/disability and further deterioration of vulnerabilities after more than 8 years of protracted conflict and disruption of health system in Syria. INTERSOS proposes to ensure re-establishment of disrupted essential care services for women children and elderly. A comprehensive quality PHC package of interventions will be implemented by rehabilitation of 4 HFs in Dier-az-Zor; Dar’a (Izra, H’rak); Rural Damascus (Kisweh, Qatana, Said Zeinah), including repair of WASH facilities and electricity and supply of medical equipment; support to treatment of conditions with high impact on maternal, neonatal and child survival, and clinical and psychosocial services for victims of trauma, violence/abuse. The intervention package targets approximately 585,000 PIN (105,350 U5 children and 24,580 PLW) in IDPs, returnees and host communities. Although minimum level of services is being maintained, urgent renovations will offer safe and inclusive access to beneficiaries, including PwD/CwD. Capacity development of health staff on First Aid, RMNCH and MHPSS, NCD, support to mobile teams, awareness campaigns to increase community resilience for positive health outcomes from pregnancy to adolescence and adoption of safe family health behaviors at household level will be carried out. INTERSOS will abide by Health Sector priorities and Guidance Notes committing to active inter-agency coordination, info-sharing, coordination of services and referrals, and feedback to affected populations.
Targeting approach and vulnerability criteria are described here below: The interventions will be rolled out in six sub-districts of three governorates (Rural Damascus, Dara and Dier-ez-Zor) categorized under health sector severity of 5, 4 and 3 and will support highly vulnerable returnees, IDPs and host populations with safe access to health services. INTERSOS, together with its national partner, proposes to support minor rehabilitation of health facilities damaged by the war and contribute to restoring health services through 4 static units and provide outreach through 2 mobile teams in six sub-districts. The project is designed to target 585,272 highly vulnerable individuals (men: 158,024; women: 175,582; Boys below 18: 134,613; girls below 18: 117,054) with a focus on families who have faced multiple displacement, women-headed households, children under-five, people with special needs/CwD/Pwd, PLW and the elderly taking into account age and gender. The protection concepts will be included as part of the overall initiative

4 - Needs

The physical damage to HFs and lack of qualified staff seriously affect the availability and quality of PHC services in Syria, at the time when increase in returns in stabilized areas and new displacements occur. From Dec. to date, 586,000 people fled from their homes in NWS due to the ongoing hostilities.Many of them have been displaced many times during the crisis. In 2019, approx. 1.5 M returns from among IDPs occurred with 20% in Rural Damascus. Aleppo, Homs, Dar’a Govern. also receive high inflows of returnees. The effort of the interagency community continues to overcome access barriers to overstretched HFs. Hazards from mines/UXOs still pose a threat in several areas increasing the risk of trauma impact. IDPs/Ret, particularly in NE/NW, are vulnerable to infectious diseases outbreaks due to unsanitary living conditions, overwhelmed health services and low routine EPI coverage.UNICEF/WHO estimate that over 577,000 newborns require routine immunization and 320,000 children (13-59 M) are not fully immunized. In 2019, WHO confirmed 43 incidents impacting HFs or health personnel in NW Syria. NES remains one of the most complex operating environments due to ongoing hostilities, wide destruction of health infrastructure, and population movements: UNICEF indicates that approx. 160,000 people (70,000 children), have been displaced since the start of last October security operations.Structural repairs are urgently needed to ensure safe access to health services for IDPs/returnees/host populations & to revitalize the health sector.This includes repairs, provision of essential equipment & quality improvement training of health staff. Exclusion from the health systems adds to the growing vulnerability Syrian populations live in.Key sectoral analysis findings are fragmented.INTERSOS abides by needs-based humanitarian assistance in line with humanitarian principles and bases its interventions on HeRAMS, assessments, severity scale for identifying PIN & potential partners.

5 - Implementation of activities

INTERSOS will: A)Support health system, resilience building and work with partners to expand availability of key lifesaving and coordinated interventions on areas with severe needs; B)Improve integrated PHC service delivery, ensure quality medical procedures through static&mobile services: RMNCH, NCD & physical rehabilitation services and case-referral for specialized treatment. MHPSS services backed through specific support to 4 Community/Family Wellbeing Centers. C)Make HFs functional-Conduct minor rehabilitation of WASH/electricity; Procur./supply/replacement of medical equipment to expand access to PHC package. Ensure HF upgradation done according to international standards of disability inclusion. Supervisory activities for 12 months. D)Coordinate to ensure that restoration, water testing/training, hygiene promotion & toilets conform to gender norms for protection of girls/women. E)Support cold chain maintenance, generator fuel, basic clinical examination tools, H&N essential commodities for a uninterrupted supply as part of health system strengthening. F)Support Mobile Teams in DeirEzZor(2); Daraa(1); Rural Damascus(3),covering shifts for volunteers, transport costs, activity material and communications. G)Develop capacity by Quality Improvement Training for deployed/under recruitment HWs,CHWs and Volunteers to fill existing gaps: a) Skills-oriented training by ToT &direct participation (eg: Infection prev./control; First aid,standard protocols/rational use of drugs; b)ANC, assisted delivery, PNC services; Obstetric complications referrals; Managem. of urinary tract/reproductive infections; NBC at home; Prevention, mitigation&treatment of undernutrition; Adolescent girls services); c) PFA, Resilience and MHPSS training package part of the MH-Gap. H)Awareness on essential health messages to empower communities to prevent risky health practices.Quality improvement of messaging on First Aid,ANC, EBF,NBC,early marriage,IYCF,optimal develop. of children/adol.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The promotion of the rights & inclusion of PwD/CwD, including the provision of support & quality improvement to rehabilitation services in selected sub-districts is part of the PHC integrated package. This will be extended to WASH facilities during renovations. When looking at rehabilitation HFs, gender considerations in WASH rehabilitation (separate F/M WCs) and safety concerns for persons accessing the services will be taken into account by screening areas & adequate signage. Facilitating access for PwD by adapting the infrastructure as per CRPD norms & international standards will be given high attention.Vulnerable populations with special needs,conflict-affected, AGD, acute health conditions (children <5, PLW, persons >59) will be supported by sensitization of service providers and CHWs and community participation. Confidentiality of individual health info will be enhanced. INTERSOS project specifically focuses on building resilience of persons living with disability through its interventions.Referral for distressed cases to specialized treatment/service is inlcuded. All actors will be briefed on PSEA and will sign an undertaking, upholding highest ethical standards in line with UN/INTERSOS standards. Capacity building & training will include gender and PSEA. INTERSOS has internal regulations that establish “duty of care” guidelines for national/internat. staff working in conflict zones. Data protection measures and exit strategies are in place in the event of exigencies. Internal review and reflection meetings take into account ‘Do No Harm’, security environment and mitigating measures. Staff are briefed on the Syria environment which requires impartial,carefully thought out & crafted communication at all levels. INTERSOS has relationship with local partners for better and more equitable access to HHs and communities; it is envisaged that monitoring of awareness activities on key HHs practices will enable more effective networking & a feedback loop.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: areas of operation remain with relative level of safety with regular access; no massive movement of population are registered; Bureaucratic procedure remain unchanged, financial crisis & sanctions are not additionally limiting the access to good and services; cash flow is granted by the donor. Health Stakeholder maintain stable support to the action. R1 Security context deteriorates, and access is not regularly granted in the selected areas Mit.Measure: The Partnership with local implementing partner will assure a degree of predictability and safety that could allow adequate precautions for health staff and beneficiaries. Regular risk analysis, appropriate monitoring and applying good management and through diligent coordination with UN/INSO. The implementation team will communicate closely with INTERSOS and keep the field office/staff informed about security situation. INTERSOS will use humanitarian and conflict data analysis to inform strategies by context. All staff of INTERSOS/Partner/Contractors will be briefed/trained on security/personal safety and mitigation measures using existing training material and mockup drills.R2 Bureaucratic impediments May pose a hurdle this could affect import of supplies and clearances and timely deliveries at project site as well as deployment of internat. staff in the field. Mit Measures: INTERSOS will coordinate on time to obtain necessary permits prior to and during the implementation phases.The issue of procurement and supply and accelerated deliveries will be reviewed carefully by close monitoring to ensure that prior supply clearances are obtained, and safety of consigned deliveries of equipment assured.R3 Theft or burglar at INTERSOS premises and or at MU/HF - Mit Measures data related to humanitarian relief, staff/contractors/finances transactions, with particular attention on SGBV and lists of names/beneficiaries, will be kept with high confidentially, respect of INTERSOS SoP for cash accomplished.
