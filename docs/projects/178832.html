Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at addressing the needs of conflict-affected people in Syria in a deteriorating context due to the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic and to the constantly worsening economic crisis. The draft SNFI 2021 HNO states that in 2020 the number of people requiring shelter assistance remained high, with 5.88 million people in need. Idleb it is considered as one of the areas with more displacement and return cases among the country. It also holds a large number of districts and sub-districts considered high in the severity scales (extreme and catastrophic).
The intervention will foster synergies with TGH planned Education and WASH activities in Khan Shaykun. Indeed, TGH has already conducted an emergency distribution of water tanks to households in March 2021 and is planning to rehabilitate a primary school as well as a water system in 2021. Adding a Shelter component will further foster early recovery. The targeted area was selected based on its accessibility, the severity of its needs, TGH field assessments and consultations with SARC, local authorities and municipalities. Since late 2020, the area also experienced an influx of returnees who now face the need to rehabilitate and fix their houses damaged by the conflict. Access to markets in the area is still limited reducing the capacities of returnees to get a hand on construction material and NFI.
In line with Objective 1 and 2, activities 1.2 and 2.1, the project aims at addressing the Shelter and seasonal NFI needs of returnees in South Idleb. TGH will distribute emergency shelter kits and seasonal winter kits to a total of 350 returnee households in Khan Shaykun. The shelter support will be tailored based on the needs of each household, taking as a basis the basic Shelter Kit content from the Cluster. Based on the individual needs of each household, the kit will be adapted. As for the winter kits, TGH will distribute the kit to the same household who will receive the shelter kit. This kit will support the families to deal with winter hazards.
The proposed intervention has been designed based on TGH 3-year experience in implementing NFI projects in Syria. In 2020, TGH contributed to improving access to basic hygiene items to a total of 236,804 individuals in order to contribute to reducing the spread of COVID-19 in vulnerable areas. As part of intervention related to Education and WASH sectors, TGH has also been conducting distributions of student kits in schools as well as distributions of water tanks in highly vulnerable communities.
As part of its activities, TGH puts a specific emphasis on abiding by protection principles through tailoring NFI kits to specific needs of different members including children, women, elderly and people with disabilities. Regarding the latter, TGH always adapt its distribution modalities (through door-to-door visits) to ensure that the most vulnerable effectively benefit from the intervention.

4 - Needs

TGH has already done a distribution of water tanks in Khan Shaykun and had the chance to assess the area in late March 2021. Khan Shaykun used to host around 50,000 individuals. While the area was in the frontline of the conflict, all the population left. Since late 2020, some families started to return and currently there are around 2.100 individuals. The area has been heavily hit by the conflict and as a result all the houses are in need of repairs, especially sealing of windows and doors and, in some cases also structural needs. In addition, the area has limited access to markets and they rely on doing trips to areas such as Hama to be able to purchase basic items. Seasonal support is needed, especially for winter as houses are still in need of repair and access to the markets it is still limited. Access to water is still limited, even if the pumping station of Hawiyeh is operational (after being rehabilitated by TGH in 2021), as the water network is damaged. Returnees rely on shallow wells and water trucking to get water. There is only one health facility in the area, which is newly open, and access to education is non-existent for the moment in Khan Shaykun, forcing children to go to other locations. At the time of the assessment the number of returnees was increasing, especially in Khan Shaykun and the authorities are expecting to get more returnees in the coming months, which will be in need of assistance. The aim of this project is not focused on COVID-19 since TGH already worked on improving access to water and basic hygiene items in the past. Seasonal and shelter kits distributions will also indirectly improve the population’s sanitation capacities to address COVID-19.

5 - Implementation of activities

TGH will also provide returnee households in Khan Shaykun with shelter kits and seasonal winter kits. Both kits will be locally purchased with suppliers already identified by TGH. If needed the procurement can also be done at Damascus level, where more suppliers are available. By assessing the needs of the targeted household, TGH will identify the needs to be repaired in each household and prepare tailored BoQs. The support can be related to sealing of the windows and doors, electrical works, wooden materials in the building and small construction works. The objective being to improve the current living conditions of the returnee families in an area heavily affected by the conflict. As the needs identified in Khan Shaykun are big and the number of returnee households varies between 250 and 500, TGH plans to distribute both kits to all the households, covering all the current population. As the number of households is not huge andshelter kits tailored to individual needs, TGH will conduct door-to-door distributions, ensuring that each family signs a handover certificate. Since TGH has already conducted NFI distributions of water tanks in the area in early 2021, no delays or issues are expected in getting the necessary approvals for such intervention.Baseline and end line household surveys (PDM), and monitoring visits, will be conducted to evaluate the impact of the project on living conditions. To support M&E activities and ensure accountability towards beneficiaries, TGH also set up a strong feedback mechanism through hotline, complaint boxes and regular consultations of the local community in order to allow beneficiaries of all age, gender and background to reach the organization.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Content of kits not gender sensitive: TGH follows the SNFI Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits. The seasonal kits to be distributed are not gender oriented and all the items can be used by both genders. The shelter kits will be tailored for each household and if any special need is found within the household it will be taken into consideration. - Sale or exchange of NFI content: as shelter kits will be tailored made, the risk of sale or exchange will be highly reduced as the content will be agreed with the beneficiary based on their needs. - Risks in distribution areas: these include potential physical and physiological harm and violence. Distributions will be made house-to-house. By doing so, the risk of harm within the distribution process is reduced as the beneficiary will receive the items in their safe place. – Inter-communal tension: TGH will reduce the risk of inter-communal tensions by ensuring a transparent process and collaboration with the beneficiaries and the authorities and by developing a clear selection criteria based on needs. – Explosives and Remnants of War: TGH communicates with local authorities and mine action organizations when encountering ERWs. Activities will be put on hold while the diffusion or removal of the ERW.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Increasing of the conflict related dynamics. TGH constantly monitors the security situation in the areas of intervention. There will be preventive measures in place in order to ensure the safety of the staff. - Inflation rate increases. TGH has already considered a reasonable percentage of inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous heavy inflation. - Sudden upsurge of the COVID-19 cases in Syria and the restriction of inter-governorate movements thus disrupted access to project locations. TGH has put in place IPC measures for its staff in order to avoid a potential increase in the cases among the organization. Presence of TGH staff in all governorates. - Intervention of the authorities among the selection of beneficiaries. TGH will ensure transparency in the selection of the beneficiaries based on criteria previously agreed upon. It is also planned to do a blanket distribution in Khan Shaykun, including all the returnee families. - Suspension of TGH activities by authorities. Trustful and transparent relationship with all counterparts and authorities and seek for authorization for assessments and implementation of activities to the relevant Ministries and Directorates.- - Difficulties obtaining materials: TGH counts with a list of suppliers from the area and from other areas of Syria, including Damascus. This will ensure access to enough material. - -Risk of theft of kits: TGH will conduct door-to-door distribution of seasonal and shelter kits. The kits will be distributed based on an agreed plan and will be carried out from the suppliers warehouse to the area of delivery on the same day. Not owning or utilizing a warehouse reduces the risk during the stocking period.
