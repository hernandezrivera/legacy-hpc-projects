Project Summary

During field assessments in East Ghouta for a preparedness plan to assist the returning population, around 300,000 returnees to various towns in the governorate, but they are unable to reach to stability in their daily lives due to the accumulated heaps of debris in the roads near their houses. The project thus aims to: 1. Address immediate emergency needs (access to services, by contributing to the disposal of debris); 2. Contribute to the resumption of economic and social activities; 3. Support target communities to regain their path towards resilience. In response to “Improve safe access to basic and social essential services and infrastructure for affected people and institutions the SO3 Increase resilience and livelihood opportunities and affected people’s access to basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.”
The countless mounds of debris and collapsed structures pose an overwhelming problem which is beyond the capacity of the local authorities in these neighborhoods and the local communities to address. The staggering amount of debris that remains to be cleared is an obstacle to the recovery and stability of communities, preventing their return to normalcy while limiting people’s access to basic services and the development of their livelihoods. Rescate will continue the debris clearing work beyond the main access roads, which is critical for ensuring access to homes, businesses and basic infrastructure. It is recalled by inhabitants that rodents are visible threat in these neighborhoods, debris is full of dangerous items which are direct threat to inhabitants especially children during their movement from-to school and to women and men during their daily trips to work or to collect daily needs. This in addition to the other environment negative impact, and the hindrance to implement infrastructure projects like water and sanitation pipelines and facilities, and to rehabilitate damaged houses. Debris removal comprises the first step to achieve normal life for returnees and to evade dangers on inhabitants and pave the way for restoring normal life in the areas after long years of tension. The main objective is to enhance living standards, build the resilience of the population, and facilitate access to social services and other services, to public market, to work and to schools. On the basis of locally assessed needs, with a view to support the spontaneous recovery efforts by the affected populations, debris removal is the corner stone to revive normalcy that will encourage others to return to their original domiciles. Rescate will ensure that the contractor will provide livelihood opportunities during the removal of debris in each of the areas, at least 20 individual per town will work with the contractor during the project cycle in each of town reaching to 120 persons in total. Although an intermittent job opportunity, but still helps some families meet their living needs.
Upon a field visit to Quneitra (Bir Ajam, Jibata Al Khashab, and Bath), we found urgent need for street lighting to encourage returns. Since people in these towns are without access to safe, affordable and reliable electricity and due to the critical role of electricity as an enabler for enhanced resilience, and building on other actors’ experience in the last years in providing solar lighting units in different governorates all over Syria and to respond for the needs of the population to have healthy living conditions upon their return to their houses, Rescate was asked to provide immediate assistance for street lighting on the main roads where the main market, houses, and government departments are located. Rescate intervention in coordination with other key partners will focus on providing solar street lighting systems which can be easily installed in the streets, near health centers, schools and other facilities to ensure the safety and security of children, youth, women, men, and PWD.

Needs

According to the UNOCHA East Ghouta various Situation reports, the need for removal of debris was considered an urgent priority. The presence of significant amounts of debris hinders many rehabilitation and recovery efforts as vehicles are not able to access neighborhoods. The same report also mentions lack of assessment of buildings and infrastructure as roads and access pathways are simply clogged with debris. Teams on the ground also reported widespread destruction and crippling amounts of debris. During field visits to East Ghouta, staff members observed a significant number of female-headed households. Many of the women interviewed had lost their husbands or fathers for reasons including death, family separation. The staff identified the following urgent ER&L related priorities, and even more so for females heading households: debris collection and removal to ease access, enable returns, prevent deteriorating health conditions; and a severe need for livelihood opportunities to help the affected population meet basic needs. Finally, with the recent increase of return interest, it becomes particularly urgent to accelerate the debris removal so as to contribute to improved access and enhanced rehabilitation of shelter and infrastructure. There are 300,000 population living in these areas and are in dire need to be safe and live normal life without piles of debris and the entailed dangers to this situation. Needs for solar street lighting is essential for all communities under the recurrent electrical outages for long hours. People in the area will be able to resume their activities despite the electrical cuts. Girls, women, children, youth, men and PWDs need for safety and security is enhanced, thus the society resilience is enhanced. Solar Lighting will enhance access to livelihoods and work in the main markets as movement will not be discontinued due to darkness, or unsafe neighborhoods. There are 48,750 population living in the three towns who are in dire need to be safe and live normal life with possibility of safe movement and without the entailed dangers on men, women, youth and especially children.

Implementation of activities

Improved living conditions in three neighborhoods in East Ghouta. Up to 100,000 cubic meters of debris will be collected, removed and disposed in technically set landfills in Deir Hafeer. Assess the daily capacity of different supply chains to ensure compatibility and smooth flow of debris. Set up verification system to measure the amount of debris removed from sites and dumped in landfills. Improved economic situation of the most vulnerable families in six neighborhoods in East Ghouta directly benefiting from CFW opportunity (at least 120 workers, total direct beneficiaries 600 individuals). The first level of monitoring collects basic information about activities conducted. To support data collection processes in accordance with reporting plans, monitoring activities are conducted using established monitoring tools and templates. Programme staff will be responsible for collecting activity level data for all operations. Monitoring and Evaluation will directly involve participating communities, so that observations are fully owned and appropriate solutions identified in a fully participatory and transparent manner. Street Lighting for estimated population (48,750 individuals) with consideration that 40% are returnees (19,500) while 60% are the original inhabitants in the towns (29,250) mainly work in farming and livestock, therefore; will be the primary beneficiaries because it will facilitate their movement and ensures safety and resilience. The project is in line with the Early Recovery sector strategic objectives to reach to people, prioritizing efforts to improve access to livelihoods and income generating opportunities as well as access to basic social services and infrastructure for the most vulnerable communities, while promoting social cohesion among and between communities, lightening is a major aspect to meet these goals. • Set up clear implementation plan with stakeholders and local communities in the targeted areas • The Electrical engineer with team will check the current status of light poles, identify needs for maintenance. • Identify specific light poles sites on the streets (with focus on population needs) • Announce tender and follow the procurement procedures to assign the contract to the most competent ensuring value for fund. • Day to day management and follow up by the project team • Regular progress reporting in accordance to targets and relevant indicators (monthly, midterm and final) • Regular Site visit by the relevant team and M&E Reporting. Moreover, the project will set a mechanism to ensure consideration of the gender perspective, particularly. Rescate mainstreams Protection by means of undertaking activities to safeguard people from violence, coercion, deprivation, and discrimination and is aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual. Protection mainstreaming implies implementing projects in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance. Rescate will implement the projects in the selected areas (East Ghouta, and Quneitra) directly through local contractors under the supervision of engineers from Rescate within the Project Management Unit (PMU) and in close coordination with the teams from the local partner for the coordination during implementation and receipt phases in addition to coordination with the ER&L sector and local authorities.

Protection Risk Analysis

To ensure impartiality, a dedicated M&E project staff will review project activities, progress and quality in project implementation areas where he/she is directly responsible for project activities. Rescate is planning to intervene with debris removal in East Ghouta 6 towns, and street lighting in Qunaitra 3 towns. The areas of intervention in Rural Damascus and Qunaitra Governorates where the severity level is 5 and 4 (2019 HNO). The project objective is providing life-saving and life-sustaining support, and to alleviate suffering and increase access to humanitarian response for vulnerable people and those with specific needs. The intervention will consider privacy and safety for women and girls amongst others and will contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving community/public infrastructures. Support the resilience of affected local communities, households and individuals within the humanitarian response by protecting and restoring livelihoods and enabling access to essential services and rehabilitation of socio-economic infrastructures.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Since Rescate is well aware that resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension, therefore; 1. Rescate had ensured that a comprehensive needs analysis and the response formulation is informed to the concerned community and authorities, it will be openly documented and made transparent.2. Rescate have used the existing community mechanisms to identify needs and arrived at a consensus as of who should be assisted. The mechanisms will be communicated with the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the local authorities and communities concerned.3. Rescate will be keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress to efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’4. Rescate will ensure that the process of receiving feedback and of responses to it is put in place by establishing a claim mechanism at the Project Management level reachable to all beneficiaries. 5. Rescate will set up and have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities), in consultation with concerned communities. 6. Rescate will ensure that Do No Harm is established and abide to by all staff, partners, and contractors.
