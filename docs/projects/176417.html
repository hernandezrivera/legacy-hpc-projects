Project Summary

The Medair WASH Project will be implemented in Pibor, Renk, Leer and any other priority locations identified by UN-OCHA, WASH Cluster, NAWG and ICCG across South Sudan. Medair has been running multi-sectoral response in Renk and Leer counties since 2011 and 2015 respectively and will continue supporting these locations in 2021. According to the HRP2021 and WASH Cluster severity mapping, the WASH sector needs and gaps in Pibor country are highlighted as severity level 4 and at critical condition. As a result, Medair has started long term multi-sectoral responses in Pibor County (Lekuangole and Gumuruk). Additionally, the emergency response team delivers rapid assistance in areas identified as WASH severity level 3, 4, and 5 and IPC 4/5. Medair aims to integrate WASH activities with nutrition and health to maximise the impact and reduce excessive morbidity and mortality of children under five and to contribute to the overall WASH cluster objectives. Medair will provide standard WASH NFI kits along with hygiene promotion to 14,551 SAM/MAM children and their family/caretakers at facilities and depending on the COVID-19 situation follow up visits will be conducted for households hosting the children. The WASH NFIs include household water treatment products, a bucket with lid, a bucket with tap and lid, soap, and filter cloth. The WASH NFIs are intended to improve access to safe water and safe hygiene practice for three months. In addition, Medair provides access to safe water and sanitation facilities to 43 nutrition facilities. Medair also supports communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rate through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. WASH activities will also be integrated into health activities in the context of disease prevention and response. It aims at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrhoeal, cholera, vector borne diseases and COVID-19. This is intended to be achieved through multiple integrated approaches (improving access to safe water and hygiene promotion) and ensuring minimum IPC WASH in health facilities in each supported facility of Renk and Pibor. The emergency response team employs the same WASH technical strategies stated above and supports people in need of life saving WASH services across South Sudan. Medair will work with protection and disability focused partners, community leaders and religious leaders to identify people with disabilities, elders, marginalised and financially disadvantaged community members. Then, focus group discussions will be conducted with these groups of people or their representatives to identify their needs, barriers, and obstacles that limit them from accessing WASH services. During project design, these groups of people or their representatives will engage with Medair and forward their views and opinion and design preference. In all the project locations including health and nutrition facilities, Medair will thoroughly assess protection concern related to women and girls accessing WASH services and take robust action to mitigate the risk of exposing women and girls. Depending on the contexts, Medair will install 14 new water points where there is low coverage, operate four SWATs, upgrade six existing handpumps with solar power system, rehabilitate 65 existing water points and/or distribute water filters or household water treatment products. Medair employs a Care Group network approach in its static locations and conducts general community wide hygiene promotion in any other emergency locations across South Sudan. Hygiene promotion at community level emphasising disease prevention (such as COVID-19, cholera and AWD), safe excreta management (and advocating for cathole method), safe water storage and handwashing with safe water and soap. 30 communal toilets will be constructed in crowded settings where their absence will create a public health risk. The toilets will be maintained by the community.

1 - Needs

The WASH Cluster conducted WASH severity mapping and people in need (PIN) across South Sudan for 2021. This analysis shows that an estimated 5.6 million people’s living standard and wellbeing will be negatively impacted by a lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. This analysis also estimated 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. These people are distributed across the country including Pibor (severity 4) where Medair has started a long-term multi-sectoral response, Renk (severity 3), Leer (severity 2) and other priority locations where Medair will deploy rapid emergency response teams upon request from UN-OCHA, WASH Cluster, and ICCG. In December 2020 Medair conducted needs assessment in Pibor. The assessment team conducted focus group discussions (FGD) with different age and gender groups and key informant interviews (KII). The assessment report highlighted in Pibor 90% (9/10) of the FDGs and 85% (11/13) of the KIIs respectively mentioned untreated surface water as their main source of water for drinking and domestic purpose. 80% of the FGDs attendants and 100% (13/13) KIIs reported lack of basic sanitation facilities in their compound and 92% (12/13) FGDs reported lack of handwashing facility on their premises.. In addition, in 2020, Medair conducted 12 rapid multi-sectoral needs assessments across the country through the emergency response team. The assessment report findings corroborate the HRP 2021 report that states only 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64% of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41%), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19%), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4%). Unfortunately, due to COVID-19 pandemic, Medair could not conduct SMART surveys in the project locations. However, the June 2019 Renk County SMART survey revealed GAM prevalence of 32.1% among the host communities and 24.4% among IPDs/camp settlement in both set up the GAM rate is above the emergency threshold. Whereas in Leer County, the April 2019 SMART survey showed a GAM rate of 16.8% which was also above the emergency threshold. Undernutrition is directly caused by inadequate dietary intake and/or disease and indirectly related to many factors, including contaminated drinking-water and poor sanitation and hygiene. Due to this reason, there is a need to integrate WASH and nutrition to reduce GAM rates. According to HRP 2021, around 15% of households have at least one disabled family member. Most WASH responses focused on reduced mobility rather than other disabilities during project design and implementation. The main emergency triggers Medair identified during the assessments are recurrent inter-communal violence particularly in Jonglei and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA); that claimed the lives of hundreds, damaged water points and sanitation facilities, and crops, unprecedented flooding, the risk of COVID-19 and cholera, and food insecurity or high GAM rate above the emergency threshold (15%).

2 - Project activities

Provide all activities 1.1 WASH and nutrition integrated responses: Medair will continue supporting SAM/MAM children and caretakers by providing a standard WASH NFIs kits along with hygiene promotion at the facilities and follow up visits to the households depending on the COVID-19 situation. The WASH NFIs include household water treatment products, a bucket with lid, a bucket with tap and lid, soap, and filter cloth. The WASH NFIs intend to improve access to safe water and safe hygiene practice for three months. Hygiene promotion targets caregivers or mothers at the facility level with priority hygiene messages such as hand hygiene, safe handling of food and drinking water, and safe excreta disposal. In addition, Medair improves access to safe water and sanitation services in the facilities. Safe and adequate drinking water will be provided at facility level for appetite tests and for patients, caregivers, and staff. Medair will also ensure all the nutrition facilities are implementing the basic IPC WASH services. Medair also supports communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rates through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. Depending on the contexts, Medair will install new water points where there is low coverage, operate SWATs, rehabilitate existing water points and/or distribute water filters or household water treatment products. Medair employs Care Group network approach in its static locations and general community wide hygiene promotion in any emergency location. Hygiene promotion at community level emphasizing on disease prevention (such as COVID-19, cholera and AWD), safe excreta management (and advocating for cathole method), safe water storage and handwashing with safe water and soap. 1.2 Integration with Health: WASH activities will be integrated to health activities in the context of disease prevention and response. This aims at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrhoeal, cholera, vector borne diseases and COVID-19. This is intended to be achieved through multiple integrated approaches. In communities where there is high prevalence of WASH-related disease outbreaks (such as Renk, Leer, Pibor and any other emergency locations), Medair will improve access to safe water by rehabilitating existing water points, installing new water points if there is no other source and low coverage, and ensure water points are safe from contamination. In addition, Medair will consider water filter distribution in an area where there are no other safe water sources and the population is transient. Communal toilets will be constructed in crowded settings where their absence will create a public health risk and the toilets will be maintained by the community. Medair conducts protection analysis and focus group discussions with women and girls before constructing both water points and communal latrines. Hygiene promotion follows the same approach mentioned in activity 1.1 but the messages will be tailored depending on the disease outbreak. Additionally, Medair ensures minimum IPC WASH services in all the health facilities including installation of adequate handwashing facilities and training of health staff. 2.1 Access to basic services in emergency: Medair will deliver rapid assistance across the country where people are affected by sudden or rapid onset of disasters as a result of man-made and natural disasters. In acute emergencies, temporary and rapid solutions such as WASH NFI distribution, repair/rehabilitation of existing water points, operating SWATs, water trucking, communal latrines construction in congested situations and if their absence creates a public health risk, and tailored community wide hygiene promotion using community hygiene promoters. Depending on context and needs, Medair will consider installation of semi-permanent water schemes such as solar water system, new boreholes, and pipeline extension.

3 - Project indicators

Medair has a separate monitoring, evaluation and learning team which is responsible for measuring projects’ success using standard and custom monitoring tools. The WASH technical team also plays a pivotal role in making sure all the critical project indicators are accurately traced, compiled, and reported to the respective donors, clusters and other stockholders. The WASH project has offline and online standard output and outcome indicator tracking sheets that will enable the team to track and upload each data in real time. As an example, when tracking the indicator “number of SAM children provided with standard hygiene kits is paired with hygiene promotion”, when a child is admitted in the programme nutrition team notifies WASH team about the details of the child admitted in the SAM/MAM programme. Then, the WASH team provides pre-designed standard WASH NFIs along with hygiene promotion and demonstration. The WASH team ensures there is no double counting in admission and enter the data in the tracking sheet. These data will be collected and analysed on a regular basis and reported to WASH Cluster in 5Ws and Medair keeps all project related data in the project data sheet for further reference and future programming. During water point construction, Medair aims to conduct beneficiary mapping to accurately count water point beneficiaries and gender and aged segregated data will be entered in the pre-prepared beneficiary tracking sheet. WASH NFI distribution will be conducted after carefully registered beneficiaries on a house-to-house basis and data will be entered in offline and then online distribution sheet. In Care Group network, Medair registers lead mothers and neighbour women before launching the approach and only the number of lead mothers and neighbour women that attended the hygiene promotion session will be counted and reported on a monthly basis. In community wide hygiene promotion, Medair has a tally sheet where enumerators enter number of people who attended the session that are above school age. The M&E team ensures all the collected data are accurate and without double counting and evaluates the project against the indicators. If gaps are identified and the project is not on track, corrective measures will be taken, and the project will be re-aligned. Medair also collects outcome and intermediate outcome indicators such as beneficiary satisfaction, coverage, and beneficiary perceptions using the KPC survey. Other data collection methods employed include; exit surveys, post distribution monitoring, filter monitoring survey, household water treatment system and monitoring survey. In order to protect the health of the community and staff and to curtail community transmission of COVID-19, these surveys will be conducted depending on the COVID-19 situation in South Sudan. Medair also conducts health and nutrition facilities appraisal related to IPC WASH standards.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Medair will be transparent with the affected population by providing them with accessible and timely information about PSEA and set up confidential feedback collection mechanisms as mentioned above. Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities. Additionally, Medair will provide information about PSEA to beneficiaries through focus group discussions, community and community leaders meetings and during WASH NFIs distribution. To give more emphasis on PSEA, community wide awareness raising session will be conducted.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Medair ensures all members of the affected population’s voices are equally heard and their concern, comments, and perceptions will be taken into consideration during programme design and implementation. The feedback collection mechanisms and focus group discussions particularly target most vulnerable members of the community such as marginalised community members, people with disability, individuals who are not literate, women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities. In addition, before project implementation, Medair will carry out assessments, focus group discussions with different gender and age groups, and key informant interviews that will inform the type of assistance the affected populations need, and the modalities they would want to receive. Barrier analysis will be carried out to engage community participation in identifying and overcoming obstacles that limits them from implementing safe hygiene behaviour and practice. During implementation, Medair will undertake regular meetings with the affected communities and community leaders throughout interventions and feedback mechanisms will be put in place. Medair will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. A beneficiary telephone hotline, community focal point, information box, post distribution monitoring and exit surveys will be conducted to collect feedback on the affected population concerns, perceptions, and interests. Medair will respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and conducts and community wide awareness raising session will be conducted.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback will be collected with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct by programme staff. Medair analyses all feedback thoroughly to adjust the projects design according to the beneficiaries’ interest, context and resources. In situations where Medair is unable to make any change due to seen and unforeseen reasons, the affected beneficiaries will be contacted to recommend alternative options.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

During beneficiaries’ registration Medair engages with local community leaders, chiefs and community members to reduces the risk of violence and the registration will be conducted in house- to-house approach to accurately target the most vulnerable groups. Medair consults the affected beneficiaries on most appropriate, safe and central locations that minimises travel time. Distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before dark. All COVID-19 prevention measures will be enforced to avoid the risk of transmission during distribution. Medair mitigates the risk of PSEA through training of all staff and a clear reporting mechanism for both beneficiaries and staff. Medair will also clearly communicate that any assistance is delivered free of any charge or compensation. During intervention delivery Medair works in teams rather than individuals providing services, and in activities that involve home visits have staff travel in pairs ideally with both genders. General protection mainstreaming is done throughout Medair’s project to prevent beneficiaries from harm. Medair assesses available protection assistance in all project locations. In many areas of operation, no or very limited protection assistance is available. Medair is involved in national and sub national coordination, including the protection cluster, to advocate for addressing gaps in coverage. Medair further designs project interventions to be able to support survivors of protection incidents. All Medair staff are trained in psychological first aid and Medair supported clinics are equipped to provide medical support through clinical management of rape in a confidential matter. Where possible Medair will support the referral of the beneficiary for further protection assistance. Medair’s experience has learned that marginalised groups have less engagement in general community engagement activities. In order to address this Medair specifically targets these groups during community consultation. This is done by facilitating access for marginalised groups to participate in meetings or focus groups discussions or, if appropriate, having consultation with these specific groups. Medair further advocates with community leaders and other stakeholders for the inclusion of marginalised groups in all interactions with providers of humanitarian aid and in coordination mechanisms.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The WASH project aims to provide resilient water supply schemes in areas repeatedly classified as IPC 3, 4, and 5. The resilient safe water sources include flood and extended dry season resistant handpumps, upgrading existing handpumps to solar system, and creating ownership of the constructed water points and awareness among the community. Community water user committee will be established and trained to manage the water points and a revenue collection mechanism for sustainable use of the schemes. In addition, Medair is implementing Care Group network to disseminate hygiene, health and nutrition messages in the community.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Medair ensure access to essential services – WASH, health, nutrition – for all groups including women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities. These groups of people will take part during assessment, design and implementation phase of the project aiming to remove barriers and obstacles that limit them from accessing the WASH services. In all the project locations, Medair intentionally targets these groups of people and leaves no one behind.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The WASH project targets communities identified as severity level 3 and 4. Medair will serve Pibor (severity 4), and Renk (severity 3) using the static projects and all other counties fall under severity level 3 and 4 using the emergency response team. Medair has also been supporting Leer county since 2015 even though it’s now severity level 2. Medair provides access to safe water to the affected communities through temporary, and semi-permanent solutions for household water treatment such as PuR/Aquatabs and water filters, operating surface water treatment system, rehabilitating existing water schemes, and installing new safe water sources. This activity will be complemented by hygiene promotion using the Care Group network and community level house-to-house hygiene promotion focusing on safe water and food storage, excreta disposal, and handwashing.
