Project Summary

IOM's 2020 HRP project for health will increase access and utilization of primary healthcare services among the most vulnerable and crisis-affected populations in the country. IOM will operate 35 static health facilities with integrated community outreach, as well as 10 mobile medical teams; many of the facilities are in areas that are hard-to-reach, remote, conflict-affected and isolated, lack other health actors, and where the population does not have access to basic life-saving health services. IOM will provide comprehensive primary healthcare services in line with the Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS), continuously build the capacity of health workers to ensure high quality of medical service delivery, and preposition medicines and medical supplies in regional hubs to ensure continuous supply to all of IOM-supported health facilities, as well as to the region at large in the event of a sudden emergency. At IOM-operated static facilities, health workers will conduct outreach into the surrounding and nearby communities, including preventative and curative services as well as health education aimed towards behavior change. At many of IOM-supported static clinics (wherever there are gaps), therapeutic services for children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) will be provided through integration of outpatient therapeutic programmes (OTPs) into the healthcare clinics. IOM will upgrade several existing health posts to provide basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEMONC) services, particularly in geographic areas where there are no other health facilities for safe deliveries. To mitigate against recurrent and expected shocks and the effects of continued conflict, IOM will conduct mass vaccination at the entry points to IDP sites, thus increasing immunization coverage and reducing outbreaks of communicable diseases. IOM will provide health education at the health facilities and in their catchment communities to effect behavior change in health, nutrition and hygiene practices that contribute to high excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand and utilization of preventative services such as antenatal care, facility delivery, and immunization. IOM will address gaps in mental health care and psychosocial support (MHPSS) through mainstreaming and integration of MHPSS into primary healthcare, coupled with community-level approaches (community-based PSS focused in IDP settlements). The project will benefit 526,500 individuals, including 319,001 children under 18 years. The project will be part of IOM’s integrated emergency response program which includes shelter and NFIs, camp coordination and management, WASH, and displacement tracking and monitoring.

Project Activities

IOM proposes to implement the following activities across Jubbaland, Southwest State, Puntland, Somaliland, and Benadir: Activity 1: Operate 35 static health facilities which will deliver front-line primary healthcare (PHC) services in crisis-affected communities, prioritizing hard-to-reach or underserved regions which lack consistent access to primary healthcare services (with OTPs integrated into 20 of the facilities). Additionally deploy 10 mobile rapid response teams in response to acute emergencies and to larger, hard-to-reach areas with dispersed populations. Activity 2: Preposition essential medicines, medical supplies, and equipment in 6 strategically located regional hubs which supply all of IOM’s PHC service delivery sites (35 static, 10 mobile). The regional hubs will always carry minimum buffer stock of 3 months, enabling immediate and fluid responses to health emergencies in any of the regions. Activity 3: Upgrade 4 current health posts to health centres by providing staff, equipment and infrastructure for provision of Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BeMONC) in locations lacking maternity and delivery services (Dollow and Kismayo in Jubbaland; Baidoa and Afgooye in Southwest State). Activity 4: Conduct training and capacity building for health workers (178, including 90 women and 78 men) on outbreak response, national treatment guidelines, emergency management, maternal child healthcare, immunization, obstetric care, screening and management of malnutrition, travel health, and health promotion and education approaches. Activity 5: Provide health education at health facility and community level to 223,200 individuals to effect behavior change in health and hygiene practices that contribute to high excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand for preventative services (static and mobile clinics, outreaches, targeted campaigns). Activity 6: Conduct training on psychological first aid (PFA), MHGAP, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), diagnosis and basic clinical management of common mental illnesses, clinical management of rape, referral mechanisms, and psychoeducation for 50 primary healthcare workers (26 women, 24 men) at 10 static clinics; mainstream diagnosis, care and treatment of mental illness into primary healthcare at 10 static health facilities in IDP settlements. Activity 7: Provide mass vaccination of IDP children arriving at entry points at 6 large IDP sites (2 in Dollow, 2 in Baidoa, 2 in Kismayo) to increase immunization coverage among IDP populations and help mitigate against communicable disease outbreaks. Children missing immunizations will be vaccinated at the entry point to the IDP sites and linked to the nearest health facility for follow-up and completion of immunization series. Activity 8: Support travel health services including provision of health screening, transit healthcare and fitness to travel for emergency movement of 10,000 displaced and IDP returnees (3,500 girls, 3000 boys, 2000 women and 1500 men).

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures 2a - Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance i. IOM will gather data (age, sex, diversities) of communities and beneficiaries for effective and informative programing. This will help identify vulnerable and marginalized persons for planning, inclusion purposes and engagement ii. A systematic outreach process will be done to reach marginalized/minority vulnerable groups with health promotion to avoid exclusion iii. Efforts will be made to ensure that information regarding provision of health services will be disseminated to all members of communities (including women, girls, boys, men) through appropriate channel of communication such as radio iv. An inclusive feedback mechanism that will include all beneficiaries will be put in place which help evaluate selection criteria and overall services gaps. v. In all health service delivery points, a mechanism will be established that will give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time. 2b- Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes i. Women, girls and boys will be consulted during programme design to gather accurate information about their specific health needs and preferences for location and design of health clinical and non-clinical services. Issues of privacy and access to persons with disability will be discussed. ii. Community health committees (CHCs) will be comprised of minimum 55% females, including young women. iii. Efforts will be made to ensure that women are included as healthy and hygiene promoters so that they actively contribute to the overall program implementation at community level. iv. The project will hire female staff members at project locations to ensure that women and girls will feel more comfortable talking to them and this will also provide a feedback mechanism v. Girls and boys will actively be engaged in children health hygiene promotion groups/campaigns 3b- Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. i. Health service delivery locations (static facilities and mobile units) will be put in central, visible places and not more 500 meters away from settlements, and ideally within settlement areas. This will help protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing services ii. Separate toilet facilities with internal locks and secondary enclosures will be in place for women and men in health facilities with clear labels such pictorial/signs to ensure that they are used by intended gender iii. In setups where threats are anticipated frequency of health and hygiene kits distribution will be modified and door-to-door distribution will be considered, to avoid sexual abuse and extortion while carrying health and hygiene kits. iv. Coordination will be done with Shelter/NFI partners to put solar light installations at health centers to deter any risk of violence against women and girls v. Community outreach medical and promotive services will be done during daylight hours to allow beneficiaries who are often women and girls to reach home early.
