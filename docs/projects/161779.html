Project Summary

Street Child will provide crucial livelihoods support to 6,000 vulnerable households through a tailored combination of cash grants and business training, aiming to improve access to sustainable income-generating activities and support livelihood stabilisation. Street Child will prioritise households which include vulnerable or at-risk children, including out-of-school children, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and children associated with armed groups (CAAG). Aiming to reach the most vulnerable households and also increase equity in access to livelihoods opportunities, female-headed households will be targeted. The targeted beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability, and comprise IDPs in camps and host communities, returnees and vulnerable host community members.
In Yobe, LGAs which will be targeted are Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the targeted LGAs will be Hong, Mubi North, Mubi South and Michika. In Borno the targeted LGAs will be Biu, Damboa, Dikwa, Gwoza, Hawul, Jere, Konduga, Mobbar and Monguno.

1 - Needs

The ongoing protracted crisis in North East Nigeria has left 5.6 million people in need of early recovery and livelihoods support across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. Some of the effects of conflict include widespread damage of infrastructure, disruption of markets and lack of access to farmlands, all of which have severely restricted the livelihoods opportunities of different communities. 25% of conflict-affected households report having no economic opportunities, as a result of the already-poor socio-economic conditions in the region being further weakened by conflict and displacement. In particular, high levels of displacement have put additional strain on inadequate infrastructure and services. Lack of livelihood opportunities have led households to resort to negative coping strategies, compounding protection and child protection risks such as child labour, trafficking, early marriage, survival sex and other forms of exploitation and abuse. Without addressing the root causes of vulnerability, including economic marginalisation, protection needs will remain severe. Female-headed households are amongst the most vulnerable, with women often economically marginalised and least likely to have access to income-generating activities. Despite the insecurity, there is growing demand amongst IDPs and returnees (many of whom have lost most of their productive and financial assets) to restart their means of livelihood. There is therefore an urgent need to support income recovery, both to meet immediate household needs and to strengthen livelihood stabilisation as part of early recovery efforts. In addition to supporting individual households, these responses are needed to promote resilience and reduce tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities as a result of competition over limited opportunities and access to services.

2 - Activities

Street Child will provide crucial livelihoods support to 6,000 vulnerable caregivers (minimum 70% women) across North East Nigeria, through the unique family business scheme (FBS) model which combines cash grant with business mentoring and enrolment in a savings scheme. This model seeks to enable beneficiaries to build sustainable sources of income and to contribute to building resilience as communities transition to early recovery. The focus is on supporting the most vulnerable caregivers to improve their access to income, enabling them to better provide for their children's needs and improving the wellbeing of their entire household. i. Rapid Market AssessmentAn initial rapid market assessment will be conducted in each community with the aim of identifying the most profitable types of business in each area of operation, mapping existing value chains and lessons learned from previous interventions. These assessments will form the basis of the business training and support provided to beneficiaries, including which vocational trainers are selected to teach the beneficiaries skills which are marketable in their local communities. ii. Business TrainingFollowing the rapid assessment, beneficiaries will receive business and vocational skills training from Street Child's business officers. The trainings cover skills including business awareness, record keeping and management. Basic vocational skills training (such as soap making) will also be offered. At the end of the training, each beneficiary will be guided to develop a tailored business plan. iii. Cash-based TransferAfter receiving business training and drawing up a viable business plan, each beneficiary will receive a cash grant of 30,000 NGN to set up a small business or strengthen an existing small business. iv. Business Monitoring, Mentoring & Savings The businesses will be mentored over a 15 to 20 week period, with livelihoods officers visiting each business on a bi-weekly basis, supporting the beneficiaries to grow their businesses, and addressing any challenges early on. The caregivers are also encouraged to take place in a savings scheme over this period, saving a pre-agreed amount every week (approximately 200 NGN), which helps to entrench a culture of saving. To increase sustainability, VSLAs may be set up or strengthened in the target communities, with beneficiaries transitioning from individualised savings support to peer-to-peer savings support through the VSLA system by the end of the project. v. Integrated Programming In line with our commitment to integrated, holistic programming for maximum sustainability and impact, all livelihoods activities will be linked to Street Child's other education in emergencies and child protection activities. The beneficiaries will be vulnerable caregivers, selected based on specific vulnerability criteria including number of out-of-school children and number of meals eaten per day. A focus will be on selecting caregivers of children vulnerable to protection risks, such as unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and children associated with armed groups (CAAG) to promote their socio-economic reintegration. A targeted 70% of the caregivers will be women from female-headed households, recognising both that women are often the most economically marginalised and that investing in the livelihoods opportunities of these women has a high impact on the wellbeing and education of their children. Throughout the business mentoring and savings period, the childrens' progress at home and in school is monitored and any issues referred for follow-up, with the aim of ensuring that improving caregivers' access to income-generating opportunities benefits the entire household.

7.3 - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

Street Child seeks to both respond to the immediate needs of vulnerable caregivers by providing a cash grant to enable them to establish income-generating activities, whilst simultaneously building sustainable small businesses to build household resilience in the long term. All activities therefore seek to support vulnerable households and support the transition from humanitarian to longer-term recovery and development. Nexus activities will therefore be undertaken in all targeted LGAs: Gujba, Gulani, Mubi, Michika, Madagali, MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Mobbar, Dikwa and Konduga.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Street Child will utilise proven mechanisms for engaging stakeholders and beneficiaries throughout all stages of project planning, implementation and evaluation. A needs assessment will be conducted before implementation, and will include focus group discussions (FGDs) will different community groups - boys, girls, men and women. FGDs will also be held at baseline and endline to provide community members with the opportunity to give feedback and to understand community satisfaction with the project. A volunteer project management committee (PMC) will be established in each community of implementation. This PMC will be empowered to hold Street Child accountable to the planned project aims, activities and implementation timeline, and will be able to raise complaints and concerns at monthly meetings with Street Child and partner staff. A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) officer will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and Street Child to share this feedback. The CRM officer will hold weekly help desk sessions in each community, where beneficiaries and community members can share feedback. A register of all complaints made, and responses to these complaints, will be kept. The help desk will also be set up during all key activities, such as grant disbursement. Anonymous feedback mechanisms will also be established in each community. Anonymous complaints boxes will be placed in each community, and will be checked weekly. Street Child will provide a toll-free line which can be called at any time by community members to provide feedback or report urgent concerns (such as safeguarding concerns). All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with accountability commitments.
