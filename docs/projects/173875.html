Project Summary

The continued insecurity and increased action of armed opposition groups (AOGs) in North East Nigeria has resulted in widespread civilian displacement, destruction of critical infrastructure and the collapse of basic services. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 7.9 million people require assistance across the Borno Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States , an 11% increase since 2019. The insurgency has displaced an estimated population of 1.7 million persons displaced form their home communities with Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States worst affected.
The continued conflict has seen new arrivals and displaced populations residing in over-congested formal and informal camps without the ability to meet their basic needs. Of those affected by these realities, children and adolescents continue to face high protection risks as child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children living with disabilities and adolescent girls and boys. These risks are exacerbated by other emergencies such as fire outbreaks, floods and infectious disease outbreaks and are further compounded by a lack of adequate services. Recently, the COVID-19 outbreak and its attendant concerns has led to family separation, isolation, stigmatization, bereavement and loss of livelihood, further worsening the vulnerability of children, adolescents and their caregivers.
The IRC is proposing a comprehensive and community-based approach to addressing identified child protection needs drawing on its expertise in child protection programming and over five years presence in the Northeast. The IRC Protection response will focus on providing case management through direct services and linkages to specialized services as well as structured age and gender sensitive Psycho- Social Support (PSS) for children, adolescents and their caregivers who have been affected by the insurgency, COVID-19 and its related issues.
IRC Child Protection (CP) intervention will provide specialized case management services to UASC, former CAAG and other children and adolescents (including children living with disabilities) that have suffered or are at risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence. This response will also create linkages to specialized services that will cater for the particular and immediate needs of children and adolescents arising from the effect of COVID-19 on the one hand and those associated with the humanitarian crisis in the BAY states on the other. This response will be closely coordinated with the IRC GBV, Protection and Rule of Law (PRoL), Health, Nutrition and WASH services together with Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities, mainstreamed through a collaborative approach while also working closely with other Child Protection partners. The IRC tool kits for Socio-Emotional Learning (SEL), Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE) and the Families Make the Difference (FMD) parenting skills, which have been adapted to meet the COVID-19 preventive measures of physical distancing, will be used to meet the psychosocial needs of children, adolescents and their families. The adaptation of these activities – while observing the preventive measure of social distancing - aim to provide children and their caregivers’ with the much needed social and emotional support, helping them manage the stress they face daily as a result of their displacement, loss of homes and livelihoods and COVID-19 associated stress. The intervention will also strengthen community led structures which will support the IRC Child Protection team in implementing these activities in their communities. To enhance its area of coverage, the IRC will partner with identified National Child Protection NGOs across the BAY states.

1 - Needs

On the 8th of May 2020, the IRC began conducting weekly COVID-19 Child Protection Situation Monitoring surveys aimed at identifying the children protection trends in communities of intervention. This survey is conducted with the use of the Kobo Collect application and supports Child Protection officers and case workers collect information from members of community-based CP committee (CBCPC) established or strengthened by the IRC who support I the identification of cases and report them to case workers for assessment and documentation. The analysis of the data collected between June and November 2020 from 117 individual (with 34% adult female older than 25years, 36% adult male older than 25years, 19% adult male younger than 25years, 11% adult female younger than 25years) who were interviewed during the survey indicates that the three most common child protection concerns are physical abuse, child neglect and child labor. 60% of the individuals interviewed reported that there is an increase in child protection concerns while 14% reported that the severity of the concerns were due to COVID 19. A further breakdown of identified child protection concerns shows that children 0-11 years were most affected by neglect while children aged 12-17 years were most affected by physical abuse and psychosocial distress being common among children living with disabilities. The survey also showed that loss of jobs, sources of livelihood and income generation were the main factors that increased the vulnerability of caregivers of children.

2 - Activities

As part of its ongoing protection programming in the BAY states, the IRC provides child protection case management for Unaccompanied Separated (UAC) and other at-risk children, psychosocial support for children, adolescents and their caregivers through structured sessions, strengthening of community structures and awareness raising activities. The IRC also creates linkages for children, adolescents and their caregivers to specialized services and provides in kind and cash support to targeted individual vulnerable caregivers. In addition, the IRC also conducts safety mapping with children and adolescents enrolled for its PSS activities to identify areas within the community which they consider unsafe in order to support community structures set up and act on mitigation measures. In all these services, the IRC gives particular attention to internally displaced persons including women and girls, with persons living with disabilities given priority. The IRC intends to replicate these same activities under the proposed project in communities to be identified in Konduga, MMC, Mubi North and Michika, Gujba LGAs of the BAY states. Affected persons in the proposed locations will benefit from these protection activities since the IRC is already well established as a leading protection agency and has so far achieved community acceptance in these communities and other neighboring communities. The IRC has a long presence in the selected LGAs areas responding to the different needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities through its different interventions, including adapting to the use of community structures recently put in place since the COVID-19 outbreak.The IRC will also work on building the capacity of National NGOs and providing them with technical support in case management and working with community structures to provide child protection services.
