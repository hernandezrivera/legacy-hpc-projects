Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen water, sanitation and hygiene response to affected population in Far North region of Cameroon with a focus on IDP camp, communities hosting IDP and returnees. In water domain, rehabilitation of boreholes and construction of small water supply network powered with solar energy will be prioritized. On hygiene and sanitation issues, communities approached for total sanitation will be implemented at municipality level. Construction of community latrines and showers will be limited to IDP sites. Hygiene promotion with distribution of minimum WASH kits will be implemented in target communities. The area targeted cover the division of Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga and Logone and Chari. With these interventions, this project targets 60,000 people for access to safe water, 44,000 people for acces to adequate basic sanitation and 150,000 people reached with critical WASH supply (including hygiene items) and services.
This project will also include a component relative to capacity building of WASH actor at regional level on key issues such as design, installation and maintenance of solar system for water supply, cholera response, flood response, protection and gender mainstreaming in WASH sector.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Situation In Far North region of Cameroon, internally displaced persons living in the main IDP sites of Mayo Sava (Kolofata, Mémé) and Mayo Tsanaga (Zamai, Tourou) do not have 15l / person / day and are more than 20 people per latrine. Investments made in 2018, 2019 and 2020 did not significantly improve this situation at standard level due to the continuous increase in people in the different IDP sites and insufficient investment in this sector.A multisectoral evaluation was carried out in 2018 where needs of different groups were collected. Off-site, in general, the situation is most critical in the Mayo Sava division. The practice of open defecation is a common practice in the region regardless of the sex of the individual. It is more common among men, women, girls and boys of IDPs, off-site refugees and returnees than among host populations. The most affected division is Mayo Sava where at least 34% of the population practices open defecation and more than 25% of IDPs. Also in Mayo Tsanaga, there is a very high rate of open defecation (at least 26%) among refugees outside the camps. The highest proportion of people who share latrines is observed in Diamaré with 47.8% of households (63% of women and 37% of men) while in Mayo Sava this proportion is 35% including 41% among women and 31% among men (OCHA, 2018). Which increases the risk of gender-based violence and undermines the privacy of female and male usersTo this situation is added the risk of spreading from these regions of the cholera epidemic declared since May 2018 to the whole of Cameroon and in the Lake Chad basin.Activities The main activities planned among wash sector member based on needs assessment and field situation includes:  The provision of safe drinking water. This will be done by • Construction and rehabilitation of boreholes• Construction of small-scale water point (with solar energy) through new borehole or exploitation of non-functional borehole with a minimum of 2m3 /hours of water• Water trucking will only be used in sites hosting new IDPs and should be limited to a period of up to 3 months from the start of the influx. Provision of basic sanitation services. Interventions will be limited: • Construction of emergency latrines in relocation and transit sites. Attention will be paid to the dignity and safety of users: The shower blocks and toilets will be separated and differentiated between women and men thanks to pictograms. • Construction of family latrines will be done through CLTS and PHAST in the host communities hosting internally displaced persons, returnees and in sites hosting displaced persons from more than 3 months. • Improving access to basic sanitation within institution. This will be limited to child-friendly spaces and health facilities in the affected areas. Promotion of good practices related to water, hygiene and sanitation. This will be done through community leaders mainly youth at community level and children (boy and girl) at school. This will include Menstrual Hygiene Management and distribution of WASH kit to girls and women below 59 years who have menstrua. Affected population will be consulted as far as concern the content of WASH kits and an end user evaluation will be done after each distribution.  Capacity building of Implementing partner. UNICEF will support the technical and logistic organization of Implementing partners training on key subject’s design, installation and maintenance of solar system for water supply, cholera response, protection and gender mainstreaming in WASH sectorA feedback mechanism will be put in place to collect feedback from affected people and assistance provided.
