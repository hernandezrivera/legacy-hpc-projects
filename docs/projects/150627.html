Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is a key protection concern in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) and not a new phenomenon in East Jerusalem. Nonetheless, reliable and a continuous flow of data and information emphasizing the challenges of these vulnerable Palestinians communities remain scarce. The number of GBV incidents were found to be increasing due to mainly two factors: entrenched, superseding patriarchal norms and the impact of the Israeli occupation. The main difference for survivors of GBV residing in East Jerusalem compared to residents of the West Bank is the availability of Israeli services and protection mechanisms. However, Palestinians living in East Jerusalem do not have unrestricted access to GBV-related services provided by the Israeli authorities. The Separation Barrier, severing off Jerusalem from the West Bank, has resulted in jurisdictional ambiguity between the obligations of the Palestinian National Authorities (PNA) and the Israeli Police, further fragmenting the vulnerable communities in East Jerusalem. In addition to barriers related to availability, accessibility and jurisdictional authority, survivors of GBV report that other challenges include lack of awareness of the Palestinian service providers coupled with a fear and stigmatization to turn to Israeli organizations for help. Palestinian organizations providing GBV services, on the other hand, state that sustainable, long-term funding and a lack of political will to prioritize the particular needs pertinent to East Jerusalemites are the most pressing obstacles. In a study from 2017 by Juzoor, single and divorced/separated women reported that within the past twelve months, the most common form of GBV they had been exposed to was physical violence by a relative (30% and 25% respectively). Married women reported that GBV in terms of economic violence, such as denied inheritance or earnings forcibly taken by husband or family member, was most common together with physical assault and emotional abuse. Economic violence could also include abuse of women’s status as Jerusalem ID holders. For example, women reported that their husbands would apply for loans and purchase SIM cards in their name. In the case of divorce, the financial obligations would remain in the name of the women causing economic and legal burdens. Also, women holders of West Bank ID but lived in Jerusalem without the “proper” documentation reported cases of abuse, such as forced family separation, lack of access to justice and risk of losing custody of their children to the husband. Thus, given the fear of legal and social reprisal many women West Bank ID holders would experience, many forms of emotional and physical abuse went unreported. The scoping mission conducted by the GBV Sub-Cluster in 2018 found that the perpetrators of GBV is oftentimes a husband or former husband. Single women and younger girls reported that mainly strangers had exposed them to sexual harassments. Factors that would exacerbate the incidences and intensity of GBV included poverty, violence by the Israeli police or settlers, societal factors including attitudes towards sexuality, reproductive health and sexually transmitted infections (STIs) as well as entrenched patriarchal norms, discriminatory Israeli polices and structural inequalities. In addition, women living in poor neighborhoods in East Jerusalem stated that male unemployment often lead to an increase in drug addiction, which intensified the violence against the women and girls at home. Boys are also at greater risk of childhood violence (e.g. in schools) than girls according to a study conducted by UN Women in 2017, making them more prone to become perpetrators of GBV. Overall, the lack of adequate prevention and protection mechanisms and services had a significant impact on women and girls’ vulnerability to violence. Activities

In 2017, UNFPA together with PFPPA established two safe spaces (one stop shops) for GBV survivors in existing health clinics in East Jerusalem; one being the in Bab al Zahra and the other in the Old City. During the first half of 2018, 2,155 GBV survivor received GBV services at these two safe spaces. This project will ensure the continuity of these two space spaces. In addition, the project will establish new safe spaces in East Jerusalem in highly vulnerable areas. Improved availability of safe and high quality multi sectorial services and protection vulnerable women and girls in the affected communities in East Jerusalem. - Providing case management for 1,000 GBV survivors including women with disabilities and breast cancer survivors in East Jerusalem through GBV Safe Spaces (one stop shops) and hospitals such as Al Makased and Al Mutala.- Provide GBV health care and sexual and reproductive health services for 1,000 GBV survivors including women with disabilities through counselling centers or health providers- Providing MHPSS services (psychological counselling, and individual and group MHPSS support) for 2,000 GBV survivors through counselling centers or health clinics- Provide GBV health and MHPSS services to 500 GBV survivors reached through mobile clinics in East Jerusalem- Refer 100 GBV survivors to advanced mental health and psychosocial support services.- Conduct 10 open recreational days for in total 1,000 GBV survivors.- Providing dignity kits for 2,000 women and girls who are GBV survivors including women with disability and breast cancer patients. - Conduct focus groups interview to assess the survivor’s satisfaction on the provided services (communication with communities). Enhanced the knowledge and awareness of vulnerable groups of women and girls, in the affected communities on GBV, it consequences and the available services in their communities. - Conduct needs assessment in East Jerusalem with women, girls, men, and boys in risk of GBV to assess their knowledge about GBV and the available services.- Conduct awareness raise sessions on GBV and on available GBV services among 3,000 women and girls in high risk of GBV. - Produce material to be broadcasted through TV, radio, printed materials, and internet on GBV issues and available services.
