Project Summary

This project is a roll over project from 2019 HRP HPSE19-EDU-150687-1. The proposed project is in line with the education sub-cluster framework objectives 1 and 2 and aims to ensure that boys and girls have access to a protective and enabling educational experience in vulnerable MoEHE schools in the Gaza Strip. This project - fitting under a broad multi-year NRC education intervention applying psycho-social support in formal schools- is intended to focus specifically on Education in Emergency (EiE) based on the following three-fold approach: i) provision of technical support through the constitution of an Emergency Task Force under the Education sub-cluster to steer the EiE priorities; the mapping of teaching and non-teaching staff around the Designated Emergency Shelters (DES); the provision of tailored EiE training; and the delivery of pre-positioned educational kits to 20 school assigned Designated Emergency Shelter (DES); ii) implementation of the psycho-educational ‘Better Learning Program’ (BLP) reinforcing the system through the training of new cohorts of BLP Master Trainers at General and Educational Directors, Subject Supervisors and Head of Departments levels; contributing to the evidence based improved well-being and learning outcomes by finalizing the development of the M&E BLP toolkit; capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff in 30 target MoEHE schools; provision of emergency kits to the schools, awareness sessions for parents; and provision of a ‘Supporting the Supporters’ package for teaching and non-teaching in need; and iii) Advocacy pursuing enhanced protection from violations of the right to education.

1 - Needs

Since 30 March 2018 Gaza Strip has witnessed a significant increase in casualties in themass demonstration that took place along Israel perimeter’s fence resulting in 18,739persons injured including 1,642 children (OCHA Aug 2018). The increase of needs takeplace against a backdrop of an overall precarious humanitarian situation. 56% of childrensurveyed by NRC in March 2018 were suffering from traumatic nightmares, and whenrepeating the survey in April findings indicated an increase to 60%. Recent research(Safwat Diab, Jon-Håkon S., 2018: Factors Contributing to Students’ AcademicUnderachievement in War and Conflict: A Multilevel Qualitative Study-Gaza) found thatthe number of underachievers estimated by teachers/counsellors in sixth-grade classeswas between 18–22%. The findings also showed that parents and their children facedhigh levels of stress mainly due to their low economic and living conditions, as a result ofthe repetitive wars and years of economic siege of Gaza. Consequently, the familypriorities changed and became more focused on facilitating basic needs for their children,while academic achievement became less important. The research findings havedemonstrated how factors from students’ society, home, school, and personal psychologyare interrelated and contribute to psychosocial difficulties, leading to their academicunderachievement. Teachers and counsellors often lack the skills and responsemethodology to identify these children and respond in an appropriate manner. In the NRC‘Better Learning evaluation’ (“Improving Children Wellbeing: An evaluation of NRC’s BLP inPalestine”, 2017), it was reported that 36% of counselors suffered from sleepdisturbances, over 70% of them suffered from consistent feelings of sadness and 51% felthelpless. The above data is reinforced in the October NRC 2018 rapid- assessment carriedout in East Khanyunis where it was found that 50% of the teachers interviewed wereplanning for early retirement due to the economic crisis burden (presently teachers on thePA payroll receive only 50% of their salaries while teachers on the MoEHE Gaza payroll arepaid 40%). Students interviewed showed significant symptoms of frustration where 49%had lost hope in the future and 58% had problem in sleeping and nightmares. Theassessment also showed an alarming trend of 18% male and 10% female students (inhigher grades) thinking of dropping out. The latter was confirmed in the Sept 2018 NRC‘Eviction Assessment’ where it was found that 20% of FGD participants in Gaza hadstopped sending their children to school because they were not able any longer to pay forbasic school-related expenses (such as stationary, technical aids, daily expenses etc.) and10% out of them sent their children to work in order to help cover house expenses andmeet basic needs. Teaching methods continue to rely on memorization and note learning,60% remain unqualified (EDPS 2014-2019). 60% of children (“NRC Improved Educationevaluation”, Sue Nicholson, 2018) reported that their teachers were sometimes angry andpunished them, with 15% saying this happened a lot. In the September 2018 consultationscarried out by NRC -in close collaboration with the education sub-cluster- with education,protection main key stakeholders in Gaza, it was reported that the EiE file consisting in i)the mapping of teaching and non-teaching staff living near the Designated EmergencyShelters (DES), ii) their training on the EiE packages, iii) the revision of an acceleratedlearning curricula for catch up classes and iv) the update of the contingency plan, had notprogressed due to the lack of resources. It was also highlighted that the only prepositionededucational material stock available for emergencies consisted of very lownumbers of school bags, recreational kits, ECD, stationery, drawing notebooks, remedialfolders and child protection kits not sufficient to cover the basic educational needs in caseof emergency.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are now required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s new Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support.The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, all beneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits….etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general classroom-based intervention approach targeted at all children in target schools, its suitable for all children in any context, as its designed to reach larger numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee the equal chances of access for all children both males and females and their specific needs. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to children who need additional support, to support resilience a mong them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilised. iii) The third dimension is a specialised, clinical one designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptoms of traumatic stress. The equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls, special need or without special need children are affordable based on the real needs, beneficiaries interest and potential. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach.
