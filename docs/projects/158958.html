Project Summary

This project is a roll over from 2019 HRP project HPSE19-SHL-150673-1. The project overall objective is to ensure protection for most vulnerable Gazan households who are either displaced/evicted or at immediate risk of displacement/eviction by providing adequate temporary shelter support (Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 1). Providing essential shelter support meets basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation (Strategic Objective 2) and strengthens their capacity to cope with the protracted crisis (Strategic Objective 3). 286 families (1.602 individuals) with the worst score of socioeconomic vulnerabilities were identified as being in critical need for temporary shelter support. Factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability include sociodemographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD-headed), income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. To quickly restore temporary access to adequate shelter for the identified 286 most vulnerable households already evicted or displaced/in process of eviction/at risk of eviction or displacement, NRC will provide temporary shelter support. This includes cash for rent for a period of up to 6 months, essential household NFIs including water storage, re-integration kits, winterization and summarization NFIs (e.g. shelter kit, bedding kit).

1 - Needs

Lack of cash, social transfers, employment opportunities severely impacted the ability ofvulnerable households to afford safe & adequate housing. Most affected among those inacute poverty are those living in rented accommodations unable to pay rent & eitherevicted or at risk of eviction becoming temporarily homeless or living ininadequate/unsafe shelter conditions. Based on available data from MoPWH, 160 renter families are currently at risk of eviction pending final legal action. 20 out of them havetheir head of household already imprisoned for the inability to pay accumulated rentaldebt. Data from Technical Office of Supreme Judicial Council shows that highest numberof imprisonment orders in 2018 were issued in Gaza and North Gaza Governorates. Also,evicted families without alternative shelter or hosting solutions are sporadically visible inpublic places setting up temporary shelters or moving to inadequate & unsafe places. InSep/Oct 2018, NRC conducted 2 FGDs with 20 male and female renters in Gaza & NorthGaza, KIIs with 7 landlords, 19 household vulnerability assessments of male and femalerenters to identify the severity of evictions/risk of eviction including gender, age, andprotection concerns. Main findings are: - 10% of renters change residence every fewmonths, 50% changed their rental units several times in recent years due to their inabilityto pay rent. - 95% face difficulties finding new rental unit as land lords request regularincome. - 95% of renters mentioned that landlords took measures to guarantee theirdeserved rent: cut of utilities (10%), complaint to the police (15%), forced to sign payablebonds (20%), taking assets (10%). - 40% accumulated rental debts up to 9 months. - 25%were evicted due to inability to pay rent/receiving TSCA irregularly. - Evicted familiessometimes separate core family to live with different host families or move to live instorage places or garages inadequate for living - 86% of landlords confirmed inability tocollect rent, for 72% it is the only source of income. - Lack of legal awareness amongtenants & landlords on HLP rights/responsibilities/eviction. 42% of landlords rent theirhouses based on verbal agreement. - 60% of renter-households show moderate to highsocioeconomic vulnerability. - 5% are hardship cases registered with MoSD but have notreceived assistance. - 80% are unemployed (53%) or reporting unpredictable employment.- 79% lack basic furniture, household equipment (95%), kitchen items (47%), bedding sets(63%). - Coping strategies: stop paying utility bills (9%), change place of residence (11%),sell assets (13%), stop paying rent (15%), accumulate debts (24%). 4% live with othernuclear families to share rental costs. Children drop out of school to support lifeexpenses. Conducted FGDs show that eviction/risk of eviction affects males & females ofall ages but women/girls/boys face specific concerns. Female breadwinners (especiallydivorced) feel a particular sense of insecurity and fear from the landlord. Children facediscriminatory behaviour from landlords. Evictions/risk of eviction leads to familyseparations of parents & children which adds psychosocial burden on wives taking careof children separately. Negative gender/age related coping mechanisms: early marriage ofgirls & child labour to reduce financial burden; sharing rental unit with more than one familyaffects privacy especially at night for girls & boys of different ages; school dropout;irregular school attendance especially girls; frequent change of residencies affectspsychosocial stability of boys/girls at home & in school resulting in isolation,aggressiveness, shouting, trauma.

2 - Activities

To quickly restore temporary access to adequate shelter for most vulnerable householdsalready evicted or displaced/in process of eviction/at risk of eviction or displacement,NRC will provide temporary shelter support. This includes cash for rent for a period of upto 6 months for 286 families, essential household NFIs including water storage, reintegrationkits, winterization and summarization NFIs (e.g. shelter kit, bedding kit) for 226families (out of the 286 families that will receive cash for rent). The latter will considerspecific needs of males and females of all ages in the household. When cash for rent ispaid, NRC will ensure that the landlord provides minimal physical and climate protectionin exchange for receiving rent in advance. NRC identified the most vulnerable rentersbased on a socioeconomic household vulnerability assessment. The factors reflectingsocioeconomic vulnerability includes socio-demographic and socio-economic data, suchas characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD headed),income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative copingmechanisms, the status of household assets. The scoring system is designed to prioritizefemale/child/elderly/disabled headed households, heads of households with highdependency rates, large family size and overcrowded housing situations, and those withprotection concerns. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries of all ages in designingthe most suitable response to each renter-households’ specific gender/age/protectionconcerns. If NRC cannot address it directly, it ensures proper referrals. When choosing themodality of transfer, male and female family members will be included in choosing themodality best 􀂡t to their needs. Complementary NFI support will – case by case –consider gender/age specific needs and concerns especially as females are culturallyresponsible for household-related responsibilities. To minimize identified protectionthreats due to evictions and eviction-risks especially for women, boys and girls, thefollowing activities are tailored to meet the identified gender and protection related needs:1) Shelter field staff is trained to identify and refer cases with protection threats such asGBV, child marriage, violence against children. Boys and girls displaying behaviouralproblems due to having frequently moved residencies and schools or out of fear beingevicted any time will be referred to actors providing psychosocial support outside schoolor referred to be included in NRC’s Better Learning Program that provides psychoeducationsupport in a large number of schools in Gaza. 2) In case of cash for rent, it will be ensuredthat rental contracts are signed with both husband and wife and that males and femalesare provided with HLP information relevant to their rights and responsibilities as rentersbut also to understand rights and responsibilities of the landlord. It may also includenegotiating an agreement with landlords to redeem bonds securing previous debtresulting from unpaid rent. This is ensured through NRC’s Information, Counselling, andLegal Assistance Programme. 3) NFI-contributions will consider specific needs of malesand females of all ages in the household.

Community outreach and engagement: during the designphase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affectedcommunities especially youth and elderly were engaged during the identification of needsreflected in NRC’s conducted needs assessments (October 2018). During implementation,identified gender/age/protection concerns are addressed, if not directly by NRC thanthrough ensuring proper referrals. At the start of the project, essential project informationincluding selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism iscommunicated to community representative structures. Project information iscommunicated to both male and female family members in direct communication andthrough information leaflets which also informs them about NRC’s feedback andcomplaint mechanism. NRC communicates a feedback and complaint mechanism totarget-households to continuously assess the quality and accessibility of the providedresponse. Due to the identified risk that cash for rent might raise expectations for affectedhouseholds and incentivize vulnerable households to purposeful displacement in order toaccess cash, community representative structures will be involved to manageexpectations about the limited and temporary fund and to channel potential complaints.NRC conducts post-distribution monitoring and post-project surveys with beneficiaryhouseholds to measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance and thesatisfaction of male and female beneficiaries of all ages. NRC’s local partner IDCO will beinvolved in the post-distribution monitoring and beneficiary satisfaction survey at the endof the project. At the start of the project, each household benefiting from NRC’s temporaryshelter support will be provided with an information leaflet about the main projectinformation and how to access NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanisms. Male andfemale beneficiary-households are engaged when defining the assistance modality thatbest meets their needs, are fully engaged in the implementation process, and are visitedagain after the end of the project as part of a post-project review to collect feedback onthe efficiency and effectiveness of the project. Outcome indicators The below 3 outcomeindicators relate to the delivered services and will be measured through M+E monitoringvisits and post-distribution surveys. The total number of benefiting household numberswill be disaggregated based on gender and age. % of assisted households living inadequate temporary shelter (target: 90%) % of households who remain in their housingunit 3 months after the provided support (target: 95%) % beneficiaries who report/areobserved utilizing the shelter contribution as intended within 1 month of distribution(versus trade or selling, etc.) (target: 100%) Output indicators The below output indicatorsrelated to individuals will provide sex/age-disaggregated data. # of individuals receivedTemporary Shelter support target: 1.602 (785 females, 817 males) # of HH provided withclimate protection assistance (emergency shelter or household NFIs in-kind or in cash)target: 226

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to prioritize safety and dignity of vulnerable families and communities over other considerations, NRC will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt a conflict and context sensitive approach in dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, NRC will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions by coordinating and referring cases to specialized protection actors.NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff. At the community level, NRC will support multi-sectoral, sustainable, and integrated programming with all NRC’s Core Competencies and with other humanitarian actors, which will allow people to preserve or increase their resources and opportunities, thus making them more resilient, and more likely to find durable solutions. Shelter, WASH, ICLA and UDOC will ensure community engagement on different levels of implementation and echoing community feedback. NRC will support OCHA in the assessment and dissemination of information in order to ensure coordinated prevention and response to emergency. NRC will actively and regularly participate in the Shelter Cluster, Emergency and NFIs Technical Working Group and collaborate with other Clusters and actors on awareness raising, sensitisation and information dissemination activities to ensure that people have meaningful access to information and feedback mechanisms. NRC Advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation of Gaza on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the blockade through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the individuals involved, while upholding the do no harm principle.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC programs are designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, to reduce their vulnerability and build resilience. The community engagement at the design and implementation stage of the project will minimize the risk of impartial access to aid, as NRC will define the vulnerable groups in accordance and coordination with all local stakeholders and sectorial groups (youth, elderly, people with special needs and women representatives) regardless of age, social status and gender.
