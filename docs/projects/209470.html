Project Summary

According to HNO 2023, of the 15.8 million people in need, about 11 million need emergency assistance for life-threatening needs. Meanwhile, all people require life-sustaining support to meet minimum living standards. as of, 24th of Jan 2024, about 7.6 million people have been displaced in Sudan since the start of the conflict. The Gender-Based Violence Cluster's 2023 PiN is 3.1 million. Women and girls fleeing conflict and tribal clashes are engaged in livelihoods coping mechanisms that requires mobility until night times putting them at SGBV risks. situation among IDPs and vulnerable host communities are further aggravated by inadequate lighting in camps and settlements. access to energy and water supplies requires women and girls to travel long distances to collect water and firewood, exposing them to harassment and violence. The massive displacement and deteriorated humanitarian needs are bringing vulnerabilities to new levels, with war causing damage to Critical infrastructure, houses, livestock, farmlands and specifically electricity grids and services. There is a need to secure access to clean and renewable energy sources to ensuring the availability of basic services, protection and promote self-reliance and resilience.
The project is focused to provide assistance at two levels: 1. Households solar powered solutions, and 2. community level solar powered generators and hybrid power bank systems. 1. Provision of standardized household NFI kit/solar items to access affordable, clean and sustainable energy focusing on 3 in 1 solution by providing solar powered device that consist of highly efficient Lantern, Powerbank and FM radio. Each of the functions is enabler for a life-saving activities and contributes in a different aspect: - Lanterns are providing sense of security and are proven important element of prevention of GBV incidents. Powerbank is extremely important in the situation in which the electricity supply is widely interrupted and unavailable, solar powered powerbank can provide enough power to charge laptops, cell phones and similar electronic devices enabling access to energy and online services (banking and similar). - FM radio is a conflict situation is widely used as an early warning system and provides important aspect to access to information, which is crucial in the times of uncertainty and conflict. The project will utilize UNDP's existing presence and local partnerships with CSOs and CMCs to identify the most needed households and vulnerable groups. To ensure that the gender component is put into the consideration, the project aims to empower and protect women by distributing at least 70% of the NFI kits to vulnerable women. - Solar Lanterns with FM/AM radios and mobile chargers target population 30.000 x unit price 50 USD
Distributing solar powered generators and Hybrid Powerbank systems to communities affected by conflict facing power shortage is a critical way of providing energy and enabling access to water, health, education, communication services, as well as regenerate critical livelihoods. Hosting communities are at the extreme pressure to provided needed services to the thousands of IDPs and refugees and as such must be supported with solutions to provide power to Emergency Rooms, cooling facilities for medicaments, to power water pumps and provide power for all other services and needed community assets. The project will consider provision of All in one Hybrid System providing enough energy to power small hospital, Emergency rooms, schools or other service provider places. Approximately 10 units x unit price 10,000 to be distributed across the targeted communities.
Solar streetlights will provide additional sense of safety and security contributing to prevention of GBV cases. The project will install 100 streetlights x 500 USD unit price in critical locations as identified by the local community and coordination systems.

Implementing Partners

UNDP works closely with a wide range of national civil society organizations, including women's groups, youth organizations, activists, and peacebuilding actors.
UNDP has been able to reach out to some of these partners, most of who have expressed interest and ability to deliver services for the proposed intervention. The communication channels are restored, and partners are alerted about possibilities to be engaged in the project implementation, and also relevant communication about needs assessments is performed on daily basis.
Additionally, UNDP has previously established grassroots level community structures (community management committees – CMCs) in over 70 communities across 10 states in the country. UNDP has invested in building the capacities and skills of these CMCs through a series of training in various domains including service delivery, monitoring, follow-ups, outreach activities, gender equity, human rights, mediation and conflict management, social cohesion, etc.
These community-based structures ensure the effective mobilization of communities to action and will play a key role in the identification of beneficiaries, management and storage of product inputs and the effective distribution to beneficiaries.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will contribute to Sudan’s protection environment by conducting awareness raising trainings and campaigns on civil, social and economic rights of displaced population to increase self-reliance and opportunities for solutions. Focus will be on awareness and sensitization initiatives on basic rights with local authorities, host communities and service providers to reduce possibilities of GBV, tensions and promote social cohesion.The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions and protection. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. Protection specific activities have been incorporated in planned activities. The project will ensure vulnerable people have access to project assistance in addition to ensuring the use of a well-defined selection/identification criteria of beneficiaries for project assistance. The project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. The project will also ensure stronger coordination with other UN agencies working in the same geographical areas to avoid missed opportunities for integrating protection information in displaced population's profiling for project assistance. The project will seek for cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered during project implementation.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

All UNDP staff are carefully trained in codes of conduct and PSEA, prior to any engagement with affected communities. Trainings are being refreshed on a frequent basis. The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration for planning and implementation of interventions. UNDP will consult with local authorities and community members including vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities, older persons and women. UNDP will ensure that there is an agency-coordinated community-based feedback mechanism to take into account community complaints and suggestions and ensure that community members participate in the project processes. In addition, a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) form that can track the implementation of project activities and solicit feedback from beneficiaries face to face through visiting random households. PDM activities will include interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on quality and appropriateness of support provided. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in HQs during the ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings. To achieve this objective, the PDM plan has been developed and outlines data collection methods (household interviews, Focus Group Discussion and observation), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and handover procedures. Methodologies used will include a desk review before the field exercises are conducted.
