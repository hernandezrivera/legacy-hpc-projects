Project Summary

This project aims to support 7000 farmers and 21000 livestock breeders in Al-Bab, Jarablus, Afrin, A’zaz, Idleb, and Harim districts as the following:
1. Supporting 2000 wheat farmers with agriculture inputs and cash for irrigation and harvesting to planting one hectare for each farmer.
2. Supporting 1000 potato farmers with agriculture inputs, and cash for irrigation and harvesting to planting one hectare for each farmer.
3. Supporting 2000 HHs who usually grow summer, and autumn vegetables with Smart Climate Agriculture kits to plant one donum of vegetables and minimize climate changes effects on the land productivity. This activity is part of Modern Agricultural Systems and Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) to address climate change.
4. Supporting 200 IDP HHs with all necessary inputs to produce vegetables using a hydroponic agriculture system, and 200 Female HHH with oyster mushroom seeds. It is worth mentioning that IYD will support them with technical training and follow up all production stages and this activity is part of Modern Agricultural Systems and Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) to address climate change.
5. Supporting 1000 olive farmers and 500 Pistacia farmers with agriculture services, inputs and CFW activities based on value chains assessments. Here we should mention that the harvesting will be conducted as CFW modality (2000 workers).
6. Supporting 100 trees nurseries owners with needed tools and production inputs: agricultural bags - organic fertilizers - soil and 100 unskilled workers. Here we should mention those unskilled workers will work for 5 months, and they will get a salary for daily work.
7. Rehabilitating the irrigation canals of Jarablus and Al-Ra’ee by recruiting 200 workers as Cash for Work (CFW) to clean and maintain the canal edges.
8. Supporting 1000 livestock breeders with all agricultural Inputs and tools to plant and produce forage crops to feed their livestock (Azolla and Sudan grass). This new approach has been adopted by IYD in NWS to produce fodder to feed animals and to tackle the high price of fodder as well as minimize the climate changes effects.
9. Supporting 3000 breeders( 700 cow breeders/2300 sheep breeders) owning 10,000 cows and 100,000 sheep with pellet fodder distribution for 4 months in the winter season. Each cow and sheep will be supported with 10 and 1 kg respectively on a daily basis.
10. Supporting 2000 vulnerable HHs (Female HHH or PWD HHH) with 10000 turkey chicks. Here we should mention that the chicks will be fed, vaccinated, and bred for about four months before the distribution.
11. Providing health and reproductive treatment and vaccines for the livestock owned by breeders in the targeted areas to enhance the animals' productivity and reproductivity. 20 mobile clinics will run to provide the mentioned services aiming to enhance productivity and reproductivity which will lead to more profit for the breeders. It is worth mentioning that approximately 15000 participants will be supported during one year of project duration.
Approximately, 50 % of this project's fund has been secured.

4 - Needs

Since 2011, livestock breeding and agriculture have decreased throughout the area, which has had a negative impact on the local economy. The decrease appears to be driven by the absence of extension services, fodder rations, cultivation of pasture seeds and seedlings, digging of wells to provide water for the population and livestock, grading and improving activities, artificial insemination, vaccination, livestock treatments, veterinary drugs, improved breeds, and research services, and provision of agricultural inputs. IYD's Needs Assessments conducted during 2021 reflects the following facts: Agriculture component:  All targeted communities have a cultivated area, and farmers depend on the wells (surface, artesian) and irrigation channels to irrigate their land. The average cost to irrigate one dunum is (800 TL, about $ 100 per donum). The available crops’ types are (wheat, barley, legumes, and summer, winter vegetables). The available fruit trees are (olive, pomegranate, figs, nuts, and apple). ). The farmers' main obstacles are the lack of the capital to purchase the inputs in addition to the farmer's need for technical follow-up to deal with climate change and its effect on their crops. Livestock component: There are breeders for (sheep and cows) in the targeted communities. Although the availability of good-quality fodder, health services, and vaccines, the breeders' main obstacle is the lack of money to provide these services for their cattle.

Impact: HighMitigation: Printed guiding instructions that clarify the selection criteria. Active complaint mechanisms to get feedback from people who haven't benefited from servicesRisk 2:The project activities may cause harm for some women, girls, and PWDs, as well as other vulnerable groups during their participation in these activities either through a cause of action or failing to take necessary actionsLikelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigation1-Analyze programs from different perspectives will be conducted by asking the local community for feedback on what is not going well and ways to improve it, in addition, a constant assessment will be taken if project activities are having negative consequences on the beneficiaries and may threaten their safety and dignity2- IYD will establishe robust feedback and complaint mechanisms. Such as:• Customize a text messaging hotline and secure feedback box.• Conduct face-to-face interviews with beneficiaries• Organize community meetings to discuss the beneficiary list and gather feedback• Conduct FGD with beneficiaries, including vulnerable groups (women, PWD) to give them the opportunities to participate and thus, help them protect themselves, claim their rights, and allow them to provide input on program design and define the priorties


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

•IYD has adopted the inter-agency Code of Conduct Which condemns the SEA ( Endorsed by the Humanitarian Liaison Group - 2020) for the Syria cross-border response, which is intended for all staff and representatives of humanitarian organizations as well as their associated contractors, and volunteers•IYD is an active member of the PSEA network, and it is already familiar with the network’s monthly reporting tool where IYD reports its PSEA monthly activities such as (the number of beneficiaries reached by the distribution of awareness-raising materials, the number of beneficiaries reached by providing PSEA awareness-raising sessions, the number of field staff with direct interaction with beneficiaries who trained on PSEA)•IYD has established an internal SEA reporting and investigative mechanism which will not tolerate an atmosphere of impunity toward SEA. Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct•Complete PSEA Checklists: The organizational checklist was be completed by the management and the HR unit in communication with the CEO. Also, the Field Observation Checklist for PSEA Mainstreaming will be completed by IYD's program managers who will utilize these tools in all our interventions during the implementation and monitoring stages to identify gaps and risks related to PSEA


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the project leading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. However, IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge • Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up the service providers day by day, to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them. • Social risks: From a social standpoint, this project presents no risk to the IYD and local communities. Although women are still underrepresented in decision making bodies, their concerns have over time been taken into account, and this project will seek to ensure their better presence and representation. •Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas, and deterioration of the security situation. IYD has a strong relationship with neutral communities’ leaders and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities or IYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety •
