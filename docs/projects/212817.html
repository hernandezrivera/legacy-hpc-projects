Project Summary

The intervention is aligned with HRP 2023-2024 Strategic Objectives 1 and intends to complement nutrition sector efforts in Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. The project aims to Provision of nutrition specific preventive services to children (boys and girls) aged 6 - 59 months, Adolescent girls, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), preventive and awareness activities targeting children under five years and PLWs in Al-Hasakeh (Al-Hasakeh city and Quamishli) and Deir-ez-Zor (Deir-ez-Zor city) for two years (2024-2025) to reach 6,000 beneficiaries for 2024. In addition to address some of the key determinants of malnutrition by promoting improved IYCF practices and healthy behaviors associated with unnecessary deaths among vulnerable populations in the above areas. By integrating the CMAM program with the implementing partner's existing PHC clinics in the targeted areas, which will be supported by an outreach team to screen and identify cases of malnutrition among boys, girls, and women among IDPs and the host community. TdH will repeat the screening process quarterly. CMAM clinic will also be established in the target areas supported by a team of trained volunteers from the project implementing partner to address the unmet needs of acutely malnourished boys, girls and women among vulnerable Host communities, returnees, and IDPs in the target areas. With coordination with the nutrition sector, TdH will focus on equitable and inclusive access to essential, life-saving humanitarian assistance based on vulnerability targeting. Also, TdH will focus on identification of malnutrition cases through screening with appropriate referral.
Estimated 650,000 children under the age of five in Syria are stunted, indicating a rise of 150,000 compared to 2019. Stunting rates have consistently risen across Syria, increasing from 12.6% in 2019 to 16.1% in 2023. Alarmingly, five out of 14 Governorates report unacceptably high stunting rates exceeding 20%, including Aleppo, Idleb, Deir-ez-Zor, Al Hasakeh, and Ar Raqqa. Certain areas within Deir-ez-Zor, Al Hasakeh, and Ar Raqqa report catastrophic levels of stunting exceeding 30%.
Alarming statistics reveal that one in four women aged 15–49 years suffers from anemia, with an even higher prevalence observed among teenage girls, where 1 in 2 girls are anemic. Additionally, 1 in 10 women is wasted (MUAC <23cm). Iron deficiency anemia and poor maternal nutrition during pregnancy and breastfeeding contribute to an increased risk of preterm delivery or low birth weight, leading to stunted growth in the child.
Protection Risk Analysis:
PR1: The beneficiaries may have no access to the centers. M1: TdH will consider this point upon the selection of the center location and specifications in addition to the involvement of women and girls in the activities timing and center shifts to ensure their accessibility during the day.
PR2: The volunteers may not understand the cultural context. M2: TdH will ensure female-to female communication, and male to male communication between staff and beneficiaries to respect the cultural background of the area of intervention .
PR3: Abuse from staff to beneficiary during the project implementation. M3: all TdH staff and local partner volunteers will undergo a CP training, and PSEA training. Also, all of them should sign the code of conduct, in addition to the assignment of the CP commit.
PR4: An outbreak of Cholera and COVID may occur during the activities M4: TdH will provide PPEs to staff and beneficiaries during the activities , and will ensure social distancing as well as dividing the beneficiaries into different shifts and groups to avoid any potential gathering.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The targeted areas don’t have thematic CVA interventions, for that reason TdH will provide Cash For Nutrition to ensure social protection with cash availability for PLWs for 8 cycles per year and 4 cycles for SAM-MAM cases . The focus will be on improving availability of diverse nutritious food for PLWs with MAM cases and those who are at risk to be MAM to improve maternal dietary diversity. The project will provide focused training for local partner volunteers to strengthen their capacity to provide IYCF services and activities to those in need, in addition to the capacity building activities for local partners through training of volunteers and community members, and support to facilities and communities for counselling on infant and young child feeding practices recommended by WHO, with coordination with stakeholders to identify and divide sectors for outreach activities. The local partners' centers are located in the targeted areas and are accessible to beneficiaries according to Sphere standards. All activities will be coordinated with the Nutrition Sector and working groups to avoid overlap or duplications. TdH is an active member of the Nutrition Sector. TdH Project Manager and M&E Officer will conduct field visits on a regular basis and dealing with process monitoring basically, the methods that will be used to ensure implementing partners and donors to learn whether the beneficiaries are targeted properly and whether they are receiving the correct services in a timely manner through monitoring the efficiency, effectiveness and accountability of the project. PSEA TDH adapts a zero-tolerance policy against sexual exportation and abuse, and to ensure all PSEA standard are respected: All TDH staff have signed the Code of conduct upon recruitment. All TDH staff are oriented on PSEA topic via induction sessions upon recruitment TDH has its own internal reporting mechanism on PSEA, and has clear reporting channels that are accessible for all staff. All TDH staff including field staff that is working directly with beneficiary are regularly trained on PSEA, and reporting channels. Beneficiaries are oriented on PSEA reporting Mechanism, through multiple channels (lines, Posters, email, Focal points). TDH includes Gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection services in its intervention to support women facing sexual Abuse. Risk and Mitigation:R1: Local partner lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities. M1: SARC displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations,R2: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities M2: TDH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of SyriaR3: Fraud Usage of cash assistance by households. M3: The cash will be provided via banking system to ensure tracking, TDH will follow up with them via PDM and open feedback channels.
