Project Summary

The purpose of this intervention is to provide vulnerable populations, including persons with disabilities and the elderly, access to safe and quality health services. The intervention targets at least 16,000 individuals in various locations and the entry point will be through door-to-door outreach activities conducted by at least 16 community based volunteers trained by HI and deployed by local partners Mousawat and CBRA, who will be assessed for health, mental health, and rehabilitation needs.
This intervention includes door-to-door outreach activities conducted by trained volunteers who will raise awareness on health-related topics and assess the health needs of at least 16,000 individuals in various locations. Based on the needs assessed, social workers will refer and follow up on at least 2400 vulnerable persons for health, rehabilitation, and/or mental health needs. Additionally, 4 mobile health care teams will be deployed to provide care to at least 800 patients with limited mobility. Furthermore, at least 3840 women and girls, including those with disabilities living in highly vulnerable conditions, will receive tailored health kits. The overall goal of this intervention is to improve the health outcomes and well-being of vulnerable populations through a comprehensive and community-driven approach.
HI and its partners will implement this intervention in different areas across North, Akkar, BML, and Bekaa regions. The targeting criteria for this project are vulnerable populations, including persons with disabilities, the elderly. The project will address different nationalities, including, Lebanese (60%), Palestinian (37%), and migrant (3%), living in highly vulnerable conditions, while considering a 50-50 gender ratio. This encompasses a wide range of individuals who may face various barriers to accessing healthcare services, such as physical and economic barriers, social isolation, and discrimination. If individuals from other nationalities are identified during outreach activities, they will be referred to complementary projects conducted by HI. The intervention's scope encompasses a wide range of vulnerable populations and aims to provide equitable access to healthcare services. The project's multi-disciplinary and community driven approaches, involving trained volunteers, social workers, and mobile healthcare teams, allows for a comprehensive and tailored response to the diverse health needs of these populations.
All HI and partner staff have undergone sensitization on HI's policies, including PSEA, AAP, risk identification, and DGA (Disability, Gender, and Age) marker. This will enable partners to identify the most vulnerable households and ensure that all activities are inclusive and accessible to everyone. Given the highly vulnerable nature of the targeted groups, AAP will be ensured throughout all phases of the project, through continuous assessments at the field level. This will enable partners to identify and respond to any protection risks that may arise.
The beneficiaries will receive fundamental protection messages through outreach efforts, and they will be informed about all HI FCM channels, including the complaint phone line, email, boxes, and FGDs. This will ensure that all groups are aware of how to express feedback or complaints in a safe and confidential manner.
Overall, the project will ensure that all activities are accessible, safe, and dignified for all vulnerable populations, and will incorporate protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion across all phases of the project. This will ensure that the project meets the diverse needs of the targeted populations and promotes the wellbeing and rights of all individuals.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Vulnerable populations, including persons with disabilities and elderly, will have access to safe, and quality health services through a community and multi-disciplinary approach. Under SO1 of the appeal 1) HI will support partners Mousawat and CBRA to train and deploy at least 16 community-based volunteers to conduct door to door outreach activities. Throughout this process, 2) the outreach volunteers will conduct awareness activities on health, mental health, and disability, and other topics, and assess the health (including MH and rehabilitation) needs of at least 16,000 individuals in the various locations targeted by the project. According to the needs assessed, partners’ social workers will 3) refer and follow up on at least 2400 vulnerable persons for health and/or rehabilitation and/or mental health needs by referring them to identified PHCs, HI rehabilitation partners, and other actors in order to address their needs. For identified persons with mobility challenges, 4) HI will support local partners Mousawat and CBRA to deploy 4 mobile health care teams to provide care to at least 800 patients with limited mobility to meet their health and/or functional needs. In parallel, under SO4 5) Based on their needs identified by the outreach teams, HI will provide at least 3840 women and girls including those with disabilities living in highly vulnerable conditions. These kits will be designed and tailored considering the specific need of girls and women with disabilities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As part of its implementation strategy, HI mainstreams protection in all its projects and activities, which allows to focus on providing access to the most vulnerable, including persons with disabilities and elderlies, and ensure that activities are safe and dignified. Project activities are designed to include all critical vulnerable individuals within the Lebanese, Palestinian and migrant population. All HI and partner staff have been sensitized on HI’s policies (PSEA, AAP, risk identification) and DGA (Disability, Gender and Age) marker to recognize all vulnerabilities, while Leaflets, posters conveying Safeguarding messages are displayed in accessible areas for all.HI will conduct tailored activities based on previous and continuing beneficiary consultations to ensure that their needs are promoted during all phases of the response.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

AAP is ensured throughout all phases of project design, implementation, and evaluation. The project design is based on continuous assessments at field level that analyze the changing needs of affected populations and the relevance of ongoing and potential activities. The project will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders have access to project information via posters, brochures, and the existing coordination working groups. The project will ensure that all HI and partner staff receive PSEA and FCM training/refresher. Beneficiaries will get fundamental protection messages through outreach efforts, and they will be informed about all HI FCM channels (complaint phone line, complaint email, complaint boxes, HI staff, FGDs) to ensure that all groups are aware of how to express feedback or complaints in a safe and confidential manner. A complete MEAL plan, including an indicator matrix, will be established, and systematic monitoring will take place to oversee activity execution.
