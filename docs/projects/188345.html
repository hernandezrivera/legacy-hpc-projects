Project Summary

The project is designed by responding to the most crises- affected people in NW Syria with life-saving interventions Food baskets, RTEs, hot meals, and free and subsidized bread distribution and this project will be integrated with Qatar Charity’s Wheat value chain projects conducted in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate in these districts (Maaret Tamsrin, Ariha, Dana, Salqin, A’zaz, Afrin, Ma'btali , Alba bans Jarablus districts) to contribute and enhance stabilization of people.
The project will be targeting both IDPs and host communities to empower both to face the negative coping strategy and mitigate the tension that could come out between the communities.
The project will contribute to increase access to monthly emergency food provisions for recently displaced households and the most vulnerable host community and contributing to improving the food security status of food insecure people in Idleb Governorate over two years, 2022 and 2023, with secured fund about 30% from SCHF and QC fund. The project concept is in line with Sector objective 1: “Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance”
In 2022, QC will be response to vulnerable people (IDPs and host community) in two governorates Idleb and Aleppo through the following interventions:

4 - Needs

Based on the HNO report 2022, 2020 Food Security Assessment (FSA) and 2020 Food Security and Livelihoods Assessment (FSLA) findings, WFP food prices data an estimated most IDPs and other vulnerable crisis-affected people in Syria remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, including food. The national assessment showed that 52.8 % of IDPs were food insecure, indicating a higher level of vulnerability than residents (49.7 %) while Newly Displaced IDPS (NIDPs) in the north-west,322 found that 41.5 per cent of all NIDP are food insecure and in need of assistance, 50.1 % had experienced hunger.Debts and credits are also a common coping strategy, with 52.9 % of households having taken on debt.In 2021, climatic and human-caused shocks affecting natural resources, particularly water, have intensified. Erratic rainfall in combination with historically low water levels in the Euphrates River have not just reduced access to water for drinking but also triggered substantial harvest and income losses, Across the country food insecurity has grown – with an estimated 12.8 million severely food insecure people, Syria ranked amongst the ten most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021. Families across Syria are continuously changing daily habits to maintain some level of food consumption. Household’s capacity to cope with growing food insecurity has diminished, indicated by the regular consumption of poor diets as reported by 44% of the population.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

QC remains committed to the Do No Harm principle. By taking into consideration equal access, safety and dignity and ensuring accountability to affected newly IDP in the camps and host communities. The targeted beneficiaries will be selected based on defined vulnerability criteria to ensure that the needs of the most vulnerable (especially elderly, women, PWDs, pregnant women) had been covered.The followings are some expected protection alongside with its mitigation measures. To protect beneficiaries and Women particularly from any exploitation, discrimination and PSEA, posters will be published showing that all services are free of charge and QC adhere to Zero-Tolerance principle.Protection and GBV risks: 1- Difficulties for PWDs to access for the proposed support. Mitigating measures: • adopt door to door modality to reach the food items and bread for PWD - training for the field staff to deal with vulnerable .2- Crowding during the distribution of bread that may cause infection with COVID19Mitigating measures:• Appling all protective measures (based on WHO recommendations) • Managing separate waiting and entry lines at distribution points3- Violate PSEA risk: Signing PSEA and child safeguarding by all staff. Getting training on PSEA for all staff. PSEA awareness. Assigning complaint mechanism for SEA issues. policies of Code of conduct, PSEA, child safeguarding is mandatory for all staff and vendors working with QC.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

QC has a clear strategy to mitigate the risks of PSEA, the strategy includes direct and indirect beneficiaries of our programming, adults in wider communities in which we work in. It also includes QC’s staff, partners, suppliers sub-contractors and any other individual engaged by QC's programs. Particular attention is needed for vulnerable groups, including women and children, and those most at risk of discrimination based on disability, gender identity and sexual orientation, age, and religion. Our approach to PSEA is through the following:1- Ensure that all staff, representatives and third parties are aware of the high standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect individuals of SEA. 2- Having an organizational culture based on accountability where there is zero tolerance for PSEA. 3- Have an endorsed reporting mechanism for staff, beneficiaries and partners to feel safe to report violations, and then 4- ensure that reports are handled in a manner that respects due process, and swift and credible investigation for any violations. And actively promote fair investigation and sanctions.5- mitigating risks in programming by identifying the risks and designing the programaccordingly. Starting from needs assessments through project design (shelter layouts, kits components) to the implementation to closing phases. 6- Comprehensive operational, HR and mgmt. measures are also required to PSEA. By conducting relative vetting and checks of staff, suppliers.

Do-no harm approach is not consideredMitigation: Good community engagement to explain the project scope and adopted criteria.3- Financial Risk: Fluctuation in prices and USD exchange rates Mitigation: Conduct an in-depth market assessment for project deliverables during designing phase, and pre-procurement process. 4- Health risks A-Low quality of flour and food items (The validity period has expired) Mitigation: Quality Assurance will be essential criteria for procurement and contracting for flour. Technical skilled committee will be established to check procured items according to the samples received from the suppliers. B- COVID -19 outbreak:Mitigation: Address bread to those people who are in quarantine because of COVID-19 through advance coordination with health centers and health cluster, avoid crowding keeping of social distancing between the BNFs (at least 2 meters according to WHO recommendations , persons who will be included in distribution should be commit with prevention measures.
