Needs

The needs of the Rohingya children and adolescent in camp are vast and complex, extending across all sectors. This includes a lack of adequate food and nutrition, unsafe & unprotected environment, lack of clean drinking water and sanitation facilities, health care facilities specially lack of psychosocial and mental health support and limited access to learning opportunities. Children in camp and in the host community face specific vulnerabilities, different from adults. Many of the children have come to Bangladesh unaccompanied; some children have lost their parents or caregivers in the camp because they got lost or were kidnapped. Child protection and child safety is a constant worry across all groups of children. Physical and mental insecurity in camp environments have resulted in negative coping strategies adopted by parents to protect their children, especially their daughters who are kept in or around the shed as much as possible. Child & adolescent girls trafficking is a major protection risk in camp specially for unaccompanied and separated children. Children in camp are currently unable or limited in their ability to play freely or learn in preparation for their future. There is very limited space to play in the camps because sheds are placed close together to accommodate as many people as possible, although playing and learning are key priorities for children.In the camp adolescent girls’ movement is limited and they mostly stay in their shed, which limits their access to services and resources. It also denies them the opportunity to develop support networks.Gender based violence, including sexual violence, as well as domestic violence and child early and forced marriage persists in the lives of adolescent girls from Rakhine state in Myanmar. After fleeing their homes and arriving in Bangladesh, adolescent girls face difficult camp conditions that contribute to the ongoing risks of gender-based violence including intimate partner violence and child early and forced marriage.The children and adolescent need to learn, play, feel safe, eat and live healthily. Child friendly spaces (CFS) have become a widely used approach to protect and provide psychosocial support to children in emergencies. It become a key interventions to protect children from physical harm and psychosocial distress and to help them continue learning and developing both during and immediately after an emergency. The CFS may provide health, nutrition and psychosocial support and other services and activities, which can restore children to normal living. Child-friendly spaces provide psychosocial support to children and adolescents, stimulating their learning and well-being in a protective environment. Activities range from sports to learning about hygiene to parental sensitization.

Activities or outputs

1) Operating 11 CFSs to provide safe and supportive environments for children.A Child Friendly Space (CFS) provides children with a protected environment in which they can participate in organized activities to play, socialize, learn and express themselves. "Essence of Learning" methodology, which fosters learning of children affected by crisis, will be followed, and daily three sessions will be held segregating the children into the following age groups 1) up to 5 years 2) 6 to 8 years 3) 9 and 10 years 4) 11 to 13 years and 6) 14 to 18 years (adolesent clubs). All groups will include girls and boys together except for adolescents, which will have separate groups. No Strings International’s film, theatre, puppetry, songs and other creative arts approaches will deliver culturally appropriate messages to communities, developed with local stakeholders and using local talent whenever possible. A new film will be developed in 2019, which will take place in the camp and will focus on PSS issues of children and families. Messages will cover topics such as normalizing emotions, stress management and identifying and reporting abuse. Metholodology developed by Clowns Science Dream will also be used by the project staff to encourage children to play drama games and develop ability to express their emotions and gain confidence. 2) Provide specific support to adolescents.Adolescent groups of boys and girls will provide life skills activities and positive socialization and will explore ideas for leadership opportunities, and promote positive masculinity for boys, and healthy relationships and positive coping for both boys and girls through NSI methodology. Vocational trainings will be explored for adolescents. They will also be targetted by the Clowns methodology. 3) Provide integrated support to family members and engage the community in Child Protection.The project will support mothers/caregivers and babies, and many activities running in the CFS also aim at supporting parents.The community will be involved at different stages of the project, and different ways and layers of participation will be set up to accommodate the different needs of family members. At the start-up of the new CFS, community members will be gathered regularly to give their suggestions on content and design of activities as well as practical issues around CFS, so as to ensure their participation from the start. A survey conducted with households of the area will inform what are the preferred ways and channels of participation of caregivers.A CFS Management committee which includes representatives of fathers, mothers, religious and community leaders will be constituted and gather regularly to review and inform activities in the CFS. Specific youth group, mothers group, and fathers group will also gather in the CFS. All of them will receive orientation on Child Protection issues and principles and discuss about the issues affecting them and the children so as to identify community-led initiatives. 4) Establish specialized support for children in need.The CFS has a dedicated team for specialized support to children, which includes both PSS providers and Case Managers. Providers have received dedicated training and coaching and are part of a ongoing capacity building plan over the coming year to provide focused support to children and families. PSS providers are in charge of outreach activity, identification and referral. Case Managers are addressing cases following due process.Coordinated referral mechanisms will be established with other service providers to ensure children have access to relevant services.
