Project Summary

In 2023 DRC will have been integrating both its global strategy and country strategy into this joint response plan to enhances coordination with livelihoods and skill development sector (LSDS), the newly establish sector, in the field. Through this project, DRC will aim to contribute to enhancing the decent and resilient livelihoods to the selected Rohingya and host community people towards the effects of climatic change, environmental degradation, and natural disasters through overall protection services which specifically includes livelihood and skill building on green agriculture, vocational training, cash/grants assistances and climate sensitive innovative interventions for Rohingya men and women under economic recovery. Training will be provided with standard curriculum /module aligned with sector guidance with the support from hiring experts and government line agencies skilled trainer. The qualified and interested trained people will receive relevant inputs and cash grants by DRC to utilize the gained knowledge for income earnings . DRC will provide some market development and linkage training to make the enterprise more market healthy and link them with bigger market actors and financial bodies ,where applicable ( Host only). Backstopping services will also be ensured throug DRC and sectoral support at Camps and host level.During this project targeted both Host and camps communities will be benefited and increase the relationship among them by getting economic benefits.
DRC will aim to address the project in Camp 5, Camp 8E and Jalipalong, Palong Khali and Rajaplong union under host community. Under skill development initiatives, DRC will focus on improving the skill and awareness on livelihoods and emphasis will be given to strengthen the institutional capacity of the community-based organizations/community groups on market systems approach- business plan, entrepreneurship development, women friendly market space identification and improvement through renovation work. At individual level, DRC will ensure inclusive and locally accepted livelihood skills and aim to improve the skills and awareness for adapting climate smart green agriculture and eco system-based adaptation practices, bamboo made handicrafts production, land stabilization through plantation, soil conservation, re/construction of community livelihood assets through mobilizing cash for workers. Under green agriculture a good number of Rohingya women and host community women will increase their skill and knowledge on vertical gardening, compost prepartion and using in their garden. DRC will also initiate technical capacity building of the community volunteers, CBO representatives, implementing partners and relevant field officials of government technical agencies on conservation, green technologies and CBOs sustainability approach for better services. The selected vulnerable Rohingya women through providing skills training and in kind and cash support to start and continue their small-scale business will be another small intervention under livelihood support.
Apart from Rohingya, vulnerable host community households will also receive unconditional cash grant support as per Cash working group guidance to cope with the recent price hike shocks. The Livelihoods team will continue to support the most vulnerable and marginalized individuals such as FFH,PWD, transgender, survivors of GBV, through cash grants and livelihood inputs such as cattle, poultry, sewing machine, vegetable seeds, nursery plants. Last but not the least, DRC will establish strong linkage and coordination with government and other actors in camps and host by developing capacity of local volunteers and community-based organizations. Besides, DRC will closely coordinate with the District Commissioner, RRRC, sectors, Food Security Cluster and other agencies to ensure alignment with govt. rules and align the intervention in line with sustainability nexus.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly among Rohingya households and host community since 2020 where 95 percent of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance and 52 percent of the population being moderately to highly vulnerable These results reflect the slow economic recovery of an already fragile population with no income sources nor livelihood opportunities for Rohingya and economic contraction and decline in economic activity across most sectors during Covid-19 lockdown in a population highly dependent on daily wage labor ( REVA 5 Report, March 2022). The price hikes of community’s consequences of the Covid pandemic also excavated by the Russia Ukraine crisis (the financial express, August 2022). Moreover, Cox’s Cox’s Bazar and its surrounding districts of Bangladesh are among the lowest-performing districts in terms of education and skills training (World Bank, 2019). The district is historically lagging behind both in the economic performance as well as the human development index. About 33 per cent and 17 per cent of the population live below the poverty and extreme poverty lines, respectively. The low performance is even more evident when considering gender parity and women empowerment. There is significant discrepancy in terms of gender parity in labour market and educational attainment. Women are disproportionately engaged in low productivity agricultural sector and low productivity occupations leading to low earnings. Though livelihoods and some case -centered interventions are still restricted in Rohingya camps where the Rohingya do not get work permits and their movement outside the camps for daily work also restricted. But Rohingya are allowed to work as volunteers and cash for workers within the camps. Ban on normal employment, limitations on mobility, exclusion from formal financial services, landlessness and hopeless conditions make the Rohingya livelihoods harder and harder. The livelihoods of Rohingya’s most on humanitarian aid except some recent initiatives on home gardening at limited scale. The basic needs of Rohingya’s are met by humanitarian aids where Rohingya are facing economic pressure to cover their family expenditures- like children education, by clothes, purchase other commodities and medicines, perform marriage and other social functions, etc, are not available at camps. Debt, selling rations, shelter materials and NFI, child marriages, unhealthy competition with daily labour wage, motivating to fleeing tendency to third countries for survival are the negative coping mechanisms adopted by Rohingyas to cover unmet needs. The host community people also face the similar problems basically for the competition of natural resources and the labour market. In some cases, limited humanitarian aids and less economic supports also make the host people vulnerable along with natural shocks, land tenure systems, forest, fuel and persistent poverty. Limited or no access on natural resources on some ban period-restricted fishing almost 3 months over the year). Surroundings critical biodiversity are also at risk where environment issues soil erosion and landslides are very common in the area, affecting water availability for irrigation facilities thus ultimately hamper the overall households level food security. Apart from this still there is a need to strengthen collaboration with government departments, local government, communities and agencies to create a strategy for integrating efforts for better facilities and services provides to the vulnerable. Considering the above challenges, the DRC’s proposed project will contribute to enhance the decent jobs and livelihoods situation by implementing the selected interventions- green agriculture, environmental protection and cash-based interventions support.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is aligned with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) and recognizes the importance of focusing on integration among the interventions and the actors for better services to the targeted people on livelihoods and skill development and food security both for Rohingya and host communities. In line with the rationalisation Principle #3 for promoting integrated approach, DRC will aim to fill the gaps in livelihoods services for targeted women and men where all ages of people including people with disabilities and vulnerable people also get benefits from livelihoods interventions. DRC will also seek to improve the inter-sectoral camp-based coordination in a coordinated manner through CCCM and integrating apprpriate service delivery to refugees and host communities while maintaining a collaborative and complementary relations with the Government/CiCs and other sector agencies. DRC will maintain the guidelines on LSDS and its landscape at large, protection risks, needs and gaps in services provision in areas where it works. Considering the Principle #4 for ensuring predicability and continutiy, DRC will ensure its continutity and stabiity of the programme together with other donor funded projects by DANIDA, ECHO, GAC, 4UN and other major donors. Additionally, DRC has a livelihoods and economic recovery component through which it will ensure integration with protection and livelihood/economic recovery and continue integration of the interventions in both camp and host community to improve relations with refugees, the Government,and other partners in the camps. DRC expects to collaborate with UNHCR, partners and local actors in line with the “One Camp” approach for utilizing efforts and resources. DRC will place communities at the center, with refugees’ and host communities’ own capacities to play a key role in preparing and responding to livelihood challenges and adopting innovative approaches. DRC is currently having sectoral assessment for partner & actor integration

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

As DRC is a protection focused organization, so all protection policies and compliances will be followed in its LLH programming acordance with the guidance and coordination of protection sector. All four protection mainstreaming principles - safety, dignity, no harm; meaningful access; accountability; participation and empowerment-will be followed during the implemrntion phase align with the DRC's current practices general protection, protection monitoring, case managemnet support. The support will be seek from the internal protection sector and capacity of the LLH staff will be developed by DRC's protection sector. DRC is already an active member of the ISCG PSEA networking group. In association with ISCG PSEA networking group, PSEA incidents are being reported to the sector coordinator and data regarding volunteers and staff’s training on PSEA and reported PSEA incidents tracker are being shared by our organization.

3.2 - Gender

DRC has a zero tolerance policy to sexual abuse and exploitation and an active member of PSEA network and has a PSEA policy and an internal mechanism to report to SEA concern. Through DRC protection programming PSEA training to all of its staff and volunteers will be arranged where livelihoods sector is integrated. Gender equality is programmatic priority, and gender-sensitive and appropriate interventions have been designed to gender mainstreaming. All activities and report wll include sex, age and disability disaggregated data. Safe and inclusive programming principles and confidentiality will be maintained throughout the implementation phase. Besides, a general framework for mainstreaming gender components in projects is provided by the Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming Policy, which exists at DRC and will be utilized for the project.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

DRC is communited to ensuring age including safeguarding approach and principle (best interest of children, confidentiality, do no harm) accorss its programming cycle. DRC's LLH staff and vulunteers capacity will be strengthen through the DRC's Child protection team when necessary in particular the new onbaord staff. For this purpose data collection tools and sampling approaches are being revised to ensure data collection can be disaggregated by AGD profile. All DRC employees and volunteers have received training on safeguarding concerns. In addition, the reporting system is in place. Besides, all aged group will be covered where adult men and women,elderly men and women will be the primary target and children and under 18 people will be the secondary beneficiaries.

3.4 - Diversity

DRC is also committed to follow the AGD (Age , gender and diversity ) policies and principles, thus all sorts of diversity principles-respect on race, ethinicity, cast, social and political beleives, etc will adhere during the project cycle. While implementing its projects, DRC will seek to increase the accessibility of services for all age and gender categories. The vulnerable segments of the community including People with disabilities (PWD), elderly people, will be the priorities targetee under this project. DRC will continue to involve the beneficiaries in each stage of planning and implementation for identifying the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities, and coping mechanisms in line with the social cohesion mandate and following Do No Harm Principle.

DRC is committed to the principles of AAP and mechanism has been put in place to ensure these principles are respected during implementation and while interacting with partners.
