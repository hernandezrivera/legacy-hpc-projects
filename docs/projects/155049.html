Project Summary

The overall goal of the WHO emergency response strategy is to reduce morbidity and mortality among the Rohingya population and host community in Cox’s Bazar. The specific objectives of the project are to:
1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases.
2. Encourage healthy living; improve health seeking behavior and utilization of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups.
3. Strengthen health sector coordination and information management with a focus on rational and accountable health service delivery systems, including monitoring of minimum standards and the quality of the services provided.
The project targets the entire 1,242,441 Rohingya and host population in need in Cox's Bazar.
WHO supports the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Bangladesh, the Civil Surgeon’s Office, Cox’s Bazar, Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner’s Office (RRRC), and works in conjunction with sector partners, in particular those in the health, WASH, protection and nutrition sectors. This project will focus on health systems strengthening as a part of transition from emergency response to sustained development. A sustained development strategy will be developed and implemented to deliver health services and strengthen the health system to the benefit of the refugees as well as the host community. The underlying principles will be to support national health systems to deliver an effective response, increased direct investment in public and local institutions, and meaningful and measurable capacity development. This will include strengthening all building blocks of national health systems as relevant to the Cox’s bazar set up, namely liaise with national government on the governance and leadership through Civil Surgeon’s office and DGHS coordination cell; availability of health workforce, vaccines and essential technology, health information, financing of health sector, service delivery based on minimum services package, amongst others for both the Rohingyas and the host community.
WHO will continue to support government institutions to establish more sustainable emergency preparedness and response capacities to ensure continuity of emergency services during the monsoon and cyclone seasons. WHO has a separate DRR/contingency plan to respond to crisis caused by natural disasters or disease outbreak.
Following the risk communication strategy, WHO will undertake studies to gather community perceptions and beliefs that affect the health and health seeking behavior of the community with due consideration to the gender, social cohesion, and accountability to affected population. WHO will develop appropriate risk communication materials and reference documents on priority areas. The needs of different population sub-groups and their feedback on interventions will be taken into consideration while formulating activities targeted at improving healthcare services.
WHO will continue to co-lead and coordinate the health sector, monitor the health needs of the target population and quality of healthcare services and ensure equity in distribution of the services. WHO will focus on strengthening information management systems and coordination structure at all levels, closely collaborating with other sectors such as WASH, nutrition and protection and CWC working group to address emerging health crisis and ensure the best possible health outcomes for the refugee and host populations.

Objectives

The overall goal of the WHO emergency response strategy is to reduce morbidity and mortality among the Rohingya population and host community in Cox’s Bazaar. The emergency response builds on existing health infrastructure, partner network, community resources and structures. The response integrates resilience building components to foster sustainable solutions to the Rohingya crisis and for the affected host community in a system strengthening manner. In support of this aim, WHO supports the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Bangladesh, the Civil Surgeon’s Office, Cox’s Bazaar, Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner’s Office (RRRC), and works in conjunction with sector partners, in particular those in the health, WASH, protection and nutrition sectors. Needs

The Rohingya crisis is one of biggest humanitarian crises of our time. Since August 2017, almost one million Rohingyas fled violence in Myanmar’s Rakhine State to find refuge in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. They arrived in Cox's Bazaar with poor health and nutrition status, augmented by the horrors of emotional trauma, violence, and forceful displacement. Over the past year, the health sector has made significant achievements including increasing childhood immunization coverage for vaccine preventable diseases including measles and cholera; meeting the sphere standards for number of health facilities to population ratios; and establishing surveillance systems to monitor and control disease outbreaks. However, critical gaps remain in the quality of healthcare services, number of health facilities that run 24/7, surgical capacity and blood transfusion services, smooth referral linkages, essential medicines and diagnostics. Immunization coverage is low in some pockets, increasing risk of vaccine preventable diseases. The rudimentary living conditions in the settlements, overcrowding and limited water hygiene and sanitation continue to pose high risk of water borne diseases. The mid-term review of the Joint Response Plan 2018 highlighted gaps in sexual and reproductive health (SRH), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSSS), non-communicable diseases (NCDs), and health care waste management system. Less than one third of deliveries in the refugee population took place in health facilities while family planning needs for the high risk and special need groups (sex workers, survivors of gender based violence and adolescents) are largely unmet. Diagnosis and management capacity for NCDs and MHPSS is limited.Cox’s Bazar is one of Bangladesh’s most vulnerable districts. Malnutrition, health status and food insecurity are at crisis level, and the poverty rate is well above Bangladesh’s national average. The refugee influx has put an additional pressure on health services and exacerbated challenges on food security and nutrition. The emergency response to the immediate needs of the refugee and host community is essential to prevent morbidity and mortality. More than half of the refugee population is children and adolescents (less than 18 years old); 52% of the population in both refugee and host community are females. A large number of refugees are still recovering from the emotional and physical trauma faced before and while fleeing from their homes including violence, rape and torture. Cultural and social barriers exist for girls and females to utilize available services on sexual and reproductive health, mental health and psychosocial support. Moreover, incidents of gender based violence continue to be reported in the camps and safety and security of the campsites is the biggest concern while accessing health facilities at night. Therefore, health interventions need to target population of all sex and age groups, paying particular attention to women, girls, adolescents, elderly and children.

Activities or outputs

WHO will focus on health systems strengthening as a part of transition from emergency response to sustained development. The underlying principles are to support national health systems to deliver an effective response, increased direct investment in public/local institutions, and measurable capacity development. The activities are as follows:• Equip/train health facilities (HFs) on NCDs diagnosis and management• Develop locally adapted strategies, guidelines, treatment protocols for quality delivery of essential health service and support PHCs to run 24/7• Monitor essential medicines availability and improve local procurement practices • Improve quality assurance of essential medicines through minilabs• Conduct Mental Health Gap Action Programme training/supportive supervision for MHPSS• Support prevention/management of neglected tropical disease and communicable disease • Equip/train HFs on safe blood transfusion• Provide training/supportive supervision on SRH• Establish gap-filling financing pool for referral costs• Maintain/improve EWARS and outbreak investigation• Train/equip national rapid response teams on field investigation, response to public health events, and outbreak preparedness/response • Train/equip HFs on IPC and provide supportive supervision• Provide technical assistance and supportive supervision on EWARS• Conduct assessment of disease burden, public health situational analysis and epidemiological analysis of disease conditions • Expand routine immunization and conduct campaigns • Establish/support fixed vaccination points, mobile vaccination teams/vaccination posts at entry and transit points • Conduct VPD surveillance• Conduct entomological survey for mosquito fauna and support interventions based on recommendations • Conduct water quality surveillance in refugee settlements, HFs and Diarrhea treatment centers• Conduct Water and sanitation for health facility improvement tool survey and comprehensive WASH assessment of district/upazila health complexes• Conduct comprehensive assessment of HF waste management • Train/equip HFs on WASH/medical waste management and provide supportive supervision• Provide support to field laboratory• Conduct field laboratory services assessments and expand services in Upazila health complexes • Provide training/supportive supervision on good lab practices, tests/equipment use, biosafety and reporting • Forecast, procure and distribute essential medicines/supplies to fill critical gaps in service delivery• Assess/improve storage capacity and temperature controlled environment in HFs• Install/maintain temperature controlled container in the camp to store sensitive medicines• Procure medical supplies for Civil Surgeon, District and Upazila Hospitals• Conduct risk communication campaigns for VPD, safe blood transfusion, vector borne diseases, NCDs, MHPSS, etc.• Develop/produce IEC materials/reference resources in relevant languages• Conduct focus group discussions with target population on how they experience health/health seeking behaviors/services• Engage with communities to promote health seeking behaviors and HF deliveries• Coordinate health sector for humanitarian response • Conduct sector coordination monitoring review • Engage in health policy/advocacy discussions• Establish functional system to monitor quality of services • Conduct assessments/operational research as per information gaps• Support health sector with information management• Hold regular meetings and coordinate with sector counterparts on common issues • Undertake joint field visits and/or monitoring assessments Data on disease surveillance will be segregated by age/sex to tailor response to specific groups. Community engagement activities will pay special attention to women, girls, adolescents, elderly and children and include feedback and satisfaction survey on available services. Based on analyzed data, specific actions will be targeted to appropriate sub-groups.
