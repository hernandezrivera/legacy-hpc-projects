Project Summary

Through the Humanitarian Response Plan Project (HRP) 2025, the World Health Organization aims to increase access to quality lifesaving nutrition services for children with acute wasting and medical complications, significantly reducing mortality due to malnutrition in children under 5. To achieve this, the WHO strategy will focus on providing integrated nutrition, health and WASH services targeting 10% (45,380) of children under five suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition who are likely to develop medical complications.

WHO’s HRP project interventions will prioritize provision of PAEDS/SAM kits to nutrition partners supporting stabilization centers (SCs) and other treatment centers for management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications (SAM-MC), and improving the quality of service provision in these centers by conducting a baseline and endline survey, jointly with the WASH sector, on quality of care provided and infection prevention and control and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH/IPC) standards of all SC facilities. The outcomes of these assessments will inform targeted training, supportive supervision and advocacy activities aimed to address the gaps identified, for example by conducting capacity building of nutrition partners on improved inpatient management for severe wasting with medical complications or advocacy with the WASH cluster for improved WASH/IPC. Additionally, the project will support improved data management and reporting for the SCs and the Nutrition Cluster.
Jointly with WASH cluster actors the project will also support improvement of water quality for safe preparation of F75 and F100 in the SCs through provision of water candle filters. Finally, the project will facilitate integration of the remaining stabilization centers in health facility services delivery. Through technical support to health sector partners, including the Ministry of Health, the project will support the development / updating of nutrition-related training materials and guidelines, the training of health and nutrition staff, and provide guidance for the updating of strategy papers related to nutrition in emergencies.
The project thus addresses all four of the Nutrition Cluster's Strategic Objectives for the 2025 HNRP, with the major focus on SO2.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, children and women continue to suffer from acute malnutrition driven by high food insecurity, poverty, inadequate water and hygiene infrastructure, poor child feeding and caring practices, high child morbidity associated with diarrhea, malaria, and upper respiratory infections, and the effects of gender-based violence on households. Complicating the existing high levels of food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions exacerbate household nutrition conditions as over 50% of the population lacks access to safe water or sanitation. The deteriorating economy in South Sudan due to hyperinflation, corruption, extensive flooding during 2024 affecting crops and livelihoods, and other deteriorating macro-economic conditions does not bode well for food security in South Sudan for 2025. South Sudan remains among the poorest countries in the world with 85% of its working population surviving on non-wage agriculture and livestock; four out of five people subsist below the poverty line ($1.90) a day. The government's ability to provide basic services is low and inconsistent across the country due to years of under- investment, climate variability, and conflict, resulting in high dependency on humanitarian aid. There is a general deficiency in service level availability across all sectors due to poor performance of the economy characterized by low production and limited access to essential goods and services, hyperinflation, and erratic market prices for essential goods and services contributing to high protection risk and low quality of life (UNDP 2020). In 2025, approximately 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, are expected to need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 1.1 million undernourished pregnant and lactating mothers (PLM) requiring urgent treatment (IPC-AMN 2024). In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. By mid-2025, eight out of ten children under five are anticipated to be at risk of acute malnutrition, with 7.69 million people, including 1.5 million young children, facing severe food insecurity. In response to this, the South Sudan Nutrition cluster's response strategy aims to provide timely, quality treatment for 0.45 million children with acute malnutrition out of the PIN of 2 million severe or moderate cases. It is estimated that up to 10% of SAM cases will likely develop medical complications, reflecting a target of 53,000. Treatment will also be provided for a targeted 0.8 million pregnant and breastfeeding women. Preventive interventions will reach 2 million children aged 6-59 months, 2 million pregnant and breastfeeding women, and adolescent girls. Nutrition interventions will follow a multi-sectoral approach, coordinating with WASH, Health, FSL, Protection, and Education clusters.

2. Project activities

The World Health Organization seeks to support the implementation of the HNRP 2025 by increasing access to quality lifesaving nutrition services for children with acute wasting and limiting the potential mortality associated with the high prevalence of malnutrition in South Sudan. To achieve this, the WHO strategy will focus on providing integrated nutrition, health and WASH services targeting 10% of children under five suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition who are likely to develop medical complications (target: 49,379). The project activities will include the following: Under the Nutrition Cluster's Logical Framework approach, the WHO team will support the following: Sectoral Objective 1, 1. Support the development/updating of training materials for health staff on MIYCN/prevention of malnutrition2. Provide technical support to the health sector in ensuring micronutrient supplementation is available and administered according to protocol Sectoral Objective 23. Support the health sector in promoting and ensuring screening and referral services are in place in health facilities, and meet minimum standards4. Provide core pipeline supply of SAM-MC kits and related materials to nutrition partners supporting Stabilization Centers and other treatment centers for management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications (SAM-MC) across the country (92 facilities in 79 counties, with the option to support expansion if required).5. Jointly with the WASH cluster, conduct a baseline and endline quality of care and IPC/WASH assessments of the stabilization centers to inform priority actions for quality improvement in the SCs. 6. Print and distribute reporting tools (daily, monthly & Critical care pathways) to the stabilization centers7. Provision of water candle filters to ensure adequate water quality for the preparation of therapeutic food (F75 &F100). This is budgeted for in the Nutrition Cluster HNRP activities (as it is in line with NC indicators) but implementation will be supported by the WASH cluster partners.8. Conduct refresher training on the inpatient management of SAM-MC in locations where the assessment indicated that care was below standard 9. Conduct supportive supervision and on-the-job mentorship to improve the quality of care in stabilization centers 10. Support advocacy efforts and negotiate with health authorities and partners to facilitate integration of stabilization centers and other nutrition centers into routine health facility services delivery where possible. Sectoral Objective 311. Conduct training of data clerks and managers on data collection and reporting on stabilization center indicators and MUAC screening data to improve nutrition information on DHIS2Activity 5 also contributes to this sectoral objective. Sectoral Objective 412. Through WHO's support to the MoH and the Health Cluster, Health sector actors will be supported to ensure activities addressing the management of diarrhoea are being implemented.Additional activities supporting this objective have been integrated into activities under Sectoral Objectives 1 to 3
