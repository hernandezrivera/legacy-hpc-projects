Project Summary

The project has two main components:
1) Strengthening of WASH services for vulnerable households in North Gaza. This intervention is in line with WASH Cluster objective 2 “to ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza.
2) Flood mitigation interventions at community infrastructures level in Gaza. This activity is in line with Objective 3 “to strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks”.
In North Gaza the project will be implemented in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA). The household component of the project aims to improve the hygiene condition of the vulnerable families through rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities including improving the water storage capacity for 240 HHs (Alsyafa, Al Amal, Al shayma ,Al Salateen and Izbit biet Hanoun), to improve the HHs hygiene knowledge and practices and with a distribution of family hygiene kits. Moreover, SC partner will distribute 240 family hygiene kits to the target families with the most vulnerable girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. SC in coordination with the partner will develop hygiene awareness messages in coordination with the WASH cluster. The project partner will deliver the awareness messages through home visits in parallel with the distribution of the hygiene kits.
The project will select the beneficiaries in Gaza based on their vulnerability through socioeconomic and protection assessment conducted through home visits. Beneficiary selection will prioritize vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, HHs with malnourished children and HHs with dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, injury or disability, children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services.
SC will consult the Community Protection Committees (CPCs) and youth group in the selection of the beneficiaries and the planned working modalities with the targeted HHs.
To mitigate the flooding risk in critical flooding areas, SC already coordinated with Costal Municipality Water Utility (CMWU) and PWA to identify the needs of the required intervention. SC will prepare the engineering design in close coordination with CMWU for the construction of new storm water pipeline in Beit Hanoun to install 550 m of storm water pipeline and rehabilitate the infiltration basin in Mashro’ Biet lahia. The intervention will help approximately 10,000 people in the most vulnerable group to enhance their ability to survive, prevent or alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity.
To ensure full integration with other WASH actors, SCI will closely coordinate with the WASH cluster and Hygiene Working Group in designing the awareness messages focusing on hygiene and flooding management at the HHs level to strengthen their mitigation abilities to these kinds of risks.
To ensure no-harm, SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members. The overall project implementation will be adapted to the recent COVID-19 national and international guidelines to minimize the risk of transmission. SCI commits to Do No Harm through ensuring all areas of SCI’s work are designed and resourced appropriately to minimise the opportunities for perpetrators to act exploitatively and to maximise the safety of those engaging with our programming and otherwise involved with SCI.

1 - Needs

More than 50% of land in the Gaza Strip is residential due to high population densities, where overcrowding and land scarcity skew the pattern of urban development towards housing and away from basic public infrastructure. Due to the absence of storm water network in most of Gaza residential areas, communities used the sewage network to draining the rain water, putting heavy load on the networks and the sewage pumping stations. In many cases this pressure caused breakages in sewer pipes, causing storm water mixing with sewage water that later on over-flooded residential areas and sewage pumping stations. According to HNO -2021 “almost 118,000 Palestinians, two-thirds in Gaza and one-third in the West Bank, reside in low-lying areas at risk of flooding events during winter due to heavy rains, as well as overflow of storm water facilities and sewage pumping stations which increase exposure to health risk”.According the results of recent vulnerability assessment conducted by WASH Cluster in Gaza, the center of Beit Hanoun and Mashou’ Biet Lahia has been identified as flooding critical areas . Biet Haoun center has been affected repeatedly by storm water floods, more than 1500 people were their homes are directly affected by the flood, while the flood caused restriction to the movement of 5000 persons. In addition, the flood affects their access to the only hospital in the town, and access to schools in the area. The low efficiency of infiltration basin in Mashrou’ Biet lahia causes repeated storm water flooding and affects the live and movements for 5000 peoples living there. The municipality constructed new temporary and unsafe storm water pools to mitigate the flooding risks. These pools are not safe and at risk of collapse in the event of heavy rainfall. In addition, the low HHs income areas together with the shortage in water leads to poor hygienic conditions of the HHs and increased their vulnerability to health risk. In 2020, SCI partner Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA) conducted 650 home visits in deferent areas in north Gaza to update the assessment of HHs hygiene condition and the health impact on the children. The results show that visited families suffer from inadequate WASH facilities inside their homes, increased parasites, and diarrhea cases among the children. Malnutrition is strongly linked to recurrent infections such as gastroenteritis and respiratory tract infections. As a result, there is a clear link between poor WASH conditions (e.g. quantity and quality of drinking water availability, presence of waste disposal facilities) and malnutrition. It is vital to ensure that all health and nutrition interventions integrate key elements of water, sanitation and hygiene (e.g. promotion of handwashing, provision of soap and latrines). The result of home visits to 268 malnourished children in north Gaza conducted by SCI health partner Ard el-Insan showed that only 6% of their homes have adequate sanitation facilities.

2 - Activities

The project is designed to be in line with WASH Cluster objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank. SCI, in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA), will work to improve the hygiene condition of the vulnerable families through rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities for 240 HHs in north Gaza ( Alsyafa ,Al Amal ,Al shayma , Al Salateen and izbit biet Hanoun), improve the HHs hygiene knowledge and practices and distribution of family hygiene kits. The project will also respond to WASH Cluster objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks. The project will contribute in eliminate the flooding risk on approximately 10000 people in Beit Hanoun and Biet Lahia, SCI will work in coordination with CMWU and PWA to improve the storm water drainage capacity in two Flood prone areas. Key activities will include:1) Rehabilitation/upgrading the HH’s sanitation facilities in 240 homes. SCI and implementing partner will select HHs in need, conduct technical assessment and provision of rehabilitation and upgrading of target HHs sanitation facilities. This includes maintenance of toilets, kitchens, provision of HHs water tanks and HH water and sewage connections. SCI partner will select the contractor to implement the rehabilitation work.2) SCI partner will distribute 480 family hygiene kits to the target families and distribute the hygiene promotion messages. SCI and partner will conduct the hygiene promotion activities will include home visits and delivering awareness messages to HHs with poor hygiene situation and HHs at risk of flooding.3) SCI will coordinate with Costal Municipality Water Utility (CMWU) and PWA on the engineering design for the construction of new storm water pipeline in Beit Hanoun. Through a public bid, SC will select a contractor to install 550 m of storm water pipeline. The intervention will help the most vulnerable group to enhance their ability to survive, prevent or alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity. The interventions will be carried out in partnership with local implementing partner BLDA.4) SCI will coordinate with CMWU to rehabilitate Mashrou’ Biet lahia infiltration basin, the work includes removing sludge and increasing the volume of basin by 12,000 m3 and support the basin walls. The project will select the beneficiaries based on their vulnerability through socioeconomic and protection assessment, and verification of home visits. Beneficiary selection will prioritize vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, HHs with malnourished children and HHs with dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, injury or disability, children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services. Implementing partner will conduct a pre-intervention survey to identify health and hygiene needs to develop the intervention plan with community-based organizations and active community members. BLDA will select the contractor to implement the rehabilitation work. It will also procure and distribute hygiene kits to households. In integration with other SCI programming, the local partners, and in coordination with SCI, will coordinate the intervention with the two existing Community Protection Committees (CPC), composed of 50% female members, and CBOs. The CPCs and CBOs will facilitate coordination with the community, and facilitate raising the target communities concerns and urgent needs. To ensure full integration with other WASH actors, SCI will closely coordinate with the WASH cluster and Hygiene Working Group in designing the awareness messages. SCI will coordinate with CMWU and PWA to construct the new storm water.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SCI will ensure “Do-no-harm” through our project intervention in terms of mitigation of potential social tensions in communities resulting from community members feeling excluded. Interventions are based on needs assessments and transparent selection criteria. Appropriate targeting of beneficiaries will be the result of quality assessment of design and planning processes, coordinated through regular meetings with the partners and communities during the implementation.In 2018 SC conducted a “Do No Harm” assessment in the targeted areas and will use the recommendations while working in the targeted communities. Furthermore, the 2019 SCI “Child-Safeguarding in WASH” policy will guide the WASH interventions of Save the Children ensuring that not children is harm during any WASH intervention. Beneficiaries, staffs, contractors, and any other stakeholder will be protected by complying with the policy. To ensure no-harm, SCI will conduct strong community engagement through the already established linkages with the Community Protection Committees and youth groups to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits and cluster consultation. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SCI is partnering with Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA) and coordinate with Ared El Insan (AEI) (Health partner) for the selection of the most vulnerable households specially with malnutrition problems. To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. The selection criteria will be clearly explained to the communities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks. Additionally the sanitary units at school level will be built and rehabilitated taking into consideration gender segregation and improved access for people with disabilities.

The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse of children is dealt with under our Child Safeguarding policy. This policy is specifically concerned with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) of adults. This includes direct or indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in the wider communities in which we work. For anyone under 18, refer to the Child Safeguarding Policy. For issues in relation to staff refer to the Global Anti - Harassment policy which covers sexual harassment, SEA and bullying of SCI staff. The PSEA policy contains essential principles and rules that reflect the behaviours and standards expected from employees, trustees, volunteers, interns, trainees, consultants, partners, and other representatives working for, or contracted on behalf of SCI in any capacity and covers our international development and humanitarian work.SCI is committed to reducing the opportunities for abuse and exploitation to happen through effective prevention, ensuring that survivors are not exposed to further harm through good reporting and responses, and ensuring that survivors receive appropriate care. Effective prevention and providing safe responses requires all staff, volunteers, interns, partners and other representatives to be clear about their individual accountabilities and responsibilities. It also requires all senior staff and managers to ensure that their working environments minimise the opportunities for sexual exploitation and abuse, and to ensure that reporting and response do not expose survivors or reporters to any further harm. Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is a gross breach of both standards of aid, and of the rights of those in need23. The PSEA policy applies 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and 365 days a year. Any breach of the policy will be taken extremely seriously and responded to. Breaches of the policy may be grounds for termination of engagement with SCI. SCI takes the position that early reporting of any concern supports both prevention of further abuse and safe response through the hotline available 24/7 (1800-724-726). There must be increased awareness about reporting, including the importance of reporting of any and all concerns, however apparently minor. SCI’s commitment to Zero Tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse means all programme design and planning must include a gender power analysis, which also looks at the roles and structure of SCI staff, volunteers, interns and partners and a safeguarding risk assessment SC has a PSEA Focal Point at the country level and the field sites, who is responsible for driving awareness of the policy and on how to report, inviting, receiving and collating all causes for concern. When these collected pieces of information generate a picture of a site, or a programme, or a particular person, the PSEA Focal Point must escalate the matter to trigger further inquiry and/ or investigation.
