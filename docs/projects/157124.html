Project Summary

WHO 2019 Emergency program aligns and respond to its main functions and commitments for humanitarian response. Through the well-established 12 central and field offices will continue to ensure jointly with MOH the coordination and leadership of the humanitarian response of international and national stakeholders. Technical and operational support will be provided by the WHO to strengthen the SMOHs coordination and emergency response teams up to community level. The collection, centralization, analysis and dissemination of essential health data dis-aggregated by sex and age-groups will be maintained and strengthen by WHO field teams jointly with all partners. Sectorial and inter agency assessments will complement the information for evidence-based needs and gap identification, prioritization, planning, task distribution and monitoring of life-saving humanitarian actions. WHO technical and cluster coordination team will ensure that the standard health cluster tools, such as EWARS, HERAM, 4Ws, Risk and vulnerability mapping, HNO, HRP, epidemiological briefings, bulletin are timely and regularly updated and utilized by health partners. The focus of WHO and partners will be the district and community level structures contributing and aligned with the national health strategies, adherence to national and international standards for an equitable, and quality emergency health services.
The projects significantly contributes to increase the access to, and the quality of emergency health services for the most vulnerable, IDPs, Returnees, and host communities in 69 localities (11 states) as prioritized by the 2019 HNO exercise through; procurement and distribution of free of charge medicines to support the NGOs and SMOH deal with humanitarian case load at all levels, while ensuring a rapidly revolving pipeline for addressing acute events in 2019. Rapid Response Kits adapted to the protracted crisis in Sudan address specific needs of children (boys and, girls), women in general and women of reproductive age, men and elderly. In addition medicines for case-management and lab regent for confirmation during outbreaks, and PEP for rape management. Training of health workers directly related to humanitarian action(Universal Precautions, case management, IMCI, EnNOC, RRT, surveillance/EWARS), small repair and rehab of damaged HFs; operational costs for scaling up the humanitarian health services as necessary, and bridging for short periods funding shortages in essential areas (rapid surge of health partners), and community mobilization and healthy behavior. An integrated approach that facilitates the access to services of all age groups, male and female with their specific health needs and adapted to the local cultural customs, including advocacy and synergic linkages with ongoing recovery-development interventions (GBV, FGM, Protection, FSL, WASH, Nutrition, NFI, and Education)
Optimal capacity for early notification, investigation, confirmation and immediate response to public health threats (outbreaks) is another priority of WHO emergency preparedness and response significantly contributing to the health security of targeted groups and beyond through; technical, and operational support for EWARS, alert investigation and response operations by the RRTs, response vaccination campaigns and strengthening the routine immunization, reagents and medicines to fill in the gaps during eventual outbreaks. Well established linkages with the development initiatives such as IHR, Cross Border collaboration, event-based community surveillance, and real time EWARs are complementary and aim to a stronger preparedness and quick recovery. The target for the project is 2,44 million vulnerable communities – IDPs, Returnees, Host Communities in 11 states and 69 localities. The activities related to outbreak control are not restricted only to the 69 targeted localities, but wherever the needs arise.

Needs

According to Health Facility Survey (FMOH 2017), 1,274 (22%) out of 5,790 PHC facilities are not fully functional either due to staff shortages (49%), poor physical infrastructure (43%) and lack of medical equipment (8%) . More than half (51%) are without water supply, and 41% without latrines increasing the health threats for patients, saff and communities. The HeRAMS Jan 2019 attested that 295 (24%) out of 1,229 HFs in five Darfur States were closed in 2018 lacking funding and human resources. Of the functional HFs in Darfur, only 458 (49%) offer the minimum PHC package, with 32% (299) run by NGOs, resticting the access to services for the most vulnerable, women and children. In 2019 the need for external humanitarian health support will increase due to the aggravation of medicines shortages. Medicines importation declined by more than30 % and humanitarian community will have to fill in the gap. Only 34% of HFs in Darfur region provide BEmONC leaving the vast majority of women with no or limited access to life-saving services. The national measles immunization coverage in 2018 is 88 % (lower than 2017 – 90%), with SK 63%, ND 73%, SD 78%, CD 81% and WK 82%) well below optimal coverage (95%) creating massive risk for severe outbreaks. Information is largely missing on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), and strengthening these services is an urgent priority . Currently only 134 HFs are providing SAM in-patient care across the country for 26,000 children, reaching only 55% of HRP target for 2018. Establishment of any stabilization centers can be done only in a fully Functional HF. In 2018, 15 large scale epidemiological events, along with 184 alerts of smaller outbreaks in in all 18 states including measles, chikungunya, AWD, malaria, dengue, dysentery, typhoid, Schistosomiasis, RVF (suspected), rubella etc. Measles outbreak affected all 18 states with 4,825 cases (2018), seven folds higher than 2017. A large chikungunya outbreak hit mainly Kassala, then Red Sea state with more than 40,000 cases to date. The first wave of nationwide AWD outbreak ended in March 2018, and was followed by a 2nd wave totaling 691 cases and 6 deaths in 2018. Malaria infection continues to be endemic and considered the major public health problem in terms of caseload, with more than 1.7 million cases (224 deaths) reported from seven states (Khartoum, ND, Northern, River Nile, White Nile, ED and SD). Dengue is another endemic disease in Sudan and last year 1,004 cases reported from 13 localities of 9 states. There are only 1,659 (27% of HFs) sentinel sites for disease surveillance, and only 240 (26%) %) HFs as EWARS sites, and physical access remains a significant challenge in SK, CD and BN states. In 2019 health partners identified 3,6 million PIN, and 2,4 million vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities are the target for 2018 humanitarian response.

1.1. Coordination and leadership; WHO cluster coordination teams (11 states) and subsidies/incentives for 18 SMOH emergency health coordinators. HeRAMS, 4 Ws matrix, State’s vulnerability Profile, Cluster bulletin, updates regularly. Inter-cluster coordination, and inter-agency needs assessment promoting complementarities. Planning and Resource mobilization; HRP, HNO, CERF, SHF allocation strategy, and proposals. Monitoring based on agreed indicators and framework. 1.2. Access to equitable, affordable integrated PHC and referral services; Repairs, including water, and sanitation, hand washing facilities and biological waste disposal for environmental protection. Provision of equipment for HFs . Medicines and supplies for NGOs partners and MOH, the case-management of outbreaks, and pre-positioning of surge stock. Sub-contracts to NGOs and SMOH running HFs. Capacity building of NGOs, and MOH clinical staff on PHC, Maternal and reproductive health services; female qualified medical staff and specific supplies; private space for women; referral system; PEP kits in coordination with UNFPA, UNHCR and IOM. 1.3 Prevention and control of outbreaks; Expand EWARS to cover all targeted communities including cross border and surge during unexpected events. Co-lead with MOH Response Task Forces at all levels for planning and monitoring. Laboratory support including confirmation in WHO certified labs; international notification as per IHR. Response to outbreaks vaccination campaigns. Joint alert investigation and response with MOH through operational and technical support. RRT; training, technical support, and operational costs for outreach activities Community awareness campaigns and event based surveillance 1.4. Transition and resilience System capacity building for EPR; MOH EOC at state level, EPR plan, implementation of protracted IDP strategy linked to Health Insurance, real time EWARS, UHC inclusive of refugees, and district health system strengthening.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The prioritization of vulnerable communities and geographical location are the most affected by the climate changes; drought and floods with consequent food insecurity and high acute malnutrition levels. Climate change also influenced the pattern of the outbreaks of vector and water borne diseases that indeed increased over the past few years with re-emerging and expansion to new geographical areas of Sudan of Dengue Fever, Chikungunya, Rift Valley and AWD Fever (suspected). There is sufficient evidence that climate changes also influence the transmission of measles. About 40% of the project inputs are factored in for the prevention, early identification, confirmation and response to outbreaks of water and vector borne diseases as well as water related diseases. Any health service delivery involves working with sick people, some of them suffering of communicable diseases with epidemic potential. Without implementation of Universal Precaution, aggressive pathogens (microbes, Viruses, protozoa, fungus, etc) and medicines, sharp medical tools, disinfectants, lab reagents etc would contaminate/affect staff, other patients and environment outside the clinics creating severe risks for population living in the areas. The training program of WHO includes modules (initial training and re-fresher) on Universal Precautions that includes Infection Prevention, Injection safety, Solid and liquid medical waste safe disposal for all levels of care. The national guidelines are distributed to all participants to the training, and to the SMOH so all HFs in targeted area are covered. All WHO medical kits include barrier protection equipment, and disinfectants that are biodegradable. In addition, the WHO/MOH monitoring and supervision check list includes indicators for Universal Precaution, and the repairs of any HF takes into account the standards for environmental protection through correct medical waste disposal (pits for sharps and placenta, incinerators, fence, septic tanks, etc. The disposal of any expired medicines is done according with WHO/National Guidelines by incineration in the presence of Health Authorities.
