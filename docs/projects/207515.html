Project Summary

Under the Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector (LSDS), BRAC has been working in Cox’s Bazar since October 2017 to ensure food security and self-reliance of the refugees and host communities affected by the Rohingya influx. BRAC will implement projects under this sector to continue its work in attaining the sector’s objectives along with BRAC’s strategic objectives of localisation, humanitarian development nexus, resilience building of the affected Rohingya and host communities and strengthening coordination among the humanitarian actors and stakeholders.
This project will assist refugees in acquiring transferable and portable skills relevant to the labour market needs in Myanmar and the camp contexts in Bangladesh. While this initiative enables refugees to maintain hope for the future and have dignified and productive lives in the camps, it will also support sustainable return and integration (in Myanmar) when conditions permit. It includes their engagement in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, minor livelihood activities, and community works, with the utilisation of acquired competencies for livelihood options. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees for durable solutions.
BRAC plans to provide support for skills and livelihood activities in 12 refugee camps including existing camps no. 2E, 3, 4, 4Ext, 5, 6, 7, 14, 15, and proposed camps no. 8W, 17, 20; and existing 3 host community unions of Ukhiya sub-districts: Raja Palong, Haldia Palong, and Ratna Palong.
The project will target 2,650 individuals in which 500 from Rohingya camps for vocational training and 2,150 (HC-730 & RC-1420) for non-formal technical training. The participants of this intervention will be youths and adults, including men and women, others, and persons with disabilities. A total of 1,150 non-formal training participants (HC-730 & RC-420) will be linked with the Ayesha Abed Foundation’s production center as wage earners i.e. hand embroidery, tailoring, block-print, screen-print, dying, machine embroidery, etc.
The project will create self-employment opportunities for the 800 skills-training and non-formal training graduates by establishing new training-cum-production center and Rohingya market. The production of geo-bags, female hygiene materials, and jute bags for distribution in the camps will also be facilitated through production activities on a payment basis. Besides, the project will also facilitate volunteer engagement for 250 vocational training graduates to other sectors.
Based on the identified needs of skill trades, the project would provide center-based vocational training in different trades, including sewing machine operation, community health worker, and agricultural crop production. The training module on the above trades is developed aligning with Myanmar National Qualification Framework (MNQF) and ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework (AQRF) standards. Each of the vocational training courses will be 360 hours in duration. The project will arrange a formal assessment of the training participants, where successful candidates will receive a certification to help them pursue their future education/training. It will also assist them in uplifting to the upper skills category in Rohingya camps and Myanmar as a skilled professional. The project will provide 120 hours of non-formal technical training in camps and host communities on different technical issues, production of jute products, repairing gas stoves and solar panels, tailoring, weaving, knitting, repairing household items, etc. These trainings will contribute to integrating disaster resilient and green entrepreneurship and livelihoods options for vocational skills development activities to improve the resilience of the project participants from external shocks, minimise environmental degradation and increase food security and income.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to J-MSNA, 18% of households in Rohingya refugee camps were unable to access to employment, where 42% of households in host communities were unable to access income-generating activities. Besides, 3% of the households in refugee camps and 6% of households in host communities could not afford access to conditional cash (cash for work) compared to 2022. On the other hand, 71% of households in refugee camps and 36% in host communities reported barriers to income opportunities. 53% of households in refugee camps and 29% of households in host communities faced obstacles, and 27% of households in refugee camps had to wait a long time for volunteer engagement and cash for work in camps. 6% of households in refugee camps and 1% of households in host communities faced obstacles due to lack of opportunity for women (e.g. no other female staff). According to the Joint Government of Bangladesh-UNHCR Population Factsheet, the total number of Rohingya in August 2017 was 767,324 individuals (154,264 households) and 965,467 individuals (200,560 households) as of September 2023. These additional populations have a social, natural, environmental, legal, and financial impact on Bangladesh. The skills and livelihood development support for these Rohingya people mostly depends on the humanitarian assistance of the Bangladesh government, donor organizations, UN agencies, and national and international NGOs.A need assessment conducted by UCEP/UNHCR in 2022 showed that 76% of the Rohingya Youth have not received any skills training since they arrived in the camps. The trade for skills training under this project will be selected considering and assessing the UN/GoB framework, the demand by the Rohingya youths, the portability of skills to Myanmar, the needs as well as the possibility for volunteer engagement and market linkage within the camps. Moreover, as the experience of ongoing projects shows, there is not only a huge need and demand to offer vocational training but also to offer non-formal technical training to project participants of host and Rohingya communities on different issues.Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally have no regular income opportunities and lack the skills that would allow them to adapt quickly to bring change to their situation. Besides, half of the refugee workforce was not engaged in any type of income-generating activity. Therefore, offering skills development opportunities in different trades that can be utilised in the camps and following possible repatriation as well as creating income opportunities, is key to improving livelihoods and self-reliance. Scaling up vocational and skills training, socio-economic empowerment, and self-reliance activities with special attention to uneducated and unskilled persons, youth, and women in the camps and host communities is critical to enhancing access to economic opportunities. To ensure equal opportunity for women and Persons with Disabilities and increase their empowerment, BRAC will take a wide range of activities focusing on women both in the camps and host communities. Persons with Disabilities will also be included to enable them to contribute to community and family so that they can be considered as resources rather than burdens for their families.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Livelihood and Skills Development Sector (LSDS) has already begun the rationalisation process as part of the initiative of ISCG. To comply with this process, LSDS has been adopting an integration approach to foster livelihood and skills development initiatives with the multi-sectoral camp-based activities. The integrated approach will ensure the optimisation of resources of LSDS by reducing duplication and integrated service delivery modalities, which is already initiated across the BRAC's operated programmes. BRAC will work in 14 camps which they already received through the rationalisation process of ISCG. In this regard, BRAC will adopt integrated skills and livelihood options, including home-based and commercial agriculture, community health workers, sewing machine operations, solar maintenance, etc. BRAC will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach with other partners and stakeholders to enhance the livelihoods and resilience of the vulnerable host community, promote self-reliance of Rohingya refugees and strengthen sustainable natural resource management for both communities. The activities of this project are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy and through the guidance received from the relevant Sector and/or Working Group and coordination meetings. This project will regularly share the 5W reports with the sector, which will help them to understand the gaps within the sector. Furthermore, BRAC has a strong relationship with the govt. stakeholders to obtain the relevant approvals on time. BRAC has a strong fundraising team and donor mapping strategy to work for funding opportunities, which will be utilised to continue the service for BRAC’s LSDS. Moreover, BRAC will prioritise localisation by developing the capacity of local Community-Based Organisations (CBOs), youth clubs, Local Government Institutions (LGIs), and other government stakeholders and engaging them in the implementation mechanisms.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Livelihood and Skills Development (LSD) sector will collaborate closely with BRAC's community-based protection, legal assistance, and Gender-based Violence (GBV) sectors to ensure that appropriate services for the project participants are provided. In this initiative, GBV-related cases received within the Livelihoods and Skills Development sector will be referred to the relevant sectors (such as CBP, GBV, etc.). The project will also offer training to its livelihood and skills participants on GBV, and other protection concerns. BRAC will incorporate protection principles in the implementation of the intervention in both the host and Rohingya communities. Moreover, as a member of the PSEA network, BRAC has established policies that prioritize protection and safeguarding, with a specific focus on 'Do No Harm' to prevent any unintended adverse effects of any programme.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The program will be designed to address and respond to the needs of more vulnerable communities irrespective of gender, sex, religion, ethnicity, culture etc. All the activities in this project will be designed in persons with disability friendly manner and their inclusion in the project. The project will target including 12% persons with disabilities based on the availability of the project participants within the selected age groups for the target intervention areas. This project will also identify vulnerable LGBTQI persons of concern and will respond separately to the needs of them if they face any barrier in the program due to social stigmatization and barriers. Besides, this project will be sensitive toward local culture and practice. In this regards, all the modules and materials will be designed considering local and cultural context and practiced. The age categories of project participants’ are important to consider for Skills Development among the refugees and host communities for formal and non-formal sectors. Moreover, the project will ensure the participation of girls, and women in the skill development training through including a minimum of 70% participants as female. In addition to that participation of the members of families, persons with disabilities and LGBTQI will be prioritised. The project will have a strong team of GESI and gender mainstreaming team to ensure the participation of women and persons with disabilities. BRAC will conduct periodical community-based inclusive approaches (e.g., PRA) and gender analysis to understand and tailor to potential trades for Skills Development of the participants. Project will consider the age group of 18-59 years old.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the affected population (AAP) signifies a dedicated commitment to exercising power responsibly, recognizing the importance of providing an account to, and being held accountable by, the individuals whom humanitarian organizations aim to aid. BRAC is committed to granting project participants from the target host and Rohingya communities’ substantial influence in decision-making through inclusive and non-discriminatory structures. The project will ensure two-way dialogue for information, communication, and transparency with the community, even during crisis responses, including determining what kind of information they require and in what formats and will establish information channel. BRAC acknowledge that all relevant project participant groups have a voice, including children, men, women, the elderly, minority cultural groups, and persons with disabilities. BRAC ensure community participation during all phases including needs assessment and program planning, program implementation, distribution and service delivery, monitoring and evaluation. The Do No Harm framework or equivalent approach will be applied with the participation of stakeholders, to ensure that the operation’s humanitarian interventions will not exacerbate conflict/crisis. Besides, the project will establish a functional feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms. The project will consistently learn from its outcomes of monitoring and evaluations and improve its performance. The findings of monitoring and evaluation will be routinely fed back to communities as each of the groups’ issues will be taken and addressed accordingly. The project will collaborate with other stakeholders, such as peer agencies, sectors, partners, and local authorities, to fulfil this commitment.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC believes that staff are the foundation of the organization. They will be an example of the organizational values and principles. BRAC is committed to following all sorts of policies and guiding principles. For Safeguarding and PSEA mainstreaming, BRAC has a code of conduct document for staff and relevant personnel. BRAC strictly imposes and abides by the BRAC’s Safeguarding policy, six core principles of PSEA in a zero-tolerance approach. These issues are also reflected in all the assessment processes of BRAC. In line with the organisational strategies and policy guidelines, BRAC is committed to ensuring the archiving of a signed code of conduct from the project staff and volunteers. While conducting any recruitment for the project, the BRAC HR department always checks out staff history related to violations of PSEA and safeguarding issues. All new staff also receive induction sessions covering the BRAC code of conduct, including violations of safeguarding and PSEA policies, punishment, mandatory reporting lines and so on from the respective HR department. One dedicated Safeguarding and PSEA champion is usually nominated and selected from the project, who directly reports to the Safeguarding Manager, BRAC HCMP. This champion is primarily responsible for staff and volunteers’ capacity building, ensuring referral and linkages, ensuring adequate visibility of Safeguarding and PSEA posters, and listening to the community’s voices while conducting gender awareness sessions with them. The Safeguarding champion also closely follows up on issues that come from the project participant via Complaint Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) boxes, which are installed in the skills development centres under this project structure. Besides, all centres have UNHCR hotline number and BRAC Safeguarding and PSEA Manger contact numbers. When any issue comes through CFRM boxes, hot line or email, then it is promptly addressed as per the PSEA policy and procedures.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC already has an EOC (Emergency Operation Centre) and ERT (Emergency Response Team) structure with representatives from all the sectors to respond during emergency situations. BRAC's DRR sector provides basic Disaster Risk Management and Emergency Response training to project staff, including community volunteers, to respond effectively to an emergency crisis situation. They also provide first aid training to all staff and volunteers as a part of their skill development. Furthermore, BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which applies a science-based precautionary approach to project design and implementation. BRAC’s interventions anticipate and address any potential harm before it happens. Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, circumstances of not having scientific certainty will not be used as a reason for avoiding cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation in BRAC’s project implementation mechanism.
