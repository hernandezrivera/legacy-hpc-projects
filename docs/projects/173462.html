1 - Identified Needs

Following violence in Myanmar in August 2017, a large influx of Rohingya men, women and children fled into Bangladesh. An estimated 9,80,000 new arrivals joined over 3,10,000 existing rohingya people who had fled previous conflicts. Together with people in the host communities, this has led more than 1.2 million people in need of support. Community engagement and feedback is more challenging for the most vulnerable individuals, such as older persons, women and girls, PWD, illiterate persons, and those who can communicate only in Rohingya, given their reduced access to public spaces, smaller social networks and possible inability to complete a written complaint if required. This not only impacts their communication with humanitarian staff and other authorities, but their willingness and ability to provide feedback. The Rohingya expressed distrust and a lack of open communication between themselves and humanitarian agencies and government authorities. They also stressed their desire to play an active part in decision-making that affects their daily lives. Qualitative data collected during the COVID-19 response through the COVID-19 Explained series found that the impact of refugees not receiving adequate feedback from health facilities about when and whether problems they raised could be resolved led to frustration and dissatisfaction and negatively affected the overall perception of humanitarian actors, with refugees questioning the reliability and trustworthiness of the response.
