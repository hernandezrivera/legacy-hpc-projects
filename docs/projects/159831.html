Project Summary

Widespread acute food insecurity and malnutrition persist in many parts of Somalia with an estimated 6.3 million Somalis facing acute food insecurity through the end of 2019. The food security situation reflect the cumulative effects of below-average rains experienced across most parts of Somalia during the 2018 Deyr (October-December), followed by harsh weather conditions during the dry 2019 Jilaal (January–March) season and the delayed and poor performance of the 2019 GU (April-June). These consecutive seasonal poor performances have been exacerbated by limited recovery from the extended impacts of the severe 2016/2017 drought. An estimated 2.6 million Somalis across Somalia remain displaced primarily due to conflict, lack of livelihood opportunities and drought.
The project focuses on the delivery of basic lifesaving and protection/restoration of livelihoods of the target population as well as community resilience building activities in Bakool, Banadir, Bay, Bay, Galgaduud, Gedo and Togdheer regions with a special focus on all locations with FSC prioritized persons in Need, high prevalence of malnutrition (GAM/SAM), including IDP camps and host communities that are classified in IPC Phase 2 in the post GU 2019 projection but have been in worse phases in the recent past while those that are in IPC 3 & 4 are considered priority in terms of response.
The project is aimed at improving the ability of vulnerable HHs to meet their basic needs, supports protection and recovery of livelihood assets through life-saving UCT, animal treatment services, provision of locally preferred high quality seeds kits and training, and rehabilitation of community productive assets through CFW to support the resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks. The project targets 12,238 vulnerable drought affected pastoralists, Agro-pastoralists and IDP HHs including women, men, boys, girls, and female headed households who are classified as persons in need by the FSC.
COOPI prioritizes respect for the rights, dignity and safety of the people affected by crisis and is committed to taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the populations benefiting from the intervention. During implementation, the beneficiary women, girls and the disabled will be included in decision making processes, monitoring so as ensure that the most marginalized and vulnerable are represented and have influence. The project will be guided by the principles of DO NO HARM, ensuring safety and dignity of target population is upheld and at the same time providing for accountability, participation and empowerment.
In summary the following activities will be carried out:
1. Provide unconditional cash transfer to 3,616 HHs that are faced with severe levels of food insecurity (crisis and emergency) with the aim of increasing immediate access to food for 3 months.
2. Provision of locally preferred high quality seed kits, trainings and fuel for irrigation for 1,229 vulnerable female and male headed vulnerable riverine farmers in Gedo region.
3. Provision of locally preferred high quality drought tolerant seeds kit and trainings for 2,584 female and male headed vulnerable agro-pastoral farmers in Bay region
4. Emergency livestock treatment (treatment and deworming) campaigns for animals belonging to 2,909 HHs will be dewormed and treated against endemic &prevalent diseases.
5. Rehabilitation of community productive assets through CFW targeting 1,900 vulnerable HHs.

Project Activities

OBJECTIVE 1 ACT1: Provide unconditional cash transfer to 3,616 HHs that are faced with severe levels of food insecurity (crisis and emergency) with the aim of increasing immediate access to food for 3 months. These groups will be consisting of vulnerable IDPs and drought affected rural communities with a special focus on marginalized and minority groups OBJECTIVE 2 ACT1: Provision of locally preferred high quality seed kits (locally available and nutritionally dense vegetables), trainings and fuel for irrigation for 1,229 most vulnerable female and male headed vulnerable riverine farmers in Gedo region. ACT 2: Provision of locally preferred high quality seeds kit (drought tolerant) and trainings for 2,584 female and male headed vulnerable agro-pastoral farmers in Bay region ACT 3: Emergency livestock treatment (treatment and deworming) campaigns for animals belonging to 2,909 HHs will be dewormed and treated against endemic &prevalent diseases. OBJECTIVE 3 ACT 1: Rehabilitation of community productive assets through CFW targeting 1,900 vulnerable HHs. Local community assets (farmland, irrigation canals, and feeder roads) destroyed by the floods and water catchments in water deficit areas will be selected and rehabilitated through cash for work approach prioritizing vulnerable HH and minority groups. Women will be involved in selection & design. HHs that cannot work or are labour deficit will be provided with unconditional cash grants.

Mitigation Measures

Lens 1: 1b• COOPI will support initiatives that promote self-reliance and access to basic services, such as livelihood inputs, rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets as well as disaster preparedness, that can help reduce the need to seek such support elsewhere. • The proposed intervention is intended to not only alleviate the target communities of the impacts of shocks through short-term cash transfers, but also to build their resilience while at the same time providing increased livelihood sources and strategies as well as access to productive assets for beneficiaries. This is expected to reduce instances of displacement in search of livelihood opportunities.• COOPI will build the capacities of target communities in early warning early action and contingency planning in order to be prepared in the event of shocks so as to protect their livelihood assets and resilience gains.• Shortage of water was attributed to displacements from rural areas. Through the project, COOPI proposes to rehabilitate water sources to improve water availability during dry spells.• COOPI will engage with influential stakeholders, such as regional or international organizations or relevant government ministries/local authorities, to encourage and support their involvement to minimize the risks of displacement in target districts/villages.Lens 2: 2a. • Providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria and COOPI targeting procedures. • Physical verification exercises will be conducted by the project staff to ascertain the weaker members of the target beneficiaries are not excluded. • The project staff and village committees will be selected to represent all groups within the community including members from marginalized clans. • The project staff and members will be trained on protection mainstreaming while all COOPI staff signed and adhere to COOPI’s Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect the humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. • COOPI has an established feedback and complaint mechanism where the beneficiaries can call to air their grievances and their issues responded to. All the target beneficiaries will be informed of the complaint mechanism and the telephone numbers through sensitization and banners posted at the project locations.Lens 2: 2b. • To ensure that women are empowered to influence decisions and outcomes, the project will ensure at least 30% representation of women in all the local institutions.
