Project Summary

The project will focus on provision of children’s education that will develop each child’s personality, talents and abilities to the fullest. It will encourage children to respect others, protect the environment, human rights, understand their own rights and other cultures that will lead to peaceful living and co-existence among themselves irrespective of religion, gender, ethnicity and educational backgrounds.
Through ADRA’s Education experts; the project will focus her intervention in the hard to reach but secure communities of General hospital, Central primary, Hausari Camp, Abori Camp, SSS QTRs camp and Old Secretariat camp Ward in Damboa LGA in Borno state. Adventist Development and Relief Agency (ADRA) will carry out a massive “GO BACK TO SCHOOL” campaign through advocacy to traditional rulers, religious leaders, local government authorities, SUBEC and state ministry of education to facilitate scale up of school enrollment, especially for the marginalized girl child in and around the IDPs camps in Damboa. In order to scale up education interventions in Damboa LGA, the project will give priority to activities that would boost access to education to meet the ever-increasing unmet needs. ADRA’s key targets will include; 19,600 children (12,152 girls and 7,448 boys) from different households with major focus on both girls and boys of school-going age who are not attending school due to numerous reasons in Damboa LGA especially in IDPs camps and surrounding communities this will be done in close collaboration with UNICEF and other stakeholders involved in education sector. Also, 400 teachers will be trained to support the education system targeting 240 female teachers and 160 male teachers. The target population distribution will focus on having every child in school with the objective of having at least 90% of the school-going age children in the General hospital, Central primary, Hausari Camp, Abori Camp, SSS QTRs camp and Old Secretariat camp camps and host communities in Damboa LGA having optimal access to education. Particularly; specific interest groups will be catered for with priority; these will include but not limited to; girl children, un-accompanied minors, children living with the elderly people and children with special education needs. The above highlighted categories of people constitute more than an estimated 45% of the affected population in all IDPs camps and surrounding communities. Source: IOM DTM Round 24 site profile. To achieve Comprehensive Emergency education, the project will focus on provision of comprehensive emergency education that will cater for; massive enrollment of all school-going aged children through educational motivational approaches like sports, social clubs, education ambassadors, promotion of early childhood education through caregivers and dialogues with communities and advocacy. The project will also focus on provision of scholastic materials, school fees, creation of safe-learning spaces, construction of semi-temporally learning shelters (TLSs) with separated latrines, child friendly spaces, orientation and accelerated learning systems for out of school children (OOSC). The intervention will focus on establishment and training of School management Committees including training of teachers. This will ensure that teachers are trained in pedagogical issues, lesson planning, protection and SGBV issues, basic special needs education handling skills, while the School Management Committees (SMCs) will ensure psycho-social support, counseling and proper education monitoring systems that will ensure daily class-attendance monitoring and increase retention to counteract school drop outs. The project will ensure integrating children’s continued access to quality education in a safe and protective environment while paying particular attention to cross-cutting issues like diversity and inclusion, malnutrition in schools, water sanitation and hygiene in schools, gender and human rights to facilitate and promote the schooling of all vulnerable groups especially the girl child without discrimination specifically in Damboa LGA, where; like in any other northeast region LGAs, male chauvinism has an upper hand in sidelining female children in regards to education and public activities. It is recognized that low levels of school enrollment, retention and completion and school dropout represent a basic violation of child rights and an urgent priority for action. The issue of school fees remains a key impediment to education access and this will be addressed with immediate responses, the learners who are in Junior Secondary School without learning facilities will also be given attention. The project will as well embark on a vigorous “GO BACK TO SCHOOL” campaign in camps; which will encourage and entice children to enroll schools in big numbers by reaching out to all the IDPs and host community school-aged children and adolescents (3-18 years), focusing on the most vulnerable girls and boys. The project will identify safe spaces and construction safe-rooms for girl children at every schools center for protection purposes. This will be facilitated with basic materials that include; beds, laundry rooms, sanitary wares and hygiene kits for supporting girls at school, which will promote safe learning environment for girls thus increasing retention. This will ensure that girls are not traumatized by the unsafe environment during their studying hour and hence can spend more hours at schools other than feeling insecure during school times. The project will also adapt to the double-shift system of education. The double shift education system is adapted to meet the inadequacy supply of educational facilities versus the students’ population. ADRA will apply her educational expertise in educational in Emergency world wide to ensure that at least 85% to 90% of the IDPs children are in schools, ensure massive voluntary enrollment into schools in the right grades and Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL), monitoring daily school attendance using fellow trained children school ambassadors and teachers. ADRA’s approach in all its interventions for both development and Emergency response is to enhance community sustainability and continuation. This will be achieved by adopting to “home grown solutions and enhancing community managed programs other than community based” meaning that communities will be empowered and trained to manage and own their program interventions as part of exit strategy.

Needs

This project will address various needs identified in the targeted area of operation which include:(1)Congestion in classrooms: there is lack of access to education in Damboa based on the lack of education infrastructures that need to be addressed immediately. The few classrooms that are available and not in adequate conditions for any education environment due to lack of rehabilitation or even furnishing with facilities that can allow children to study with less stress. The assessment revealed that the average classroom in Damboa takes between 80-90 children in class. Type of existing school infrastructure were Temporary infrastructure, permanent infrastructure, and semi-permanent infrastructure(2)Insufficient sanitation facilities at schools: The respondents that included children, teachers and parents and FGD revealed severe lack of sanitary facilities in schools those that operate. The facilities present are not sufficient compared to the number of children attending the school. The facilities are not well secured and used by the community surrounding the schools and hence makes it very unsafe for the children especially the girl child hence leading to schools dropouts and increased number of out of school children. (3)Lack of scholastic materials: Need for basic educational materials including books, school supplies, and sanitary products, furniture, and teaching materials are key hindrances to the education system in Damboa LGA where the assessment was carried out. IDPs children and parents can not afford paying the 100-300 Naira asked of them per term at schools, on top of that the demand of school uniform has kept many out of school. (4) Low levels of special needs education in the community: Inadequate facilities and a lack of understanding of special needs children has also incapacitated the children with special needs for education to access education and are completely out of school. In the community they are seen as outcasts and cannot be considered whenever there is an opportunity for any education access. (5) High dropout rates of girls and early marriages among young girls: There is a great number of children out of school and dropout rate is high. This is however associated with many issues including lack of school fees, scholastic materials, lack of teachers at schools and parents see to it as a wastage of time for their children, security for children and culture. (6) High illiteracy rates of adults: The adults have a high level of illiteracy. This means that most of the parents are not literate also affects the rate at which children will be enticed to attend schools. This needs a lot of advocacy and sensitization for the children to go back to school. This project plans to have innovations that will keep and entice children to be in schools and also campaign the parents to send their children in schools showing them how important it is for the future the young children whose future is at stake now. (7) Psychological and sexual abuse of children: there is also prevalent cases of psychological trauma caused by the insurgents that has hindered the children from attending schools even when there is an opportunity, the fear for abduction, rapes, disappearance and sexual abuse has cause a lot of trauma and this has also enhanced a big number of out of school children in Damboa like in any camps of IDPs and host communities. (8) Gender based violence (GBV) against women and girls: Most IDPs and Returnees in the target areas lack the basic understanding of gender equality and have a low level of understanding of gender dynamics. The dignity and rights of girls, women, and other vulnerable segments of the population have been overlooked culminating in high levels of SGBV in the IDPs informal and formal leading to early marriages and increased increased levels of divorcees, children abandonment, separated and unaccompanied minors, increased destitution and street kids in the town centers.
