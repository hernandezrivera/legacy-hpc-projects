Project Summary

South Sudan is currently facing a multitude of crises, encompassing sub-national conflicts, inter-communal violence, extensive flooding, an economic downturn, and widespread criminal activities. These factors all contribute to the primary causes of humanitarian requirements in South Sudan, as identified in the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2023. The closing of schools has been observed as a consequence of wars characterized by ethnic divisions, resulting in the displacement of children. These displacements have led to situations where children are either separated from their parents or relocated to areas where their educational needs are given lower priority than more pressing concerns. The one-year programme aims to provide education support to four counties which include Wau, Tonj North, Terekeka, and Pibor. The programme includes provisions for ensuring access to basic education, maintaining quality standards, and enhancing the capacity of education stakeholders to respond effectively. These objectives align with the strategic goals outlined in the South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy 2023-2025. The cultural dynamics observed in Tonj North, Pibor, and Terekeka Counties are intricately intertwined with the pastoralist way of life, resulting in significant marginalization of women and girls. This has led to the pervasive existence of gender gaps and discrimination against girls, persisting throughout their transition into womanhood. The project intervention modality in all targeted locations will employ a community-driven approach. This approach involves engaging and empowering local chiefs, PTAs/SMCs, and education stakeholders at the grassroots level. Their role will be to lead the mobilization efforts aimed at addressing the issue of Out of out-of-school children (OoSC) who are forced to work in cattle camps instead of attending school. This initiative aligns with the Education for All (EFA) campaign and serves as a response strategy to Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) number four (4), which emphasizes the right of all children to education. Help a Child International, an organization that prioritizes children's well-being, intends to establish Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs) and peace clubs within schools. This initiative aims to fulfil the role of schools in emergency settings by creating a zone of peace. The primary objective is to promote safety, protection, and social cohesion among children as they engage in learning and recreational activities within the school environment. This will adhere to the principles of non-maleficence, also known as the "DO NO HARM" principles. In addition to receiving training on South Sudan's codes of conduct, life skills, and emotional support, teachers will also be equipped with knowledge and abilities in risk management, mitigation, and readiness. The measures above will be customized to guarantee that, although children engage in educational and recreational activities within the school setting, their overall welfare will be prioritized to facilitate the process of healing and recuperation from the adversities endured as a result of the crisis. Help a Child currently employs an Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Officer who is strategically placed in the locations where the organization carries out its operations. The officer will serve as the central figure responsible for managing community responses and feedback mechanisms implemented in those regions. This role aims to empower community members to voice their sentiments regarding the interventions and facilitate the management in implementing appropriate steps to address them. The preservation of confidentiality is a matter that Help a Child duly acknowledges and prioritizes to safeguard the anonymity of those involved. The project will prioritize the inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable persons.

1. Needs

With the current humanitarian crisis in the country, the education needs continue to rise. The education sector in the country continues to receive little annual allocation from the annual budget allocation which places the National Ministry of General Education and Instructions (MOGEI) in a very limited position to respond to some of the challenges facing the sector.A UNESCO study in 2018 established that there were 2.2 million Out of School children and the current figures as established by Education Cluster 2021 indicated that 2.8 million Out of School Children (OoSC) in South Sudan with 53% of them being girls. This increase means that there are many more disruptive factors to enrolment and retention than previously.According to HNO 2023, 3.4 million people will be in dire need of education services while only a small proportion of that (0.9 million) people will be targeted by the interventions. This underscores the entirely incapacitated education sector’s response capacity to address the myriad of challenges affecting the sector.The assessed education-related needs cover access, quality, and stakeholders’ response capacity and the three constitute the top priority areas of interventions of the education cluster strategy 2023-2025.Access to education in the country remains highly alarming with several factors contributing to it. The lack of permanently built classrooms or temporarily built structures has been exacerbated by the economic crisis that has affected the communities negatively limiting their efforts in contributing to such initiatives to substantiate the government interventions. Floods and localized droughts are the primary cause of poor agricultural yields for communities that practice subsistence farming where their crops and livestock are both affected.The lack of gender-sensitive and inclusive pit latrines in the school constitutes another access constraint to education which leads to schools being insensitive to the needs of girls and children with disabilities. The presence of functional water sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities in the school is key to girls' enrollment and retention as they would be able to confidently address their menstrual hygiene challenges as they continue taking lessons.There is a serious lack of qualified teachers in the schools which compromises the quality of the instructions issued to the learners. The lack of adequate teaching and learning materials also impedes enrollment and retention which are key aspects of access. With the economic crises coupled with the localized drought and flood spells in many parts of the countries, farming has drastically been affected especially for communities that practice mixed subsistence farming. This has contributed to massive dropout of school as children are forced to join smaller income-generating activities to support contributing to the family livelihood.The lack of well-structured temporary learning spaces (TLSs) constructed using both MOGEI and Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) standards taking into consideration child friendliness and inclusivity is another very serious challenge as most of the lessons are conducted under the trees in Wau, Tonj North and Pibor Counties where complex crises comprising of conflicts, climate change issues like rain and wind can disrupt learning.The lack of teaching and learning materials also constitutes another access issue affecting the education sector in South Sudan. Across the country, scholastic materials are expensive for some parents to purchase especially those families with more than 4 children. This makes learning unrealistic as the lessons are supposed to be copied by the learners for studies in preparation for exams, tests as well as personal understanding of the subject matter.

2. Project activities

Through this intervention, Help a Child will address the most pressing educational needs to enable access to the Out of out-of-school children (OoSC) to schools in Wau, Tonj North, Terekeka and Pibor counties. That will be possible through the implementation of the following activities.The construction and rehabilitation of 19 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs) is in line with the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MOGEI) to enroll and accommodate out-of-school children.Rehabilitation of the TLSS will include improving the damaged classrooms which will be ready for use upon completion of the renovation process. The construction of 40 gender-sensitive and inclusive pit latrines with changing rooms and ramps will be of great importance given the girl's empowerment agenda it comes with. The project will also distribute teaching and learning materials.Training of 48 teachers, local education actors, 65 PTAs/SMCs, and local chiefs to enhance their response capacities.Distribution of dignity kits equipped with sanitary pads, pants, bathing and washing soaps, handkerchiefs, comb, drying rope, toothpaste, and brushes to adolescent girls.Through that, the project will conduct 4 Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns in all the locations as part of the enrollment drive for the out-of-school children. Girl Child Education Day will also be celebrated on 7th July 2024 across the project locations to empower the girl child to prioritize education by engaging parents, teachers, community leaders, and local education stakeholders. This will aim at addressing forced and early marriages, teenage pregnancies, and other negative practices hindering girls from achieving their educational potential.The formation of school clubs (peace, debate, hygiene, sport, disability, child protection, and inclusion). Those club members would be trained in their specified roles based on the thematic area of focus. The activation of such clubs will enhance peaceful coexistence, social cohesion, and the appreciation of the existing differences among them fostering a sense of the beauty and admiration of the country’s cultural diversity. That will also promote the agenda of schools being zones of peace as well as instilling other cross-cutting issues like sanitation and hygiene, school gardening which will promote sustainability, and provision of school meals which constitutes one of the lifesaving components in the education sector as it is linked to school functionality and enrolment and retention of the learners.To furthermore provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), Help a Child will apply its innovative approach BoB to provide sessions to learners and parents on MHPSS to aid healing and recovery for children and parents who experienced conflicts and other forms of abuse, the project would organize and conduct inter-school sports competitions (Boruboru for girls, football for boys). Boruboru is a typical South Sudanese traditional dodgeball game played by girls. It has been used by HAC in many locations in South Sudan with proven evidence of its being a great tool for advocacy by girls on their rights and needs. While the boys will play football, the girls will play Boruboru in all the locations of the project.Nonetheless, the intervention will initiate the payment of volunteer teachers’ incentives after formally identifying and training them to enable them to continue providing teaching services in the schools.The establishment of school meals and the inception of school gardening as part of the exit strategy will be a very important component of the project. The gardening and agricultural clubs will support the cultivation of food crops in the school compound for use as school meals.Distribution of Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials to create awareness of the project and its critical components.
