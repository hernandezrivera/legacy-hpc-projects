Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) aims at providing critical protection related assistance, prevention and mitigation of protection risks to 13,760 conflict affected persons in Mundri East, Mundri West Counties in Western Equatoria State and Jur River County of Western Bahr el Ghazal State where we already have physical presence with our staff on the ground currently implementing various activities on protection, rehabilitation of persons with disabilities, education, food security and livelihoods projects supported by UNDP and other agencies.
We shall integrate with the child protection, GBV,HLP and mine action sub clusters to ensure that our services are able to address specific protection issues related to age, gender and diversity. This will enable this project to make efficient use of synergies and avoid duplication and facilitate achievement of a complete protection response.
The complex operating environment where we shall implement this project needs an elaborate approach to meet the needs of the most vulnerable persons through activities that we shall undertake like: case management, referrals, psychosocial support, community awareness raising, peace building, protection assessment and monitoring. We shall place social workers in every boma, who will move from home to home identifying, assessing and determining the support required for every vulnerable persons identified who include IDPS, and IDP returnees. For established IDP camps like the one in Mundri West, we shall allocate a social worker who shall take lead in service provision for the IDP`S.
We shall deliver psychosocial support intervention inorder to improve the adaptive coping among conflict affected persons. This will increase their resilience and create an environment that will promote psychosocial wellbeing. Our intervention will respect the rights of the beneficiaries, responsive to gender and age, and sensitive to the diverse cultures of people in Mundri East, Mundri West and Jur River Counties.
Our approach includes a component of provision of case management services that will focus on supporting the most vulnerable and high-risk persons as well as other vulnerable persons within the community. Our primary goal for case management will be to prevent and respond to needs of vulnerable persons experiencing or who are at risk of abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation through individual social work services. We will provide timely case management and follow up to needy persons to ensure their safety while taking into consideration factors that may hinder effective communication like the language of the person, communication for persons with mental health and use of sign language interpreters for persons who are deaf.
We shall have an effective referral mechanism which will ensure that vulnerable persons have been identified, their needs assessed and ensure that they receive inter sectoral services being delivered in Mundri East, Mundri West and Jur River Counties, and ensure that good outcomes have been achieved.
Through using various modes like community public rallies, sports, cultural dances peer to peer sessions, we shall raise awareness to the community on protection issues. We shall target various groups via different modes, for example the young people would benefit more through peer to peer educational programmes. The sessions will help the community to widen the social responsibility of protection, where the communities will understand they all have a role to play in laying a stable ground for their own protection.
By enhancing capacity of community members to resolve conflict, we shall promote strong relationships among community members and cement peaceful coexistence that will respect rights of all persons including vulnerable boys, girls, women, men and persons with disabilities.

1 - Needs

A situational overview conducted in Jur River County by REACH (January- March 2019) revealed that a number of communities were affected by cattle raids in March 2019. This insecurity likely contributed to the increase between February (75%) and March (96%) in the proportion of assessed settlements in Jur River reporting that most people felt unsafe.Similarly, an IRNA conducted in Amadi State from 27th March to 11th April 2019 with an objective of assessing and understanding the needs of the conflict affected populations (IDPs and returnees) gave findings related to either forced displacement or a fear of forced displacement (reported in 64.3% of towns), restriction of the movement for civilians (reported in 60.0% of towns), and an increased vulnerability to sexual and physical violence (reported in 56.3% of towns). These continuous cycles of conflict undermine people’s dignity and long-term psychological wellbeing, as well as having a detrimental effect on the social fabric of displaced communities. Therefore, SEM will aim at addressing the needs of the people in Jur River, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties by drawing attention to the fundamental needs of the affected persons and make positive changes towards the fulfillment of their rights.IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix assessed three locations in Kuarjena, Jur River of Western Bahr el Ghazal in March 2019 following recent clashes between pastoralists and farmers. The unrest resulted in the displacement of 9,672people who fled Akau, Werbet and Madhon (all within Kuarjena area). At the time of assessment, IDPs continued to arrive at a spontaneous settlement in Akwo as well as within host community settings in Kuajiena Village and Mapel (all within Kuarjena).The impact of long-term displacement, particularly for a community already traumatized by conflict and violence becomes a concern cannot be overlooked, and will therefore require to be addressed by psychosocial support to increase their resilience and create and integrated development approach to promote their psychosocial wellbeing. By implementing this project, SEM seeks to empower the targeted vulnerable communities to mitigate threats they face from the ongoing effects of armed conflict by enabling them to have a better understanding of the diversity in their communities, threats that they face and resources available within reach that they are able to utilize to promote their own protection. They will also be empowered to identify and adopt strategies aimed at protecting themselves from the effects of conflict.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Throughout the implementation of this project, we shall aim to facilitate the recognition of human rights among all vulnerable boys, girls, women and men as well as promote community awareness of human rights. We shall recognize the basic rights of all beneficiaries, their freedoms of expression to enable them participate fully in this project. We shall encourage the participation of all targeted beneficiaries and disseminate information on available services to them without discriminating them on basis of their cultural, economic, social status or gender.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project will target internally displaced persons and the host community residing in various locations in Mundri East, Mundri West and Jur River County. We shall support and promote the resilience by enabling them actively participate in the various priority activities that we shall deliver to respond, prevent and mitigate gender based violence.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project has been linked to existing systems like the referral pathway. We shall use the referral pathway as a tool for providing direct services to the beneficiaries where they shall access inter sectoral services from government and nongovernmental organization present in the project area.Other community systems in place, e.g. local authorities, representatives of youths, women shall be involved in community mobilization for promoting their involvement in protection and gender based violence prevention.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

While handling issues of children, we shall refer to the South Sudan child act inorder to promote and protect the best interests of children. All children shall have their rights respected and various stakeholders be urged/ supported/ assisted to take up their roles to support children to be safe, and to develop as expected.This project is also linked with the South Sudan National Action plan 2015-2020 on women, peace, and security that promotes the protection of women in post independence South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The current context in Mundri East, Mundri West and Jur River County is sufficiently stable at the moment. It allows voluntary participation of project beneficiaries in the activities that we shall implement and enable their voices to be heard and build the level of trust of the project with the community. For vulnerable persons whom are internally displaced, they have been locally integrated and already receiving various forms of assistance.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

By promoting community participation, we will facilitate the community members to receive interventions that reflect their real needs and priorities, and increase ownership among community members and increase the chances of sustainability after the project. They will be able to feel that the project belongs to them, enable them to give their input in practice and make decisions that affect them during implementation.Sport activities for the youth will facilitate continuity beyond the project period as they shall form teams that they will us to pass information to the community using the kits that they shall get from this project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Members of marginalized community’s/minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance-Apart from consulting the marginalized/ minority groups, we shall systematically collect fully disaggregated data that shall reflect sex, disability status, age including older age, location and other factors affecting people’s access to assistance where appropriate all through the life of this project. This data will reflect on the access, coverage how our services meet the diverse needs of the affected population based on their gender, age, ability and other vulnerability factors. Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example)-We shall maintain a current list of local resources, inter sectoral services that are available in Mundri East, Mundri West and Jur River Counties and make it a standard part of the information available to survivors of protection incidents and their family/ caregivers.We shall further disseminate information through leaflets, posters and information cards to the community to reach as much people as possible.Through training of frontline service providers on referral, and how to use the existing referral pathways, they will be trained on how to identify various inter sectoral services that will be of benefit to the survivors of protection incidents and enhance their understanding of importance of implementing effective referrals within their communities.Unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries-By making use of our code of conduct, all our staff shall be expected to abide by the clauses that refer to safety of data and responsible use of collected data. This will prevent distribution, publishing, or sharing of any collected data without the consent of the beneficiaries and SEM to safeguard the confidentiality of our beneficiaries.
