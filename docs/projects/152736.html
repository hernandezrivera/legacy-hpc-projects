Project Summary

The purpose of this intervention is to save lives, reduce severe food insecurity and stabilize the condition of vulnerable groups including the elderly, IDPs, persons with disabilities, child-headed households, women and children in South Sudan. The interventions will further enhance community resilience by addressing the main drivers of vulnerability in the targeted areas of Kapoeta East, Tonj North, Tonj East and Terekeka. The project will seek to enhance both food security and livelihoods of vulnerable people by improving livestock production and access to animal health care services, improving access to agricultural production units & extension services and supporting communities to develop alternative income sources / livelihoods diversification by providing financial services through Group Savings and Loan Associations (GSLAs). Cross-cutting themes will be to promote gender equity and increased women’s participation in building resilience, peace building and conflict reduction, disaster risk reduction and sustainable natural resource management. The geographical focus of the project is Kapoeta East County (Kauto 5,064 HH; Jie-900 HH, Mogos-3,650 HH, Lotimor-1,550 HH; and Nating'a-990 HH; payams); Tonj North County (Alabek-2,400 HH, Akop-2,100 HH, Rualbet- 1,900 and Marialou 1,600 payams), Tonj East (Kacuat-2,080 HH, Paweng-2,150 HH Malualchum 2, 200-HH payams) and Terekeka County (Terekeka-2,400 HH, Muni-1,800 HH, Reggo-1,750 and Nyori-1,900 payams). Proposed interventions are aligned to FSL HRP Cluster objectives as shown below:
• Under unconditional food assistance, IRSS proposes to provide emergency regular life sustaining and life-saving food assistance to prevent famine, improve food consumption, improve dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the most vulnerable population. This will target the most vulnerable households including IDPs
• Regarding food production for the most vulnerable households, the proposed interventions will seek to increase food production by vulnerable household in order to protect the erosion of livelihoods through improved agronomic practices i.e. use of ox plough animal traction, agricultural seeds and other faming inputs.
• Under the transitional emergency food assistance, IRSS proposes to provide emergency targeted life sustaining food assistance to strengthen households and community coping capacities by building productive assets and reduce reliance on general food distribution transfers. Food for Asset modality will be used to enable communities produce their own food in the long-term.
• In order to reduce dependency on food and agricultural input support, IRSS will through livelihood & conditional cash based support for more sustainable livelihoods seek to prevent and reverse the erosion of vulnerable rural & urban households and community assets.
The proposed interventions will therefore be aimed at providing a link between rapid humanitarian response on one hand and building community resilience on the other. This will in turn contribute to reduce community vulnerability and build their capacity to bounce back in the face of livelihood shocks resulting from drought,conflict and others.

Needs

Across the four counties of Kapoeta East, Tonj North, Tonj East and Terekeka where the proposed interventions will be implemented, food availability and access continue to be severely constrained by the widespread and protracted conflict, increased drought necessitated by climate change, large-scale displacements, high food prices, market disruptions, macro-economic collapse and exhaustion of households’ coping mechanisms. The situation in Kapoeta East is currently Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and is anticipated to recline into Emergency (IPC Phase 4) due to the massive crop failure in the main agricultural season for 2018. Kapoeta East is in former Eastern Equatorial state where according to the current IPC, 39.2% of the population is estimated to be in Crisis, Emergency & Catastrophe and therefore in need of humanitarian assistance. The nutrition status show Critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM 15.0%- 29.9%). The nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate significantly possibly reaching the upper limit of the critical levels as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhoea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services due to insecurity and heavy rains in the second season. Majority of the inhabitants of Kapoeta East County are livestock keepers and depend entirely on livestock for their livelihood and also practice seasonal crop production. However, due to drought in Kapoeta East for three consecutive years, the population have become vulnerable as their livestock lack water and pasture. The crop production cycle has also been disrupted drastically. Hunger remains a key challenge. Some household spent days without having a meal on the table. Livestock owners have to migrate to far distances looking for pasture and water for their animals at the neighbouring borders of Kenya, Ethiopia and Uganda. There are also frequent clashes with neighbouring communities over pasture and water especially between the Toposa and the Turkana of Kenya. Due to cross-border conflict, resident of Nating’a payam have been displaced by insecurity and are currently living as IDPs in Narus payam. In Tonj inter and intra-communal conflicts have distracted the agricultural production cycle in the target payams of the two counties of Tonj North and Tonj East. Homes and farms have been abandoned and populations displaced to market centers as IDPs. Tonj East although currently in IPC 2, it is expected to recline into Crisis i.e. IPC 3 at the beginning of the lean season because of poor harvest due to insecurity. Similarly politically instigated insecurity has also destabilized once peaceful and agriculturally vibrant communities in Terekeka county in Centreal Equatorial. There was no data provided for the Terekeka under the current IPC due to the same insecurity in the area.

Activities

The proposed interventions are therefore aimed at enhancing community economic empowerment by addressing the main drivers of vulnerability in the targeted areas. The project will seek to enhance both food security and livelihoods by improving livestock production and access to animal health care services, improving access to agricultural production units and extension services and supporting communities to develop alternative livelihoods modes among other strategies. Specific activities will include the following: i. Provision of food packs to vulnerable IDPs and host communities during the lean season by way of conditional food assistance. This is expected to improve access to immediate food for target IDPs and host communities affected by drought and conflict. IRSS will ensure that the food packs will be sufficient to help the target vulnerable households to maintain food security for at-least 30 days in one cycle distribution. The target for the food distribution will be vulnerable households from both IDPs and host communities. ii. Unconditional cash and voucher-based transfers with food security objectives iii. Conditional Food Assistance through Food for Assets (FFA) and transitional emergency food assistance iv. Through provision of access to agriculture inputs, extension services and, IRSS will contribute to improved agricultural production, harvesting and storage of food, and hence its security through building beneficiaries capacities. Proposed activities include distribution of ox ploughs, distribution and establishment of demonstration plots using the Farmer Field School Model and Capacity building for the County Agriculture Departments v. Provision of animal health services and development of the livestock sector through provision of vaccination and livestock disease prevention and treatment services, training and refresher courses for Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and Animal Health Assistants (AHAs) and capacity building for Animal Resources County Departments vi. Support to Value Chain Addition through milk hygiene, handling and preservation in Kapoeta East , Tonj North & East and Terekeka. vii. Support to Value Chain Addition through fish, drying and preservation in Terekeka viii. Support to Female Headed Households to develop alternative sources of income. This will be achieved through the formation of support and training for women’s groups to develop small-scale poultry production activities and developing of solar irrigated farming system for production of assorted vegetables all year round. xi. Support to livelihood diversification by developing and strengthening of financial services through Group Savings and Loans Association (GSLAs), Introduction of bee keeping ix. Integration of cross cutting issues of gender, disaster risk reduction, conflict and natural resource management in all FSL project activities. Awareness raising sessions will be conducted to communities and Local Government Authorities (LGAs) due to the recognition of their importance and relevance to the food security and vulnerability of communities. As a result, the target communities and local government institutions will gain an increased understanding of key cross-cutting issues.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

To ensure inclusive participation, IRSS will allow and encourage project beneficiaries to speak out and influence decisions. IRSS regards information and communication to be critical forms of humanitarian assistance aid, without which beneficiaries cannot access services, make the best decisions for themselves and their communities, or hold the organization and other agencies to account. This is done by sharing information, listening carefully to beneficiary communities and involving them in decision-making which in turn contribute to more effective FSL programmes and improve the quality of services delivered. Whenever project beneficiaries have the opportunity to voice their opinions, this enhances their sense of well-being, helps them adapt to the challenges they face and better enables them to take an active role in their own recovery process. Through this project, IRSS will ensure beneficiaries (including the most vulnerable) are made aware of their rights and entitlements, have timely access to relevant and clear information and are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the humanitarianism response on food security and livelihood.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In terms of context,food insecurity may be the result of wider macro-economic and structural socio-political factors, including national and international policies, processes or institutions that have an impact on the targeted population’s access to nutritionally adequate food. This is usually defined as chronic food insecurity, a long-term condition resulting from structural vulnerabilities that may be aggravated by the impact of disaster. Local and regional food security information systems, including famine early warning systems and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, are important mechanisms to analyse information. IRSS shall carry out a participatory analysis of vulnerability to enlist meaningful participation of different groups of women and men and appropriate local organisations and institutions in all stages of the project. This project will build on local knowledge, based on need and tailored to the local context. Areas subject to recurrent natural disasters or long-running conflicts may have local early warning and emergency response systems or networks and contingency plans which should be incorporated into any assessment. It is critical to engage women in project design and implementation

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS and its senior management are responsible for ensuring that complaints mechanisms and procedures for Sexual Exploitation Abuse (SEA) and or harassment are in place, safe, transparent, accessible and confidential. We include specific statements about cooperating with investigations into SEA cases, where appropriate in the partnership agreements. Overall, complaints about sexual exploitation and abuse are investigated immediately by staff with relevant competencies and an appropriate level of authority.IRSS will integrate proactive measures to minimize Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) risks, through staff capacity development and codes of conduct. As a policy, all new IR staff complete a mandatory induction which includes the Code of Conduct (reviewed and signed), and where a session (1:1 or group) is provided by both Protection and Gender staff. IRSS also has policies on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), child protection and a Safeguarding focal point to address any allegations of sexual exploitation or abuse; all staff are trained on these policies. In addition, programme staffs participate in training sessions on protection and gender (general principles of gender mainstreaming and safe programming and specific themes). The project also considers the protection from SEA through the placement of feedback mechanisms; the following of FSL Sphere standards on quality and quantity of food; formation and training for food committees, particularly on being able to detect indicators of violence and ask the right questions; the training of project staff on gender equality and protection; undertaking GBV risk analysis food distribution to ensure that no-harm is done and collection of monitoring data that is sex and age disaggregated.

Yes

IRSS will make all efforts to enable project beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them. Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IRSS believe that project beneficiaries have the right to complain and to receive an appropriate and timely response on food safety and quality . Formal mechanisms for complaints and redress are an essential component of its accountability and give affected communities some element of control over their lives. Complaints containing specific grievances have in the past alerted IRSS to serious misconduct or failures in the response, allowing the organization to take timely action to improve programme quality. Like all organizations, IRSS is susceptible to fraud or abuse of power and a complaints system helps to recognize and respond to malpractice, manipulation and exploitation. To realize this IRSS has put in place strategies to ensure: •Consultations with project beneficiaries on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. •Welcoming and accepting complaints, and communicating how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address •Managing complaints in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritises the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages. •The complaints-handling process for project beneficiaries is documented and in place, covering programming, sexual exploitation and abuse, and other abuses of power. Through this processes, IRSS expects to receive and handle complaints related to favoritism, discrimination etc in FSL. A feedback and complaints committee comprising community, representatives, field staff and Juba office staff will be formed to handle any complaints that may arise during the project implementation.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Proposed food security responses will be aimed at meeting short-term needs, i.e ‘do no harm’, reduce the need for the affected population to adopt potentially damaging coping strategies and contribute to restoring longer-term food security.An accurate assessment has been done to examine appropriateness and feasibility of the potential response options. The food security responses in the proposed project will be grouped into standards for general food security, food transfers, cash and voucher transfers, and livelihoods & resilience building responses.Where the food is required, the appropriate form of transfer shall be considered and food basket carefully chosen for both in-kind and voucher transfers. Our livelihood responses include primary production, income and employment, and access to market goods and services.Cash and voucher transfers will be used for a range of goods or services in food security, as well as other sectors. Understanding the market capacity and the appropriate modality for delivery will be critical designing food security interventions.Food security standards will consider the resources to meet the food needs of both the general population and specific vulnerable people at increased nutritional risk. Until these needs are met, any response aimed at the treatment of malnutrition will have a limited impact since those who recover from malnutrition may return to a context of inadequate food intake and their nutritional status is likely to deteriorate again.Targeting, delivery and distribution methods shall reduce the risk of inclusion and exclusion errors as much as possible. This includes the risk that food, cash or other assistance is misappropriated. It is important that food security interventions are not diverted to worsen conflicts. Specific measures to prevent, monitor and respond to gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation associated with food distribution, shall be enforced, including segregating men and women.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concerning coverage, access and acceptability, project beneficiaries and their characteristics will be assessed and their numbers, disaggregated by sex and age; estimated before determining the level of participation of different groups (paying particular attention to vulnerable people). Participation will partly be deter¬mined by ease of access and the acceptability of activities to beneficiaries. Where food security responses will be targeted at the economically active, care will be taken to ensure it does not discriminate unfairly and should be accessible to vulnerable people and protect dependents, including children. Constraints may limit participation, including reduced capacity to work, heavy workload at home, and pregnancy, feeding and caring for children, illness and disability. Overcoming such constraints will involve identifying activities within the capacity of the groups or setting-up appropriate support structures. Targeting mechanisms based on self-selection will be established after full consultation with all groups in the population. In regard to access to knowledge, skills and services, IRSS organisational structures shall be designed and planned together with users, so that they are appropriate and adequately maintained, where possible beyond the life of the intervention. Some individuals have very specific needs, e.g. children orphaned as a result of AIDS may miss out on the information and skills transfer that takes place within families, which can be provided by appropriate services.The needy will access humanitarian assistance according to need and without adverse discrimination. Assistance won't withheld from people in need, and access for humanitarian agencies provided as necessary to meet the Sphere Standards.Ensuring access for all parts of the affected population to humanitarian assistance,any deliberate deprivation to parts of the population of the means of subsistence shall always be challenged legally & support will be need-based

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring and evaluation will be carried out at all levels of the food supply chain to consumption point. At distribution points, check that arrangements for distributions are in place before they take place (e.g. for registration, security, dissemination of information). Random weighing will be carried out on rations collected by house¬holds to measure the accuracy and equity of distribution management, with recipients interviewed. Random visits to households can help ascertain the acceptability and usefulness of the ration, and also identify people who meet the selection criteria but are not receiving food. Such visits can also discover if extra food is being received, where it is coming from, what it is being used for and by whom (e.g. as a result of commandeering, recruitment or exploitation, sexual or otherwise). Monitoring shall analyze the impact of food transfers on the safety of the beneficiaries. The wider effects of food distributions shall also be evaluated, such as implications of the agricultural cycle, agricultural activities, market conditions and availability of agricultural inputs.•Risk of insecurity and diversion: Handing out resources in the form of food or cash for work (e.g. loans or payments for work done) introduces security concerns for both programme staff and the recipients. •Caring responsibilities and livelihoods: Participation in income-earning opportunities should not undermine childcare or other caring responsibilities as this could increase the risk of under nutrition and other health risks. Programmes may need to consider employing care-providers or providing care facilities. It may not be appropriate to introduce increased workloads into people’s lives, especially women’s. Programmes should not adversely affect access to other opportunities, such as other employment or education, or divert household resources from productive activities already in place. Participation in income generation to respect national employment laws

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

RSS will to monitor the wider food security situation in order to assess the continued relevance of the interventions, determine when to phase out specific activities, introduce modifications or new projects and identify any need for advocacy. The evaluation shall be based on established Development Assistance Committee criteria recorded by the OECD, which measure the following: appropriateness, connectedness, coherence, coverage, efficiency, effectiveness and impact.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

IRSS will take measures to ensure food distributed under this project is stored, prepared and consumed in a safe and appropriate manner at both household and community levels. These will include: •Protect beneficiaries from inappropriate food handling or preparation. •Disseminate relevant information on the importance of food hygiene to food recipients and promote a good understanding of hygienic practices in food handling. •Where cooked food is distributed, train staff in safe storage and handling of food, preparation of food and the potential health hazards caused by improper practices.•Consult (and advise where necessary) beneficiaries on storage, preparation, cooking and consumption of food distributed and the implications of targeted provision for vulnerable people and respond to issues that arise.•Where appropriate facilitate households’ access to appropriate cooking utensils, fuel, potable water and hygiene materials.•For individuals who cannot prepare food or cannot feed themselves, ensure access to carers to prepare appropriate food and administer feeding where necessary.
