Project Summary

Through this project, the Danish Refugee Council (DRC) aims to contribute to operationalize the 'Government of Bangladesh-United Nations Framework on Skills Development for Rohingya refugees/FDMNs and Host Communities', endorsed by the Government in August 2022. DRC will build the skills and capacities of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs commensurate with livelihood opportunities available in Rakhine State to support voluntary and sustainable repatriation in Myanmar when conditions are conducive. DRC aims to uphold LSD sector objective 1, 2 and 3. Through these initiatives, DRC aims to increase socio-economic access of both Rohingya and host community young adults and middle- aged adult by placing a strong emphasis on creating livelihood opportunities through skill training as a key driving force.
A total of 600 beneficiaries (300 Rohingya refugees /FDMNs and 300 in host communities) will be targeted. In line with the sector objective 1: skill and capacity will be developed to reach the support skill and capacity building of Rohingya refugee/that can support their sustainable reintegration in Myanmar, DRC will provide non-formal technical training of 120 hours to 200 Rohingya youths. The training is designed on two modules, where 50 youths will get training on repairing of gas stoves (in camp 8W and 10), 50 will get training on Jute products (camp8E and Camp5) and rest 100 youths will gain skill on Garments Waste recycling to produce marketable high value products in Camp5, 8E, 8W and 10. Modules will be handy and standards and do ensure the validation by LSDS sector before rolling out. DRC will contribute to the sector’s objective 2 creating diversified livelihoods options for 100 Rohingyas/ FDMNs engaging them in off-farm activities-plastic waste collection and waste management as volunteers for maximum 6-month period. In kind supports and market linkage will also ensure to trained 200 youths through DRC existing (Plastic recycling through shredding machine in Host and CBO run garments recycling in Host). These activities will ultimately contribute to JRP’s Strategic Objective 1 - Work towards the sustainable and voluntary repatriation of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs to Myanmar. Other 100 Rohingya men and women will be involved in DP/ DRR activities.
Apart from Rohingya, 300 host community (men and women) will also get livelihoods and skill development support in line with the sector objective 3: support host and local communities in diversified vocational skills development and sustainable livelihoods option. A total of 300 youth (men and women) will gain skills on two market-driven modules (Module1: garments industry waste recycling for green employment creation; and Module 2: plastic waste recycling through the use shredding machine managed by the communities) with 30 days long training on entrepreneurship and business development training in two spell: 1 spell for 24 day training technical training and the spell 2 for entrepreneurship and business development training). In kind support/grants will be provided to all 300 trained graduates along with establishing market linkage. A display center adjacent to host community training venue will also be established. A standard module in Bangla will be delivered but incorporating feedback after completion of one batch training and their feedback. The targeting are vulnerable youth men and women of Rajapalong, Palongkhali and Jaliapalong unions in Ukhiya sub district.
Conditional business startup cash support (off- farm) activities will also be designed for the 300 trained individual based on respective business plans (BP) supported by DRC. DRC has already established 5 CBOs in in Ukhiya host communities and has been supporting in capacity building since 2020. A good number of CBO members have already trained on BP by DRC. The project will be implemented in partnership with a Bangladeshi NGO - Eco Social Development organization (ESD0).

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The assessment (REVA -6 Report, June 2023) findings showed that economic vulnerability has decreased among Rohingya (by 13 percent) and Bangladeshi communities (1 percent) compared to 2021. However, if the assistance is removed, 93 percent of Rohingya would not be able to afford the MEB, showing significant dependence on humanitarian assistance for survival. The price hikes of community’s consequences of the Covid pandemic also excavated by the Russia Ukraine crisis (the financial express, August 2022). Moreover, Cox’s Bazar and its surrounding districts of Bangladesh are among the lowest-performing districts in terms of education and skills training (World Bank, 2019). The district is historically lagging behind both in the economic performance as well as the human development index. There is significant discrepancy in terms of gender parity in labour market and educational attainment. Though livelihoods and some case -centered interventions are still restricted in Rohingya camps where the Rohingya do not get work permits and their movement outside the camps for daily work. Ban on normal employment, limitations on mobility, exclusion from formal financial services, landlessness and hopeless conditions make the Rohingya livelihoods harder and harder. The Rohingya population consists of a large working age population with 42 per cent adult, 55 per cent children and only 3 per cent elderly. The top three occupation categories held by Rohingyas, according to the UNHCR profile, include domestic work, agriculture (including fisheries and animal husbandry), and community work. Over 35 per cent also either reported that they were unemployed or did not report any work experience. Overall, 93% of young Rohingyas did not receive any training in Bangladesh (Population Council report, November 2022). However, Government of Bangladesh through its National Task Force (NTF) allows to support and facilitate a dignified and safe return as well as prepare them for sustainable reintegration in Myanmar both socially and economically and agreed to allow skills training for Rohingya youth and adults in the Cox’s Bazar camps in Bangladesh. The DRC Bangladesh survey report at camp level (August 2022) shows that 96% surveyed youth aged 18 to24 are unemployed,9 out of 10 respondents aged 18 to 24 are in debt and women aged 18 to 24, unemployment rates border to 99%. The above findings clearly indicates that there is opportunity to work with camp & host communities on skill based and vocational training. In most of the camps have solar system's installation and frequently becomes disorder and damage due to lack of repairing and maintenance activities. To activate the disordered and damaged solar, it is needed to have skilled technicians at camp level. The project intended to make good number of trained skilled Rohingya youth adults who will later ensure to provide technical support at camp level. Beside this, non-formal skills development training on recycling garment jhoot waste has also create some opportunities to manage waste to wage in both camp and host community thus will also generate income and employment and to lessen environment pollution. 100 vulnerable host women and 100 Rohingya Refugee women will receive non formal skills development training on recycling garment waste for producing households’ usable items. Apart from this strengthening collaboration with government departments, CBOs, private sector,Micro Finance Institutes, and other actors for integrating efforts for better facilities and services to the vulnerable. Take into account of the above challenges, the DRC’s proposed project will contribute to enhance the decent jobs and improve the livelihoods situation through implementing the mentioned skill delivery and associated assistances.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In line with the rationalization Principle #3 for promoting integrated approach, DRC will aim to fill the gaps in livelihoods services for targeted women and men where all ages of people including people with disabilities and vulnerable people also get benefits from livelihoods skill interventions. Additionally, protection and SCCCM sector of DRC will also get benefit from Livelihoods where we can practice integration with livelihood/economic recovery with protection. DRC will also seek to improve the inter-sectoral camp-based coordination in a coordinated manner through SCCCM and integrating appropriate service delivery to refugees and host communities while maintaining a collaborative and complementary relations with the government/CiCs and other sector agencies. Considering the Principle #4 for ensuring predictability and continuity, DRC will ensure its continuity and stability of the programme together with other donor funded projects by DANIDA, ECHO, GAC, SIDA, UN and other major donors. DRC has a livelihoods and economic recovery sector through which will ensure integration with protection and continue integration of the interventions in both camp and host community to improve relations with refugees, the government, and other partners in the camps. Furthermore, as part of its sustainability strategy, DRC has already been delivering the interventions through its national implementing Partner, Eco Social Development Organization (ESDO). In terms of predictability and continuity, DRC has ECHO and DANIDA-funded projects, which will ensure the activities continuation. In addition, at camp level, all coordination will be maintained through camp management and at host level through Upazila administration. Coordination and guidance will also be followed from ISCG’s Livelihoods and skill development sector and Environment and Energy Network (EEN).

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Given DRC's organizational focus on protection, the LLH programming will strictly adhere to all protection policies and compliances under the guidance and coordination of the protection sector. The practices of general protection, protection monitoring, case management support will be sought from the DRC’s internal protection sector and the capacity of the LLH staff will be developed by DRC's protection sector. Moreover, four Protection Mainstreaming principles such as a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability and d) participation & empowerment will be promoted throughout the project implementation, while the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (hotline for complaints and feedback) will be constantly upgraded in line with the DRC CFM Policy, procedures, and the DRC CFM Handbook. DRC has further collaborated with the PSEA Network to provide training for camp volunteers during inductions, and a designated PSEA focal point within DRC will address reports related to DRC staff throughout the project lifecycle, including needs assessment, risk analysis, project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluations.

DRC is committed to ensuring age including safeguarding approach and principle (best interest of children, confidentiality, do no harm) across its programming cycle. Learning and feedback will be incorporated into necessary processes and pathways, considering contextual factors and needs to demonstrate accountability to beneficiaries. DRC’s protection sector assessment data and findings will be used and followed towards the project period while volunteers and staff capacity will be enhanced through awareness and training sessions. 3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In the arena of livelihood and skill development, the incorporation of measures to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) holds paramount significance, particularly within the confines of refugee camps and host communities, where displaced populations are especially susceptible. Prioritizing the implementation of comprehensive PSEA strategies is pivotal for organizations to safeguard the dignity and well-being of beneficiaries during crises. This entails the establishment of explicit policies, the cultivation of a culture of accountability, and a resolute stance of zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse. Foundational to this approach are training and capacity-building initiatives, equipping both staff and community members with the knowledge and skills needed to identify and respond to instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. Outreach programs within refugee camps and host communities play a crucial role in disseminating awareness about PSEA, fostering community engagement, and promoting the reporting of incidents without fear of reprisal. Efforts to mainstream PSEA will not only safeguard the immediate well-being of vulnerable populations but also will contribute to the overall resilience and stability of communities affected by displacement. The holistic approach of the initiative reflects a commitment to the protection principle, ensuring that humanitarian action not only provides immediate relief but also contributes to the long-term well-being and resilience of the communities involved.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the repairing and maintenance of solar panel is green energy related activity that contribute in mitigating of climate change. Furthermore, skills development training on recycling garment Jhoot waste for producing households’ usable items is under waste management activity that substantially mitigates environmental pollution. However, the proposed garment jhoot recycling interventions will also address and enhance technical skills of business entrepreneurs to recycle and reuse garment industry leftovers turning to households useable bedding and domestic usable materials and minimized environmental hazardous impact of garment manufacturers in Bangladesh.
