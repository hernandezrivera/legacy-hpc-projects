Project Summary

Leveraging our existing presence in the targeted areas, contextual understanding and over 7 years of operational experience delivering WASH activities in northern Syria, the proposed action will directly support the implementation of the 2021 HRP thru increasing access to improved and durable WASH services for conflict-affected populations in areas of Idleb & Aleppo ranked high in severity of WASH needs. This will be achieved thru delivery of an integrated & comprehensive response to WASH services of approx. 255509 people of which an est. 127352 IDP. Areas were selected based on People in Need (PIN) and WASH Severity of Need as presented in the 2021 HNO, in addition to our understanding of the targeted systems, well-established operations & high levels of acceptance in targeted areas. Targeted water systems are in urgent need of essential rehab & support to maintain operations essential for safe water provision to highly vulnerable populations in areas with high severity of need. The proposed action will deliver an evidence-based program of assistance to targeted water systems to immediately increase access to safe water, while also safeguarding WASH service provision beyond the implementation period thru working in coordination with LCs and water units to establish cost recovery mechanisms. To further support sustainability of service provision, planned activities include installation of innovative hybrid power solutions to reduce long-term costs associated with service delivery. Complementary works to improve sanitation systems and water networks were assessed and costed as part of 2021 planning. Hygiene promotion activities will continue to be delivered in catchment areas, with particular focus on COVID-19 prevention and response.Targeted Locations are below:
-Al Gazl station and Street 30 borehole: (Idleb City). Station operations and maintenance plus COVID-19 prevention measures are fully funded under the SCHF up to 30 Jun 2021, reaching 60500 direct beneficiaries of which 28030 IDP.
-Ariha City stations: (Ariha sub-district) Station operations and maintenance support, COVID-19 prevention measures and technical support to establish CRM are fully funded under the SCHF up to 30 Apr 2021, reaching 31900 direct beneficiaries of which 6656 IDP.
-Jeineh Town: (Atareb sub-district) Station operations and maintenance support, COVID-19 prevention and technical support to establish CRM are fully funded under the SCHF up to 30 Apr 2021, reaching up to 8298 direct beneficiaries of which 2026 IDPs.
-Hazano, Zardana and Sahara towns (Maaret Tamsrin and Atareb sub-district): Funding is urgently sought and requested under the SCHF SA1 to immediately increase access to essential WASH services in response to critical gaps in areas with high severity of need. This action will place considerable emphasis on ensuring sustainability of service provision through the introduction of renewable energy sources (solar) to reduce operational costs and the design and roll-out of community-led tariff systems for cost recovery. This, planned action will directly benefit 34784 people of which 12857 IDP.
-Sarmada and Maaret Tamsrin stations: (Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts): Funding is also sought to respond to critical WASH gaps in Sarmada and Maaret Tamsrin. This will include initial support to station operations and maintenance to immediately increase access to essential WASH services and the design and roll-out of tariff systems for cost recovery across both catchment areas. This will directly benefit 117220, of which over half – 76596 – are IDP.
Note: the water systems prioritized for funding during 2021 are all located in areas with high WASH severity of need (4).
In addition, funding is secured to provide MPCGs to 5000 HHs during 2021, with funding sought for MPCG to reach a further 7400 HHs. Based on CWG guidance, an est. 15% of MPC assistance is anticipated (secured and sought) to be used by BNFs to meet WASH needs.

4 - Needs

WASH sector analysis for the draft 2021 HRP estimates 12.2mil people will require WASH assistance during 2021, 59% of the population. 2021 HNO Severity Ranking estimates that up to 437286 people are in need of WASH support in Maaret Tamsrin and Atareb sub-districts alone, of which 369236/84% are considered in acute need. Water & sanitation services affordability are highlighted as particularly severe needs for both Maaret Tamsrin and Atareb by WASH 2021 HNO severity ranking, a finding that has directly informed the focus of our project on reducing the operational costs of service delivery. Our project design was informed by our recent technical infrastructure assessments of targeted WASH systems, in addition to a RNA completed in Idleb & Aleppo in Apr2021. These confirmed the viability of solar as a renewable energy modality for all targeted stations. Infrastructure assessments also identified the need for urgent rehab and improvement of water networks to ensure access to adequate water for the catchment population and to increase the viability of tariff systems for the recovery of operational costs. All HHs interviewed in our RNA stated that access to income generation opportunities decreased since the start of the conflict, with 74% of respondents also stating they were less able to meet basic needs as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic. Given the lack of income sources, reliance on water trucking as the primary or sole source of water is likely to further undermine the ability of conflict-affected HHs to meet WASH needs and further undermine the health and resilience of already vulnerable groups. As such, our proposed activities were informed by recent, technical assessments of network & station infrastructure.

5 - Implementation of activities

Planned activities and 2021 activities already under implementation will improve public health and mitigate against the risk of COVID-19 through ensuring adequate access to improved WASH services. Sustainability of WASH service provision is built into program design through the provision of technical support to establish and maintain CRMs. During 2021, we will deliver essential rehabilitation works to water stations, provide operation materials, ensure access to safe water through water chlorination and training of water station staff on water treatment. When required, essential rehabilitation of water networks will be completed to ensure all households are connected to the main water network. Technical assessments have been completed to inform innovative, hybrid solutions to long-term water services using solar panels. The installation of hybrid power was found to be viable in many of the stations targeted and will reduce operating costs by up to 50%. Complementary hygiene promotion and sensitization activities will increase awareness and understanding of COVID-19 prevention and response, in addition to other positive hygiene practices. This component will include highly targeted distributions of essential hygiene kits and water storage tanks to the most vulnerable. To further support sustainability and durability of service provision, we will provide technical and operational support to create and manage community-level tariff systems for Cost Recovery of WASH services. All activities will adhere to our guidelines for COVID-19 prevention in WASH programming.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The 2021 Protection Risk Analysis for WASH highlights areas of risk for which we have already established effective mitigation measures. In line with the WASH sector, we will ensure ‘Do No Harm’ principles are applied to identify and mitigate potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities. We will ensure that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation. Project design seeks to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female‑headed HHs) to protection risks, particularly GBV. For ex, water provision will be through the main networks to reduce protection risks and/or access constraints associated with central water distribution points. This will also reduce security-related risks of gatherings at central water points. Activities will benefit all residents in the catchment area of supported systems, reducing the risk of tension between different groups / BNFs and non-BNFs and ensuring equal access to improved services. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls & people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation & monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non‑discrimination so that all potential BNFs will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. We also ensure that all staff sign our Code of Conduct.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

We have strict protocols related to PSEA and Child Protection specifically, with refresher trainings completed regularly with all staff on the organizational PSEA and CP Policies and staff responsibilities as these relate to conduct, monitoring and reporting completed during 2020. All staff sign the organization’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report any concerns regarding SEA. Gender balance in our WASH and M&E teams will continue to ensure that activities are responsive to the particular needs of men, women, boys and girls, with female staff responsible for leading hygiene promotion and M&E activities with female beneficiaries. Our Complaints Response Mechanism will continue to be widely promoted, both through information displayed prominently in public places and through face-to-face interactions with project beneficiaries and key stakeholders. Routine, monthly beneficiary satisfaction surveys – completed by male and female staff with a representative sample of both male and female beneficiaries - will also continue to monitor for any SEA risks or concerns. As referenced under ‘Protection Risk Analysis’, the risk of SEA associated with vulnerable community members – specifically women and children – accessing potentially unsafe or risky centralized water distribution points will be mitigated through the immediate and sustainable provision of essential services through the main water network.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Our continually updated contingency plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Real‑time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. During temporary, security‑related suspensions, our field teams employ various techniques to maintain communication & support to project stakeholders and BNFs. Should security deteriorate, day‑to‑day operations will be managed by water unit staff under the remote management of our WASH staff. Planned WASH activities for 2021 were informed by recent technical assessments completed by our staff, and are therefore evidence based, feasible and accurately budgeted. We also have well‑established working relationships with the relevant Water Directorates and Water Units, which will again facilitate implementation‑in particular planned activities to establish CRMs. We will continue to establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time. MoUs will be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials & rehabilitated facilities. CRMs will be promoted for longer term maintenance & ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. In line with current SOPs, proper storage of supplies will be ensured with clear provision of care and safety consideration. We will continue to coordinate closely with all partners on the ground and to work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehab works only in areas declared as cleared. All activities will will adhere to our C-19 Prevention Guidelines for WASH programming.
