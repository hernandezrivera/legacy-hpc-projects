Project Summary

This project aims to deliver lifesaving child protection services to the most affected children and families in Panyikang, Baliet, Malakal and Maiwut counties of Upper Nile State, as well as Fangak and Akobo Counties in Jonglei State. Over the course of 12 months, the initiative will target a total of 6,100 individuals, including 2,442 girls, 1,872 boys, 1,188 women, and 598 men. The population served will include internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities.
ACRA will conduct 150 child protection awareness-raising sessions focusing on child protection, associated risks, available services, and positive parenting practices. Various outreach methods will be employed, including campaigns in public spaces and house-to-house visits.
To enhance the effectiveness of child protection services, ACRA will utilize existing referral systems to ensure most needy persons are refered for specialized care. This effort will ensure coordinated services for vulnerable children. Additionally, ACRA will provide specialised, structured and non-structured, community-based mental health and psychosocial support.
Capacity-building initiatives will focus on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) by training community leaders, humanitarian workers, and government staff, reaching 100 individuals (50 females and 50 males).
The project will also identify and register 260 unaccompanied and separated children (128 girls and 132 boys) and implement family tracing and reunification activities.
ACRA's implementation strategies will align with Protection Cluster Objectives, which include ensuring the provision of critical protection assistance tailored to the specific needs of individuals, upholding human rights through strengthened coordination among humanitarian actors, and promoting a transition from emergency assistance to long-term development solutions. This approach addresses the systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women, girls, and persons with disabilities, ultimately fostering productive relationships with key stakeholders and informing critical strategic planning.
Community consultations will be integral throughout the project to ensure local concerns are addressed in the implementation process. ACRA is already providing lifesaving assistance in health, gender-based violence (GBV), food security and livelihoods (FSL), and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) to communities in Baliet, Maiwut, Ulang, Panyikang, and Akobo. All activities will adhere to a “Do No Harm” and conflict-sensitive approach. The project will prioritize the inclusion of persons with disabilities, applying an age, gender, and diversity lens to ensure their specific needs are met while maintaining safety and dignity.

1. Needs

The immediate child protection needs in the Upper Nile counties of Panyikang, Baliet, Malakal and Maiwut, and Jonglei State counties of Akobo and Fangak are critical and require urgent attention due to the ongoing humanitarian crisis. According to the Protection Cluster's 2025 Needs and Response Plan, approximately 5.3 million people in South Sudan will need protection services, with a severe child protection crisis anticipated. Key needs include:Protection from Family Separation: Many children face the risk of separation from their families due to conflict and displacement, increasing their vulnerability to exploitation and abuse. Reports indicate that thousands of children have already been separated due to violence in the region (Protection Cluster Upper Nile State – Flash Report, September 2024).Prevention of Recruitment into Armed Groups: Children continue to be forcibly recruited by armed groups, making this a pressing concern. UNICEF reports that thousands of children are currently used by armed forces and groups in South Sudan (UNICEF, Protecting Children and Young People from Violence and Exploitation, 2023).Addressing Gender-Based Violence (GBV) amoung children: GBV is rampant, particularly affecting adolescent girls. Reports indicate that 52% of girls in South Sudan are married before the age of 18, which increases their risk of violence and abuse (UNICEF, State of the World’s Children Report, 2024). Specific support services for GBV survivors, including psychosocial support and safe spaces, are critically needed.Support for Children with Disabilities: Children with disabilities face heightened vulnerabilities and often have limited access to services. Focused interventions are necessary to ensure their protection and inclusion in emergency response efforts.Access to Psychosocial Support Services: Many children in these counties experience trauma due to violence and displacement. There is an urgent need for comprehensive psychosocial support services to help children recover and thrive (Protection Cluster Upper Nile State – Flash Report, September 2024).Comprehensive Case Management: Significant gaps exist in case management services to handle reports of child protection violations effectively. Enhanced capacity for case management is essential to ensure that vulnerable children receive the necessary services (UNICEF, South Sudan: Humanitarian Situation Report, October 2024).Emergency Education Services: Ongoing conflict has left many children out of school, disrupting their education and increasing their risk of exploitation and recruitment into armed groups. Urgent educational services are needed to provide safe learning environments (UNICEF, Education Cluster Report, August 2024).Community Awareness and Engagement: Community-based awareness programs are needed to inform families about children's rights and available support services, fostering protective environments for children (Protection Cluster Upper Nile State – Flash Report, February 2023).The immediate child protection needs in Upper Nile Counties and Jonglei State require concerted efforts to address family separation, recruitment into armed groups, GBV, support for children with disabilities, psychosocial services, case management, education, and community awareness. Addressing these needs is vital for safeguarding the rights and well-being of children affected by the ongoing humanitarian crisis. ACRA will implement a lifesaving project to collaborate with women, girls, boys, men, community leaders, local actors, and persons with disabilities to provide essential child protection services.

2. Project activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct child protection awareness sessions for 6,000 individuals, including 2,442 girls, 1,872 boys, 1,088 women, and 598 men. These sessions will cover child protection issues, risks, counseling, and positive parenting to promote understanding and safe practices.Activity 1.2: Provide capacity-building training on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) for community leaders, humanitarian workers, and government staff to enhance understanding and promote better practices. The training will target 100 individuals, including 50 women and 50 men, encompassing community leaders, humanitarian partners, and government social welfare staff.Activity 1.3: Conduct joint monitoring of activities with partners and project beneficiary groups. ACRA will collaborate with partners to address the needs of identified unaccompanied and separated children by providing referrals to services such as child-friendly spaces. ACRA will ensure that children in need of case management services are referred to programs focused on child-centered healing and well-being, comprehensive case management, and material support, including a cash-based initiative.
