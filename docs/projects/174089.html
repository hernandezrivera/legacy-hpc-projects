Project Summary

UNICEF Child Protection project, in line with the Minimum Standards for Child Protection and Gender-Based Violence in Humanitarian Actions, will focus on strengthening access to quality preventive and responsive child protection services that address the needs, rights and dignity of children and their caregivers’ in the conflict affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. UNICEF child protection project will ensure that emergency response prioritizes the most vulnerable including at-risk children and their families. UNICEF’s efforts to address CPiE and GBViE is part of its over-all strategy on prevention and response to all forms of violence against children (VAC) through adopt community-based approaches, system strengthening and localization strategies throughout the project lifespan. The present project module is part of ongoing efforts to provide a comprehensive, multi-year child protection and gender based violence programme interventions that aims to support the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict affected children, quality case management services for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and survivors of violence, and reinsertion together with community-based reintegration of children and adolescents formerly associated with Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs) within their communities. Girls and boys’ survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), including conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) will also have access safe spaces and benefit from case management, information and referral systems to specialized services as needed. All interventions will adapt the COVID19 prevention guidelines issued by Nigerian authorities and WHO.
This project aims to prevent violence against children and respond with child protection services building on previous experiences and lessons learned in north-east Nigeria including child protection response during the COVID 19 pandemic.The project will empower affected communities to break the culture of silence and bring about significant change in social norms through awareness raising on child protection issues and GBV education. In addition, the project will further focus on child protection system mapping, system strengthening such as the domestication of federal Child Rights Act (CRA) and enhancement of the capacity of the social service workforce to improve the quality and coordination of response and sustain the outcomes. The System building approach will complement the humanitarian-developmental nexus approach of UNICEF and the Resilience Integrated Child Protection Programming for Children in north-east Nigeria. The project will strengthen the localization agenda working with both international, national NGOs and civil Society organizations to strengthen capacity for local and sustainable development in the North East.Results will be achieved by providing integrated child protection case management services for children affected by conflict including UASC & delivering quality socio-economic reintegration services to children formerly associated with NSAGs as well as other vulnerable children and their families. Conflict-affected children will have access to community-based MHPSS,including structured recreational activities and adolescents’ life skills education to build and retain their resilience to shock. The project will also contribute to strengthening capacity of service delivery to GBV survivors. Child survivors of GBV will in addition will have access to comprehensive minimum lifesaving GBV services including health, psychosocial and safety and protection services and access to legal aid be. Children and caregivers living in explosive hazards contaminated areas will be provided with risk education information’s through schools and child friendly spaces with the support of Mine Action sub sector. The geographic locations will be prioritized according to the severity of needs and access

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in North-East Nigeria, now in its eleventh year is a protection crisis compounded by challenges in the availability of and access to basic services. Violent conflict continues between non-state armed groups (NSAGs), including ‘Boko Haram’ and the Islamic State’s West Africa Province (ISWAP).In addition to the general needs of children impacted by the conflict, girls and boys have also been subjected to grave child rights violations. Many have been separated, recruited, abducted or held by non-state armed groups (NSAG), during which time they have experienced sexual violence, while some may have been forced to participate in physical violence. The Humanitarian response plan estimates that 1.7 million people (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need child protection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities due to the complex and protracted conflict, with greater vulnerabilities anticipated in inaccessible areas. The protection and well-being of over millions of children and adolescents in the north-east Nigeria remains seriously at risk, with a weakened level of resilience of communities for the protection of children and worsened by the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. Girls and boys continue to bear the brunt of the violence that has continued throughout 2020 and propounded with the COVID-19 pandemic limiting children’s access to basic services and increasing their risk to abuse and violations. An estimated 17,528 girls and boys (28% girls, 72% boys) are in need of child protection case management and other specialized services to help prevent or address exposure to abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence, family separation (affecting 52 per cent girls), abduction and recruitment of over 6,000 children by armed groups (affecting 60 per cent girls), among other grave child rights violations and sexual violence have directly affected at least 49,500 girls and boys (51 per cent girls) and estimated 20,257 girls and boys with disabilities are in need of child protection services. An estimated 650,000 person including children are at risk of being injured or killed by unexploded devices and remnants of war across north-east Nigeria . Most children have been exposed to one or more of the following: violence, loss of friends, family members and neighbours, accumulated stress, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, increased military presence, divisions in societies, and lack of access to services. It is estimated that over 6,500 children have been separated from their families and are either unaccompanied or separated. These experiences have a significant impact on the psychosocial wellbeing and development of children and have both immediate and long-term consequences for children, families and communities. They can also hamper their ability to access other services, such as education, further damaging their life chances. In north-east Nigeria, all forms of gender-based violence (GBV) are chronically under-reported. Survivors (women, girls, boys and men) are deterred from reporting sexual violence due to the risk of stigmatization, discrimination, reprisals and/or marginalization from the community and a lack of confidence in the capacity of the judiciary to bring perpetrators to justice. Though several years of investments have been made in strengthening CP and GBV response capacity in the NE of Nigeria, additional work is needed to improve quality and timely service delivery, including case management, socioeconomic reintegration and psychosocial care and support, and skills building to improve reinforce livelihood opportunities. Additionally, improvement of referral pathways, community outreach and engagement, and mainstreaming to reduce risk of and mitigate GBV is vital.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Due to ongoing conflict girls, women and boys face increased risk to violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. The way in which humanitarian aid is delivered can further increase these risks. Sometimes harm is caused directly due to humanitarian workers’ actions or non-actions. All humanitarian actors have an important contribution to make to the protection and recovery of children. To mainstream child protection means to ensure child protection considerations inform all aspects of humanitarian action. It also minimizes the risks of children being violated by programmes designed without proper consideration for children’s safety or wellbeing. Mainstreaming child protection is an essential part of compliance with the ‘do no harm’ principle that applies to all humanitarian action. UNICEF is committed to enforcing compliance to zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) policy. All forms of SEA are a violation of human rights and abuse of the vulnerability of members of the affected population that the UN has pledged to protect. SEA can lead to serious, sometimes life-long, adverse consequences for the survivors. Furthermore, SEA undermines the integrity and reputation of the UN and other humanitarian and development actors and can threaten the security of their personnel and operations. Proposed actions • Strengthen or establish multiple child and gender sensitive SEA reporting channels at all levels from community to state and national level, with feedback from community. This will include written: SMS, Emails; verbal: PSEA Focal Points, facilities (Child friendly spaces, women and adolescent safe spaces, heath, schools/ learning centres/ distribution points NFIs, etc); and named and anonymous: call centres/ hotlines, suggestion/compliant/ feedback boxes. • UNICEF has designated PSEA email and hotline (broadcast on IEC materials, posters, brochures, banners and U’Reort), PSEA focal Points, CFS, Schools/ learning Centres, health facilities and service delivery points used by IPs as part of integration of PSEA into programmes. • UNICEF will conduct mandatory PSEA training and refresher trainings for partners personnel and to sign child safeguarding policies/ code of conduct for zero tolerance to SEA. • Create awareness through dissemination of PSEA communication materials • Develop and implement protocol for referral and provision of services for SEA survivors (in line with UN Victim Assistance Protocol and GBV referral pathways). This involves mapping of available services and developing an up-to-date service referral pathway (list of service providers with contacts by services by locations and a description of referral process/ service standards/ job aids to guide service providers in referral of survivors who report allegations of SEA).
