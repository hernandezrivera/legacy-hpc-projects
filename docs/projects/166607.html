Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe is deteriorating, with the economic crisis (rising food prices, under-employment, high unemployment among the economically active group) compounding the impacts of erratic rainfall which has been characterised by recurrent El Nino-induced droughts and the recent Cyclone Idai. The 2018/19 agricultural season was severely affected by these weather phenomena resulting in delayed start of the season which was followed by prolonged dry spell. The Eastern and parts of Southern parts of Zimbabwe was affected by Cyclone Idai. About 45% of the rural population is in Crisis or Emergency (IPC Phase 3 and 4) while 29% is Stressed (IPC Phase 2). The situation is likely to be worsened by the current COVID19 pandemic that has forced the nation to be in lockdown hence reducing people’s movements and their activities in their various livelihood activities. Zimbabwe recorded its first case of COVID-19 on 20 March and had 48 confirmed cases by 20 May, including four deaths (all with co-morbidities). Of the 10 provinces in Zimbabwe, seven (Bulawayo, Harare, Masvingo, Matabeleland North, Mashonaland East, Mashonaland West and Midlands) have confirmed COVID-19 transmission as of 20 May 2020. Some of the new cases being recorded are coming from returning residents coming from neighbouring countries like South Africa and Botswana. Over 8,000 Zimbabwean returning migrants are expected from neighbouring countries, mainly South Africa, Botswana, Zambia and Mozambique. This number is expected to increase in the coming months due to the socio-economic impact of COVID-19, creating additional pressure on already vulnerable communities.
The prospect of significant deterioration in the health and nutritional status of the communities cannot be ruled out unless support to these communities is delivered. Women and children particularly girls are disproportionately affected by as they are the first to drop from school and assist bin seeking food, women carry double burden of feeding the family and caring for the children at home especially in time of crisis like the current COVID19 pandemic, drought and the recent cyclone idai disasters.
In response to this emergency, Save the Children proposes to implement health and nutrition interventions in 12 Districts across the 7 provinces thus Binga, Kariba including Kariba Urban, Gokwe South, Wedza, Chimanimani, Chipinge, Bulawayo, Shamva, Mt Darwin, Mazowe, Rushinga and Hurungwe districs. Save the Children seek to improve access to health care services within the selected districts.
This will be realized through capacity building health workers and community volunteers on prevention, detection, referral, and management of suspect and confirmed COVID-19 cases, Risk communication and Community Engagement through sharing COVID-19 information using suitable possible platforms, identification, referral and first line treatment of diarrhoeal disease (ORS), Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR), management of medical consequences of malnutrition and common communicable diseases. The response also seeks to support immunization campaigns to reduce the prevalence of vaccine preventable diseases within the 12 selected districts for a period of 12 months
