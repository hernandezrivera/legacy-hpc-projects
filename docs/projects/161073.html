Project Summary

The aim of INTERSOS is to reduce vulnerability, to build resilience to a reasonable level of preparation, and to reinforce the coping capacity of communities in order to decrease suffering and to prevent future loss of life. The proposal intends to achieve this through a combination of Community-Driven approaches (to empower people in poverty by putting resources and responsibility for decision making) and the provision of trainings and awareness on safe and sustainable shelter construction.
In line with the 2020 HRP Shelter/NFIs Cluster, the main objective of this project proposal is to support the most vulnerable returnees, host communities/affected not displaced and IDPs, rebuilding lives and improve the access to safe, sustainable and appropriate shelter durable solutions in priority areas such as Torit and Magwi Counties through a combination of NFIs provision (tools) and vouchers assistance (roofing material and material for one door and widow).
The selected implementation strategy (progressive assistance) and the particular situation of markets availability in Torit and Magwi, s uggested to complement the assistance to beneficiaries with the distribution of Voucher for the roofing material and for essential housing components such as one door and widow. Vouchers, has been recognized as a cost-effective means for delivering decent, safe, and affordable housing to low-income families .
Shelter continues to represent a major concern in South Sudan. The recent rainy season left several communities affected by flood. Many houses have been damaged or collapsed, most of them belong to families hosting returnees, increasing the number of households in need of shelter. During the last 6 months, Torit and Magwi registered an increased number of population due to returnees (6,380 individuals in Torit and 2,200 individuals in Magwi as registered at RRC office), who preferred to resettle close to town instead of making return to their original rural areas, which are still insecure and lack basic facilities. These new informal settlements appeared around towns, situated in inadequate site locations (flood prone areas), are now challenging the provision of the already reduced basic facilities turning Torit and Magwi town to an emergency shelter priority.
INTERSOS gained experience of the surrounding areas of Torit and Magwi counties through 3 years of NFIs and WASH projects, implemented with different donors. Currently, a WASH project funded by UNICEF, is under implementation and targeting 32 villages with integrated CLTS approach and Behavior Change Communication (BCC) campaign. Some of these villages are now partially flooded.
Another proposal in WASH sector have been submitted to HRP in order to plan a more integrated and extend intervention in a wider holistic approach.
Where the vulnerability of local communities is extremely high, and where environmental risks are clearly life-threatening to local communities, INTERSOS integrates the shelter construction with component of Disaster Risk Reduction at household and community level. The provision of DRR and, a participatory approach for Safe Shelter Awareness are essential components of shelter programming and they are key element to preserving life, preventing and alleviating suffering, maintaining dignity and strengthening resilience in communities affected by natural disasters.
Furthermore, INTERSOS, within the existing partnership framework, intends to continue providing support to ES/NFIs Cluster, through the position of the State Focal Point and continuously monitoring the movement of returnees and related rising ES/NFIs needs in EES.
INTERSOS in order to timely respond to newly displaced population will integrate this shelter durable approach with the provision of life savings Emergency Shelter/NFIs.

1 - Needs

According to the shared 2020 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview, an estimation of 2.4M of individuals (24% women, 54% children and 17% people with disability) require Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) assistance in 2020. Out of 2.4M in need, HNO counts that 1.3M are IDPs and 662 K are returnees. A small number of host community members (5% of most vulnerable HCs) will also require assistance as they use their already limited resources to support and assist the displaced population. Outside PoCs and Collective Sites, the cluster assumes 70 per cent of the displaced population are in need of S/NFI assistance. Both protracted and new displacements are situations where people are often forced to leave behind basic household items and shelter materials as a result of conflict compounded with food insecurity and economic deterioration. Prolonged conflict and displacement have eroded people’s livelihoods subsequently eroding their coping mechanism. Host community constitutes the third most affected population in need, standing at 16%, the Host Community copying mechanisms have been eroded due to sharing of resources with IDPs and returnees. This based on SNFI distribution data in 2019, the cluster has calculated to include approximately 5% of the most vulnerable Host Community to be in need of SNFI assistance as they shared their resources with the displaced population. The majority of people have been displaced within their state of origin; therefore, the bulk of returns will happen within the boundaries of each state. A few returnees have been witnessed returning from neighboring countries and its projected that returnees both inside and outside South Sudan may increase in 2020, based on 2019 trends as the situation at home normalizes. More than 70% of newly displaced population outside will be unable to meet their basic household needs and will require interventions from S/NFI cluster.The targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and often unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other resources. Although the displaced population can have access to naturally grown shelter materials, one of the primary challenges for them remains to complement the construction of their shelter with additional materials and other essential household items, usually available in a functional and accessible markets of the main towns such as Torit and Magwi. The lack of functional and, in particular, of accessible markets in the rural areas have made the IDPs subject to multiple displacement. Additionally, returnees need more support in understanding their rights for Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) in cases where their land has been given away or reused. Furthermore, current laws and policies do not guarantee security of tenure to marginalized groups particularly women. As returns continue to happen in many parts of the country, several cases of multiple claims to land and property are being recorded by HLP Partners. These challenges may stoke tensions among affected populations and exacerbate conflict. In many cases local systems have not been created to support returnees, and humanitarians should work quickly to support since land issues are key drivers of conflict. INTERSOS looks into alternative support modalities, such as technical trainings, distribution of construction materials through vouchers, and other engagement strategies with communities.INTERSOS will continue to collaborate with the ES/NFIs Cluster, within the framework of the HLP Working Group to mitigate these operational challenges to benefit all marginalized groups. Furthermore, INTERSOS, within the existing partnership framework, intends to continue providing support to ES/NFIs Cluster, through the position of the State Focal Point and continuously monitoring the movement of returnees and related rising ES/NFIs needs in EES.

2 - Activities

INTERSOS aims at assisting 300 identified HH of returnees, IDPs and approximately 5% of the most vulnerable Host Communities to rebuild their damaged houses (by war or recently by flood) in a safe, sustainable, appropriate durable solution of sheltering that meet established technical requirements. The target number can be increased up to 600 to meet bigger shelter needs in case of large number of returnees during 2020.INTERSOS intends to achieve this through a combination of Community-Driven approaches (to empower people in poverty by putting resources and responsibility for decision making), the provision of trainings and awareness on safe and sustainable shelter construction and voucher distribution (CVA) to complement the self-constructed houses with basic components such as roofing material, a door and a window. In a preliminary stage, INTERSOS will establish in each targeted communities a Village Committee formed by representatives of each social group (as guarantee of inclusion). INTERSOS will work closely with the Village Committee creating a clear and agreed implementation plan, as well training and promoting transparent procedures and criteria related to beneficiary selection, AAP approach, HLP and to mitigate GBV risks caused by challenged gender/power dynamics after Voucher distribution. Participatory Approaches for Safe Shelter Awareness and for Disaster Risk Reduction will be used in order to develop the local capacity to mitigate shelter related risks by raising awareness and developing skills in joint analysis, learning, and decision-making at HH and community level. This process, facilitated by trained volunteers, will guide groups of 5 beneficiaries (called Community Construction groups) through participatory activities which enable the participants to rebuild their home progressively with the technical and financial support of the project resources. INTERSOS will train 3 volunteers in each village with some technical background in Safe Shelter Awareness and Disaster Risk Reduction to support the Beneficiary Construction groups (formed by 5 beneficiaries) during the construction phase. The Beneficiary Construction Groups aggregate the beneficiaries in group of 5 in order to receive initially the shared tool kit and to collaborate with each other during the erection of the walls. Once all the 5 shelters reach the agreed construction level, the group will receive the voucher to complete the house with roofing material, one door and one window. At the same time the project team will ensure that the most vulnerable families can perform their tasks and participate, monitoring continuously if additional assistance is required, in order to mitigate the risks of incompletion. The selected implementation strategy (progressive assistance) and the particular situation of markets availability in Torit and Magwi, suggested to complement the assistance to beneficiaries with the distribution of Voucher for the roofing material and for essential housing components such as one door and widow. Progressive assistance can’t be implemented through one-time general distribution and vouchers will provide the flexibility of timely delivery. Special support t will be provided to those groups will be in need of transport due to distance of the reference market.In order to develop the local capacity to mitigate flood related risks by raising awareness and developing skills in joint analysis, learning, and decision-making at village level, INTERSOS will support the communities with provision of tools for the implementation of Community DRR activities such as Surface Water Drainage for Low-Income Communities, the Village trained volunteer will guide the planned DRR activities at Community Level.Out of 20,000 individuals reached throughout the intervention, 10,000 individuals will be reached through in kind distribution, and the other 10,000 individuals will be reached through cash-based intervention.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

All the governamental and CS stakeholders have been deep involved in the need assessment and strategy identification steps. INTERSOS strives to incorporate participation of stakeholders in all interventions that it conducts. Throughout the assessment, verification and distribution processes, it will involve stakeholders, meaningfully respecting their decision-making abilities regarding needs. Arrangements with local authorities and leaders will facilitate the involvement of stakeholders at all stages of intervention (assessment, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). INTERSOS team will always involve local authorities and community leaders to share design and objectives of the interventions. During CBI interventions INTERSOS will conduct sensitization activities for beneficiaries and stakeholders. Moreover, INTERSOS will form a "CBI committee" to have an oversight on the CBI targeting and selection process (both vendors and beneficiaries). The committee include beneficiaries, marginalized/minority groups, RRC and/or local authorities, community leaders and traders representatives.In every intervention INTERSOS will coordinate with local authorities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The intervention is aligned with SNFI Cluster Strategy and within the framework of HNO 2020. In the specific, the intervention aims at improving access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced in the identified areas of Eastern Equatoria with confirmed cross-borders movements and settlments relocation.Furthermore it aims at improving the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non- displaced, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs, through an ad-hoc assistance provided in the framework of the intervention.Overall, it aims at supporting the most vulnerable categories with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions.
