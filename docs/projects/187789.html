Project Summary

GAN is planning to target about 20 camps in 2022 and the same for 2023 in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin ( 3,000 HHs and 18,000 individuals, 8461 Men, and 9540 Women).
Selection of sites will be done in coordination with SNFI and CCCM clusters, local authorities, and camp management and based on needs assessment after ensuring HLP rights through due diligence process according to relevant clusters guidelines.
The aims of the project are providing integrated services to enhance resilience and protection from fire risks, and protection risks and to promote safe and dignified living conditions for IDPs living in camps and IDP settlements, especially for women, adolescent girls, the elderly, and people with disabilities.
with the following activities:
-Establishment of a reliable camp management system, set up a management office, and provide supportive tools.
-Improve the management quality and accountability of camp managers in IDP sites.
-Monitor the provision of streamlined humanitarian life-saving, multi-sectoral assistance IDP sites through creating coordination mechanisms for providing inclusive services (Food, health & nutrition, protection, and education) in cooperation with relevant clusters and NGOs.
- Ensure taking COVID19 prevention measures with regards to sterilization, provision of PPE, appropriate to promote handwashing and hygiene, social distancing in general and during distributions, health screening of IDPs, and sensitizing them to follow precautionary procedures.
-Enhance community participation and form committees, strengthen the capacity of staff and committees, and build accountability mechanisms towards IDPs.
- Increase the awareness of IDPs residing in those camps on safe practices against fire incidents from small to large scale, causes and harmful effects; the targeted age/sex groups will include children and women.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of IDPs in the world.In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration has become an additional key driver of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022, 14.6M people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2M from 2021. With a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, PWD, and childrenToday, 2M IDPs live in last-resort sites: 1.72M people reside in 1397 last-resort sites in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates in Northwest Syria, of which 80% are women and children.45% of IDPs in NWS are living in IDP sites that need camp management systems. Camps are exposed to fires for various reasons, the most important of which is the wrong use of cooking and heating stoves, and these camps are exposed to floods and winds annually, so there is a need to establish emergency committees in these camps. GBV continues to be a real and significant barrier in the lives of many women and girls. Inequitable gender norms relegate women and girls to positions of subordination and justify the use of violence against them. so there is a need for more awareness and support for community and women’s committees.

5 - Implementation of activities

GAO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct a need assessment survey to collect up to date about CCCM needs of IDPs in addition, to collecting any other necessary data. Then, GAO will select beneficiaries based on clear vulnerability criteria to ensure the provision of support to the most vulnerable IDPs. Along with the implementation, the MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators. CLCCM/CA1: GAO will coordinate with humanitarian actors and develop MoUs to provide multi-sector assistance including Shelter & NFI, WASH, food, health services, protection, and education, with applying protection mainstreaming activities. CLCCM/CA2: GAO will identify gaps in services, and advocate for improved service delivery, especially on their adherence to sphere standards and equal access for all, particularly for the vulnerable groups. CLCCM/CA3: GAO will conduct Camp operational and Regular maintenance activities including repair of lighting, fence, and other camp facilities, and graveling of communal areas and roads, especially after heavy rains, cleaning of sanitation and communal facilities, provision of fire extinguishers, and fuel for running generators and other maintenance costs. CLCCM/CA6: GAO will Promote participatory management committees including women.CLCCM/CA7: GAO will assist in the formation of Rescue Teams and provide them with training on Firefighting, First Aid, and Rescue Principles in case of emergency.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. mitigation measures: GAN is a member of the PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN has developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, staff members will sign the policy and received systematized and refreshed training on PSEA. GAN Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies are in place, practiced and monitored. Polices cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. The Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level. GAN will provide awareness sessions about the organization’s policies and procedures. GSN ’s staff and targeted communities will have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously.
