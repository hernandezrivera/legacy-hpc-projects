Project Summary

Blanket distribution of hot meals in areas severely affected by the explosion, through local NGO partners with industrial or community kitchens and existing food safety standards; provision of provision of ready to eat packages for families without access to cooking facilities. Distribution of food parcels for affected and displaced families, including high vulnerability groups like FHH and the elderly, who are at high risk of COVID or have no access to markets. Parcels also include essential hygiene items including menstrual pads, and baby items. Targeting for hot meals and food parcels includes area-based coordination with the Food Security sector, priority for locations with known economic vulnerabilities and/or explosion-effected, and categorical targeting of households with children, female-headed, and other high risk groups. For food parcels, eligibility related to income poverty will also be calculated.
Multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) enables the most vulnerable families to meet their basic needs with dignity, and prepares them with financial stability and increased choices to access decent work. This activity is essential both in terms of economic stability while Lebanon continues to address COVID-19 pandemic and aftermath of the Beirut blast, and as a response to ongoing economic distress. Social assistance is a right for the most vulnerable, and for those with labor capacity it is also an essential stepping stone for families to pursue future livelihoods and decent work. For families directly affected by the explosion, a separate cash envelope will also be available as an immediate protection need, in line with sector guidance.
Finalizing a coordinated and joint assessment with 8 other international and national NGOs to assess the damage to MSMEs in areas most affected by the Beirut blast. This assessment will conclude in a report as well as determine eligibility of the MSME for a cash grant, the value harmonized with other livelihood agencies. Small to medium cash grants and in-kind rehabilitation to MSMEs to help restart their business that have been damaged by the explosion, addressing damage to structures, assets, restocking, and other immediate needs to reopen. Only viable businesses are eligible, meaning they must meet certain criteria related to customer base and cash flow, and have the capacity to recover within the current economic context. Priority will be given to MSMEs in productive sectors or those linked with provision of essential goods and services such as food and vegetables, or repair services such as carpentry, electricity, etc. Grants will be combined with business development services, including tailored coaching sessions on business risk resilience, to continue in Phase III.
Targeting mothers and children under two years, those that influence and support their decisions (men, fathers, grandmothers) with integrated approaches to address the many underlying causes of malnutrition so to increase access to safe and nutritious food, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), protection, and ensure that women are supported so to make the right decisions for their children and themselves. This will be achieved through partners and referrals, aligned with the Nutrition Sub Working Group members to improve the delivery of quality nutrition services, including Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices. Providing support to mothers and caregivers to navigate difficult conditions in a humanitarian setting so they can feed and care for their youngest children safely, and we do this through different actions at the household, community, health system and national level. Establishing private and secure breastfeeding safe areas as needed, with trained IYCF staff to support mothers to initiate and continue recommended IYCF practices. Training volunteers and community workers to conduct support and education sessions for families with young children on IYCF topics.

1 - Needs

Established through multiple agencies that at least 300,000 people are affected, with > 778,000 people living in a two-mile radius from explosion. SCI has participated in the joint Lebanon RC assessment to identify shelter and socioeconomic risks, and additionally, SCI's HEA report shows that almost 1 million people living in Beirut were below survival thresholds pre-blast. Food has been prioritized in the early days of the response in order to support the most vulnerable households, particularly those who are at risk of contracting COVID, the elderly, and female headed households.Cash has been clearly articulated in multiple assessments as the primary need of affected households, who are also suffering from the impact of the economic crisis and COVID-19 lockdowns. SCI's HEA report analysis shows that almost 1 million people living in Beirut were below survival thresholds pre-blast. MPCA will support these households to reach the basic survival needs, particularly in an urbane context where they can access markets.Leading a rapid needs assessment in highly affected and vulnerable locations of Rmeil and kamr el Zeitoun, MSMEs have multiple needs in terms of rehabilitation and business continuity. Small to medium cash grants and in-kind rehabilitation to MSMEs to help restart their business that have been damaged by the explosion, addressing damage to structures, assets, restocking, and other immediate needs to reopen.Over 6,500 people were injured, estimated 1,000 of whom are children, and 100,000 total children are estimated in need. With parents and caregivers under duress, community capacity has been mobilized including organizations providing support for mothers with difficulties in breastfeeding. more is needed to ensure that women and their young children receive the needed skilled IYCF support, in conjunction with other relevant and critical aid support to malnutrition increase disease among affected children. IYCF specific interventions are not sufficient to prevent and eventually eliminate the burden of malnutrition, first responders require skills to respond to multiple needs for children in the affected zones, and community-based systems are needed to provide preventative and curative services that will, in a comprehensive and concerted way, prevent, reduce, and treat malnutrition.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of hot meals and food parcels. Targets: 18,000 beneficiaries will receive hot meals and 25,000 beneficiaries will receive food parcels. 2. Multi Purpose Cash Assistance and Emergency Cash Support. A total of 15,000 people will benefit from the MPCA.3. Cash grants for MSMEs, combined with in-kind major rehabilitation and business development activities. This activity will have a total target of 140 MSMEs4. Sensitize and strengthen the capacity of our first responders to provide basic IYCF support5. Mobilize community support and participation of pregnant women, mothers, grandmothers, and men (and other relevant influencers); 6. Integrate IYCF-E counselling and support in relevant sectors/platforms where we are operating (FSL, protection, MHPSS. WASH, Education) with a focus on women empowerment and gender sensitive programming
