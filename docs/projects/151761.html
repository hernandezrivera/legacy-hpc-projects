Project Summary

For the 2019 HRP nutrition project, IOM will contribute to the reduction of emergency levels of acute malnutrition through an integrated approach for management for Severely Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in children under 5 years. Outpatient Therapeutic (OTP) services for SAM will be integrated into all of IOM’s static and mobile health facilities across Somalia. In addition to reduction of SAM cases, the project will improve family and community capacity to promote appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, as well as provide micro-nutrient supplementation and link to immunization services particularly measles vaccination. The project will ensure effective referral mechanisms to and from other nutritional services. The project will target 120,000 children (58,000 girls and 61,200 boys) aged 6-59 months living in IDP settlements and their host communities. A further 20,000 pregnant and lactating mothers will benefit from learning and adopting proper IYCF practices, with continued counseling and follow-up. The project is aligned with protection mainstreaming to ensure safety and dignity for affected populations without causing harm; there will be meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the delivery of nutrition services. The project will be a part of an IOM integrated emergency response program consisting of health, WASH, CCCM, shelter, NFI, and protection activities.

Needs

Severe and moderate acute malnutrition remains persistent in Somalia, and Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates remain well above the emergency threshold of 15%. Displacement is a major contributor to Somalia’s extensive humanitarian needs, with 1 million IDPs displaced in 2017 alone (2.6 million total IDPs). The 2018 Food Security and Nutrition Assessment conducted by FAO’s FSNAU indicated overall improvement in food security across the country, attributable to improved Gu rains in the second quarter of the year. Despite this, nutrition surveys done by partner agencies from May-July 2018 revealed marginal improvement in GAM rates in some areas, while rates in most regions haven't improved at all. The surveys revealed serious critical levels of GAM (15%-29.9%) in 12 of 33 populations surveyed (7 IPD, 5 rural). OCHA estimates that in the coming year 954,007 children will be acutely malnourished, and 173,594 severely malnourished, during the coming year. Food insecurity, low immunization coverage, poor health seeking behavior, and lack of clean water are among the drivers of poor nutrition outcomes. IOM will contribute to the reduction of emergency level of acute malnutrition through an integrated approach to management for Severely Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in children under 5 years. All IOM supported static and mobile health facilities across the regions will be integrated with Outpatient Therapeutic (OTP) services, which will provide RUTF coupled with micronutrient supplementation. The project will improve family and community capacity to promote appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. The project will target 120,000 (58,000 girls and 61,200 boys) children aged between 6-59 months living in IDPs settlement and their host communities. A further 20,000 pregnant & lactating mothers will benefit from improved IYCF practices. The project will be a part of an integrated IOM emergency response program (nutrition, health, WASH, CCCM, NFIs/Shelter).

Activities

Project Activities: Activity 1.1: Integrate 30 OTP centers into static facilities currently supported by IOM, and into 10 mobile clinics in Somaliland, Puntland and Jubaland. Activity 1.2: Identify and treat 120,000 children aged 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition through access to equitable, high-quality lifesaving OTP services using RUTF and micro-nutrient supplementation. Activity 1.3: Provide 20,000 pregnant and lactating women with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling and training, combined with micronutrient supplementation and linkage to immunization services especially measles vaccination. Activity 2.1: Preposition and distribute ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) and medicines to 30 static OTP and 10 mobile OTP sites. Activity 2.2: Establish referral mechanisms from community level to OTP sites, and from OTP sites to/from other nutrition programs— to/from therapeutic supplementary feeding centers (TFSC) for moderate acute malnutrition, and to/from stabilization centers (SC) for SAM with complications requiring inpatient care. Activity 3.1: Provide training and on-the-job mentorship for 200 clinical health and nutrition staff (100 women, 100 men) across all integrated OTP and primary healthcare centers on IMAM and IYCF guidelines. Activity 3.2: Train 300 community outreach nutrition volunteers (150 women, 150 Men) via on-job training who will provide MUAC screening, referral to OTPs, and counseling in catchment communities of the OTPs.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In Somalia's context there are inherently risks of exclusion based on displacement. However, this project is largely focused on IDPs, bring services to where IDPs are, involves consultation with local, state and federal government to ensure that IDPs are fully accessing services as intended, and involves consultations with communities from project inception through implementation. IOM's memorandum of understanding with the Ministry of Health for this project will include provisions specifying that all services provided to IDPs are free of charge, and that no restrictions are to be placed on individuals accessing nutrition and healthcare services based on any other factor.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

There is risk of gender-based violence (GBV) against women and girls, which is prevalent in Somalia especially among displaced and vulnerable populations. In this project, IOM engages community leaders/gatekeepers before and during the project to ensure maximum mitigation measures are put in place, and IOM's programmes incorporate mobilization and awareness of communities around GBV, training of health providers on screening and referral, and establishment of referral pathways from health facility/community to sources of legal/social assistance as established by the Protection Cluster.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

This project is not implemented directly in areas of armed conflict, so this is not a protection risk for beneficiaries in this project.
