Project Summary

The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) and Plan International Cameroon (Plan) will conduct an Education project that will run in parallel with the Accelerated Education Programme (AEP) launched in May 2020 with funding from ECHO and designed to cover a full primary school cycle of six years in three years in Kolofata and Logone Birni. This AEP will provide quality education and protection to children and adolescents in communities that are severely affected by displacement and conflict in Cameroon's Far North Region. Due to high insecurity and social and cultural norms that sustain harmful behaviours, children are unable to attend school and can face greater protection risks such as violence, early marriage, early pregnancy and exploitation. The proposed action supports vulnerable out-of-school children to return to education in a protective learning environment and catch-up classes and remedial classes for in-school children. NRC and Plan will coordinate with child protection actors to support families and children to obtain birth certificates and identity documents, allowing children enrolled to take part in the national exam (CEP) and progress to secondary education systems. The action will draw on the lessons learned from the previous and on-going ECHO EiE actions implemented in the Far North. Due to lessons learnt from Covid19 pandemic, NRC and Plan started identification of context appropriate distance learning opportunities that include alternative learning platforms as part of the innovative approaches that they will adopt during the action. In
addition, the proposed action will include an exit strategy within Makary and Igawa Meme AE centers to ensure that accelerated education impacts and benefits for out of school children are sustained in the longer term. NRC and Plan will work with key education community stakeholders to identify resources that could support AEP centres to continue functioning once the project ends.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In February 2021, the Education Working Group for Far North led by UNICEF conducted a Joint assessment of education needs in the areas most affected by the crisis within the Far North region of Cameroon Of the six departments in the region, the divisions most affected by the Non state arms group security crisis, were targeted for evaluation, namely Mayo-Tsanaga, Mayo-Sava and Logone etChari. In these divisions, 6 nursery schools, 32 primary schools, 22 secondary schools and 7 Koranic schools were selected for this study. The EWG used the following data collections methods: desk-reviews of existing assessment reports on education (UNICEF, OCHA, UNHCR/IOM); official statistics (MINEDUB); information from the UN agencies and INGOs present in the Far North; Focus Groups Discussions (FGDs) with stakeholders; face-to-face interviews with relevant stakeholders; consultations with national anddecentralised education and social protection authorities. For the needs assessment 62 public schools the Fotokol, Makary, Logone-Birni, Kolofata, Mora, Mokolo, oza and Mozogo subdivisions were involved. 319 community members were surveyed and 267 FGDs were organised in those Subdivisions. The FGDsenabled us to collect the perceptions of the people living at the sites; to explain social behaviour regarding the problems, their causes and the corrective measures to be taken; to give these people the opportunity to express themselves; and to get a clear idea of the educational needs. To inform the current intervention, a new needs assessment was conducted by Plan and NRC in early December 2021. A total of four communities were identified and assessed, 370 community members were surveyed and 16 FGDs were organised in the Subdivisions of Kolofata, Mokolo, Kousseri and Logone-Birni. The FGDs were attended by 192 people 50% female and 50% male) including school principals, teachers, representatives from MINEDUB, parents and children. The FGD investigated on priority education needs at the level of vulnerable communities and households in terms of access to safe, quality education in the target localities affected by the crisis. Plan and NRC complemented the information gathered with an updated desk review of data available from various relevant humanitarian actors in Cameroon.During NRC’s assessments in the Far North Region conducted in 2020, IDPs and affected communities reported lack of birth certificates for themselves and their children as a challenge. In January 2021, according to the National Civil Status Registration Office (BUNEC), an estimated 400,979 out of 950,574 pupils in the Far North do not have birth certificates NRC conducted a MSNA need assessment in the proposed project North West and South West locations in coordination with local implementing partners in December 2021. Specific locations for the need assessment were chosen based on the IOM data on displacement, information shared at cluster level, NRC field visits, the current context and actor’s presence mapping. The process began with a session with local partners for a common understanding on the way forward including selected locations, resources mobilization, and timeframe and data collection instrument that focus on addressing needs in a holistic manner. A total of 1446 household questionnaires were administered and key informant information was obtained from 46 individuals in target locations. In addition to the current MSNA done by NRC, this proposal also draw inference from some assessments and evaluations conducted by other partners. For this MR, NRC and PLAN conducted a rapid need assessment (RNA) within Mayo sava (Igawa Mémé, Manawachi), Diamaré (Bogo), and Logone and Chari division (Makary, Bodo and Maltam) in December 2022.
