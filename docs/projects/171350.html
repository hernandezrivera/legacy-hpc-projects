Project Summary

This project contributes to strategic objective three of the HRP 2021, (particularly SO 3.5 and SO 3.6 in Food Security Sector), by enhancing food security for highly vulnerable non-refugee population affected by conflict and COVID-19 pandemic in Gaza Strip through improving access to diversified, sufficient and nutritious food.
Based on recent IRPAL assessments and consultations with key stakeholders, and the recommendations of the ministry of social development (MOSD), it was found that there is a food security gap among vulnerable populations that should be addressed urgently in the short and medium term.
The intervention builds on previous SIDA funded projects in the food security sector implemented by IRPAL, and IRPAL’s long experience and efforts in the field of reducing food insecurity through cash-based interventions (CBI). This modality (CBI) proved to be highly effective in ensuring tailored assistance that meets the distinct priorities and needs of women, men, girls and boys in different age groups of beneficiaries, while respecting their dignity and autonomy and strengthening their resilience against future shocks. Specifically, the project’s overall goal is to contribute to enhancing food security of highly vulnerable non-refugee population affected by conflict and COVID-19 pandemic in the Gaza Strip, by the two essential components:
- CFW opportunities: this will provide temporary employment opportunity with conditional but not restricted cash assistance for one member of 400 food-insecure HHs for 5 months creating a total of 44,000 paid working days. The estimated number of direct beneficiaries will be 2,240 including 746 women, 746 men, 374 girls and 374 boys, among them 112 PWDs. The project will target 200 graduates (who will be placed at CSOs & private sector companies) and 200 labourers (who will be placed at small-scale farms and food manufacturing factories). Supporting the agricultural sector will improve the availability of, and access to local foods. Females will be encouraged to enroll to increase the women participation and engagement rates in the labour market. The participants will be paid average daily wages of 50 ILS (40 for laborers and 60 for graduates) for 22 working days which means average monthly income of 1,100 ILS (approx. $320).
- Food Vouchers: this will provide monthly unconditional but restricted monthly cash assistance to 1,200 households for a period of 6 months (6,720 direct beneficiaries including 2,240 women, 2,240 men, 1,120 girls, 1,120 boys, among them 336 PWDs) that will improve their food intake and dietary diversity. The cash provided will be adjusted to the family size and based on the average of monthly food expenditures estimates.
The nomination and selection of HHs will be from the available databases of IRPAL, MOSD and local actors. The HHs of the worst score of socioeconomic vulnerabilities with high food insecurity levels and eroded coping capacities, will be selected and higher priority will be given to FHHs, HHs with protection concerns and HHs with members who are elderly, chronically ill, or PWDs. Approximately 50% of the beneficiaries will be women or girls and 5-7% will be PWDs. The selection of CFW beneficiaries will be based on specific criteria like; persons above 18 years old breadwinner of a family with no regular source of income, unemployed, recent graduate or unskilled labourer, non-refugee. Nominated candidates will be cross-checked with UNRWA, Ministry of Labour, MOSD, FSS Cluster and peer institutions.
Finally, IRPAL adheres to protection principles and good practices as guided by the humanitarian standards (CHS, SPHERE, etc.) with special focus on accountability to beneficiaries, applying complaint systems, access to assistance with dignity and gender equality. Also, IRPAL adheres to PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) among its staff and hosting institutions through orientation before the start of the project implementation.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity poses different risks for different groups that have different dietary requirements and food preferences. Food insecurity is at crisis level in Gaza Strip, and its main cause is the limited economic access to food, which is driven by unemployment and poverty. These two indicators have been on the rise in 2020 due the overall slowdown of the economy, following the restrictions imposed to contain the COVID-19 pandemic, which created a class of ‘newly poor’ group of workers and small producers that have lost or will lose their jobs and livelihoods. The unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip during the second quarter of 2020 reached 49.1 per cent, 78 per cent among women. Women’s participation in the labour market in Gaza is already low and stood at less than 15 per cent in the second quarter of 2020. Besides, the World Bank forecasts that by end of the year the poverty rate in Gaza will increase from 53 to 64 per cent, or the equivalent to 37,300 new poor families. This crisis increases the economic vulnerabilities of population and reduces the purchasing power of the poor and further increase the levels of food insecurity. According to the humanitarian needs overview of the food security cluster (FSS HNO draft version), it is estimated that 2 million Palestinians across the oPt are severely or moderately food insecure and will require food assistance, and/or support in maintaining their farming, herding or fishing livelihoods in 2021. This represents a net increase of 300,000 newly food insecure compared to 2019. Furthermore, over 217,00 people, previously considered moderately food insecure, have become severely food insecure. Nearly 72 per cent of the food insecure, or 1.6 million people, live in the Gaza Strip. These results are supported by an in-depth primary analysis which was conducted by Islamic Relied in September 2020, the effect of COVID-19 pandemic on the most affected sectors and their labourers. The aim of this study was to identify the needs of daily workers and their family members from all ages and different abilities in the most affected professions under the quarantine/ curfew imposed on the entire Gaza strip due to the Corona pandemic. The study particularly surveyed the day labourers within the informal sector (including small farmers, fishermen, schools bus drivers, restaurants/stall owners/stall workers and industry workers/small shops/street hawkers) who have lost their work opportunities and sources of income as a result of the economic lockdown forced by COVID-19. The study targeted a sample of 2,013 labourers (13,226 family members) and revealed several findings reflecting the social, psychological and economical sufferings and requirements of day labourers. The following is a list of most relevant findings: • 94.4% of the respondents’ work nature forced them to abide by the home quarantine and lockdown measures • 86.9% of respondents are unable to financially meet the needs of the family and children. • 96% are in need of Food baskets (vouchers to buy from supermarkets) • 90% are in need of Vegetables • 83% are in need of house cleaning and sterilizing materials • 60% are in need of Cash support • 65% are in need of Personal hygiene kit (shampoo, soap, dignity kit etc.) • 58% are in need of personal protective materials (PPEs) such as Masks, gloves and others. MOSD declared a response plan targeting 70,000 HHs currently affected by the new reality of economic lockdown. Islamic Relief and other humanitarian actors have contributed to address the needs, however there is still a critical gap that shall to be addressed urgently for short and medium term. This confirms the urgent needs that IRPAL will be addressing in its proposed projects, which will participate to enhancing the economic access to food for the most vulnerable groups and thus improving their food security.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Project Initiation and planning: IRPAL will develop the detailed planning and start coordination with relevant actors (FSS, CPWG). Then, the nominating the targeted HHs from the available databases of IRPAL, recent assessment survey, MOSD database and local actors will be completed and finally a participatory rapid environmental impact assessment will be conducted by IRPAL and its partners. The selection criteria will focus on the socioeconomic vulnerabilities of the households and the gender makeup of the households. The target beneficiaries include 1,600 food insecure, vulnerable households (8,960 direct beneficiaries) among the non-refugee population in Gaza Strip. A list of vulnerable households identified will be provided to the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and FS cluster. Activity 2: Provision of CFW opportunities. IRPAL will provide temporary employment opportunities (total 44,000 paid working days) for 400 members from households selected under activity 1 by taking the following actions; identifying the direct beneficiaries from each HHs, nominating and selecting the hosting institutions, conducting orientation sessions and signing contracts with beneficiaries and hosting institutions. The orientation will include information on the project scope, type of assistance, beneficiaries’ rights and entitlements, communication channels, protection and inclusivity principles, PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), complaints and feedback mechanism. The 400 beneficiaries (graduates and labourers) will be placed at hosting institutions into two lots respectively, each lot for 5 months. IRPAL will ensure conducting ongoing follow-up and issuing the monthly financial payments for each lot in a timely manner. Activity 3: Provision of emergency food assistance, through voucher modality to the poor and food insecure households that are selected under activity 1. IRPAL will provide 1,200 households ( 6,600 direct beneficiaries) with food vouchers for a period of 6 months by taking the following actions, conducting cooperation arrangements with World Food Program for distribution of food vouchers through E-vouchers system. The selection and distribution of food vouchers for the 1,200 HHs will take place into two lots respectively, each lot 600 HHs for a period of 6 months. The monthly cash provided will be adjusted to the family size and based on the average monthly food expenditures estimates as well as the food security and cash programming working group standards. IRPAL will select and contract shops that offer e-voucher, quality and diverse food items at market prices, and ensuring the accessibility and safety of PWDs and elderly to the shops and highly considering shops owned by women. Strict monitoring and control will take place. Throughout the implementation, IRPAL will use a variety of monitoring and evaluation tools to ensure effective operations and to meet the internal and external reporting requirements. Also, IRPAL will provide technical support to the implementing partners through regular coordination to improve their capacity for providing awareness and capacity building activities for beneficiaries and CSOs. Whilst adopting zero-tolerance for corruption and bribery approach, and applying the protection principles in all practices, IRPAL will employ accountability and feedback mechanisms for successful engagement of all groups and programmatic improvement. Also, orientation is conducted for IRPAL staff as well as cooperating CBOs staff on protection and inclusivity principles as well as PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse).

The project deliverables will be provided in a manner that reduces exposure to risk and ensures the safety and protection of all groups, including women, PWDs and elderly. A Complaint mechanism will be disseminated to project beneficiaries to collect any complaint, feedback or recommendations.

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify :

Accountability to Affected Persons In accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Islamic Relief is committed to welcome and address all complaints and feedback. Comprehensive communication plan will be developed so that communities and people affected by crisis know their rights and entitlements and have access to information. The plan ensures both one way communications (Information sharing mainly regarding donor, implementing organizations, partners, policies, feedback and complaint mechanisms) and two way communications (Orientation, consultation, feedback and comments). The complaint and response mechanisms used by Islamic Relief are in line with humanitarian standards and policies, especially the CHS where all beneficiaries have a right to a say and Islamic Relief has the duty to respond. The complaint focal point is the Quality Assurance Unit. When a complaint is received, it is documented and discussed with the country director, investigated by the focal point in coordination with relevant stakeholders, and recommendations is provided for proper response. The focal point monitors the implementation of the recommended response by the project team. According to the Complaint Policy, Islamic Relief should strive to provide any help needed to make it accessible to bring complaints for all stakeholders in accordance with their needs, age, and abilities through telephone/SMS (landline and mobile), in person, in writing/through post and online through email and website.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

IR is committed to incorporate the protection principles in its project. This intervention will target the most vulnerable groups through strong analysis and participatory identification, and ensure community consultation and engagement in the needs assessments, project design while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluation. IRPAL will ensure that beneficiaries of different sex, age and disabilities will be presented from the early stages of the project and that women and men (girls and boys as appropriate) have equal voice in decisions affecting the project. IR ensures that staff has a good understanding of the local context. Staff who carry out assessments and discussions with communities will use participatory techniques and gender-sensitive approach. The cash based intervention (Food vouchers & CFW) reduces risks related to inadequate food supply, and enhance the beneficiaries’ sense of dignity and autonomy. The selection of beneficiaries for either modality will be decided upon many factors including the need of the family to cash and the presence of graduates or able-bodied laborers in the family. The project will ensure the gender equality and development of women. The CFW activities will consider the needs of special groups who may not be able to benefit so easily due to being unfit for heavy labor required in agricultural works or having problems committing to lengthy work activities such as patients and women with care responsibilities. As in any programming that may benefit some members more than others, so conflict among beneficiaries is a risk. Also, there is a potential for conflict within households on who will get the assistance. Another risk may be at the hosting organizations. Vulnerable individuals may be pressured to hand over a proportion of their earnings to relatives or organization. IRPAL will take the necessary measures to manage these risks and prevent corruption through firm monitoring and control activities and other mitigation measures in the risk management process, ensuring inclusive and participatory methods in planning activities and selecting beneficiaries. IR set up appropriate mechanisms through which beneficiaries can address concerns or complaints and give feedback, taking into consideration their confidentiality and safety. Also, IRPAL is keen to disseminate information related to intervention, considering the use of appropriate communication methods and arrangements for its accessibility to people with disabilities and older people

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This needs-based intervention targets the poorest and most vulnerable HHs from the available databases of IRPAL, recent assessment survey, MOSD database and local actors who meet the target criteria. The vulnerable households, of the worst score of socioeconomic vulnerabilities with high food insecurity levels and eroded coping capacities, will be selected and higher priority will be given to FHHs, and families with members who are elderly, chronically ill, or persons with disability. All data collected is disaggregated by sex, age and disabilities, which will help, improve the equal and impartial access to assistance. IR will ensure the meaningful and appropriate access of the vulnerable groups such as women, PWDs and elderly to assistance without any barriers or discrimination and consider their protection and safety concerns during the implementation of activities. Alternative outreach measures to distributing assistance for those who have access difficulties will be adopted. Otherwise, people with access difficulties can allocate one of their family members to receive the food assistance on their behalf. The inclusion of women and PWDs in the cash for work opportunities will be as deemed appropriate to their skills, abilities and will receive support for positive contribution while ensuring their protection and dignity. Only one member of a household will be engaged in CFW activity, to ensure that benefits are spread as wide as possible. The proposed humanitarian response will be provided without exposing targeted populations to further protection threats or risks, and, to the extent possible, contribute to improve their physical security and integrity. protection principles are considered throughout the humanitarian action. The four principles include: (1) Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: All BNFs will have clear and regular access to project information through orientation sessions, BNFs of CfW will be employed in safe places according to their specialization and clear tasks. BNFs of food assistance will have smooth access to vendors at their locations with all needed consideration (2) Meaningful access: BNFs access to assistance and services - in proportion to needs and without barriers (e.g. discrimination). Particular attention will be provided to the most vulnerable groups like: Women, Elderly, PWDs who may face particular problems in accessing services, because of reduced mobility, marginalization, or other constraints. (3) Accountability: Accessible Feedback and complaint mechanisms is in place and will be detailed in Orientation sessions and through the tools. (4) Participation and empowerment: Support the development ofself-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights through effective participation in focus groups, Orientation sessions at the beginning of the project, data collection and analysis and technical committees.
