Project Summary

In order to sustain and grow its humanitarian services to the vulnerable population in various communities within the target community, Silk Road suggests a 20 months direct ERL response.
The ERL packages will seek to assist those who are most in need in overcoming the detrimental effects that the Syrian crisis has had on their social and economic conditions by:
1- Cash-for-work (CFW) activities will include wastes and garbage collection activities Operation of water stations and Performing the necessary periodic maintenance through recruiting skilled technicians and operators in the water stations we will operate, consequently, Silk Road will secure short-term labour opportunities to 240 skilled and unskilled workers (CFW for both gender) plus 120 technicians and operators under the direct supervision of our team ( CFW for both gender). All WASH and ERL activities will be implemented and provided according to WASH & ERL standards as well as they will provide timely equitable access to the project services.
2- Morover, Silk Road will train and equip men, women and youth volunteers or through the CFW activity (Slight support) to provide temporary employment for public projects to the most vulnerable segments of a population targeted through this project. Furthermore, the training will include best practices for each specific training subject, and explain what safety risks they may face in their work and how to mitigate them. Silk Road will target 900 beneficiaries of both genders including youth above 18 years old. Silk Road will request those 900 beneficiaries to integrate themselves in small volunteer separate groups which will implement community initiatives in the targeted locations which include.
1- Removal of debris and waste
2- Rehabilitation of education facilities
3- Rehabilitation of access to basic utilities (electricity, gas, water, sewage) for around 1300 HHs.
One of the project's main objectives is to give the intended beneficiaries the means and resources to strive toward sustainability and self-empowerment by arming each trainee with the knowledge, skills, and equipment necessary to successfully enter the workforce. This might be accomplished by distributing the kit or by giving over some of the project's assets and machinery, like sewing machines.
As part of our efforts to support trainees and provide favorable conditions for them, Silk Road expects the local community to facilitate project activities. On the other hand, we will encourage them to carry out similar projects and activities, draw their attention to the significance of vocational training, and involve them in counseling. The success of the project will depend on the degree of their interaction, the program's commitment, and the seriousness and commitment of qualified trainers.
In addition to the priority given to IDPs, vulnerable groups like the elderly, the disabled, and women, taking into consideration the women and children exposed to violence based on gender, Silk Road will work to target all affected people applying aligned and reliable selection criteria to benefit from the ERL activities. The "Do No Harm approach" and the "Cash for Work Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance for NWS" will be followed throughout the project, with particular attention paid to the daily wage rate, the number of hours worked, and the overall duration.
