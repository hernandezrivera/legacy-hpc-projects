Project Summary

The project will participate mainly in addressing the urgent health needs of both IDP and Host Communities in Aleppo and Idleb governorate, through the continuity of current projects.
this project aims to enhance the quality of medical services through the provision of technical support via capacity building, equipment, medications, supplies and operational costs (i.e. salaries, fuel, etc.) within 15 primary health care facilities, 6 maternity and childhood hospitals, 1 trauma hospitals, 7 MCs, 1 physical rehab centre, and the provision of immunization services. Another component of this project is health system strengthening especially in light of COVID-19 through the continuation of the existing referral system including the COVID-19 referral system and point of entry COVID-19 triage system. In addition to the provision of capacity building training on infection prevention and control within health facilities and for COVID-19 and triage protocols, non-communicable disease (NCD) management and pharmacy management. The project will ensure and improve health access for the elderly and persons with disabilities (PWDs) through modified accommodations and mechanisms.
SRD maintains a strong working relationship with local health authorities, humanitarian actors in the area in addition to close collaboration with OCHA, WHO, Health Cluster, TWGs. SRD proposed project will complement the health services provided by operating partners within the referral system, NCD, IPC and mental health integration activities.

4 - Needs

As the ongoing Syrian crisis completed its 10th year, the health system was destructed and led to the increasing population under risk and in need of life-saving activities. According to the HNO 2021, the COVID19 pandemic and economic downturn pushed the situation to the edge knowing that just 58% of hospitals and 53% of Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) were fully functional in 2020.As the outbreak reveals the weaknesses of the health system including the preventing measures and lab capacity to testing which are also below recommended levels. It is essential to strengthening and continuing all pillars of the COVID-19 response activities in 2021, particularly in access constrained areas and areas of disputed control.Compared to 2020, the health sector PiN has increased 3%, increased by more than 200,000 people in Idleb Governorate only. According to the PIN 2021, 498,480 women are expected to become pregnant in 2021. An additional 536,753 older people are likely to require health services, as well as those with early-onset NCD, which is estimated to account for 45% of all mortality in Syria.The health sector severity scale 2021 reveals that north-west and north-east Syria particularly parts of Aleppo and Idleb are areas in extreme need.There is a need for standardized primary health service packages that guarantee integrated maternal health and nutrition programming with primary care health services and management of NCDs&MH conditions.Moreover, Ensuring coordinated referrals and availability of secondary and tertiary care, trauma services post-surgical care, specialized care including mental health care, and comprehensive emergency obstetric, newborn, and child health services is critical in NW Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

SRD activities in 2021 are in line with HRP 2021 and health cluster log frame and objectives.For HC Obj.1 and 2021 HRP Obj.1 by following activities: Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral, Provide antenatal care through 6 Maternity and Child and 1 trauma hospitals, 15 PHCs with MHPSS integration by mhGAP trained doctors and PSWs.Provide comprehensive RH services, Support TB Center, Provide integrated nutrition services through RRTs.Provide routine immunization services through 20 static and mobile teams.Provide case management for COVID-19 through 1 CCTC and implementation of triage system in all operated facilities.Provide health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies, including personal protective equipment (PPE) and report on violence against health care. For health cluster objective 3 and 2021 HRP strategic objective 1 through the following activities: Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services through Implementation of NCD integration into PHCs, IPC and other health topics for health care workers.Increase access to quality health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units and supporting referral through provision of COVID-19 referral and implementation of mitigation measures at Point of Entries, Support 5 MC and 7 RH MC, Support 21 ambulance cars and 33 non-Emergency patient transportation vehicles within referral system.Enhance the quality of health service delivery through implementation of quality protocols and standard packages at selected HFs as per WHO/UNFPA guidelines.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: The displaced population, especially IDP women and girls might face challenges in accessing SRH/GBV services due to multiple barriers on where/why to access.Mitigation 1: Mobilization activities to share information with the community focusing on IDP women and girls about SRH/GBV services at PHCs, MCs and hospital. Protection Risk 2: Referrals to/from other services might be disrupted due to the security situation. Mitigation 2: Regular coordination and updating service portfolio of available health services provider in the area of operation. Protection Risk 3: Partners’ staff working in highly insecure environments might not be able to access counselling services.Mitigation 3: Self-care training is available for SRD staff in Gaziantep but currently not inside Syria. SRD organizes social self-care days for their staff. Protection Risk 4: Adolescents with disabilities might face challenges in accessing the clinics/hospital to attend girls’ club sessions.Mitigation 4: Designated vehicles will be provided to facilitate access of adolescents with disabilities to the clinics and hospitals. Duty of Care should be taken into consideration to provide for the physical and mental safety, security, health, and well-being of its staff. In turn, staff members have a “Duty of Responsibility” to take care of themselves, to follow organizational policies, and to utilize the services available.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

SRD has a zero-tolerance policy toward sexual exploitation, abuse, and child abuse. All concerns and complaints are taken seriously by SRD concerning sexual exploitation, abuse, and child abuse involving SRD employees or related personnel. These concerns and complaints undergo a rigorous investigation to determine if these policies have been violated and ensure that appropriate mitigation measures and disciplinary action is taken, as necessary.SRD adhere to and implement the PSEA core principles on Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse of assistance beneficiaries. These principles form the basis for the protection of women, children or others from sexual exploitation, physical abuse, or mental abuse when providing any services.SRD is committed to the welfare and protection of children and families around the world. SRD believes that humanitarian agencies have a duty of care to aid beneficiaries and a responsibility to ensure that beneficiaries are treated with dignity and respect and that certain minimum standards of behaviour are to be observed.Children in Syria constitute a vulnerable population and are in need of pointed assistance. Accordingly, SRD programs seek to carve out space and targeted care for Syrian children. In all emergency situations, it is imperative that the safety and well-being of children are prioritized. Recognizing this, SRD will pursue all measures necessary to address children’s critical physical, mental, emotional, and psychological needs.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SRD closely monitoring the situation in northwest Syria given the fluctuated nature of the conflict. The uncertainty is the main character of the Syrian conflict. The safety and security of the staff, programs and beneficiaries are of utmost importance for SRD. Additionally, SRD has a duty of care policy in place with projects operations.-COVID-19 pandemic affects staff and projects.Likelihood: moderate, Impact: high, Mitigation: Staff are trained on IPC COVID19, all project staff will be provided with PPE. Wearing a medical mask, Maintain physical distancing. Washing hands frequently. Ensure that all staff, contractors, workers, and labourers follow preventive measures-AIR STRIKE - IED/VBIED: Staff directly or indirectly impacted by the airstrikes and IED/VBIED attacks. Likelihood: Likely Impact: Severe. Mitigations: Avoid potential targets. Using routes used by people all the time. On-going threat monitoring/SRA. Hiring locals who are familiar with the situation. Medical Evacuation arrangements. Facilities' locations selected away from potential targeted areas. A very good relationship with the local community. Staff first aid and security training. Compliance with the security manual. -Kidnapping of local staff, Likelihood: Likely, Impact: Severe, Mitigation: Follow code of conduct. Hire local contractors who know the context. Build acceptance with the host community and meet the community needs with very good quality.-CASH ROBBERY: Likelihood: Likely, Impact: Moderate, Mitigation: Using the banks system to pay for the vendors and staff where possible.-Overlap with other services, Mitigation: coordinate response with HC and operating partners. Share information with the community about services.
