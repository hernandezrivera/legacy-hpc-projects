Project Summary

The project aims to support the rehabilitation and recovery of the Education sector following the Beirut Port Explosions of Aug 4, 2020 which have damaged at least 159 public and provate schools, between minor to severe damages, and in turn affected the continuity of learning of at least 85,000 Lebanese and non-Lebanese students. The project will ensure access to quality education to children and youth affected by the Beirut Blasts through: 1) rehabilitating XXX private and public schools that in the Beirut and Mount Lebanon regions, and 2) providing learners affected by the blasts with learning kits and devices, such as laptops, tablets and internet bundles, to enable them to pursue their learning through remote means.

1 - Needs

Following the tragic Beirut Port Explosions that have hit Beirut on August 4, 2020, and based on the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE) initial needs assessment of the education sector, at least 159 public and private schools covering pre-primary, basic and secondary education in Beirut and surrounding cities have been damaged affecting more than 85,000 Lebanese and non-Lebanese students, aside from damages to Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) schools and institutes, and their loss of equipment. Severe destruction of the schools has been reported, including crumbling walls, broken windows and leaking roofs, broken desks and chairs and damaged equipment. School water and sanitation facilities have also been damaged. In the current condition of the schools and the overall situation in the country, children and youth are unable to access and attend school and learn in a quality, safe and inclusive education environment. Overall, the schooling system in Beirut is overwhelmed and in need of major rehabilitation to be ready for the new scholastic year. Rehabilitating damaged schools is a preliminary condition for ensuring access to primary and secondary education, and an essential part of the humanitarian assistance towards more sustainable development and resilience of the local communities.Furthermore, given this situation and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic affecting Lebanon with new cases and deaths increasing significantly, and based on the Minister of Education and Higher Education statements, students will be required to follow their studies either through fully remote learning or through blended learning approaches that rely partially on distance learning and partially on in-person classes. In addition to that, the majority of students living in the areas affected by the Beirut Blasts have likely suffered from damages to their homes, including the loss or damage of electronic devices, such as laptops and tables, and accessibility facilities, such as internet and electricity, that are essential for participating in remote education. The proposed project will address these needs by supporting the rehabilitation of 28 public and private secondary schools in the affected areas, as well as the provision of remote learning support to families and students that were affected by the blasts, to enable to access and participate in remote and/ or blended learning. UNESCO is well positioned to implement this project as it has been assigned by MEHE to lead, with the MEHE Blast Response Committee, the coordination of rehabilitation works following the Beirut Blasts. Within that context, UNESCO has been also able to secure, until now the amount of USD 1.2 million to implement rehabilitation works in damages schools and to provide remote learning devices to students affected by the current crisis.

2 - Activities

In order to respond to the needs described in the previous part, the proposed the project aims to implement two main activities/ interventions:1) School Rehabilitation: In coordination with MEHE Beirut Blast Response Committee, UNESCO is proposing to rehabilitate 28 public and private secondary schools in Beirut - Mount Lebanon (BML) area that have underwent minor to moderate damages due to the Beirut Blast. These schools require rehabilitation to repair structural damages (for instance false ceilings) or damages affecting doors, entrances, glass windows, partitions, etc. This support does not include school equipment. Based on the school needs, UNESCO will launch invitations to bid based on MEHE's school building standards and requirements, and recruit vendor(s) based on the best submitted proposal(s) to implement the needed rehabilitation works. This will be done in-line with UNESCO's financial rules and regulations and adopting the fast-track response approach to ensure the completion of the rehabilitation works within a three-months period. Rehabilitation of the identified schools will take into consideration the needs of girls and children with disabilities ensuring accessibility of relevant facilities to all - including for wash, nutrition, and psycho-social support. To ensure proper execution of the rehabilitation and repair works, UNESCO will coordinate closely with the MEHE response committee throughout the project period the monitoring of these interventions, including conducting joint field visits with MEHE engineers and reviewing progress reports submitted by the selected vendors. 2) Provision of support packages to facilitate access to and participation in remote learning: UNESCO will target at least 2500 Grade 12 public secondary students in the affected areas in BML and provide them with electronic devices (laptops) as well as internet bundles (2 large internet recharge cards per student - $25.27/card - alfa and/or mtc) to ensure that students can continue their learning whether remote learning or blended learning are adopted in the 2020-2021 academic year.
