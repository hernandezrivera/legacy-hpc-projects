Project Summary

Africa Stand For Children Development Organization ASCDO proposes this livelihood based emergency project in response to the ongoing Crisis and its associated impacts in Tonj North county, which are known for track records of severe food insecurity and malnutrition in three payam in South Sudan. The proposed action aims at saving lives, addressing the immediate and medium-term needs and improving access to improved agricultures and livestock services, therefore improving the food and nutrition security of the conflict affected communities and increasing their resilience to famine and disaster risks and shocks. The project targets primarily conflict affected children and their families in the internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities. The key interventions proposed through this action are food assistances (conditional general food distributions, food for assets), cash/voucher assistances, agricultural inputs provisions and productions with training packages on improved agronomic practices, and livestock vaccinations and treatment against diseases greatest economic importance. The livelihoods support will help target beneficiaries in meeting lifesaving and immediate food needs and also contributing to agriculture productions and livelihoods diversifications and building disaster management capacity of vulnerable communities. The improved agricultural and livestock productions can increase the availability and affordability of nutritious food and hence improve dietary diversity to women and children. Combinations of food security and livelihoods support will help the target households to improve their food and nutrition status, build resilience and reduce vulnerability to malnutrition and other shocks. The design of the proposed project is informed by findings of the multi-partner and multi-sectoral assessments, official appeals of the government, security and risk analysis and recommendations which have been conducted and released for the last six months. Most of the proposed interventions are accessible and currently multiple projects are to be implemented by ASCDO The proposed counties are selected based on food insecurity and malnutrition status,
The proposed emergency response is also developed in line with Cluster Objectives
(CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and Strategic Objectives (SO 2 and SO3) of HRP 2022, minimum guidelines, priorities, and considering the minimum standards where relevant. Starting from 01 January 2022, the proposed action will be implemented for 12 months in Five Payam, specifically Warrap State (Tonj North),
Tonj North) targeting a total of 234,000 people, which fall under IPC 5, 4 and 5.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in South Sudan with increased & protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic (IPC 15 Sep 2021). The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased commodity movement, market functionality, cross-border trade, daily labour opportunities; reducing HH purchasing power and food access .7.24 million people face acute food insecurity, 1.9 million acutely malnourished women and children & 1.47million IDPs, (WFP Situation report #283. Tonj north is in IPC 5 and at risk of famine this is due to the protracted conflict and the massive flooding which has destroyed people’s livelihoods and caused large displacements. All the cultivated lands have been destroyed by the current floods resulting to loss of farm produces .This is expected to have consequences on food security for vulnerable IDPs, returnee &host community. Immediate scale-up of humanitarian assistance is needed to save lives and prevent total collapse of livelihoods. Tonj North County has been indiscriminately affected by ongoing conflict, recurrences of flood and the impact of COVID-19 .It is placed in IPC 5 (IPC Apr 15, 2021). The County is predominately agro-pastoral community whose main livelihoods depend on rearing of livestock, subsistence farming, fishing and petty trade. Fishing activity remains to be the most well fitted livelihood activity. They also engage in rain-fed agriculture (maize and sorghum) once a year during the main season, from the different support the County has been getting from humanitarian actors, the population is gradually progressing well. The swamps create opportunities for irrigation and fishing activities to take place both in dry & main seasons. There is need to continue supporting the community to ensure they do not slide back to severe state. Tonj North is classified in IPC 5 (IPC,) Apr 15, 2021. The community there is vulnerable & It is hosting IDPs fleeing fighting In Neighboring Counties like in Tonj South And As well as in state &returnees from Sudan. We also have the recent floods in 2021 that have displaced more than 6356 people (IRNA Nov 12th 2021) .This has exposed the community to further vulnerability. There is also decreased access to food & disruptions in the supply chain due to the conflict between government & IO forces over control of the Tonga market which supplies As well Tonj North is classified in IPC 5 (Nov 2021 IPC). The community has experienced recent shock caused by flooding that caused mass displacement which left them extremely vulnerable. They are also trying to recover from the effect of the conflict between the warring parties over control of the area & are also hosting high number of returnees from neighboring Payam and Counties. The population is in dire need of immediate humanitarian support. Tonj North County in IPC 5 remains a high priority location as a result of protracted conflict which has led to multiple displacements which prevents the people from fully engaging in food production coupled with poor harvest due to prolonged dry spell &then later on flooding in 2021 due to heavy rains. The already vulnerable host community is extremely overwhelmed by the many returnees coming from Neighboring Payam and Sudan & IDPs who previously ran from the conflict hot zones. The compounding effects of widespread violence & sustained economic decline that have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their safety &livelihoods. ASCDO aims at preventing food shortage by providing production inputs (fishing, crop &vegetable kits), & also scale up the ongoing unconditional cash assistance to ensure the community access food & other basic needs. We shall also continue building their resilience by supporting producer groups through capacity building training on improved production, post-harvest handling, value addition & marketing .

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

We promote meaningful access, safety and dignity of our beneficiaries We shall ensure all the distribution sites are selected in consultations with all the stake holders and the local authority to ensure they are safe and accessible to all the groups targeted. Appropriate distribution time will be agreed upon with all the stakeholders and the beneficiaries’ representative to ensure it is convenient for the target beneficiaries and is not too early in the morning or late in the evening, which can expose them to insecurity and other protection issues We shall also work in collaboration with our GBV sector and other actors on the ground to ensure that we work with the existing structures to combat any protection or GBV related issues ensuring we link our project to the established referral pathways . In the project committees we shall have a well detailed selection criterion to ensure it is inclusive and representative .We shall also deploy the vulnerability matrix during selection of the beneficiaries to guide the process and our team will use the set criteria to verify the people to be targeted. The Feedback and complain mechanism will be localized and will also encourage the use of pictorial illustrations to ensure the community understand and utilize the established mechanisms. In the feedback and complain committees, we shall ensure all the groups and gender are represented to ensure the feedback is not biased. ASCDO objectively works and cooperates with the Community through their representatives and the local authority to ensure they are no conflicts arising from selection of the beneficiaries ,targeting of the payams and bomas . This is aimed at ensuring that the decisions made includes all the stake holders and are not left to the local authority to decide, this approach has build trust in the areas we are serving .We remain neutral and only focuses on the needs identified in the community
