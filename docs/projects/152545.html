Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to contribute to the reduction in morbidity and mortality resulting from waterborne diseases, by increasing access to safe water, sanitation and better hygiene practices among displaced populations, vulnerable communities, and host communities in South Sudan.
1. WASH in Nutrition: This project will support efforts to combat malnutrition (for SAM and MAM cases), aiming at providing beneficiaries of therapeutic nutrition with means to purify their drinking water, improve their basic hygiene and equip the caregivers to be able to offer/implement safe hygiene practices. Follow-ups with families to monitor correct use of the PoUWT items and hygiene kits will also be done. Medair plans to support nutrition centres with safe drinking water, latrine(s), hand washing stations and waste disposal facilities. The project will be implemented in Renk, Aweil, Leer and in any emergency response location that Medair will be involved in.
2. WASH in Nutrition and Health Centres: The proposed environmental health activities for this project will focus on providing Medair-supported health and nutrition facilities with appropriate waste management which may include a placenta pit, an organic waste pit, a sharps pit, an incinerator/ burn pit and destruction of the returned therapeutic food wrappers. Medair intends that all facility sites are supported with the minimum WASH package of safe water and latrine access, including waste management. For mobile clinic sites, safe water will be transported with the Medair teams and the waste taken to a centralized waste management site by the Medair teams, if necessary. Training on appropriate chlorination of water supply for use in the health and nutrition facilities will be provided with monitoring records compiled by clinic staff and reviewed by WASH technical staff to ensure safe water quality.
3. Acute Watery Diarrhoeal diseases prevention and response: With cholera, HEV, EVD and typhoid still presenting a high risk in South Sudan, Medair will seek to actively implement activities aimed at preventing such outbreaks including providing timely WASH minimum packages during interventions on a needs basis, adapted to the specificities of the outbreaks, at institutional level (health facilities) as well as for the wider community. The location of these activities will be decided after assessments by the emergency response team and after coordination with the WASH cluster and other relevant clusters.
4. WASH interventions in communities: This type of interventions is planned for static project sites, which have host and IDP populations. The main aim will be to provide water, sanitation and hygiene services that meet or exceed the Sphere Standards. These types of interventions will include water supply through various abstraction, purification and storage technologies, hygiene interventions aimed at promoting hand washing at critical times, increased knowledge on safe hygiene practices, and supporting latrine construction at HH level. This type of intervention is planned for Aweil, Leer, Renk, and to some extent, to some emergency response sites.
5. Emergency preparedness and response: In line with the proposed WASH Cluster Emergency Preparedness and Response Mobile Teams Strategy, Medair’s Emergency Response Team has capacity to respond to rapid or slow onset disasters anywhere in South Sudan, within set timelines. Medair’s ERT will continue to respond to disasters and align with the proposed WASH Cluster Strategic Objectives to provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and to enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living, through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services.

Needs

WASH in Nutrition/Nutrition and Health Centres: Malnutrition is a huge risk in various locations: A SMART survey in May 2018 in Renk County revealed GAM and SAM rates above the emergency threshold, with 22.8% and 6.0% among IDPs and 26.9% and 6.4% among the host community, respectively. A SMART survey in Leer County in April 2018 showed a GAM rate of 12.2% and SAM 3.0%. Soon after the completion of the survey, violence erupted in southern Unity resulting in displacement of the population and interruption of the services. A mass MUAC screening done in July 2018 (during the OCV mass vaccination campaign) revealed a ‘proxy GAM’ of 19%, which corresponds to very critical levels of acute malnutrition. In Aweil Centre County, results from the SMART survey conducted in July 2018 revealed the prevalence of a GAM rate of 6.3% (including a SAM prevalence of 1.3%). Acute Watery Diarrhoeal diseases prevention and response: According to RSS Cholera prevention and response plan, acute watery diarrhoea remains the second highest cause for morbidity. Cholera is endemic in South Sudan and its neighbouring countries where recurrent outbreaks continue to be reported with both local and cross-border spread. Since the 2013 crisis, three cholera outbreaks were reported in South Sudan in 2014, 2015, and 2016 - the last one lasting for over one year. Displaced persons in POCs, refugee camps and other areas with high concentrations of people have a higher risk of suffering cholera outbreaks. The current WASH situation in Jubek County carries a high risk for new cholera outbreaks. According to RSS EVD contingency plan, the general health situation across South Sudan remains fragile and the ongoing crisis has seriously affected health service delivery. The risk of EVD importation to South Sudan has been assessed to be very high and South Sudan is one of the countries prioritised to enhance operational readiness and preparedness based on the WHO EVD Preparedness Checklist.WASH interventions in communities: Medair is one of few NGOs working in the WASH sector in Renk County and is currently supporting WASH services for an estimated 12,410 people, including 3,490 displaced people living in three sites: Abayok, Payuer and Wunthou. Due to multiple shocks and instances of displacement, the populations of these settlements fluctuate and comprise a mix of IDPs and returnees, although host community members also access the services. These settlements now have a more or less semi-permanent core population yet still absorb transitory populations, including IDPs. Since 2014, conflict in Leer County has left many people displaced and with limited access to WASH services. The majority of the population practices open defecation within all communal areas, including near water sources and homes. Lack of sanitation facilities and safe excreta disposal mechanisms continue to represent a significant risk of disease transmission. According to the most recent household survey conducted by Medair in August 2017, only 12.9% of households reported using at least 15 l/p/d, only 28.1% were using an improved water source, and 26.6% of households were drinking water with zero faecal coliforms. Water, sanitation and hygiene needs still remain to be some of the most critical needs for many families in South Sudan.Emergency preparedness and response:The ongoing humanitarian crisis comes on top of pre-existing large-scale humanitarian needs and against a backdrop of some of the worst human development indicators in the world. South Sudan ranked first on the 2017 Fragile States Index (compared to second in 2016) of the Fund for Peace. Necessity for capacity to respond to disasters such as ongoing conflicts, displacements and returning communities is still high, especially with lifesaving water and sanitation services.

Activities

1. WASH in Nutrition/Nutrition and Health Centres: Medair will continue to work in Renk, Leer, Aweil and any other Emergency Response sites, to support interventions aimed at combating malnutrition by:Providing safe drinking water or PoUWT (such as PUR and Aquatabs) to MAM and SAM patients as well as ways to purify drinking water at nutrition centres for appetite test and general drinking use for patients and staff; provide latrines for patients and staff to prevent open defecation and to safely dispose of faecal matter; provide hand washing stations to promote hand washing amongst patients and staff; distribute hygiene kits to SAM and MAM patients; provide hygiene education to caregivers of SAM and MAM patients; and carry out follow ups on correct use of PoUWT items; Construct/rehabilitate waste pits at Medair health and nutrition facilities; install burning pits / simple drum incinerators or other appropriate combustion facilities at Medair health and nutrition facilities; train clinic staff on proper use of solid waste management facilities and conduct clean up activity at each site to remove existing debris and utilise proper solid waste management facilities. 2. Acute Watery Diarrhoeal diseases prevention and response and Emergency preparedness and response:Assess existing water points and repair water supply infrastructure as necessary (e.g. hand pumps, motorised boreholes, hand dug wells); distribute point of use water filter kits or PUR/Aquatabs alongside relevant training and monitoring; conduct bacteriological water quality testing of all water sources installed or repaired; provide training to pump mechanics drawn from the community members; provide training of proper management and maintenance of water points; provide safe water supply and appropriate chlorination in nutrition/health facilities, including WASH in Acute Watery Diarrhoea, IPC required by cholera or EVD; construct/ rehabilitate stances of adequate, appropriate sanitation facilities with hand washing stations and in any emergencies where latrines are not possible or appropriate, promote alternative sanitations; train households on cleaning and maintaining the latrines; conduct hygiene promotion campaigns with relevant IEC materials; distribute WASH NFIs and/or household water treatment products to IDPs and vulnerable host HHs and SAM/MAM patients at nutrition clinics; and conduct clean-up campaign and bucket washing campaign when relevant to reinforce hygiene promotion messages. 3. WASH interventions in communities: Assess existing water points and repair water supply infrastructure as necessary, such as hand pumps, motorised boreholes, hand dug wells; distribute point of use water filter kits, PUR/Aqua tabs alongside relevant training and monitoring; conduct bacteriological water quality testing of all water sources installed or repaired; provide training to pump mechanics drawn from the community members; provide training of proper management and maintenance of water points; construct/ rehabilitate stances of adequate, appropriate sanitation facilities with hand washing stations and in any emergencies where latrines are not possible or appropriate, promote alternative sanitations; train households on cleaning and maintaining the latrines, conduct hygiene promotion campaigns with relevant IEC materials; distribute WASH NFIs and/or household water treatment products to IDPs and vulnerable host HHs and SAM/MAM patients at nutrition clinics; and conduct clean-up campaign and bucket washing campaign when relevant to reinforce hygiene promotion messages.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback is gathered through the CGs. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Volunteer networks are used to disseminate outbreak-related messaging in emergency responses and health facility promotors provide information to those attending Medair’s facilities. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, equipping community members with the knowledge and skills to treat their own communities. When the emergency response team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment as well as the fact that the assessment does not automatically result in an intervention. The same message is consistently shared during the assessment at HH level, as well as during FGDs and other meetings held. Based on the needs found in the assessment, Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome. In case an emergency intervention will be conducted, continuous messaging through a mixture of channels as mentioned above will ensure the communities are well informed.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Medair does routine monitoring of use of latrines and water points as well as PoUWT items. During these monitoring visits, Medair checks that all expected users of the services are able to access them. If discrepancies are found, Medair will use focus group discussions and community meetings to understand, address and mitigate access barriers.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Before an intervention, the beneficiaries are consulted, and with information gathered from the protection cluster, assessments are done to assess general safety of beneficiaries. Specifically for WASH facilities and services, exposure to hazards or risks are mitigated, and incidences which occur that compromise safety of beneficiaries are reported and appropriate action taken.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity is one of Medair’s values. As such, it is an organisational priority in all interventions and staff are trained to understand what this means in how to treat beneficiaries. WASH staff are entrusted and charged with the responsibility of ensuring beneficiaries are treated with respect and dignity, and as part of feedback, through intervention exit interviews, beneficiaries are asked if they felt they were treated with respect by Medair staff during the WASH intervention.
