Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to save lives and sustain livelihoods by supporting crucial crop and vegetable production and productivity through the provision of crop and vegetable seeds and fishing gear. SPEDP will fully engage local government sector offices, community leaders, women’s groups, youth groups, representatives of people living with disabilities, the elderly and other social groups throughout the life of the project. It has been understood that communities (host communities, IDPs and the returnees) in the target project areas have limited productive assets and require immediate assistance to tackle crisis and emergency levels of food insecurity in Wau, Raja and old Fangak Counties.
This project will be building on the previous project implemented by SPEDP and will be coordinated by the implementing staffs and the food security cluster.
In addition, SPEDP will continue to promote crop and vegetable production as well as fishing as a means of diversifying household diet in order to address food insecurity as well as malnutrition, which remains a major concern among host community, returnees and the IDPs, as further detailed in the humanitarian context analysis sector of this proposal.
The provision of improved inputs like crop and vegetable seeds and fishing gears in a timely manner will significantly contribute to availability and access of food from own production. The project will ensure that vulnerable people have direct access to more food through the provision of crop and vegetable seeds and fishing gears.
SPEDP will maximize active role of women in the project in order to guarantee equality and equity that ensure sustainability of this intervention. Specifically, the community will be mobilized to appreciate the need to involve women in Committees leadership positions and as decision makers. SPEDP will be conducting trainings involving at least 60 percent women and will take into consideration women’s domestic work and maternal responsibilities. The project will also mainstream GBV throughout its programing. It has been understood that there is limited awareness on GBV in project operation areas. Awareness creation is therefore a key to SPEDP's programming strategy. SPEDP will continue to take a community participatory approach to GBV awareness and education. Finally, SPEDP will adhere to a Do No Harm approach (DNH) to ensure quality FSL services without causing any harm to the existing social system. SPEDP will oversee and analyze conflict-provoking factors to understand the level of conflict while discharging responsibilities. SPEDP undertakes regular conflict monitoring to ensure security and stability for its staff and beneficiaries. SPEDP will integrate the proposed project with its current food for asset creation programming in in Raja and Wau Counties in Western Bahr el Ghazal state. In order to diversify household diets and address food insecurity and malnutrition, SPEDP will also support synergies and linkages through the promotion of vegetable production. Children and pregnant and lactating women are most vulnerable and are therefore a primary focus in the humanitarian context analysis sector of this proposal. The provision of improved inputs, such as vegetable seeds, fishing gears and agricultural tools, will support the improved availability of and access to food through household production.
The project will ensure that vulnerable people have direct access to more food through the provision of vegetable seeds and tools and fishing equipment. The project will integrate and augment responses to immediate needs, restoration of livelihoods, and capacity building to pave the path for long-term recovery in order to enhance communities’ ability to cope with and recover from the ongoing conflict and natural shocks

1 - Needs

On overall situation in the country, South Sudan is still at a crossroads. The food security and insecurity is still a concern. For example, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC Oct 2020-Jul 2021), chronic vulnerabilities in the country were exacerbated by a number of shocks; floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis and the effects of COVID-19 measures. Consequently, between April - July 2021, an estimated 7.24 million people - 60% of the population in South Sudan are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, 2.47 million are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity 31,000 will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity across the country.According to IPC-SS Oct 2020-July 2021, it was projected that in Western Bahr el Ghazal from April to July 2021, the food security situation was expected to deteriorate in the lean season period of April to July 2021 with an estimated 280,000 people (43.3% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 226,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 54,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, all counties Wau and Raja inclusive will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. The deterioration is because of the depletion of harvested food stocks, high food prices and reduced household purchasing power because of the anticipated continuation of the local currency’s devaluation. However, availability of agricultural labour opportunities will provide some income for poorer households to access food. The Humanitarian situation has deteriorated in Old Fangak due to flooding. The flooding is because of excessive rainfall and overflows from Phom River, which has caused both pluvial and fluvial floods. The people of old Fangak are living in a dire situation due to the effects of flooding. The hazard of flooding has surpassed the local capacity to cope with the effects of the floods resulting in the need for external support and intervention. The situation in Old Fangak and surrounding areas now calls for external support so that the community of Fangak may bounce back better and recover from the devastating effects of the floods. The floods have indeed become a disaster in Old Fangak. Most villages in Old Fangak and surrounding areas are completely submerged in water. The flooding is a continuation of last year’s floods, where the water level did not go down even during the dry season. The onset of the rains in April 2021 exacerbated the already dire situation resulting in a humanitarian crisis. The floods have submerged and swept away people’s homes, schools, water points, health centres, markets, food warehouses, humanitarian agencies compounds, airstrips, food drop zones and food distribution pointsThe most priority needs of the flood-affected people, which needs an immediate provision, comprise of food security and livelihoods (seeds, tools and fishing gears as well as capacity training to cope up with the devastating situation), to mitigate the effects of the flooding. The project aims at addressing food insecurity and building resilience of the flood affected communities in old Fangak .The project targets an estimated 14661HH, 4,887 households per each County i.e. Wau 4887HH,Raja 4887HH and Old Fangak 4887HH particularly women, children the elderly and the poorest of the poor families, living in payams of Wau, Raja and old Fangak Counties . Assistance will help affected households to meet their immediate and long-term food and cash needs, as well as provide appropriate capacity building to the target communities to restore their livelihood.

2 - Project activities

The following will activities undertaken to address the identified shocks. Conducting community mobilization and sensitization about the project at the county and payam level. Project launching at the county level to ascertain the stakeholders and the community about the project and the beneficiaries’ selection criteria. Formation of Accountability to the affected population/ Project management committee. Project beneficiaries identification and registration segregated by gender and age. Distribution of crop, vegetables and fishing gears to the registered households. Conduct ToTs for selected f 150 lead farmers and trained, on GAP, CSA and IPM who later will train the 1600 farmers in their respective areas. These will be the champions of GAP, IPM and CSA. Conducting Training GAP,CSA and IPM Establishment of demonstration gardens for practices learning purposes. Conducting training on post-harvest handing and marketing. Conducting training on modern fishing methods, preservation and marketing. Carrying out post distribution monitoring and post planting monitoring. Transportation of Inputs Monitoring and evaluation of the project. Hire of temporary store  Printing of visibility activities. Conducting Covid19 Awareness.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary consultations shall be conducted during registration, verification, and distribution so that their inputs can continuously inform programming. Community existing structures shall be identified to serve as vehicles for consultation including ensuring that the voices of vulnerable groups (children, elderly and people living with disability) are heard. Efforts shall be made through other protection partners at field level to plan to involve marginalized groups in consultations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SPEDP will be accountable to women, men, girls and boys whose lives it aims to improve and will place this responsibility at the core of this project implementation. SPEDP will conduct a project inception workshop with all project stakeholders. During this workshop, the organization will share detailed project information including budget, project components, duration and any other relevant information to ensure that the communities through its leadership and key stakeholders are fully aware of the project goals and objectives and lifespan and to enable them own the project for their support and contribution in- kind to help in sustaining the project. Project staffs will share progress with relevant stakeholders including other FSL actors in these counties to foster accountability. Such level of community involvement shall continue to be prioritized from inception to end of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The proposed activities such as seeds and fishing kits distribution will be expected to meet the standard, as they will be provided by UNFAO. Decision making on areas such as what time and day to collect entitlements will be done in consultation with the beneficiaries. Such level of involvement shall continue to be prioritized from inception to end of the project. Community leaderships and other community groups such as women groups will be encouraged to help identify vulnerable groups (children, elderly and people living with disability) who need more assistance when accessing the inputs such as children, elderly and people living with disability.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Beneficiary feedback: A complaints and feedback book or forms will be used to record any complaints during the distributions of inputs including a temporary community help desk comprising of beneficiaries that will assist to collect this feedback. A SPEDP staff member shall be part of the help desk to swiftly respond to some simple complaints and facilitate recording of any complains that need further consultation. Besides, SPEDP will ensure that there is a complaint channel through the extension workers and project staffs where beneficiaries will be registering complaints for redress throughout the life of the project. Complaints that require immediate feedback will be handled there and then whereas those that require higher-level consultations will be registered and communicated back to them with solutions within agreeable timeframe by both parties.
