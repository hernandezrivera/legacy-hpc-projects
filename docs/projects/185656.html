Project Summary

Action Against Hunger will focus on preventive components, referral, and health promotional services. Addressing health needs for women and children constitutes a significant part of the overall response. Considering the needs, Action Against Hunger will implement key activities like Identification and referral of High-risk Pregnant Women, ANC and PNC, Anemia Prevention activity for Adolescent and Women, Consultations for clinical mental health, Psychosocial support to the patients of IPD and OPD, Rapid identification and referral of Malaria (RDT), Health awareness sessions for SRH-Adolescents group, outpatient IMCI services and health system strengthening at camp and host community. With vast knowledge and experience in emergency and disaster management, the project will have contingency plan to respond to manmade and natural disasters including disease outbreaks, monsoon season, and cyclones with close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders. The project will also focus on the beneficiary feedback/needs/recommendation at every stage through monitoring, complaint feedback mechanism and exit interview, etc.

1 - Identified Needs

"As per preliminary result, J-MSNA October 2021; 44% of households reported having experienced or expecting experiencing barriers when needing to access health care.33% of households without unmet needs (LSG severity score of 1 or 2) reported having adopted negative coping strategies to meet their health needs. 11% of individuals were reported as having had a health problem and needing to access health care. Among the mental health concerns prevalent in the Rohingya population, it has been noted that PTSD is the most prevalent. Symptoms of depression and suicidal thoughts were also prevailing among 89.0% and 13.0% of people of the Rohingya community (Joarder et. Al, 2020).According to SMART survey round 5 at camp a staggering 67.8% mothers of children 6-59 months got married before the age of 18 years which indicate a very high rate of early marriage or child marriage among the women in MS camps and we can assume to aggregate the risk of pregnancy-related complication. The prevalence of anemia among children aged under five years remains a major public health concern within the operation with an overall prevalence of 39.1% (threshold 40).During August - December 2019-Link NCA –Nayapara: More than a half of children in surveyed households 52.1% (CI 95% 46.7-57.5) were reported to have had fever during 14 days preceding the data collection, Approximately 8% of children in surveyed households 7.9% (CI 95% 5.0-10.8) were experiencing cough, difficulties breathing or fever, Almost one third of children in surveyed households 28.5% (CI 95% 23.6-33.4)suffered from diarrhea during 14 days preceding the data collection, About two-thirds of children aged 9-59 months 70.7% (IC 95% 65.3-75.5)were vaccinated against measles at the time of the data collection. Considering the COVID-19 high impact on health services ACF intended project for the following needs to address:1. Recommended ANC services.2. Identification and referral of high-risk pregnancy.3. SRH promotion and integration.4. Capacity building of frontline service providers5. Access to primary health care services for integrated nutrition referral Under 5 children.
