Project Summary

According to JMCNA 2021 findings indicate that 11 per cent of female respondents report that it takes them more than 31 minutes to fetch water and return: 14 per cent and 16 per cent respectively experience drinking and cooking water shortages while 25 per cent report shortage of water for personal hygiene. The population in need of WASH services in Somalia in 2022 is approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. Estimated that 6,058,603 people (38 per cent) in the severe category” lack access to safe water and proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to AWD and According to SCC WASH assessment in Banadir hot sport districts conducted in July 2021, SCC assessed more than 190 IDP sites in Mogadishu. The assessment found out that 70% of IDP the families have not access to affordable water. They go to other camps about 1000 to 1500 meter away for fetching water. The majority of affected people take more than 30 minutes to reach the nearest water sources which are overcrowded creating access barriers. The assessment noted that most of the latrines are either damaged by recent floods or overflowing that is harmful to the congested IDPs. According to the Joint Multi-cluster needs assessment carried out by joint partners in Middle Shebelle on 11th -16th May 2021 that SCC participated found out that river flash floods as a result of overflow and breakage of weak river points compounded by heavy downpours has led to massive flooding in both the assessed districts, Mahaday and Jowhar in Middle Shebelle region. This has displaced a total of 11000 households in 27 villages, destroyed over 40,000 hectors of farms and disrupted learning sessions in 11 schools. The floods have also destroyed 82% of all WASH infrastructures in the assessed villages where over 62 shallow wells were damaged, 1646 latrines destroyed and displaced over 92% of all affected villages. In total 66000 individuals have been totally displaced from their dwellings out of which 30% are women, 28% children,7% are people living with disability, 12% are elderly while 23% are men.
The project intervention will address the dire WASH humanitarian needs by floods, droughts and conflict affected communities in Kahda, Daynile, Jowhar and Mahaday districts of Banadir and Middle shabelle regions through access to safe water, emergency hygiene kits/education and sanitation facilities. The project will address WASH gaps and risks of AWD/cholera outbreaks, morbidity and mortality among emergency affected communities and IDPs. This project will target 18500 people (6475 Women, 5550 men, 3330 girls, 1850 boys and 370 PwDs) including disabled persons and 1280 are elderly persons. Disadvantaged families, elderly persons and disabilities in emergencies will have to be provided access to adequate safe water, sanitation facilities, and hygiene promotion services. WASH services of good hygiene and sanitation practices will be promoted to the target communities through construction of 500 emergency latrines (100 in Jowhar, 100 Mahaday, 100 in Kahda and 200 in Daynile districts) with hand washing facilities, lockable doors and with access for disability persons. The project will provide safe and clean water to 6000 vulnerable crisis affected households through construction of 15 shallow wells with hand pump installation ( 5 in Mahaday and 10 in Jowhar), and rehabilitation of 30 nonfunctional shallow wells with disability access ( 20 in Jowhar and 10 in Mahaday). The project will provide emergency safe drinking water through cash voucher assistance (CVA to 3000 households in the IDPs. Each family will have 40 liters of safe water per day for a per day for a period of 90 days.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

As mitigation measures of risk analysis, SCC will ensure WASH community engagement of all groups as a key requirement in the response mobilization phase and ensuring that marginalized groups are by default among the prioritized groups.WASH cluster proposed also to include clear strategies for reaching marginalized communities in all the program partnership documents with implementing partners.Other WASH partners reiterated inclusive consultation with different groups of households, mentioned reinforced complaint and feedback mechanisms to identify any situation of exclusion, and to condition implementation on equal and direct access to all communities. This is particularly relevant to IDP setups where informal settlement managers often control access to communities and influence the beneficiary selection process.
