Project Summary

The school feeding program in Sudan seeks to enhance enrollment, regularize attendance and address short term hunger through the provision of a daily meals in schools leading to overall better learning outcomes for extremely vulnerable and food insecure children, while working closely with other development partners.

Needs

Of the 3 million children, out of school in the basic education category (ages five to thirteen), the number of out-of-school-children of primary school going age is 1.96 million . In Blue Nile, Kassala and Red Sea States 46.8 percent, 45.1 percent and 29.4 percent, respectively, of the children of primary school going age are out of school . Gender disparity is highly varied across states. In Red Sea State, almost 25 percent of primary school going age girls are out of school whereas in West Darfur it is 53 percent. Over the past decade, the rate of enrolment has increased from 62 percent to 68.6 percent only highlighting the critical need for education sector partners to work collaboratively and holistically in efforts to achieve SDG4. Low education is identified as a factor in the high prevalence of child marriage in Sudan for girls . Moreover, families cover 63 percent of the cost of education whereas the government contributes 37 percent . Since late 2017, Sudan has been affected by economic instability as the monetisation of fiscal deficits, loose monetary policy, devaluation of the Sudanese pound, the reduction of fuel subsidies and the elimination of wheat subsidies have contributed to rapid inflation. In addition, the capacity of government institutions to tackle the emerging challenges of economic instability and climate variability is limited. External factors such as a relatively volatile regional political and economic context impact the country’s capacity to end hunger as it acts as a place of refuge for the displaced populations from neighbouring countries. According to the Government of Sudan, about 36 percent of the population lives below the global poverty line, with 25 percent being classified as living in extreme poverty. The Sudan 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview points to 5.5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Similarly, 5.5 million people are estimated to be food and livelihoods insecure in Sudan, per the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis for April 2018.
