Project Summary

UNHCR will continue to ensure the centrality and mainstreaming of protection across the shelter and NFI response, supporting the prompt delivery of immediate lifesaving shelter and NFI assistance to newly displaced people; alongside carefully targeted support for improved shelter solutions for protracted populations residing within temporary, and relocation sites or returning to places of origin as well as maintaining shelters which were implemented and provided to IDPs over the years.
UNHCR's response will focus on enabling vulnerable IDPs and host community have access to essential core relief items, in building emergency and sustainable shelters using local construction materials and practices, thereby promoting dignified living conditions, enabling privacy, security, emotional support, and protection from the weather elements and protection risks. The project aims to address needs induced by 1) new displacements requiring emergency shelter/CRI assistance 2) protracted displacements requiring shelters 3) returns to place of origin or integration within host community requiring more sustainable shelter solutions.
The project aims to support vulnerable households with adequate shelter materials that will allow a household to build a shelter and contribute in part or in whole to a more sustainable solution. Similar to other UNHCR programmes, the project will adopt a community-based participatory approach, integrating the centrality of protection, age, gender and diversity approaches. The project is centered on the need to assist IDPs and communities to construct shelters that are safe and meet established technical requirements and centered on the persons of concern as informed decision-makers. The project is defined by four fundamental principles: promoting access to sustainable solutions; building safer environments; sharing safe construction techniques; participatory decision-making. The intervention will consider potential challenges on access to local materials, their effects on the environment and safeguard support from local authorities. Together with shelter support, vulnerable households will receive the standard NFI kit.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

In coordination with the Shelter Cluster, UNHCR will undertake the following activities:A) Provision of emergency/transit shelter and NFI assistance to 11,000 vulnerable IDP: This includes supporting vulnerable households with access to tools and local shelter materials consisting of poles, bamboo, tie wire, nails, plastic tarpaulin with an aim to provide sufficient material to allow them to rapidly construct their own shelter while meeting the minimum standards on size, ventilation and privacy. Together with emergency shelter support, vulnerable households will receive the standard NFI kit consisting of 1 kitchen set; 2 sleeping mats; 2 blankets; 2 mosquito net; 1 jerry can; 1 solar lamp; 1 bucket; 1 plastic tarpaulin. B) Provision of sustainable shelter assistance to 4,000 vulnerable IDP and host community households: This will include supporting vulnerable households with adequate shelter materials that will allow a household to build a shelter and contribute (in part or in whole) to a more sustainable shelter solution. The transitional and sustainable shelter phase aims to achieve 5 key features:- Improved walls and roofing materials similar to host communities providing improved water proofing. - Upgraded and additional living space to accommodate the increase in household size.- Shelters with improved ventilation and daylighting.- Shelters provided with locks, hinges, and tower bolts for enhancing security.- Tailored shelters that meet the needs of the IDP households. Households are involved in the construction of their own shelter with technical guidance on the orientation, set up etc.UNHCR and partner technical staff will closely monitor and advise the beneficiaries on basic construction techniques, the quality of materials needed to ensure successful construction. The training of and work with local artisans is meant to increase local knowledge on positive and safe construction practices and increase the possibility of replication of the positive experience. Similar to other UNHCR programmes, the shelter program will adopt a community-based participatory approach, prioritizing vulnerable households unable to support in construction and maintenance of their own shelter. Cabo Delgado: Mueda, Montepuez, Palma, Mucimboa da Praia districtsNampula: Meconta, ERATI/Memba districts

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR staff and its partners are trained on humanitarian principles and the key elements of the centrality of protection and these principles are integrated in its programmes. In line with the centrality of protection approach, UNHCR shelter interventions and distributions of non-food items will focus on saving lives, guaranteeing the rights to safety and physical integrity of affected populations, do not harm principle, safety and dignity, effective access to services and assistance, accountability of affected populations and community participation and empowerment. In order to guarantee the dignity of people, it is planned to provide timely and quality services considering the background of the individuals, the respect for their culture and religion and the survivor-center approach, respecting their freedom of choice.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The shelter program will prioritize localized shelter solutions with respect to space arrangements, design, materials, access for persons with specific needs (PSNs), provision of partition & locks, in view of privacy and cultural norms. In addition, UNHCR has adopted the Protection cluster vulnerability targeting criteria based on the specific needs of individuals. The shelter interventions and distributions of non-food items will consider the vulnerability criteria for targeting/prioritizing its beneficiaries.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNHCR shelter interventions and distributions of non-food items will consider the targeting vulnerability criteria adopted in the Protection cluster, in which the disability is one of the criteria. UNHCR also conducts specific consultations with persons with disabilities in order to assess their specific protection issues and provide personalized assistance and material support, including shelter support and distributions of non-food items. In shelter interventions and distributions of non-food items, UNHCR will ensure accessibility for persons with disabilities including safe, equitable access to distribution points, adequacy of mode, size, and frequency of distributions, provision of inclusive NFI packages among others. During distributions of non-food items, UNHCR will consider separate/fast track queues for persons with disabilities. When feasible, UNHCR will organize door-to-door distribution for persons with disabilities, persons with mobility problems (in particular older persons) and injured persons.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

As part of its community-based protection strategy, UNHCR ensures that the communities are sensitized and engaged on the protection of the environment, emphasizing their responsibility for the local environment where they settle. With the environmental impact of the selected materials used for construction, as far as possible, UNHCR and partners will seek to ensure that wood &timber products are from sustainable local sources. As part of the community initiatives that will be supported in 2023, UNHCR through its CCCM teams will consider joint eco-friendly afforestation projects with IDPs and host communities.
