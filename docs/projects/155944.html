Needs

The widespread use of various types of weaponry in the ongoing conflict in Syria has left behind a range of explosive hazards including improvised explosive devices (IEDs), landmines, and explosive remnants of war (ERW). Contamination levels are especially high in areas of intense conflict, such as front lines and areas which were under siege. These explosive hazards prevent safe and sustainable returns, cause civilian deaths and injuries, exacerbate vulnerabilities, hinder safe access to humanitarian support, basic services and livelihoods, and ultimately compound the humanitarian crisis. The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries, and loss of access to properties, basic services and livelihoods. A wide range of infrastructure, such as houses, private property, schools, hospitals, markets, agricultural land, wells and roads are blocked by explosive hazards, impeding access to education, health services, marketplaces and safe livelihood opportunities. Respondents in the Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2019 assessed communities reported that agricultural land, roads, and private property were the most contaminated, followed by other public infrastructure, schools, and hospitals. Men and boys are more directly at risk from the immediate threat of explosive hazards, while women and girls are more impacted by indirect threat and long-term consequences of contamination, which tend to exacerbate existing vulnerabilities. Provision of risk awareness on these hazards is crucial to providing civilians with basic safety messages to reduce the daily risks posed by contamination. Survey and clearance of located items is the only way to remove perceived and confirmed threats. The need for Risk Education, Survey and Clearance is acute. The project will specifically focus on Aleppo and Ar Raqqa Governorates.

Implementation of activities

* Conduct Risk Education for at-risk groups: Train and deploy mixed-gender Risk Education (RE) teams to provide age and gender tailored RE sessions to at risk populations, specifically targeting children and IDPs returning to highly contaminated areas. The RE sessions will provide the targeted population with knowledge and skills to adopt safer behaviours with regards to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs). RE sessions will be tailored to the specific needs of boys, girls, men and women, focusing on the risky behaviour most usually associated with each group. Sessions will be delivered with a specific emphasis on boys and men as they continue to be the highest number of victims, and will encourage safer alternatives to risky behaviours. The RE sessions for younger children will use simplified and repetitive messaging, to assist with memory and understanding, as well as a variety of songs and games. The RE sessions for women will also include advice and suggestions for sharing safety messages in their homes with their children. A total of 57,400 boys, girls, men and women are expected to participate in RE sessions. Community Focal Points (CFPs) will also receive training to enable them to share very simple RE messages with their peers and people in their spheres of influence, thus helping to ensure the sharing of potentially life-saving safety messages is sustainable. A total of 200 CFPs are expected to receive this extra training.* Conduct Survey, Marking and Clearance of Explosive Hazards: Mixed gender teams will conduct Non-Technical Surveys (NTS) during which they will gather information from boys, girls, men and women about levels and types of contamination in residential areas. These surveys will include information about the impact of the contamination and will help to prioritize the project clearance activities strictly based on humanitarian needs. A total of 10,000 boys, girls, men and women are expected to benefit from the survey activities. Clearance teams will focus their activities in residential areas, based on data gathered during the NTS, in order to help save lives, and enable safe and sustainable returns. Various clearance methodologies will be followed, based on the specific location and type of explosive hazard contamination. A total of 18,000 boys, girls, men and women are expected to benefit from the clearance activities.This project is in-line with the Syria HRP 2019 Strategic Objective 2: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, IHL, HRL and through quality principled assistance. It is also in-line with the Protection Cluster Objective 4: Reduce the impact of Explosive Hazards.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk A: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. Mitigation A: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. Risk B: Community threshold of acceptable risk increases as a result of living in contaminated areas for a longer period of time, thus leading to more accidents. Mitigation B: Project staff will ensure that RE sessions include topics on effects and consequences of risky behaviour leading to explosive hazard accidents. Together withcommunity members they will discuss safer alternatives and encourage behaviour change. Community Focal Points will also encourage positive behaviour change. Risk C: Once marking of contaminated areas begins, communities mistakenly assume any area not marked must be safe. Mitigation C: During Community Liaison activities, project staff will inform community representatives and members that areas may also be contaminated with explosive hazards, even if they are not marked. RE sessions will also include the same message.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk A: Safety and security risks project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation A: The organization has a designated security focal point whoactively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place.
