Project Summary

The proposed project in Central Equatoria State of South Sudan is designed to provide protection services through strengthening protection capacity and resilience of most vulnerable in targeted communities of Kajo-Keji and Juba. A total of 5,852 individuals will be targeted with 3,804 from Kajo Keji and 2,048 from Juba. The project will have both housing, land and property rights and protection risks prevention, mitigation and response components.
This objective aligns with cluster’s 3 of the 4 objectives; provision of critical protection related assistance through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people, enable durable solutions for IDPs, and finally enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response.
The protection concerns of communities around Kajo-Keji still remain high with continuous influx of refugee returnees from neighbouring refugee camps in Uganda.
Through the proposed intervention, HDC intends to provide protection mitigation, prevention and response activities for the population of Kajo-Keji.
HDC protection team will conduct static protection monitoring and establish a one-stop center protection desk in Kajo-keji County. These activities will be complemented through the establishment of community-based protection networks (CBPNs) that will assist in identification of vulnerable beneficiaries, and provision of support to individuals with protection concerns in their communities. HDC as a long time protection partner to UNHCR will ensure that the protection needs and concerns are addressed.
Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights are some of the most critical issues for returnees, host communities and IDPs in greater Kajo-Keji County, Juba and some parts of South Sudan. As a response, HDC aims to conduct a conflict and gender-sensitive HLP assessment, Provide HLP awareness-raising sessions to the populations in greater Kajo-keji including major towns like Mere, Wudu, Leikor, Jalimo, Lire and kinyiba where major HLP cases are expected.
In Juba, HDC is already the only and strong HLP partner to UNHCR; HDC has been able to mediate land cases between the IDPs and host community and over 500 IDPs have received their lands or are in the process. Though UNHCR has been supportive in this exercise of seeking durable solutions for the IDPs, the needs are great particular in Juba POCs where over 800 IDPs have registered HLP cases with HDC.
The exercise of HLP is also informed by profiling exercise where HDC as partner to UNHCR has profiled over 20 IDPs locations in Juba including Juba POCs. The profiling of IDPs across South Sudan is an exercise that HDC has gained experienced just like in HLP and has been able to do missions in 5 key IDPs areas in South Sudan in 2019; Wau, Juba, Malakal, Yambio and Bor (2018).
The results of this exercise has informed most of humanitarian programming through UNHCR’s technical support.
The projects funding received by HDC will only be complementary to HDC’s projects and those of other organizations by filling in gaps where necessary and avoid duplication. In Kajo Keji, NRC is the only protection partner and HDC will work with it closely through close coordination and division of areas or roles in order to ensure the needs of the communities are met and avoid duplication and competition among partners.
In Urban Juba, HDC is an implementing partner to UNHCR for HLP project and other protection projects. Prior to apply for this plan, HDC had condultations with UNHCR to see where prospect funds received by each partner can be effectively spent on the needs. The needs are great and any funding received, will fill these gaps as the current HLP funding is limited and the needs continue to increase each time a milestone is reached.
The key activities to be conducted will be informed by participatory assessments conducted with the community in need before start of the project.

1 - Needs

HDC through analysis of reports from other humanitarian organizations (South Sudan IPC Analysis Sept 2019, South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot, Unicef South Sudan Humanitarian Situation, use of HDC local knowledge as a national NGO and with its static presence in Central Equatoria , Jonglei, Upper Nile (Malakal, Maban, Kodok, Aburoch), Lakes state (Awerial), Western Bahar El Ghazal (Wau), Eastern Equatoria (Torit) and Unity (Koch and Pariang) has found secondary displaced, protracted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities who are the most conflict affected groups in Kajo Keji and Juba, to be in need of protection and HLP awareness and support.The protection concerns of communities around Kajo-Keji are remain high with continuous influx of refugee returnees from neighbouring refugee camps in Uganda. The community have however experienced relative peace in Kajo-keji after the signing of the R-ARCSS, however the increasing population in Kajo Keji is facing heightened protection risks as the government is not yet stabilized and there are few protection partners and activities. Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights are some of the most critical issues for returnees, host communities and IDPs in greater Kajo-Keji County, Juba and some parts of South Sudan. HDC through its experience in HLP project implementation in Juba, Malakal, Bor and return monitoring activities has observed a high rate of returns from across the borders and within the borders of South Sudan to their areas of originMajor towns in Kajo Keji like Mere, Wudu, Leikor, Jalimo, Lire and kinyiba are where major HLP cases are expected. These were upcoming urban centers within the county previously characterized with issues of land conflicts as a result of the growing population and commercialization of land/plots for business activities before the crisis. Now that the population returns from exile after more than three years, it is expected that HLP Issues will rise and become more sensitive.

2 - Activities

-Protection monitoring is aimed at the identification of protection risks affecting persons of concern. It is an integral activity that informs HDC’s response gearing activities towards minimizing protection risks for returnees and displaced persons in Kajo-Keji. -HDC will conduct protection monitoring through static presence; Payam, Boma visits, FGDs, and KIIs to ensure that the protection monitoring activities undertaken captures the understanding of the protection environment in the area. Through generating information about the protection needs of the population, this systematic and structured methodology helps detect trends, quantify events and incidents, and to inform accurate advocacy and follow up actions in accordance with the principles of protection information management. HDC will produce detailed monitoring reports that will be shared (appropriately) widely with the clusters and relevant response actors. -HDC will establish four (4) CBPNs and provide support through training on protection and SGBV awareness-raising. The CPBN members will facilitate advocacy and information sharing to humanitarian actors when strengthened through this project capacity-building efforts. These individuals will be provided with skills and tools to strengthen the resilience of their communities through enhancing community preparedness to mitigate protection risks by implementing risk reduction activities, establishing community-based referral mechanisms and addressing negative coping mechanisms. AGDM principles e.g inclusion of youth, women, elderly and persons with disabilities will be considered. -HDC will identify and register people with specific needs (PSNs), provide referrals, and direct support to PSNs with identified needs who have not gotten support from other service providers. Identification of these individuals is carried out through static protection outreach monitoring visits, the established static one-stop center protection desks, referrals from other actors, community-based protection groups, PSN Screening on arrival and during distributions. -HDC will provide individual protection assistance (IPA) to individuals identified with protection risks. The assistance is aimed at preventing, reducing, mitigating exposure to protection risks, limiting the effects of violations on victims, which can be addressed with individual protection assistance. It is designed to primarily provide one-time support. The IPA assistance provided to individuals can comprise either in-kind or cash assistance depending on the specific case and will be determined according to the cluster guidance. -Referral services are geared at reducing protection risks of individuals. HDC will establish a multiple referral pathway to identify PSNs in need of specialized services. The process will include: identification & registration of cases, internal/external referral of the cases, follow up and case closure. When HDC identifies PSN in need of specialized services such as psychosocial support, child protection services or access to post-rape treatment. HDC will use referral mechanisms established in collaboration with the Protection Cluster to ensure that the necessary services are provided to individuals identified. -HDC will recruit one HLP legal officer for Kajo-keji to use HDC's expertise and experience from UNHCR HLP funded projects in Juba, Malakal, and Bor with support from HDC experienced HLP team to conduct HLP assessment and analyze HLP current issues and perceptions facing returnees and IDPs. -HDC will conduct HLP training for local government & customary authorities to increase their knowledge on statutory laws, acquire skills to identify and respond to HLP challenges. HDC will conduct HLP awareness-raising information sessions. -HDC will also provide HLP legal counseling and assistance to communities in Kajo-Keji and Juba through HLP mobile legal aid clinics, where the population receives HLP legal counseling services and assistance.
