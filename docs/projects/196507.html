Project Summary

UNHCR as a lead agency coordinating the NFI responses in Syria and from Gaziantep (Turkey), will provide through this project the lifesaving and life sustaining NFI support, through two distribution activities of in kind NFIs 1) Core relief items 2) seasonal supplementary items targeting the most vulnerable returnees, IDPs (Internal Displaced Persons), and affected host communities. 70% of our intervention is directed to the returnees, 30% to the IDPs and affected host communities of which 80% for the IDPs and 20% for the affected host communities.
The project is in line with both the priorities of the HRP and Sector Strategy and the project will be completed in line with Sector guidance. UNHCR’s NFI programme recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group through a range of NFI interventions. Emergency NFI response will be provided through inter-agency convoy deliveries to hard-to-reach locations, NW Syria, NE Syria situations and camp context and through distribution of seasonal and complementary items. UNHCR planning to allocate almost 20,000 kits as contingency planning for emergency response in 2023
This project aims to assist some 2.2 million individuals with emergency NFIs kits and seasonal NFI kits (winter/summer items). The response will be implemented from Turkey with targeting 35,000 HH (20,000 HH CRIs and 15,000 HH CRI winter kits) and from inside Syria with 2,177,500 (956,125 HH CRIs and 1,221,375 HH CRI winter kits). The overall project targets for two types of assistance package, however a portion of beneficiaries will receive both types of kits, depending on their needs and the season in which assistance is provided. NFI kits will be provided to IDPs, returnee families and host communities, in line with vulnerability criteria. A standard winter kit will be provided as one-time distribution in addition to the NFIs. UNHCR planning to provide cash for NFI in various locations based on the outcome of the initial pilot project being implemented. The Cash for NFI project is one-off NFI assistance to PoCs in various locations and as it will be expanded based on the pilot project outcome and it will take for the planning year up to 10% of the total budget allocated for NFI provision.

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 15 percent to 5.7 million. With funding shortages currently at a historic low, the NFI sectors’ limited response in 2022 is further expected to exacerbate needs as 2023 unfolds. Key NFI items such as winter clothing, thermal blankets, and heaters are essential and accessible yet unaffordable to most individuals.Evidence from 2022 already points to this assumption/expectation, as the average monthly shelter and NFI expenditure has risen to 26% and is further expected to rise in 2023 forcing the population to rely more on humanitarian assistance. UNHCR’s NFI response will focus on newly displaced populations, areas hosting a high number of new returns, as well as PoCs in camps and settlements, UNHCR will give special attention to the needs in NES as it is estimated during 2023 almost 50,000 Ind will be supported with NFI and winterization kits. UNHCR also is planning to support NWS IA cross-line with almost 5,000 NFI kits. UNHCR initial cash feasibility assessment’s findings show that cash-based assistance would be feasible and major market would be able to respond to increased demand as it has good capacity to absorb the increase in demand. It is planned to be aligned with the targeted HHs' preference who already reported using markets as their main source for purchasing NFIs.

4 - Implementation of activities

Resources permit UNHCR plans for the provision of core and seasonal supplementary NFIs to over 442.500 households from inside Syria including a contingency stock that can cover the need of 20,000 HH will be maintained for any emerging needs inside Syria. UNHCR also plans for the provision of CRI items to cover 35,000 Households through a cross-border modality. A core relief items kit includes blankets, mattresses, sleeping mats, plastic sheets, jerry cans, plastic buckets, and solar lamps amongst others. Vulnerable groups such as unaccompanied minors, women, and elders, and female-headed households physically or mentally challenged, will be prioritized. Identification of the beneficiaries is conducted by UNHCR FOs in close coordination and reference from authorities and sector partners to minimize gaps and avoid duplication. UNHCR will provide regular detailed 4Ws and with its partners will conduct regular PDMs and feedback will be obtained from POCs through compliant boxes and while monitoring distribution sites. In 2023 UNHCR will advocate to monetize part of its assistance through cash for NFI intervention as a second modality. New CBI SOPs are being developed for projects planned. UNHCR procures the CRIs through the current Frame-agreements signed with several suppliers the items will be delivered to Syria’s port and following inspection will be transported to UNHCR warehouses.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria are used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post-distribution monitoring, and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tension which may arise. Risk 2: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply the sector criteria in the selection process. Emphasize the importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism. Risk 3: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Mitigating measures: all staff members are required to sign and comply with the Code of Conduct, and also ensure procedures are in place to report perpetrators.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closelywith other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA networkto ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regardto the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition tomandatory training for its staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment hasbecomemandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet thestandard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership withUNHCR in the following year.In order to address the needs of the most vulnerable children in Syria, UNHCR will continue prioritizing children in its support through partners’ assessment, community centers referrals and other main child protection actors to support children and prioritizing separated/unaccompanied children.As an active member of the CP Sub-Sector Working Group Lead by UNICEF, UNHCR with other main child protection actors will work together to insure that child receive appropriate response services through a harmonized referral system across child protection actors.
