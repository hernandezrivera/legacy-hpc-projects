Project Summary

Our organization aims to improve access to basic preventive and curative healthcare services for internally displaced and conflict-affected populations in targeted areas in NWS through the provision of life-saving emergency, primary, and secondary healthcare services including sexual and reproductive healthcare and mental health services. Programs will be delivered through support to one maternity and child hospital and nine static primary healthcare clinics. Two ambulances will also provide transportation for emergency cases and those in need of more specialized medical services. The supported PHCs will provide primary health care services including treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses and injuries; the provision of essential medicines including contraceptives and treatment for chronic diseases; and management of communicable and non-communicable diseases and health education. The PHCs will also provide basic reproductive health services including antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning, and syndromic management of STIs. PHCs will operate a referral system for cases to secondary and tertiary service hospitals. Health activities will maintain and ensure sustaining and expanding essential, quality health services within communities through primary and secondary health care to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups to both IDPs and host communities. In addition, the community health care workers will provide health education and referrals for a wide range of services, support and assist communities, families, and individuals with preventive health measures, and gain access to appropriate curative health and social services. The maternity and new-born care hospitals in Idleb City will provide Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (CEmONC) and outpatient reproductive services including antenatal, postnatal care, and family planning.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in northern Syria has significantly increased in waterborne diseases and disease vectors and strained existing surveillance and response systems. The ongoing socio-economic and political crises continues to impact the health system and patients alike – resulting in increased needs such as mental health support, affordable healthcare and treatment, and disruption in basic supply chains of life-saving medicines and supplies.12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years – including an estimated 503,000 live births expected, and 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years). Just over 500,000 older people will require inclusive health services, as well as those with early onset non-communicable diseases (NCDs) which are estimated to account for 45 per cent of all mortality in Syria. Youth and young persons, which comprise 1.98 million of the health sector PiN, are also reporting increasing stress and mental health concerns. NGO conducted Multisector Needs Assessment (MSNA), in January 2021, confirmed that essential primary and secondary health services, basic Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), and pediatric care remain critical to addressing the health needs of affected populations.

5 - Implementation of activities

Our organization aims to increase access to life saving and life sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need. Our organization will provide support to nine static PHCs (Atmeh PHC 1, Atmeh PHC 2, Atmeh PHC 3, Green Idleb PHC, Kafr Yahmol PHC, Abi Zar PHC, Hafserjah PHC, Zoghra PHC, and Al Rayan PHC) which will include: capacity-building through sustained technical support, provision of pharmaceuticals, consumables, and equipment, and disbursal of staff incentives and funds to cover facility running costs. The supported PHCs will serve as a PHC-Unit and center in accordance with WHO PHC standards/levels. Moreover, the supported maternity and new-born care hospitals in Idleb City will provide (CEmONC) services. The medical activities are monitored by experienced Field Medical Officers (FMOs) who conduct regular supervisory field visits to the supported health facilities on a weekly basis using consolidated checklists. Our organization will implement outreach health activities through 72 community health workers from the targeted communities across the program sites in Atmeh Camp, Idleb City, and Kafr Yahmoul. In response to COVID-19, our organization will continue incorporating strict precaution measures to ensure staff and beneficiaries’ safety and to minimize exposure and disease transmission (i.e. conducting triage and screening at the entrance of each facility)

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The population, including victims, do not report allegations of SEA which results in the organization being unable to refer the allegation to investigation and hold personnel to account and assist victims.Greater difficulties in accessing health services, as well as increased burdens and separation from caregivers (due to quarantines, or severe illness/death), may lead to SEA against children, in particular girls (including child/forced marriage).• Our organization has prepared a safeguarding action plan to promote and make sure safeguarding measures are in place.•Our Safeguarding Policy has being distributed for all staff to read and acknowledge.•Our organization delivers MAST training which includes SEA training for new hires and an annual refresher for all staff.• Our organization has safeguarding focal points in the field for the staff to revert to in case of any concern, question, or complaint.• Our organization has safeguarding posters placed in all of our facilities along with the several complaint hotlines that staff or beneficiaries can use.• Our Protection Team delivers training sessions for all staff for the safeguarding and the referral pathways.• Our Protection Team conducts safeguarding risk assessment for all the facilities.•Safeguarding measures are implemented in the recruitment processes to make sure suitable candidates are hired.
