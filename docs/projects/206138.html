Project Summary

At least 5.1 million people are in need of protection services in South Sudan in 2023. Boys and girls continue to face various risks such as violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and are in acute need of immediate child protection. Protracted conflict compounded by prevalent cyclical waves of inter/ intra communal violence, revenge killings, children and women abduction including forceful recruitments and cattle related fighting in Panyijiar, Rubkona, Aweil East and Pibor, continue to negatively impact vulnerable populations, particularly among girls and boys who have witnessed various atrocities and at risk of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety disorders. This has been coupled with loss of friends and family members further affecting mental health and emotional development of children.
These have been further exacerbated by recurrent climatic extremes including yearly flooding extremes and prolonged dry spells with worse affected counties including Rubkona and Panyijiar where flood waters have persisted. This coupled with unprecedented food insecurity levels; with children (boys and girls), men and women with disabilities facing greater challenges to access services. The November 2023 IPC report indicate at least 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition and 1.16 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition. This coupled with deteriorating macro-economic conditions and high cost of food and other essential commodities has further contributed to negative coping strategies including child labour and early and forced marriages (UNICEF,2023; IPC November 2023) in addition to increasing cases of both forceful and voluntary recruitment of children, with recent reports in Unity (UNMISS, 2023). Most vulnerable and displaced populations’ needs are further worsened by protection concerns including GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse, predominantly among girls and women with an uptick in cases of GBV cases in 2022 and 2023; which are often under-reported due to stigma and lack of legal assistance. Women and girls continue to face increased risks of harassment, assault and sexual violence, early marriages with estimated that 52 percent of girls are married between the age of 15 and 18 (Child Rights Coalition, 2016), poor rule of law which continues to prevent perpetrators from being held accountable for violations.
Proposed project which will target 20,000 beneficiaries comprised of 2000 men, 2000women, 8000 boys and 8000 girls including people with special needs/ disabilities. Proposed initiatives, which in line key Child Protection Area of Responsibility objectives: Vulnerable and at risk girls, boys, men and women including children with disabilities have improved access to quality lifesaving child protection; and Reduce the suffering of boys , girls ,women and men including children with disabilities who are at risk or experience violence ,abuse ,exploitation and neglect including GBV through provision of specialized/life-saving child protection and multi sectoral services. Proposed action will respond to the critical child protection needs via child rights and child protection awareness, including strengthened psychosocial support, case management and strengthening community based child protection networks including families to prevent and respond to violence against children and other protection concerns including recruitment and use of children in armed forces and groups, family tracing and re-unification, child protection response monitoring, support and referrals among others.. The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, ensuring conflict sensitive programming by upholding Do No Harm ethos.

These have been further exacerbated by the Sudan conflict with increasing displacements of people including boys and girls into South Sudan border areas such as Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal among others further increasing humanitarian needs in the country (OCHA, 2023). 2. Project activities

• Identify, document and conduct family tracing and reunification services and provide support services targeting unaccompanied and Separated 100 girls and boys including those with disabilities• Provide focused and non-focused Mental Health and Psycho-social support and other related services targeting 8000 girls and 8000 boys including those with disabilities.• Identify 200 girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities at risk mental health and psychological distress and provide them with individual and specialized case management services that meets their unique needs •Mobilize 30 women and 30 men and train them on child protection in emergencies (CPiE)including case management, PSS, etc) • Identify, verify and register in collaboration with UNICEF and NDDR at least 180 CAFAAGs anticipated to be released/ released in mop up exercise and provide them with initial socioeconomic material/ livelihood support and reintegration support. • Conduct mapping and establishment of functional / existing referral mechanisms among various multisectoral platforms. • Map and established including follow ups via referral pathways for multi-sectoral support • Carry out awareness raising sessions on child protection and psychosocial support including those with disabilities among targeted 20,000 • Monthly protection monitoring, assessment, information sharing / reporting, awareness raising & mainstreaming in the targeted counties.
