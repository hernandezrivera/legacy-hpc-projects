Project Summary

This project purpose to provide nutrition specific interventions; treatment of severe acute malnutrition for children with and without medical complications in children under five, Moderate Acute Malnutrition among children under five and Supplementary Feeding Program for PLW and other other vulnerable persons as well as IYCF in Emergency. The project will also have nutrition sensitive interventions through Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) in MIYCN, campaign-based Vitamin A suppplementation and deworming while mainstreaming WASH, Livelihood support, Health and Protection.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to grapple with persistent nutrition insecurity stemming largely from disabling socio-economic and political conditions, exacerbated by a long history of shocks, including years of conflict, droughts, and flooding, as well as suboptimal maternal and childcare practices and gender disparities in the one hand and widespread and repeated episodes of childhood illnesses on the other hand. Since March 2020, the COVID pandemic has affected the entire country, negatively impacting food security and uptake of health and nutrition services, and will likely affect access, availability, and delivery of services over the next year or more.According to the 2010 survey (SHHS 2010), the prevalence of stunting among children under five years old was 31 percent. Since then, reliable data have not been collected in South Sudan to assess the prevalence of stunting among young children. The main immediate causes of undernutrition among children in South Sudan are inadequate quality and quantity of food intake and frequent illnesses affecting young children. In 2019, the prevalence of wasting among young children in South Sudan was estimated at 16.2 percent(>15% WHO Threshold). According to current projections, in 2023 approximately 1.4 million children will need care for wasting in the country, with 303,000 children expected to be severely wasted. The highest prevalence of wasting among young children is observed in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Western Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, and Northern Bahr El Ghazal.Many infants and young child feeding practices are detrimental to adequate child nutrition, including inadequate breastfeeding practices, the inappropriate introduction of complementary foods, and poor diversity of diet. The evidence available suggests that in 2020, 68 percent of infants enjoyed the benefits of exclusive breastfeeding (FSNMS 25). Breastfeeding throughout the first year of life was universally practiced, increasing from 82.3 percent in 2010 to 96.1 percent in 2019. The national rate of continued breastfeeding at two years of age increased from 38 percent in 2010 to almost 59 percent in 2019. The use of bottle-feeding among young children unfortunately increased significantly from six (6) percent to 47 percent between 2010 and 2019. Only 17.4 percent of children 6-23 months received the required quality of food diversification, while 28.2 percent of children received the required meal frequency. Only 4.1 percent of children met the minimum acceptable diet.Upper Nile, a state in South Sudan, with a projected population of 1,315,902, borders Unity State (Pariang County) to the west, Jonglei State (Fangak and Canal/Pigi Counties) to the south, and to Sudan to the north. Upper Nile has 13 Counties and 73 Payams. The recently released October 2022-July 2023 food security Inter-Phase Classification (IPC) analysis projected that an estimated 763,224 people (58.0% of the population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse with 375,000 people likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity between April and July 2023. The 2022 Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMS) revealed that prevalence of wasting among young children in Upper Nile is 19.5 % which is above the WHO emergency threshold. According to the IPC released in November 2022, an estimated 120,139 children are projected to be wasted in 2023 of which 24,298 are estimated to suffer from severe wasting. SMART survey conducted in Malakal in 2021, revealed prevalence of wasting as high as 17.8%. Key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable population in Upper Nile State are widespread food insecurity, poverty, inadequate infant, and young child feeding and caring practices, high prevalence of malaria, diarrhoea, and ARI, limited access to quality health services, lack of safe water and inadequate hygiene and sanitation. Localized conflict and climatic shocks are also among the aggravating factors.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The planned inception and community stakeholder workshops will target all community based groups chief among them women led groups and youth groups who represent the vulnerable people in the community. These groups are able to collect feedback and present it during the workshops thus ensuring that everyone's voice is heard. Also, as the community nutrition workers conduct house to house visits, they will also interact with the vulnerable and individuals with disabilities whose feedback shall be collected and presented to the relevant project teams and for consideration.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This will be done continuously throughout the project cycle. A full AAP will be at the center of this community engagement strategy. First, there will be suggestion boxes to drop anonymous feedback in each facility. The community nutrition workers will continuously collect feedback at household level and finally, there will be inception, mid-term and end term community stakeholder meetings to discuss the project and generate feedback. All these platforms will have active participation of the targeted beneficiaries and community members.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Staff will be trained on PSEA. Facilities will have PSEA posters and community members and beneficiaries will continuously be sensitized on PSEA; what it is, the power dynamics and how to report true or suspected cases. All health and nutrition sessions at the facility, community MIYCN meetings, community growth monitoring meetings and other meetings involving key stakeholders will be platforms to reinforce the rights of beneficiaries and enlighten them on PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from the beneficiaries shall be very crucial to the successful implementation of the project. It will be key in identifying additional or emerging needs from the communities on issues such as population displacement due to conflict or floods which would prompt a response to ensure aid reaches the affected population. It will also be crucial in establishing a synergistic and mutually beneficial approach to programming, ensuring the community receives quality assistance and that it also benefits them through access to opportunities i. e job creation. Feedback from organized feedback sessions, Key informant interviews or organized anonymous feedback will be streamlined into routine programming as art of learning to re-orient program activities whenever need be.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climatic shocks in the recent past has majorly contributed to flood related displacements that has resulted in disruptions in regular nutrition related activities. To mitigate this, NH shall seek to setup nutrition facilities on higher grounds away from rivers whose banks are prone to breaking, construct dykes that will prevent flood waters from encroaching on key infrastructure such as the nutrition facilities and storage spaces and early prepositioning of commodities preventing supply breaks due to access constraints.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project shall seek to further strengthen referral linkages to GBV partners. Since all community nutrition workers will have been trained on PSEA and GBV concepts, they will be able to identify and appropriately link all GBV related cases to the relevant partners for further support. Additionally, all nutrition facilities shall have a GBV referral desk where beneficiaries and community members alike may report any incidences of gender based violence. It will be manned by a member of the nutrition team who will have received additional training on GBV practices.GBV safety audits shall also be conducted across all the nutrition facilities bi-annually to ensure that risks to GBV are detected early and mitigated appropriately.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project shall contribute to outcome one since it majorly focuses on the provision of both the preventative and treatment packages of nutrition. Through the preventative package i. e MIYCN practices, establishmentof kitchen gardens and Growth Monitoring Promotion, beneficiaries shall be capacity built on feeding practices required for each age-group e.g. Exclusive breastfeeding for children under 6 months, Complementary feeding for children above 6 months and proper feeding practices for PLW's. The treatment package shall focus on treating the malnourished children and PLWs through the provision of specialized foods critical for their recovery while at the same time imparting knowledge on improved feeding practices meant to avert a relapse. By improving their knowledge on basic aspects of nutrition, so shall the project be able to lead to Improved Food security come 2023.
