Project Summary

Mercy Corps proposes to build upon the previous iterations of FFP food security responses in NE Nigeria with a multi-pronged approach that increases access to both staple and nutrient-dense food through local markets while also shaping behaviors to support optimal nutrition. The approach incorporates lessons learned from prior phases of REACH and is designed with integrated outcomes to go beyond direct food provision to encourage demand for, and knowledge around, the benefits of nutrient-dense foods.
By engaging multiple market systems, REACH 3 promotes sustainability and resilience by investing in nutrition and production capacities, ultimately enabling an estimated 129,584 people from 18,512 conflict-affected households in Damboa (13,512 HHs) and Bama (5,000 HHs) to consume an adequate and nutritious diet. Using this approach, Mercy Corps will target IDPs', host community members, and returnees by addressing their immediate food security needs, as well as commence the work of taking Damboa and Bama along the path from food dependency to food self-reliance.

1 - Needs

As of March 2019, the majority of IDPs in the northeast are clustered in urban areas of Borno State, both in Maiduguri, the capital of Borno State, and, increasingly, in Local Government Area (LGA) headquarter towns as a part of a GoN security strategy of ‘encamped garrison towns.’ This strategy involves requiring the population of an LGA live inside a secure perimeter around a major city, typically the LGA capital. Around these towns, the military and local vigilantes patrol a perimeter with a radius between five and ten kilometers, which is set aside for farming activities to sustain the population within. This effectively means that there is little to no presence of formal security actors outside of these perimeters, and those venturing beyond to access firewood as a means of fuel energy for cooking expose themselves to significant security risks. Cases of kidnapped and disappearance of individuals’ who ventured into the bushes for cooking firewood has been reported over the time. It also results in IDP concentration in urban areas, overcrowded camps and camp-like settings, often with insufficient access to basic goods and services, leading to negative coping strategies amongst displaced and host communities.Across the Northeast, at-risk households are cut off from their agricultural livelihoods, exposing them to increased hardship and vulnerability. This is most sharply evident in the aforementioned garrison towns, where tens of thousands of IDPs who cannot return to their rural homes languish, unable to engage in safe livelihoods or access basic services. The most at-risk groups include women and child-headed households, as well as the elderly and disabled. Men and adolescents are at particular risk of being caught up in the conflict. In addition to protection, other priority needs for these groups are adequate food, shelter, water and sanitation facilities, and household supplies, as well as access to basic income and health and education services. Global and severe food insecurity is most pervasive in Borno State, where 41.8% of the population is food insecure. According to the Cadre Harmonisé (CH), out of 27 LGAs in Borno state, 21 LGAs fall in the crisis and emergency phases, including Bama and Damboa, accounting for 1,750,143 individuals affected by food insecurity. Since April 2019, there has been an increase of 2.4% food insecurity prevalence reported among the displaced population compared to February 2018. The most recent preliminary findings of the REACH Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by Mercy Corps in September 2019 underscores the deteriorating vulnerability status of displaced populations in these localities. The findings show that IDPs across the state are the most negatively affected population in terms of food security and livelihoods, while returnee HHs in Borno are affected severely. 56% of HHs in Borno are considered more vulnerable (IDPs and returnees at 42%) compared to the other two states in the Northeast. Overall, Borno has the highest percentage of HHs with a poor food consumption score, mostly in conflict-affected areas. In Borno State, both IDP and returnee HHs have an average FCS in the borderline category (average FCS of 33% for IDP HHs and 34% for returnee HHs across the state), while Bama is among the top 5 LGAs out of 27 for having poor FCS at 26%.

2 - Activities

Food Assistance through E-Vouchers: The proposed intervention will provide much needed food support in the form of e-vouchers. Selected households will receive a monthly food basket for 12 months that provides 70% of their daily caloric needs, aligning with the Food Security Sector (FSS) Harmonization Guide which recommends that food security transfers cover 70% of food needs. The food items include rice, millet, maize, cowpeas, palm oil, groundnuts, groundnut oil, and salt. The exact value will be determine by HH size and market prices based on the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) which ranges from 18,100 NGN to 21,100 NGN ultimately covering variations during the post-harvest seasons and the lean seasons. Fresh Food Vouchers: Mercy Corps will also distribute Fresh Food Vouchers to up to 5,180 HHs with young children experiencing acute malnutrition (either SAM or MAM). Through these vouchers, these households will access fresh vegetables, fruit and animal protein vouchers, promoting immediate diet diversity and supporting local micro-vendors.Kitchen gardening and backyard poultry farming: Based on the Smart Agriculture experience and lessons learned from REACH 2 and 3, Mercy Corps will support simple and sustainable SMART agronomy and livelihood techniques to encourage longer-term quality diet diversification. Distribution and promotion of energy efficient stoves: Mercy Corps will increase Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) for clean cooking to 5,000 beneficiaries at the household level for the most vulnerable populations in Damboa. Mercy Corps recognizes that the supply of Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES) will not eliminate the need to collect firewood. However, the stoves will dramatically reduce the amount of wood needed to prepare food. Mercy Corps will continue coordinating with Food Security Sector to link up beneficiaries with the partners’ existing plan, and other sectors and partners in conducting market assessments, price survey of food items, and having an inclusive and robust stakeholder engagement to help implement its projects. With the identified needs, MC will register beneficiaries based on specified vulnerability criteria, select, and verify these beneficiaries to benefit from the food assistance. PSEA- Mercy Corps condemns all actions of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) in any nature to affected populations committed by staff, contractors, volunteers, partners and other actors who provide aid in communities. Mercy Corps will consider this the most serious breach of accountability by the humanitarian sector. Consequently, Mercy Corps has put protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) as a distinct subset under accountability to affected populations (AAP), with a high profile and a strong set of specific commitments. To accomplish this Mercy Corps will provide clear communication on prevention of SEA using, flyers and posters at distribution points as possible modes of dissemination.
