Project Summary

The planned CHADO integrated GBV strategy for 2019 will focus both but more on emergency GBV response including at a lesser components of transition and recovery GBV programming. The emergency response activities which comprises of provision of comprehensive case management targeting 1250 women and girls including those living with disabilities, provision of psychosocial support (PSS) through specialized counselling, group counselling and inter-generational meetings (TEA-TALKs) where older women interact with younger and girls to discuss issues regarding domestic roles to prevent domestic violence and early child marriages. The PSS activities will target 4,000 women and girls. The provision of Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) to most vulnerable women and girls including PwD’s will on be on case-by-case basis to help the most vulnerable person’s with life-saving assistance such to reduce the impact of hunger, lack of access to services, NFI’s, and those at risk stigma due to culture and traditions. The assistance will also include where appropriate, provide access to justice. Individual Protection Assistance will target 600 women and girls. As part of the life-saving activities CHADO will use the theory of “Information/communication Saves Life’s” provide of safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services through findings of 11 safety audits in the targeted Counties and the subsequent dissemination of key safety messages including advocacy to local authorities where appropriate.
The recovery and transition centered activities will basically focus on provision of support programming through establishment of 15 Women and Girls Friendly spaces, strengthening 15 community-based protection monitoring committee’s with 15 members each totaling 225 persons (65% women and girls) in each location, provide protection awareness campaigns and GBV prevention messaging targeting 15,000 men, women, boys and girls. The women support component will focus on provision protective socio-economic support to vulnerable women and girls through trainings on Village Savings and Loans Association which is Small Scale Micro-finance Enterprise (SSME). The training will also include commodity retailing and Vegetable Farming as business. They will either be provided with Conditional Cash Transfers (CCT) in VSLA groups or vegetable seeds for commercial purpose. The targeted women for protective socio-economic support are 2,200 women and the girls will be only those above the age of 18. A clear vulnerability criterion will set and the final list of beneficiaries in each location will be jointly validated with stakeholders such as RRC, local authorities and local community leaders. The use of Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES) from CHADO experience in Koch County has proven that the use of FES for cooking is an excellent GBV mitigating strategy as it limits by half the risk of GBV faced by women and girls including domestic violence. It also reduces by half the frequency of fetching firewood and reduces health risk children and women to indoor respiratory tract diseases. Therefore CHADO will facilitate trainings on moulding and use of Fuel Efficient Stoves targeting 1200 women and girls. Another mitigation strategy is the mentoring of boys and youths, including adolescent girls and young women on Comprehensive Sexuality Education (CSE) programming (mentoring youth as agents of change on risks mitigation against GBV) will target 2500 adolescent boys and girls, male and female youths in the targeted locations. The need for sustainable peace in all communities in South Sudan is of high importance therefore CHADO will engage all targeted communities in peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence to strengthen inter- and intra-clan/community peaceful coexistence o ensure durable humanitarian access and programming. The targeted population for peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence 1,100 community leaders and other community gate keepers.

Needs

South Sudan has suffered from decades of conflict and neglect, with a new civil war erupting in December 2013. According to the DRAFT Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 7 million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2019, the same proportion as in 2018, where drivers and multipliers of crisis have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal conflicts, ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, economic instability and insecure access to food and livelihoods. Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in September 2018, armed clashes have recently continued unabated. Pockets of fighting and insecurity due to banditry has been reported in Koch and Mayendit Counties in Unity State, while in Nasir County, women and girls have suffered and overwhelmed by the brunt of the after-effect of the protracted conflict due to lack of quality emergency and recovery GBV services including Yirol West, Yirol East, Cueibet and Awerial Counties of Lakes State. In the implementation of Community for Development through series of FGD’s conducted by CHADO, women and girls have indicated that “domestic violence as a common practice in the communities”. “It is acceptable for men to beat their wives if they misbehave”. With the weak judicial system, the survivors of domestic violence opt to seek traditional courts to iron their differences. However, no is justice administered. It was also reported that, women and girls in reproductive age lack sanitary and basic hygiene tools. Sanitary Pads are not available in the local market. Hence, management of menstruation becomes difficult as use isolation from the public becomes imminent to avoid stigma. The same challenges was documented from Lakes State and was also observed in a recent assessment carried out by CHADO in Wunlit town of Tonj East County of Warrap State. In Nasir there is no presence of health partners for CMR and GBV response is weak putting women and girls at the edge of prolonged suffering.In Yirol East, the population that fled from Rumbek North and displaced populations in Nasir County, Upper Nile State and Tonj East, Warrap State due to inter-clan conflicts have been forced to living conditions as IDPs, some being hosted by relatives or living in overcrowded temporary structures with no privacy which exposes them to increased risk of sexual violence (including rape, sexual assault, unwanted touching, name calling, and other forms of sexual harassment). The location are in most swampy further exposing the IDPs to malaria, water borne diseases.Food insecurities has exacerbates harmful gendered roles, increased risk of a variety of forms of gender based violence including but not limited to: sexual violence in the form of SEA and survival sex; child, early and forced marriage; and unequal access and control of resources for women and girls. The quadrant of insecurities, loss of livelihood, changes in gender roles and lack of quality GBV services have culminated into increased incidences of GBV. With the weak judicial system, in all the focus Counties the survivors of domestic violence opt to seek traditional courts to iron their differences. However, no is justice administered and hence, no sight to ending GBV. Through CHADO’s innovative integrated GBV programming which is aimed at bridging the quadrant of precipitating and perpetuating factors of GBV, the urgent needs of the vulnerable affected population especially women and girls in the target locations and in joint collaboration with local authorities and communities including other service providers CHADO will ensure timely delivery of an integrated emergency, transition and recovery GBV risk mitigation, prevention and response assistance contextually.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through community meetings and FGD's. A broad spectrum of the community will participate in decision-making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and provides direct influence in the humanitarian response. This influence will be achieved through commitment ofCHADO in communication approaches that include clear and direct feedback loops between programme design and management and the communities themselves. Protection of the most vulnerable population will further be enhanced because the disaggregated risks/needs of the diversity of the community will be recognized; this will enable a more targeted response. The drivers of the conflict will then be mitigated, thereby reducing the risk. CHADO will institutionalize feedback mechanism to allow beneficiaries, girls and boys, women and men including persons with disabilities to voice their successes, as well as complaints and concerns to stakeholders on quality and impact of the project as a means to measure accountability to affected population. The action will launch regular, structured dialogues to create platforms for target groups to engage with local government officials, service providers and CHADO partners. This will foster a sense of mutual trust and encourage teamwork from planning, implementation and project review. Context specific and culturally sensitive feedback plan or social accountability guidelines will comprise a number of sub-activities such as beneficiary scorecards about the services, complaint collection boxes; displaying activities and budget of this action in visible places, periodic joint review sessions of beneficiaries and the local authorities. Other feedback plans will include: Joint stakeholder field monitoringvisits, freedom of information petitions, community monitoring of service delivery and participatory budgeting.

All gender and age groups will receive appropriate contextual messages of their age group and gender which are also culturally appropriate. Historical, political and social dynamics within and between gender and age-groups, impact on the crisis and protection risks; Therefore CHADO through a sustained approach will ensure that there is improved interaction/communication by different age groups and gender to reduce protection and tailored messages using proven community approaches such as FGD's and open community forums or meetings.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CHADO has ratified and endorsed the UN PSEA policy and uses it as a code of conduct document signed by all employees, volunteers and contractors. CHADO has it own child safe guiding policy adhered strictly by all its staff and volunteers. Staff members will promote the human rights of all people and may not discriminate on the basis of an individual’s race, color, ethnicity, national origin, religion, age, political affiliation, gender, sexual orientation, marital status, pregnancy, or disability. Staff members will be accountable to local, national, and international laws and are aware of the consequences for violating these laws. Sexual exploitation, sexual abuse, corruption, trafficking of adults or children, forced early marriage and other abuse by staff members constitute acts of gross misconduct and will result in disciplinary action, up to and including immediate termination of employment. In accordance with international law, sexual activity with a child (a person under age 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief in the age of a person is not a defense. Staff members are prohibited from having sexual relationships with beneficiaries. These relationships are often based on unequal power dynamics and may undermine the credibility and integrity of humanitarian work. This includes the exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior, for selection as a beneficiary or for assistance as a beneficiary.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is tailored to provide protection on needs of different groups in the communities. The risk associated with all gender and age groups will be taken into high consideration and if there are concerns, a wide consultation will be made using CHADO advocacy tool. More so CHADO always carry out community risk assessment and safety audit to reduce impediment of beneficiaries from receiving assistance.
