Project Summary

(New Proposal)
There has been increasing clashes between unidentified armed groups and regional and federal government forces, along with different inter-communal conflicts and natural disasters in east wolelga, Oromia , Ethiopia. These shocks continue to cause major displacements in the area. CRS conducted a multi-directorial needs desk review and the assessment highlighted that many IDPs needs access to shelter and are forced to sleep in overcrowded spaces including emergency shelters or communal sites. In response to these challenges CRS designed a project to provide ESNFI and cash for rent for households displaced as a result of conflict in Sibu Sire Woreda in East Wellega, Oromia. The project will target IDPs living in the host community in substandard housing. The total number of displaced people targeted is 15,081 individuals (2,742 HH):
- 10,557 ind. (1919 HH) receive ESNFI V2 kits. Of the total: 7,390 are IDPs receiving ESNFI-V2 and 1,584 (15%) are members of the host community receiving ESNFI-V2 where ESNFI-V2 is budgeted at $140. 1,584 are disabled (15%) and receive ESNFI-V2 Disabled budgeted at 182USD.
- 4,524 ind (823HH) receive cash for rent for 6 months budgeted at 150USD. 3,846 indiv (102HH) receive the NFI-V1 kit budgeted at 102USD and 697 ind (123 HH) receive the NFI-V1 Disable kit budgeted at 130USD.
A series of recent attacks by armed militia have reportedly led to the mass displacement of roughly 15% of the population of Sibu Sire, primarily pastoralists and subsistence level farmers. There are also reports of militia burning homes and destroying harvests, livestock and other livelihood assets.
Based on the reports coming from the locations, IDPs have several priority needs, like
ES/NFI. The IDPs require immediate
assistance, mainly food, shelter, and health services. The IDPs are dispersed in the host
community and most of them are living outside in sub-standard makeshift shelters or rental houses which they struggle to afford.
Based on the conditions, the cluster has recommended ESNFI and cash for rent for IDPs.
CRS has a robust emergency program, implementing similar activities in West Wellega,
Gedeo and Guji, East and West Hararghe, West Gonder, South Omo, and Tigray through its
local church partners. CRS’ proposed implementing partner, Robe Catholic Secretariat (RCS) has been
operating in Wellega and Guji areas for the past twenty years through different
development and emergency project. RCS has assisted IDP/returnees. In
addition, CRS/RCS are prepared to scale up response efforts to meet the acute
needs of IDP and returnee populations more comprehensively. CRS and its partners will
map and address barriers to access that could influence the target communities gendered and
intersectional ability to participate in Shelter/NFI interventions. Additionally, responses
will ensure that Shelter/NFI services are within safe and easy to reach locations for the
affected population. To ensure quality programming and ownership, the community will
actively participate in all phases of the project cycle.
CRS will implement the project with local partner called Ethiopian Catholic Church-Social and Development Commission, Nekemt Branch (ECC-SDCON, a non- profit organization organized under the laws of Ethiopian Residents Charity in accordance with the Charities and Societies Proclamations No. 621/2009. ECC-SDCON has long years of presence and experience in the area and have good experience in emergency projects.

1. - Projects activities

The project will provide ESNFI and cash for rent for households displaced as a result of conflict in Sibu Sire Woreda in East Wellega, Oromia. The project will target IDPs living in the host community in substandard housing. The total number of displaced people targeted is 15,081 individuals (2,742 HH):- 10,557 ind. (1919 HH) receive ESNFI V2 kits. Of the total: 7,390 are IDPs receiving ESNFI-V2 and 1,584 (15%) are members of the host community receiving ESNFI-V2 where ESNFI-V2 is budgeted at $140. 1,584 are disabled (15%) and receive ESNFI-V2 Disabled budgeted at 182USD. - 4,524 ind (823HH) receive cash for rent for 6 months budgeted at 150USD. 3,846 indiv (102HH) receive the NFI-V1 kit budgeted at 102USD and 697 ind (123 HH) receive the NFI-V1 Disable kit budgeted at 130USD. The project proposes to use an in-kind modality approaches to provide Emergency shelter/ Non-food items to targeted households. CRS will conduct a market assessment before distribution of the ESNFI Lowland kits to determine the most appropriate modality in each woreda then share the assessment report to the national and sub-national cluster. Any cash transfer value will be in line with the Shelter NFI cluster standards and coordinated with other cash and S/NFI actors operating in the target areas. The transfer value will be based on Ethiopia Shelter and NFI cluster “standard composition of NFI V1and ESNFI V2 kits, and cash for rental” and account for any contextual differences in priorities/availability of items and local market prices. To ensure the targeted population can access the goods in the local market, CRS will conduct a rapid market assessment to determine the most appropriate modality of assistance. The rapid market assessment will determine if priority NFI V1 and ESNFI V2 items needed by the target population are available in the local market in sufficient quantities, and to understand if the target population can safely access markets in each woreda. CRS will transfer the cash through Awash Bank. CRS proposes to use the tenancy agreement developed by IOM and translated into the local language. Tenancy agreements will be made between the owner of the house and the beneficiaries of the program in the presence of the Kebele officials and three witnesses.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

CRS and its partner are committed to saving lives, protecting livelihoods, andenhancing resilience to shocks of the most vulnerable households in conflict-affectedcommunities. Target communities are primarily valued stakeholders at every stage ofthe project cycle. Accountability to project participants and communities is one of thecore values in CRS. CRS and its implementing partner will highly involve target projectparticipants in conducting the feasibility study, implementation, and in monitoring andevaluation activities. Regular meetings with community members that involve men,women, boys, girls, and people with disabilities will be organized in coordination localGovernment at the woreda level. Depending on the preference of project participants,CRS will design and apply Community Feedback and Response system to facilitatetwo-way communication. Emphasis on Women and Children. Project activity designsand project plans will be discussed in focus group discussions and results will beincorporated into project implementation plans and activity design. FGDs will beconducted with women and men in separate sessions. To further reinforceaccountability, promote social cohesion and mitigate conflict sensitivities, CRS andpartners will also ensure that targeting of project participants to be in consultationwith the local community representatives in which the most vulnerable segments ofthe society are fairly included in all activities. Project activity/distribution areas will beselected based on the feedback/comments from the local communities to ensure nonegative effects to people accessing sites. Verification of beneficiary lists with thelocal communities will also be done. This will be assessed by conducting postdistribution monitoring after the distributions are made. In order to avoid/minimize thepossible exploitation (abuse of power) of the affected communities with the powerimbalance dynamics there will also be posters developed with respect to individualand/or group entitlements along with expected staff conduct. There will also becommunity sensitization on staff code of conduct and program participants protectionaspects to prevent happening of possible violations and improve communityawareness on how to interact with CRS and partners staff during projectimplementation
