Project Summary

Through this project, YOPCODI seeks to improve the resilience of Children includingchildren living with disabilities affected by emergencies through providing continuousequitable access to child protection services in Yunusari, Geidam, Bade, Gujba, Yusufari,Fune and Nangere. YOPCODI will implement the project through 3 result areas.
Result area 1:
Children at risk (boys and girls) including adolescents, children with disabilities accesspreventive and comprehensive quality response services in a timely manner, this includesprovision of both access to quality to effective case management of MHPSS for childrenaffected by emergencies, including children with disabilities through structured age-specifi c, creative and recreational manner including life skill education interventions foradolescents.
Result area 2:
Parents, caregivers, children, and communities promote positive social norms that protectchildren and women from violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harmful practicesthrough awareness creation, caregiver’s economic empowerment and positive parentingsession to caregivers including caregivers of children living with disabilities.
Result area 3:
To improve sustainability, YOPCODI will enhance the capacities of child protectionpartners and social welfare workforce to deliver quality prevention and response servicesthrough a strengthened child protection system at community, LGA level, and state level.
More so, YOPCODI will engage government and community stakeholders to have an all-inclusive monitoring of project activities and also workshops will be organized to identifyand discuss solutions to problems identifi ed and develop action plans for effi cientimplementation.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022 HNO) 8.4 million people are inneed of humanitarian support, the Protection Sector assesses that 4,572,018 People inNeed (PiN) in North-East Nigeria require protection interventions, among them 409,030IDPs in camps, 516,956 IDPs outside of camps, 973,163 Returnees, and 2,672,879 HostCommunity members. Protection needs differ by groups, the most affected from amongthe 4,5M are 1,432,169 girls, 1,253,701 boys, 1,001,345 women, and 884,803 men. Thishas increased needs for 2023 as compared to 2022 (4.3 million) is due to the followingfactors: secondary displacement of IDPs due to the government enforced“returns”/relocations into unsafe areas not allowing for self-suffi ciency (movements,livelihoods, services etc.) To hasten and add, approximately 2.1 million people need childprotection services due to the absence of child protection systems and community-basedsafety networks, of which 1.3 million children need child protection services, The childprotection area of responsibility aims to reach 1.5m PiN (1,050,000 children 53% Girls and450,000 caregivers 53% women. 13 years of war in the North-East Nigeria has deprivedchildren of a normal childhood, exposing them to violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation,and conscription into NSAGs. The most vulnerable include children in IDPs camps,secondary displaced children from closed camps, returnee children, Children Associatedwith Armed Groups, unaccompanied/separated children, and children with disabilities.Girls are exposed to grave violations of children’s rights such as limited access toeducation, child marriage, sexual gender-based violence, emotional/ physical violence,and traffi cking. Boys face the risk of forceful recruitment/abduction/re-recruitment byNSAGs and traffi cking. Parents and caretakers have been exposed to stress /violence andneed child protection services. Based on the Child protection Area of responsibilitystrategy 2022-24, that is fl exible and caters for both humanitarian and developmentcontext given the different levels of humanitarian situation across BAY states will enablethis project to bridge the humanitarian and development nexus while setting up a robustchild protection system and fostering the localization agenda as part of the grand bargainwhere the Child Protection Area of Responsibility in Northeast Nigeria is quite a head. Theconcept seeks to build on this strategic direction to ensure comprehensive prevention andresponse services linked to the humanitarian country team centrality of protectionstrategy, the humanitarian response plan 2023 and the Child protection Area ofResponsibility strategic priorities in the following Local Government areas of Damaturu,Gujba, Geidam and Yunusari LGAs in Yobe State that have been identifi ed with the highestseverity levels of needs by HNO 2023.

2 - Activities

This proposed project is designed to respond to the most critical child protection needsand gaps identifi ed in Yunusari, Geidam, Bade, Gujba, Yusufari, Fune and Nangere. This isproposed to be delivered through the provision of psychosocial support through structuredand mobile recreational facilities in child friendly spaces, provision of psychosocialsupport services, counselling, and positive parenting to caregivers, reinforcement ofcommunity-based child protection mechanism. Output 1: Children at risk (boys and girls)including adolescents, children with disabilities access preventive and comprehensivequality response services in a timely manner Output 2: Parents, caregivers, children, andcommunities promote positive social norms that protect children and women fromviolence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harmful practices Output 3: Capacities of childprotection partners and social welfare workforce are strengthened to deliver qualityprevention and response services through a strengthened child protection system Activity1.1 Conduct advocacy to Community, LGA and State level Stakeholders on ChildProtection Response Activity 1.2 Conduct protection Risk Assessment in CommunitiesActivity 1.3 Construction and Equipping of Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) Activity 1.4Provide structured recreational and creative psychosocial services to boys and girlsActivity 1.5 Carry out awareness and reach girls, boys, women, and men with informationon child protection issues, laws, positive parenting and existing referral pathways andchild protection services Activity 1.6 Establish and train community-based child protectioncommittees’ members on child protection issues and best practices. Activity 1.7 Conductweekly positive parenting session to identifi ed caregivers and poster parents includingparents of children living with disabilities Activity 1.8 Support caregivers with training andstart-up kits for micro-enterprises/businesses in order to support child protection athousehold and community level. Activity 1.9 Support poster parents with children kitActivity 1.10 Training of 16 case workers, 7 LGA Team Leads and M&E Assistants onCPIMS+ Activity 1.11 Placement of children affected by protection risks into appropriatecommunity-based alternative care Description: Children affected by protection risk (UASC)to be placed into alternative care in the appropriate community-based support system, thiswould be done with support from the SMYSSCD, SMWA and the Nigerian Police in thetargeted LGAs Activity 1.12 Train CFS animators and parenting skill facilitators on MHPSSand positive parenting skills Activity 1.13 Provide and Support life skill education for 700adolescent boys and girls including children with disabilities: Through the CFS facilitators,YOPCODI will provide life skill education that support in enhancing resilience and buildingcompetencies and skills for adolescent girls and boys including children with disabilitiesacross the 7 project locations. Activity 1.14 Support to LGA and State level CPCoordination and Reporting Activity 1.15 Conduct training of trainers (ToT) for childprotection partners and social workers from the MYSSCD, and other CP partners.YOPCODI will leverage the fi ndings of the capacity needs assessments of child protectionsub-sector partners in the NE conducted by Goal Prime in collaboration with the sub-sectorto strengthen the capacities of the partners and social welfare workforce in line with therelevant training topics and priorities for staff optimal performance so as to support inaddressing critical protection needs of children and adolescents. Activity 1.16 Work withcommunity stakeholders including children to establish/ strengthen complaint andfeedback mechanism which is age appropriate and disability inclusive. Activity 1.17Organize Life skills training for adolescent girls and Boys and support the dignity/hygiene kits, socio economic reintegration

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Understanding that women, men, boys, and girls are affected differently by disastersand displacement, the project will prioritise gender equity and the promotion ofwomen's rights in all our humanitarian responses. YOPCODI policies recognize thatSGBV is a violation of human rights and seeks to mainstream work to reduce SGBVand protection into all programs. YOPCODI has an internal gender policy, a SGBVpolicy and has minimum standards for gender in emergencies as a tool to measureconsistency in promoting gender equality through interventions. The Organizationtrains its programme personnel in gender mainstreaming and has GBV and PSEA focalpoints that work closely together to mainstream gender into programmes and projects.All activities will incorporate considerations for gender and different needs of women,men, boys, and girls as well as people with disabilities

9 - Accountability to Affected Populations
9.1 - Check here if the project is based on a specific needs assessment(s)

Yes

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

YOPCODI will work within the framework of accountability designed by the AAP/CE working group that enforces provision of information, involvement of the target community/persons in decision making process related to the humanitarian response and setting up of complaint mechanism and feedback loop during the implementation of the project. Therefore, complaints and feedbacks generated through such channels will be used to make necessary adjustments to program delivery. At inception of the project, a meeting with state and LGA level stakeholders will be held in the community for easy attendance by the beneficiaries including special interest groups such as persons with disability, women and girls to explain to the beneficiary groups and government partners the project deliverables, their roles and expected results of the project, and the change the project aims to support. To ensure that the principal beneficiaries’ (Children) views are captured, YOPCODI will promote child participation in the appropriate layers of the project, to give them a sense of belonging and encouragement. This will create continuous communication with communities to inform them of about the programme (at inception), gather and respond to feedback on monthly basis and disseminate assessment findings, lessons and data (baseline). The dissemination will be followed with explanation on what the resultsmeant and brainstorming sessions on what the representatives felt could be done to improve the situation in their communities. YOPCODI will adopt community participatory approaches in planning, implementation and monitoring of the project. Examples of approaches that will be used in ensuring accountability to the affected populations include age, gender and culturally appropriate consultative forums with stakeholders at the state and community levels on key aspects of the project, community mobilization and sensitization including feedback from and to stakeholders in the affected communites.
