Project Summary

The Majorities of the IDPs, returnees, host communities and pastoralists are suffering from food shortage and malnutrition due to the recorded poor production of crops which is ascribed mainly to the nationwide conflict between RSF & SAF forces, which was also triggered by tribal disputes and displacements in Darfur states. Moreover, the deteriorated economic situation, currency inflation, skyrocketing price of agricultural and industrial food products in the market didn’t favor the poor and vulnerable people to purchase their food items and subsist the life of their family members. The aid agencies (UN agencies and NGOs) which used to provide food aid are barely able to provide assistance to the increasing number of needy people in Darfur, Aljazeera, and Kordofan states. In line with the acute hunger, children are facing severe and moderately acute malnutrition due resulted from the acute food insecurity, lack of timely and appropriate response to save their life. The restricted inter/intra state movement of supplies and the increasing demand for taxations in many checkpoints discouraged traders to transport and sale farm and nonfarm products to customers and in the rural and urban areas. Furthermore, the shortage and alarming price of fuel has direct contribution in increasing the price of essential food and nonfood items in Sudan and in the targeted intervention areas. Likewise, the production and income from the livestock sector is drastically deteriorating due to poor access to livestock feed, veterinary services, market outlets and fear of livestock rustling by armed groups and individuals.
This Food security and livelihoods (FSL) project is designed with the overall objective of saving lives and building resilience of the most vulnerable HHs and reducing acute food insecurity and malnutrition through ensuring availability of nutritious food to 78,347 HHs (391,735 individuals 227,206 female & 164,529 male) (in 26 localities in the six states including (Ar rashad, Abu jubayhah, Abassiya, Kadugli, and Al buram localities in South Kordfan state), (Altadamon, El Kurmuk, Rosaries, El Damazine, Geissan, Wa almahi and Baw localities of Blue Nile Region), (El Geniena, Jebel Moon, Kulbus and Sirba in West Darfur), (Gharb jabal marrah, Shamal jabal marrah, Zalingei, Azum, Um Dukhun and Wasat jabal marrah of Central Darfur), (Madani Alkobra, AL Hassa hissa and Jonob Al Jazeera in AL Jazeera state) and (Karrari locality in Khartoum state) .
WRS has planned the following response options in its targeted areas in cooperation with the donor community: Provision of food/cash assistance to selected target beneficiaries 309,946 individuals (179,769 female & 130,177 male) using inclusive, conflict sensitive, gender sensitive approaches through MPCA and food assistance modalities. Support poor 84,728 farmers (49,142 female & 35,586 male) with agricultural inputs (seeds) tools to enable them producing food crops for subsistence and marketing and building the technical and management capacity of farmers on agricultural production, post-harvest management, marketing, etc. Distribution of farm tools to poor farmers. Provision of restocking animals including goat distribution to 1,000 beneficiaries (580 female and 420 male) in planed localities of the six states. Provide emergency veterinary services (vaccination, deworming/treatment, and animal disease surveillance) for the control of zoonotic and trans-boundary animal diseases to 50,000 beneficiaries (29,000 female and 21,000 male) to support pastoralists through improving the veterinary service delivery systems. Facilitate FFA activities to restore the communities’ assets by implementing appropriate activities in collaboration with WFP and participation of 1,000 (460 female and 540male) (FFA participants in North and Central Jebal Marra. Provide deferent skills training sessions to 1,200 beneficiaries (696 female and 504 male) to build their resilience.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensure that all activities to contribute to improving the wellbeing of women and children and contribute towards improving women access to and control over assets created by the project. Women and girls are overburdened in domestic and agricultural activities and are sole responsible for in the household. Besides, women have almost no right to make a decision on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. This project has analyzed all the constraints that affect women and girls and develop strategies that ensure their active participation and benefits. Women and women headed households will get priority to address their needs. More than 60% of the beneficiary households will be women. Improved technologies that reduces the burden of women are included in the project so that women get enough time for domestic and other reproductive activities. WR recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable/minority groups. While implementing this project, WRS will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights including SGBV problems. Mobile veterinary services will be conducted, reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites. The organization will also be sure to promote conflict sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations will be mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. In addition, the M&E system segregates data by gender and age groups to measure and analyze how the project is affecting different groups. The organization will focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding of the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity. In addition, the organization ensure that all staff, volunteers and contractors working with the organization sign code of conduct. WR will fully utilize Do No Harm approach in the implementation of FSL interventions ensuring that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without any discrimination.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

WR strategic mission is to alleviate vulnerabilities of the target communities through meaningful engagement and promoting ownership by beneficiaries to ensure program quality and sustainability. Community leaders and Sheiks will be involved in community outreaches. Other stakeholders such as youths, women and those who are physically enabled differently to participation in determining their priorities and designs and approach to implementing the project that suites their daily and seasonal calendar. Therefore, all feedback mechanisms will be designed in consultation with all beneficiaries. WR will collect feedback from community/beneficiaries in a systematic and ethical way using different methods of feedback collection, such as surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, or participatory approaches, considering the diversity, accessibility, and inclusivity of feedback collection process, and ensure that WR representatives obtain informed consent and protect the privacy and confidentiality of respondents. WR will analyze the feedback and identify the key themes, patterns, and insights that emerge then will communicate the feedback and share it with the community and other relevant audiences.WR involves the beneficiaries and responds to feedback from them when planning, implementing and monitoring and evaluating its program. WR will be accountable to the affected populations by ensuring their participation and feedback in program identification, design, delivery and learning and will adhere to donors’ guidelines. WR will provide space for communities to shape their own recovery and for WR and partners to better deliver against its commitments to stakeholders, including the people WR serves and the resource partners who make assistance possible. In line with WR M&E system, WR will consult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give community opportunity to identify, report and get redress on program quality and protection concerns. The system will address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding confidentially and non-retaliation principles. Examples of mechanism the project is considering include monthly feedback meetings between WR staff and community members, and a phone number where communities can report sensitive issues. Moreover, there will be suggestion boxes fixed centering the people to enable them convey their opinions. WR has CFM policy and guideline internalized to all staff. Confidentiality of the beneficiaries’ complaints will be protected for the safety of the complainant, and the complainants will receive timely and appropriate feedback/action for their complaints.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The actions proposed by WR are prepared based on the needs and priorities of the communities, which has been collected and compiled through needs assessment, field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. In addition, the intended communities have also confirmed their commitment to fully participate through the entire project life cycle. Overall, the community will be involved in the project as depicted below. Through their representatives the community had been involved in needs assessment and formulation of this project; WR established three layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community-level volunteers in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-level development committees and extension workers. These committees established to oversee the work of WR in their respective areas or locality. In addition, CAHWs and extension workers, who contribute to the strengthening of livestock and agriculture-based livelihoods of the communities, and provide feedback through various reporting and communication mechanisms. The community level engagement materialized through conducting a regular meeting with community-level committees and volunteers. Sector officers and area coordinators conduct monthly meeting at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. WRS displays hotline reporting mechanisms so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided by WRS and partners to relevant staff in the organization. Furthermore, WR will continue using the existing suggestion box to get the feedback from beneficiary communities. The beneficiary and community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WR coordinators will collect information from the suggestion box on a quarterly basis and discuss with all concerned including higher management. Also, the actions taken to the community findings will be shared back to the community.The project will integrate protection activities while implementing FSL activities focusing on awareness sessions on PSEA, GBV and child protection referral pathways for project staff and community-based structures working in/supporting FSL program implementation. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and minority groups. World Relief will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights.
