Project Summary

The project targets Nasir and Ulang Counties located in Upper Nile State, which are part of the Eastern flood plains of the Sobat River Basin livelihood zones. Both counties continue to experience localized conflict, high market prices and aggregated effects of flooding with related population displacements leading to severe food insecurity and GAM rates exceeding the thresholds for emergency interventions.
The response will be implemented by Universal Network for Knowledge and Empowerment Agency (UNKEA) with extensive experience in integrated humanitarian programming in Upper Nile and in particular, Nasir and Ulang Counties. The goal of the project is to contribute to the lessening in nutrition-related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high-quality multi-sectorial life-saving nutrition interventions for the most vulnerable categories of under-five and pregnant and lactating women.
The proposed response aligns the 2022 HRP priorities and will aim to 1) increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women 2) increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age affected by acute malnutrition 3) enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information and 4) increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups. These will be realized through implementation of activities that are directly related to the above objectives as described in the activities section of this proposal.
In order to maximize output from the fund, the response will be implemented in an integrated mode with Health, Food Security, WASH, Education, Early Child Development and Mainstream child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites. Further coordination will involve working with the County Health Departments under the guidance of the nutrition sub and national clusters.
UNKEA will implement the following activities in order to address some of the major issues in the 2 counties;
• Treatment of 4669 SAM children
• Treatment of 7197 MAM children under 5
• Treatment of 7268 MAM PLW
• Provision of 9394 MIYCN counseling and guidance to caregivers of children under 5
• Establishment of 35 kitchen gardens
• Establishment of child-emotional and physical stimulation at all 35 nutrition sites
• Provision of WASH kits to 4669SAM children admitted
• Provision of LLITNs to 4669 SAM children admitted
• Provision of Zinc for treatment of diarrhoea among 90% SAM/MAM children
• Malaria testing and treatment of 90% SAM/MAM children admitted
• Conducting GBV safety audits for all 35 nutrition sites
• Conduct MUAC screening for PLWs and U5 children using the family MUAC approach
• Support the conduct of SMART & FSNMS nutrition surveys
• Rehabilitation of nutrition sites destroyed by floods.
To be able to address the key bottlenecks and other aggravating shocks, the organization will require 800,000 USD and will target 28,529 beneficiaries across the two counties of Ulang and Nasir. This will constitute; 50% of SAM admission target, 20% MAM admission target, 20% of the MAM – PLW target and 20% of the MIYCN target. The overall target is attuned as the project is designed to address to address access to access-constrained locations.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021 Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55 per cent of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighbouring countries. Climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture, which is one of the most climate sensitive economic sectors. Above normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years. An estimated 835,000 people were affected by severe flooding between May and December 2021, and 80 per cent of those affected were from Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states. Many flood-displaced people, including those who were displaced by the 2020 flood were unable to return to their homes until early 2022, if at all. As of December 2021, an estimated 5.3 million people were reached with some form of humanitarian assistance (OCHA, 2022). According to OCHA report, Upper Nile State is anticipated to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season May-July. The state was listed among states with counties with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent. The leading cause of morbidity being malaria, followed by acute respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhoea all of which are exacerbated by Malnutrition. The persistence and increase in sub-national violence leading to disruption of livelihoods and further exacerbation of socio-economic status of population creates increased need for humanitarian support. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a threat; women and girls are exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities, feeling unsafe, some women and girls avoid areas such as water points, latrines, distribution areas, markets and firewood gathering sites Ulang and Nasir counties are part of the Eastern flood plains and the Sobat River Basin livelihood zones. Food insecurity for Nasir County has alternated between Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) levels in recent years while Ulang was among the 15 counties across the country classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Malnutrition rates exceeded the threshold for emergency with assessments conducted in both the counties revealing GAM rates of 16.1% for children aged 6-59 months. The prevalence for PLW by MUAC (FSNMS) was even higher with Ulang County reporting 55% and Nasir 30.4%. Both counties continue to experience cumulative effects of flooding and associated population displacements, localized insecurity, the economic crisis, and prolonged years of asset depletion, which has led to severe food insecurity, and GAM rates exceeding the thresholds for emergency interventions. Flooding and associated population displacements in Ulang and Nasir County have created access-constrained locations that are affecting the smooth running of the UNKEA Nutrition programme supported by UNICEF. This inaccessibility has cut across all Payams of the two counties and has increased operational costs. There is therefore need to address these identified needs and to continue in provision of humanitarian interventions. This application will ensure that children in dire need of life saving nutrition interventions are not excluded due to access-constraints, floods, or any other impediment.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNKEA works in areas with one of the most vulnerable people, mainly children, women and girls. The women/girls and or caretakers/mothers of the children are mostly illiterate with heavy domestic work schedules, living in areas prone to emergencies and in a system that is devoid of a proper health system and limited livelihood options. The women of Nasir and Ulang counties who are primary caretakers of households are also not actively engaged in decision making at household level and suffer Gender based issues. UNKEA will exhibit its obligation to account to the most vulnerable by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into country strategies, programme proposals, monitoring and evaluation, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, and partnership agreements, and are highlighted in reporting. Applying this at a high and policy level will enable efficiency and continuity is receiving feedback that will cut across programing including the pertinent issues of Gender based violence. UNKEA through its community mechanisms will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions, and facilitate a dialogue between an organisation and its most vulnerable populations over information provision. In addition and where necessary, UNKEA will provide one to one services to receive information but also refer certain cases to specialised care. Through various methods discussed above, UNKEA will keenly seek the views of the most vulnerable to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaint mechanisms are rationalised, suitable and vigorous enough to listen and respond to the most vulnerable. These views will be processed, analysed and then referred to appropriate personnel or offices. Through its community structure and expertise, UNKEA will empower its most vulnerable beneficiaries to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most vulnerable and affected are represented and have influence. This will be done through community engagements and capacity building practices. Finally, UNKEA will design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of programmes with the involvement of affected populations, feeding learning back into the organisation on an ongoing basis and reporting on the results of the process.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

More than 90% of UNKEA staff live among its beneficiaries. This has enabled building of trust through regular feedback into the programme. Beneficiary accountability is one of UNKEA’s key quality standards, which is echoed as a crosscutting matter throughout its projects. All UNKEA staff will be trained on how to receive and process feedback from communities as an integrated action with program trainings. The beneficiaries of Ulang and Nasir counties were involved in the design of the current programme and especially on improving access to access-constrained locations. This was done by UNKEA’s strong community network involving community leaders, volunteer groups, County Health Departments and the Boma Health Initiative. The process of community and beneficiary engagement has remained active with community meetings ongoing on a routine basis. This will be enhanced to include more documentation and follow up. UNKEA set up suggestion boxes at Nutrition Sites. These are mainly used by a few people including some beneficiaries, staff and some volunteers who are able to read and write. The content of the box is sent to the county area manager who analyses suggestions and provides feedback to field programme managers and to the management at the head office. This method although not very effective as it requires being able to read and write, will continue to be used. What is has been effective though is the use of exit interviews. A sample of exiting beneficiaries from a nutrition facility after a day’s treatment session are subjected exit interviews usually on their program perception. The practice will be introduced at nutrition outreach sites to gather feedback even from hard to reach community members. UNKEA will introduce focus group and key informant discussions aimed at drawing feedback from community members. The focus groups format will target existing volunteer groups especially the mother group members while the key informant will target community and government leaders within the catchment locations of Ulang and Nasir counties. Further still, Community leaders will be involved in UNKEA programme assessments and related trainings. During data collection, community representatives will be assimilated in the teams. This will provided any opportunity for the leaders to understand UNKEA’s programing and thus support feedback that is based on a good understand of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Gender-based violence (GBV), women rights and protection are critical issues to the wellbeing of women in Ulang and Nasir Counties. UNKEA through its multi-sectoral programing is committed to addressing these issues by having them continuously mainstreamed. This application will be designed in a manner that maximises women and girls’ safety. By rolling out its programme to access- constrained locations, UNKEA will be able to reach out to more people with information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Staff will be trained on Protection against Sexual Exploitation, Abuse (PSEA), counselling points will be established, and referral pathways set up in support of victims. In order to ensure effective support to the victims, the UNKEA Nutrition Sector will collaborate with other sectors and actors. UNKEA will use various interactions or engagements including MIYCN counselling to identify and refer victims of sexual abuse and exploitation. This will be reported upon and victims followed up to ensure their safety. To mitigate Sexual Exploitation and Abuse further, females will be encouraged to take roles among staff and volunteers to contribute along with their male counterparts to ending PSEA. All staff will sign a code of conduct and disciplinary action will ne taken upon breach. Staff will be provided refresher orientation and will be expected to continually refer victims for appropriate care. In addition, why reporting, data will be segregated by sex and age.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The nutrition programme is largely designed by various nutrition guidelines and or policies, which at times makes it difficult to alter its implementation even when genuine feedback is provided. Moreover, even when feedback is collected there are tendencies of it not being put to use. UNKEA through its M&E and programme team will ensure that beneficiaries’ feedback is processed and adapted to project assistance. This will be feedback collated from suggestion boxes, exit interviews, focus group and key informant discussions and that obtained through assessments. Monthly programme reports will constitute a section on beneficiaries ‘feedback. Action points will be allocated to concerned people in field locations and at the head office to have them addressed. At the field level, the area manager will review and allocate action points to respective people. He will also be responsible for discussing feedback that may not be applicable or possible to be executed to beneficiaries including volunteer groups, community members and local leaders. This could be done through community and or stakeholders meetings at field level. At national level, feedback will be collated and sent to the head office and allocated to designated personnel. Feedback that is valid and concerns nutrition technical issues will be sent to the cluster or its technical working groups for revision or consideration. This will be eased by the fact that UNKEA is a strong member of the national and subnational clusters as well as their respective technical working groups. The Executive Director or the board will address issues that require change in the functioning of the organised. Beneficiary’s feedback collected as part of assessment or any sort of learning by the organisation will as well be put to use. The outcomes and recommendations of these assessments will be used in designing the programmes, which address the views of the community. Findings of all assessments disseminated to both the leaders and community/ beneficiaries. Feedback from other above sources will also be used in designing new programs or improving existing programs

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

UNKEA is a reputable national agency in South Sudan with fully established operational systems.The Agency has built strong relations in both Counties of Ulang and Nasir Counties having worked there for many years. The strong relations have been built with communities/beneficiaries, local leaders and donors. The organisation is therefore ready and committed to ensuring the security of its operations against all major risks. UNKEA employs two county area managers who are native of the two counties. These have been trained on security management and possess indigenous understanding of context the target areas and thus able to solve issues as they arise. The area managers will provide the required training on security and safety to the field based staff to ensure everyone, including the local staff, understands issues of safety and security on the ground. Further trainings on health and PSEA will be provided to all field staff as well. The organisation will strengthen its internal mechanisms to prevent SEA by expanding its complaint mechanism and creating more referral pathways through partnership with protection partners. Depending on the analysis, UNKEA will use its security protocol to determine the best course of action and will stay in regular formal and informal communication with OCHA on the progress towards the project’s objectives and any deterrents. The UNKEA Logistics team will carry out timely procurement and delivery of items so that supplies are transported to the field and prepositioned in the project sites before the rainy season. UNKEA will continue to develop its community structures involving mother support groups, community nutrition volunteers and Boma health teams to reach more people. These teams will continue to provide services, alongside the nutrition staff, in the event that there is a threat on security or difficulty is access. UNKEA will continue to constantly review progress made, receive and evaluate community/beneficiary feedback and integrate lessons learnt to achieve effectiveness and remedial measures going forward should this be required
