Project Summary

The 2020 Syria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) sets out the framework within which the humanitarian community will respond to the large-scale humanitarian and protection needs in Syria throughout 2020 by flexibly leveraging the various response modalities. Leveraging these capacities and efforts under a single strategic framework, the plan aims to increase the effectiveness of the response by improving the identification of needs and gaps, reducing overlap and duplication, and monitoring of changes. The Humanitarian Coordinator for Syria and the Regional Humanitarian Coordinator for the Syria Crisis lead and coordinate humanitarian action inside Syria. . OCHA, in line with its global mandate, supports the humanitarian leadership in ensuring the effective coordination of the response inside Syria. To do so, OCHA maintains country offices in Syria and Turkey, and a regional office in Amman. OCHA requires sustained levels of funding to be able to maintain and further strengthen the effective coordination of humanitarian responses from these various offices, facilitate key coordination platforms, provide information management services, manage pooled funding, provide support for humanitarian access and civil-military coordination, provide public information and advocacy on the humanitarian situation, and support humanitarian leadership. The funding requirements reflect the needs of OCHA's three offices involved in the Syria response.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian needs in Syria remain large-scale, multi-layered and extensive after years of hostilities. An estimated 11.06 million people continue to require some form of humanitarian assistance with 4.65 million people in acute need across Syria. An estimated 6.1 million people remained internally displaced, with well over 1.8 million population movements recorded between January and December 2019. The UN estimates that almost half of the population in Syria is food insecure or at risk of food insecurity, with pockets of acute and chronic malnutrition persisting in certain areas. Despite some reduction in violence, Syria remains a major protection crisis, with civilians exposed to multiple protection risks related to ongoing hostilities; the effects of new and protracted displacement; and the depletion of socio-economic resources triggering negative coping strategies. Against this backdrop, the need for humanitarian coordination remains significant with OCHA offices for the Syria response facilitating and supporting key coordination platforms, providing information management services, managing pooled funding, providing support for humanitarian access and civil-military coordination, providing public information and advocacy on the humanitarian situation, and supporting humanitarian leadership. Likewise, for an effective response, there is a need to coordinate identification of needs and gaps, and their analysis which should subsequently inform response planning in a Humanitarian Response Plan that provides an overarching framework for humanitarian response efforts by humanitarian actors based in Syria and from neighbouring countries responding inside Syria. Through leveraging capacities and efforts and by offering key services such as information management and sharing through tools, products, and platforms, OCHA will also strive to reduce overlap and duplication, and monitor progress, contextual and situational changes.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

OCHA recognizes the risks of instrumentalization of aid in the Syrian context and the effects this may have on the protection of vulnerable people. OCHA also recognizes risks related to GBV, SEA, and safety of humanitarian workers and the potential negative affect this may have on affected populations. OCHA will continue to support inter-agency efforts to mitigate protection risks for humanitarian workers, such as the SSG-endorsed Duty of Care Framework for humanitarian organisations working in Syria. OCHA will also continue to support and strengthen inter-agency PSEA mechanisms to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation of abuse by humanitarian workers.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

OCHA will strive to mitigate the above-mentioned adverse effects by promoting principled humanitarian action from all the hubs involved in the Syria response and quality programming by partners. It will do so in collaboration with, advice and guidance provided by humanitarian partners including the Protection sector and its associated AoRs, and the PSEA advisor. It will also continue to put affected populations at the centre of the humanitarian response and use feedback collected from affected communities to better meet needs.
