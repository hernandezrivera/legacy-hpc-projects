Project Summary

To help shape the South Sudan Humanitarian Response, REACH will work in partnership with the CCCM Cluster to provide actionable information about displaced populations. REACH will provide technical support and expertise when designing assessment tools that will eventually be taken over by cluster partners. These tools will be supported by the ongoing area of knowledge (AoK) methodology, which will implement a system to locate, identify and track the main needs and vulnerabilities of displaced populations not supported by the CCCM cluster. in turn this will guide and support partners in launching rapid assessments. At the same time, REACH will provide information management, mapping and site profiling support to the CCCM Cluster.

Needs

Since the beginning of the conflict in 2013, around 2.47 million South Sudanese have sought refuge in neighbouring countries, while 1.91 million are estimated to be internally displaced as of August 2018. Furthermore, it is estimated that there are more than 304,000 refugees within the country, mostly coming from the regions of Blue Nile and South Kordofan in Sudan. In this context the CCCM cluster objectives aim to: 1) Ensure equal and needs-based access to improve quality of services for populations affected by displacement. 2) Effectively engage and respond to vulnerable populations with priority on addressing protection gaps and building resilience. 3) Promote relevant community participation and ensure local ownership of site management. 4) Prioritize new and unreached displaced populations. Within this framework, REACH will work in partnership with the CCCM cluster in South Sudan to provide technical support and expertise by designing and creation of assessments tools for CCCM cluster that will enable better understanding of the displaced population, in the process strengthen the cluster partners capacity in taking ownership of them eventually. REACH through the ongoing area of knowledge (AoK) will implement a system to locate, identify and track the main needs and vulnerabilities of displaced populations that are currently not supported by the CCCM cluster, in turn this will guide and support partners in launching rapid assessments. At the same time, REACH will provide information management, mapping and site profiling support to the CCCM Cluster

Activities

Activity 1. Design and creation of assessments tools for CCCM cluster REACH will provide technical support to the CCCM cluster in order to facilitate the deployment of rapid assessments in informal settings and multi-sector data collection within the formal camps. Thanks to the expertise acquired in South Sudan and globally, REACH can support partners in the creation of specific tools and data collection systems to facilitate the work of Camp managers on the ground. Working closely with CCCM, REACH will deploy tools for assessments in both formal and informal settings upon validation and approval from partners. Specifically, REACH suggest the following: • Informal settings: given the dynamic nature of these type of settlements, a specific tool will be deployed. The idea being to support CCCM in the context of rapid and unpredictable circumstances, the tool itself will reflect these characteristics and will be concise, effective and adaptable to different situations across the country.• Formal settings like refugee camps or POC in Juba are by their nature more stable and predictable than informal and spontaneous settings. For this reason, a standardised data collection tool will be created in order to assess the basic humanitarian needs and gaps existing in the settlements. Cross-comparisons and trend analysis will be possible thanks to a set of similar indicators across camps. However, given the volatile situation in South Sudan and the diversity of needs and gaps characterizing each settlements, this tool needs to be flexible enough to allow actors to include specific indicators pertinent to the local context.Activity 2. locate, identify and track the main needs and vulnerabilities of informal displaced populations The needs and humanitarian condition of displaced populations living in formal settings are well known, the situation of internally displaced persons (IDPs) not supported by the CCCM cluster still constitutes a gap in the humanitarian response in South Sudan. It is also to fill this gap that the project area of AoK was created by REACH, an approach which provides regular, reliable indicative tracking of humanitarian needs over time whose aim is to support in prioritization and identification of “hot-spot” areas witnessing a deterioration of humanitarian needs. Using the AoK, REACH will support the CCCM cluster in the achievement of the objectives set for 2018 and further, in particular the objective “prioritize new and unreached displaced populations”. REACH AoK data will be used by the Cluster to complement information collected through partners’ assessments, reports and secondary data, with the aim of identifying key displacement movement and population in need of CCCM support. Activity 3 Information management, site profiling and mapping: A close collaboration between REACH and the Camp management team already exists within the protection of civilians (POC) sites in Juba. To support planning and programmatic activities in the POC, CCCM cluster requested REACH support resulting in the production of general infrastructure and thematic maps i.e., spatial analytical maps such as, access to key services, shelter technicaldensity, PoC address system. In addition REACH provided technical support in data collection as well as the creation and consolidation of a geodatabase for both POC1 PoC 1, PoC 3 and CCCM and POC3mobile team. This collaboration can be strengthened and generalisedgeneralized to all formal camps in which CCCM cluster operate. As a result REACH will produce site profiles, general infrastructure maps, thematic maps, national collective sites hotspot maps, multisectoral needs assessments factsheets, national severity ranking of the collective sites factsheets. s. The main goal of REACH in this type of setting is to support humanitarian partners to plan and evaluate the humanitarian needs in the camp and to improve the condition of residents in accordance with global standards.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Given the sensitive nature of the data REACH collects, and the vulnerability of many of potential interviewees, REACH recognizes the risk of assessment activities to expose interviewees to unintended physical or psychosocial risk. To mitigate this; REACH tightly control the release of identifying information that may expose interviewees and through its data protection policies. REACH staff received on trauma-sensitive interviewing techniques as part of a training of trainers’ session focused on avoiding re-traumatization of research participants during data collection. The training was provided by a protection expert from an international protection agency and REACH will roll out this training across its 14 field bases through these newly trained staff. REACH is currently in the process of creating awareness of this new system amongst protection agencies in field locations to ensure agencies are prepared for potential referrals before the system is rolled out across field locations. Once fully functional and with lessons learned incorporated, this mechanism could serve as a best practice for other data collection agencies / activities in South Sudan to ensure an ethical data collection approach.
