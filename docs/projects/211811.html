Project Summary

NGO X will address the multiple vulnerabilities facing households (HHs) and communities after twelve years of conflict in Northwest Syria (NWS). At the HH level, the program design links Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to NGO X’s food security, nutrition, winterization and shelter programming. The proposed intervention is in line with the objectives of NWS Cluster Response Plans and informed by previous projects, ongoing monitoring, and feedback from beneficiaries. Activities will target approximately 6,029 HHs/30,145 vulnerable displaced individuals. NGO X will coordinate locations with CWG, SNFI, WASH, and FSL Cluster partners and members before launching a response window to avoid overlap. Proposed activities will take place in locations where NGO X has access to and is implementing in.
• Needs.
Twelve years of conflict in Syria coupled with the devastating series of earthquakes that hit northern Syria and Türkiye in February 2023, have resulted in overlapping crises with food prices soaring, and loss of livelihoods, and the increased vulnerabilities of communities. This has culminated in Syrians living in ‘a perpetual state of emergency (WFP June 2023) with over 90% living below the poverty line, as coping systems crumble in the face of widespread food insecurity and unprecedented levels of humanitarian needs.
The multifaceted crisis in Syria creates continuous cycles of vulnerability, disproportionately impacting those most in need, with economic indicators, including high inflation, currency depreciation, and increases in commodity prices compounding humanitarian need. Women and girls, particularly IDPs, are more vulnerable to mobility limitations, discrimination, exploitation, and safety risks, and female headed HHs are more likely to be displaced. People with disabilities face more challenges accessing services, adequate accommodation, and humanitarian assistance. Per CCCM Cluster data (IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM), September 2023), more than 2.87 million people remain displaced in NWS, with approximately 1.99 million individuals residing in about 1,527 IDP sites. These sites often lack sufficient shelter, infrastructure, and basic services.
Active conflict continues to impede humanitarian access. In October 2023, NWS witnessed a significant escalation of hostilities, resulting in the displacement of over 120,000 people. In January 2024, a rainstorm hit 43 camps sheltering IDPs, with heavy rainfall creating massive floods, over 1700 tents damaged or partially destroyed and 176 tents destroyed.
• Intervention, Implementation of activities.
One to three rounds of MPCA:
NGO X will deploy cash immediately in response to emergency, where conditions for a cash response are met. NGO X will select HHs and distribute one-off emergency MPC transfers, and two additional rounds to HHs experiencing sudden shocks. Transfer value will be aligned with the CWG's MPC 2023 guidance. The selection team will accept or reject affected people based on defined response criteria:
- HHs displaced due to a defined shock (natural disaster/ conflict)
- HHs displaced within a defined timeframe (i.e. people displaced less than a month)
- HHs not receiving similar assistance (coordination with local authorities/Relief Committee/camp management)
- Affected people’s income level/status is verified
- Response criteria is decided in the Crisis Identification Checklist (CIC) e-mail for approval.
NGO X will conduct DM and PDM surveys. In the PDM process, NGO X will adhere to the harmonized PDM tool developed by CWG.
• Protection Risk Analysis
NGO X emphasizes the safety and well-being of participants with robust measures integrating comprehensive PSEAH (Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment) strategies into all activities, including prevention, awareness, responsive reporting, and risk mitigation, reinforced by training for staff, partners, and leaders to enhance incident management.
