Project Summary

The project aims to provide urgently needed shelter assistance and provision of non-food items to detained or stranded migrants as well as most vulnerable Libyan population in line with three sector strategic objectives. The proposed interventions include 1) provision of essential humanitarian assistance including NFIs, cash-based interventions (CBIs) 2) Shelter repair and rehabilitation 3) provision of generators to enhance access to energy (heating, cooking, lights).

Needs

Lack of appropriate shelter and lack of access to NFIs including clothing, bedding, and hygiene affects negatively all the population groups (Libyan and non-Libyan) regardless of their age (children, young adults, vulnerable adults and elderly) and gender (men, women, boys and girls) exasperating pre-existing risks including health and protection. Over 290,000 people in Libya are presently in need of shelter and NFI support. Since 2011, waves of conflict, political and economic instability in Libya caused severe deterioration in the standard of living on the community and individual levels. Damage to housing and infrastructure has been observed across Libya, mostly on the coastal areas including Sirt, Benghazi, Misrata and Tripoli. Despite the returns of IDPs being on the rise across the past two years, the number of IDPs still remains high in Benghazi, Misrata and Tripoli. It is reported that the majority of the IDPs and returnees (over 80%) live in private accommodation, with around 80% paying rent for their shelter. The remaining 20% reside in public or informal settings. Dire economic situation, limited access to livelihood opportunities coupled with the inflation and limitations on cash withdrawals result in a higher risk of difficulty making rent payments and, consequently, of eviction. Shelter represents 50% of domestic expenditures for most households. The Libyan population faces a combination of liquidity constraints and a shortage of housing, which has led to very high rental costs. Those living in public and informal settings, experience lack of access to basic services, poor WASH conditions, overcrowding, and a lack of privacy. Informal settlements are often spaces of physical insecurity due to crime and other forms of violence. In return areas, thousands of people without adequate shelter are living in damaged and/or unfinished buildings and houses, or in collective centres. These living conditions make people more vulnerable and in need of an appropriate shelter response. The shelter situation for migrants and refugees is also very challenging considering the transitory and often undocumented nature of their stay in Libya combined with various forms of protection concerns and lack of livelihood opportunities. The vast majority of the non-Libyan population cannot afford dignified and secure shelters, often sharing living spaces with others in overcrowded conditions without access to basic services and inadequate WASH provisions. Approximately 124,000 migrants and refugees are in need of shelter and non-food items support. Those detained by the Libyan state, in 2018 between 6,000-10,000 individuals at any given time, are subjected to particularly dire living conditions needing sustained NFI support. Despite goods and commodities being available in Libya, and the overwhelming majority of people having access to markets, many of the most severely affected households, especially IDPs and returnees as well as migrants and refugees are unable to afford items because they were too expensive or they did not have enough access to cash because of liquidity challenges. Negative coping mechanisms are being observed amongst the most vulnerable Libyan population. Refugees and migrants look to save money to send it back to their families at the expense of covering their basic needs including shelter and non-food items. While some damage assessments have been conducted within specific areas such as Benghazi, Tawerga and some Western areas, information on the level of conflict damage to private housing or the number of non-displaced residing in houses that are substandard remains extremely difficult to obtain and extremely challenging in terms of programmatic use. IOM will utilize its DTM as a rapid assessment tool to conduct location assessments as well as to identify the sector-specific needs of the affected population in order to better plan and tailor its response.

Activities

The planned activities include 1) provision of emergency non-food items and shelter assistance to most vulnerable Libyan and non-Libyan population including IDPs, returnees, host communities and migrants through the Rapid Response Mechanism 2) Distribution of NFIs to the migrants and refugees in detention centres and urban setting 3) Shelter repair and rehabilitation of damaged houses through the contractors 4) Cash for rent 5) Provision of basic household items including household generators to facilitate the return to normal life. Modalities of in-kind distribution and/or cash-based intervention will be used to provide the above-mentioned assistance depending on the situation. The breakdown for POC is 64% non-Libyans / 34% Libyans but the budget split to roughly 50/50.

Indicate any study/assessment that has been used, if any

For both population categories, both responses in both sectors, non food and shelter are valuable and their implementation can create security risks, including both the risk of diversion and the potential for violence. When commodities or assistance are in short supply, tensions can run high when deliveries or project are implemented. Vulnerable individuals and groups (Libyan and non-Libyan) may be unable to obtain their entitlement, or may have it taken from them by force. IOM will be assessing in advance and adopt mitigation measures, in terms of selection criteria and to minimize the risks, these should include adequate supervision of distributions and careful selection of distribution points. Adequate sensitization and consultation of the communities during the project cycle will be undertaken to avoid creating tension in the communities. Measures to prevent, monitor and respond to gender based violence or sexual exploitation associated with distributions will be considered and relevant considerations may include avoiding distribution points located nearby the presence of large numbers of men and consider scheduling distribution times for women or men only to minimize exposing women and girls to sexual harassment with distribution areas. Physical security risk due to conflict activity and access barriers for certain groups including women and girls will be taken into account. Moreover IOM follow a zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and other forms of abuse of power by aid workers or those collaborating in provision of shelter repair services (including community volunteers). Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation (that have been committed by community members and/or staff). Gender balance of assessment and distribution teams for both sectors will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point in the distribution areas.
