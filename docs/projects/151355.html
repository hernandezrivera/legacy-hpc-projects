Project Summary

The project's purpose is to reduce malnutrition effect in Dhusamareb district. Project activities will include; 1. Training of health facility and community workers on appropriate IYCF practices to provide support and counselling; 2. Facility and community based counselling for and awareness raising of breastfeeding and complementary feeding to women of child bearing age; 3. Screen all under-fives and PLMs visiting the health facilities for services for 12 sessions with 1 per month; 4. Provide lifesaving nutrition services for children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women with severe acute malnutrition at Dhusamareb Hospital; 5. Admit and treat severely malnourished children with complications at stabilization centre in Dhusamareb; 6. Training of health facility and community workers on IMAM guidelines; 7. Community mobilization and awareness sessions on IMAM programming; 8. Facility based and community based screening for acute malnutrition; 9. Training of Health workers on Nutrition information system (documentation, reporting and analysing data for decision making); 10. Establish community nutrition surveillance system. The project target will include; IDPs - 5000, Host community - 3000, Female - 5000, Male - 3000, Children - 5000 and Adult - 3000. The project will ensure that the nutrition needs of both men and women, girls and boys are taken into consideration during implementation. Children under 5 will also be given special attention to avert the severe acute malnutrition.

Needs

Gu 2018 the nutrition survey results reported GAM prevalence of 14 percent and SAM of 1.6 percent indicating sustained Serious levels of GAM compared to Deyr 2017 (14.8%) but an improvement compared to Gu 2017 (33.4%). Morbidity remains high among IDPs (41.7% in November 2018 compared to 45% in Deyr 2017). The Gu 2018 CDR (0.93/10 000/day) is an improvement to Serious compared to 1.06/10 000/day in Deyr 2017. However, U5DR has sustained Serious levels 1.27/10 000/day in Gu 2018 compared to 1.51/10 000/day in Deyr 2017 (FSNAU 2018). The acute malnutrition, high morbidity and high mortality situation among IDPs in Dhusamareb call for integrated support interventions in order to improve the protracted situation that has persisted in these population groups. Morbidity incidences in Dhusamareeb as associated with AWD was reported to be the highest in the region with 41.7%. Low measles immunization and vitamin A supplementation status were reported among IDPs in Dhusamreeb which can be attributed to poor health system in the area with many health post and hospital under staffed and lack essential health supplies. According to the Galgaduud inter agency assessment made in August 2018, nearly 80 % of the displaced people live in 56 new IDP settlements and practice open defecation due to the lack of proper sanitation facilities , this over strains the existing few sanitation infrastructures in the IDPS camps. IDP populations face considerable challenges where high pervasiveness of women headed households are chronically combating high levels of acute malnutrition. These mothers are away from the homes in search for casual labour, while children and infants are without proper care. TUOS will work with other partners in the region and inter cluster cooperation with the aim of reducing the nutrition burden of 3000 adults (2000 female, 1000 male), 5000 children (3500 girls and 1500 boys).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Dhusamareb district has enjoyed a relatively calm condition with less clan conflict over a couple of months now. Though in some recent events, the district has experience high influx of IDPs which may protract clan conflict. However this will be managed by the community elders and local government. The project is in line with Somalia 2017-2020 durable solutions strategy in place as well as Ministry of Health strategic plan. The project will also support the long term government intervention on Nutrition and health programming which aims at reducing malnutrition in the region. Community nutrition surveillance centers to be established will promote durable nutrition interventions within the community. Training of community health workers as part of the project activities will help in sustaining nutrition intervention in the health facilities thus promoting sustainability of health and nutrition activities in the district. TUOS will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project after the face out will be done through formation of structures such as Beneficiary Representatives and Community Hygiene Promoters with equal share of men and women. In order to meet the Nutritional needs of the affected community, TUOS will involve the target community through its leaders and engendered committees in the first stage of planning of the project to ensure community participation. The organization will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled to the right direction. TUOS will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The most vulnerable group to malnutrition are IDPs due to their vulnerability to food insecurity and inadequate health provision. They risk being profiled by the host community due to attention being given to them as opposed to the host community members. To avoid any form of exclusion in the project, TUOS will employ a Do-No-Harm approach principle and help the community to select project committees which will comprise of all the target group representatives and make the whole process as community driven. Beneficiaries targeting and selection committee will be formed from each group, that is host community and IDPs. A selection criteria will be agreed upon before the project starts and discussed upon with the donor for approval. Emphasis will be given to the most vulnerable individuals and households such as IDPs regardless of their age, gender and clan. TUOS will conduct due diligence of the list provided by the selection committee to ascertain the inclusiveness of the target beneficiaries. Periodic monitoring and evaluation exercise will be conducted with the aim of checking the changing dynamics in the target area and need for changes in the project implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Nutrition interventions may fail to reach the most vulnerable and, in some cases, may actually lead to further harm especially among the women and children. The risk of exclusion based on gender stereo type and age is prevalent in the community. The project targets pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months as well as vulnerable men and boys with malnutrition conditions. The selection committee will be trained on the selection criteria as such to promote inclusion of all gender and age group in the target community. Pregnant and lactating women and children under five years of age have special nutritional requirements and suffer severe consequences from undernutrition. Experience has shown, however, that other groups need to be involved in nutrition programmes as well to ensure that assistance for women and children is effective. Male family members have a strong influence on the feeding practices and eating habits of mothers and children. They also influence the healthcare practices and beliefs of mothers and other caregivers. The effectiveness of nutrition programmes can therefore depend on the active involvement of these groups. TUOS will collect sex- and age-disaggregated data on nutrition programme coverage, including: percentage of girls and boys aged 6-59 months treated for SAM; percentage of girls and boys under five, PLW in the target group covered by supplementary feeding programmes and treatment for moderate acute malnutrition; percentage of women, girls, boys and men who are still unable to meet nutritional requirements in spite of ongoing programming; exclusive breastfeeding rates for girls and boys; timely introduction of complementary food by age and sex and percentage of men and women benefitting from nutritional behaviour change interventions.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Women and children are the most vulnerable group during clan conflict and violence in the target area. To mitigate the effect of being adversely affected by the conflict when it occurs, TUOS will be working with community security team to protect women and children from attack. Also nutrition services will be provided at the community level or IDP centers thus avoiding the need to travel for such services. Also in times of conflict, TUOS will set up temporary hold up warehouse in the community to avoid supplies cut off and sustain the nutritional services to the beneficiaries. TUOS will also be working with community elders in the target areas such that the community elders themselves will be negotiating among the warring groups to allow services to continue if any of the target areas will be affected.
