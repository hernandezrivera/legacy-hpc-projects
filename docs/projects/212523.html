Project Summary

For a decade and a half, Syria has been suffering from a long and stifling crisis that has negatively affected all aspects of life, especially the purchasing power of those affected. The economic inflation resulting from the spread of the Covid-19 epidemic, along with the coercive sanctions on Syria and the physical destruction of the infrastructure, led to a decrease in the amount of usual and basic services provided by the public sector, as it constituted a large percentage of services. A number of qualified employees have left or been injured. Or, in the worst cases, they disappeared or died. Health services were mostly free before the crisis; However, being one of the most affected sectors in Syria, health services in public hospitals have become less available, with many hospitals and primary health care centers being completely or partially damaged. The number of centers that provide dialysis services for patients with kidney failure is small and very limited. In addition, there are many damaged centers, and a large number of public hospitals that were providing this service have been closed due to the Syrian crisis, especially in the suburbs of Damascus.
As for patients who suffer from chronic diseases, especially kidney failure, each dialysis session that can be performed in a private center costs more than one million Syrian pounds, which is equivalent to many times the salaries and wages in Syria. It is very expensive in Syria; Otherwise, the patient will have to wait on long waiting lists in public hospitals. Any patient who benefits from this service will have to stand on long waiting lists, which may last for two months or more, to obtain a permanent place for regular sessions. If they are lucky and succeed in getting a place, they may not get a dialysis session that should last 4 hours, but only 3 hours. The challenges we face
1- The first challenge is the lack of medical materials in the market due to the blockade and the high price of foreign currencies, and therefore we have materials for a three-month reserve
2- The second challenge is the interruption of electricity for long hours. For our part, we equipped the centers with electric generators and provided the centers with fuel tanks. In addition, because the price of fuel is rising, we installed a lithium battery system in the centers to ensure the continuity of the centers and devices in operation.
3- The third challenge is maintenance of the equipment, and therefore we work in shifts and work during vacation days so that patients do not lose their right to receive their sessions.
