Project Summary

Children and adolescents in the West Bank including East Jerusalem face severe and chronic protection challenges going to and from school. They cross Israeli military check points and closed military zones or pass by settlements where they may experience harassment and violence. The project will contribute to the 2021 HRP Education Objective 1: increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children. Children, adolescents and teachers facing obstacles to access schools or students exposed to risks of harassment will be supported to have safe access to schools; 15,000 students aged 6-16 and 250 teachers in H2, South Hebron Hills, Bethlehem, South Nablus, East Jerusalem and other selected areas in the West Bank will be targeted. This intervention has been developed in consultation with the affected communities and will also be coordinated with the Protection Cluster. It supports efforts to operationalize the guidelines of the Safe Schools Declaration creating a safer learning environment for children.
Several assessments continue to show the realization of the right to education remains severely compromised in these locations. In response, families may adopt negative coping mechanisms, such as taking students out of school to limit their exposure to Israeli violence and harassment, a practice more common among girls than boys. The lack of safe access has significant negative consequences for student’s schooling and results in a decrease of school hours, an increased drop-out rate of children, discouraging teaching staff and the displacement or separation of families as they seek solutions in getting their children in schools elsewhere. These negative factors affect children’s and adolescents’ well-being. The findings of a UNICEF evaluation of protective presence (March 2020) emphasized the need for a comprehensive approach to safe access to schools that complements protective presence with other mechanisms such as local solutions by communities. The project will be expanding the format so that interventions ensure and respond to issues of access, equity and gender equality, increased continuity of education and protection of crisis-affected children and adolescents. UNICEF will support the most vulnerable schools in the WB and EJ in the development and operationalization of a holistic schools plans where objectives, priorities and coordination mechanisms are clear for all actors, for the protection, wellbeing and safe access of students and teachers.
Interventions will include:
i) Establishment/strengthening of school management committees (SMCs), including training on how to provide a protective learning environment and safeguarding of children’s and teacher’s access to school.
ii) Training of teachers on how to provide Life Skills and psychosocial support with those identified as needing protection services referred to relevant protection service providers;
iii) Provision of school recreational and life skills activities for children and adolescents in schools or local community centers to serve as safe hubs;
iv) Support to the development and operationalization of holistic schools plans for safeguarding of student’s and teachers’ safe access.
The COVID-19 outbreak which resulted in school closures since the 6th of March 2020 has led to the exacerbation of existing needs among children and to emerging psychosocial needs which will be addressed as part of these interventions. The project will ensure equal opportunities for both girls and boys to participate in the activities as events will be announced publicly, encouraging participation of any gender. Recognizing that participation of girls in some communities is socio-culturally more sensitive, interventions will be adapted to ensure access and acceptance by girls and caregivers. Methods will be re-adjusted to respond better to the different needs of male and female students, SMCs and teachers, appropriate to the local context.

1 - Needs

The West Bank and particularly Area C, Hebron H2, and East Jerusalem, are witnessing a non-permissive environment, incidents of settler violence, increased rates of demolition and displacement, humanitarian needs and increased distress. Children continue to face violence and challenges in accessing schools. In the H2 area of Hebron, the withdrawal of over 60 Temporary International Presence in Hebron (TIPH) monitors in early 2019 has left a gap in human rights monitoring and protection especially for school children. In the year following the withdrawal, there has been an increase in settler attacks and harassment, as well as friction and clashes between Palestinians and Israeli forces. H2 was the most affected location in terms of violations against education in 2019, with 35 schools in the area serving 5,561 males and 7,167 females. The majority (88 per cent) of these children cross checkpoints daily to attend school, and parents report that their major concern is military harassment and settler violence against their children on the way to school. According to the OCHA Household Survey in H2 (2018), about “4,200 pupils, boys and girls, have their journey to school obstructed by checkpoints every day, frequently facing harassment, intimidation and delays that result in lost school time.” Teachers and school personnel who need to reach H2 also report frequent harassment, searches and humiliation at checkpoints. Contributing factors which put these particular children more at risk than others include having to pass through hot spot areas on the way to and from school; schools not providing a protective or supportive environment; difficult domestic backgrounds; the lack of safe places to play; and age of the child, with adolescent boys being the most vulnerable to being in conflict with Israeli Forces. In addition, there is an increased need for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and the creation of child-friendly spaces to increase the resilience of children whilst allowing them to continue learning.The discontinuity in education in 2302 Palestinian Authority (PA) schools in oPt (1,881 in WB and 421 in Gaza) after the announcement of the state of emergency in the country had a negative impact on the children, raising levels of anxiety and psychosocial distress. Children have no place to go or to connect with their peers besides their family at home. The situation impacted not only children, but also parents and teachers, who are also going through a hard time trying to cope with a totally new lifestyle. According to the Secretary General ‘s Annual Report on Children and Armed Conflict (June 2020), the United Nations verified four incidents of the military use of schools by Israeli forces and 242 other interferences with education by Israeli forces (229) and Israeli settlers (13). The overwhelming majority has been in Area C, the Hebron H2 area and East Jerusalem, affecting over 48,000 Palestinian children. In the West Bank, tear gas and rubber bullets are frequently fired at students, and Israeli forces make regular incursions into school grounds. Frequently, children must pass through one of 59 checkpoints, where they are subjected to bag and/or body searches, and at times harassment and detention. Students also face the risks of confrontation, intimidation and violence while walking past settlements or buildings occupied by settlers. These children can be exposed to conflict-related violence and delays in reaching schools, which can, coupled with other push factors such as a poor learning environment in school, lead to higher school drop-out rates. Girls face a higher risk of school drop-out due to the fear of parents for their safety during the school commute. Several assessments clearly demonstrate the need for protection interventions.

2 - Activities

1. Establishment/strengthening of school management committees consisting of school principals, teachers, students, parents, community leaders and community-based organizations (CBOs) and training them on how to provide protective learning environments and safe access to school for children, adolescents and teachers 2. Training of teachers on how to provide Life skills activities and psycho-social support (PSS) to children and adolescents that may have been traumatized after being exposed to conflict going to or returning from school (in collaboration with child protection) 3. Provision of school recreational activities for children and adolescents in selected schools or local community centres to serve as safe hubs for different art, sports and educational activities4. Support Safeguarding of children’s and teachers’ commute to and from school In each targeted school, school management committees (SMCs) composed of representatives from: the school principal, teachers, students, parents, community leaders and community based organization (CBO) will be formed and trained on how to identify and provide local solutions to provide protective learning environments for safe access to school for children taking into consideration local context. The SMCs will develop holistic school plans including an operational plan with clear objectives, priorities and coordination mechanisms for the protection and wellbeing of students and teachers. The composition of the SMCs will ensure gender representation. The SMCs will work closely with Child Protection networks to ensure protection mainstreaming. Teachers will be trained on how to provide PSS in collaboration with child protection and children identified as needing protection services will be referred to relevant protection service providers.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Central to upholding Do No Harm principles, is the implementation of the Partner’s Child Safeguarding Policy and reporting mechanism in which all staff, partners, and volunteers are trained to prevent harm to children they work with. NGOs and partners will be made aware of the importance of a child safeguarding policy and will be supported to develop their own. In addition, ensuring that the implementing partner’s staff are trained in the Core Humanitarian Standards for Quality and Accountability, as well as on Inclusion, also ensuring the application of confidentiality and best interests in all activities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The targeted schools/areas will be based on the Cluster list of most vulnerable schools cross referenced with the vulnerable community assessment list developed by UNICEF and relevant authorities.During implementation, the project we will ensure equal and meaningful participation of children. This will be measured through a group exercise or survey to measure how children perceive the quality of their participation in the programme. This allows children to provide feedback on different aspects of their equal and meaningful participation in activities.
