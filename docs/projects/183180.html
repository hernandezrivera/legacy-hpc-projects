Project Summary

Concern is targeting two distinct geographical locations due the volume and vulnerability level of the people suffering food insecurity in these governorates. 1: 665 migrant community HHs in Beirut and Mount Lebanon and 2: 714 vulnerable Lebanese HHs in Northern Lebanon - the North and Akkar Governorates.
This project includes the food security component of the LHF-funded food security and nutrition project entitled “Food security for 665 migrant households and nutrition prevention for 1000 migrant children in Beirut and Mount Lebanon”, under the 2021 first standard allocation.
In Beirut and Mount Lebanon, HHs will receive balanced and nutritious food (both dry rations and fresh vegetables) for a period of 11 months. All recipients will also receive multi-sector messaging translated into languages appropriate for migrant workers in Lebanon (Amharic, Bengali, Singhalese, French, English). The project will work with members of three Migrant-Led Initiatives (Mesewet, Together Forever and AUCL) and focus on building their capacity in warehouse management, distributions, M&E, accountability to affected populations and referrals; ensuring benefits that last beyond the duration of the project. Mothers and caregivers of children among the beneficiary cohort aged 6-23 months will receive IYCF; and of those aged 6-59 months family MUAC and micro-nutrient supplements (see nutrition sector application for details). Children aged 5-18 years who are undocumented will be enrolled in the migrant school (see education sector application for details) and be provided with hygiene and baby kits (see WASH proposal). This project is embedded within Concern’s strategic approach to working with migrant workers in Greater Beirut, which combines addressing immediate and basic needs with long term efforts to address the underlying and structural causes of migrant vulnerability, and works with and through migrant-led initiatives in a migrant-community based, bottom-up approach
For the North, Concern will conduct a mixed targeting approach to provide in-kind dry food ration distributions to the most vulnerable Lebanese households that are beneficiaries of CWW’s exiting shelter, protection and livelihoods projects in the north. The vulnerability criteria prioritises people with disabilities or care takers, women headed household, families with a high number of household individuals, and unemployed people. Of the total 714 HHs targeted,: 414HHs are struggling farmers and livestock holders who have been unable to produce enough for sale and subsistence due to the increased cost of imported agricultural inputs and fuel; 200 HHs under Concern’s protection and case management programming that are not receiving any WFP or MOSA assistance and whose children may be at risk of child labour; early marriage or school dropout; and 100HHs in the Concern Shelter interventions who are receiving cash for rent but no food assistance and are unable to meet these basic needs.
Food distributions will be conducted door-to-door in Beirut and in Akkar and Tripoli through designated food distribution points. The distribution points will be assessed for security and additional precautions will be taken to ensure all staff/beneficiary safety, including COVID 19. Beneficiaries will receive a transport allowance to access the points.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Identification and verification of vulnerability of beneficiaries. For migrants Concern will: define food preferences for adapting the food parcels; warehouse operation for the safe and correct storage of food items (Beirut warehouse will be managed by MLI members and will include dry and fresh food for distribution); door-to-door distributions by MLI members on weekends to ensure access to beneficiaries over a period of 11 months. Each household will receive dry and fresh food every month, plus two rounds of hygiene and baby materials and; translation and dissemination of IEC materials. Dry food parcels will be distribution to 714 vulnerable Lebanese HHs through designated food distribution points (FDPs) for 11 months. Additional activities include: post-distribution monitoring; identification of additional protection concerns and referrals to appropriate service providers; dietary diversity assessments (pre- and post-distributions); implementation of complaints response mechanism.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Concern globally adheres to its internal standards for protection, with policies in place. All staff, partners and contractors with Concern will adhere to Concern’s Code of Conduct and associated safeguarding policies with well-established investigation teams and disciplinary processes. Concern also carries out training on PSEA for its staff and partners on a regular basis. Concern's team maintains a service map and close relationships with service providers. The referrals procedures follow all inter-agency guidelines. Concern's Outreach Officers will assist in making referrals, receiving feedback and addressing complaints. Concern has an experienced protection team who are available to assist in protection risk analysis, including identification of safeguarding risks as well as appropriate mitigation measures, regular review and adaptation of risk mitigation measures.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Concern Lebanon has an established Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) to ensure accountability towards all beneficiaries. It collects any complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and ensure a genuine beneficiaries’ complaints are investigated and addressed. Dedicated communication lines are managed by Concern staff to receive concerns and provide responses in a timely manner. Project participants are also encouraged to give verbal feedback, especially through Concern’s outreach officers.
