Objectives

Improved access to quality survivor-centered services responding to individual needs, prevention and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowerment of women, girls and survivors of GBV

Needs

1. A high number of women and girls have been exposed to widespread and severe forms of sexual violence in Myanmar before and during flight. Following displacement, they continue to be at risk of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), including domestic violence, forced and early marriage, and sexual exploitation. 17% of households are female-headed therefore being exposed to increased risk of violence. Abuse and neglect inside the home have reportedly increased since the beginning of the influx, along with traditional practices like polygamy. Response to trends of Intimate Partner Violence is rendered difficult by the lack of access to justice and the perceived impunity in the camp. Mobility of Rohingya women and girls to access services is restricted by a number of factors including cultural practices, perceived insecurity within camps, and severe overcrowding. The most urgent need to respond to this high risk of GBV for all women is quality and accessible GBV response services, which include survivor-centered case management services, and psychosocial support services. 2. Many women and men are not aware of the existing GBV services and the possibilities of support in the camp. The need for appropriate and widespread information on GBV, and prevention and response services is therefore acute, through awareness sessions targetting different groups of the population. 3. A significantly high number of women and girls are illiterate, with few opportunities to learn as education opportunities for adolescents and adults, vocational training, and livelihoods, are restricted. The lack of opportunities for women increases their vulnerability, their exclusion of the community life, and adoption of negative coping mechanisms that are potentially harmful. There is a need for women to be engaged in lifeskills activities that build their resilience and decrease their vulnerability, such as vocational trainings, and livelihoods where possible, to foster social and economic empowerment.

Activities or outputs

Under GBV Sub Sector, Caritas will conduct the Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) project which aims at the Protection, empowerement and resilience of Rohingya women and girls. The project will build and run 3 WFS. 1 will be in Camp 20 Extension (ongoing), and the location of the 2 other WFS are still to be determined according to the needs and gaps assessed both by GBV SS and Site Management agencies, and by Caritas capacity to implement a service in the areas. The WFS focus on 3 key areas as detailed below. 1. Life skills and resilience programming. The goal is to make women empowered through activities such as sewing, tailoring, handicraft etc. Technical trainings will be conducted. Livelihoods will be considered to increase self-sufficiency and skills. An assessment will be conducted at the beginning of the project to define the most relevant lifeskill sessions, and develop appropriate content and material. 2. Psychosocial support and case management. PSS workers will facilitate individual and group counselling sessions in the WFS. WFS will allow identification of individuals at risk and survivors of Gender Based Violence. Case management will be conducted by case workers, following Case Management international standards and guidance. Caritas Bangladesh will be part of the GBVIMS, and align its information management system in coordination with GBV Sub Sector. 3. Awareness raising and community-based protection. Awareness raising on issues such as early marriage, intimate partner violence, trafficking, etc will take place in the WFS. Awareness sessions will also inform on GBV prevention and response services available. As issues evolve very rapidly in this context of protracted crisis, we would review the specific activities regularly. Issues such as disaster preparedness and risk reduction, legal advice, parenting sessions etc. would be included. Women and girls would be encouraged to propose their ideas and facilitate sessions themselves.Women groups will also be encouraged to advocate for their issues at community level. In Camp 4 Extension where elected committees are closely associated to Site Management, in replacement of Majhis, women will be encouraged to be engaged in the block leaders' groups. Complementarity between this project and the Barefoot Counsellors project will be developed. Barefoot Counsellors are Rohingya refugees conducting protection outreach and counselling, with a focus on GBV. Barefoot Counsellors will conduct outreach and be able to inform and orientate women towards the WFS, and refer for case management if needed. New barefoot counsellors could be identified from the women who are the most active and engaged in the WFS. 4. Targets: We target 300+ women for regular attendance and participation in each WFS. In total, 1000 women will be involved in the structured skills and empowerment. 1000 women are also estimated to benefit from structured PSS service, depending on the needs. The awareness raising sessions target more broadly with an estimate of 2500 participants over the year. Targets specific to each camp will be set up when location is finalized, according to the needs and population figures in the camp.
