Project Summary

A HIPAA compliant digital healthcare platform to provide multi-cluster life-saving telehealth services for crisis affected people to reduce mortality & morbidity and improve vulnerable people’ s access to life-sustaining basic services. It enables granular data to be collected from the community level of the health system in real time to quantify needs, capacities, logistics, projections for operations & budgeting/resource allocation, as well as for coordinating humanitarian aid, operational alignment by state, reporting, scaling, & strategic planning. This program can greatly enhance reporting for the HC, optimize operations, & bring the HC closer to target. It can align health data from the community with the national Health Information System & the WHO strategy.
It is not a standalone solution, but a digital tool across multiple sectors of the Health cluster to enable the existence of healthcare online when in person visits are impossible during conflict. It reduces commute to hospitals & exposure to risk of death/kidnap/GBV/looting/etc. by allowing increased access to healthcare & reliable lifesaving information & it frees up space in congested overwhelmed hospitals. Thus it provides protection to vulnerable populations & bridges the communication gap between authorities including the Health Cluster [FMOH-WHO-UNICEF-UNFPA-INGOs-NGOs] & the community [civilians-clinicians-diaspora]. It can be used to raise health awareness for clinicians & the communities they serve with instructions & communication from policy makers during disease outbreaks or health system disruption to increase disease surveillance through detection/screening.
Patients are able to access the system through designated health facilities or make appointments directly through the Platform to consult with a provider. Physicians are able to manage cold cases, set their schedules & view patient information & medication to better manage treatments. Pharmacies are able to provide service online. Hospitals are able to view patient data anonymously which allows them to manage their inventories & triage patient influx. Health Insurance organizations are also able to access data anonymously which enables them to track usage, spending, and reimbursements, while encouraging patients to sign up for universal coverage. Health authorities such as Sudan Federal Ministry of Health [FMOH], Health Specialties Center, & the Medical Council are able to use this data to make informed decisions on healthcare capacities, consumption, needs, HR distribution, and healthcare outcomes to improve healthcare delivery and the healthcare system long term.
In partnering with the Sudanese Family Planning Association [SFPA] centers nationwide [24 stationary clinics & 37 mobile clinics] this platform will be offering maternal and child health services, including antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care, SRH, and GBV. In partnering with UNICEF, it can support nutrition activities for malnourished children, pregnant & lactating women, & adolescent girls in terms of screening & detection, prevention, evidence generation, advocating, monitoring, complaints & referrals. Similarly, in collaborating with the FMOH-NCD directorate, 261 FMOH centers can be utilized to manage chronic conditions that have been neglected & exacerbated as a result of recent focus on conflict & emergency services. As NCDs account for 52% of all annual deaths in Sudan, telemedicine can be used in cold case management for NCDs, Oncology, & Disability including neurology/autism as per FMOH.
Telehealth coordinators on the ground will liaise between patients & providers, 10 pharmacies are available to provide medication. There are 4 tech support specialists to train & onboard clinicians onto the platform, starting with Port Sudan to arrange logistics & timelines with local teams. Reporting will be agreed upon along with monitoring & evaluation for monthly & quarterly reviews. Complaints & suggestions are welcomed.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Online healthcare service reduces commute to hospitals/medical facilities & exposure to risk of death/kidnap/GBV/looting/etc. It provides safe space with privacy & confidentiality for women to speak with healthcare providers, ask questions, & be encouraged to seek the help needed. By allowing increased access to healthcare & reliable lifesaving information online telehealth provides protection to vulnerable populations. Risk mitigation will be addressed using the proposed interventions from the HC, situational analysis to identify standard activities for girls & at risk groups, identification of related risks such as seclusion, especially during conflict escalation, etc. Our aim is to raise awareness regarding the repercussions of GBV and the significance of the initial 72 hours to seek healthcare. Clinicians providing care can be regularly trained on HC guidelines & recommendations to triage or refer patients to relevant specialties. Midwives & female health workers can be directed to help survivors & their families overcome stigma, create safe environments to provide the required attention, maintain their own safety, highlight lifesaving interventions & provide or recommend ‘’Dignity Kits’’ explaining the standard item list & the vulnerability criteria. Social media will be used to raise awareness about possible scenarios, HIV, STDs, mental health, eliminate guilt, mitigate risk from family misconception/retaliation, recommend nearest sites for treatment, options to consider, sources for ongoing support, mechanisms for reporting, etc.Some mainstreaming activities may be to ensure telehealth facilities are known to survivors, safe & accessible to GBV survivors while monitoring services in place to ensure commodities & follow-up care are accessible. Other activities may be generating data & evidence to inform national & local policies to mitigate the risk of exploitation. ,.Identifying & referring cases of GBV, enabling hours of operation convenient for women, girls and other at-risk groups based on their household duties & school times, providing staff who is specialized in GBV case management. Educating at risk groups on factors that may exacerbate the risks of GBV in the particular setting (e.g. displacement; closeness to armed forces; unsafe routes for firewood/water collection, etc.) & educating survivors & their communities on strengthening the ability of community protection mechanisms (e.g. child protection committees, watch committees, families and kinship networks, religious structures) to monitor risks of GBV against children and adolescents. It is also in our interest to ensure service providers understand national &/or local laws, policies, the procedures related to mandatory reporting of violence & apply these best practices. It is our duty to carefully manage sensitive information related to GBV, especially data storage & access control.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will share information and collect community feedback via one on one consultations, group consultations with aid workers, Webinars, surveys, phone calls, SMS, social media, flyers, emails, complaints mechanism or suggestion box, etc. The project aims to measure GBV-related points for monitoring & evaluation by tracking progress & adverse effects on GBV mainstreaming activities, allowing the participation of women, girls & other at-risk groups, including outcome-level indicators to measure program impact on GBV-related risks, disaggregating indicators by sex, age, disability & other vulnerability factors.Community feedback will be discussed in-house & analyzed to be used for improving & adapting activities that further enhance the service in addressing protection. This can be categorized into HC sectors that can be addressed through telehealth such as health, nutrition, WASH, food security, livelihood, education, & protection. Each component can have a set of questions & action points to be discussed with survivors/patients as they receive care/therapy via telehealth or in group sessions depending on topics. The content for awareness can be structured to set the scene with potential scenarios to ensure recipients can identify situations & precursors if any, understand their options & resources, are confident how to react & know where to go for further support. They also need to know how to report, seek advocacy, overcome trauma, take steps to heal & move on with their lives, know how to deal with any health issues that may arise, not blame themselves but be free of guilt, know how to protect themselves, & educate their families/care takers/communities.Telehealth will use the 3 main types of responsibilities: programing, policies, and communications & information sharing in accordance with HIPAA guidelines. It will put GBV-related risk reduction responsibilities into practice to improve the overall quality of GBV-related prevention & mitigation strategies, establish GBV-related responsibilities common to all actors, recommend strategies for humanitarian actors to reduce risks, maximize immediate protection of GBV survivors & persons at risk and foster longer-term interventions to eliminate GBV. It will identify & collaborate with local organizations and undergo regular training with the HC whenever possible.Engaging the community in the process & incorporating their feedback in the efforts with rewarding incentives will give the community ownership of the concept & allow it to be absorbed by community leaders & youth. This will increase awareness of SEA in both the affected population, local & humanitarian staff, including how to report SEA incidents. As more cases are identified & the number of reported SEA incidents in the target area visibly increases community acceptance of the mechanism will increase. Bringing healthcare closer to home at the palm of patient hands literally via telehealth will ease the tension of seeking care outside immediate environment of survivors. Providing 130 doctors & 100 lawyers online via Athiq Organization members will facilitate advocacy for clinicians & patients alike to enable faster uptake of community feedback. Customized automated dashboards will feed data for analytic visualization of deliverables for evidence based decision making, process excellence, & strategic formulations.Telehealth is a multi-cluster, lifesaving, preventive, protective, & curative service during & beyond conflict in Sudan. It provides healthcare OPD as an emergency service for quick results & big impact in collaboration with implementing partners such as SFPA, UNICEF, FMOH-NCD. These dashboards will enhance collating health information & generating evidence to inform decision making & policy planning. This can also shift the patient mindset to initiate transformation of PHC 2024 into preventive, patient centered, cost effective, health system strengthening approach for LMIC such as Sudan.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Evidence generated data & its analysis will be anonymously shared in accordance with HIPAA guidelines. This will enable partners to obtain granular data in real time from the community level to collaborate, share best practice, align with national strategy, share training & resources, increase reporting & extend the reach to achieve targets & maximize impact. Personal patient information will be encrypted. An inter-agency community-based complaint mechanism to handle reports of sexual abuse & exploitation by humanitarian aid workers will be established based on the existing interagency guideline & customized for digital use. In order to aid identified & potential survivors, it will be culturally sensitive to maximize its safety & effectiveness. It will aim to facilitate sexual exploitation & abuse SEA reporting & allegation referrals, & prevention through training & raising awareness. The inter-agency approach will ensure that the mechanism can receive complaints against actors from multiple organizations, & that the complaints will be referred to the proper unit within each organization for follow-up in order to comprehensively respond to SEA in an emergency response operation. This will increase awareness of SEA in both the affected population, local & humanitarian staff, including how to report SEA incidents. As more cases are identified & the number of reported SEA incidents in the target area visibly increases community acceptance of the mechanism will increase. Gradually progress will be achieved through a systematic & coherent organization-wide approach as an integral part of good quality programing in development & humanitarian situations. Accountability to affected populations will comprise of a telehealth protection program planning cycle, management decision-making, operations & resource management, as well as partnership agreements, & monitoring & evaluation. Telehealth will use the established pillars of accountability principles & good practices that put vulnerable people at the center of both development & emergency programs such as; Participation, information & communication, feedback & complaints, protection from sexual exploitation & abuse, strengthening local capacity, evidence based advocacy & decision making, coordination & partnerships. Telehealth is a multicluster, lifesaving, preventive, protective, & curative service during & beyond conflict in Sudan. It provides healthcare OPD as an emergency service for quick results & big impact, yet it simultaneously establishes a system for long term health system strengthening & alignment from the bottom up community level with the top down health authority objectives. It does this in collaboration with implementing partners such as SFPA, UNICEF, FMOH-NCD for sustainable measures & in order to extend the reach & enable HC partners to achieve their targets in providing healthcare as well as collating health information & generating evidence to inform decision making & policy.
