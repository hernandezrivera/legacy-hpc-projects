1 - Needs

According to the October 2022 Cadre Harmonise analysis (projection for June-August 2022), around 3.5 million people in the BAY states are projected to be food insecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations classification in June to Sep lean season among them 392,248 are residing in hard to reach areas. The security situation in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states remains fragile as seen in the pockets of attacks predominantly in Borno and some parts of Yobe and Adamawa, which continues to induce displacement, livelihood depletion, and limited functionality of basic services in the most affected areas. These factors have impacted negatively on the food consumption, livelihood, and nutritional situation within both displaced population and host community households. The food security and nutrition situation remain extremely fragile, particularly in Borno State. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food needs, including cooking energy, which particularly increases protection risks for women and children. Access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets, and livelihood activities remains limited. Market and trade routes remain disrupted, restricting trade flows and activities. This is further exacerbated by route disruptions during the rainy season, increasing cases of sporadic hostilities, and the shrinking of humanitarian space In addition, the Covid -19 pandemic the increment of food price and exacerbated an economic fall out. Furthermore, the Borno government has started the closure of camps, which in turn push the humanitarian organizations to work under pressure with higher coordination from sectors to meet the dire needs of the people. . Vulnerable people arriving from inaccessible areas are in critical need of access to nutritious foods. Meanwhile, almost 392,268 thousand people live in inaccessible areas and do not have access to the life-saving aid they urgently need. Limited information suggests some level of farming activities, critical nutrition situation, and usage of severe coping strategies among the inaccessible population. The following are the specific needs: • Food assistance through the most appropriate modalities. • Assistance to support safe access to fuel-e client technologies through the most appropriate modalities. • Agriculture livelihood support including provision of inputs/assets through the most appropriate modalities, community-based productive asset creation/enhancement activities, provision of agriculture extension services, entrepreneurship, technology transfer, value addition, and processing capacity-building (Agricultural livelihoods refers to crop production, livestock, fisheries, forestry, and natural resources management). • Coordination among all stakeholders with evidence-based FS analysis and advocacy to inform the response. • Protectionintegration/mainstreaming in FS response. To effectively deliver the above-planned activities, it is crucial that robust and effective coordination mechanisms with strong information management capacities remain in place at the federal and state level. The coordination within the sector can generate useful information that can be used by other sectors and WGs for synergies, complementarities, and integration. This will help ensure timely, appropriate, collaborative, and integrated food security response to people affected by the crisis in northeast Nigeria.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Support the Food Security Sector coordination at federal and state levels -including support payment of sector coordination personnel; conduct regular FSS, SAFEWG, Livestock and Fisheries Taskforce, and Agriculture & Livelihood Taskforce meetings at state and federal level; undertake and lead sector planning, strategy development, emergency preparedness, and monitoring Activity 2: Support information management and food security analysis for the northeast crisis - including support payment of sectorIM personnel; support data collection and information analysis, and develop IM tools and maintain FSS website; Activity 3: Strengthen local capacities including the national sector leads at state and federal level to lead and support FS coordination - ensuring the involvement of authorities through "learning by doing" approach; strengthen state-level coordination mechanisms built on existing structures; provide logistics, equipment, and materials to build local capacities. Activity 4: Conduct joint protection monitoring missions with protection partners, protection integration capacity building, and develop related localized sector-specific guidance/checklists. In line with the Centrality protection strategy, the sector aims at continuous advocacy/sensitization among partners to ensure a minimum of 5% of a project budget to be used towards protection mainstreaming activities as well as integration of commitment to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in any FSL project design. Ex: Sector partners' minimum standard: each partner should have a Code of Conduct that addresses PSEA issues and staff are committed to it. Activity 5: Continue working with other stakeholders (e.g. camp management) and key partners to strengthen and streamline existing complaint and feedback mechanisms and the referral pathways among partners. Activity 6: At least one sector/Cluster Coordination Performance Monitoring survey will be conducted to monitor and evaluate the sector's performance based on stakeholders' feedback.
