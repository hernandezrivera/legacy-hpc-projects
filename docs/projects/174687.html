Project Summary

East Region of Cameroon in currently hosting CAR refugee in a bordering area which is among the poorest of the country. The civil registration sector appears to be a more structural and global issue, with cross-cutting consequences affecting other sectors. In addition to the poor coverage in civil registration centres (main and secondary) and the inadequacy of infrastructure, equipment and staff, socio-cultural factors affect people's access to civil registration, both for host populations and refugees. Indeed, the populations still do not understand the importance of issuing birth certificates to their children, thus not respecting the legal deadlines for registering births. This situation very often leads to costly legal proceedings to establish supplementary judgements after the fact. There are thus many school-age children who do not have a birth certificate and who are often forced to stop their studies in CM2 classes because they cannot register for official examinations. Similarly, this situation undermines the protection of personal identity and puts people at risk of statelessness. This is all the more worrying for refugee populations who, once they return to their country of origin, risk not being recognized by their country.
The majority of refugees are mostly "Off-Site" for 70% of them. Their subsistence is limited to the hospitality of host populations and costly humanitarian care, both of which tend to run out of steam. The brutal intensification of population growth in these areas, where poverty indicators were already worrying, has led to strong pressure on natural resources, infrastructure and basic services, which are already insufficient.
Faced with these problems, the overall objective of the ICLA intervention will be to help the populations affected by the Central African conflict to claim and assert their rights to protection and legal personality. NRC will use its long experience in legal aid to provide information, advice and legal assistance on legal identity and human rights, as well as on capacity building and advocacy to promote the search for sustainable solutions.
Since July 2019, NRC, through its core competence ICLA, launched activities in the Kadey division, and plans to expand into the divisions of Lom and Djerem and Boumba and Ngoko of East Region of Cameroon. For that:
NRC will develop activities related to legal identity through the working group on civil documentation, in collaboration with BUNEC, communes, humanitarian actors and government representatives.
NRC will strengthen its response and provide support to refugee populations and vulnerable host communities so that they can access their LTP rights. NRC will organize information and awareness raising sessions for refugees and host communities on issues related to LTP and collaborative conflict resolution.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The right to a name, identity, and to be registered at birth is a right of all children which is stated in various human rights instruments, including the Convention on the Rights of the Child. Unfortunately, millions of children around the world continue to go unregistered despite increasing awareness and efforts to improve birth registration systems; many refugees, other displaced persons, and stateless persons often face significant barriers with respect to registering the birth of their children. To escape the historical repetition of violence, many Central Africans are forced to move within their country, but also outside it, in search of safety. According to the United Nations Refugee Agency (UNHCR), nearly 593,000 are refugees in neighbouring countries. Cameroon, which shares some 800 km of border with the Central African Republic, is the country hosting the largest number of Central African refugees. According to the UNHCR, 47.8% of refugees, or 296,190 Central Africans, are currently settled in the camps and host communities of East, Adamaoua and North regions. In the Eastern region, it should be noted, however, that the refugees at the sites represent only about 28 % of the total number of refugees are settled in camps. Possession of a birth certificate is already difficult for hots communities, though it has become challenging for refugee households. In East Region of Cameroon which is among the poorest of the country, access to registration services is hampered by distance, poor functioning of existing centers, aggravated by ill practices and ignorance. As a whole, both the demand of services and service offer are weak. Being in remote areas, locations hosting the greater number of refugees along the border with Central African Republic neither have access to practical information on legal framework governing registration, nor do the civil status registrars who still interpreting the law and context on their own. Despites improvements on laws, most of the duty bearers keep thinking that delivering a birth certificate to a refugee child equates the attribution of Cameroonian nationality. As such, declaration of birth which is already rare in this areas since most of the deliveries are done by midwifes in homes, becomes irrelevant as concerns issuance of birth certificate. In emergencies, one issue, which at the very least is paramount, is not directly addressed as a priority for intervention: land tenure issues. The permanent stay of the refugees and the implementation of the various activities (setting up sites, support for agropastoral activities) require secure and rational access to land that preserves the rights and uses of the host populations. Despite this good will, some restrictions built on community land tenure practices have been observed in the nature and extent of land rights transferred to beneficiaries. In the host villages, there is now a mixed perception of the presence of refugees. If at the outset, the discourse on the need for assistance to populations in distress was sufficient to facilitate integration, cohabitation and the provision of land, the strong pressures resulting from the permanent settlement of refugees encourage a latent climate of conflict. On the one hand, the presence of refugees has accentuated agro-pastoral conflicts, alongside with land and property issues. NRC launched its activities in East region in 2019, and access to rights to legal identity is one of the key component; during these two years, the root causes handicapping access to civil and legal documentation as welle as Housing land and Property rights were challenged and resulted in structural positive changes thanks to partnership work at field level and advocacy.The aim of this project is to ensure the effective fulfilment and enjoyment of legal documentation rights and housing, land and property rights in the project area.
