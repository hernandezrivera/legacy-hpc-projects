Project Summary

iMMAP’s Syria programming is framed under providing support to Operational Hubs under the framework of the WoS coordination approach/structure. iMMAP’s IMRC encompasses the core of iMMAP’s strategy to improve outcomes and decision-making through a three-tiered approach to IM, coordination and analysis which extends support to all levels of the humanitarian community - partner, operational, and strategic levels. Through this tiered approach, the IMRC aims to provide innovative solutions, establish best practices, analysis, and participate in coordinated IM strategies and assessments. As it has since 2015, iMMAP will continue to provide throughout the duration of this project extensive direct support to the annual HPC cycle at various levels across hubs, including through the development of HNO Syria report, HNO microsite, the mid-year and end-year Periodic Monitoring Report (PMR) as well as the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). iMMAP’s support will cover the IM lifecycle, ranging from assessment design, sampling strategy, data collection, data analysis, GIS and data visualization, to information process reviews and change management.
The activities proposed by iMMAP are the result of its involvement in the coordination structures across the response – these include, but are not limited to, Sector/Cluster coordination, hub level coordination and working groups, Inter Sector Working Groups, Information Management working group, HNO/HRP coordination, and Durable Solutions platforms at hub and regional level, amongst others. Therefore, iMMAP IMO’s analysts, and thematic experts are fully engaged in the structures and are aware of the gaps, activities, and operational challenges faced by partners. iMMAP support and services continues to be one of the primary channels for data-sharing and enhanced coordination within the sectors, hubs, and at the WoS level. iMMAP’s IMRC team is composed of specialists with expertise in data management, geoinformatics, data analytics, sector experts, data visualization and conflict analysis. The majority of the IMRC team are locally hired and trained officers, many of whom are alumni of iMMAP’s capacity building programs provided through previous BHA grants.

3 - Needs

The iMMAP Information Management Resource Center (IMRC) utilizes a matrix structure with units designed to integrate the full service of information management and research and analysis, while also dedicating technical capabilities and thematic expertise that allow iMMAP to respond to the specific needs of BHA and its partners in the area of information management and technology. The ultimate goal is to fill gaps by providing critical information management (IM), analysis and coordination support to humanitarian actors responding to the Syria crisis. Namely, for the FSS cluster, iMMAP has continued in 2022 and will continue in 2023 to be in line with the needs of organizations working in the FSS and in close coordination with FSL cluster leads, as well as direct support to partner needs when requested and within available capacity addressing the full range of factors that contribute to food insecurity and livelihood loss in Northern Syria, as overlapping crises continue to mount, driving increased food insecurity and livelihood loss in recent years. As for the need of filing information gaps, the research&analysis unit produces geopolitical reports and presentations to relevant working groups and sectors that can shift based on the changing information needs landscape. Finally, IM pillar supports all levels of coordination framework gap and ensure that works-streams are driven by operational priorities and consistent with the strategic direction set by the WoS clusters.

4 - Implementation of activities

Delivers dedicated and targeted IM services to operational hubs, UN agencies, sectors and partner organizations. IM support to NGO Fora responding to the Syria crisis (e.g., Syria INGO Regional Forum (SIRF), North-east Syria (NES) NGO Forum) through 1) IM expertise for assessing information flows, coordinating general IM activities, encouraging information sharing, improving existing tools, as well as developing and implementing IM strategies for sub-working groups, and 2) coordination and implementation of thematic activities in the various areas of responsibility. Specifically for the Food Security & Agriculture, iMMAP works to develop capabilities and combine findings from FSS deliverables on crop production, market analysis, and food security and livelihood outcomes to build a fully integrated approach to monitoring food security and livelihood, and to coordinate over these analysis products. The Research & Analysis unit provides monthly geo-political updates and responds to request of partners through punctual request of information and Ad hoc report.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security plans are in place for iMMAP NES office, covering hibernation, relocation and evacuation. Contingency planning and safety considerations are covered by specific SOPs to mitigate against the identified risks within the overall iMMAP Security Risk Assessment. Specifically, SOPs cover day to day movement as well as Relocation and Evacuation and Medical Evacuation. A weekly operational meeting includes delivery of security updates to ensure all staff are aware of any contextual changes and to maintain the safety and security of staff, assets, premises, and operations.
