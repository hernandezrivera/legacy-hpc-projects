Project Summary

LEAP is informed by proven, evidence-based approaches, programmatic lessons and consultations with stakeholders, including girls, boys, women, men, community leaders, partners and government actors. The project duration is 36-month and projected budget is $6.8 million covering the following working areas : CXB Sadar, Ramu, Ukhiya, Teknaf and 3 Camp- 1W, 3 and 4).But under the provision of JRP, the project will cover 7 locations- Teknaf- Sabrang & Teknaf Sadar, Ukhiya- Jaliapalong & Holudiapalong, Camp-1W, 3 and 4 of Ukhiya. The project will employ a rights-based health services , gender-transformative (GT) and three-pronged strategy to: i) improve the individual and collective agency and resilience of adolescent girls and young women to exercise their SRH and protection rights; ii) strengthen health systems to provide sex- and age-responsive quality health and protection services; and iii) enhance the capacity of local women's rights organizations (WROs), including girl-led organizations (GLOs), and youth-led organizations (YLOs) on evidence based advocacy for Gender and Adolescent Responsive and Inclusive (GARI) health and protection services while advocating for long-term change for evidence-based GARI, SRH, SGBV and MHPSS services and programming. Improved, integrated SRHR outcomes for girls have shown to contribute to better lifelong outcomes in education, protection, livelihoods and overall wellbeing (Brookings, 2016). LEAP’s multi-sectoral and feminist approach works across health and protection sectors to strengthen horizontal linkages while integrating women and girl's agency throughout and engaging WROs/GLOs and YLOs as influencers to improve accountability for GARI SRH and SGBV services and policies. Aligned with national and local humanitarian, health, protection and GE policies and strategies, as well as the SDGs and Canada’s Feminist International Assistance Policy (FIAP), LEAP will contribute to poverty reduction by targeting marginalized groups in Rohingya camps and host communities facing multifaceted dimensions of exclusion. In partnership with four local partners (three implementing and one technical), LEAP will directly reach 97,774 people, where 60,578 female + 37,196 are male including four Unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts, as well as three Rohingya refugee camps (37,529 where 21,462 is in 4 Host Union + 16,067 is in 3 camp).

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of 20 districts in Bangladesh lagging the national average for development indicators, with approximately 33% of the population of 2.29 million (2011) living below the poverty line compared to the national average of 31.5% (Care, 2018; World Bank, 2016). The influx of Rohingya refugees since 2017 has brought new and compounding challenges to the already weak local health and protection systems (UNHCR, Policy Brief, June 2020.) Approximately 949,000 people live in host communities (49.6% women and girls) and 860,000 Rohingya refugees live in camps (51.7% women and girls), most of whom face deteriorating situations in SRHR and protection (UNOCHA, 2020 COVID-19 Response Plan – Addendum to the JRP: Rohingya Humanitarian Crisis, Apr-Dec 2020). Families, especially women and girls, are exposed to threats from criminal groups, trafficking, violence, child abuse and neglect and child, early and forced marriage (CEFM). The incidence of CEFM has increased during the pandemic due to the disruption of education and economic activity and rising food prices. There are still gaps in the outreach of safe and quality GBV services and referral mechanisms (SAFERWORLD, June 2021). Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services are non-existent. Poor mental health can be associated with risky sexual behavior and substance abuse, resulting in negative impacts on the SRHR of adolescent girls and young women, including heightened vulnerability to unintended pregnancy, unsafe abortion, Reproductive Tract Infections (RTI), Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) and Diseases (STDs) and SGBV. Harmful social, gender and patriarchal norms, attitudes and behaviours present in both host community and camp settings disempower women and girls. Unequal power relations, compounded by the impacts of COVID-19, confine women and girls with the burden of unpaid care work, increase their risk of intimate partner violence (IPV), hinder their access to and control over resources, exclude their participation in family and community decision-making and limit their mobility, which makes them vulnerable to GBV and restricts their SRHR (SAFERWORLD, June 2021). The contraceptive prevalence rate is only 34% compared to 62% at the national level (Family Planning 2020, BDHS 2014) due to widespread, deep-rooted stigma, misconception and societal expectations of women and girls. This is especially true for adolescent girls who face unique barriers in accessing GARI SRH and SGBV services due to lack of awareness of their rights and unresponsiveness of services. In Teknaf, 82% of adolescents felt that health providers did not address their issues (Cox’s Bazar CPSS, REACH, Child Protection (CP) Assessment, May 2020). To respond to these interrelated barriers, Plan International's proposed LEAP (Lifting Healthy, Empowered and Protected Girls and Women) project will advance the realization of SRHR of the most vulnerable adolescent girls and young women in all their diversity in refugee and host communities.LEAP will be implemented in partnership with four local partner NGOs for whole working areas but there will be three partner NGOs : 1) Samaj Kallyan O Unnayan Sangstha (SKUS) and 2) HOPE Foundation for Women and Girls of Bangladesh and 3) Partner's in Health and Development (PHD) within the scope of JRP. This ultimate outcome is aligned with the 2024 Joint Response Plan (JRP), emphasizing building the capacity of both Rohingya refugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar District. Specifically, pillar 3 of the protection framework for the humanitarian response, guiding the 2024 JRP, focuses on fostering the wellbeing of host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas and refugee camps to promote alternatives to negative coping mechanisms and promote peaceful co-existence.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project was in the JRP of 2023(RBGD23-HEA-196120-1) & 2022(RBGD22-HEA-185777-1) and aligned with health sector’s strategic plan (SO1: To strengthen the health systems capacity to deliver effective and efficient quality health care services, SO2: Maintain equitable access to essential health care (Preventive, Curative, Rehabilitative, Palliative health care) in a sustainable manner and of acceptable quality at all levels (Community, Primary and Secondary Health Care Levels). According to the reference #3 - Adopt an integrated approach, and # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity the project will not establish any new facility but work on capacity building of the service providers of enrolled health facilities on Adolescent Friendly Sexual and Reproductive Health Service (AFSRHS), SGBV (reporting protocol also) and MHPSS. In each camp the project will collaborate with one health facility (total 3 by three camp) and will contribute to the facilities by refurbishment support (need based). Dignity Kit supply for adolescent and young women and materials supply to the health facility. As per Health Facility Rationalization Results- SAG Recommendation of Health Sector, primarily the project is decided to work with following health facilities: In Camp 1W - 1. Green Hill/CPI-418, in Camp 3, HF_033 PHD and in Camp 4, HF_029 Hope Foundation/UNFPA/IPAS.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

To protect children, adolescents and young people, the project works to ensure health rights specially SRHR including sexual and gender-based violence related issues. The targeted beneficiary will learn SRHR, SGBV, MHPSS and Life Skills which help them to be sensitize and claim their rights in the mentioned aspects. Again, to ensure enabling environment for the children, adolescents and young people, the project included their parents and different stakeholders under positive parenting and community sensitization activities. To provide adolescent and youth friendly service on SRHR, SGBV and MHPSS the project has taken capacity building initiatives for service providers and refurbishment activity for facility. The project is also working to strengthen the referral linkage system in this purpose.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Child safeguarding is in the core of this project. The project conduct safeguarding risk assessment for each activity and take necessary steps in this regard. The LEAP project takes into consideration the specific needs and vulnerabilities of the Rohingya refugees and Host Community People, including issues related to age and disability. The project employs a comprehensive and integrated programmatic approach that includes services related to Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR). These services are designed to address the diverse needs of the population, including vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities and older persons. The project aims to bring about transformative change, particularly benefiting the most vulnerable adolescent girls and young women in both host communities and the Rohingya camps. Additionally, the project emphasizes community-based approaches, strengthening community protection mechanisms, and ensuring meaningful access to specialized services for persons with disabilities. The inclusion of these elements in the project design and implementation demonstrates a commitment to addressing the unique challenges faced by different groups, including those with age and disability-related concerns, in the context of the Rohingya refugee response.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To uphold and safeguard the fundamental principles of the AAP (Accountability to Affected People), the project has implemented several key interventions, including the Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), and Community Reflection Meetings. These interventions serve as integral components in ensuring that the project remains responsive to the needs and concerns of the affected individuals. The Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Team, dedicated to overseeing the project's activities, will play a crucial role in monitoring the entire process to guarantee accountability throughout the project duration. Concurrently, the Finance Team will be responsible for maintaining transparency and financial oversight, ensuring that resources are allocated efficiently and in accordance with project goals. Addressing a spectrum of pertinent issues such as stakeholder participation, access to information, capacity building, and compliance, the project team is committed to fostering appropriate practices among its members. By encompassing these comprehensive strategies, the project aims to not only meet its objectives but also foster a culture of accountability, transparency, and responsible conduct within the team.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The LEAP project takes a comprehensive approach to address Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming by embedding principles of safety and accountability throughout its program. This is achieved through several key strategies:Firstly, the project actively engages diverse stakeholders and promotes inclusivity in its approach, focusing on the agency and resilience of adolescent girls and young women. Providing GBV prventive and responsive services and creating a protective environment where girls and women are better equipped to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse.Secondly, the LEAP project strengthens health systems to provide sex- and age-responsive quality health and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) services. This involves not only improving the availability and accessibility of these services but also ensuring that they are delivered in a manner that is sensitive to the specific needs of different age groups and genders.Lastly, the project builds the capacity of local women's rights organizations (WROs) and youth-led organizations (YLOs) to advocate for evidence-based Gender and Adolescent Responsive and Inclusive (GARI) health and GBV services. These organizations play a crucial role in holding service providers and authorities accountable for the prevention and response to sexual exploitation and abuse.In summary, the LEAP project mainstreams Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by fostering a culture of safety and accountability within the communities it serves, strengthening healthcare systems, and empowering local organizations to advocate for improved services and policies. By doing so, it contributes to a protective environment that safeguards the rights and well-being of adolescent girls and young women in Cox's Bazar, aligning with its core mission to promote gender equality, human dignity, and address sexual gender-based violence.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. The project has some refurbishment works undertaken at selected government health facilities and medical waste management activities. To mitigate the potential negative environmental effects of this activities, the project has an Environmental Action Plan which already submitted to GAC and approved by them. The EAP also outlines the mitigation, monitoring, and institutional measures to be taken during LEAP’s implementation and operation to avoid or minimize potential adverse environmental effects while maximizing environmental benefits, and the actions needed to implement these measures. Furthermore, The LEAP project defines how PIB is going to mitigate the potential negative effects of the project while considering climate change and how they will contribute to build awareness on environmental and health linkages when possible.
