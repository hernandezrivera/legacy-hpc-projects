Project Summary

In 2022, 14.6 million people need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021.
Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue and increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss as well as unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need, most notably in areas historically less affected by hostilities and displacement. (HNO, 2022).
The proposed project is aiming to contribute to achieving the SO3 and SO1 by achieve two goals:
1) Supporting the widows’ camps in addition to the most vulnerable populations (IDPs in Camps) in the following camps in Idleb-Maaratmisrin: Al Dahroj, Maryam, Ebd Alrahman(Kafrehmul), Sham, AlOkhwa, Aljub, Alhareth, Aldahouk, Alsaro, Alsadaqa, Alqasha, Almaqbara and Zaytun Office in adition to the follwing camps in Afrin and Albab: Qebat Alsheih, Mazrat Shwiha, Alwadi Alakhdar, Bershaya, Matenla, Kurtek 2, Alsatur, Alhezb, Aljasem, Albalata, Mhamadia, Mhamadia 1 and Deir Ballut.
HIHFAD will keep high level of coordination with FSL and CCCM clusters to ensure avoiding any potential overlaps in adition to keep the consideration for the coordination on the field level.
2) Enhancing wheat value chain in two governorates (Idleb and Aleppo), and consequently enhancing food security in the target areas through inter-linked components.
The project was built on achievements of the previous interventions of HIHFAD (irrigated farms and, a farmer association, agricultural inputs for the vulnerable households and free bread distributions) in the target areas.
20300 HHs in total will directly benefit from this project.
The project consists of five main components:
1) Supporting 800 vegetable farmers with supplementary irrigation costs enough for 2 Dunums (Using of diesel vouchers modality), in addition to do a light rehabilitation to 20 km of the water canals which serving 800 hectares by cash for work scheme for the irrigation works.
2) Supporting the wheat value chain through purchasing wheat seeds from 2000 MT from 2000 farmers (1500 from Idleb and 500 from Aleppo).
3) Supporting 8000 farmers (5600 in Idleb and 2400 in Aleppo) with agriculture inputs (wheat seeds purchased from the local farmers and supplementary irrigation).
4) Supporting 500 barley farmers with agricultural inputs such as seeds, fertilizer and harvesting cost in
5) Processing 900 MT of purchased wheat into 700 MT of wheat flour and distribute it as a fresh bread bundle over 9000 HHs, this component will target the widow’s camps at the targeted subdistricts in addition to the IDPs in camps for 6 months of distribution 26 days per month via contracting local bakeries.
Climate smart agricultural training will be given to 650 BNs regarding the appropriate and recommended dosage and amount of pesticide and fertilizer the proper irrigation techniques to save water and decrease the negative impact on the environment.
the Project is considered under planned.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world . In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021.The similar trends in food insecurity were confirmed in HIHFAD specific needs assessment, where stated that the crisis brought devastating impact on the infra structure including irrigation systems, accessing to the core agricultural inputs, and the economic effects on farmers income. Many people were obliged to discontinue a gricultural production, including crop and livestock production, because they did not have the means to afford through the crisis, despite the deterioration in farming activities across the country, agriculture still represents an important source of income and food in all assessed areas.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

All the team members will be trained on the code of conduct and PSEA. 3. Effective complaint mechanism to receive feedback from the ground. 4. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two time sat higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit theircomplaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way. The system provides diverse options for the convenience of the beneficiaries to deliver their feedback including WhatsApp hotlines, feedback boxes presented at all HIHFAD supported facilities in addition to the direct feedback.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Regarding the COVID-19 Pandemic Risk assessment for the proposed interventions, the probability of this Risky is moderate:Mitigation Measures:-HIHFAD already assigned a special medical task force to follow up all of its activities and provide SOPs.-all of the distribution points will follow the agreed distribution SOP under COVID-19.-all of the training session will take in consideration of the COVID-19 mitigation measures, or it will be online.-HIHFAD will print out a big awareness signs and banners.regarding the risk of agricultural crops:R1: drought season and decrease of the yield.Mitigation: the project suggest to purchase one MT from one farmers which is considered as a quarter of the hectare production.R2: the fluctuation of the exchange rate between USD and Turkish Lira.Mitigation: HIHFAD is planning to purchase the crop from farmers by US Doller.R3: flour availability, P: Moderate.Mitigation: the project will allocate part of the purchased wheat for milling and backing then to be distributed to the IDPs HHs as free bread in the camps.R4: Fuel Prices, P: Moderate.Mitigation: - HIHFAD will sign a contract with fixed prices with Mills, bakeries and trucks from the begining of the project based on genaral natunal tender.-If Fuel prices increased significantly, in this case HIHFAD will try to import the fuel from Turkey.-Coordinate with the FSL Cluster and done to adapt the quantities according to the new prices.
