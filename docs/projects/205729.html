Project Summary

In response to food insecurity and malnutrition driven by recurrent and overlapping sub-national violent conflict and climate-related shocks, including extreme flooding and severe drought), CRS will deliver the USAID/BHA funded Sustained Humanitarian Action to Reduce the Effects of Disasters (SHARED) Project: a robust, multi-sectoral program of flexible humanitarian interventions to meet life-saving needs in Akobo and Duk counties of Jonglei state, and Budi and Kapoeta North Counties of Eastern Equatoria State.
The project will undertake activities Focused on Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition in Emergencies through community based SBC activities focused on MIYCN-E and essential hygiene actions, targeting 13,200 individuals, primarily pregnant or lactating women (PLW) adn other caregivers. The program will promote a community-centered behavior change approach to enhance community capacity and develop caretakers and influential household members as change agents and nutrition resources within their communities. This project will build on the Care Group model established under CRS’ previous Pathways to Resilience (P2R) Project: under the SHARED project a scaled down version of the care groups curriculum will be implemented, focusing on key WASH and MIYCN-E behaviors. The project will also support integration of MIYCN-E with other project sectors and increase community leadership for greater nutritional impact. Nutrition interventions under this project will be integrated with Hygiene Promotion activities; CRS will carefully coordinate with health and nutrition actors to ensure SBC efforts harmonize messaging and contribute to broader health and nutrition programming across the target areas, and will promote referral for malnutrition to rationalized actors.
The nutrition interventions will take place at community level and CRS will ensure accessibility to diverse groups including PLWDs. Bringing nutrition services closer to communities through care groups will enhance the safety and accessibility of the services for groups who have limited mobility due to their gender, disability, age, etc. and/or who may face barriers traveling to receive services, including financial, cultural, physical or other challenges. The community outreach through hygiene and nutrition promotion messaging will contribute to the provision of culturally appropriate and gender and age sensitive information. The inclusion of other caregivers, including men will also help with engagement of men in critical nutrition topics.

1. Needs

IPC Projections for lean season 2024 (April-June 2024) indicate that all four target counties will experience IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4 (Emergency) conditions. (IPC predictions released 6 Nov 2023). “In 2022, around 1.34 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS), and program admission trends.” The highest burden is in 4 states including Jonglei. “The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases and inadequate feeding practices of infant and young children. Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC Acute Food Insecurity Phase 3 or above) in most counties also contribute to acute malnutrition.” ( IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Analysis Report. July 2022 - July 2023. Published on November 23, 2022.)Acute malnutrition remains a significant public health emergency in South Sudan, including in Eastern Equatoria (Budi and Kapoeta North Counties) and Greater Jonglei (Akobo and Duk counties). The major contributing factors include but are not limited to: weak purchasing power of target household/ community, increased inflation, poor harvest, continued drought in Budi and Kapoeta North and anticipated flooding in Akobo and Duk. The report from a SMART Survey conducted in Budi County in July 2022 estimated GAM prevalence at 17.5.0%, and prevalence of SAM at 2.6%, noting that: “The current nutrition status of the county is classified as ‘Very High’ based on UNICEF/WHO classification threshold; this could be mainly associated with poor feeding practice of infants and young children.”( Action Against Hunger USA. Nutrition and Mortality SMART Survey: Budi County of Eastern Equatoria State, South Sudan. Final Report. June, 2022)

2. Project activities

The overall objective is to support improved household maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) practices. The project will promote adoption of behaviors and practices for optimal maternal, infant, and young child nutrition during the critical first thousand days. Key activities will include: 1) Train an additional 12 volunteer community health promoters. 2) Provide facilitation support (materials, training for community volunteers and lead mothers, soap for demonstration, etc.) to existing 120 care groups 3) Print and distribute laminated pictoral materials for care group volunteers, grandmother and fathers group members. 4) Support 40 grandmother and fathers groups. 5) Print and distribute existing WASH and MIYCN-E flipcharts for care group volunteersCRS’ previous Pathways to Resilience (P2R) project introduced the care group model, forming 240 care groups (CGs) across the 4 counties: each is supported by a volunteer community health promoter, supported by project officers in turn. Each CG has 10 members who reach out to 10 neighbor women. This evidence-based model directly empowers women to act as change agents and resources within their own communities, and supports HH level change through outreach and peer support. Under this project, a scaled down version of the CG curriculum will be implemented, focusing on key WASH and MIYCN-E behaviors. Further prioritization will be undertaken by county and Payam to focus on sub-behaviors least practiced within the targeted community. New groups established will target areas with the highest levels malnutrition and poor MIYCN-E behaviors. A subset of laminated pictorial materials will be provided to each group member. Recognizing the complexity of behavior change, particularly within a multi-generational and often polygamous household, CGs will be complemented with groups targeting fathers and grandmothers. CRS will facilitate peer discussions with mothers, husbands/fathers, and mothers-in-law/grandmothers to directly address intrahousehold barriers to behavior adoption and facilitate change through experience sharing and peer support. CRS will deliberately engage members of CRS-supported famer-producer groups, savings and internal lending communities, etc. to promote adoption of improved diets particularly for children <2years and women of reproductive age and to strengthen supportive behaviors. CRS will organize 20 community meetings on improved nutrition practice and tackling barriers to positive behaviors; will support senior and influential community members to lead discussions at the community level to change social norms on gender roles, myths, and malpractices related to optimal MIYCN practices; and will conduct MIYCN and SBC training for 80 community change agents drawn from CGs and community leaders. Outreach to community leaders will address gender-based norms that negatively impact maternal and child nutrition, including HH decision-making dynamics and women’s domestic and agricultural workload. Messaging to community leaders will support a greater role for women in decision making at HH level as well as spending on greater variety and quality of food for women and children within the HH. In close coordination with UNICEF nutrition partners and CHDs, CRS staff and CGs will promote referral of malnourished under-five children and pregnant women to the nearest nutrition facilities and encourage communities and households to support such referrals where security permits. The lead mother or and Community Health Worker Promoter (CHPW) will motivate children, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) to attend screening sessions provided by other nutrition actors. CRS will also directly engage nutrition partners and provide them with information on locations that require outreach services. CGs will follow up with cases to ensure that they are correctly using nutrition supplies and that they adhere to scheduled follow up visits to prevent relapse and improve outcomes.
