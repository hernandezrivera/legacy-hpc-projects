Project Summary

In line with the Protection Cluster priority areas, WATAN will provide critically needed Protection services to an estimated 6,000 vulnerable people in Albab -Afrin- Azaz districts -Aleppo Northwest Syria
WATAN will implement integrated protection services to prevent, mitigate, and respond to harm exploitation, and abuse for the most vulnerable boys, girls, men and women in northern Syria.
The proposed intervention will include working with communities to shift social norms that subordinate women and girls and condone violence as a means to control them; engaging men and boys in addressing GBV; responding to the immediate and long-term needs of GBV survivors.
WATAN intends to provide support to the most vulnerable groups in particular children, boys, girls and female-headed households, women and girls which continues The aim is to establish community centers in the targeted areas to create as a safe environment where women, children, girls, and boys can interact and socialize with women coming from similar backgrounds, thereby allowing the center serves the psychosocial needs of the women, children and helping the women and girls, including the GBV survivors, in their coping mechanism, the WGSS as part of community center will emerge as a key strategy for supporting the protection and empowerment of crisis-affected women and girls A safe space is a place where women and girls feel physically and emotionally safe, free of violence (or fear of violence) or abuse; free to express themselves without fear of judgment or harm.
These community centers will serve as a foundation from which to engage the community in providing direct activities The aim of proposed activities is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection concerns by the provision of quality and integrated protection services including prevention activities such as awareness-raising, Provision of PFA and PSS activities including case management response activities targeting people at risk of GBV concerns.
The project is designed to be twelve months, this includes one month of staff technical training to build their capacity on identifying protection risks, needs and response and mitigate the GBV, CP risks, as well as establishing referral pathway.

4 - Needs

As indicated in HNO (March 2021) and up to January 2021, around 13.4 million peopleare estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including6 million in acute need, 25% of them have disabilities and 75% of them are women andchildren, where children with and without disabilities comprise 44% of the people in needacross Syria. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021alone), Women and children represent 80% of this caseload. As for protection: 42% of theassessed communities reported the presence of at least one of the assessed eightprotection issues and 53% reported the presence of five or more out of the assessedeight protection issues, and 61% reported lack or loss of civil documentation. Accordingto the Voices 2021, 62% of communities confirmed that cases of early marriage affectedadolescent girls between the ages of 12 and 17, and 62% of communities surveyedagreed that adolescent girls between the ages of 15-17 drop out of school in order towork, while that number jumped to 84% in relation to adolescent boys of the same age.And according to the Protection Need Assessment conducted by HRO during Februaryand April 2021 in Jisr Ash Shugur, Kafr Jales, Darkosh, Gandoura, and Afrin, 43% reportedthat the violence has increased during COVID-19 outbreak, and as for the legal servicesneeded, it was reported by 40% in Jisr Ash Shugur and Kafr Jales that they lack housing,land, and properties documents (HLP), while it is 65% in Darkosh and 67% in Afrin. Asreported in the 2021 Mine Action Needs, 10.3 million people live in areas affected byexplosive ordnance.

5 - Implementation of activities

After ensuring the preparedness of the project logistics and recruiting procedures, staffwill be trained on CP Minimum standards, GBV basics, CP & GBV Case Management, FTR,Structured PSS, the inclusion of PwDs, MA, GP. Afterwards, teams will proceed withconducting the following activities: GP, legal, RE, CP & GBV prevention and mitigationincl. CP Structured, semi-structured and PSS activities including PwDs, to target 49,336beneficiaries through the fixed facilities and outreach teams, as planned. Awareness-raising sessions for parents about parenting and dissemination of key messages about CP(CP principles, Alternative care, parenting skills), HLP/CSD issues, and Life Skills Activities (Girls dreams): the centres will continue to focus on (adolescent girls, divorced andwidowed women, women, and girls with disabilities and the elderly) to socialize and re-buildtheir social networks, in addition to providing Vocational Training andInformation/sensitization sessions about GBV related services. As for the responseactivities: Specialized CP services including (case management, case fund, and referral) forgirls and boys, Providing legal advice/counselling on civil status documentation/registration on (HLP) issues. Building the capacity of humanitarian actors and localauthorities to improve the quality of protection services provided. Conducting protectionand CP monitoring assessments to identify protection needs/risks in the targetcommunities. As for Mine Action: outreach teams will sensitize risk education using MASub-working group IEC materials. Complaints’ mechanisms are in place to ensure quality,transparency, and access of vulnerable groups to all available services.
