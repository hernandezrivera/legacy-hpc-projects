Project Summary

Aligned with the ES-NFI Cluster's 2024 Response Strategy, Acted will respond to emergency NFI needs in the volatile hotspots of Khartoum and Al Jazeera States. This response will be channeled through assistance to evacuation centers managed by Community-Based Organizations (CBOs). Furthermore, Acted, in accordance with the Cluster's guidelines, will prioritize a community-based, urban-centric shelter approach to provide support to vulnerable IDPs and address the challenge of inadequate sites, thus alleviating the strain on schools occupied by IDPs. In total, Acted will aim to support 25 evacuation centers, reaching approximatively a total of 3,675 individuals throughout the response.

Due to access challenges and security risks, established humanitarian entities, both international and national NGOs, as well as UN agencies, are unable to directly operate in Khartoum state. Consequently, community-based organizations and mutual aid groups, particularly CBOs, have emerged as the primary service providers in the state. While some UN and NGO entities have begun collaborating with these groups, CBOs continue to lack the necessary support from the humanitarian community and require additional resources to implement their initiatives. As part of the Emergency Response Rapid Mechanism in Sudan, Acted will contribute to provide timely NFI assistance to crisis affected populations. Under its proposed project, Acted will provide Shelter and NFI support through cash-based assistance to CBO-run evacuation centers to ensure that centers are properly furnished to create a safe and dignified living environment. Supported centers will receive monthly grants to cover the provision of mosquito nets, institutional hygiene kits/items, and other critical SNFI items. Acted will also contribute to the general evacuation centers’ development to ensure adequate shelter solutions that provide protection, safety, security, and space to live in a dignified manner. Acted will therefore Support to CBOs to their cover operating costs which include utility costs (such as water and electricity), the costs for operating the centers’ communal kitchens, and the costs for ensuring a hygienic and sanitary environment. Additionally, based on needs, Acted will provdie immediate financial support to CBOs to set up and operate new evacuation centers.

Support for these centers is critical as the people who are utilizing them are among the most vulnerable in Sudan as they did not have the means to evacuate Khartoum when the conflict first broke out. Evacuation centers were established organically by CBOs after internally displaced Khartoumis arrived at communal kitchens and other locations where CBOs were implementing activities. As a result, these centers are not equipped to adequately host evacuees. Under the same approach and, due to the escalation of the conflict mid-December in Al Jazeerah State, Acted will also support evacuation centers and other CBO-run actions in the area to deliver critical humanitarian assistance despite restricted access.

Finally, due to the significant changes in context mentioned above, Acted will also be committed to provide a more flexible, responsive, and adaptive approach through multisectoral assistance to a broad range of community-led support initiatives. In volatile contexts in Khartoum and Al Jazeerah States, while still prioritizing physical evacuation centers, Acted will aim to also support unformal volunteer initiatives providing support to IDPs living with host families. In addition to ES-NFI related intervention and to ensure a multisectoral approach, Acted-supported activities may include communal kitchen, PSS and WASH support.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages a gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedded in the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Before and during the intervention, Acted will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. Acted is working strongly with the CBOs to ensure that assistance is available to all people, regardless of gender, age, disability, or demographic group, and to ensure that specialized modes of response are available to meet the needs of different gender and age groups. CBOs supported with funding under this action will be required to ensure safe spaces are provided for women and girls, the elderly, and others within the sites as well as gender-specific living areas for individuals residing in the centers. Acted will work with the CBOs to link the work of Women’s Groups to the evacuation centers to ensure that residents have access to psychosocial support throughout the response. Acted will also display its Acted Feedback Mechanism (AFM) at all sites supported under this project with the CBOs informing residents verbally and in writing that they can report any complaints, including those related to PSEA issues, regarding their experience in the evacuation centers to Acted. Acted will provide training on protection standards to CBOs covered under this project to ensure a minimum standard of protection mainstreaming in the short-term, while developing stronger service mapping and referral pathways in the longer term that incorporate the work of CBOs in Khartoum.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The proposed project has been designed to promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency in its interventions, in line with IASC’s Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations. The choice of activities proposed is based on information collected on beneficiaries’ needs and preferences several rounds of key informant interviews with national NGO partners and community-based organization (CBO) representatives in Khartoum conducted by Acted, covering Bahri, Sharq an-Nil, and Jabal Awliya. Under this intervention, Acted aims to address emergency ES-NFI needs of displaced populations while empowering local organizations to continue and scale up their participation in the ongoing emergency response in line with humanitarian principles and best practices. Acted will rely on its partners’ extensive ties within targeted communities and comprehensive knowledge of the rapidly changing situation on the ground to incorporate beneficiary voices during the project. Throughout implementation, Acted will work closely with the CBOs to identify areas where additional support, tools, and guidance can be provided to assist them in their operations. Acted will work with the CBOs during the project duration to build standards and information-sharing mechanisms to ensure that evacuation center residents have full information on available services across the displacement process and to establish a minimum set of site management standards within the CBO-led evacuation centers.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated member of staff will be responsible for managing the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Besides its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed and inform future projects’ design. cted has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. Acted also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to coordination through a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination. Acted staff are trained on preventing and held accountable in case of Sexual Exploitation. All reported SEA incidents will be taken seriously, and any violation can result in disciplinary action, dismissal, or legal action. False accusations and reports are also seen as a breach of the Code of Conduct. Any stakeholder witness or victim of SEA can anonymously report to Acted / IMPACT through dedicated internal website/email hotlines monitored at HQ-level. Under the Code of Conduct, Acted and IMPACT staff who consider themselves to be a witness or victim of SEA must report it to their line manager and/or to their personal contact within Acted and in any case to the dedicated hotline.
