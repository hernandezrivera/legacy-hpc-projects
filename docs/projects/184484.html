Project Summary

The proposed project will provide essential and life-saving integrated health assistance to vulnerable communities in Awdal, Bay, Lower Shabelle, Banadir, Bari, Galgaduud, Hiraan, Lower Juba, Mudug, Nugaal, Sanaag, Sool, Toghdeer, and Woqooyi Galbeed. The project aims to reach 928,490IDPs and host communities contributing to 20% of the cluster target of the population affected by conflict and displacement in the targeted geographies using fixed and mobile health facilities.
The project will seek to contribute to a 5% reduction in excess morbidity and mortality rates from crisis-driven diseases and outbreaks among target vulnerable communities by end of 2022 while scaling up provision of regular health services and providing safe, dignified and meaningful access life-saving assistance (provided directly or through referral) with sensitivity to age, gender and disability status. This will be achieved by supporting the Somalia governments and communities to be able
to delivery free essential package of health services to communities with acute humanitarian needs using development programs as a platform for immediate responses. The proposed project has also been designed with full awareness of the prevailing gender norms and deeply rooted gender inequalities in Somalia that limit women and girls from accessing basic services such as health and nutrition, and expose them to a higher risk of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV). In order to address
these challenges, Save the Children (SC) will identify and work to remove the physical, cognitive and social barriers by collaborating closely with communities and particularly influencers (community and religious leaders) and gatekeepers. SC will also address the intersectionality of vulnerability factors, such as people with disabilities, minority clans who are likely to face multiple levels of discrimination. SC will also include appropriate child safeguarding measures at every phase and within every programming activity.

1. - Projects activities

To improve access to quality lifesaving health services, SC proposes a multi-sectoral, integrated health, nutrition and WASH response to respond to vulnerable IDPs, and marginalized communities in Somalia. In partnership with the Ministry of Health (MOH), SC will improve access to essential and lifesaving health services for affected populations through static and mobile health clinics. To reduce excess morbidity and mortality due to disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and conflict among IDPs and non-displaced, the project will support data collection, collation, analysis and use for action in targeted districts for routine use and emergency response; Conduct community mobilization, and education on health, nutrition, hygiene promotion, and sanitation, including COVID-19 Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities; Support MOH in targeted locations on emergency preparedness and response to outbreaks of measles and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) as well as surveillance of communicable diseases. The project will support health awareness, social mobilization, education and advocacy activities in disease control, health and nutrition, establish and equip emergency trauma management centers, set up and strengthen Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) systems in target sites including COVID-19 prevention. The project will train Health care workers on case management and emergency management and support static health facilities and mobile sites to improve the physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement among target populations through integrated Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) for Health workers’ and community training; provision of Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) services and Psychosocial support and counselling. The proposed project will also increase health awareness/community mobilization/education and advocacy on MHPSS and disability - linkage to other mental health, psychosocial, health, and protection services. To improve equitable access to quality emergency, essential and disability-inclusive life-saving health services for crisis affected populations, the project will support provision of medical supplies to supported health facilities, basic health services – provide integrated life-saving primary health care services including maternal and newborn health services - antenatal and postnatal care services and skilled birth attendance and basic emergency obstetric care through fixed and mobile outreach services; Strengthen referral services for complicated and emergency cases to referral hospitals; Support provision of immunization services through health facilities and mobile sites; Conduct regular supportive supervision to improve and sustain quality health service delivery; Noncommunicable diseases (NCD) treatment;Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STD) / HIV treatment; Tuberculosis (TB) screening and treatment; and WASH and waste management in healthcare facilities. To improve case management and referral services for survivors of SGBV, the project will provide immediate first aid management (including emergency contraception, Post exposure prophylaxis, STI treatment and vaccination according to clinical management of rape guidelines); Psychosocial support and counselling for rape survivors; Establishment/strengthening of referral pathways for management of rape survivors with complications; and Adolescent specific services and outreach. The proposed project is expected to improve access to essential and life-saving health services for vulnerable people affected by climatic shocks or conflict related displacements, and contribute to a 5% reduction in excess morbidity and mortality rates from crisis-driven diseases and outbreaks among target vulnerable communities by end of 2022.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

• Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. • Install lights outside and on access route to facilities. • Ensure that health services are respectful and cultural, religious, and gender sensitivities considered e.g. separate waiting and examination areas for males and females • Having an proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, we may consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women will be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available.• Employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children. • SC will Establish an information sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him to further trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishes concerning the care provided. • Ensure patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private. No sharing of information or identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). • Consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.• To prevent improper use of medical supplies, ensure proper storage and that labels and instructions on their usage is in a language or pictorial form that is understood by users. • Design and clearly mark separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females and account for persons with disabilities and children.• In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents concerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and military use of hospital
