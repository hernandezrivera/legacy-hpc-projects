Project Summary

Northeast Nigeria has reached alarming levels of food insecurity and hunger after 11 years of conflict. More than 8.7 million people – four out of five – require humanitarian assistance and protection in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States. As a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, more and more vulnerable populations are experiencing a catastrophic loss of income, undermining their coping mechanisms, and purchasing power. Close to million people have been internally displaced because of conflict, and many have lost their livelihoods in agriculture – the mainstay of the BAY States’ economy. Pursuing agricultural activities is also fraught with risk, as farmers and fishermen are frequently attacked by Non-state Armed Groups (NSAGs).
The most recent Cadre Harmonise (CH) (March 2021) indicated that up to 4.4 million people in Northeast Nigeria’s BAY States would require food assistance during the lean season (June-August 2021). The projected number of acutely food-insecure people has increased, as compared to the 2020 peak of the lean season (with COVID-19). This is the highest number of food insecure people in the BAY states since March 2017. Some 775,000 people faced emergency (IPC Phase 4) during the 2021 lean season, which is an 84% and 16% increase compared to 2019 and 2020, respectively. According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) acute malnutrition analysis (IPC AMN) of March 2021, the number of acutely malnourished children and women significantly increased in 2021 compared to 2020.IRC supported facilities experienced significant increase in admission in 2021.Stabilization Centres IRC operated admitted a total of 5,077 in 2021 compared to 4,066 in 2020.During the same period the IRC OTP clinics admitted 46,384 SAM children in 2021 compared to 30,856 in 2020the trend is the highest levels of women and children since 2017, when the crisis in the region was at its peak. According to the IPC AMN, over three-quarter (75%) of all LGAs in Northeast Nigeria were projected to be classified as critical (phase 4) or serious (Phase 3).

2 - Activities

The IRC will maintain two SCs, Umaru Shehu Hospital in MMC and Mashamari SC in Jere, and retain partial support of Gujba SC. These facilities will accept SAM cases with medical complications from the surrounding communities and other partners, and likely remain at maximum occupancy based on current trends. Between January and October 2020, 3,500 SAM cases were admitted to the three SCs, compared to 2,900 during all of 2019, which indicates the need to provide this lifesaving activity. Bed capacities in these facilities constantly remain above 50% with the occupancy rate going above 100% during peak periods. In the SCs, the IRC will maintain doctors, nurses, and other auxiliary staff to ensure quality of care. In addition, MoH doctors and nurses seconded from the MoH will work alongside the IRC staff to improve the capacity of MoH staffs in managing in-patient treatments for SAM children. IRC will procure pharmaceuticals and medical supplies and maintain the additional costs for two SCs (MMC and Mashamari). In addition, in line with a request from the Nutrition Cluster, the IRC will establish a diagnostic facility in Mashamari. Funds for the structure will be provided by the Nigerian Humanitarian Fund and laboratory reagents are to be funded by BHA in the proposed award. The purpose of the diagnostic facility is to provide basic tests such as malaria, blood tests that include a full haemogram, uninalysis, and stool tests for children, pregnant and lactating women and others accessing the IRC’s Mashamari Stabilization Center as well as patients of other nutrition cluster agencies in Mashamari LGA. These will be used to verify accurate diagnoses for patients. The laboratory will also reduce lead times and transportation costs to receive a diagnosis for those within the LGA as patients will be referred to the laboratory for testing whereas previously, they traveled much further to the Maiduguri Teaching Hospital to receive testing. The laboratory will be used by all the partners across Borno state.In addition, the proposed intervention builds on the current 75 OTP IRC is currently supporting across the BAY states and will work by strengthening and scaling up CMAM services in Monguno, Konduga, MMC, and Jere LGAs in Borno, Michika LGA in Adamawa, and Gujba LGA in Yobe. The proposed LGAs are all within the IPC 3 emergency phases of nutrition, indicating acute food and livelihood crisis. The IRC will maintain fixed OTP sites in the proposed LGAs except in Michika LGA of Adamawa state and Gujba LGA of Yobe state where the IRC will deliver programming in MoH-supported health facilities utilizing MoH staff in the delivery of services, supplemented by incentive MOH staff in hard-to-reach areas. IRC will facilitate quarterly capacity building for MoH staff focused on inpatient and outpatient treatment, referrals of complex cases, and CMAM reporting, as well as facilitate the provision of supplies, including RUTF, through a monthly requisition to UNICEF followed by a timely delivery from IRC Warehouses. In addition, the IRC will work with a minimum of five CHVs per mobile OTP on the case detection and referral of SAM children. SAM caretakers will also be trained using the MAMA MUAC program, explained in further detail below. In Monguno LGA, Borno, the mode of delivery will be through four mobile clinics as the majority of MoH PHCs are non-functional. The IRC will expand on the MAMA MUAC program to support the timely referrals of SAM children to the nearest SC or OTP. In addition, rather than refer to partners, IRC will integrate MAM treatment into SAM services and initiate admission and treatment of MAM cases at IRC facilities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC is also a global leader in protection mainstreaming. The IRC’s Protection Mainstreaming Monitoring and Evaluation System and global protection cluster resources will guide the project’s protection mainstreaming interventions. The project will integrate four key elements of protection mainstreaming: (1) meaningful access to nutrition assistance and services; (2) prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm; (3) accountability and participation; and (4) empowerment of beneficiaries. The Protection and Rule of Law team together with Nutrition will ensure these elements will be promoted through updating the current protection mainstreaming strategy at project inception. The update will be informed by findings from beneficiary assessments, staff capacity assessments and findings from various project activities. Foundational capacity building activities for project staff will be conducted to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, including child safe guarding principles, and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC will sensitize the community on IRC’s accountability for quality services and on the ways they can provide confidential feedback, drawing on the IRC’s in-depth guidelines on client responsiveness. Using feedback mechanisms and hotlines, the IRC will continue to gather feedback on services and other protection needs as well as conduct safety audits to inform risk mitigation strategies for vulnerable groups. The IRC employs a Protection Coordinator based in Maiduguri in order to ensure staff and partners are trained on how to ensure the sustainable incorporation of protection principles. The IRC will also work with local stakeholders in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states to inform the assistance they provide and help ensure that protection considerations are integrated into the full package of services for IDPs and host communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions specifically with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect beneficiary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxes and community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month with women and girls in the IDP camp to understand the nutrition protection related risks. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs.
