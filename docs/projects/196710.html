Project Summary

SEMA will continue to support two offices in northwest Syria: one in Idleb and the other in Aleppo. These two offices will provide logistical support to the outreach teams (help desk) (four mobile teams in Idleb governorate, Harim district, and two teams in Aleppo governorate, A’zaz district) and will be as static/private points to conduct the complementary comprehensive case management, where the beneficiaries will be already informed about the available services. In addition, there will be a CM center in Idleb which will provide comprehensive case management and psychosocial rehabilitation for PwDs.
Displaced and conflict-affected people in Northwest Syria have improved access to protection services. Boys, girls, men, and women, including children and adolescents with special needs, are provided with integrated emergency protection services through mobile teams. The project will continue providing preventive and responsive services, as well as a rehabilitation program for people with disabilities in Idleb and Azaz. The beneficiaries of the services to be provided through this project are estimated at 75000 by the end of Dec 2023. The following services will be provided throughout the project cycle:
-Provide integrated, community-based protection services, including awareness-raising and psychosocial support sessions, Information dissemination on essential services. Distribution of Kits (Hygiene, dignity, and PSS).
-Providing Psychological First Aid (PFA) and recreational activities to reduce acute distress.
-Provide legal awareness and consultation including civil documentation and HLP issues.
-Conduct protection monitoring and identify protection needs/risks
-Continuing to provide comprehensive case management CP/GBV, referrals to the needed services, and specialized psychosocial support for PwD in addition to providing parenting skills for caregivers of children with disabilities in Idleb and Azaz.

3 - Needs

According to HNO-2023, The absence of longer-term solutions to displacement, ongoing violations of IHL and human rights law, extensive destruction of infrastructure, and inadequate access to services and to the population in need are likely to continue impacting the protection landscape in NWS. Displaced women, men, girls and boys in camps and informal settlements are amongst the most vulnerable populations, living in some of the hardest places to reach. Women and girls are subjected to physical, psychological, emotional, sexual and social (stigma and discrimination) violence, as well as forced and early marriage and denial of rights, and resources. Grave violations against children remain a major concern including the risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. The presence of explosive ordnance compounds the humanitarian and protection crisis, causing civilian casualties, exacerbating the vulnerabilities of people at risk, and hindering access to humanitarian support, services and livelihood. Civil documentation issues remain particularly prominent, where only 13 per cent of household’s report having all the official documentation they need. *comment box

4 - Implementation of activities

SEMA, by adopting Help Desk SOP’s developed by the GP cluster will achieve rapid success as this approach has proved effective in reaching beneficiaries during emergency situations and ensuring their access to service. The teams will reach different communities and camps with their services which will include: Prevention and risk mitigation activities: 1-Awareness raising sessions on prevention of COVID-19, risk education (RE), (GBV), (CP), (PSEA), and Housing Land and Property (HLP) rights, birth registration, civil documentation, and hygiene promotion for communities. 2-Protection monitoring service through conducting service mapping, establishing referral pathways, and reporting on protection concerns and issues. Responsive services: 1-Case identification and referrals for most vulnerable cases: GBV, CP, GP cases will be identified to be referred to specialized and non-specialized available services. 2-Case management of the most vulnerable cases will be provided. All case managers will be under the supervision of the case management supervisors. 3-Psychosocial Support (structured and semi-structured including recreational activities) and PFA: targeting boys, girls, and women to support their wellbeing and develop their personal and social skills using approved curricula. 4- SEMA will also provide information dissemination on essential services to the displaced and conflict affected people in the area.5- Providing SN Rehabilitation Program for PWD in Idleb & Azaz CM center

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA ensures the privacy of the beneficiaries and vulnerable groups by adjusting and keeping the data of the beneficiaries secured, being responsible for the principle of “Do-no-Harm” to the beneficiaries and keeping their dignity. There is effective feedback and complaint mechanism, and beneficiaries are made aware of how to access it. This mechanism includes developed practices and channels for reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The concept of PSEA is also considered in the awareness-raising topics offered for children and adults to raise their awareness of their rights and safety and the safety measure to report such misconduct. IEC materials, banners, and posters will be available in all facilities and distribution points. Staff will be oriented in the contracting stage on SEA issues and will sign the CoC. Refresher training for the staff will be conducted on a regular basis. All complaints will be taken into consideration and will be investigated following SEMA’s investigation policies, Disciplinary measures will be taken against any breach of CoC.
