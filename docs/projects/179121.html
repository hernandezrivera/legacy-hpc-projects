Project Summary

In response to the demonstrated nutrition needs on the ground, Human Appeal Turkey (HAT) and Social Development International (SDI) proposes to jointly operate 11 nutrition rapid response teams (RRTs) across North West Syria (NWS) targeting the children under 5 (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) among others of the most vulnerable groups within the areas of priority (As per the nutrition cluster and this allocation paper) with essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services as follows:
- HAT: Establishment and operation of 4 RRTs in Dana sub-district for a duration of 11 months (Locations are coordinated with the nutrition cluster).
- SDI: Operation of pre-existing 7 RRTs: 2 in Afrin, 1 in Maaret Tamisrin, 3 in dana, and 1 in Salqin sub-districts for a duration of 12 months.
The project will be implemented in 23 communities across 4 sub- districts (Dana, Maaret Tamisrin, Afrin and Salqin) provide nutrition services for 83546 beneficiaries including (19200 Pregnant and lactating women 44800 children under five years and 19546 other caregivers (men and women)) (79918 IDPs and 3628 HCs) (1800 PWDs).
HAT and SDI will collaborate to promote the ultimate utilization of the pre-existing resources and expertise relevant to the intervention including the use of the trained staff at both organizations and the shared usage of the assembly points, warehouses, OTPs as well as the exchange of relevant technical expertise and measures of access and coordination at the field level. It’s imperative to mention that both HAT and SDI will make sure that the above will not result in increased costing of the proposed intervention. On the contrary, having such complementarity measures in place will potentially lead to overall cost effectiveness of the project.

4 - Needs

Despite the relatively stable safety and security conditions across NWS since March 2020, the nutrition needs for the conflict- affected population particularly the pregnant and lactating women (PLW) as well as children under 5 (CU5) remain extremely high. Furthermore, and since the confirmation of the COVID-19 first positive case across NWS in July 2020, the pandemic has been largely affecting the humanitarian conditions of the affected population. The pandemic has been resulting in increased rates of morbidity and mortality among the population particularly within the most vulnerable groups of children, women and people with disabilities. The nutrition surveillance report by the nutrition cluster as of March 2021 covering 35 Sub-districts across NWS, and involving 8470 PLW (43.3% of whom were married at an age younger than 18) and 31727 CU5 (Out of whom are 2.48% children with special needs and 20.31% are unaccompanied children): That report informed an overall GAM rate across NWS of 2% with average severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate of 0.1%, average moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) rate of 1.9% and a MAM rate of as high as 11.9% among the PLWs. According to the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Program (HNAP) for Syria, NWS as of March 2021 hosts more than 4.3 million populations some 2.7 million of whom are IDPs. Also, and within the region, sub-districts like Dana, Salqin, Harim, Maaret Tamisrin, and Afrin are among the highest in terms of overall population and IDPs content (Dana sub-district hosts around quarter of the total population across the whole NWS with an IDP VS HC ratio of more than 81%).

5 - Implementation of activities

• Capacity building of 60 frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF-E and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19.• Establishment and maintenance of the technical, managerial, and operational support to the concerned RRTs.• Screening of children 6-59 and PLWs for acute malnutrition, referral and follow up on the cases of malnutrition.• Provide emergency community-based one-to-one counseling on WHO-recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding and maternal nutrition practices for caregivers of children under 2 years including PLWs.• Disseminating messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, for caregivers including PLWs.• Distribution of micronutrient supplements for preventing anemia and malnutrition for children 6-59 months and PLWs.• Conduct satisfaction survey with the beneficiaries.• Conducting GBV and Protection against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA) training HAT and SDI will monitor and evaluate this project, where they will use the indicators for the nutrition sector to determine whether the project is meeting its targets and objectives. And according to the data received from the field, both organizations will adjust the project implementation to meet the planned objectives.The monitoring teams will be guided in terms of collecting appropriate and timely data and information required to report on progress and impact as defined by the indicators. At the organizational level, HAT and SDI treat the M&E activities as a critical function for learning and adapting to changing circumstances, in an ethical manner and to apply the humanitarian principles at all times.

In doing so, a cross section of project beneficiaries, hosts, the field team and other relevant actors will be engaged to gain more understanding of the operational contexts, concerns, interests and social dynamics as part of such analysis. 1. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Inadequate acceptance of the intervention within the targeted camps, communities and houses.Likelihood: medium. Impact: High.Mitigation Measures: Special consideration will be paid to contract staff members from within the same communities targeted with the intervention, and ensuring gender balance within the teams to increase their acceptance among the served populations. High level of coordination with the local authorities was and will be taking place to ensure proper facilitation of the teams’ mobility within and among the planned locations. Risk 2: Lack of qualified nutrition staffLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: HAT and SDI have pre-existing staff members that are familiar with nutrition related topics and interventions. Both organizations will conduct comprehensive capacity building for the newly recruited staff members CMAM and IYCF- E in the context of COVID 19 so they achieve the desired level of technical capacity needed to provide quality services.Risk 3: Increase influxes of IDPs due to security-related incidence, what may result in increased rates of COVID-19 transmission due to overcrowding within the targeted areas.Likelihood: Medium. Impact high.Mitigation Measures: HAT and SDI will apply a triage system that enables categorization of the beneficiaries according to their symptoms to limit the risk of transmission of coronavirus especially for the most vulnerable groups including children and women with malnutrition. PPE, IPC and other preventive measures will be applied to avoid the risk of COVID-19 transmission as well. The follow up of the more stable cases will take place while considering the social distancing or remotely via WhatsApp when feasible.
