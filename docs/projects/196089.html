Project Summary

Through this action, NGO X will address the multiple vulnerabilities that households and communities are facing after eleven years of conflict. At the household level, the program design creates links between its multi-donor funded emergency response program to its food security, nutrition, winterization and shelter programming, and complements food assistance programming with survival needs assistance via Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA).
The proposed intervention is in line with the objectives and strategic direction of the various Northwest Syria Cluster Response Plans. These activities have been informed by Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNAs) conducted in Idleb and North Aleppo during 2022 as well as lessons learned from previous projects, ongoing monitoring, feedback from beneficiaries and other assessments conducted by NGO X.
Overall target:
The proposed activities outlined below will target approximately 7,000 HHs/35,000 vulnerable displaced individuals in North West Syria (in Idleb and North Aleppo).
Project budget across sectors for the one-off MPCA and eventually two rounds is (based on the SMEB):
· FSL: 70 %
· SNFI: 15%
· WASH: 15%
The cash value of the MPC will be aligned with the SMEB. The currency applied is USD. The number of households beneficiaries who will be targeted with one-off emergency MPCA and eventually two rounds is 7,000 HHs/35,000 individuals. Prior to launching any response window/round, NGO X will coordinate response locations with CWG, SNFI, WASH, and FSL cluster partners/members to avoid overlaps and duplications. The sub-districts included in the response locations are the locations where NGO X have access to and currently responding in. NGO X is following area/location-based coordination. The total budget of this submission is $1,386,000 contributing to one-off emergency MPCA and eventually two rounds which also includes support and HQ costs.

3 - Needs

The HNO 2021-MSNA data from August 2021 indicated that average household expenditure exceeds income by 50%, compared to 20% in August 2020. Only 10% of households have an income above the cost of Syria's MEB. Food insecurity remains extremely high with an estimated 12 million severely food insecure people. Just under two thirds of October IDPs to camp settings, with the greatest proportion arriving in Idleb's Dana subdistrict. In January 2022, the CCCM Cluster has reported 293 incidents (162 snowstorm, 117 flood and 14 wind) in IDP sites in north-west Syria. In total, 1,300 IDPs arrived at Syria primarily from Türkiye 98%. The primary reason for displacement was perceived access to humanitarian assistance in the place of arrival 40%, 53% of camp IDPs reported residing in tents, while 78% of IDPs in residential areas resided in finished houses. Top three priority needs were livelihoods 62% , basic services 56% and food 55%. An annual pattern of seasonal flooding continues, In January 2022, the CCCM Cluster has reported 293 incidents (162 snowstorm, 117 flood and 14 wind) in north-west Syria. In October 2022, the displacement rate across NSAG & TBAF areas increased with 3% compared to September.As a result of the heavy snow, 939 tents have been destroyed while 9,570 tents have been damaged, affecting 57,008 Individuals and IDPs.NGO X’s needs assessment show the top priority needs included MPC and NFIs including tents, plastic sheets, water, hygiene items and kitchen sets.

4 - Implementation of activities

NGO X will ensure that cash can be deployed immediately in response to emergencies, crisis or sudden shocks, where conditions for cash response are present. NGO X will first conduct a multi-sectoral needs assessment. NGO X will carry out 100% selection to inform this one-time emergency assistance. NGO X will distribute one-off emergency multi-purpose cash transfers and eventually two rounds to households experiencing sudden shocks/onset. The currency of distribution is USD and the transfer value would be aligned with the CWG's MPC 2023 guidance. The selection team accept or reject affected people based on the defined response criteria that includes the following:• HHs displaced due to a defined shock (natural disaster/ conflict)• HHs displaced within a defined timeframe (i.e. people displaced less than a month)• Displaced HHs haven’t received similar assistance based on the coordination with LCs/Relief Committee/camp management, other orgs and clusters• Verify affected people’s income level/status as much as possible.• Response criteria is decided in the CIC e-mail for approval. NGO X will conduct Distribution Monitoring (DM) during the distributions and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) surveys following distributions. In the PDM process/phase, as part of the PDM harmonization, NGO X adheres and uses the harmonized PDM tool developed by the Cash Working Group.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: physical harm at distribution points (security, safety)Mitigation: Ensure staff are trained and aware of how to establish organized distributions due COVID-19 mitigation measures and application of checklist. Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, PwDs, elderly and pregnant/lactating women. Door-to-door distribution for beneficiaries with movement restrictions. Risk: Certain groups (especially older persons, women, children and PwDS) have limited access to or are unaware of GOAL’s (CFM).Mitigations: Diversify CFM and ensure accessibility for everyone, in particular the most vulnerable. Consult communities at early stages of projects on needs and preferable reporting and communication channels. Ensure gender inclusion is in place. Conduct surveys and consult beneficiaries during the data collection activities about their knowledge of feedback mechanisms and ask questions to understand barriers to existing CFM channels. Conduct regular awareness sessions during the project life cycle to increase communities’ awareness about the CFM and how to use it. Distribute relevant materials about GOAL’s and/or partner organisation’s CFM and that it can be used for suggestions and complaints to improve programming, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Ensure complaints handling in a timely manner. Ensure that the Accountability team is present during distribution activities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Abuse of power by distribution actors (GBV, physical coercion, monetary coercion)Mitigation: Ensure all staff sign CoC and receive training on NGO X’s anti-fraud policy and whistle-blowing policy and safeguarding and PSEA policy, Safeguarding Reporting Framework is in place. verification team consists of one female at least. CFM is in place and beneficiaries are fully aware of it does function. Risk: SEA against beneficiaries at/or travelling to/and from distribution points Mitigation: Distribution site considerations include proximity to active hostilities, presence of armed actors, and proper timing. Distribution points are located close to beneficiary villages. NGOX ensures proper timing for each distribution. Home-Delivery Mechanism in case of movement restrictions. Ensure all stakeholders involved in GOAL’s programming are informed, understand and sign GOAL’s suite of Safeguarding Policies and CoC. Communicate targeting criteria, and assure beneficiaries that services are free of charge. Ensure staff have access to GOAL’s service mapping about where to refer survivors for care and support.Risk: Child-headed HHs/Orphans: Abuse from the children guardians/authorized people for the assistance Mitigation: Commitment from the authorized people on the PSEA policy. The protection team try to contact the children regularly (after obtaining the consent from the delegates) to check their status and provide awareness sessions on how to prevent/report cases.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:Strong planning and arrangements to support efficient, effective humanitarian response among NGOs and INGOs to any conflict or UNSCR nonrenewal. Sufficient and timely access to required inputs through procurement, reliable supply and available transportation continues. NGO X's strategies and mechanisms to prevent armed and sanctioned actors from interfering in programmes remain effective. Sufficient and good quality USD notes are in circulation in NWS to distribute at the scale NGO X intends to.Risks: Closure of banks in Iraq or interruption of cash flow into the region, disrupting NGO X’s liquidity and ability to disburse MPCA. Scarcity and quality of small banknotes. Increased pressure from armed and sanctioned groups. & Assistance reaches those for whom it was not intended. Syrian pound and TRY depreciation against USD Mitigation: Arranging alternative methods for getting cash into Syria is an ongoing process. NGO X can shift cash resources to in-kind under currently active FWAs. Ensure rigorous beneficiary selection and verification processes so cash assistance reaches most vulnerable HHs. Cash distributions are provided in USD, which is the most stable currency in circulation which protects the transfer value provided to the beneficiaries. Switch to backup modality options as vouchers or in-kind if cash is unfeasible in line with other feasibility factors in consultation with donors and stakeholders.
