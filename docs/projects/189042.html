Project Summary

In 2022 and 2023, IOM Shelter and Settlement seek to meet immediate and long-term SNFIs needs of affected populations across South Sudan. IOM will prioritize newly displaced people, IDPs in protracted displacement and transition sites, flood and conflict-affected, vulnerable host communities and returnees through improved access to safe emergency and transitional shelter and lifesaving household NFIs. IOM will customize the response into full SNFI package, light response, flood kit, and cash and voucher assistance based on needs assessment following the Cluster new strategy. IOM aims to reach 350,000 people in deep field locations through frontline and static teams in Juba, Wau, Bor, and Bentiu. IOM is one of the leading agencies to implement CVA in the S-NFI Cluster, assisting over 35,000 people in 2021 through MPCA, restricted vouchers, Cash for Work, and conditional grants. IOM seeks to reach 55,000 individuals with CVA in locations with functional markets like Wau, Raja, Bor, Kajo-Keji, Bentiu and Nasir. In addition, IOM plans to increase the CVAs and reduce dependency on pipelines while empowering vulnerable families. The settlement program in Wau, Bor South, Raja and other locations will support the long-term shelter needs of the returnees and host communities through innovative shelter construction and environmentally friendly practices and the use of local material. The Housing, Land and Property team supports mainstreaming HLP in all shelter and community infrastructure projects while ensuring conflict sensitivity and assessment in contested areas. As SFP for Naivasha IDP camp, IOM will provide SNFI assistance and shelter upgrade to people living in IDP sites. In 2022, IOM will further strengthen integrated and multisectoral interventions in coordination with WASH, CCCM, Protection and Food Security.
In addition, IOM focuses on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse through coordination with protection actors, referral, training and prioritizes the needs of persons with disability, minority groups, child and women-headed households through needs assessments and special support. IOM interventions are aligned with cluster strategy, priorities and overall objectives. Focus on return and stable locations; the settlement program approach will enhance resilience, peace, tranquility, community development and decrease dependence on assistance.
As SNFI Cluster lead, IOM continues to coordinate responses, build local organizations' capacity and promote joint and integrated responses with other Clusters (WASH, CCCM, Protection and Food Security)
In addition, IOM continues to host and co-lead National CWG and the HLP. IOM mainstreams HLP rights, AAP, CFM, GBV, disability and inclusion, communication and community engagement, PSEA, seeks to enhance community capacities resilience and reduces dependence on assistance.

1 - Needs

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates 8.9M of South Sudan population in need of assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. An estimated 2.4M people face insufficient access to safe shelter and lifesaving NFIs. In 2021, an estimated 1.8M people lived in inadequate shelter, which increased the number of people who will need shelter support in 2022 if events in 2021 continue. Besides the existing displaced population, more than 100,000 people have returned from neighbouring countries, and 250,000 IDPs returned to their areas of origin. In the last three years, consecutive floods damaged shelters, property and displaced populations to secure areas. Western Bahr el Ghazal: In 2021 and 2022, IOM conducted over seven needs assessments in WBeG. The needs assessment and IRNA reports observed huge shelter and NFIs needs in WBeG. The 2021 floods affected more than 15,000 people in three payams in Jur river, destroying shelters and property. Affected counties are at severity level 4, with around 42.2% of the population considered under 'extreme need' situation. December 2021 population headcount reported 10,900 IDPs in Naivasha IDP camp and Hai Masna site. Shelter rehab and upgrade needs are projected throughout 2022 and 2023 as future conflicts may result in new arrivals to IDP sites. In mobility tracking, DTM reported 46,292 IDPs in WBeG. In 2021, Tambura's violence displaced more than 1,328 IDPs to Wau. There is an increase in returnees in Wau (171,394), Jur River (46,281), Raja (26,157). According to DTM reports, 67% of IDPs and 44.8% of returnees in WBeG lives in collapsed or occupying abandoned shelters. Northern Bahr el Ghazal: Recent IRNA reported 11,580 conflict-affected individuals from NBeG. Flooding also destroyed shelters Aweil (South, East, and West). According to DTM, non-displaced crisis-affected communities in NBeG is 309,920 people. Warrap: The state hosts majority of IDPs; 388,166 individuals. The conflict triggered by local politics, cattle raiding, and revenge attacks led to huge displacements. Floods equally displaced the majority in Tonj, Gogrial and Twic. Tonj (East, South, and North) are in IPC levels 5 and 4. DTM reported 103,126 individuals displaced in 2021 to neighbouring payams. These clashes affected Awul, Kirik, and Rual-Bet payams. As most locations remain inaccessible in Twic due to insecurity and flooding, the ongoing tension with Abyei will exacerbate reported needs for over 40,000 flood-affected people in 2021. Jonglei: DTM data indicated 71,625 conflict displaced individuals from Jonglei still lived in Mingkaman. In 2020, RRC recorded 37,215 people influx from Bor to Mingkaman due to flooding. The IDP leaders reported 53,169 people from Twic East, Duk, and Bor South. The ongoing conflict in Baidit, Bor South neighbouring areas, and other payams will likely result in more displacement. The returnees, IDPs, conflict, and flood-affected HC in Bor South and Awerial -Mingkaman have huge needs for shelters and DRR awareness-raising. Upper Nile: IOM conducted a needs assessment in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut and recorded that over 6,000 households need SNFI support. Over 100,000 flood-affected people need SNFI support.Unity: The heavy flooding of 2021 is only the most recent and has created further constraints for people who were barely beginning to recover from the cumulative impacts of conflict and floods. In addition, 19,100 displaced individuals have settled in five newly established internally displaced person (IDP) sites in Bentiu Town. The population of the former PoC site has also increased to 118,313, with 12,000 new entries triggered by flooding in Rubkona. Similarly, according to SNFI Cluster data, there are huge needs for SNFI support in other states, including Equatoria states, where around 136,000 flood-affected individuals need SNFI support.

2 - Project activities

Needs Assessment and analysis in flood and conflict-affected areas: IOM response to emergency needs of the affected population through mobile and static teams based in different locations. For emergency and recovery response, appropriate modality is determined upon completion of detailed needs and market analysis. IOM undertakes interagency, multi-sectoral, and sector-specific assessments with partners, NNGO capacity will be built through a joint review. IOM conducts technical flood assessments, use standard SNFI Cluster tools for data collection, develop tools for DRR, and CWG tools. Assessment findings are shared with the SNFI Cluster. During needs analysis, IOM consults with RRC, local authorities, men, women, PWDs, youth, and minority groups to understand their needs. The needs analysis process includes a review of existing reports, data, DTM updates and other IRNA reports.Verification and distribution of Shelter and NFI:Need analysis is followed by verification/registration of target beneficiaries. IOM ensures safe/dignified access to SNFIs/cash assistance in consultation with affected communities. Distribution committees are formed, messaging about the date, time, location, and items are done as a standard process. The distribution process includes waiting in the shade, lining for registration, separate lines for vulnerable persons, item receiving points, and CFM desks. IOM will assist affected communities through in-kind, cash and shelter support. IOM follows MOH guidance and Cluster COVID-19 response strategy. Interventions will have CFM procedures and feedback offered. IOM implements an in-kind standard package, flood kit, light SNFI support, and cash-based interventions depending on needs Assessment. IOM engages local communities, CBOs and NGOs during the distribution process to build their capacities.Shelter upgrade and rehab:Shelter team will respond to shelter rehab needs in IDP camp and Collective sites. And CCCM established CFM used to report needs for shelter rehab. The SNFI team will assess and construct shelters. IOM has trained IDPs in shelter construction to engage in shelter rehab and construction through CFW modalities. Cash and Voucher assistance:IOM will respond through CVA modalities where markets are functional and can absorb cash. CBI enhances the safety, privacy, and dignity of affected populations. And give beneficiaries freedom and autonomy to make choices. IOM will conduct baseline and end line for CVA response targeting affected people.Settlement programming: IOM will undertake long-term settlement programming besides emergency interventions to provide a people-centred response and enhance the resilience of communities. IOM will form village development committees and construct 500 low-cost shelters with high returnees. IOM has created a slate of settlement-centric programming that incorporates establishing community DMCs, building capacity, making flood resilient shelters and mitigation infrastructures. Coordination and technical support: IOM continues to lead SNFI Cluster, co-lead and host Cash Working Group, and serves as SFP for WBeG. In addition, IOM will dedicate resources for capacity building of SNFI Cluster and CWG partners on response methodology and CVA.Awareness of HLP, GBV, PSEA, and disability inclusion:IOM will ensure that Housing, Land and Property is mainstreamed in all interventions and due diligence/assessment is completed using the standard checklist. IOM all activities will ensure that GBV & PSEA risks are identified through safety audits and mitigation measures are taken. Under the current HRP, IOM will mainstream DRR activities into other activities and will focus on awareness raising activities only.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

IOM shelter and settlement cash and Voucher Assistance response is informed by detailed market and needs analysis, context, and capacity; hence, the right modality is decided based on an in-depth understanding of the operation's needs, context, and environment. If there is proper market functionality, access and capacity, IOM will prefer CVA. As per IOM's needs assessment and PDM, more than 80% of individuals prefer cash over in-kind. Based on the feasibility and market analysis recommendation, the appropriate CVA modality is decided in the form of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA), conditional cash assistance (cash-for-work), or restricted vouchers. CVA will stimulate the local economy as it will have a multiplier effect. IOM believes that CVA empowers affected communities to have freedom and autonomy in choosing the appropriate good and services that meet their immediate needs using available local resources, contributing to enhanced resilience.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CVA depends on local market functionality, availability of financial infrastructure for secure, accountable, responsible cash delivery, and target populations' preference and financial literacy. The market assessment is the first and foremost step for any CVA. IOM has the wise country data of market status in different areas, and members of JMMI, IOM support CWG for data collection. In addition to primary data, IOM will collect secondary information through existing reports and analysis. Services of financial service providers are important, and IOM is already in consultation with Aidonics to digitize the beneficiary data and transfer cash using mobile wherever infrastructure exists.As a member of the Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives (JMMI) and Cash working group (CWG), IOM monitors the market status, vendors' behaviours, prices, goods/services availability, restocking periods, supply routes, and traders' willingness to participate in CVA response. Before any CVA response, IOM conducts a feasibility assessment then compares it with the in-kind response to make an informed decision and modality and the type of the financial service provider. IOM has implemented CVA in locations like Bor, Pibor, Bentiu, Wau, Raja and Kejo-Keji and will continue to expand the cash assistance to new areas based on the market functionality. In 2022, IOM aims to support CWG for FSP mapping and support JMMI data collection.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IOM’s CVA and SNFI assessment processes are majorly informed by feedback from affected communities. IOM consults these communities through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and household interviews. IOM shelter and settlement consult beyond the gatekeepers in the affected communities and with all social groups, the Women, men, boys, girls, elderly, vulnerable groups, and their leaders, hence the information gathered from the extensive community engagement is used to inform design and implementation of projects. Through the long-term programming where infrastructures are constructed, IOM consults with the potential beneficiaries or the affected communities on the design so that it is culturally appropriate and acceptable, once changes are recommended, IOM modifies the design based on the feedback. IOM’s longer-term and resilience programs such as the settlement and DRR programs are people-centered, participatory, and designed based on feedback from the affected communities, IOM will continue to engage communities in the initiation, design, execution, monitoring, and evaluation stages of the project cycle. Robust CFM is used to collect feedback; hence feedback is provided in a timely manner.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IOM Shelter, NFIs, HLP, and DRR activities are tied to an exhaustive all-inclusive assessment before the responses. Based on the outcome of the assessment, IOM shelter and settlement interventions ensure equal access to all Shelter and settlement assistance provided to the crisis-affected vulnerable populations, ensuring the dignity, safety, and well-being of affected communities. Community members, IOM staff, and volunteers will be oriented to attend to the specific needs of vulnerable beneficiaries throughout the project cycle.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM’s overall objective is to support crisis-affected persons in accessing lifesaving assistance and support by integrating the principles of doing no harm, promoting safety, participation, non-discrimination, and empowerment across all programs. In line with the GBViC framework, IOM will: 1. Ensure all IOM staff and implementing partners are trained on IOM's Code of Conduct, PSEA, and AAP. PSEA training for all staff, enumerators and casual labor is mandatory. IOM will dedicate time and resources to ensure all staff is trained on PSEA principles, protocols, and reporting mechanisms. 2. Uphold PSEA protocols and reporting mechanisms throughout the humanitarian system. 3. In coordination with IOM GBV, translate PSEA educational material in IOM offices (into English and Arabic). 4. Educate beneficiaries (women, girls, boys, and men) about their rights, how and where to report SEA incidents, and confidentiality of information.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IOM Shelter and Settlement will ensure beneficiaries actively participate in the processes and activities that affect them. Project teams will conduct detailed needs analyses using mixed methods of data collection (qualitative and quantitative data collection tools). Project teams will continue to use Cluster-approved tools for accountability to the affected population (AAP) for mainstreaming AAP during the interventions including distribution and assessment checklist, response rapid monitoring tools and debrief tools after each intervention. The use of AAP tools will help IOM teams form Community Committees involving women, men, boys, girls, and elders’ representatives of different groups for assessment and distribution, ensure appropriate targeting, identify needs and context-specific project risks, and enable community feedback. At all stages of the project, the beneficiaries will freely provide feedback and raise complaints through CFMs that will be established for each response. IOM shelter and settlement will keep a record of each complaint and update the status of the issue. PDMs tool has an important section to monitor the CFM desk's performance, which will inform on the performance and recommend measures in case of any changes. IOM will also continue to explore the use of technology for CFM in locations where phones and the internet are functioning. In the settlement program area, IOM established village development committees and placed Complaint Boxes to gather feedback on service provision. Following Do No Harm principles, IOM will prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

IOM is committed to ensuring a safe environment for the registration and distributionprocess. IOM conducts safety audits and selects distribution sites based on accessibility,safety, and availability of other services. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries, IOMselects locations that are close to the majority of the potential beneficiaries and easilyaccessible to beneficiaries, IOM follows the cluster methodology for beneficiaries'registration and distribution. To mitigate the risk of violence, IOM ensures clearchannels of communication with the communities. IOM established distributioncommittees before each intervention and inform local authorities before eachintervention. Before any response/intervention, IOM conducts safety audits andcollaborates with protection and GBV partners to ensure that the GBV associated risksare identified and incorporated into the overall project cycle. IOM works withcommunities to build confidence and regain trust through equitable access to servicesand gender. Community participation is ensured at every stage of intervention. IOMshare selection criteria with the community and ensure that vulnerable households areincluded in the selection process – qualifying the selection criteria. IOM purposivelyselects persons with disabilities, women-headed households, and other minorities groupduring needs assessment and post-distribution monitoring. IOM also establishes aCFM desk during assessment and distribution. The CFM desk is placed in a prominentlocation and every hour, the distribution team, through the announcement, informsbeneficiaries to contact CFM for any complaint. IOM conducts rapid monitoring duringthe distribution and refers issues to CFM desks. IOM conducts safety audits prior toany intervention and by ensuring strict adherence to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelinesthrough assessment and response. IOM also incorporates HLP related issues in theresponse planning and beneficiaries' selection criteria. IOM employed a full-time HLPexpert to support HLP related issues both and the field and policy level.
