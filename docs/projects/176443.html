Project Summary

The recurrent conflict and disasters in South Sudan especially since 2013, have enormously contributed to the humanitarian crisis that has seen families separated and many children going unaccompanied into places strange to them but in search of survival. This project seeks to provide Lifesaving Child Care Services and Psychosocial Assistance for Girls and boys affected by disaster and armed conflict in Koch County, Unity State and Pariang County, Ruweng Administrative Area; who on a daily basis are at risk or indeed exposed to violence, early and forced marriages, child labor, neglect and all sorts of violence especially, limited recreational centers/facilities, physical violence has given their vulnerability is providing personal protection. Our already existing child protection field team, our tested community child protection networks are to aid identification and documentation of unaccompanied and separated children as well as, identify befitting foster care families that are in effect to provide a durable solution to the UASC. All activities are to embrace messaging for peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence, avoidable Gender-based Violence, and awareness of the dangers of UXOs.
The project will provide life-saving child protection prevention and response services through the provision of Comprehensive case management services and referrals for children with critical protection concerns; Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities; establishment of 8 child-friendly spaces( 4 for each County) to provide psychosocial support and recreational services, Child protection awareness raisings or campaigns and Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection concerns targeting 23,960 most vulnerable individuals ( 17610 children both boys and girls and 6350 adults both male and female). The activity will target 13530 persons who will be targeted in Rubkona and 9830 individuals in Koch and Pariang Counties in Ruweng Administrative Area. The target group includes unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed forces and armed groups, children victims of or at risk of GBV, domestic violence, physical and sexual abuse, GBV survivors and women and girls at risk of GBV. There is a timely need for protection services in Koch and Pariang Payams.
GUNO’s current program in Koch and Pariang identifies of child abuse and exploitation and UASC have been identified and there is still consistent need to proceed with further identification, registration and reunification with their families and this is to be full accomplished through proposed activities such as; establishment of child friendly spaces, Unaccompanied and Separated girls and boys identified, documented, and receiving family tracing and reunification services (UASC) ; Psychosocial Support for Children who are affected by the Conflict; Mine Risk Education; Education for special need for disability Children and Adult; Cash based Individual Protection Assistance; GBV Prevention Awareness and Case Management; UXOs/Mine Risk Awareness community campaigns; establishment of 8 child friendly spaces in Payams of Koch and Pariang Counties( 4 for each location), Peace Building engagements among affected children and adults and training on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including Case management and PSS. GUNO will work closely with other child protection partners to avoid overlapping and duplication of efforts. GUNO will also coordinate with CP partners through referrals of CP cases. This project will ensure close collaboration with the Child Protection and GBV Sub clusters and will be realized in a period of 12 months in Koch and Pariang Counties.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

GUNO will use through out the minimum standards, the expression “marginalised groups” when they might also be described as having specific needs, being at risk, vulnerable or excluded. In all cases, marginalised groups are understood to include older people, children (including adolescents), persons with physical, sensory or intellectual disabilities, persons with mental health disabilities, survivors of SGBV, victims of trafficking and people living with HIV/AIDS or other chronic illnesses. In different contexts, other forms of diversity, including ethnicity and nationality or lack thereof, migration and/or legal status, religion, caste, class, sexual and gender minorities, as well as intersectionality between forms of diversity and marginalised groups will be key issues to consider when talking about marginalised groups. Users of these minimum standards are therefore encouraged to analyse the situation carefully and understand exactly which individuals and groups are included in “marginalised groups” in their context as well as assess their own assumptions. A term is used in this publication that is meant to encompass all marginalised groups: “persons of all gender identities, ages, disabilities and backgrounds”.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

GUNO aim to identify the prevalence of malnutrition (underweight, stunting and wasting) among children under 5 years old living in Koch County Unity state, South Sudan. Material and methods A cross-sectional community-based descriptive study will conducted, to collect primary information from households using a scientific questionnaire, anthropometric measurements (mid-upper arm circumference – , weight and length/height), and clinical evaluations of the malnourished children to check the presence of severe protein energy malnutrition (PEM). And this information will be shared with relevant institution or service providers.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

When implementing the minimum standards, it is important to note our role in times of disaster and conflict. In accordance with international law, it is the primary role and responsibility of State authorities and relevant non-State actors to protect, ensure the security and fulfil the rights of persons on their territory or under their jurisdiction (including those beyond its borders). In the case of duty bearers that are willing to protect, and possess the capacity to do so, the approach of humanitarian 6 International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies actors is likely to be one of proactive and supportive engagement for GUNO. In the case of duty bearers that are willing to protect but have limited capacity, humanitarian actors will likely take on a supportive role to strengthen existing protection architecture and identify roles and responsibilities between protection actors. This will ensure complementarity of expertise and mandates and minimise the potential of duplicating efforts of the State or other expertise and mandates and minimize the potential of duplicating efforts of the State or other protection actors as well as avoid undermining the capacity and will of duty-bearers to fulfil their obligations.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The National Center for Health Statistics reference showed that the prevalence of global malnutrition, moderate malnutrition and severe malnutrition was 23.1%, 10.2% and 12.9%, respectively. We conclude that improvements in child feeding, and better maternal education are needed to maintain the children's nutritional status. GUNO will always encourage food production to the families of children where it operates through parents’ empowerment in food production through its’ other livelihood projects and other livelihood partners where it operates.
