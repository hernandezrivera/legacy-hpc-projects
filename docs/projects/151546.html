Project Summary

The education support for emergencies and crisis affected boys and girls in Somalia/Somaliland project will target a total of 25,000 (50% girls) in 60 schools in Lower Juba, Bay, Banadir and Hiran regions in South Central Somalia; Nugaal, Karkaar, Sanaag, Sool, and Mudug regions in Puntland state of Somalia; and Awdal, , Togdheer, Sool and Sanaag Regions of Somaliland. The project will build onto the gains realized by the ended ECW, 1st window opportunity, GPE Drought Response project, Sagar Response Project and SHF 2018 projects being implemented in the drought affected schools in all Regions of Somaliland, Hiran, Kismayo, Baidoa, Garowe, Dangoreyo, Gardho, Rako, Galdagob and Galkioa Districts in Nugaal, Karkaar and Mudug regions.
This project will strive to expand access and improve retention for boys and girls by providing quality and equitable education to ensure that the children do not lose out on their right to education. This will be done through continuous mobilization of community to enroll their children in school, promoting safe learning environment through construction and rehabilitation of classrooms and Temporary Learning Spaces, provision of safe drinking water, supplementary school feeding, teaching/learning and recreational materials, trainings as well as paying incentives for their teachers since the parents can afford to pay the fees of their children. The massive displacement of children in the new locations means additional pressure on the already overstretched facilities like, classrooms, teaching learning materials and hygiene and sanitation facilities.
The project will be implemented in the Districts of Baidoa, Kismayo, Beletweyne, Boroma, Zeyla, Lughaya, Burao, Odwayne, Aynabo, Lasanod, Hudun, Erigavo, El-Afwayn, Gardho, Rako, Bocame, Badhan, Galkacio, Galdagob, Dangorayo and Garowe districts. . The project will directly target 18,300 (50% girls) children ) of different ages from communities most affected by the effects of conflicts, drought, floods, tropical storms, and returnees from Kenya with the most needed, life- saving interventions like supplementary feeding, hygiene and sanitation facilities. The project will be school-based, targeting 60 schools hosting children most affected by the drought. All this is aimed at ensuring that IDPs, returnees and host community children, both boys and girls are enrolled and retained at school, and are protected.
Save the Children will also provide incentives to some teachers in the targeted schools, especially to those who are not benefiting from incentives from the Ministry of Education or any other agency including communities. With the massive influx of new children to their schools, it means additional responsibilities on the shoulders of these teachers, and the incentives will motivate them.
The project will also work with the respective Ministries of Education in Somaliland, Puntland and Central and Southern Somalia by enhancing the capacity of the Ministry of education staff through trainings, policy and strategy strengthening so that they are empowered to effectively manage education matters in their respective states, regions and districts.

Needs

Out of the 2.6 million internally displaced people in Somalia, approximately 1 million are school aged children and of the 30,000 refugees, 12,000 are school aged children. Currently, 1.83 million school aged children are in need of assistance with a focus for increased access, retention as well as of sustainability. Those suffering the greatest educational inequities are IDPs, females, nomadic/pastoralist, rural coastal communities (HNO2019). According to assessment done by Save the Children on barriers to enrollment and retention in Baidoa, school fees was found to be the leading detrimental factor as it uses up more than 50% of the household income, 36% of IDPs reported that they don’t have schools within their settlements and distance is the major barrier particularly for girls. This situation puts children at increasing risk of exploitation, abuse and getting enlisted by armed groups. Combination of domestic responsibilities and child labor also significantly contributes to the increasing number of out of school children. There are 40 poorly equipped and facilitated schools serving 6,400 children (3,100 girls). There are an estimated 10,000 school aged children within the estimated population of 154,467 in the 40 camps. With the current total enrolment in the 40 schools, there are still 6,400 (41%) estimated out of school children. The current student classroom ratio in the 40 schools averages 85 students per classroom. There is therefore dire need for more classrooms and the old classrooms also need rehabilitation. A number of schools do not have WASH facilities and facilities in many schools require rehabilitation and there is a need for continuous awareness and improvement of knowledge and sanitation practice among the students. Schools also do not have enough basic learning materials including recreational materials. There are 215 including volunteer teachers in the 60 schools.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

• IDPs are prone to exclusion because of host communities’ reluctance to integrate them and also constant eviction in the case of Mogadishu in particular but there is no significant obstacle in the full participation of IDPs, including from marginalized communities in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects.• Though the assistance targets vulnerable IDP communities but does not discriminate of exclude host communities and hence the assistance or services provided will not aggravate the tensions between IDPs (including those from marginalized groups) and host communities or trigger violence against IDPs.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

• Undeniably there is a risk of possible exclusion based on gender, age and disability. The project will identify such risk groups at the beginning of the project and deliberately priories services to these vulnerable groups.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

• Beneficiaries and their communities face particular risks relating to armed conflicts and violence and as such this contributes significantly to internal displacements. We will uphold the humanitarian principle of neutrality and impartiality and will take the service as close to the affected communities as possible but the project will not be expected to aggravate the exposure of targeted communities to insecurity and other impacts of armed conflicts.
