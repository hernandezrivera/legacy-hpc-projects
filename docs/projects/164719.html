Project Summary

Part 1. Nutrition surveillance system generates evidence-based information
To extending The nutrition programme routine surveillance provides up-to-date regular information on GAM , SAM , MAM , MAM within PLWs, Stunting at burden communities and supports mapping of interventions considering the “need of children and PLWs ”.
Community and facility-based surveillance are planned to scale up the next 6 months of 2020 in the Northwest of Syria.
With the assumption that the UNSCR for cross border humanitarian support is renewed for 2020 which will provide the necessary humanitarian space to continue service delivery
Part 2. Activation of 15 Rapid Response Team provides lifesaving essential integrated health, nutrition and child protection field services. And 5 mother and Baby areas (MBAs)
Part 3. Community healthcare workers support, promote and guide communities for IYCF recommended child feeding practices.
Part 4. Ensure compliance with five key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) ,Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violance (GBV).
Part 5: UNICEF as they are the main pipeline manging agency in North West Syria and to ensure no break in the pipeline is happening , PAC will receive the supplies based on annual and quarterly forecast and in accordance to the estimated needs among mothers and children
Effective and efficient coordination and information management for nutrition cluster (salaries and other cost of the cluster co-coordinator and IMO).
This is to support the nutrition cluster’s activities through allocating Information officer and cluster co-coordinator
Part 7:
Evaluation of Nutritional Status among IDPs in the camps targeting children and mothers and evaluating Anemia status among mothers
This survey will focus on the IDPs in the camps including newly IDPs , this because of the massive displacements started on 15 Dec 2019 till now.

4 - Needs

The latest escalation of violence in Northwest Syria is having devastating consequences on the civilians inIdleb. Since 16 December 2019, aerial bombardment intensified in southern Idleb displacing tens of thousands of civilians from Ma’arrat An -Nu’man, Saraqab and the surrounding towns and villages.From 1 to 25 December, some 244,000 people fled their homes -mostly in southern Idleb Some 80 percent of the new IDPs – 194,867 individuals – are women and children. More people may be displaced as many civilians packed their belongings and are ready to flee, should the hostilities expand to affect other parts of the Idleb area.And according to Nutrition surveillance team has reported that there has been a 0.5% increase in global acute malnutrition among children under the age of 5 years during this past week of displacement.Moderate Acute Malnutrition (in children) has increased by 0.1 % and at least 21.6% of displaced pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) are acutely malnourished.This sharp increase in cases in only one week calls for maximizing the provision of high energy biscuits (for children and PLWs) and Lipid-based Nutrient Supplements (LNS) which function to prevent acute malnutrition in such situations and is currently out of stock. UNICEF is currently mapping its available pipelines to redistribute to areas of high demand.Supplies will be airlifted from other UNICEF hubs in the region within one week.The project aims to fill the selected/complement identified gaps for the period of the allocation in key geographical areas (Atareb / Daret Azza/ Dana/ Idleb/ Qourqeena/ Darkosh/ Salqin / Bennsh / Kafr Takharim/ Teftnaz).In order to prevent and decrease the malnutrition rates within people in need especially boys and girls U5 and PLW.And responding to newly IDPs Children and mothers in Northwest Syria by scaling up Rapid response teams and referral mechanisms.
