Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face significant threats from landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW), with over 22,361,378 m² of land suspected to be contaminated, according to the October 2024 NMAA/UNMAS contamination highlights map. The anticipated increase in returnees in 2025 further raises the risk, as many lack awareness of explosive ordnance hazards and how to respond when encountering them. These explosives restrict civilians from accessing essential resources like water, firewood, and farmland, as well as attending school or receiving healthcare. They also hinder humanitarian organizations from reaching vulnerable populations and providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas.
To address these risks, CINA will conduct Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) activities in highly affected areas such as Maban, Malakal, Melut (Upper Nile), Akobo, Duk, Twic East (Jonglei), and Kapoeta East and Lafon (Eastern Equatoria). These activities aim to raise awareness and promote behavioral change to reduce injuries and fatalities. South Sudan Mine Action AoR plans to reach 380,000 individuals (227,000 residents, 109,000 IDPs, and 43,000 returnees) with risk education messages before they reach their destinations. Within this, CINA will directly target 14,776 individuals, including 7,684 children (3,996 girls and 3,688 boys) and 7,092 adults (3,688 women and 3,404 men). This group includes 2,955 returnees, 2,955 refugees, 2,955 IDPs, and 5,911 residents, including people with disabilities.
Each county will have an EORE and Community Liaison (CL) team, trained and accredited to deliver risk education in accordance with SS-NTSG and CINA EORE SOP. CINA will collaborate with other mine action organizations to prioritize locations that pose the greatest risks to humanitarian access and hinder displaced communities' return. This effort seeks to ensure access to essential resources, facilitate safe returns, and support humanitarian aid delivery while promoting safe behaviors to prevent accidents.
The EORE project will integrate with other programs, such as protection and livelihood efforts, to enhance access to land, improve socio-economic benefits, and foster self-reliance. These interventions will uphold human dignity, address vulnerabilities, and reduce risks from explosive ordnance, making EORE a critical component of mine action and essential for saving lives.

1. Needs

The October 2024 UNMAS-South Sudan contamination map highlights the widespread threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) across the country, with an estimated 22,361,378 m² of contaminated land. This includes 117 AP minefields, 53 AT minefields, 26 roads, 104 cluster munition strikes, and 39 battlefields, much of which lies along routes used by refugees and returnees. Safe land is critical for resettlement, agriculture, and ensuring the safe return of displaced populations, while Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) is vital for promoting safe behaviors and reducing accidents. Despite donor-supported efforts by organizations like UNICEF, SSHF, GFFO, DANIDA, and UNOPS to clear contaminated areas and raise awareness, a significant gap in Mine Action persists due to extensive EO contamination caused by the Sudanese civil war and subsequent conflicts. Explosive ordnance continues to pose severe humanitarian, health, and socio-economic risks, causing fatalities, injuries, and disruptions to daily life even after active conflicts have ended. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has led to a large influx of Sudanese refugees and South Sudanese returnees in 2024, with more expected in 2025. Many of these individuals, along with host communities, lack awareness of EO risks and often engage in unsafe behaviors, emphasizing the urgent need for continued EORE activities. CINA, with its trained EORE teams and experience implementing donor-funded projects, will work to mitigate EO risks in affected counties. Collaboration with other mine action operators will be essential to ensure effective operations. The project will include needs assessments and pre-deployment evaluations to prioritize and inform EORE and Mine Action interventions effectively.

2. Project activities

To address the needs of at-risk populations, CINA will provide lifesaving EORE activities in alignment with the 2025 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP). CINA aims to reach 14,776 people; 7,684 children (3,996 girls, 3,688 boys) and 7,092 adults (3,688 women, 3,404 men) through EORE and CL sessions, including people with disabilities. EORE/CL Teams shall adhere to the EORE SOP, which outlines the duration of each session (45 minutes) and the maximum number of participants (40-50) allowed per session. This activity will take place in schools, villages, churches, and other public spaces to ensure broad coverage in the areas of operation. As part of public information dissemination efforts, CINA will conduct radio talk shows in locations with functional radio stations to share these safety messages and ensure they reach everyone. The teams will use the EORE banners approved by NMAA and UNMAS. EORE messages will be delivered in a non-judgmental manner that is sensitive to factors such as gender, age, and other aspects of diversity. These messages will aim at empowering individuals to make informed decisions that reduce risks for themselves, their families, and their communities. Conduct pre and post-EORE session tests: Pre and post-EORE session tests will be conducted with a sample of beneficiaries from the EORE sessions to evaluate the increase in their knowledge about EO risks and related safe behaviours. The EORE sessions will be adjusted based on the results of these tests. CINA will use approved data collection and analysis tools fundamental to tailor EORE interventions based on the needs and priorities of the at-risk population. Conduct community-based Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) training for EORE focal points to prioritize local approaches to EORE. Recently, during the Mine Action Liaison Conference held in Juba in November 2024, community representatives expressed concerns regarding the lack of community engagement in mine action operations. In response, CINA will establish a committee of ten Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) focal points in each location to facilitate effective communication with communities. This committee will include teachers, women, youth, religious, IDP/Refugee camp leaders, people with disabilities, and local chiefs. This committee will help in achieving community liaison activities (Communication exchange) aimed at fostering community engagement and involvement in Mine Action operations. This will enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of Mine Action work within the community and contribute to the project's sustainability. Conduct a three-day training session for adolescent peer educators on Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) to help deliver safety messages in schools and the community. The most at-risk populations primarily include children and women. By providing training and support to peer educators, we can disseminate EORE messages to a larger audience and contribute to the sustainability of the project. Organize a two-day training session for Parents Teachers’ Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) focused on Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) messages. The goal of this training is to equip participants with the knowledge necessary to inform children at schools and their families about EORE. Members of the PTAs and SMCs will receive training on essential EORE messages to provide guidance in schools, homes, and their communities. These two school-based EORE training sessions will also contribute to the project's sustainability. CINA will be sharing daily reports (EORE/CL Sessions by IMSMA through survey 123. The above mentioned activities will be implemented with a budget of USD 300,000 for a period of one year 1st January – 31st December 2025.
