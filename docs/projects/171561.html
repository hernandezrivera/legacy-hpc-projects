1 - Needs

According to the Ministry of Health (MoH) and UNRWA, anaemia prevalence in Gaza and West Bank in women and children is at the level of public health concern. The latest available data from MOH revealed 30.7% of children 6 to 59 months were anaemic, and the 2020 HNO puts this figure at 40%. Moreover, 27.5% of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) were anaemic. (MOH 2018 annual report). Based on a data review (2006-2018), anaemia prevalence amongst PLW has been consistently at the border line and above public health significance (40%). The 2019 assessment conducted by UNICEF, World Food Programme (WFP) and Save the Children (SCI) outlined a deterioration in the nutritional status of the most vulnerable children under five, pregnant and lactating women in Gaza. There were 18% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers in access-restricted areas (ARA) who were undernourished. Above 6% of infants were never breastfed and 86% of children under five living in ARA did not consume a minimum acceptable diet. 210,000 children in Gaza under five are particularly vulnerable due to micronutrient deficiencies; that is seven out of ten children. There is a pocket of 36,400 children under five living in ARA who are particularly vulnerable and prone to incidences of watery or bloody diarrheal disease and respiratory infections, exacerbated by the deteriorated access to safe drinking water and health services. The combined effect of the poor maternal nutrition status, sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices, child morbidity and inadequate water and sanitation may lead to an increased risk of acute malnutrition, an impaired physical growth and cognitive development. This has been exacerbated by the current pandemic and lockdown in Gaza that led to further socio-economic deterioration and limited access to health and nutrition services. In the West Bank, the findings from the OCHA H2 multi-sectoral household assessment revealed high food insecurity. Only 6% of households indicated that the family had enough food. This is considered as anecdotal evidence that malnutrition, anaemia and food insecurity became evident given the economic degradation and restricted access to goods, services and employment. Moreover, the access to quality maternal, newborn and child health and nutrition is further compromised by the inconsistent supply of essential medical items, including those for treatment and prevention of anaemia and other micronutrient deficiencies. An additional assessment by UNICEF and a national NGO partner in January 2020 in H2 communities confirmed the anaemia prevalence with 34% of children under five years and 19% of pregnant and lactating women diagnosed with anaemia. All anaemic cases were referred for further follow up and supplementation plan in MoH clinics of the H2 area. Accordingly, this joint project aims to address the critical gap in nutrition services in Palestine, across the West Bank (Hebron H2 and Area C) and the Gaza Strip. It will address life-threatening micronutrient malnutrition among the most vulnerable women of reproductive age and under-five children including moderate and severe wasting through provision of appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling, food and non-food items, including essential micronutrients and treatment jointly delivered by UNICEF, WFP and Save the Children.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will procure and deliver the annual requirement of essential micronutrients for at least 36,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) (Gaza: 24,000; WB:12,000) and for 40,000 children aged 6 to 59 months (girls: 19,600; boys: 20,400) in Gaza and the West Bank (Gaza: 30,000; WB: 10,000) who are attending the Ministry of Health (MoH) and NGO facilities. UNICEF uses its international procurement mechanism and channels for the procurement of supplies. All micronutrients will be predefined jointly with relevant partners and authorities to ensure specifications meet the national protocol for maternal and child nutrition. WFP will provide US$ 20 unconditional cash transfers (e-voucher) on a monthly basis for most vulnerable pregnant and lactating women and mothers of children (2-5 years) with high anaemia and micronutrient deficiencies. WFP will target 1,500 (1,000 PLW in Gaza and 500 PLW in the West Bank mainly in Area C—Al Rawain, Arab AL Rashayida, AL Bweib, Birin, Susiya, Um Al Khair and Zanota—and H2 area in Hebron). This intervention will be combined with nutrition and COVID-19 counseling activities to increase the dietary impact of the e-voucher as well to raise awareness on protection from COVID-19 by healthy nutrition. The activities will target the same 1500 women who will be receiving e-vouchers. WFP will implement the counseling activities in the West Bank, while Save the Children will implement it in Gaza. The counseling activity will target 100 men (50 in Gaza, and 50 in West Bank) and 100 children (50 in Gaza and 50 in West Bank). SCI will train 60 health workers (40 females and 20 males) from INGOs and NGOs in the 5 Gaza governorates on 1) detection and management acute malnutrition, 2) prevention and treatment of micronutrient deficiencies and 3) provision of quality IYCF counselling. The capacity development initiative will consider important emergency preparedness considerations for health workers and their facilities. WFP will coordinate with relevant authorities on selected hardship cases for the e-voucher and in identifying the targeted groups for IYCF counselling. SCI in partnership with a local NGO will provide supplies (safer breastmilk substitutes kit, hygiene kit and generically labelled infant formula) for the safe and appropriate management of 200 non-breastfed infants <6 months living in the most vulnerable families in the ARA (boys:100, girls:100). SCI through its technical nutrition specialists will train 20 NGO’s protection caseworkers (15 females and 5 males) on basic signs and symptoms for the detection and referral of malnourished children to nutrition centres in Gaza city and in Khan Younis. SCI, in partnership with a local NGO, will support the provision of individual counselling on maternal nutrition and IYCF counselling to 1000 PLW of children under 2 years of age that are receiving the WFP voucher support /cash support. The counselling will help build also the capacity of the women to self-manage potential issues and help their neighbours experiencing similar concerns. SCI, in partnership with a local NGO, will support the creation of 60 community-based support groups with lead mothers to among PLW of children under the age of 2 years of age that are receiving the WFP food voucher/cash support. The activities will aim to build community resilience and emergency preparedness, to increase the community capacity to respond and cope with future shocks and crisis escalation. All partners will leverage resources through their networks to ensure referral of suspected victims of GBV to relevant service providers. UNICEF, WFP and SCI through their networks will coordinate with UNFPA to ensure local partners receive capacity building on detection and referral of women subject to violence. UNICEF, WFP and SCI will be contributing to improved nutritional status of women and their children under the age of five years, through building the capacity of staff at service providers, provision of case management and treatment services to children, in addition to supporting caregivers to have improved knowledge to provide adequate IYCF to their children. All capacity building and counselling activities will ensure building the capacity of health professionals and care givers to enhance their skills to address severe and moderate malnutrition, which includes the management and support of IYCF in emergencies. Through this project, UNICEF, WFP and SCI will support delivery of key nutrition interventions as a part of the emergency nutrition response and preparedness. All agencies will target children with malnutrition with focus on children with developmental delays and disabilities, as the early detection of malnutrition is essential for early intervention to reverse potential complications of disease or developmental delays. All the activities both health facility and community based will seek ways to facilitate the accessibility and inclusivity of women and children with disabilities. The nutrition activities will ensure that there are disability conscious considerations and adaptations considered as needed, so to be more inclusive of women and children with disabilities and increase their participation. Partners will work with communities to address the underlying problems that create these dire situations and to build resilience in households, communities and preparedness systems to withstand crises and cumulative stresses

3 - Indicators

In line with the intervention logic and strategic objective, a set of indicators will be assigned to measure the level of success in achieving intended changes on target children and women. All three agencies will be reporting against the following cluster indicators; # of < 5 children receiving targeted health and nutrition interventions (Target 40,200, of which 50% are female, including 2,000 with acute malnutrition and 200 non-breast -fed children) # of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services (Target 36,000 PLW) # of healthcare workers trained on the prevention and management of Malnutrition (Target 140 health, community and protection workers, of which 70% are female) # of people directly benefitting from awareness sessions and health education (Target 1,500 women, 100 men and 100 children, of which 95% are female) UNICEF, WFP and SCI will be developing different pre and post surveys with targeted beneficiaries, mainly the health and community workers. In addition, a Knowledge Attitude Practice (KAP) survey will be conducted by end of the project to measure actual changes on caregivers’ knowledge and practices and to identify percentage of targeted pregnant women, mothers and caregivers (men, women) who demonstrate improved knowledge and adoption of recommended maternal and IYCF practices. For the e-voucher intervention WFP has a process monitoring that is conducted at all contracted retail shops on a regular basis for a representative sample. WFP conducts post-distribution household monitoring (PDM) visits with a view to monitor the improvement or deterioration in the food security status of assisted people and assess the effectiveness of its interventions.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

All three agencies protect and uphold the rights of their beneficiaries, to receive safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries and their guardians in case of children is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies, joining training and community interventions. All three agencies will ensure applying international standards, and WHO standards for micronutrient supplements and other supplies. All three agencies have developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end users.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All three agencies conduct independence need assessment prior distribution of supplies or materials. This is verified through different cluster members to ensure targeting the most vulnerable. Three agencies agreed to target common communities in both Gaza and West Bank that have been identified as the most vulnerable by the cluster and where all three agencies have been operating previously and have conducted several assessments last year and earlier this year. The relevant assessment will ensure an equal and impartial interventions are designed.
