Project Summary

GBV Response strategies will enhance access to specialized services for survivors, including GBV case management, psychosocial support, safety and security, and legal aid. Safe houses, Women and Girls' spaces, and referral pathways will be reinforced, with cash assistance included in GBV case management to empower survivors.
Data collection and analysis will inform programming and advocacy, by utilizing systems like the GBVIMS
The project has a response strategy employs both static and mobile service delivery to reach remote, underserved communities, prioritizing in Leer, Guit and Payianjar Counties .
The project will tailor the following activities in the three counties including the following
1. Training of the volunteers social workers on GBV Case management for case workers supporting survivors to assess their multisector needs (health, mental health/psychological, legal, safety and security, livelihoods, etc.)
2. Documentations of cases and recording related to GBV and violation of rights in Leer , Guit and Payianjar. This activity will be carried out by three paralegals officers ; one in Guit, 1in Payijiar and 1 in Leer .Data will be collected on a monthly basis while visiting health centres, police units, customary courts and Payam chiefs in each of the two locations to collect and document these cases.
3. Provisions of pro-bono services to GBV survivors and violation of rights. WAO will work with gender officers/paralegals/officers to offer probono services to 150 GBV survivors in Leer , Guit and Payianjar .These will include trauma counselling, legal aid(pay police/court fees, legal fees ,represent GBV survivors in the customary courts and process of referrals of certain cases to High court),facilitate local transport to help GBV report and follow up cases in Police units and customary courts, offer psychosocial support, facilitate access to medical care especially victims of rape and defilement taken for clinical management in the health centres, and offer meals such as sugar,rice,Posho and beans to the GBV survivors.
4. Conducting Community dialogues to influence local authorities on laws and policies affecting women rights. WAO will conduct 3 dialogues with women, men, local chiefs, customary court authorities, commissioners, traditional leaders and local police. These dialogues will be to influence local authorities to change laws harmful to women’s rights. This activity will target 30(15m/15f) beneficiaries in locations of Leer county; Guit and Payianjar Counties .
5. Organize campaign/meetings to sensitize women and men on gender discrimination and women's, rights. WAO will organize 6 sessions on early and forced marriages, GBV , women discrimination and women's right to own land and property, targeting youth, women, local authority, men, customary and statutory courts, state government, local authorities in Leer , Guit and Payainjar counties.
6. 3 sessions (1 in Leer and 1 in Guit and 1 in Payianjiar ) on Engaging Men on Accountable Practices (EMAP) targeting 150 men especially youths a who seem to be the biggest perpetrators of violence against women and girls.
7. Provide income generating Activities and psychosocial support to GBV survivors: WAO will provide in-kind IGAs to survivors of GBV, targeting 90 women (30 in Leer, 30 in Guit and 30 in Payianjar ). This is a form of economic empowerment to the women and GBV survivors. 1. Needs

In areas bordering Sudan, particularly in Unity state and Upper Nile and 50 to 130 percent in Unity State Gender-based violence (GBV) is rampant , driven by harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality, severely impacting the safety and rights of women and girls. The Protection Risk Monitoring System reported GBV as the main protection risk in 38% of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. The ISNA revealed that 36% of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns, while 53% of households face barriers to accessing vital services. The ISNA revealed that 36% of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns, while 53% of households face barriers to accessing vital services. Gender-based violence (GBV) is deeply entrenched in South Sudan where in locations where this project is tailored to in Leer, Guit and Payianjar Counties especially where women and girls continue at high risk in both public and private spaces. Gender based violence is not only manifested in the physical and sexual violence, but also through overall lack of equal opportunities for women and girls to lead a fulfilling life. High rate of illiteracy amongst girls and women, high rate of child marriage, high maternal mortality due to preventable causes, limited access to livelihood opportunities are some of the indicators that highlight the dire need to address the gender inequality at the root causes to end gender-based violence. According to South Sudan Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBV IMS) annual report of 2021, out of the total cases reported, 23% survivors were below the age of 18 years, and 37% of the reported incidents were physical violence, 25% were emotional violence and 17% were sexual violence. Government data generally shows that about 40% of girls are married before age of 18 years. Clearly, there is a need for urgent and scaled up action against GBV in South Sudan as well as in Southern Unit State. GBV in particularly, sexual violence, is widespread in Unity State and one of the largest crises emanating from the protracted conflict in South Sudan and across Unity State towns, Counties, Payams and Bomas perpetrated by unknown Armed attacks including gang groups fighting. The use of sexual violence and the brutalization of women and girls have been well documented that 1 out 3 women experience violence in their lives. Even in displacement settings, threats, and risks of GBV against women and girls persist particularly sexual violence, growing levels of intimate partner violence (IPV), sexual exploitation, harassment and early / forced marriage. Additionally, all forms of GBV such as rape, sexual harassment, physical violence, psychological violence, denial of resources, and others, remain prevalent in three Counties of Unity state. The project will target GBV through Prevention, Risk Mitigation, and Response strategies in there Counties of Unity State . GBV Prevention aims to challenge harmful norms via community programs like “’SASA”, “Community Cares Program”, and “Engaging Men and Boys”. It also includes community engagement initiatives such as awareness raising on GBV and PSEA. GBV Risk Mitigation involves integrating GBV actions in cluster/sectoral responses, safety audits, and distributing dignity kits and fuel-efficient stoves. The GBV Response will focus on improving survivors' access to services like case management, psychosocial support, and legal aid, safety and protection while enhancing the availability of safe houses and GBV referral pathways.
