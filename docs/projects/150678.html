Project Summary

The project responds to Cluster Objective 2: Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, catch-up classes and school-based psychosocial services
.Arabic and Math classes provided to 5,000 boys and girls between 8-12 years old and enrolled in grades 3, 4, 5, and 6 who are at risk of dropping out in vulnerable public schools in the Gaza strip. The project will enhance their basic skills in Arabic Language and Math as a way to strengthen their abilities both in these and other subjects. A training will be provided to more than 180 teachers (50% females) on remedial education. Learning materials and stationary will be provided to learners and teachers to run the classes. These activities are categorized under the responsive and remedial objectives of the HRP education cluster.
Project Locations: 60 vulnerable and marginalized public schools located in the following education directorates: Beit Lahia and Jabalia in North Gaza Directorate, Gaza city in West and East Gaza Directorates, Al Maghazi, Deir Al Balah, Al Zawayda in the Middle Area Directorate, Khan Younis city in Khan Younis Directorate, Abasan, Bani Suhaila, Khuza’ah, Al Qararah in , East Khan Younis Directorate, and Rafah Directorate.

Needs

The education system in Gaza is facing pressures exacerbated by the deteriorating humanitarian situation. For school year 2018/2019, the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE) has enrolled additional 5,000 students whose parents could no longer afford private school fees or transportation to UNRWA school. There are 409 public schools operating in Gaza of which 62.8 per cent operate on a double or triple shift system and with student density of up to 50 per classroom, which reduces instruction time to about four hours a day. This limits the time available to reinforce learning, support slow learners, offer remedial education programmes, and extracurricular activities. According to the education cluster revised framework for Gaza, 2018 , there are 24,389 school children (50% females) in need for remedial classes and 303,607 school children (49%) females in need for school-based psychosocial support services in both UNRWA and public schools. Thus, these boys and girls are in need for structured play activities that help them learn, recover from distressing experiences, and develop social and emotional skills (INEE Guidance Note on Psychosocial Support) . In addition, the shortage of teachers due to limited recruitment of teachers in the last three years is another factor affecting student learning. According to MoEHE Gaza, there is currently a shortage of 800 teachers and education staff (650 teachers and 150 education staff) . MoEHE Gaza appointed 150 temporary teachers with support from UNDP via the “Job Creation Project” for the first semester only (September to- December 2018). Additionally, the new teachers are lacking the experience in particularly in the interactive teaching methods as well as in dealing with children who are falling behind which will can negatively affect the quality of teaching and in turn could increase school drop-out particularly in low grades. UNICEF Out-of-School Children study, 2018 revealed that in SOP girls and boys leave schools after 3-4 years if they have not acquired basic literacy or numeracy skills .

Activities

The remedial education programmes are of essence to help students cope and overcome learning difficulties and ensure no student is left behind by recovering the lost school time. This project addresses the Strategic Objective 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan that identifies as critical internvetion remedial education of children who are at risk of drop out in Gaza. The project will ensure provision of remedial classes and tutoring for 5,000 basic school children (age 8 to 12; around 50 per cent females) in Gaza who are experiencing difficulties in learning and in mastering foundational skills of reading and writing of Arabic language and Math and who are at risk of dropping-out. Children will be selected from schools located in 116 vulnerable localities identified by the vulnerability mapping conducted by the Islamic Relief, UK, 2017 . Children will be selected through the examination of the learning achievement records in most vulnerable schools and selection of those who have shown continuous or rapid decline in learning achievement over the past 5 months. The project will focus on foundational skills in the Arabic language and Math as key priority among core subjects where students have been falling behind. The project will target particularly those who are failing to meet minimal normative standards in the subjects. Therefore, special customized tests will determine which students will need support. The project will build on catch-up / remedial programmes that were implemented by UNICEF between 2015 and 2018, and will benefit from materials that have already been developed and tested in partnership with MoEHE. These materials considered the recent changes in the curriculum and have been completely updated as of 2017. UNICEF will use methodology already proven effective through the on-going and previous interventions implemented with the MOEHE. Children will be instructed in each school by three teachers, one teacher being from the targeted school itself and the other two support teachers who are university graduates who obtained high scores in the final university exams. The project includes in-service training to teachers (50% females) in their role as remedial education teachers as well as mentoring/on the job training for the university graduates to enhance their teaching capacities. This modality was recommended by the MoEHE based on feedback received and the evaluation findings of remedial programmes carried-out in previous years.
