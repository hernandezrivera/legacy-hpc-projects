Project Summary

This project is designed for durable food security and livelihood support to the Rohingya and host community. Our range of activities directly meet all three sector objectives: Sector objective 1 (Ensure and sustain timely provision of life-saving food assistance for Rohingya refugees and host community.) Sector objective 2 (Promote self-reliance opportunities for Rohingya refugees and enhance the livelihoods & resilience of host communities) and sector objective 3 (Address and mitigate environmental impact caused by Rohingya refugees’ influx and support social cohesion through enhancement and restoration of natural resources)
Problem area 1: Absence of income generating opportunities for self-reliance for FDMN: The UMN populations are forced to primarily rely on humanitarian assistance for their basic needs. According to the JRP-2018, all UMN rely on food assistance from humanitarian actors. Our needs assessment found that UMN men frequently engage in informal income like selling casual labor etc. outside the camp settlements. Female UMN lack opportunity to engage in income generating activities.
Problem area 2: Lack of access to food and livelihoods for HC: Cox’s Bazar is an economically disadvantaged district in Bangladesh where the poverty rate is well above Bangladesh’s national average. The effect is that 38% of the HC population is vulnerable to food insecurity (JRP- 2018). Our needs assessment found that people see agriculture as the most reliable opportunity for income generation. But due to land scarcity, especially in Ukhiya & Teknaf, only few households (HHs) can afford land to cultivate. And the poorer households mostly rely on unskilled, casual and seasonal work. The recent influx has exacerbated existing vulnerabilities while putting enormous pressure on already scarce resources. Increased food and transportation prices and decreased economic opportunities have led to further impoverishment including diminishing purchasing power. Host communities have reported daily labour, farming and petty trade as the three most common sources of income. However, due to competition on the labor market, especially casual/ daily labor, they also complain of decreasing wages. Large-scale pollution and deforestation as a result of expanding campsites have put pressure on cultivable land, meadows and other natural sources surrounding the HC.
Problem area 3: Protection issues and increased vulnerability, including GBV: High levels of economic vulnerability, both in the Rohingya Refugees and HC communities are leading to negative coping strategies to obtain food and other life-sustaining goods. Borrowing money and buying food on credit are the most commonly used coping strategies, as well as consuming less nutritious food. Other negative coping methods could include; selling of assets, sex-trafficking, and child marriage. These negative coping methods, pose a threat to the protection of women within the camps and could contribute to gender based violence (GBV) - which already is a big issue for the FDMN in the congested, makeshift settlements, as well as for women and girls in the HC.
Problem area 4: Land scarcity and environmental degradation: The rapid expansion of the camp settlements have encroached areas of cultivable land, meadows and forest. Moreover, waste (both liquid and solid) generated from the camps is posing a large environmental and public health hazard to the UMN in camps and HCs living in close proximity. During our needs assessment, people from the HC mentioned that some of their cattle had died due to the consumption of plastics from the camp. As of October 2017, approximately 2,500 acres of forest had been cut for firewood collection and building of shelters. As the rapid deforestation eventually leads to land degradation, the already vulnerable coastal environment will be further subjected to environmental hazards, including extreme events of landslides and flooding in the long run.

Activities or outputs

ICCO proposes activities that cover 3 intervention areas1. Graduating Ultra-poor Women Out of Poverty in Host Communities and Vocational and Technical Training for UMN Employment Opportunities: The major activities are- Community engagement (participatory needs assessment (PNA) & participatory action plan development (PAPD) sessions with beneficiary from FDMN and host community- Skills development including vocational education based on PNA - Livelihood assets distribution as per PNA and PAPD- Cash for Work, etc (monthly cash stipends to (host community) to maintain IGA, Cash for training for FDMN).- Life-skills education and awareness building§ Business and technical skills development§ Income Generating Asset & Components distribution§ Support for food manufacturing IGAs§ Life-skills education and awareness building§ Product promotion and marketing support2. Community Engagement for Social Cohesion: We engage with existing community based committees to build their capacity to better advocate for their community. This capacity building includes technical skill capacity building, business skill capacity training, arranging monthly meetings, developing inter reliant business opportunities that includes members of both the host and rohingya community (for example solar grids managed and covering both communities.3. Immediate Complimentary food assistance through voucher modality: Food Security Sector interventions on dietary diversity of food distribution through tried and tested complimentary food distribution through a voucher modality. Beneficiaries receive a monthly amount of tokens which can be used in allocated shops within the camps. We will scale up what has been a successful project in 2018, to reach more beneficiaries in the first half of 2019 before WFP food assistance through e-voucher modality is rolled out.
