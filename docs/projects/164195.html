Project Summary

The 9 year of conﬂict in Syria continues to have a devastating impact on every child. According to UN OCHA, more than 13.1 million people, including 5 million children, need urgent life-saving humanitarian assistance in 2020. An estimated 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services. The intervention is designed according to the CP needs identiﬁed during Rescate team ﬁeld visits and assessments, in Rural Damascus and Aleppo, coupled with HRP identified needs.
The hereby action aims at providing life-saving and life-protecting assistance to vulnerable Syrians in EG and Aleppo, responding to acute needs in terms of CP. Both targeted areas have been identiﬁed as relevant for intervention, considering Rescate operational capacities, the needs identiﬁed, and the potentialities for new emergency needs, such as population displacement and returns.
According to the 2020HNO, 5M children, 1.3M (0-4) and 3.7M (5-17) are in need of Protection (51% boys and 49% girls)
The HNO mentions that Aleppo and EG are the locations with more people in acute need (1,01M and 0,98M respectively) of all Syria. In Aleppo, 45% of the people in acute need are children in need of protection, while in EG this figure represents the 36%. These locations are also identified as target areas of high severity of needs by the HRP. Because of the grade of severity 3 and 4, Rescate has chosen these locations.
In response to the Protection SO1: The protection of population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers.
Accordingly, the project aims to protect children and parents from the risks to which they are exposed by providing CP services. Rescate will work with its local Partner SSSD in 5 centres in Aleppo and EG. These centres will act as safe spaces for children, providing specialized and professional CP services while coordinating and complementing its efforts with other centres for referral services. The staff from the Centers and MoE will be trained on CP, PSS, PSEA, Do No Harm, and GBV. The centres, besides offering a safe zone for children, will also engage the community through child protection forums, managed by a local committee created as part of the project. This platform will encourage the community members to identify the most significant child protection concerns affecting the community, as could be the impact of tension between IDPs and host communities, sexual abuse, parental neglect and other vulnerabilities.
Awareness sessions will be carried on child protection and children rights.
The activities are mainly focused on CP services, and it will support children education through informal and remedial courses to bridge the gap for them to return to education. Food baskets for families whose children attend regularly the courses (to compensate the potential loss of income as the child would stop working) will be distributed in coordination with WFP programs.
Besides the reasons given above for the location selection, Rescate has successfully implemented in Aleppo a diversity of projects funded by different donors (OCHA, UNDP, WHO), thus; Rescate work in Aleppo is well known and accepted. Whereas Rescate partnership with SSSD, who also has a strong presence in Aleppo and EG, will add value to the project in both areas. This partnership will allow to build on the expertise of both organisations, especially that Rescate has experience providing effective educational programming for “at-risk” children who are in the age of school (6-17 years) in Lebanon and Jordan including CP and PSS services to children and parents.

4 - Needs

The substance of the needs to which the action aims at responding have been assessed via field visits, visual observation & interviews held with different stakeholders. For EG, Rescate has carried out needs assessment through interviews and info gathering, from which a list of potential interventions has been drawn. This info has been reﬁned with the ﬁeld visits that followed which allowed verifying the collected info as well as going into more details; school enrollment ﬁgures. Needs are dire due to limited humanitarian response capacities and funding. Based on FGD in Aleppo & EG with the population and interviews of vulnerable people, Rescate identiﬁed children as particularly exposed to the effect of conﬂict and displacement. The main needs are related to children at high risk of being out-of-school, to suffer from violence, abuse, to be forced to live on the street, with an important ratio of child labor. Women and girls are at risk and signiﬁcantly vulnerable especially when FHH, conﬂict affected people are suffering from limited access to assistance and basic services. This situation is worsened by the inappropriate response to individual cases in line with case management guidelines which can lead to further harm to the child, due to lack of trained staff working with children. The limited knowledge among stakeholder responsible to make key decisions regarding children protection. Case management training and coaching of partners will be delivered by professionals with special emphasis in complying with do not harm principle. Assess impact of the trainings introducing appropriate methodology and base lines. Ensure training programs are designed to assess impact of the training. Community structures are in need of support to be able to perform their protection functions; therefore, this action will create and support a community-based team to maintain the actions taken and support to sustain the results obtained at the end of the action.

5 - Implementation of activities

The objective of the intervention is to provide life-saving and life-protecting assistance to vulnerable Syrians in EG & Aleppo, responding to acute needs in terms of CP SO5: Increased and more equitable access for boys & girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the CP Minimum HA StandardsThis objective will be reached through 2 Results: 1) 5 children safe spaces are active in EG & Aleppo; 2) Provided child protection services, informal and remedial education courses.R1 will be achieved by the activities: 1.1 Establish 5 Child friendly spaces; 1.2 Conduct minor needed rehab in the safe spaces; 1.3 Training for centres and MoE staff on CP services & Do No Harm & PSS; 1.4 Training on PSEA & GBV to centers staff & MoE; 1.5 CP services Mapping in other centres for coordination. 1.6 Creation of a community committee, including women leaders, to identify & respond to protection risks & implement appropriate responses; 1.7 Provide informal & remedial courses for children; 1.8 Link with other capacity building initiatives to ensure synergyR2 will be achieved by the activities: 2.1 Provide specialized Children Protection services including PSS, PSEA, & GBV; 2.2 Case management & referrals to other centres in case of need 2.3 Child protection forums with the community & committee; 2.4 Awareness sessions on CP, children rights, PSEA; & GBV The modality of implementation for the PSS & PSEA will be via SSSD specialists. The segregation of activities will be as follows: Rescate coordinates the project & holds the communications with the donor & sector. Due to its expertise, coupled with the partners’ experience in the same area of intervention, Rescate will implement the trainings on protection services & conduct the rehabilitation works in the centres (if needed). SSSD will manage the centres & provide CP services, including PSS, case management and referrals, awareness raising & lead the CP community forums.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

An identified risk is the potential of misunderstanding with the community in rural areas where no protection services exist. If the population doesn't understand the purpose and goal of the activities proposed, it might create a negative effect and prevent the quality of the implementation of the project. Therefore, the mitigation measure will be to communicate in regular basis with the community to explain the project and activities, and also to set a complain mechanism available to all the community. An assumption is that the Ministry of Education and the community is interested on learning about child protection and fulfilling children’s rights. As a measure to ensure this, Rescate and its partner will be in constant communication with all stakeholders for them not only to understand the project activities but to actively participate on them such as in trainings and awareness raising sessions. Another assumption is that the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour will grant the approvals needed to Rescate perform the project. This assumption is well funded since the project also responds to MOSAL and MOE priorities. The targeted areas, besides having high severity needs as identified by the HRP, are also nominated by stakeholders, local communities, MoE, and MOSAL.
