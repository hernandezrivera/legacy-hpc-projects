Project Summary

Sekota woreda is one of the woredas affected by different recurrent emergencies including conflict and droughts in Wag Himra zone of Amhara Region. About 34,000 children are affected by the conflicts and drought in the woreda. There are also 2060 Teachers impacted due to the damage and loss of 61 schools in the woreda. Most important infrastructures including Schools were destroyed, damaged, and overcrowded host communities’ facilities. This project is intended to address the key education priority bottlenecks that children in Sekota and Sekota Zuria woredas of Wag Himra zone of Amhara Region are facing to go back to their learning. This includes the distance children are going to get to school, shortage of scholastic materials, and short-term hunger of students. The project will give attention to providing capacity building to teachers and local institutions on education management, basic pedagogy, and MPHSS. Due attention will be given to the cross-cutting issues of gender, PSEA, and AAP. The project is proposing activities that are designed to address the needs of learners, teachers, and parents, as detailed below a list of interventions:
 Construct temporary learning spaces (One Block consist of two classrooms with semi-permanent structure)
 Provide children with emergency school feeding support( high energy Biscuit)
 Support school hygiene sanitation materials for COVID-19 Prevention
 Conduct Back to school campaigns
 Provide school furniture 3 Temporary learning spaces
 Provision/distribution of the learning Supplies (stationary)
 Conduct Training on mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS)
 Production and distribution of age and gender-sensitive IEC materials on how to report sexual exploitation and abuse and how to access survivor-centered assistance.
 Training for teachers and school supervisors on child safeguarding/PSEA and safe learning environment
 Establish feedback mechanism in target schools through capacity building of school management committees on Accountability and feedback.
After implementing these activities, the two woredas will be able to provide access to safe, protection, and conducive learning for 8000 children (50%) are girls. The Project will also strengthen the capacity of the local offices and schools to manage and plan ahead of the educational needs of the children affected by the emergencies.

1. - Projects activities

The project aims to Ensure equitable access to quality safe, protection, and conducive learning environment. The response provides assistance to the educational needs of IDP and Out of school children affected by the conflicts in Sekota woreda of Amhara Region. The project will also increase the capacity of the local institutions and communities to provide crisis-sensitive education in emergencies for crisis-affected girls, boys, and children with disabilities. The project will ensure that children living in the IDP camps continue or resume their learning. The project will establish/expand learning spaces by constructing semi-temporary learning classrooms, provision of emergency school feeding, providing scholastic materials, back-to-school campaigns, and psycho-social support for teachers. The Project will also strengthen the capacity of the Regional and Zonal Education Bureau to provide access to quality and relevant education to children living in the emergency setting. This Proposal will also support the objective of localization of aid as this project will further strengthen the capacity of CoC as a national NGO to respond to the immediate needs and access to funds. The major activities are 1. Provide school hygiene sanitation materials for schools on COVID-19 Prevention for 4100 conflict affected children2. Conduct 4 Back to school campaigns3. Provide school furniture for 3 temporary learning spaces (TLS)4. Provision/distribution of the learning Supplies (stationary) for 4100 learners5. Provide Training on mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) for 40 teachers6. Training for 100 teachers and school supervisors on creation of child safeguarding and safe learning environment (Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender and accountability of affected people (AAP).7. Provide Training for 70 Community Members and Establish feedback mechanism in target schools through capacity building of school management committees on Accountability and feedback.8. Production and distribution of 2000 age and gender- sensitive IEC materials on how to report sexual exploitation and abuse and how to access survivor-centered assistance.9. Training on the basic pedagogical skills for 80 teachers10. Training of 49 PTSA on their role and responsibilities11. CoC is working in collaboration and coordination with different humanitarian organizations and Amhara region education offices in order to avoid duplication of limited resources in the project implementation areas. Effective coordination mechanisms exist at Amhara regional level and at national level to utilize the resources effectively. CoC is actively participating in the national and regional cluster meetings as well as in the recently started zonal coordination meetings. CoC ensured participation of community groups, authorities and other actors at local administrative and traditional levels. Being a national NGO, and after completion of two emergency Reponses in Sekota woredas recently, CoC are well acquainted with the local culture, customs and rules.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Targeted communities will be informed of their rights and entitlements, will have access to information, and will be consulted on project decisions. Regular quality assurance, effective and accessible complaint handling mechanisms, and monitoring field visits that address cross-cutting concerns and identify gaps to fill with the capacity building will all help to ensure this. A series of community consultations will be held with women, men, boys and girls, the elderly, and persons with special needs. KAP and PDM will be conducted at the beginning, end of the project, and after distribution to ensure the quality of the project and obtain feedback from beneficiaries, which will be addressed appropriately throughout the implementation, with the goal of ensuring the satisfaction of beneficiaries including women and men, girls and boys, and people with disabilities. The results of these surveys will be documented and used to improve future projects. In addition, all sites will have complaint handling mechanisms in place, such as a complaint box and community reference committee. All target communities will be sensitized about the complaint handling process and how to launch complaint-step by step and will be encouraged to file their complaints. During monitoring visits, discussions will be held with communities to seek their feedback and input from them regarding the project implementation.
