Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Somalia, compounded by COVID-19, floods and droughts, has resulted in massive challenges in meeting the WASH needs of the Somali population. According to the JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by poor WASH conditions. For 2021, it is estimated that 4.6 million people remain in need of humanitarian WASH assistance in Somalia in 2021.
According to the JMCNA 2020, 41 per cent of Somalis do not have access to a regular and stable improved water source for drinking, while over 30 per cent walk more than 15 minutes to reach their main drinking water source196. Due to these constraints, one in five Somalis do not have enough water per day to cover their basic needs for both for drinking and domestic use.
As of September, a prolonged water shortage conditions, which have persisted since mid-June 2021, continue to impact in many parts of Somalia and exposing a higher number of vulnerable people to significant water shortage risks. The month of August was particularly not good for most drought prone areas of southern Somalia with Jubaland being the hardest hit state. Currently, the water shortages conditions are severely alarming and deteriorating in Afmadow district. The pastoral population living in the hard-to-reach areas are the worst affected. Poor performance of short rains (Oct-Dec) caused water shortages in Sool, Sanaag and South-Central Somalia causing displacement of pastoral communities. Of the people in need and highly vulnerable, a large percentage are children, women and the elderly, who critically lack access to clean water and are acutely at risk of gender-related violence and high morbidity and mortality due to water-borne disease outbreaks. Many households lack access to adequate sanitation leading to frequent open defecation while safety of people, particularly women and children to access latrines and water points remains a serious concern and a serious risk for exposure to violence.
ACTED will therefore respond to these augmented risks and crisis through a WASH and market-based intervention in Bay, Gedo, Lower jubba and Sool region. Through this intervention, ACTED will reach a total of 12,863 households (77,180 individuals) in the proposed area of intervention. ACTED intends to upgrade five shallow well with mini solar lights in Kismayo district.
ACTED will construct 130 twin flood-resistant latrines with hand washing stations in Baidoa, Caynabo and Garbaharey district. 30% of the total target latrines to be constructed will be completed through the market-based scheme in seeking to enhance WASH market systems and increase the purchasing power of the affected population. In addition to this ACTED will construct 100 single brick latrine construction with solar light, handwashing facility in Ainabo region while 150 single brick latrines will be constructed in Baidoa, Afmadow and Garbaharey districts.
The proposed latrines for persons with disability include special features including access ramps instead of stairs, with the latrine having hand rails and thick plain concrete around the latrine pit. Furthermore, with proposed construction of the hand washing stations, for persons with disability, the hand washing stations will be lower than the normal ones for easy access.
Regarding hygiene, ACTED intends to include hygiene promotion sessions and hygiene kit distribution through a voucher system. The full suite of activities aims to meet both the acute and long-term needs of the vulnerable IDP communities, whilst establishing relationships with the target communities for continued maintenance and operation of these assets.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1 water point construction and rehabilitation ACTED will upgrade 5 Boreholes with pipeline extensions, hybrid solar installations and construction of elevated water tanks in Baidao, Garbeharey, Afmadow, Ainabo, Xudun districts These boreholes accustomed to heavy consumption by the residents and water trucking during drought seasons. Through this intervention, ACTED will reach an additional 4,167 HHs (25000 individuals) in the target districts who have remained water stressed due to the prolonged drought. ACTED will also upgrade five shallow wells with mini-solar water pump installation, fencing, construction of water tanks and pipeline extensions to IDPs in Diinsoor, Baidoa and Kismayo districts and also construction of one shallow well with mini-solar water pump installation, fencing, construction of elevated water tanks, pipeline extensions in riverine areas in Kismayo district. Through this intervention, ACTED will reach a total 1750 HHs (10500 individuals) in the target districts. Activity 2.1 Latrine constructionACTED will construct 130 twin flood resistant latrine construction with solar light, handwashing facility in Baidoa, Caynabo and Garbaharey to benefit 2080 HHs (12480 individuals) and will be served with the vulnerable HHs with no access to human excrete management facilities. The Latrines will be built as double -door latrines and as per WASH cluster standards, 30% of the latrines will be constructed via Market based approach.In addition to this ACTED will construct 100 single brick latrine construction with solar light, handwashing facility in Ainabo district and another 150 single brick latrines in Baidoa, Afmadow and Garbaharey districts. The 250 single brick latrines will benefit a total of 2000 HHs (1200 individuals) in the target districts. The proposed latrines for persons with disability include special features including access ramps instead of stairs, with the latrine having hand rails and thick plain concrete around the latrine pit. Furthermore, with proposed construction of the hand washing stations, for persons with disability, the hand washing stations will be lower than the normal ones for easy access. Activity 3.1 Hygiene kits distribution ACTED will distribute hygiene kits through the voucher system to 3700 HHs (22000 individuals) in these areas. The voucher system provides beneficiaries the ability to access the hygiene items at the local market and promotes the supply of WASH NFI items to the local market to provide the opportunity for income generation boosting the economy. Activity 3.2 Community Hygiene promotionCommunity Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) will be selected in all the areas of interventions by the communities with support from Village Relief Committees and project staff among willing and available local residents. A total of 100 CHPs and one community mobilizer will be selected to attend a 3-day training delivered by ACTED Senior-WASH officer. ACTED will train CHPs on and disseminate key messages on critical topics, including: Water treatment using aqua tabs, Safe water supply chain from collection to storage, cleaning of storage containers, Solid waste management practices, Critical handwashing times, Separate menstrual hygiene management (MHM) conducted only by female CHPs. A door-to-door hygiene promotion sessions will then be conducted over period of five months. ACTED aims to reach a total of 1000 HHs (6000 Individuals) each month through the sessions.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

ACTED and implementing partners will prioritize cash assistance to provide the beneficiaries with the much-needed purchasing power, enabling them to respond to their basic needs while offering flexibility and diversity of procurement at household level. Cash preserves the dignity of the households and reserves their right to purchase items according to their needs.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

In the designing of the project, ACTED carried out a market analysis in theproject implementation areas. Prior to project kick-off, all markets werewarranted as functional and the local inflation and market dynamics analysedin order to provide beneficiaries with adequate amounts according to theprevailing conditions. In the unlikely event of an nation-wide inflation, ACTEDwould revise the amount accordingly after due consultation with the donor.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Due to Somalia’s fragile and unstable security situation, ACTED closely monitors the security dynamics liaising with the local communities including traditional elders, local authorities and regional state government representatives. Over the years ACTED has implemented a strategy of maintaining a low profile to protect, the staff and beneficiaries. Working closely with targeted communities, engagement with local partners and prioritizing community sensitization and participation, ACTED and partners build acceptance of activities from the bottom up. ACTED has a thorough Somalia Safety and Security Guidelines Manual in place to ensure the wellbeing of employees on mission. This security guide includes contingency planning depending on the gauged local security level which can fluctuate depending on the effects of political or local turmoil. Evacuation, kidnapping crisis management, stress-handling plans are also in place in the event of either general outbreaks or individual cases of violence, disease or general turmoil. A communication and movement strategy is in place, with a combination of communication means in all offices and for all movements in urban and rural areas. ACTED procurement, financial and logistic policies require a clear and transparent process for validation of suppliers. ACTED reviews selection of suppliers with field teams to maintain a distribution of resources across clan/sub-clan groups within areas of operation. ACTED ensures identification of beneficiaries and allocation of assistance are impartial and transparent, and offers mechanisms to appeal decisions to VRCs and through the complaint response mechanism. All E-transfer agents are verified against US OFAC Office of Foreign Asset Control list, EU proscribed Lists and UN sanctions lists.
