Project Summary

HALO with its implementing partners will deliver a comprehensive approach to Mine Action in NW Syria, involving Contamination Impact Survey (CIS), an enhanced version of CIS (ECIS), Risk Education (RE), Victim Data Collection (VDC) and Victim Assistance (VA). Note: the key difference between ECIS and CIS is the identification, marking and reporting of ERW items. HALO will also seek to extend the scope of its activities to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Disposal.
To better understand the threat that ERW poses, HALO will conduct systematic CIS in 111 communities and ECIS in 692 communities across Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Under this activity, teams will visit communities, with the overall goal to identify, mark and report ERW contamination and to record its impact on the community. Data will be used to identify areas of highest priority for RE, create contamination maps, and provide essential information for future ERW clearance. The scale of this activity is unprecedented but is required to inform current and future ERW disposal/clearance, as well as immediately improving the awareness and safety of affected communities. ECIS activities will be coordinated from HALO’s operation room in Jordan, staffed by technical personnel. If the relevant authorizations are granted from the Turkish government, HALO will also establish ERW survey and disposal teams trained to complete surface-located ERW tasks (excluding IEDs) via a 6-week course derived from International Mine Action Standards (IMAS). They will be deployed under the direct control of HALO’s operations room in Jordan. At a later stage in 2019, they will complete advanced training relevant to the detection and disposal of sub-surface ERW (excluding IEDs). Extension to IED disposal requires a separate 4-week course. All disposal activities will be conducted via the use of thermite torches (T-Jets). No high explosives will be used. HALO’s ERW survey and disposal objectives for NW Syria are based on its experience in southern Syria in 2018.
HALO will provide RE to 108,472 beneficiaries in 2019 (18,364 men, 17,692 women, 38,248 boys and 34,168 girls). A particular focus will be placed on those who are most at risk of accident (including children, young men and IDPs). Two new RE ‘Training of Trainer’ (ToT) Trainers will build on the work already underway by HALO’s existing RE ToT Trainer in NW Syria. Together, they will support NGOs currently delivering RE to vulnerable communities and IDPs, as well as NGOs willing to deliver RE along with other activities; improving the relevance, integration and reach of RE messaging across all relevant sectors.
HALO will also collect information about ERW accidents and victims (survivors) through VDC which will be used to develop a better understanding of how and why accidents are occurring, enabling HALO, and other actors, to better tailor RE and VA activities.
In 2019, HALO and one of its implementing partners will implement a new VA initiative, where approx. 200 survivors of ERW accidents will benefit from initial basic health screening, referrals to reputable service providers and a one-off distribution of basic medical and hygiene items. The project will also support survivors (approx. 1,200) through the establishment of a rehabilitation centre and 2 x mobile outreach teams, providing physiotherapy, mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS), peer support counselling, and referrals to a prosthetics centre. A social protection scheme providing ‘in-kind’ support to selected survivors will also be implemented. Additionally, in order to promote workplace inclusion, 5 survivors will be recruited, trained and employed by HALO’s partner.

Needs

For more than 8 years, the Syrian crisis has resulted in the widespread contamination of ERW and the associated threat of harm to vulnerable communities and IDPs. More than 10 million people (about half of the population) are at risk of ERW in Syria, with close to 2,000 communities reporting contamination and explosive incidents (draft 2019 HNO). As a general guide, 10% of all explosive ordnance (e.g. aircraft bombs, missiles, rockets, artillery projectiles, mortars, grenades) fail to function as designed and remain in a dangerous ‘armed’ unexploded state, contaminating urban and rural areas and posing a threat to local communities. The malfunction percentage for cluster munitions is as high as 30%. ERW contamination also includes IEDs and land mines. The presence of ERW in NW Syria inhibits livelihoods, restricts access to land, basic services, humanitarian aid and causes injury or death. The true extent of ERW contamination in NW Syria is not known making it difficult to prioritise and tailor RE to suit affected communities ‘requirements in order to better promote safe behavior. It is not possible to effectively inform near term ERW disposal priorities, longer-term ERW clearance objectives, and resource requirements. A need exists to improve the quality, scope and consolidated relevance of CIS. While survey and marking, followed by clearance, will remove ERW threats to human safety, providing awareness to conflict affected populations on the risks relating to ERW is essential to reduce those risks and prevent further accidents (2019 HNO). UNMAS advised that over 90% of ERW victims/survivors in Syria had not received RE prior to their accident. UNMAS also indicates less than 50% of the population in Aleppo has received RE, and less than 12% in Idleb. Continued investment in RE is therefore viewed as an essential element of Mine Action in NW Syria. With over 20 NGOs delivering RE to vulnerable communities and IDPs, a requirement exists to further improve the relevance, integration and reach of RE across relevant humanitarian sectors. Persons living with disabilities (PLWD) are disproportionately affected during situations of conflict and emergency. Aggravation of existing impairments due to environmental change, stress and limited access to services, in addition to the creation of new impairments from ongoing violence, all contribute to the burden of disability. Women and girls with disabilities are particularly vulnerable to violence, exploitation and abuse. Despite these risks, data on persons with disabilities in conflict situations remains limited. As of the end of 2017, an estimated 2.9 million people in Syria are suffering from temporary or permanent physical impairments (2018 HNO), which will cause long-term impacts beyond the individuals, on the Syrian society as whole. ERW accident survivors are in great need of physical rehabilitation, psychological and socio-economic support (2019 HNO) and are especially vulnerable to the lack of long term livelihood opportunities, social stigma and Gender Based Violence. In Idleb, only 5 centres can support the long-term needs and rehabilitation of PLWDs, and only 1 facility provides acute psychiatric support (IRC service mapping, Idleb, 2018). In June 2018, HiHFAD conducted an impact assessment on 122 beneficiaries from their War Survivor Assistance Project. A lack of transportation was considered a major constraint to accessing basic services. Additionally, less than half of the survivors received an income, 20% reported relying entirely on humanitarian assistance and 15% depended on family support. Most of the respondents (57%) indicated they could not meet their daily needs. While three quarters of the respondents were of working age, 81% neither worked nor studied.

Implementation of activities

Conduct RE for at-risk groups: RE ToT Trainers will deliver ‘face to face’ training and ongoing guidance to RE teams, based on requests for support received from relevant NGOs. HALO will use the Mine Action Sub Cluster and UNMAS’s multi-sector contact network to promote the availability of the trainers. RE teams will conduct RE sessions to 108,472 beneficiaries including children, adolescents and adults in schools, formal and informal community settings, homes, and IDP locations. Conduct survey, marking and disposal of ERW: Existing CIS teams will continue to visit communities in order to collect foundation information about ERW contamination. Existing and new ECIS teams will visit all accessible communities across NW Syria to identify, mark and report ERW contamination and to record its impact on the community. CIS/ECIS data will be used to identify areas of highest priority for RE, create contamination maps, and provide essential information for future ERW clearance. HALO will share all collected data with UNMAS and any NGOs conducting ERW disposal. ECIS teams will maintain direct communication (via WhatsApp) with HALO’s operations room in Jordan, staffed by EOD technical officers, to ensure the correct identification of ERW items and the employment of safe procedures when approaching and marking ERW. Subject to access approval from the Turkish government, HALO plans to establish a new ERW survey and disposal capability which will receive survey, disposal and medical training relevant to surface-located ERW tasks (not involving metal detectors and excavation techniques, and excluding IEDs) via a 6-week course derived from IMAS. They will then be deployed under the direct control of the operations room in Jordan. After six months of operational experience, the teams will participate in four weeks of advanced training, involving the use of metal detectors and the detection and disposal of sub-surface ERW, excluding IEDs. Extension to IED disposal requires a separate 4-week course. All disposal activities will be conducted via the use of thermite torches (T-Jets). No high explosives will be used. Provide VDC/VA services in areas affected by ERW: VDC and VA initiatives will be integrated with health programming, in that during VDC activities, medically trained team members will also provide basic medical screening and one-off medical support to approx. 200 beneficiaries. To address urgent needs of ERW survivors and conflict related accidents, a new VA initiative will include the establishment of a rehabilitation center providing physiotherapy, MHPSS, and peer support counselling to 700 beneficiaries. Survivors in need of prosthetics will be referred to a prosthetics centre, with the initiative covering transportation costs and medical services for 50 cases. Two mobile support teams will provide medical support and material support to 175 beneficiaries. The VA initiative will also support the most vulnerable survivors through a social protection scheme, providing 292 beneficiaries with in-kind support. The initiative will also promote an inclusive working environment by recruiting and training 5 survivors. Conduct MEAL activities: HALO’s Field Monitoring Teams will conduct regular spot checks to validate the implementation of all projects. They will attend all activities – including trainings - and complete QA forms. KIIs and FGDs will also be conducted to assess the impact of projects. Feedback on lessons learnt and best practices will be regularly communicated to relevant project teams. Partners’ field MEAL staff will also compile monitoring reports and feedback forms separately. To ensure accountability, a leaflet including a WhatsApp number and an email address will be provided to each beneficiary from all projects. A response to each feedback/complaint will be provided within a month, urgent cases will be treated immediately.

Protection Risk Analysis

Perception of the teams as impartial and negative impact of the security situation: (likelihood: Medium, level of impact: High) Only the partners’ visibility will be added on uniforms, equipment and materials distributed to limit risks for field staff to be misidentified. The partners are well known in the areas of operation and are considered trusted organisations. Appropriate coordination will take place with the local authorities to facilitate safe access to communities. The work plan will be reviewed daily in relation to security and temporary suspension of activities will be imposed as necessary. All staff receive training on HMA and ERW recognition to mitigate the risk of injuries. Lack of sustainable and sufficient support to survivors: (likelihood: Low, level of impact: Medium) Early health intervention, referrals and promotion of self-reliance through peer support activities will increase their socio-economic participation, and reduce dependence on humanitarian assistance. The project will also contribute to building local capacities, hiring and training medical staff who will support communities in the long term. Difficulties to access girls and women: (likelihood: Medium, level of impact: High) Recruit female staff who are better positioned to reach out to women, gathering useful data on their needs and feedback on the project. HALO will ensure that recruitment standards are respected. All data collected will be age and sex disaggregated. The teams will make a concerted effort to ensure women make up half the number of interviewees. Lack of community acceptance: (likelihood: Low, level of impact: High) Lessons learned and needs assessment informed the project design including activities, project locations and selection of beneficiaries. The teams will conduct appropriate community liaison to understand the initial requirements and any changes during project implementation. Feedback will be gathered through MEAL activities and accountability mechanisms. Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse during activities: (likelihood: Low, level of impact: High): As part of their induction, all project staff are trained in PSEA, gender mainstreaming and the principles of 'Do No Harm'. Activities are designed taking into consideration the needs of men, women, boys and girls. RE sessions are conducted in schools, households and community spaces to reach all beneficiaries and guarantee their safety. A leaflet including a WhatsApp number and an email address will be provided to each beneficiary. Overconfidence of the beneficiaries to the threat of ERW following RE sessions: (likelihood: Medium, level of impact: High) The teams will conduct pre and post KAP surveys during each RE session to measure increased safe behavior and improved knowledge regarding RE messaging. HALO and its partners will each independently monitor RE sessions, including regular spot checks, followed by a monitoring report including comments and recommendations for each activity. HALO will also hold FGDs and community KIIs to receive feedback and recommendations on the activities conducted. Injury to team members &/or local community members during the conduct of survey and disposal tasks: (likelihood: Medium, level of impact: High) Team members will be trained directly by highly experienced EOD Technical Officers, with particular attention paid to individual member safety, collective team safety and safety of local populations during the conduct of tasks. Team members will receive comprehensive medical training from a specialist including first aid for traumatic blast/fragmentation injuries. Teams will deploy to task locations with suitable mobile phone communications (including internet access) and information on the nearest medical facilities in the event of injury. Teams will be under the direct control of the operations room to ensure tasks are properly planned and executed, including all necessary safety precautions.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security situation deteriorates: HALO and its partners have established and will maintain threat contingency plans and SOPs for local use. Field supervisors, informed by field security staff, can adjust the daily/weekly work plan and avoid areas of elevated threat. All teams deploy with mobile phone communications and a first aid kit, knowing where the nearest medical facilities are located. Partial or total suspension of the activities (including fixed facilities and mobile teams) will be temporarily imposed if necessary. If activities cannot be resumed, HALO and its partners will assess the possibility to operate in other areas. In collaboration with the Health cluster/Health directorate, the partner would hand over the provision of health services to an adequate alternative entity, advocating for the retention of qualified field staff. Weather: Likely restrictions encountered during colder/wetter months will be taken into account and the use of more permissible seasons will be maximized to get ahead of targets, where possible. Inability to recruit or retain staff: A wide range of candidates will be interviewed and competitive salaries and contract duration will be offered. Inability to properly control or confirm the quality of training: Ensure training locations facilitate the best possible outcomes (including internet connectivity for any remote training). HALO’s senior RE ToT Trainer will undertake QA on RE ToT training. Health experts will conduct face-to-face training to VDC/VA staff. Any technical training (including ECIS, and ERW survey and disposal) will be delivered by experienced HALO staff. ECIS training can be delivered remotely but there is currently no scope to deliver ERW survey and disposal training remotely due to its technical and complex nature. All ERW survey and disposal team members will be individually assessed and certified as competent for operational employment. Any member who fails to satisfy competency requirements will not be employed. HALO’s Field Monitoring Teams and partners’ field MEAL staff will conduct selective QA on training relating to all projects. Funds are misappropriated: HALO Syria finance staff will conduct monthly scrutiny of all financial transactions undertaken by implementing partners to ensure strict compliance with approved project budgets. HALO Syria staff will also make regular visits to Gaziantep to engage directly with finance staff from implementing partners. Financial, procurement and HR policies, including audits, whistleblowing will be appropriately applied.
