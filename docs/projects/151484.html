Project Summary

Up to 5.4 million people are currently in need of emergency health services. Insecurity, displacement and limited WASH interventions, amongst other factors, have complicated the health crisis in Somalia. There is an urgent need to continue the prevention, detection and response to water- and vector-borne diseases, including cholera and malaria. (Somalia Humanitarian Dashboard September 2018)
In response to this, READO intends to improve access to essential life-saving health services for crisis-affected and host populations aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality. This will be done through Primary health care services, consultations, measles case management, immunization campaign, emergency preparedness and response capacities, awareness and mobilizations campaigns.

Needs

More than 5.7 million people require basic health services, including critical needs in maternal and child health, as one in eight Somali children die before the age of five. Disease outbreaks such as AWD/cholera and measles continue to represent a major threat to children with 7,368 suspected measles cases (73 per cent being children under-5) and 5,931 suspected cases of AWD/cholera with 41 deaths reported in 2018. (UNICEF Situation Report August 2018)In Baidoa Bay region of Somalia, low measles immunization and vitamin A supplementation status were reported among IDPs - Measles Vaccination <50% of children 6-59 months -34.9% (FSNAU July 2018). In Baidoa was 369 measles reported. Cumulatively, 9,206 cases of Malaria cases have been reported across Somalia since the beginning of 2018 and Baidoa is one of the most affected districts with 1,762 cases reported (Early Warning Disease Surveillance and Response Bulletin October2018). In regards to Bloody Diarrhea BD, Baidoa was placed as one of the most affected districts 504 cases (Early Warning Disease Surveillance and Response Bulletin October2018). Baidoa IDPs recorded GAM and SAM prevalence of 17.7 and 3.6 respectively indicating critical levels of nutrition situation. Improvement of Crude Death Rate (CDR) and Under-Five Death Rate (U5DR) were reported (0.58/10 000/day and 0.75/10 000/day respectively) when compared to Gu 2017 (>1/10 000/day and >2/10 000/day, respectively). This sustained critical malnutrition mainly is attributed to high morbidity and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreak (FSNAU July 2018)Even though mortality rates reported during Gu 2018 assessment are slightly higher compared to Deyr 2017, most of the already malnourished children are susceptible to diseases. Therefore, nutrition and health support interventions in these areas should be complemented with sustained efforts to reduce morbidity by educating households on proper care and hygiene practices and improving health seeking behaviour.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In regards to health, READO will train community health workers who will, in turn, transfer the knowledge to the beneficiaries even after the project comes to an end. The transfer process will create a strong community in the long-term, consisting of people with appropriate basic knowledge and skills necessary for improving the health status and enhancing basic education facilities and effective maintenance of all community facilities. Furthermore, in all its activities READO will employ a bottom-up approach which allows for community ownership of the project and in the long-run the sustainability of the project.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Furthermore, READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. READO, will ensure that the lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. In addition, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which included who has access to health care, who manages facilities, who is able to influence decisions on the provision of health services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for health interventions.In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will provide the local/host community with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. In addition, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.Flood or other emergencies: Target communities affected by flooding, AWD, influx or IDPs or forced IDP evictions etc., unforeseen by EWS. To mitigate this, READO will scale-up the beneficiaries targeted with health interventions, to provide emergency assistance to those worst affected.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. This will be done through introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities eg. Mobile health services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks). In addition, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for health center location, design and services. Moreover, READO will engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. READO will also have have a proportionate number of female health staff. READO will also establish complaints and response mechanism. READO will use information boards to display Complaint Response Mechanism phone number during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection and at health centers. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. In turn, the call center will be in charge of calling beneficiaries as part of the monitoring system.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. READO will also advocate for install lights outside and on the access route to the facility with the government of South West State Somalia. In addition, READO will ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private. This will be done by establishing an information sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him to further trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishes concerning the care provided. READO will not share information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). READO will also be aware of how power dynamics between a health staff and the patient can affect the patient’s response and attitude towards the staff/ organization and service provided.
