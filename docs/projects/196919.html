Project Summary

This intervention takes a participatory, community-based approach to recovery programming, ensuring market-sensitive livelihoods is provided to vulnerable Syrian households. Increased income, assets and consumption hold the key to promoting an increase in individual economic well-being. Social well-being stems from human capital and positive social relations. The vocational training and on-the-job training or apprenticeships are provided to persons with disabilities, youth and women, who will be offered a selection of enterprise and employment pathways. For those seeking to set up their own small/micro enterprise, a further package of business skills training and business grant will be provided based on their business proposition. Moreover, vocational training graduates are supported to seek employment with small-medium businesses. All skills trainings are identified based on a rigorous assessment of labor market to ensure addressing local sectors of economic growth, gaps in skills, barriers facing persons with disabilities accessing livelihood opportunities and women’s role in the market. Small and micro-business owners whose business has been negatively affected by conflict are supported to relaunch their businesses through a combination of skills, business grants and coaching. Business trainings are provided, particularly for those new to labor market such as youth, women & persons with disabilities. Moreover, Cash for Work schemes are identified to provide benefit to the entire community. Community acceptance for targeted livelihoods activities is ensured through community participation in selection of types of businesses needed in community, beneficiary selection and monitoring project progress. Whether directly or through the potential partnership with other livelihood actors, the Inclusion of persons with disabilities mainly women with disabilities will be ensured through using twin track approach, the logic of intervention will combine a package of activities aimed at empowering persons with disabilities and building more inclusive and accessible working environment at community level. Activities will be based on assessment of barriers faced by persons with disabilities. While the organization will conduct several actions to overcome barriers such as awareness raising on persons with disabilities rights, tailored case management support, and intensive coaching support. Participation of women will be supported through awareness and advocacy with their families, provision of female trainers and skills training for home-based businesses.
Complementary services such as Psychosocial Support (PSS) will be provided based on community needs & vulnerability assessments. The organization provides technical support to other livelihoods agencies to ensure the design and implementation take an inclusive approach and that they provide persons with disabilities with equitable access. This support also includes guidance on the meaningful engagement of older persons and women in activities.

3 - Needs

After 11 years of conflict, food security and livelihood need for many families, further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity estimated 13.9 million, 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 2 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure. FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023 (HNO 2023). Early Recovery and Livelihoods needs continue to rise, resulting increase in the cost of living, including, reduction of subsidies, and general prices increases as well as volatility in the exchange rate, among others, are projecting onto an additional deterioration in terms of income and purchasing power across all population groups. The sector foresees the income/ expenditure gap to continue increasing in the coming year across all population groups (HNO 2023). From DGA perspective, key concerns from surveyed persons. were i)community attitude and cultural behaviors limiting PWDs and women from involvement in Livelihoods activities; ii) accessibility barriers related to inaccessible transport, and infrastructures; iii) attitudinal, information, and communication barriers. 2022 agriculture also decreased due to rainfall decrease and led to a scarcity securing clean water for people living in NES.

4 - Implementation of activities

Cash for Work participants are the mostly vulnerable households including at least 25% of women and 15% of people with functional limitations. After discussion with the community, activities may include infrastructure rehabilitation projects such water infrastructure and accessibility of marketplaces. Labour Market Assessments (LMAs) will be conducted in selected communities to establish local economic growth, new opportunities, and skills in demand. This will feed into a response options analysis for livelihoods program design in line with the needs of the local labor market. LMAs will also identify barriers faced by women, men, young & older people and persons with disabilities in accessing employment and make recommendations addressing them. Provision of business skills training, grants & coaching for small & micro business recovery to reestablish businesses will be offered to eligible applicants. Provision of vocational skills, life skills and business skills training will be offered to eligible applicants. Vocational trainings (VT) will be based on the LMA findings of sectors of economic growth and skills in demand in the local market, VT will be certified where feasible and include an apprenticeship or on job training and establishing links to local employment opportunities. Young people and women will be prioritized for VT & apprenticeships, people graduated from the VT and/or apprenticeship will have the opportunity to apply for the micro-business grant scheme.
