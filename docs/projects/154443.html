Project Summary

Various forms of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) continues to be perpetrated against women and girls, in particular in the current protracted humanitarian environment, with a particular exacerbated focus on displaced women and girls and returnees. In seeking access to safe areas, women and girls are at heightened risk of sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation. Similar violations are perpetrated in camp settings, particularly where armed actors are present, and in temporary settlements which fail to provide a sufficient protective environment. In displacement, many women and girls experience high rates of physical, sexual and mental abuse, and denial of resources and opportunities. Negative coping strategies such as early marriage and survival sex have become more commonplace. The provision of GBV prevention and response activities is critical at all stages of the displacement cycle, as service gaps pose grave risks to the safety and dignity of women and girls. With the continuous movements of displaced people and returnees during 2018, it goes without saying that women and girls still face vulnerabilities related to their access to minimum services and the additional GBV risks. When the return movement is accelerated, it is important to ensure proper follow-up of open GBV cases in the return areas and to support establishing a proper protection environment for women and girls. This necessitates strengthening of existing GBV services as well as establishment of new GBV services in retaken/return areas where IDPs and vulnerable groups are concentrated. Through this project, UNFPA and its Implementing Partners will provide holistic prevention and response to women and girls, in particular survivors of GBV. By involving the whole community including men and boys, UNFPA adopts an inclusive approach that will lead to long-term changes for IDPs, Returnees and Host community members. Interventions will be in continuity of the ones in 2018, in particular in hot areas as designed through the HRP process.

Needs

GBV remains a protection concern in Iraq, particularly resulting from the fragile political situation, ongoing armed conflict and civil unrest as well as cultural dynamics. • The Iraq GBV Sub Cluster reported that GBV of all types continues to be documented. According to an assessment conducted by UNFPA in 2018 which comprised 1,000 survivors and 200 case managers, emotional assault accounted for 30% of the survivors, followed by denial of resources at 25%, domestic violence accounted for 16%, physical assault at 15%, sexual violence accounted for 4%, forced marriage 4% and rape accounted for 2% of survivors and forced prostitution 1% (Data from the in progress Survivors Assessment). There are some incidences /cases that are hardly ever reported, for example: forced/child/ early marriages, sexual exploitation, incest, and honor-related violence, including killing, due to deep-rooted cultural and economic factors, gender inequalities, power dynamics, no or inconsistent laws for protection and penalties for perpetrators, limited awareness/information on GBV among communities, including understanding of Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and reporting mechanisms. Even though the military operations completed in December 2017, GBV risks still exist due to the protracted displacement, coupled with the deteriorating economic crisis where access to livelihood opportunities for those outside of host-communities and in returnee areas is increasingly difficult, and vulnerability of women and girls in displacement has even heightened. Women, girls and children from Internally Displaced persons (IDPs), refugees, returnees and host communities, continue to experience sexual harassment, denial of resources, child /forced marriage, domestic violence and sexual exploitation and abuse during displacement and return processes. Social exclusion and stigma limit the ability of vulnerable women and girls to access adequate social protection and assistance. Support towards child and adolescent survivors is one of the gaps identified by both GBV and CP actors. Iraq has piloted Child Survivor Initiative in 2018 targeting Anbar and Salah al-Din, and this experience needs to be further rolled-out/expanded in other locations to further strengthen response towards child and adolescent survivors as it requires specific set of skills and interventions. There is limited community education to ensure prevention, risk mitigation and easy/timely access to the relevant multisectorial services irrespective of the distance, with need to strengthen implementation of referral pathways where services are available. There is also a greater need for public sensitization on GBV prevention and risk mitigation. Other critical gaps identified by GBV partners include comprehensive services for children born out of rape; protection of pregnancies and delivery of babies conceived through rape; protection of women and girls from families and relatives in terms of risk of honor killing due to sexual violence, unsafe abortion of unwanted pregnancy; engaging men and boys in GBV programming and responses; effective inclusion of the physically challenged, strengthening the participation and capacities of other sectors to mainstream gender and GBV in their respective programs among others. The displacement of populations has disrupted livelihood activities of the IDPs, leading to poverty and economic challenges, and further exposing women and girls to exploitation including higher risks of SEA. As the return process continues, there is a need to make basic social services, including protection service for women and girls, available in the areas of return. At the moment, lack of basic social services is of great concern in return areas, and this is also one of the key factors preventing return of IDPs. Protection service for women and girls in return areas is needed so that they have a safe and comfortable place to receive critical information on available services, to interact with other women and girls, and to join psychosocial and/or recreational sessions based on their needs. For those who were receiving GBV services during displacement, it is also important that they continue receiving the same services in the areas of return to ensure complete rehabilitation and healing.

Protection Risk Analysis

In order to address above-mentioned critical needs and human right violation, support to and protection of women and girls are very much needed. This project will ensure partners are supported to deliver specialized GBV prevention and response interventions to IDPs (both in camp and out-of camp locations), returnees and vulnerable host communities in a secure and safe environment. Community mobilization and engagement will be also part of the interventions for prevention, strengthening resilience and reintegration, and effective referrals.

Mitigation Measures

A thorough and regular monitoring and evaluation, not only of the interventions; but also of the security of the service providers and implementing partner staff, will be put in place to ensure a safe and secure work environment. Regarding SEA, regular refresher training will be organized.

How is your project accountable to affected population

As mentioned previously, UNFPA and its implementing partners will ensure that all members of the targeted communities are involved in the various interventions related to prevention and response activities, including recreational activities, psycho social support, case management, referrals, awareness raising, etc. and throughout the cycles of planning, implementation and monitoring.
