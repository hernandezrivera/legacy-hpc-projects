Project Summary

Within the context characterized by the deteriorated living conditions as a result of the prolonged occupation, intra division and the COVID-19 pandemic, this project aims at contributing to prevent and mitigate its consequences on the Palestinian people in the Gaza Strip. The project will deliver quality specialized mental health services to the vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys as per the following interventions:
1) Provision of specialized mental health services for children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of psychosocial distress. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multidisciplinary teams at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khan Younis. Therapy plans include psychotherapy, play therapy, medications, family counseling and home visits. Those cases will be detected and referred by the case managers and psychosocial support staff working in family centers and Child Protection and MHPSS networks using the existing referral system. Additionally, other cases will be detected and referred through structured activities implemented by GCMHP professionals and mobile teams. A professional group supervision will be provided for GCMHP professionals. Supervision will provide a safe and confidential environment for staff to reflect on and discuss their work and their personal and professional responses to their work. The supervision sessions will be provided by senior professionals in GCMHP. Prevention measures are taken into consideration at the community centers.
2) Provision of free telephone counseling service for children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centers due to economic, social or emergency settings' barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psychoeducation, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centers to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary. This service helps overcome stigma attached to mental health problems. This service provides another differentiated aspect, where all GBV and protection cases will be detected and referred to women protection organizations and other service providers in Gaza Strip.
3) Building capacities of 50 child protection workers through training them on the guidelines of mental health and psychosocial support.
4)Building the capacities of 50 protection staff on remote psychosocial support (Free telephone counselling services).
5) Building capacities of 50 protection staff (MHPSS) through training them on self-care and stress management techniques.
6) Building the capacities of 50 healthcare workers by providing them with training on MHPSS including mhGAP.
7) Conducting five online awareness and MH promotion sessions by using social media platforms.
8) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns of psychological disorders among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.
In addition, GCMHP will strengthen coordination mechanisms: GCMHP will share the project updates on OCHA's 4Ws and 5Ws online system under the protection cluster including area, gender and age disaggregated information. In addition to report on indicators to HRP periodic reporting. Also, GCMHP has already a referral mechanism in which all referrals and needed information are followed up and traced. It is worth mentioning that GCMHP is already a part of the SOPs' manual which managed by UNICEF and includes all referral pathways.

1 - Needs

According to the WHO, more than 1 billion people are currently living with a mental disorder. Also, 75% of people with mental, neurological, and substance use disorders in low-income countries do not receive treatment at all. The WHO and their partners decided to campaign this year for investing in mental health. These facets came within the current global conditions related to COVID 19 which cause heavy implications on the psychological wellbeing of the people. GCMHP is considered as the only NGO that provides specialized mental health services to the population of the Gaza strip. In the ﬁrst half of 2020, GCMHP provided specialized therapeutic services to 2,076 cases (including 173 boys, 129 girls, 968 men, and 806 women) at its three community centers. Also, it has provided psychological support including Psychological First Aid (PFA) to 354 quarantined people through its crisis teams. The PFA teams visited the quarantined people (after they return to their homes). Through these visits, GCMHP assessed 474 cases and their family members, in addition, to refer 50 cases with severe symptoms to the community centers. GCMHP scaled up its free telephone counseling service and succeeded in reaching 855 persons (including 34 boys, 43 girls, 342 men, and 436 women) who received psychoeducation, individual counseling and family counseling. The data received from clinical records of clients who received interventions at community centers showed differences between men and women in the symptoms. as 23% of men were diagnosed with depressive disorders, 16% with anxiety disorders and 17% with obsessive-compulsive disorder and psychosis with 19%. while 37% of women were diagnosed with depressive disorders, and 9% with other anxiety disorders and 15% with obsessive-compulsive disorder, psychosis with 23%, and the remaining rates distributed on other disorders. Regarding boys, 13% of them were diagnosed with Anxiety Disorders, and 4% with Depressive Disorders, and 17% OCD, and 5% with elimination disorders, and psychosis with 1%. While 14 % of the girls were diagnosed with Anxiety Disorders, and 21% with Depressive Disorders, and 15% OCD, and 1% with elimination disorders, and psychosis with 5%., and the remaining rates distributed on other disorders. GCMHP conducted a general needs assessment for MHPSS in May 2020. GCMHP surveyed 17 key organizations providing MHPSS services within the context of COVID19, and issued a fact sheet titled “Impact of COVID19 on the Mental Health Situation in the Gaza Strip”. The results showed that COVID19 pandemic has had a negative psychological impact on people in Gaza, especially those who were quarantined. The effects included a high level of anxiety, aggravated stress, stigmatization, and domestic violence. As for women, they had a heavier responsibility for household management during a crisis and that placed them under more pressure. As per children, their level of irritability has increased due their long stay at home, and therefore, being berated or physically punishedby the parents. The conjugal conflict between the parents is also reflected on the children. This has been clear through the deterioration of children’s behavioural problems. The tense atmosphere and the stress created during the parents’ conflict cause anxiety among the children. Add to this anxiety is their fear of the future. Besides, there are general and fear anxiety over infection spread in the community. Many families have shared their anxiety over their children’s school achievement as a result of schools’ closure. GCMHP observed also that lack of protectionstandards in quarantine centres has resulted in negative social impacts such as isolation, hostility, depression, and suicidal thoughts. Also, it presented factors that contributed to increasing the negative psychological impact. These included the deterioration of the economic situation of the ﬁnancially vulnerable groups, especially the daily-paid such as the cleaners, restaurant workers, and taxi drivers; the suspension of the educational process at schools and universities and the ensuing negative impact on students and their families; the closure of specialized clinics and educational organizations; and staying at home for a long time. The factsheet also included the ﬁndings of the online survey which was ﬁlled by 512 participants (45.4% women). The results showed that 87.8% of the respondents agreed that their children needed more information on the coping techniques used to deal with stress resulting from COVID-19 crisis. Moreover, 78% of whom pointed out that they constantly follow coronavirus pandemic. Pertaining the MH services, 55.8% referred that they have enough information on how to access mental health and psychosocial support services in case of a complete lockdown is enforced. The survey also showed that there was a need for knowing the ways of protection against the pestilence and the mental health and psychosocial support services which are available in the Gaza Strip.Besides, caregivers themselves need intensive supervision sessions to help them overcome their professional burdens, according to the results of the survey. On the other hand, GCMHP has conducted a webinar titled “Covid-19 Renews Needs for Mental Health: The Reality and the Challenges” in September, 2020. The webinar was attended by MoH, MoSD, MoE, UNRWA and other service providers working in the ﬁeld of MHPSS and human rights. All participants stressed the importance to provide mental health services for severe depression that contribute to an increase in the suicide cases, besides the victims of violence, the elderly, and people with disability. They also agreed on the importance of supportive supervision for caregivers and professionals who work in the frontlines to help them deal with their concerns and the stress through stress management and psychological support.

2 - Activities

The longstanding Israeli blockade, intra-Palestinian conflict, recurring hostilities, clashes, and on top of all the COVID-19 global pandemic, have placed a crippling burden on the mental health state of Gaza population and exacerbate existing protection and MHPSS needs, resulting in humanitarian needs to ensure psychosocial support interventions. This project will support the delivery of quality specialized mental health services to the women and men, girls and boys, affected by the conflict-related violence and spread pandemic in the Gaza Strip through the following: 1) Provision of specialized mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS) for children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of psychosocial distress, the clinical services will be provided by specialized multidisciplinary team at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khan Younis. Therapy plans include psychotherapy, play therapy, medication, family counseling and home visits. Those cases will be detected and referred by the case managers and psychosocial support staff working in family centers and Child Protection and MHPSS networks using the existing referral system. Additionally, other cases will be detected and referred through structured activities implemented through other venues at GCMHP community centers, mobile station and their teams. GCMHP used clinical procedures manual as a main guideline for providing the specialized mental health services. 2) Provision of free telephone counseling service for children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centers due to economic, social or emergency settings' barriers. The telephone counselors will provide psychoeducation, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centers to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary. 3) Building capacities of child protection workers through 3 training courses on the guidelines of mental health and psychosocial support. This will be provided through two days training course, for each of the three groups, each training session will be five hours per day. Prevention measures and physical distance will be taken into consideration when implementing this activity (17 trainees at max. for each). GCMHP is using the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings as a training guideline.4) Building the capacities of 50 protection staff on remote psychosocial support (Free telephone counselling services). The training will be conducted online through one session, five hours per session. The GCMHP is using its manuals for documentation, Counselling, referring and following up in addition to a specific internal training material.5) Building capacities of protection MHPSS staff through 3 training courses on self-care and stress management techniques. GCMHP is using international manuals for self-care for women and men. Prevention measures and physical distance will be taken into consideration when implementing this activity (17 trainees at max. for each). This will be provided through two days training course, for each of the three groups, each training session will be five hours per day.6) Building the capacities of healthcare workers through 3 training courses on MHPSS including mhGAP, that would contribute to integrating mental health into health facilities. Prevention measures and physical distance will be taken into consideration when implementing this activity (17 trainees at max. for each).7) Building capacities of protection MHPSS staff through 3 training courses on self-care and stress management techniques. GCMHP is using the Mental Health GAP intervention Guide v2. Prevention measures and physical distance will be taken into consideration when implementing this activity (17 trainees at max. for each). This will be provided through three days training course, for each of the three groups, each training session will be five hours per day8) Conducting five online awareness and MH promotion sessions by using social media platforms. 9) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns of psychological disorders among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.GCMHP Users: the users of GCMHP community centers are adults/parents and children who are direct or indirect victims of human rights violations, e.g. any individual has direct/indirect personal experience in the context of violence and COVID-19. The access of beneﬁciaries to the community centers is ensured through addressing any economic, physical or social limitations (eg. providing transportation for cases, conducting home visits and availability of free telephone counseling (hotline). Complaints Mechanism: GCMHP is always in place to respond to and address complaints through the following :1) Complaints boxes at community centers and Head Quarter of GCMHP. All these boxes are easily accessed by beneﬁciaries. 2) A speciﬁc part at its account page on oﬃcial website and Facebook to receive complaints and inquires, these tools are available for all audience). 3)An employee is allocated and responsible for following up these complaints if founded and sent them to the High management. 4) All issues and publications of GCMHP such as brochures, billboards, radio spots, magazines and newsletters, etc.. include Full Contact Information of GCMHP (addresses of centers, telephone numbers, mail, and Hotline number).

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

GCMHP has been committed to being accountable to the people benefited from its intervention. This has been achieved through three main mechanisms:First: targeted populations were given influence over decision making regarding the psychological services they receive and were provided with accessible and timely information in a transparent and effective manner. The responsible for reviewing and responding to the complaints are the directors of the GCMHP community centers, as they are key decision makers.Second: GCMHP actively sake feedback and complaints from the targeted communities. Complaints were received using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons and appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback and recommendations, as there are three complaint boxes at the three community centers and the HQ , and one in each mobile clinic. in addition, there is a specific side at GCMHP official website and Facebook page to receive complaints and inquiriesThe complaints recording sheet is used to gather all complaints and follow them properly.the population are encouraged to express their complaints via a promotional poster above each complaints box, so no hesitation can exist.in addition a promotional educational video has been created and broadcast via GCMHP media outlets showing the complaint's different tools and the complaints process.Third: targeted populations have a transparent access to information, and all GCMHP publications and media tools materials include all contact information of GCMHP HQ, community centers and mobile stations. Amid COVID-19 related restrictions on movement, and in order to make the complaints policy more accessible including people with disabilities, GCMHP had disseminated all its professional work numbers to the community and announce that they could be reach via calling or via remote applications. GCMHP scaled up the free hotlines to be five active lines instead of one.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

First: GCMHP will implement the project activities at its community centers with all arrangements and safe measures for the clients and staff are guaranteed, with full adherence to precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion, as defined by health authorities. In addition, all the staff are regularly monitored about upholding hygiene and prevention measures via a designated staff member. Besides, GCMHP has established clear policies and procedures for the status of emergency during the COVID-19. . In addition, the GCMHP Therapeutic services provided to clients at the community centers were continued with taking mitigation measures which include reducing the exposure through increasing the duration between the follow-up sessions for clients (from 3-5 weeks instead of 1-2 weeks), in addition to conduct home visits and deliver medications needed to clients. GCMHP also set a protocol for COVID-19 preventive measures and impose strong protection measures in the GCMHP facilities including reception offices, therapeutic rooms, etc. It also distribute sterilizers, thermometers in all GCMHP community centers. The sterilizers will be used by clients and staff according to the instructions. GCMHP also has three PFA satellite teams including a male and female in each team and these teams are providing the PFA services and distributing sanitization and educational kits among people in the most marginalized places and who probably can’t access the remote services. Second: GCMHP made adaptations such as ﬁtting ramps and railings to its facilities so that all individuals and groups can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. Third: all GCMHP facilities, policies, clinical procedures, systems, forms, templates and instructions ensure that conﬁdentiality and privacy of patients and respected all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing. On the other hands, the therapy rooms are well separated from public spaces or the waiting area. GCMHP facilities and services respect the gender needs and concerns especially the women and girls’ needs, (GCMHP services can be accessed by women and girls, as more than 50% of GCMHP beneﬁciaries are women and girls). GCMHP was and still is requesting consent to collect and use data and makes sure the beneﬁciary is clearly informed of all services and has the capacity to give informed consent. GCMHP has also a well-designed and protected MIS system for patients that secure data storage. Fifth: GCMHP has many policies such as a gender mainstreaming policy, child safety policy and security policy that make sure that there is a variety of staff (male, female) who are well trained on Gender policy. Sixth: GCMHP was and still distributing PFA toolkits/gifts to the children and medicine in the times of lockdown at home away from the public eye. Seventh: The GCMHP services including telephone counseling ensure that services are provided at an adequate time when individuals can realistically access them. Eighth: GCMHP has M&E system that considers data disaggregated by age, gender, and location or speciﬁc group (e.g. persons with disabilities).

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Given the new emergency context of COVID-19, the GCMHP adapt its activities in line with hygiene and safety measures, and considered the barriers that may prevent affected communities, including the COVID-19 crisis affected groups, from accessing your services, so for example, GCMHP tried to overcome the movement restrictions via reaching the communities via online facilities such Zoom and Skype, WhatsApp, Social media outlets and SMSs. In addition, GCMHP tried to coordinate with MoH and gets permissions to deliver the mental health medications for the patients at their homes via the GCMHP authorized cars via the heads of community centers and supportive teams of nurses and psychologists. Also, GCMHP disseminated its psychiatrists and psychologists work specific phone numbers to reach the service anytime, anywhere.Moreover, the GCMHP staff had trained on delivering the needed services remotely in addition to delivering that training to many service providers and staff to be able to delivering quality remote services.Different awareness materials through different outlets and encouraging referral for those in need of mental health support had always considered. A special emphasis is placed on reaching out to the most vulnerable including persons living with a disability or suffering from high levels of trauma. A ﬂexible response approach ensuring marginalized groups, areas impacted by new eruptions of violence, or emerging and urgent needs can be responded to. The GCMHP strategy has also a value regarding the independence and non-political aﬃliation, as GCMHP is a non-political, independent organization. It is not inﬂuenced by political parties or ideologies. Mental health care is provided solely on the basis of need, without discrimination or preference. GCMHP is a key member in many clusters such as PC, HP, MHPSS and CP WGs, and PNGO’s rehabilitation cluster. GCMHP is always consults with the Protection Cluster including the GBV and Child Protection sub-clusters, about power dynamics in the area of intervention and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access or discrimination against particular groups. GCMHP coordinate also with specialized agencies to identify individuals and include them in the program interventions. On the other hands, GCMHP has an existing referral system to receive referrals from other organizations. This system is generalized among all PC members and well operated by GCMHP and partners. GCMHP was and still is sharing factsheets and information with the Protection cluster, including the Child Protection sub-clusters by using many forms like 4 Ws and 5Ws matrix and other templates. GCMHP do not assume an “open door” policy is enough. It makes sure that there are other possibilities for submitting complaints that do not require the beneﬁciaries to expose themselves to project staff. It opened all social media platforms and websites to public to provide any feedback or complaints.
