Project Summary

Project title: Provision of gender-transformative life-saving economic opportunities to women and girls IDPs from Cabo Delgado conflict and in host communities to prevent harmful coping strategies at individual and community level and build their economic resilience.
Summary:
Humanitarian crises in Mozambique are becoming increasingly complex (armed conflict, cyclones, flooding, etc. are occurring simultaneously) and protracted, with the majority of displacements lasting multiple years, meaning that provision of only short-term, lifesaving services are becoming less and less sufficient, with people affected by crisis needing to have additional skills and opportunities for survival, more so, women and adolescent girls.
Pre-existing harmful practices, gender-based discrimination and inequalities exacerbate the impact of crises on women and girls and reduce the likelihood of them receiving the humanitarian services they need to survive and recover. Inequality tends to worsen in humanitarian contexts, leaving women that are already facing enormous challenges, doubled with exacerbated inequality and fewer options. Intersectional vulnerabilities can additionally further determine how people are affected, among other women with disabilities, LGBTIQ, women-headed households, adolescent girls, older women, or women belonging to a minority or marginalized groups. When displaced, women face an elevated risk of gender-based violence trafficking, prostitution, child- and forced marriages and pregnancies.
This project aims to enhance the ability of women, girls and their families to fend for themselves by providing access to empowering life-saving livelihood and economic opportunities with a view to minimizing the risks resulting from increased vulnerability in the humanitarian context. The intervention will target women and girls both displaced and in hosting/families communities.
The proposed project aims to prevent the forced adoption of coping strategies which undermine the human rights of women and girls and heighten their vulnerability in the humanitarian context. This will be done by providing women and girls, both displaced and in host communities, with access to solid and gender transformative life-saving livelihood and empowering economic opportunities. UN Women will use the village saving and loans association (VSLA) methodologies to promote saving, credit and investments in micro-businesses, complemented by awareness-raising on GBV including referral to response services and on gender issues with a focus on control over resources and participation in private and public decision making. This is expected to contribute to reducing GBV and harmful practices which disproportionately affect women and adolescent girls, empower them and ultimately to promote gender equality at the community level.
To prevent the spread of COVID-19, foster digitalization and financial inclusion, saving groups will be capacitated to operate using mobile money platforms. Phones will also be used as a medium for security and enabling safety networks among women and girls including access to integrated GBV response services. Among the business opportunities to be promoted emphasis will go to the marketing of green alternatives. These include, solar lanterns due to their multiple benefits in creating security in the community especially for women and girls in public spaces, reduced health hazards resulting from exposure to pollution due to use of fuel, and clean cooking stoves to contribute to income savings, mitigate the high demand of firewood in overcrowded neighbourhoods while reducing women and girls workload to stimulate participation in public decision making or uptake of paid job opportunities.
Direct beneficiaries: Women and adolescent girls IDPs and from the host community, including women survivors of GBV, women with disabilities, women living with HIV.
Indirect beneficiaries: Men and boys IDPs and from host communities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

- Social mobilization, gender norms programming and advocacy to reduce protection risks to women, girls and other vulnerable groups and promoting acceptance of support for women’s leadership, livelihoods, and income generation.- Identification of promising business opportunities and selection of beneficiaries and vocational orientation in line with the “leave no one behind principle”.- Financial literacy sessions and establishment of VSLA.- Awareness raising sessions on gender and GBV issues and access to response services- Awareness of men and boys on gender equality and GBV prevention in the host community to create enabling environment for women’s empowerment- Training on business skills and coaching for running a successful business at the community level - Coaching sessions on business management in humanitarian context including COVID-19 related- Linkage with mobile money service providers based on pre-existing partnership with the private sector.- Provision of productive assets and market linkage assistance All above-mentioned activities will be undertaken in Pemba City and Pemba Metuge (Province of Cabo-Delgado). Note: These activities will cover both Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces, where UN Women has a strong presence, a network of civil organizations and 3 complementary ongoing projects on Promoting Women’s participation in peace and conflict management processes, Spotlight Initiative for eliminating violence against women and girls and the Rapariga Biz sexual and reproductive health joint programme (economic empowerment component).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UN Women will be guided by a human rights-based approach (HRBA) and will make sure that core protection principles of non-discrimination, equality, participation, accountability, and the rule of law are in place. Moreover the design of this project derived from the systematic use of the aforementioned principles in Cabo Delgado Province.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

To respond to the increasingly protracted nature of conflict and crisis, there is a need to address humanitarian needs in a more inclusive, integrated and holistic manners, that simultaneously addresses women's and girls most critical and urgent needs and the more longer-term empowerment and resilience. Due to limited viable income security for IDPs in Cabo Delgado, women and girls are at an increased risk of being forced to engage in and rely on harmful coping mechanisms. This is especially true amongst the most economically vulnerable communities and female-headed households in humanitarian settings and protracted crisis in Mozambique. This project is transformative and empowering to women across age groups. It entails humanitarian assistance that builds on women's activism and leadership. The initiative will work towards unleashing the entrepreneurship potential of women IDPs through their active participation in business planning, management of financial resources at the household level, promoting the access and control over productive assets, effective participation in the public life, including the market place. The rapid spread of COVID-19 led to the unprecedented socio-economic effect that presented additional risks on displaced populations and has posed a great risk of reversing gender-transformative progress achieved before the crisis. Previous gains in gender equality and empowerment of women are being lost, adversely affecting women’s leadership, their decision-making capacities, care responsibilities, control over resources, and access to basic services. UN Women will ensure that crisis-affected women and adolescent girls have better access to capacities, tools, skills and services to address their specific humanitarian needs, to facilitate increased protection including from GBV, empowerment and recovery. Additionally, UN Women will work with women’s local organizations to increase their leadership and participation in humanitarian coordination, implementation, and accountability.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UN Women’s Strategy: The Empowerment of Women and Girls with Disabilities – Towards Full and Effective Participation and Gender Equality was developed to ensure a more systematic approach to strengthen the inclusion of the rights of women and girls with disabilities in UN Women’s efforts to achieve gender equality, empowerment of all women and girls, and the realization of their rights.The systemic marginalization, attitudinal and environmental barriers they face lead to lower economic and social status; increased risk of violence and abuse including sexual violence; discrimination as well as harmful gender-based discriminatory practices; and barriers to access education, health care including sexual and reproductive health, information and services, and justice as well as civic and political participation. This hinders their participation on an equal basis with others. Women and girls with disabilities face additional risks in humanitarian crises, they may find it harder to flee or are left behind making them vulnerable to attacks. The loss of assistive devices, separation from carers and protection networks may result in greater dependency on others and increase the risk of exploitation. Women and girls with disabilities are also at higher risk of sexual and gender-based violence, especially those withintellectual, psychosocial and hearing disabilities due to a combination of stigmatization, isolation and communication challenges. Girls with disabilities are also at an increased risk of child marriage due to the context of protracted crises. The project will ensure that all IDPs and host community women and girls with disabilities, in all their diversity are included as equal partners across the humanitarian-development continuum, and their rights and agency are fully realized during the implementation of this project. All activities will be screened to make sure they are gender-responsive and disability-inclusive during planning, implementation and monitoring phase.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project will creatively integrate environmental protection into the life-saving related livelihood opportunities for women and adolescent girls. UN Women recognizes that women are disproportionately affected by climate challenges. The purpose of this project includes reducing the use of kerosene and other unorthodox traditional lighting and cooking methods with cleaner renewable energy alternatives through developing entrepreneurship initiatives for IDP and host community women. It recognizes the rapid increase in the population in the hosting neighbourhood will increase the pressure on the environment (high demand for charcoal and firewood, burning of kerosine for lighting). Being still the main users of cooking energy due to traditional gender roles, there is need to support women’s access to renewable energy that is environmentally friendly, more convenient, safer and less labour-using thus releasing crucial time for participation in public life, leisure and access to empowering opportunities such as education or paid job while challenging gradually challenging the status quo. A broad view of the entire household fuel cycle needs to be taken, including not just improved stoves but the kitchen and housing design, food preparation and processing, and improved technology for the ergonomic collection and transportation of firewood. Activities will include (i) Capacity, Skill (business and technical) and knowledge development as well as the provision of equipment and accessories in Clean and Renewable Energy technology for cooking, lighting and productive uses (e.g. solar lanterns and clean cooking solutions); (ii) Develop entrepreneurship initiatives for local women in Sustainable Energy Technology. UN Women has a strong record in implementing women and renewable energy initiatives and lessons from the implementation will be applied. Other elements of the project such as awareness sessions and training will also take into consideration the environment by avoiding the use of pollutant inputs.
