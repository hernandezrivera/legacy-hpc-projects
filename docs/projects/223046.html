Project Summary

To achieve the goal of improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions for Rohingya refugees in camp 13 as well as in targeted host community in Ukhiya, World Vision aim to implement comprehensive strategies aligned with the WASH Sector's Joint Response Plan (JRP) which involves enhancing WASH facilities functionality and interventions while addressing key issues through community engagement to increase access to sustainable WASH services.
In camp 13, World Vision will prioritize community involvement, focusing on gender-responsive approaches to ensure the participation of women and girls in decision-making regarding essential WASH services. This will involve the repair maintenance of WASH facilities, hygiene practices, waste and fecal sludge management and provision of critical supplies such as soap and menstrual hygiene management materials. With a focus on accountability, protection, and considering age, gender, and diversity, the project aims to implement activities following the principles of 'do no harm.' This includes emergency support for various situations like disease outbreaks (AWD, scabies, natural disasters likefire, flood, monsoon, cyclone), and environmental considerations. World vision will also try to align with the WASH sector technical priorities to focus on be removing accumulated settled solid sludge, reflecting on communal bathing facilities given current shelter-based bathing habits, operationalization of the new water strategy, and stronger community involvement and ownership in WASH activities.
The initiative builds upon World Vision's previous experience in implementing essential WASH projects in different camps since 2017, now aiming to ensure quality water supply, improved sanitation facilities & hygiene practices and improved waste & fecal sludge management for approximately 2,077 households, covering around 10,423 individuals in one block of camp 13 in respect with Sphere WASH Standards and WASH Sector guidelines. Operations and maintenance of essential WASH facilities and services will be a core focus, ensuring functionality and accessibility to sanitation facilities for all, in collaboration with camp WASH focal points and Camp-in-Charge officials (CiCs). Also, coordination among various partners and engagement with camp authorities, local government, and existing consortium partners will be crucial in achieving these objectives.
In the Host community of Ukhiya, Worrld Vision would like to adress the hygiene issues like hygiene awareness sessions in the host community, developing various community structure for effective hygiene management, special hygiene sessions for school going children. This integrated approach aims to not only address immediate WASH needs but also to promote sustainable solutions, community resilience, and comprehensive support aligned with humanitarian goals and standards.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

It is imperative that WASH actors continue to step up their efforts to guarantee that all women, men, girls, and boys, as well as people of all ages and disabilities, have respectful, adequate, equitable, and quality access to WASH services in light of the significant number of refugees. In consideration with the following gaps World Vision will implement the sustainable solution of safe drinking water, improved sanitation facilities and appropriate hygiene education in cooperation with community structures.According to ISNA, 2024 report, 15% of the Rohingya population in FDMN camps reported not having enough water for their family needs. It also stated that, 68% of HH using tap-stand as Primary Source for Drinking Water per Camps, moreover only Average 20 liters Domestic Water Collected Per Person per Day in Camps. Day by day, As the groundwater table descends, numerous tube-wells face disfunction, particularly in the dry season. Hand pumps continue to serve as a primary source for drinking water, bathing, and domestic use. Taking these circumstances into account, this project will prioritize ensuring high-quality water at the household level, recognizing it as a crucial stage in the safe water chain. In 2024, the WASH Sector’s strategic focus will be on operation and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost efficient solutions. 68% of Female Family Member feel safe using the communal latrine at night as per ISNA 2024. Solid waste management is another mentionable challenge as only 68% of the households are Segregating Waste at household level. As per ISNA 2024, 48% households with visible waste in the vicinity of their accommodation sometimes. Proper waste and fecal sludge management is another concerning issue in this area. The latrines are getting filled with accumulated solid sludge at the pit bottom reducing the holding capacity of those pits causing frequent desludging. The FSTPs are also in poor condition and there is scope of upgradation to meet the standards.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: As per ISNA, 2024, 5% of the community have not access to soap for hand washing. Most of women and girl accessing menstrual hygiene materials, nevertheless, the situation at the practical level is far from ideal. Many females continue to face obstacles in accessing MHM facilities due to stigma, taboos, and conservative practices. There are instances where we have observed that Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials related to MHM lack contextualization. Moreover, there is a rising trend in communicable diseases such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Dengue cases at the camp level. Taking into account these crucial factors and the existing gaps that demand attention, World Vision is committed to sustaining its ongoing WASH program in camp 13. World Vision Bangladesh will actively contribute to the operations and maintenance systems required to broaden and secure the sustainability of WASH infrastructure. This commitment involves fostering community participation and engagement, utilizing a multi-level approach. This approach aims to identify and resolve issues at the individual, household, community, and environmental levels in a complementary manner, building upon existing projects for greater impact.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Through this initiative, it will be made sure that every person is safe from harm and has equitable access to WASH services. When design this project, consideration has been given to all age groups. Since children make up a higher percentage of the community, it will be ensured that they participate effectively in every stage of the project cycle. The expectations and perception of children will be reflected through WASH facility design. By constructing walls and fences around exposed water sources, such as tanks or wells, this initiative will guarantee that WASH facilities are designed with children's safety in mind. The project also takes the elderly population very seriously. This project will ensure aged friendly WASH facilities, access road to the WASH facilities, and make sure that WASH facilities are closer to the elderly.This comprehensive WASH initiative has been crafted with the aim of inclusivity for various demographics, including women, girls, men, boys, older individuals, and those with disabilities. By following the guideline of Washington questionnaire, project will identify the person with disability and provide friendly & need based infrastructure. The primary goal of this project is to guarantee fair and non-discriminatory access to WASH services for all genders, age groups, and individuals with disabilities. The planning and design phase involves extensive community consultations, ensuring the engagement of a diverse range of people. Implementation and monitoring will also prioritize the active involvement of this diverse group. The project specifically focuses on creating WASH facilities that are accessible and accommodating for individuals with disabilities, and certain facilities will be upgraded to better meet the diverse needs of the community.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

World Vision will fully accountable to the affected people through this project and beside ensuring their need of quality and equitable access to WASH facility, World vision also aim to make them aware of their rights and entitlements under the project. This project will ensure the participation of the affected people in every phase which will be effectively reflected in this project. All of their opinions will be counted and responded accordingly by establishing Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) system which will collect their needs, perspectives, and priorities in a regular basis with prompt response. Adequate mechanisms, which are accessible to aged people and person with disabilities, will be put in place to allow beneficiaries to raise their complaints in an accessible, safe and dignified manner. For better learning and effective intervention community consultation meeting, focus group discussion, user group meetings etc. will be conducted as some open discussion platforms. Moreover, community-led monitoring mechanism will be reinforced for effective monitoring.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project will integrate with climate action. We prioritize disaster risk reduction (DRR) and disaster risk management (DRM) by integrating anticipatory actions that are informed by climate data and trends. This includes maintaining and repairing WASH facilities, stabilizing surrounding slopes, and safeguarding the WASH site. Disaster-resilient features will be incorporated into the WASH facility, encompassing activities like water point disinfection and securing the WASH structure. World Vision holds contingency supplies (water jars, aqua tabs, desludging machines, bamboo, bleaching powder, etc.) to offer immediate post-disaster support. Environmental impact will be minimal, with a focus on safety systems for toilet pits, ultimately leading to a positive impact. Additionally, during the construction of WASH facilities, consideration is given to averting potential risks, such as average flood levels. Also for the emergency WORLD VISION has a dedicated centralized response team who will be ready to deploy within short notice. Early warning system, appointing security focal, preparedness plans are developed in every year for the smooth emergency response of any disasters. Our initiatives not only aim to enhance community resilience but also promote sustainable practices that benefit both the environment and local populations. By working collaboratively with stakeholders, we are committed to making a positive impact on climate resilience and environmental sustainability.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, our WASH project includes several important emergency preparedness measures. We have developed a continuity plan to ensure that our water, sanitation, and hygiene services can continue during crises, like natural disasters or health emergencies. Our emergency response plan outlines specific actions to take in various scenarios, such as water contamination or sanitation facility damage. This plan includes clear communication protocols to keep the community informed. During an emergency an EPR (emergency preparedness and response) team will be formed as a current practice to assess, follow preparedness plan and implementing response plan within the working camp. Emergency preparedness will be in place predicting the possible hazardous period specially in the monsoon period in Bangladesh specially June to September. Emergency preparedness will involve keeping the contingency materials in stock, ensuring the emergency fund if possible. As camp 13 will be under UNICEF working area, World Vision is well known for their EPR structure and methodology. Additionally, this project will seek for contingency fund to address unexpected challenges in emergencies, like urgent repairs or increased demand for services during an emergency. Overall, these measures strengthen our ability to respond and maintain essential WASH services when they’re needed most. Alongside all these, World Vision will alignment with EPR plans with ISCG’s Multi-Hazard Response Plan 2022, Standard Operating procedures, National Anticipatory Actions protocols, and the National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025).

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Our project is designed to promote self-reliance and self-resilience among the Rohingya refugees in camp 13. To ensure effective WASH management, we will actively involve community members in various voluntary services within the camp. By equipping individuals with WASH management skills, we not only ensure a consistent supply of safe water, sanitation but also empower them to enhance their self-reliance. To further bolster hygiene practices, we will conduct regular awareness sessions led by community-based hygiene volunteers. These sessions will focus on lifesaving hygiene practices, enabling individuals to take charge of their health and that of their families. By participating in these initiatives, refugees will gain critical skills and knowledge related to WASH management, which will improve their immediate living conditions and prepare them for future challenges.

This intigrated WASH project will design the activities alaigning with the development initiative and the development priorities in Cox's Bazar district. Follwing with the district development and growth priorities of Cox's Bazar World Vision will address the hygiene realted issues in the host community following the DDGP Phase II strategy. Lack of awareness about the benefits of handwashing with soap and public health implications should be improved by local level message dissemination, Habits of handwashing with soap should be improved by ensuring soap and water together at handwashing location, school awareness sessions will be adressed through this project which will perfectly alaign with the DDGP phase II & Phase III strategy.
