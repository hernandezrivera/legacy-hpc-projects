Project Summary

The project will address the immediate and long-term health needs with focus on mental health and psychosocial support, physical rehabilitation and disability inclusion needs of the most vulnerable persons that are the center of humanitarian response to cope with recurrent shock and build resilience.
The project will target returnees, IDPs and host population with specific focus on persons at risk of exclusion specifically people with disabilities, people with injuries and chronic diseases, people experiencing mental health and psychosocial problems, children, older persons; who are often invisible to mainstream humanitarian organizations. The project activities will target an estimate of 14,970 (5,988 Male, 8,982 Female among them will be 2,994 persons with disabilities, 1,780 elderly persons, 200 children and 12,990 adults ) Vulnerable IDPS, returnees and host population. The focus will be on newly and protracted displaced households with a key attention to support positive coping mechanisms and resilience strengthening. HI will have static presence in the target project locations ; with support from local community structures, local organizations of persons with disabilities, health and protection partners; HI will identify the most vulnerable to MHPSS and physical rehabilitation needs and provide direct services. HI will critically focus on mainstreaming protection, gender and disability Inclusion issues at all stages of the intervention, identifying and giving priority to vulnerable female headed households and persons with disabilities. HI has one (01) MEAL manager and two (02) MEAL officers who will independently conduct all post distribution monitoring and share the outcomes with the team and cluster partners at state and national level.
The activities will be implemented by HI static team based in the Juba ,Pibor, Rubkona, and Aweil Center, with a mobile response to Morobo and Lainya from Yei where HI has a static base.

1 - Needs

The health cluster estimates that in 2022, over 5.47 million people are in need of basic essential life-saving health services due to the protracted crisis in South Sudan. Statistics or comprehensive information on the number of people with disabilities in South Sudan is lacking. However, recent surveys and estimates suggest that it is likely to be even higher than the global estimate of 15% due to the protracted crisis situation and the limited access to basic services including health, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement leading to higher prevalence of disabling conditions. Floods, Conflict, displacement and instability have led to the deterioration of the health system across the country, and prevented access to essential care for a vast proportion of the population, particularly persons with disabilities. The health care system has suffered the effects of the protracted crisis that has affected service delivery in the areas of mental health and functional rehabilitation services for persons with disabilities . In 2021 national budget allocations , only 1.9% was for health services; with such a limited resource basket amidst vast health needs in the country, prioritization takes precedence with greater focus towards reduction of mortality and morbidity rates with little attention to other illnesses of low mortality but with disabling long term adverse effects to the population if not given attention such as mental health problems and physical disability and injury. WHO estimates the prevalence of mental disorders to be approximately 2.5M in conflict settings of South Sudan, this constitutes approximately 22% of the country's population at risk of developing long term impairment. The recent attacks on health workers and health facilities in some of the active conflict areas have continued to escalate the distress and trauma among service providers and negatively crippling health services at large, hence affecting access and availability of mental health and functional rehabilitation services to the persons that are in need. This project will specifically seek to address the gaps in the provision of mental health and functional rehabilitation services for persons with disabilities in South Sudan, and meet further demand through capacity building of healthcare staff and community groups on MHPSS and disability inclusion including supporting joint assessments and coordination on mental health and disability specific risks and needs. At present limited knowledge of these areas prevents sustainable and context relevant interventions, and exacerbates marginalization and stigmatization in these areas so such training is critical to address the high needs. Limited understanding and prevailing stigma related to mental health disorders and psychological distress also prevents people living with acute , mild, moderate or severe psychosocial difficulties from accessing appropriate services. Similarly, for persons with physical impairments, injuries and chronic conditions, the availability of functional rehabilitation services and assistive devices is limited, particularly in hard-to-reach areas. Despite the high rates of conflict related injury, and physical conditions linked to displacement, poor access to healthcare, nutrition and other basic services, the availability and coverage of functional rehabilitation services is severely limited.

2 - Project activities

The project aims at responding to both MHPSS and physical/functional rehabilitation needs of the most affected IDPs, returnees and host population with extra focus on persons with disabilities. The project proposes a comprehensive and complementary approach: Situation analysis and beneficiary identification: HI will conduct rapid MHPSS situational analysis and proceed to Beneficiaries’ identification and assessment of needs. Beneficiaries’ identification will be based on the most critical needs and vulnerability criteria including among others: people with disabilities, people with injuries and chronic diseases, people experiencing psychosocial distress or mental health problems, children, older persons Provision of direct MHPSS and Rehabilitation services: HI team - highly trained and experienced – will provide goal oriented individual, group psychosocial support activities and awareness raising sessions on mental health issues. This will be done respecting COVID19 preventive measures. In addition – and depending on individual assessment conducted by HI team - physical and functional rehabilitation will be provided for persons with disabilities and/or injuries. For specific cases, HI in coordination with other partners will refer for specialized MHPSS services such as psychiatry or prothesis and orthosis for functional rehabilitation. Throughout the project, HI will provide capacity building of health workers and the Boma Health initiative structures in the targeted implementation areas to ensure efficient service delivery and better sustainability of the intervention. This will include; * Capacity-building of health workers on MhGAP-HIG, identification and referral of MHPSS cases * Capacity-building of health workers on physical and functional rehabilitation, capacity building on disability inclusion strategies, capacity building of boma health initiatives on stigma , identification and referral of MHPSS cases. In addition, HI will support and strengthen healthcare facilities on accessibility for disability inclusion, including infrastructure modifications and awareness campaigns. HI will lead community-based awareness sessions with key messaging on accessibility, disability inclusion and MHPSS. The project also aims to work at National and State levels together with the MoH to provide support on strengthening physical and functional rehabilitation & MHPSS capacities of health workers. *HI as a MHPSS and disability actor will participate actively in MHPSS technical working group and disability working group at National and State levels to ensure coordination on issues of MHPSS and disability. *HI will participate in joint assessments conducted by Health cluster & EP& R partners including reviewing data collection and rapid needs assessments tools looking at collecting disability data including data on mental health and mental wellbeing. HI has experience in supporting other sectors like Protection in making their data collection tools and processes disability inclusive. *HI teams will provide training and capacity building to mainstream health service providers on basic rehabilitation for persons with disabilities and basic MHPSS skills like Psychological First Aid and basic counselling skills. This will be in line with building local capacities of health workers. Some of HI Rehab and MHPSS activities will be conducted as facility level and HI staff will continue offering periodic support supervision to the health facility staff. *HI will also support Health cluster information management systems and 5W reporting to mainstream disability data collection within the information management system. These will include supporting integration of the Washington Group Questions and training of partners on how to collect and analyze disability data using the Washington group questions. HI will also focus on capacity building for health cluster members on Disability Inclusion and on Inclusive MHPSS across their response.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Any complaint related to HI programming and areas of operation that is non-sensitive is categorized as an operational complaint. This includes but not limited to complaints such as: • dissatisfaction with the provided goods and services • quality and quantity of the distributed items • failure to provide timely assistance or request for additional assistance • issues related to eligibility, registration, or distribution• lack of general project information • non-serious, yet impolite or irresponsible behavior of HI staff or partner staff Operational or a programme relevant complaints are critical for the FCMS focal point as they need to track them until they are resolved and closed. For operational complaints, the procedure is as follows: 1. Operational complaint is forwarded as email to the relevant project/program manager, copying area manager or Senior Project Liaison Officer (PLO) and where appropriate technical specialist of respective sector (with MEAL Manager in cc) on the same day of receiving the complaint 2. Complainant receives a written receipt (see External Acknowledgement Form) in their preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message within max. 7 calendar days 3. Relevant Program staff reviews the complaint, brings forward recommendations for action, and shares these with Complaints Focal Point and/or MEAL Manager. Some complaints may require Country Director oversight or consultation. 4. Complaint Desk provides feedback to the complainant in his/her preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message, and informs about the appeals procedure within max. 21 calendar days The complainant has the right to appeal the feedback provided by HI within a period of maximum 30 calendar days. Appeals shall be dealt with on a case-by-case basis unless case-type precedence has been established.Complaints may require immediate action, for example an issue that poses a security threat to beneficiaries HI staff, HI resources/assets or HI reputation should immediately be acted upon. In such a scenario the complaint focal point needs to contact the relevant person, either by phone or by visiting them (If they are in the same area) such as area manager, safety advisor or his/her replacement and country director, deputy country directors etc. These managers should decide the future course of action by assessing the severity and the nature of the complaint. Following this, the complaint focal point should write an email containing all the details about the complaint provided by the complainant to ensure the formal submission along with adding this to FCMS centralized data sheet. All non-programmatic HI related complaints should be forwarded to the relevant staff head of the department by copying their immediate supervisor. The relevance of the complaint will be determined by the nature of the complaint as explained above. Operational complaints will be processed and delegated internally by the complaints and response focal person by forwarding the complaints and response log to the Area Manager. If the complaint entails a senior member of staff or is about the Area Manager/PLO, the complaint will be sent directly to the CD in Juba. If the complaint is about the CD, the complaints will be sent to the HQ via email : alert@hi.org However for most other programmatic complaints the Area Manager/PLO will review the complaints listed and refer them by email to the relevant department and person For urgent action, the feedback will directly be communicated for immediate reaction. It will be included in the project action plan as soon as possible to ensure the problem will be solved timely. Regarding project/program related feedback - the feedback will be taken into account through usual coordination mechanism, including coordination meetings (including operations and support services), to ensure the project adapt to the feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneficiaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text and phoneline as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews and post intervention monitoring surveys– designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can access feedback and complaints mechanisms, HI will include considerations such as the use of local languages, reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities (i.e. accessible venues, sign-language interpretation, sighted guides, accessible information materials), as well as gender specific mobilization and messaging to minimize any barriers these key groups encounter. Moreover, HI is proposing many entry-points for feedbacks/complaints to reach all: 1. In-person visit to FCMS Desk 2. HI community & group meetings 3. Complaint/Feedback Box 4. Toll free telephone Hotline and SMS 5. HI email address 6. Beneficiary satisfaction surveys.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HI benefits from a professional whistleblowing mechanism, upgraded in July 2020, with a technical solution delivered by a specialized professional service provider (NAVEX Global). HI has reinforced its system for reporting suspicions and incidents related to fraud, corruption, non-compliance with humanitarian neutrality, sexual abuse and exploitation, harassment and child protection, through the dedicated web platform. This simplifies the reporting of suspicions of unethical acts, in complete safety and confidentiality. It optimizes the analysis and qualification of reports, the monitoring of investigations and the management of the incidents by the authorized officers and the follow-up by the management bodies. This centralized mechanism complements the proximity systems and initiatives open on the field to beneficiaries and partners. Thus, HI has all the components of the alert and incident management chain: reports, evaluation and investigation (including a specific handbook) and case management. HI’s Feedback & Complaints Management System (FCMS) in South Sudan aims to ensure accountability and improve the relevance, effectiveness and suitability of HIs interventions. Through both solicited and non-solicited feedback, its purpose is to provide a safe, accessible and effective channel for beneficiaries and project stakeholders to obtain information about the organization and its programs, offer feedback and complaints, and to be provided response or redress. Further, it protects beneficiaries from any kind of abuse or potential corrupt behavior of HI or partner staff. To foster stronger relationships with the community, to allow community feedback to shape and improve programming and to ensure that HI South Sudan provides relevant, accessible and appropriate assistance. Further, HI aims to integrate and implement with adherence to the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS). This document specifically focuses on Commitments 4 and 5 of the CHS, namely, that communities and people affected by crisis know their rights and entitlements, have access to information and participate in decisions that affect them; and have access to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The risk that beneficiaries may be exposed to violence due to the distance particularly for women and girl’s men and boys with disabilities is there. However, HI will mitigate those risk in consultation with beneficiaries incorporating their feedback in the project implementation, and bring project activities in closer and safer places to beneficiaries, and will keep possible and passible distances. HI also has well trained protection, Inclusion and MHPSS staff who quickly provide urgent response to individual survivors in all project locations. HI will work in close collaboration with other humanitarian partners and cluster members and local authority when providing materials and during the registration process. Access to humanitarian assistance is very challenging, due to insecurity caused by the inter communal conflict, gang groups and unknown armed forces, which are all factors that might put people at risk when accessing services. However, HI will mitigate these, through coordination with protection partners during monthly protection monitoring. HI has PSEA and child safeguarding protocols as part of the code conduct and compliances of all employees to abide by. And HI will provide open door policy of the beneficiaries to come forward and report any PSEA cases committed by HI staff and that of other partners through the pre-established complaint system. HI will coordinate closely with GBV partners, camp management (if available), UNICEF and UNHCR and health providers to ensure survivors are supported and referred in timely manner, as well as to develop solutions to address risks with protection partners. Given that the project is intending to support persons with disability and most vulnerable beneficiaries it is essential that needs based selection criteria are adhered to. HI will create holistic environment where everybody particularly person with disabilities, elderly, boys and girls are able to actively participate in project consultations and feedback sessions. Reporting tools will also capture gender, age, disability disaggregated data on a regular basis. HI will ensure people have safe access to the available complaint mechanisms in place and will promote participation in open feedback sessions. HI has safe and password encrypted data bases. The field data collection forms use coding instead on names. To mitigate external interference, HI will utilize standardized vulnerability and targeting criteria, and consult all local stakeholders on this process to avoid any bias in the process. To mitigate this effectively, HI will monitor HLP issues in communities. Strong coordination with HLP partners, as well as government is required to mediate issues effectively and mediate disputes. Given that certain target locations particularly away from urban centers are heavily contaminated with cluster munitions, ERW and UXOs it is critical that due diligence and coordination with mine-action partners is completed before conducting outreach activities in rural areas. The presence of explosive hazards in communities would heavily impact the delivery of activities, but also factor into risk awareness and livelihood approaches.
