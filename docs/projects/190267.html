Project Summary

This EHF response aims to address the critical and emergency nutrition needs of 89,665 (Male: 23,011, Female: 66,654) most vulnerable, conflict affected and internally displaced peoples (IDPs) in Waghimera zone (Abergele, Sekota and Tsegbeji districts) and Central Tigray zone (Endafelasi and Edega Arbi districts). The response is mainly informed by the current and anticipated dire nutrition and food security situation in the proposed intervention districts. The prioritized nutrition responses considered under this action are, early identification, referral, and treatment of severe acute malnutrition with and without medical complications, early identification, referral, and treatment of moderate acute malnutrition. (TSFP), Promotion, protection and support of adequate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF-E) practices at times of emergencies and Blanket Supplementary Feeding (BSFP).
Moreover, complementary health system strengthening, including capacity building of frontline health workers on CMAM/ IYCF, SFP will be provided. Provision of gap filling essential medicines and supplies required to delivery nutrition services are also taken into consideration. Surge capacity support in terms of positioning of additional health workers in selected Stablisation centers where the health system could not withstand a surge in acute malnutrition cases will complement the nutrition response. With apparent issue of shortage of supplies and access difficulties, AAH will follow adaptive approach and support the rollout of the simplified protocol in the targeted locations once endorsed by MoH. Besides, to maximize impact and ensure continuum of care across the health system, an integrated Nutrition, WASH and Protection services will be implemented through this EHF reserve allocation on top of the existing complementary projects. All nutrition emergency responses in this project will be carried out in line with the MoH Ethiopia and WHO COVID-19 standard protocol and guidance

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Action Against Hunger has a participatory approach to ensure active involvement of beneficiaries and local authorities at all levels of the project (from design to M&E). In order to ensure accountability for what the organization is doing, Action Against Hunger will conduct serious of consultation sessions with the local community including women, men, youth, religious leaders, influential men and women, minority groups, people with disabilities and local administration to better understand their needs and to help the designing of interventions that are responsive to these needs. The design of this program will be made to reflect the cumulative learning acquired through the engagement of the population. Community representatives, beneficiaries and partners will also participate in project launching workshops and targeting process. Information about the project, target beneficiaries and beneficiary selection criteria, their entitlement, and distribution time and places will be shared through media that are accessible for every partner involved in the process. The effectiveness level will also be tracked through Post Distribution Assessments (PDMs). MEAL Specialists and Accountability Officers at field office strengthen the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms in their respective base. Beneficiaries and communities are always consulted on how they would like to raise their concerns, and provide feedback about the program. Based on the community consultation, multiple feedback channels that are assumed to be relevant to the beneficiaries will be established. These could include focus group discussions, exit feedback, individual discussions, listening boxes and telephone calls through Action Against Hunger dedicated hotline. The field office team collect and document the beneficiary’s feedback. Those issues related to information gap etc. will be provided to the beneficiaries immediately by field staff who collected the feedback, while complaints will be transferred to senior management for decision /action. It is also well understood that the NFI distribution are high potential areas where abuses and misconducts take place. In response to that, Action Against Hunger conducts remote monitoring after every NFI distribution /assistance to track if there is any abuse in all the NFI assistance process (explained more under the monitoring Section below). Action Against Hunger feedback and complaint mechanism considers potential dangers and risks to all parties and incorporates ways to prevent injury and harm. This includes ensuring confidentiality and addressing the possibility of retaliation against witnesses.
