Project Summary

MF’s project will deliver a strategic intervention in the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector to strengthen the community resilience and reduce of socio-economic vulnerabilities. This will be achieved by providing vocational training and skills development for 300 individuals in Babysitting and parenting, electronic literacy, knitting wool, manufacturing homemade detergents, sewing, and hairdressing administering a startup business grant for 100 of them program in 13 camps located in SCHF’s prioritized sub-district of Dana in Idleb governorate.
MF will ensure the participation of all relevant stakeholders at all stages of the operations management cycle. MF will be responsible for the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation (M&E), and closure phases. MF will manage the project, provide the contractual services, ensure the required coordination among clusters, and ensure high-quality results and efficient progress through the provision of capable programmatic specialized staff.
Additionally, MF will rehabilitate 10 Kms of access roads to the targeted camps to enhance the mobility of goods, IDPs and HC members. Through this rehabilitation works the IDP sites will be protected from flood risks and instability to their sites and situations. Within the rehabilitation works MF will provide 100 CFW opportunities.
The project will have a positive impact on all beneficiaries in the selected areas , where the total number of the targeted persons approximately: 54,221 IDPs (12471 men, 13555 women, 17351 Boys, 10844 Girls) from different age groups (Young children, Children, Adolescents, Young, Middle-aged adults and elderly).
This includes about 2,530 Persons with Disabilities; 2,530 IDPs (624 men, 678 women, 686 Boys, 542 Girls)

4 - Needs

Per the 2021 Needs and Response Summary report (February 2021) there has been an “almost 20 % increase in the number of people in need compared to early 2020 with a disproportionate impact on women and children. As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.” According to the most recent Humanitarian Situation Overview in Syria (HSOS) NORTHWEST SYRIA -Reach- February 2021, the most reported overall priority needs were Livelihoods, Winterization, Food, and Healthcare. During early 2021, heavy floods occurred in NWS. More than 100 IDP sites suffered unusual floods and bad weather. In addition to the urgent need of livelihood and cash-based assistance reported by monitoring humanitarian institutes, these floods have come to add additional level of vulnerability to these affected HHs. According to last reported risky and flooded sites by CCCM/ERL clusters, there are 98 flooded sites in Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, Idleb and Qourqeena. In these sites, 11330 HHs were affected by the floods. Typical sheltering unit are tents. More than 43 sites of them have difficulties or/and blocks in roads accessing to camps. These data build together a proposed intervention, where floods added new level of vulnerability to the already existing vulnerability aspects.
