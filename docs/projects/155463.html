Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve the immediate nutritional well-being of under five refugee children by tackling the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition in Tongogara Refugee Camp (TRC), located in Chipinge District of Manicaland.
The total people in need is 2,093 (1,044 girls; 1,049 boys) and the target group includes all children (3-5 years) 639 (324 girls; 315 boys) enrolled at the Early Childhood Development Centre in Tongagara refugee camp to enhance nutrition status, disease prevention and improve school attendance; all refugee children in TRC aged 0 – 2 years breast feeding and in need of supplementary feeding. The specific objective of the project is to ensure adequate protection, promotion and support for breastfeeding of infants and supplementary feed for young children are available, and that infants younger than 6 months who are not breastfed have access to alternative food that is appropriate and adequate. Special support will be extended to underweight children identified at the clinic during growth monitoring sessions. Cases identified are usually the chronically ill and new arrivals. The project will also equip Health Promoters with skills to perform anthropometry measurements in the community. This will enhance early detection of malnutrition/ obesity in Under 5 children in the refugee camp.

Needs

In emergency refugee response operations, protection, food security and nutrition are closely linked as refugees may take risks to acquire food, or adopt unsafe coping strategies. The situation is exacerbated by drought as the small fields which supplement the cash transfers are affected adversely impacting on the feeding and care (including breastfeeding) of infants and young children. Malnourished individuals may suffer long-term effects, such as impeded growth or development. As refugees cannot obtain food in the camp, they are likely to become food insecure and may adopt unsafe coping mechanisms that endanger their security. In Tongogara Refugee Camp a total of 2,093 (1,044 girls; 1,049 boys) under five refugee children are in need of nutritional support and protection and over 1,000 women have children under 5 years old. All the new arrivals arriving in TRC will need to be screened, for acute malnutrition so that individuals with acute malnutrition and those children underweight or suffer nutritional deficiencies are identified and treated, reducing the likelihood of death or that their nutritional status will deteriorate further.Infants and mothers in TRC who have difficulty breastfeeding need assistance with nutrition education and appropriate supplementary feed as these infants are at greater risk of serious malnutrition and death. The same is true for infants younger than 6 months who are not breastfed (orphaned or with mothers failing to produce enough milk) if they are not identified and supported, such infants face a higher risk of serious malnutrition and death, as a result of eating inappropriate or contaminated food.The project intends to promote and support good feeding and caring practices for infants and children younger than 24 months, and school based supplementary feeding for 3 to 5 year old to improve school attendance and reduce the risk of being malnourished.

Activities

Proposed activities include;- Early Childhood Education Supplementary Feeding Program: extended to all children (3-5 years) enrolled at the Early Childhood Development (ECD) Centre to enhance nutrition status, disease prevention and improve school attendance. The 2018 enrollment stood at 639 (315 male: 324 female) children.-Community Supplementary Feeding Program: Extended to children in the community (0.5 – 2 years) -Supplementary Feeding/ Nutrition Basket for Underweight Children: Extended to underweight children identified at the clinic during growth monitoring sessions. Cases identified are usually the chronically ill and new arrivals.- Nutrition education for at least 1000 mothers: Mothers trained in breastfeeding techniques, benefits, child health status, etc. Cooking clubs established to train mothers in nutritious food preparation, storage and preservation. Clubs will be established in the 9 residential sections. Clubs will lead nutrition campaigns in respective residential sections. Mothers will be supported with food hampers to prepare at home. -Supplementary Feeding (Milk) for babies-Training of Health Promoters in Anthropometry: Propose to equip Health Promoters with skills and anthropometry equipment to perform anthropometry measurements in the community. This will enhance early detection of malnutrition/ obesity in Under 5 children in the refugee camp.- Bicycles for Health Promoters (x 7): To facilitate mobility and quick response to health issues in the growing settlement. -Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey 2019: To guide programme delivery with respect to Under 5 nutrition, IYCF, vaccination coverage and household dietary diversity.
