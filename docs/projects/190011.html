Project Summary

This project encompasses food assistance through emergency kits to populations that lack access to standard food distributions, including the newly displaced, returnees, vulnerable host community members. Seasonally appropriate livelihoods activities will support vulnerable households to engage in income-generating or food producing activities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Through this project, Solidarites International will provide food assistance to the most vulnerable populations to meet basic needs and prevent deterioration in health and nutrition status.Solidarites will target newly accessible and hard-to-reach districts including Nangade, Muidumbe, Macomia, Mocimboa da Praia, Mueda, Quissanga, and Palma, based on needs, operational capacity, and gaps. These districts remain hard-to-reach, and as such, humanitarian aid has been intermittent and limited to a small number of front-line organizations. The security situation is tenuous, and the limited evidence available indicates that IDPs and returnees are often the same – returning if possible to assess the status of property and any remaining crops, and returning to the security of relocation sites away from the conflict. Both IDPs and the slow and tentative returning population have already missed the main planting season for grains and staple crops; as a result, these highly vulnerable and hard-to-reach populations will face food insecurity and lost income for months to come. Food distributions will support household-level food security through direct provisions of staple food items. Indirectly, food assistance will support protection by reducing negative coping mechanisms as a result of household food insecurity. SI will also support livelihoods activities when seasonally appropriate.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

This approach will combined rapid distribution of 14 days food ratios for persons in movement and transit; regular fodd distribution in Nangade to suppot newly returned population, who were not able to catch up the planting season due to insecurity. Also, livelihood activities will be implemented to prepare the coming agricultural season, and supporting families in finding back means of development as per fishing or agricultural tools.The support will be based on vulnerability criteria, integrating especially PWSN (women, disable etc). All activities will be designed in collaboration with the affected population and community designed to integrate PHP, age and gender specificities. Training, and capacity reinforcement of the targeted population will be ensured. The intervention will consider potential challenges regarding access to local material, their effects on the environment, safeguard support from the authorities and existing community structures. The implementation of the project will allow integration of IDPs and host community, based on peace building. In complement and to ensure transparency, accountability, monitoring and evaluation, the monitoring and evaluation (M&E) strategy is based on the principles of accountability and community participation. At an institutional level, SI has an established Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policy.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Beyond program design or activity selection, certain choices will be made in order to minimize the protection risks during the implementation stage and to integrate protection. Persons of heightened risk – including female headed households, very large families, child headed-households, single women living alone, single parents or caregivers, individuals with medical conditions including physical and mental disabilities, survivors of GBV or trauma, and others, will be carefully identified through community consultations ensuring a “do no harm” approach. Implementation of the activities will be community-based, and sensitization will be tailored to support integration of vulnerable individuals and households. The Complaints Response Mechanism will be adapted to ensure that written, verbal, and confidential reports can be received regardless of access to mobile phone, charge, or literacy level.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

SI will participate in WFP’s Vulnerability-Based Targeting (VBT) exercise to ensure that food activities target not only IDPs but the most vulnerable displaced, returnee, host, and conflict-affected households. Based on the outcomes of the VBT exercise, SI will lead on identifying key vulnerability criteria, and conducting beneficiary identification and verification. SI encourages and facilitates people's participation at all stages of the project (initiation, selection of beneficiaries, implementation, and monitoring/evaluation). In addition, the establishment of a feedback mechanism for information, complaints and recommendations will allow the population to orient the intervention.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

All distribution sites will incorporate additional needs for those with disabilities or who require additional support, including hiring daily workers to support with carrying distributed food supplies. People with disabilities are consulted during project design to ensure siting and design of infrastructure are appropriate and user-friendly.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

SI projects are part of a logic of protecting environmental resources by promoting, through awareness-raising and training of beneficiaries, practices aimed at preserving the ecosystem.
