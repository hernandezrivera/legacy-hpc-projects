Project Summary

In line with findings from J-MSNA 2020 coordinated by ISCG, the project will focus on addressing the unmet needs in WASH sector for both targeted camp blocks and host communities. The proposed project timeframe of one year will enable Tearfund and its partner to embed long term change while adapting to the emerging and dynamic needs of these population groups in Cox’s Bazar.
As a result of ongoing Tearfund supported WASH interventions, one of the partners (CCDB) has been assigned as WASH Focal Organisations by UNHCR in Camp 1E, block- D and Camp 1W, block-G. In addition, also proposing to scale up its intervention to Camp 1E, block-A in the coming year 2021.
The project have prioritised women and girls involvement in design, management and monitoring. All interventions will follow ISCG-WASH sector and working groups’ guidelines. All beneficiary data disaggregated by age, gender and disability for informed decisions and decrease barriers to accessing services.
To address most vulnerable people WASH needs the project will mainly target women, adolescent girl, elderly and disable people. The project also focus on targeted entire blocks regular, sufficient and dignified access to WASH facilities for all age and gender groups through its planned interventions.
The project’s major activities includes; repair Tube well/water points, Installation of community water points in host community, repair latrines and bathing place, Latrine installation, Regular desludge Latrine pit, repair & maintenance of Faecal Sludge Management point, strengthen WASH management committee, Collect waste regularly from the targeted blocks, Drain repair, Maintenance of secondary waste dumping places, HHs and community waste bin distribution, Construction of waste dumping points, Family Hygiene Kit Distribution including MHM kit and Hygiene promotion session for community people.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar has been identified as one of Bangladesh’s “lagging districts” due to its underdeveloped critical infrastructure. These issues have been compounded by the influx of refugees fleeing from Rakhine State, Myanmar due to violence. An estimated 862,277 Rohingya refugees now reside in 34 camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts. Host communities are also affected by the crisis as many of the refugees have resided in these camps for over three years with no indication of an early return back to Myanmar. These factors contribute also to tensions between the refugees and host communities. The Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) 2020 preliminary findings identified gaps in access to services, in particular water, sanitation and hygiene. 12% of households reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs, only 60% of respondents (FDMN) were able to mention three critical times to wash hands. 27% of households (FDMN) reported having often or always found visible waste in the vicinity of their accommodation. Almost one fourth of households (Host) reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs. Achieving faecal sludge management (FSM) continues to remain a challenge, as only a limited number of agencies are undertaking interventions in FSM. A large number of latrines constructed inside the camps and host communities by other NGOs during an earlier emergency response phase are no longer usable due to lack of repair and maintenance. Tearfund WASH assessment in September 2020 identified some specific priority gaps, 84% of camp leaders interviewed felt that their latrines needed to be desludged. In addition, over 73% felt latrines needed to be repaired. Tearfund Gender Analysis Study 2020 identified some major concern around gender needs which includes; Women have to collect water from the different water sources. Walking on uneven surface to collect water is a risk, particularly, during the rainy season. Girls are often eve-teased on their way up and down. Women and girl not fully aware of basic hygiene like regularly washing hands with soaps and wear sandals. There is lack of safe bathing place for girls and women in the camps. While construction of latrine, the area of disability has not been taken into consideration. Insufficient light is a safety concern for women and girls at night-time. One of the recent project’s external evaluation commissioned by Tearfund also emphasized on continuation of regular operation and maintenance of WaSH structures both for FDMN community and host population. There is immense need to continue awareness on hygiene promotion for reducing the magnitude of waterborne diseases including current pandemic Covid-19.
