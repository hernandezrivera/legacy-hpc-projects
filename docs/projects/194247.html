Project Summary

Awerial County lies in the South Eastern part of Lakes state and have been one of the areas that received hundreds of thousands of people displaced by the 2013 and 2016 crises from Bor County of Jonglei State. Awerial county, given its proximity to Bor ounty experienced one of the biggest influx of civilians escaping the wrath of the conflict that had ravaged their villages and livelihoods hence the establishment of an Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) camp to provide for their humanitarian and security needs. The host communities and the returnees have been coexisting with the displaced population and have also benefited immensely from the support humanitarian partners deliver to the IDPs and returnees as part of DO NO HARM policy.
The county, inhabited by the pastoral community of Aliab section of Dinka, is one of the most underserved in terms of education facilities and most children are out of school but are instead found in the various cattle camps spread throughout the county. Based on UNESCO 2018 Report, there are about 2.2 million children out of school in South Sudan with Awerial county inclusive hence the serious need to enroll them back to school, especially those in the cattle camp, so as to achieve Sustainable Development Goal 4 which is, “Right to Inclusive and Equitable Quality Education for All”
HelpEducation South Sudan (HESS) has a strong operational presence at Awerial County where it is implementing Education Cannot Wait (ECW) project in consortium with Windle Trust International (WTI) and Community In Need Aid (CINA). The field office is headed by a County Education Coordinator who oversees the implementation of projects, coordination of activities as well as representation of HESS to the various platforms at the field level. In terms of effective contribution to the country program and county education department, HESS has been deputizing Finn Church Aid (FCA) as the County Education Cluster lead and does the coordination, chairing the County Education Cluster meetings and taking of minutes in the event that FCA was not present. At the national level, it is an integral member of the National Education Cluster, National Education Coalition (NEC) where it holds the treasury position as well as the South Sudan NGOs Forum.
This project, with funding from the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2022 will respond to the immediate education needs of the affected people who were displaced by the December, 2013 and July 2016 crises.
With the dilapidated classrooms, displaced and untrained teachers, low salaries, widespread poverty caused by war, gender discrimination practices of boychild preference to girls on access to education , early and child marriages, trauma associated with conflict and other harmful cultural practices, the future of education for the IDPs, host communities and returnees remains in dilemma.
However, with this proposal, HESS, with the County Education Department and other education stakeholders, will roll out the project in Awerial County of Lakes State targeting school going age children, parents and teachers, with increased access through construction of TLS, distribution of school supplies, training of volunteer teachers and paying them incentives, enrolling Out of School Children, training PTAs/SMCs and conducting psychosocial support training, Provision of WASH in school (Construction of gender-sensitive and inclusive pit latrines, drilling of boreholes and distribution of dignity kits to adolescent girls). The program, based on the above outlined activities envisages a multi-sectoral intervention aimed at achieving a wholly unified evidence based programming using education and schools as the entry points.
The County Education Coordinator, together with other field based staffs will ensure effective, timely and critical response to education needs for the IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities with constant field visit as well as reporting of the progress achieved against the targets.

1 - Needs

Awerial County with a severity level of 1 has one of the highest educational needs and other cross-cutting components that immensely contributes to the lifelong learning opportunities and success of the children.Based on the summary of this project, it is evidence that the county has one of the highest illiteracy rate owing to the huge proportion of children out of school who are instead involved in other activities majorly pastoralism and in rare cases, farming.From the list of counties shared with their various population in need, Awerial County has a total of 54,742 people in need (PiN) which is composed of the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), Returnees as captured on the assessment data shared to partners prior to the start of the proposal development process of which the project will target 18,932 people with educational services. And since HESS is the only organization actively implementing ECW project there, the level of need overwhelms the services delivered hence, this project will help to substantiate as well as ensure vast coverage of the various schools to ensure inclusiveness and representativeness with exception of accessibility challenges, caused by poor road networks, flood and insecurity, remaining the only hindrance to reaching out to the hard to reach areas.The war, having also had its share of the spoil have rendered some of the previously established school infrastructures like fences, classrooms and other facilities damaged and have instead exacerbated the fragility and the safety risk of those structures to house children during classroom learningAn educational need assessment conducted by HESS in July, 2020 covered 5 schools in the far North of Awerial Counties which included; Abuyung, Makur, Abuot-Bai, Alel and Mabor-Adhiek. The assessment report indicated that out of the five schools, only Abuyung had structures which were in bad shape and only needs renovation, however, the other 4 lacks classrooms with classes conducted under trees. With accessibility challenges, Alel and Abuot-Bai primary schools in Alel and Abuyung payams respectively are the only one accessible hence guaranteeing the construction of 1 TLS per school.Pastoralism rules everything and defines the livelihood dependency of the families and livestock is regarded of the highest value which explains why school going age children would get sent to the cattle camp to help in the various activities there such as; milking, cleaning of the animal wastes, herding, disinfection of the animals using ash, and cooking of food. Such activities are designed in a way that they draw all the teenage boys and girls from the villages into the cattle camp to take up the task.Traditional norms practiced value girls in terms of dowries hence all female children are viewed as sources of dowry hence riches to the family and such families endowed with many girls obviously flaunt their wealth in readiness for their daughters marriages, a factor that have continued to prohibit girls, and to some extent, boys access to learning. Boys, on the other hand, are sometimes given priority in terms of school access since they are viewed as the heirs to the family lineage hence the need to help them achieve their educational goals hence undermining the prospect of equal access to education for both boys and girls.Lack of qualified teachers and the displacement of the few who were running the schools have impacted negatively and have compromised the quality of teaching and learning hence the pupils’ incompetence.With Covid-19 emergence to the global scene, the need to improve water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in the school have been echoed further and amplified through various platforms and other media publications, especially at the onset of the school reopening following the one year span of lockdown which had tremendously weighed negatively on children' enrolment and retention. As a result of delayed resumption of schools, many teenage girls dropped out.

2 - Project activities

Based on the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2022 Sectoral Objectives, access, quality and strengthening the education system response capacity emerged out clearly and strongly as the basis from which activities would be formulated hence the project, through a well-designed logical framework will ensure that the 3 objectives must be satisfactorily met at its end so that the practical impact would be experienced in the life of the targeted population.In terms of improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for 18,932 emergency affected IDPs, returnees and host communities, HESS will construct 1 TLS at Alel primary school of Alel payam and another 1 TLS at Abuot-Bai of Abuyung payams due to their level of need and accessibility while renovating 1 TLS at Abuyung primary school of Abuyung payam. HESS will ensure that the construction and renovation takes into consideration the safety concerns in line with INEE standards to ensure that it friendliness to children is achieved.In a bid to ensure child friendliness of the learning spaces established, HESS through the project will purchase 100 customized desks for the 2 TLS constructed for proper spacing and sitting arrangement in light of the pupils teachers ration (PTR) as well as the social distancing protocol of Covid-19 mitigation. For the Early Childhood Education, HESS will purchase 20 rolls of propylene mats which will be used for their sitting.HESS, in conjunction with the County Education Department, PTAs/SMCs and other community leaders will mobilize the community so that the schools can enroll as many children as possible to schools. The program will also target young mothers who are willing to continue with their education through enrolling them in formal education setting or, in some cases, the alternative education system like the Accelerated Learning Program (ALP)In terms of capacity building, HESS through the PTAs and the community members will identify and train 30 (10 females) on basic pedagogical skills, Life Skills and Psychosocial Support (PSS) to equip them with the necessary skills of teaching as well as managing children affected by the negative impact of the crisis. Nonetheless, HESS will also conduct PTA training for 5 schools through formation of new bodies and strengthening the existing ones. The training will equip them on their roles in effective school monitoring and coordination as well as linkages with the communities. As part of the safety concern and accountability for affected population (AAP) HESS, will make sure that all teachers sign the Code of Conduct for Teachers in Emergencies to ensure that they understand their roles in fighting Gender Based Violence (GBV) related issues in the school to promote the protection of female learners from all sorts of exploitation by teachers.One key mainstreamed components that will strongly be featured in this project is WASH activities which plays an integral component in contributing to schools being safe. Under it, the project will construct 2 gender sensitive and inclusive pit latrine with a romp and changing room for disabled learners and girls respectively at Awerial Complex primary school and Door ALP centre with 100 learners. 2 gender-sensitive pit latrines will also be renovated at Manjur and Riaga primary schools. This will contribute to ensuring menstrual hygiene maintenance which will be further advanced through the distribution of dignity kits to 500 adolescent girls to improve on their attendance and retention even while on their cycle.With water as a lifesaving component, HESS will therefore drill 1 borehole at Abuot-Bai primary school in Abuyung payam and Mariik primary school of Puluk payam to improve access to pupils’ attendance and retention.Furthermore, the project will ensure that 5 schools will benefit from hand washing facilities. 3 Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns will be conducted on termly basis to mobilize children to get back to school.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HESS will work with the Child Protection Help Desk where beneficiaries will be encouraged to place their complains regarding any protection issue affecting them or their immediate loved ones. This will be done through a referral pathway mechanism which is swift and responds to beneficiaries concerns while taking into consideration their confidentiality.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HESS, through the project will ensure effective use of its Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy by staffs by ensuring constant refresher training on all its staffs on the policy including the child safeguarding policy of the organization to ensure strict observance and adherence to the policies while dealing with the beneficiaries.HESS will also use the existing community structures like the PTAs, SMCs as platforms for dissemination of such policies through incorporating it in all the manuals which makes it easier to reach out to the communities through them.The Child Protection Help Desk available in the area will also help in sensitizing communities through those accessing their services.HESS will use its developed reporting mechanism where staff and beneficiaries can report on while taking into consideration closer attention to it in order to protect those reporting from further harm by the perpetrators. HESS will also ensure that all its staff sign the Code of Conduct (CoC) as part of soliciting their cooperation and commitment towards ending PSEA and all other forms of abuse suffered by the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback collected through the suggestion box as part of the beneficiaries effort in having their concerns heard and incorporated will help the project managers in orienting their staffs on the arising issues of concern from the beneficiaries for immediate redress.In so doing, it makes it easier to align the implementation strategy to accommodate the beneficiaries' concerns which will make the project very responsive in terms of responding to the their concerns.Future similar projects targeting the same beneficiaries will greatly benefit from the wide range of beneficiaries' feedback collected as it will help in tailoring the activities and other aspects of the project to suit the local people since it is one way of carrying out context analysis for informed programming purpose.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

With this project approved, HESS, through its policies which includes the Child Safeguarding, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and other international instruments like the 9 Commitments of the Humanitarian Standard, The Convention on the Right of the Child (CRC) and the South Sudan Codes of Conduct for Teachers in Emergencies will employ serious strategies aimed at curbing and mitigating all sorts of abuse perpetrated by a person of power against the beneficiaries. Such actions will amount to dismissal as well as serious criminal investigation procedures which can lead to imprisonment.In order to mitigate all forms of risks associated with disempowerment of people of concerns that the project targets, like vulnerable women, girls, persons with disability (PWD) and other at-risk group who have been hampered vulnerable by the crisis, HESS will exploit the existing local structures on the ground through the use of PTAs and other community leaders in addressing the challenges identified as affecting the people identified.Alternatively, HESS, through the PTAs, local education actors and other humanitarian agencies will create awareness in the communities of Awerial County to enlighten them on their rights to free services rendered by the humanitarian organizations. They are also supposed to know their rights in being able to report any abuse experienced and perpetrated by aid workers in favor of a service delivered or denial of any beneficiary access to the services entitled to them. This way, they will be empowered to own the project, knowing that it was designed in light of their context and needs earlier assessed. It is also worth noting that all stages of project cycle must involve the local population to whom the project was meant for so that their voices are captured to include their needs based on their context.As part of the mitigation measures, HESS, will use the PTAs to inform them of the existing feedback mechanism for the beneficiaries to share their views on the progress to see whether or not it was addressing their needs. They will be empowered and enlightened on the available provisions of confidentiality to encourage them to have their say.HESS, will continue to use the PTAs and the community as well as church leaders to gather and solicit feedback on the status of the project.HESS, from the start of the project will conduct a meeting with the community to inform them of the project and all the activities that will come with it. Such meetings with the beneficiaries will be conducted throughout the project implementation period until at the end when an evaluation meeting will be conducted to enable the local people rate the performance of the project an its weaknesses.The early and child marriage challenges shall be addressed with the help of the response interventions that will demonstrate sensitivity to protection through project design, inter-sectoral approaches that mitigate protection risks through regular monitoring of education project implementation and assessing predicaments that school children are facing in the school and its surrounding environment including the communities.GBV cases like rape will be handled and addressed with the help of the law enforcement agencies present in the County and a swift and flexible strategy will be employed using the referral pathway to agencies implementing GBV response and prevention so that the survivors are availed with life saving services including medical attention and well designed Psychosocial Support (PSS) services to meet their mental health concerns resulting from the incident. Such processes must be facilitated with utmost adherence to confidentiality to protect the victims and survivors from exposure to further abuse and target by the perpetrators and their family members. The criminal, will be handled by the Police and legal procedures must lead to them being arraigned before the court of law where they would be judged and punished.
