Project Summary

The project aims to support 7,200 vulnerable beneficiaries from 1,200 HHs in Kahda, Baydhaba and Daynile district by providing Emergency shelter kits as well as non-food items (NFIs). The proposed intervention will aim to scale up the SNFI services in Human Appeal’s run IDPs camp in Kahda district in Mogadishu. Also, Human Appeal will conduct camp decongestion by establishing new campon the overcrowded camps where make-shift shelters are scattered in the settlements. This means that HA will provide emergency shelter kit and NFIs. in both districts of Kahda and Daynile. As part of the project interventions, Human Appeal will be supporting 7,200 vulnerable IDPs in Kahda, Baydhaba and Daynile with Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs). Similarly, the project will ensure that 10,800 vulnerable beneficiaries from 10,800 HHs are provided with Core relief items/NFIs including plastic sheets, jerry cans, blankets, mosquito net, kitchen set, sleeping mat, and solar lamp. Similarly, 600 HHs who are currently living in HA’s IDP camp who will receive 600 Emergency shelter kits.
The project will ensure the NFIs & ESK kits distributed are in line with cluster guidelines and standards to ensure habitable covered living spaces that can provide healthy and acceptable living environment and enabling the beneficiaries’ privacy, dignity & protection. Throughout the project period, target beneficiaries will be registered by using the online KOBO data collection tool for the purpose of ensuring structured data management, ease access and better transparency.
Human Appeal will also arrange communal training on camp coordination and camp management for the camp leaders and community representatives to build/ improve their capacity in regard to the management and maintenance of the camp facilities and infrastructure (Including and is not limited to health, sanitation and water). Similarly, target beneficiaries will be mobilized on awareness against the spread of COVID-19 pandemic where the outbreak of the second wave has escalated. Human Appeal will develop training manuals for its participants with reference to SNFI/CCCM clusters guidelines and SPHERE standards on settlement planning. It will also be coordinated with the relevant clusters and government authorities including district and regional administrations.
The project will also entail advocacy for the use of land tenure agreements through awareness raising and capacity building of the relevant stakeholders (government, landlords, beneficiaries and community leaders) on land tenure agreements, land disputes and eviction policies. Trainings for persons of concern on building their own homes will also be provided to promote community engagement and self-reliance.
The project will ensure total community participation of the beneficiaries’ assessment and design stages of the project to ensure that the beneficiaries are part of the decision making. Persons living with disabilities will be prioritized in the decision making to ensure that the project demonstrates the needs on the ground and also provision of adequate support & for securing their feedback. To ensure effective participatory approach, HA Somalia will organize community leader during the project design to identify the needs and propose the appropriate response for them.
A comprehensive monitoring and evaluation plan will be developed, including a baseline survey, technical tracking sheets for distributions, post distribution monitoring, weekly/monthly progress reports, monthly financial reports & a project completion report. After the provision of the NFIs to the targeted population, a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercise will be conducted to inform and assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the intervention. Furthermore, a Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) will also be conducted to assess the quality and standard of the shelters constructed.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Human Appeal prefers Cash transfer than in-kind due to the following points that are used to support the choice for Cash Transfer. • Flexibility and choice: Using cash or vouchers recognizes that beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their households need, and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities. • Dignity: Cash transfers allow the beneficiaries to control what goods and services their households need and the beneficiaries consider this a dignified modality for supporting them. • Empowerment: The availability of cash gives households a sense of restored power over their immediate situation. In addition, there is evidence that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household. Access to financial structures such as bank accounts may empower beneficiaries, in particular women, in the longer-term as well. • Supports or revitalizes local trade: Injections of cash or the use of commodity vouchers with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation can potentially also provide a link between relief and longer-term economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savings. • Reduces negative coping strategies: In the modern world, most households depend on cash to some extent. When aid is given in kind, households are often forced to seek cash in ways that may be detrimental to longer-term livelihoods, for example selling productive assets, or giving children to the care of relatives. Evidence shows that cash transfers reduce the negative coping strategies used by beneficiaries. • Multiplier effects: By stimulating local markets, cash based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend. • Time and cost efficient for agencies and participants: When goods are available and accessible locally, cash or voucher-based interventions have the potential to be more rapid than bringing supply externally. When beneficiaries use local markets, agencies also eliminate logistical and import costs. However, the additional staff, security and logistical resources sometimes required to distribute cash or manage a voucher programme must also be factored in.
