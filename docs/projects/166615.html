Project Summary

The COVID-19 pandemic came at a time of high humanitarian needs in Zimbabwe battling recurring
WASH related disease outbreaks, such as cholera and typhoid, and the health sector experiencing
shortages in essential medicines and services. essential service systems -including for health, nutrition and WASH- were already strained pre-COVID-19 and will struggle to cope with additional pressures. Decreasing access to clean and safe water in both rural and urban areas is heightening the risk of the spread of COVID-19 in townships in urban centres due to crowded housing, poor sanitation, lack of water, immune-compromised populations due to HIV and lack of services. Decreasing access to clean and safe water in both rural and urban areas is heightening the risk of disease and malnutrition. Only 49.4% of water points across the country are fully functional, according to the October 2019 report from the Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS). Urban areas continue to suffer from overloaded and ageing water and sewage infrastructure. The situation is further compounded by the economic situation, which is limiting access to foreign exchange by urban local authorities to procure water treatment chemicals and maintain the existing water systems. As a result, the City of Harare is currently producing nearly 200 megalitres of water against a daily demand 1200 megalitres. This is likely to heighten the risk of communicable disease outbreaks to 3.7 million vulnerable people. Access to clean water and improved hygiene practises are the most effective ways of reducing the spread of COVID-19.
The country is experiencing an influx of people from neighbouring countries who are being put on
mandatory quarantine. Most quarantine centres have inadequate water supplies and other WASH
supplies. According to the Rapid Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment conducted by Oxfam at the
established Quarantine Centres in Bulawayo and Plumtree, the major challenges include limited
hand-washing facilities adjusted for the pandemic context like mechanised foot operated tippy taps and
elbow mixing taps as well as a lack of sanitizers, sodium hypochlorite and soap. Oxfam's target areas have
been hotspots for cholera and typhoid outbreaks and where people face challenges to access to water
as they sometimes go for weeks without accessing the commodity. These areas are highly vulnerable to
COVID-19 pandemic whose strategies of prevention is hinged on hygiene practices which were already
lacking in these areas. In addition, COVID-19 and the extended lockdown has increased the negative impact on the women's and girls, including gender-based violence. For survivors who have lost their jobs or have no income, leaving with an abusive partner becomes even more difficult during the COVID-19 pandemic owing to financial dependence. In addition, the COVID-19 pandemic makes it more difficult for survivors to seek and receive help, due to movement restrictions, limited availability and knowledge of services.
The intervention will target 167,054 COVID-19 affected population in rural areas (Bulilima, Chirumhanzu, Harare Rural, Mangwe) through setting up of hand washing stations at public places, mass media campaigns on COVID-19 prevention, support for the health system to ensure availability of basic water and sanitation services in the key prioritized health care facilities and providing access to critical hand-washing facilities including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers, WASH-related hygiene kits, also ensuring the field and front-line staff engaged in WASH humanitarian responses have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices like social distancing. The action will also mainstream protection and gender ensuring information support on referral pathways for women experiencing domestic violence.
