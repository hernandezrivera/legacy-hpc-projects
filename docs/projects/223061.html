Project Summary

This project aims to significantly improve water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions in Rohingya camps and host community locations, particularly addressing the challenges of safe drinking water access. This project is a co-funding initiative with UNICEF's regular fund where CARE is UNICEF's partner for LTA WASH project implementation in camps 15 and 16. CARE plans to establish water distribution network in these communities to enhance water accessibility.
The organization also emphasizes safe sanitation, providing technical support for decentralized desludging in densely populated camps and implementing mechanized sludge extraction to protect public health and the environment. Additionally, CARE is emphasizing to construct and maintain high-quality, safe, and secure latrines and bathing cubicles. The sanitation facilities are also designed incorporating the gender-sensitive consideratins to reduce gender-based violence risks. This includes installation of latrines with Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities, using gender markers and featuring private washing areas and disposal units, are integral to this initiative.
CARE gives importance on imrpoving personal and community hygiene practices. More efforts will be given on maintaining regular hygiene practices at their daily life, which includes maintaining WSP practices to make water safe both at source and HH level, keep their latrines clean inside and outside, strengthening handwashing practices at five critical time, disposal and segregating the HH solid wastes properly, etc. Importance of handwashing greatly learned from COVID-19 becomes crucial as regular practice as part of the hygiene promotion.
Another crucial element is strengthening WASH committees by enhancing their skills in maintenance, water safety, and facility management, and training community leaders to disseminate effective messages and promote behavior change. CARE emphasizes community engagement, organizing local-level activities to heighten health and hygiene awareness and encourage participatory efforts.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the ISNA 2024 report, 26% of households still rely on tube-wells with hand-pumps as their primary source of drinking water. Many of these tube-wells provide shallow, contaminated water and were constructed during the emergency phase. Additionally, 6% of households have private piped water connections, which could pose a risk of diarrheal diseases if the water is unsafe. While technical solutions (installation of new water distribution network) may be the right answer in certain situations, CARE will focus on "community engagement" and "hygiene promotion" to be effective. These approaches address root causes and ensure sustainable improvements.Many households face challenges accessing adequate sanitation, relying heavily on communal facilities. Limited land for latrine construction in camps and the poor condition of existing latrines, built during the initial influx and now requiring significant repair, are major concerns for all age groups (children, adolescents, adults, elderly men and women). A WASH sector analysis indicates that, although overall latrine coverage meets the standard ratio of 1:20, achieving this is difficult in 13 of 33 camps, including Camp 15, due to high population densities. Many latrines, constructed in 2018, need major rehabilitation or reconstruction.The J-MSNA report from November 2023 reveals key statistics about water and sanitation in the camps. In terms of water supply, 59% of households in Camp 15 and 77% in Camp 16 rely on tap stands for drinking water. Additionally, 88% in Camp 15 and 92% in Camp 16 reported having sufficient water. The average daily water requirement per person is 21 liters in Camp 15 and 20 liters in Camp 16. With UNICEF funding, CARE has started constructing a new water distribution network and upgrading three WDNs but still needs further investment to meet requirements.Regarding safety and sanitation, 59% of women in Camp 15 and 71% in Camp 16 feel secure using communal latrines at night. However, only 1% in Camp 15 and 4% in Camp 16 have private latrines. Around 81% of households in Camp 15 and 85% in Camp 16 practice waste segregation. The distribution of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits shows disparity: 67% of households in Camp 15 and 45% in Camp 16 received kits in the past six months.Presently, CARE is working on a UNICEF-funded project conducting regular repair and maintenance. However, there are around 1,300 old latrines and bathing cubicles requiring rehabilitation. While private bathing spaces have increased, many are poorly maintained and misused, posing public health risks. Women and girls, particularly, face overcrowding, lack of privacy, and harassment in public facilities, preferring makeshift private spaces in shelters.These conditions underline the need for new latrines and maintenance of existing facilities, including fecal sludge management units. Another concern is solid waste management; over half of households use designated disposal areas, with the rest dumping waste in open spaces, creating unsanitary conditions.Promoting hygiene is vital for preventing diseases, especially in densely populated areas. Despite awareness and access to soap, changing behaviors remains challenging. Women face barriers accessing MHM resources due to social stigma and lack of tailored kits. Inadequate sanitation, poor hygiene practices, and these barriers increase vulnerabilities, emphasizing the need for holistic WASH interventions.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project incorporates protection mainstreaming as a key component, focusing on the welfare and safety of all community members, particularly those most vulnerable. Emphasizing the importance of gender-sensitive approaches, the project utilizes CARE's "Women Lead in Emergencies" methodology. This approach empowers women’s groups to take active roles in decision-making and leadership within WASH-related activities. These groups will identify, create, and implement action plans to address specific protection needs within their communities. Joint safety audits by the WASH and protection teams are critical to this strategy. These audits assess potential risks and barriers in accessing WASH facilities and are essential for adapting WASH safety tools and procedures to effectively mitigate protection risks within the community.In addition, the project focuses on the coordination and distribution of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and materials. Working closely with shelter/NFI teams and protection actors, the project ensures these resources are readily available and accessible, prioritizing privacy and convenience. This effort includes careful planning to avoid duplication between different types of aid kits, such as dignity kits and emergency kits.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This project prioritizes the needs of individuals with disabilities and those of different age groups, ensuring inclusive and accessible WASH facilities. Training for the WASH team will include a focus on addressing the unique challenges faced by these groups, enhancing their ability to provide safe and effective services.Joint safety audits, involving the WASH team, will identify and assess barriers to accessing WASH facilities for people with disabilities and those of various age groups. These audits will inform the adaptation of WASH safety audit tools to ensure they are inclusive and cater to the diverse needs of the community.In collaboration with shelter/NFI teams and protection actors, the project will ensure the accessibility and appropriateness of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and materials, with a special focus on the requirements of women and girls with disabilities. The distribution of these resources will be managed to respect privacy and ease of access, especially for those with mobility issues.To avoid duplication of resources and ensure efficient service delivery, the project will maintain close collaboration with relevant actors and focus points, ensuring that the specific needs of people with disabilities and different age groups are effectively met.Finally, the project commits to adhering to best practices in the design and operation of WASH facilities, prioritizing accessibility, and inclusivity. This includes setting aside specific hours at water points for the exclusive use of people with disabilities, ensuring that these facilities are accessible and safe for all community members, regardless of age or disability.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CARE Bangladesh has established a comprehensive and efficient Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) across all humanitarian projects in Cox’s Bazar to maintain accountability. This system encompasses a wide range of channels, both solicited and unsolicited, for receiving information, recommendations, feedback, opinions, questions, complaints, or requests from various groups including beneficiaries, stakeholders, staff, volunteers, government agencies, and other organizations. Key channels include feedback and information centers, info hubs, hotlines, mobile CFRM teams, community meetings, complaint boxes, household visits, and focus group discussions. CARE ensures that all members of the affected community have equal access to this safe and responsive CFRM, irrespective of their sex, age, disability, race, caste, or color. The system is managed and overseen by a dedicated focal person, supported by a specialized CFRM team in each project. This team, comprising CARE staff and volunteers, is responsible for issue collection and field-level coordination. The CFRM team is committed to addressing all received issues promptly, depending on their nature and urgency. On average, CARE handles about 3000 to 5000 issues monthly, successfully resolving 2000 to 3000 of these cases. Accountability focal points regularly share CFRM reports with various donors and projects, providing updates on a weekly, monthly, or quarterly basis, as required.To efficiently collect and manage CFRM data, CARE employs a variety of platforms and tools. These include KOBO, ODK, Activity Info, CFP, and MODA, among others. Staff and volunteers are well-trained in these platforms, ensuring the effectiveness and reliability of the CFRM system.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

This project is committed to integrating Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into its WASH interventions by addressing various gender-related issues, vulnerabilities, and opportunities. At the onset or during the program's implementation, a rapid gender analysis will be conducted or existing data will be analyzed, with a specific focus on gender concerns. The project will regularly collaborate with the Gender and Protection sector to provide gender-related technical support for the implementation strategy. This collaboration will include capacity training for project staff and volunteers on identifying gender-related challenges in project interventions, guided by the technical expertise of the Gender and Protection sector.The project aims to efficiently address and overcome gender barriers, enhancing the reach and effectiveness of its activities. Crucial to this effort is ensuring women's meaningful participation in community-level groups and adopting CARE’s Global Gender in Emergencies framework as a gender mainstreaming approach.Accessibility of WASH facilities for all genders, persons with disabilities, and other diverse groups (including LGBTI individuals) is a key goal. This will be achieved by establishing gender-segregated latrines, disability-friendly latrines and bathing facilities, and accessible water points. The project has already made significant strides in PSEA training, having facilitated basic PSEA training for 430 volunteers, laborers, and 64 vendors, in addition to refresher training. Furthermore, a group of 12 Master trainers specializing in PSEA has been prepared, who have conducted these training sessions for the community.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Through WASH Project CARE Bangladesh WASH team will consider water network construction, Fecal Sludge Treatment Plant, Solid waste management Plant operation, Upgrade latrine and Bathing cubicles construction, Menstrual Hygiene Management Block and Persons with Disability WASH facilities construction concerning the environmental and social hazards. For site selection of WASH facilities Physical, social barriers will be considered to overcome the risks. Strong slopes for latrine, Bathing, water distribution network and high protection of solid waste management plant, Fecal Sludge treatment plan will be considered during operation and maintenance. At the same time community mobilization will continue to cope up the climate change actions accordingly. This project emphasizes DRR/DRM and climate change mitigation measures. Cox’s Bazar, prone to extreme weather events such as heavy rainfall, landslides, and flash floods, has become even more susceptible due to environmental strain from the influx of Rohingya refugees. The consequent reduction in natural vegetation has negatively impacted soil and water quality, further diminishing the region's resilience to natural disasters.To address these challenges, the WASH team will work closely with the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) team to engage in monsoon preparedness interventions. These efforts will include measures such as securing and tying down structures to withstand extreme weather events. By collaborating with the DRR team, the WASH team aims to enhance the resilience of the infrastructure and the community against climate-related risks.These coordinated efforts are crucial in mitigating the impact of climate change and natural disasters in one of the country's most vulnerable regions. The focus will be on not only protecting the environment but also ensuring the sustainability and effectiveness of WASH services in the face of these increasing challenges.
