Project Summary

The insecurity and unpredictable volatile situation, pandemics combined by climatic shocks have exacerbated the already fragile education system in the country. Children who do access to education in Somalia face tremendous challenges that hinder to realize their full potential. In a context like Somalia where teachers are under qualified, classrooms are overcrowded, and materials and facilities are scarce, teachers are often using verbal or physical punishment of children to maintain control of the classroom. This behavior affects children negatively and hampers their learning outcome and well-being. Additionally, barriers to access to education for children with disabilities are considerable. The low enrolment of children living with disabilities can be explained negative attitude towards children with disabilities and unavailability of favorable learning environment compounded by untrained teachers on inclusive education. Schooling provides essential learning and when schools close, children and youth are deprived opportunities for growth, development and cognitive. The disadvantages are disproportionate for under-privileged learners particularly girls, IDP and Rural children who tend to have fewer educational opportunities beyond school. According to JMCNA findings the regions with the highest proportion of school-aged children in need of humanitarian assistance are in Southern and Northeastern areas. In Bari region, 63% of school-aged children in Qardo, 62% in Qandala and 61% Calula.
TASS proposes to address this gap through an integrated approach by ensuring that 5,918 (3,254 boys and 2,664 girls) school-children and particularly girl child and children with disabilities are able to access quality education in schools through the provision of inclusive basic education package in a protective learning environment combined with life-saving activities such as: Construction or rehabilitation of gender/disability friendly latrines with hand washing facilities, Provision of sanitary materials to adolescent girls, School equipment and material learning distribution, Incentive for teachers, Water distribution in schools, School feeding: Take-home food, Psychosocial support for learners, CEC training and Teachers Training-Pedagogy- additionally during construction or rehabilitation of Classrooms and latrines TASS will take into consideration children with disabilities through creating rams to enable access both classrooms and latrines hence of the 5,918 targeted children 591(326 boys and 265 girls) will be children with disability.
Despite the advantages female education brings (increased economic productivity, increased awareness of rights, improved maternal health, and reduced infant mortality and fertility rates), girls face a unique set of barriers that prevents them from going to school, including many cultural and economic hurdles. Many families, when faced with costly school fees, choose to educate boys rather than girls as the girl will eventually join the home of her husband and so the parents do not expect to see the benefits of her education. Other reasons cited by Somali parents and teachers for gender disparities in education are: the inadequate number of female teachers, lack of gender-segregated latrines, and inaccessible schools amidst conflict and heightened insecurity. The project will, through its community sensitization activities, will specifically promote the benefits and value of educating girls, to boost the enrollment and retention of girls in school. This will be complemented by girls involvement in school clubs, particularly girls forums, and the provision of sanitary kits to girls as they will often not attend school during menstruation. The project will also take ensure the inclusion of female teachers and female CEC members in capacity building the activities to ensure the female voice is heard and ensure their involvement is productive and not just tokenistic.

Project Activities

Cluster Objective 1: Ensure emergency and crises affected children and youth have access to a safe and protective learning environment: •Activity 1.1.1: Psychosocial support for learners through recreational activities, e.g. sports additionally children with protections concern will receive both individual and group counselling through trained Psycho-social counselor. •Activity 1.1.2: Construction or rehabilitation of 30 gender/disability friendly latrines with hand washing facilities in 15 targeted schools: Cluster Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion • Activity 2.1.1: School equipment and material learning distribution (TLM) for 5,918(3,077 boys and 2841 girls) students • Activity 2.1.2: Incentive for teacher: 60 (36 Male and 24 Female) teachers are targeted by this action to receive a monthly teacher incentive of 100 USD. • Activity 2.1.3: Water distribution in schools: Water will be supplied to 15 schools through water trucking at a rate of 3 liters/day/student for 5,918(3,077 boys and 2841 girls) children • Activity 2.1.4: School feeding: 5,918(3,077 boys and 2841 girls) school-children will benefit from school feeding; • Activity 2.1.5: Provision of sanitary materials to girls: 1,800 adolescent girls will be provided with sanitary kits to maintain hygienic condition as well as to attend school without any disruption.Cluster Objective 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies preparedness and response within the education system • Activity 3.1.1: Teacher training – pedagogy-60 (36 Male and 24 Female) teachers will be trained on managing education in crises to ensure that learning is not interrupted during conflict or in times of crises, including child rights, child protection, water management, hygiene and sanitation and psycho-social support • Activity 3.1.1: CEC training: 60 (30 male, 30 female) Community Education Committees (CECs) of the 15 targeted schools will be trained.

Mitigation Measures

To provide holistic support for the learners, the project will be mainstreamed with the protection projects implemented by TASS and other partners in the targeted regions. The project will renovate learning spaces and Latrines into Gender/Disability friendly through creating rams to enable them access both classrooms and latrines in order to provide adequate protection and safety in Schools. TASS in collaboration with the MOEHE Puntland and the community will conduct regular sensitization on the abolish of child negative disciplinary methods including child beating. All teachers will be trained on alternative and positive disciplinary approaches. In addition, teachers, CEC members and children (child clubs) will be trained on child protection and referral mechanisms. All teachers will be trained in the provision of psychosocial and psychological first aid support. Proper feedback mechanisms will be set in place to continuously improve on the quality of programming and ensure that TASS is responsive to the community needs. Additionally, TASS will establish complaints and feedback boxes in all schools to ensure feedback and complains from the children are captured hence Complaints Response and Feedback mechanism with a hotline is already available for use. The use of this system will be enhanced by making beneficiaries more aware of its existence in the proposed project locations.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed project will support IDP's children in accordance with the Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) framework and principles on durable solutions for internally displaced populations, which commends that IDP's should not be discriminated, a minimum access to basic education among other rights, also as part of building their resilience in the face of the challenges they face. Prior to the project implementation, TASS will conduct project kick off meeting for the stakeholders and the beneficiaries in order to explain the project, clarify deliverables, scope, duration and the donor so as to have common understanding of the project and the mechanism to share their concerns and complaints on the project with TASS. Not only this informing sharing will strengthen the communities’ and stakeholders’ position to held TASS accountable but will also enhance the community ownership and sustainability of the project gains once the intervention comes to an end. TASS will diligently work with the Ministry of Education, communities, and education actors to ensure successful implementation of the project, build ownership and ensure sustainability of the schools after the project ends. Throughout the project period and stages, the ministry of Education will play key roles in the project. TASS will sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the MOEs which clarifies the cooperation between the two parties in the project implementation and each party’s role and responsibility. The MOEs will support the effective management and implementation of the project through provision of technical support, guidelines, training manuals etc., recruit the teachers, supervise the schools and serve as focal point for coordination and the developing strategic areas of education including development and application of strategies, guidelines and curriculum. The Puntland MOE will also be responsible for the disbursement of the teachers’ incentives transferred to the ministry by TASS on monthly basis through agreed Money transfer. The communities will also participate in the project planning, implementation, monitoring and review meeting. Coordination and information sharing with the clusters and partners working in the target areas will be given utmost priorities to synergize the project.
