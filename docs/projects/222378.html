Project Summary

Multiple shocks in Jonglei (Ayod, Fangak, Uror, Nyirol, Renk and Nasir) have detrimentally affected living standards mostly affecting women, girls, boys and persons with special needs. WASH needs are high in the counties targeted as a result of low levels of availability of clean and safe water, access challenges, poor infrastructural designs, protection concerns amongst other concerns. Sanitation coverage and practices are low; Hygiene behaviors contradict best practices, mostly driven by culture and norms. Behavioral change is key to fostering acceptable hygiene practices and improving sanitation amongst mostly agropastoral communities. Low WASH standards impact health, nutrition, food security, education and protection services; extremities spreading across multi-sectoral indicators. The project seeks to provide much needed WASH services to mainly IDPs, returnees, host communities in the counties of Ayod, Fangak, Uror, Nyirol, Renk and Nasir with emphasis on women, girls, boys and persons with special needs. CMD will provide both institutional and community support through the rehabilitation/setup of water points, rehabilitation/setup of sanitation facilities, participatory hygiene promotion activities, distribution of NFIs, community led WASH durable solutions in the areas of sanitation, hygiene and environmental health management. Safety audits as well as AAP protocols will be adhered to throughout the implementation of the project. A total of 18,246 people will be reached through this project; (60% female, 40% male); 45% of the persons targeted will be IDPs; 30% vulnerable host communities, 25% of these returnees in the counties of Ayod, Fangak, Uror, Nyirol, Renk and Nasir.

1. Needs

Jonglei and Upper Nile states exhibit some of the lowest standards of living in South Sudan mainly attributed to limited/lack of basic service provision as a result of insecurity, inaccessibility, erratic weather patterns, displacement amongst other factors. Latest 2024/25 Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) figures indicate spiraling levels of food insecurity and malnutrition. 2025 Q1 projections lie within IPC 4 (emergency phase) for the counties of Ayod, Fangak, Uror, Nyirol, Renk and Nasir. Poor WASH standards contribute to high levels of food insecurity, high disease burdens, malnutrition and GBV. (UNICEF, 2024). Less than 20% of the populations in the counties targeted have access to safe and clean water from a protected water source. Difficulty in securing enough water is compounded by the low coverage of water infrastructure, with over 30% of the households surveyed during the last FSNMS relying on surface water as their main source of water. Availability of WASH NFIs is low, less than 19% of HHs having at least one Jerrycan. Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption, coupled with high levels of open defecation, thereby creating substantial public health risk especially in areas prone to flooding as latrines seep into waterways. IDPs and returnee populations especially from Sudan and neighboring counties have created above capacity demands for WASH services, thereby stretching the ability of communities to withstand shocks. Economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of WASH services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of infrastructure. Access to water is affected by insecurity, over 20% of HHs feeling unsafe while fetching water. Women and girls bear most of the responsibility of availing water for both domestic and livelihoods use, depriving girls time for school, and lactating women time to breastfeed and nurse infants. Livelihood opportunities are further stifled as a result of this. Sexual assaults occur amongst rural communities to females in their quest for water. Latrine access is less than 60% in Jonglei and Upper Nile, as a result of long-lasting development challenges. Cultural stereotypes make use of latrines in some locations a taboo especially amongst rural pastoral communities. Floods have destroyed latrines, and lead to frequent population displacement making communities hesitant to invest in sanitation facilities. Hygiene standards are compromised by behavioral patterns, lack of soap and water. Average price of soap rose by over 35%. These directly impact public health outcomes. High cases of AWD in Jonglei and Upper Nile contribute to high levels of malnutrition especially amongst PLW and U5s. Cholera, MPOX, HEV outbreaks in Jonglei and Upper Nile are all linked to low WASH standards. Provision of WASH services is critical to alleviate suffering and provide lasting solutions aimed at improving living standards amongst rural and semi-urban populations.

2. Project activities

Immediate WASH interventions are planned for targeted counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile states that will include but not limited to; Conducting of Community safety audit assessments before any WASH intervention to help to identify safety issues and generate solutions to improve the safety of the community; Rehabilitation/Repair of broken water points in areas deemed most vulnerable; Conduct water quality testing for all the water points; Provision of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits to women of reproductive age and adolescent girls; Upgrade of boreholes to solar powered water supply system; Training of water management committees to support in the management of the community water points; Training of Hand pump Mechanics to support in the technical sustainability of the community water points; Conduct hygiene promotion; construction of emergency flood resilient community shared and institutional latrines in IDPs concentrated areas; Conduct community feedback meetings throughout the project implementation period to collect feedback from the community, including concerns, suggestions, and complaints; Monitoring and supportive supervision to the project sites by the technical project staff
