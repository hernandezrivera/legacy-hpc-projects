Project Summary

The action proposed by AVSI is focusing on measures capable of coping with the situation of displacement and spontaneous return in South-Rural Aleppo, Zarbah district, in Syria caused by exposure to conflict. The intervention is in line with HRP objective one in providing life-sustaining humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people and objective two in enchanting the prevention and mitigation of protection risks.
With this proposal, AVSI is seeking the protection of civilians through a comprehensive response based on the specific needs in the area, on the lessons learned of previous actions and benefitting also from the coordination and complementarity with existing projects put in place by the partners in the same geographical area. The project wants to answers to the basic needs of the population through an unconditional cash assistance in order to ensure that the families will be able to cope with their basic needs after the return, to support families they have lost their income and at great risk of negative coping mechanism, to support IDPs and separated members of HH, to provide an opportunity to cover protection gaps in age-gender-disability sensitive households, to support GBV cases, etc. . In collaboration with Syrian Commission for Family and Population Affairs (SCFPA), the cash assistance will be completed by GBV actions, PSS activities, safe identification and referral and mine awareness. All activities will be implemented following Covid-19 prevention measures. This intervention has been planned with the active participation of the target community through an assessment conducted in January 2021. The action will be directly managed and implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Social Affairs and Syrian Commission for Family and Population Affairs (SCFPA), the latter already guaranteeing to AVSI the access to the area.

4 - Needs

After ten years of crisis in Syria, the economic situation has seen an accelerated deterioration during last months, due to the regional financial crisis and COVID 19. In Syria, the people in need reached 13.4 million (HNO 2021), 2.7 in Aleppo area. The targeted locality of Al Zarbah has been identified in collaboration with the local Protection cluster. The on field assessment of AVSI and SCFPA has been conducted in January 2021 on people residing in Zarbah and in the surrounding villages. The unemployment rate is very high. By reading the data and listening to the target group through field visits, most of the needs were mainly material, protection, psychological support, in addition to some medical conditions. The rapid deterioration of the economic situation and the suspension of the main community protection services have put these groups in additional risks, bringing them to resort to negative coping mechanism, marginalization, impossibility to procure food items as well as hygiene or dignity products, household tensions, GBV, etc. The most important social needs evidence by our assessment is linked to early marriage, female breadwinners and the large number of pregnant women. The vast majority of returnee population is in need of an economic support in order to have the possibility to stay and restart their life in their place of origin. The risk of mine remains high according to the multi-sectorial assessment conducted by the protection sector partners (UN Interagency mission report to Zarbah 11/2020). The AVSI-SCFPA assessment has also recorded the presence of different persons with special needs and and no specific services for people with disabilities are provided. We already identified 1258 HH in need.

5 - Implementation of activities

The following activities are planned:- Community focus groups/outreach activity to better define activities, criteria and campaigns- Emergency unconditional unrestricted cash transfer for vulnerable households/individuals (3 distributions planned for 1258 households) according to CVA sub-group indications and MEB to ensure that selected households will be able to cope with their basic needs after the return, to support families they have lost their income and at great risk of negative coping mechanism, to support IDPs and separated members of HH, to provide an opportunity to cover protection gaps in age-gender-disability sensitive households, to support GBV cases, etc.- Training in PSS/GBV to social operators and volunteers- Community PSS for women (focus on GBV, women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls) in Women Friendly Spaces with referral cases to UN/SCFPA (Protection/GBV) and AVSI Open Hospitals project Aleppo (Health) and case management- Distribution of women and adolescent dignity kits according to content of UNFPA (including Covid19 PPE and awareness material)- Local awareness campaigns on Protection/GBV (early marriage, prevention of GBV, etc.)- Training on mine awareness to social operators and volunteers in collaboration with UNMAS- Local awareness campaign on mine awareness together in collaboration with UNMAS- Specific mine risk education meetings with community leaders, teachers, farmers, etc. .

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

AVSI identified and assigned context-specific weights to protection risks in terms of safety and dignity, access (both as the possibility for the real most vulnerable to access the project as generally the beneficiaries' access to the forms of aid suggested), data protection, risk of GBV, people with specific needs, social relations, for this specific action. To mitigate safety and dignity risks, we develop complaints mechanisms for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries (specific telephone), two-way feedback mechanisms between community and AVSI and its partner SCFAP, providing clear and written information about that. We involve in all the phases of the project local communities, beneficiaries and we use different channels and referral to identify protection cases, and we ensure qualified PSS and GBV staff. We design the intervention with flexibility in service delivery (including home delivery), we discuss with local stakeholders and partners in order to follow specific vulnerability aimed to not leave anyone excluded. To reduce privacy risks, data protection policy on the identity of beneficiaries will be in place (not disclosure of information), beneficiaries will sign consent forms, and contracts with service providers include provisions in line with data protection policy, modality of cash transfer, which allow more privacy, preferred to other forms like vouchers. The distribution of in-kind assistance will be done with reduced number of persons daily (for privacy and Covid19 reasons) and with just the necessary staff. Moreover, to mitigate the risks linked with social relations, well-designed eligibility criteria and targeting based on context, community inputs, evidence, and objectives of transfers are in place.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

There are clearly risks of PSEA which have been considered. AVSI is ensuring that all staff and volunteers involved in this program are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Child Safeguarding polices, and the internal reporting mechanisms for CP and SEA cases, through the mandatory annual trainings and the signature of the code of conduct and the SG bulletin. AVSI has worked on enhancing its internal PSEA minimum standards through participation in trainings, coaching provided by UNICEF in 2020. For this project AVSI will reinforce field monitoring, outreach activities, case managers' presence and work (including reporting and referrals), awareness among personnel, beneficiaries and local populations regarding PSEA.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumptions are that the security situation in the area are not worsening and access will remain possible for INGOs as it is now. There are other risks linked with the financial situation, due to the possibility of difficulties in receiving funds from abroad, high possible inflation and volatility of the exchange rate. AVSI already mitigates these risks by changing international and local banks, by increasing local contingency funds and by accelerating procurement procedures. There are other risks linked with the action itself, mainly cash transfer, which can lead to fraud, or to create local inflation having a bad impact on local market, to possible reinforce local mafia type system, sharpening family clashes over the use of resources. For fraud, AVSI has clear structured policies and procedures to mitigate this risk (DIGE8/2020) with whistleblowing mechanisms and random monitoring. For market local impact of intervention, a proper initial market analysis followed by continuous monitoring with normal price fluctuation and advocacy with local authorities will alleviate the risks. The project will be well coordinated with the partner and local education authorities and actors, within the cluster Protection both at central and local level, with UNHCR and O CHA. Population have been already consulted and it will be regular consulted through regular meetings and specific M&E initiative (like focus groups).
