Project Summary

South Sudan became an independent nation on 9 July 2011. In December 2013, a fighting due to political power struggle broke out in Juba and spread across the states in the country. Since then, pockets of armed and tribal conflicts have continued in South Sudan and the food security and nutrition situations have deteriorated progressively since the conflict started in 2013, exacerbated by multiple displacements, economic crisis, isolation during rainy seasons due to impassable roads and high dependence on food imports.
As a result, traditional livelihood patterns have been disrupted by war, droughts and floods as well as pest infestations and hunger and malnutrition have risen to historic levels in 2017.
In Mundri East County, food insecurity remain high and is expected to worsen due to continued insecurity, which persisted since 2015, causing massive displacements, loss of lives and properties and prevented local farmers in Mundri East from accessing their farms and disrupted existing sources of livelihoods. These fragile situations have limited household access to food, schools and basic health and nutrition services.
In Nagero County, the recent multiple displacements due to conflicts between the government and opposition forces from 2015 - 2018 and inter-communal conflicts with the Bazia community IDPs from Nagero County and the Kuarjina cattle keepers of Wau State have triggered new wave of forced displacements. These repeated displacements have led to extended disruption of livelihoods, loss of lives, destruction of schools and health facilities and personal properties. Nagero, Zomoi, Bandalla, Rengu and Kuro Payams currently host over an estimated 6,500 returnees who have limited humanitarian assistance since the start of their voluntary and conflict-induced returns in March and June respectively.
As a result, Mundri East and Nagero Counties are facing deteriorating food security situations. And these are critical short-term and long-term indicators on whether households can cope, recover and build their resilience’s to shocks, reduce their susceptibilities to common infectious diseases and retain their children in schools and ensure they continue to learn. Therefore, in line with the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster priorities, AMRES seeks to provide nutrition-sensitive food assistance to 22,000 and 18,000 food insecure households of IDPs, Returnee IDPs and vulnerable host community populations at risk of severe food insecurity (IPC phases 3 and 4) who are affected by the fighting between government and opposition forces and recent inter-communal conflict, in Mundri East and Nagero Counties respectively. This proposed Food Assistance and livelihood support in Mundri East and Nagero Counties will therefore enable vulnerable households, including IDPs, returnee IDPs and host communities affected by shocks meet their minimum levels of food security through the provision of unconditional food assistance and support the restoration of livelihoods and improve household food security through the creation of community assets with food and cash transfer support; enhance community cohesion; reduce food insecurity and promote school enrolment and retention of girls and boys in WFP-assisted schools; and support skills training to establish and rebuild livelihoods through creation of sustainable community assets and increase local production capacities, quality and access to markets through SSHRF procurement and strengthen the skills and capacities of local farmers in Mundri East and Nagero Counties.
The specific project objectives will include:
1. To improve food consumption over assistance period for targeted households and/or individuals in Mundri East and Nagero Counties.
2. To restore or stabilize access to basic services and/or community assets, including community market infrastructure in Mundri East and Nagero Counties.

1 - Needs

In Mundri East County, food insecurity remain high and food security situation is expected to worsen due to continued insecurity in the County, which persisted since 2015, causing massive displacements, loss of lives and properties and prevented local farmers in Mundri East from accessing their farms and disrupted existing sources of livelihoods. Mundri East County also hosts returnee IDPs from the neighboring Counties and the continued insecurity is hindering their food production capacities and efforts. In Nagero County, the recent multiple displacements due to conflicts between the government and opposition forces from 2015 - 2018 and inter-communal conflicts with the Bazia community IDPs from Nagero County and the Kuarjina cattle keepers of Wau State have triggered new wave of forced displacements. These repeated displacements have led to extended disruption of livelihoods, loss of lives, destruction of schools and health facilities and personal properties. Nagero, Zomoi, Bandalla, Rengu and Kuro Payams currently host over an estimated 15,000 returnees who have limited humanitarian assistance since the start of their voluntary and conflict-induced returns in March and June 2019 respectively. Therefore, in line with the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster priorities, AMRES seeks to provide nutrition-sensitive food assistance to 22,000 and 18,000 food insecure households of IDPs, Returnee IDPs and vulnerable host community populations at risk of severe food insecurity (IPC phases 3 and 4) who are affected by the fighting between government and opposition forces and recent inter-communal conflict, in Mundri East and Nagero Counties respectively. The specific project objectives will include:1. To improve food consumption over assistance period for targeted households and/or individuals in Mundri East and Nagero Counties. Activity: Cash Voucher food assistance/general distribution for targeted households or individuals. AMRES will undertake interventions with the view to increase the scope of livelihoods and resilience activities in Mundri East and Nagero Counties and target food insecure households/individuals following shocks or displacement through refined targeting and regular prioritization exercises taking into consideration existing county-level food security and nutrition assessments (such as Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System surveys, and ad hoc Emergency Food Security Assessments or Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessments), food commodity availability and logistics and access constraints. Food assistance will comprise cereals (such as sorghum or maize), pulses (such as beans or lentils), fortified vegetable oil and salt, and will be calculated based on WFP recommended rations per person in subsequent distributions cycles. 2. To restore or stabilize access to basic services and/or community assets, including community market infrastructure in Mundri East and Nagero Counties.Activity: Food assistance for assets to severely food-insecure IDPs and host communities will meet their Short-term food needs while contributing to improved livelihoods through the creation of community assets and skills training. AMRES will also provide technical support and monitoring coverage, given its primary focus on addressing beneficiaries’ immediate food gap. Beneficiary households will receive 20-day ration of cereals, pulses, fortified vegetable oil and salt on a monthly basis for 12 months. Outcome 1: Stabilized or improved food consumption over assistance period for targeted households and/or individualsOutcome 2: Improved access to basic services, including community and market infrastructure. The project will provide emergency live saving food assistance through Food or Cash based Transfers for crisis affected households of 18,000 in Nagero and 22,000 in Mundri East: In Nagero to: 4,500 women, 3,400 men, 4,900 boys and 5200 girls and in Mundri East to: 5500 women, 5200 men, 5800 girls and 5500 boys.

2 - Activities

• Distribution of in-kind food assistance and cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/nutrition objectives to vulnerable child & women headed households, households headed by people with special needs and the elderly and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). • Distribution of agricultural inputs and tools to farmers to boost food production.• Provision of Nutrition vouchers to targeted households with reported cases of malnutrition.• Training of farmers, fishermen and livestock owners on modern farming techniques to improve their production and provision of fishing gears • Conduct routine livestock vaccination and disease prevention & treatment.• Distribution of conditional food assistance through food for asset (FFA) crop production programming. • Training farmers groups on food processing and support with kits to support value chain addition. • Training of local communities groups on production and use of improved mud stoves, the energy is always necessity for people for different purpose including cooking requirement, food insecurity is exacerbated when vulnerable population have limited access to cooking fuel. • Conduct routine participatory monitoring and evaluation of the project activities together with stakeholder across the project locations.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

AMRES will ensure the food assistance and livelihood support is provided on Do No Harm principles and assistance is provided in a respectful and dignified manner. AMRES staff will observe our code of ethics and ensure the rights of our beneficiaries are observed and recognized, including the rights of SGBVl survivors, mentally ill persons, women and girls, boys and men.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project is designed to respond to the needs of IDPs, Returnees IDPs and host communities that are stressed due to mutiple displacements as result of the conflicts. Project will provide safe and adequate nutritious food assistance and build their livelihoods through provision of agriculture inputs based on the community priorities and needs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, this food assistance and livelihood support is planned jointly with County Agriculture and Social well fare department; the County Relief and Rehabilitation Commission; local community leaders; and representatives of the returnee IDPs and other stakeholders to enhance community ownership and full participation. The stakeholders are the main planners that AMRES consults whenever the needs arises and will collaborate with other implementing partners of Food for Assets and Cash for food to improve the long term adaptive, adoptive and transformative capacity of the returnee IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Nagero County

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is strictly aligned to the FSL cluster objectives and priorities and Ministry of Agriculture strategies for increasing food production and alleviating hunger among vulnerable women, men and children in South Sudan. AMRES also aligned the project to its food security programme objectives aimed to provide adequate, safe and nutritious food assistance to food insecure households of IDPs, returnee IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Nagero County.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Nagero is currently relatively calm. This project will collaborate and be linked to the durable solutions for food security and sustainable livelihoods implemented by other food for asset and cash for asset partners. AMRES will work together with the County leadership, developmental partners and beneficiaries to solve emergency food security problems and other threatened sources of livelihoods and supplement existing development projects and allow timely preparation for long term resilience projects to take place so as to avert food insecurity emergencies in the future.Based on the Do no Harm principle, AMRES will use the livelihood and food assistance based activities to enhances peaceful co-existence among returnee IDPs and host communities and strengthen collaboration with Food for Asset creation projects to improve sustainable livelihoods and resilience.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project will be handed over to the County and community leaders and existing development partners that provide food assistance and livelihood support. Such handing over will be to arranged and discussed. AMRES will ensure greater involvement of local county leaders during the implementation phase to enhance sustainability of the project. After handover, the County and existing partners will be expected to collaborate with the beneficiary communities to ensure food security.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AMRES strives to engage beneficiaries in any decision making process that affects impact beneficiaries and AAP is one of our deliverable for every staff to ensure existing community leaders and members participate in implementation process and take ownership of the project rather than watching what AMRES is doing afar. During project implementation, a mechanism called participatory review and reflection (PRR) will take place, providing beneficiaries the space to voice their opinions about the project and express their level of satisfaction in the services provided. The PRR will also serve as a mechanism for beneficiaries to participate in joint monitoring activities and express their feeling in owning the project. Beneficiary participation is required to improve the quality of the project implementation, these mechanisms worked very well during first allocation and we will make sure the same way AAP procedures is used at the second allocation of SSHF so that community feels involved in their services. The feedback mechanisms can be sent to most vulnerable through leading groups such as women, youth (girls and boys). Disabilities individuals will be involved in decision making and implementation modalities to get the information (Feedbacks) from project through local language that the community use. Everyone will be informed be the People Living with Disability (PLWDs), girls women and men through local committee that meet regularly with the AMRES project staff
