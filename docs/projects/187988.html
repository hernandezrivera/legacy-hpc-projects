Project Summary

Chronic malnutrition remains high, so nutrition interventions concentrate not only on identification and treatment but also on the prevention of malnutrition, illness, and death. In this regard, the response will target vulnerable mothers and caregivers to optimize prompt and appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and care practices through nutrition and health education and IYCF counseling for health and nutrition staff. Capacity building will include comprehensive training in IYCF and community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) best practices. Local capacity building will focus on prevention and strengthening community health workers, including scaling up the family MUAC approach and caregiver referral system. Multisector coordination will involve the nutrition sector collaboration with the health sector to improve the health status of mothers and young children and reduce the risk of disease.
The project will cover Jan 2022 till Dec 2023 and will aim to deliver facility and outreach-based nutrition services:
CMAM:
2.1Outpatient therapeutic program (OTP): includes Severe acute malnutrition (SAM).
2.2Supplementary feeding program (SFP): includes Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) in all supported health facilities.
2.3Stabilization Center (SC): includes Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with complications in Bab Al Salam and Hope hospitals.
IYCF:
· Exclusive breastfeeding for six months.
. Monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes.
· Enter complementary food after six months old.
· Begin breastfeeding during the hour after delivery.
raising awareness services: At the community level, the IYCF and CMAM facilitators will distribute messaging that cover topics on Family health, NCDs, CDs, EPI, Lifestyle, Nutrition module, and make referrals to the unique services at the nearest HFs as needed according to the WHO referral guidelines.
the services will be provided within 24 nutrition centers, 4 child-mother-friendly spaces, and 4 rapid response teams in eight districts of Aleppo and Idleb governorates;Afrin, Al Bab, Harim, Azaz, Jarablus, Idleb, and Jabal Saman targeting reproductive-aged women, PLWs, and U5 children of the host and IDP communities.
the RRTs activities:
- Within the CMAM program: Screening, referral and follow-up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished.
- Within IYCF-E program: Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers with moderately malnourished children. Distribute micronutrients for preventing of anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women. Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices.
- WaSH: Support hygiene promotion
- CP: Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs during Nutrition screening.
The services will be provided through fixed facilities and outreach in Kafr Karmin, Azaz, Soran, Bab Iman, Bab Noor, Ekdeh,Shamarekh, Al Bab, Jarablus, Al Rayyan, Zoghra, Al Salam, Afieh, Hezri, Kelli, Mariamin,Dana, Afrin, Bulbul, Medanki, Bza'a in addition to 2 hospitals (M. Wassim Maaz and Hope) and1 MC in Azaz, 4 child-mother spaces in Azaz camps and 4 rapid response teams in Azaz,Jarablus, Al Bab and Afrin districts.
The target groups will be children U5, and pregnant and lactating women including beneficiaries with disabilities and as well as caregivers of the children U5. The project response is committed to a holistic approach with enhanced coordination among nutrition actors within and across sectors to achieve a reduction in life-saving and life-sustaining needs. the services will be guided by the severity of conditions in the targeted areas.
the project will target 97,056 beneficiaries including 72000 children U5 and 25,056 PLWs. the children U5 will 50% will be male and 50% will be female, 70% IDPs and 30% Host. The PLWs will be 100% female, 70% IDPs and 30% Host. of the total beneficiaries 10% will be people with disabilities.

4 - Needs

According to the HRP-2022 Humanitarian response plan for Syria and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) issued by 2022, the nutrition sector observed an increasing trend in malnutrition compared to 2020.The nutrition situation in Syria is expected to be influenced by the dynamic of the COVID-19 outbreak and the magnitude of the food insecurity situation affecting consumption of nutritionally balanced and age-appropriate diets, ultimately affecting maternal health, and nutrition status. Escalations and increasing hostilities will likely cause a deterioration in the nutrition situation of mothers and children because of repeated displacements and limited access to lifesaving health and nutrition interventions, as well as increasing prevalence of harmful coping mechanisms such as child marriage and child labor. The proportion of people with acute needs tends to be higher among IDPs. On the level of subdistrict where the project will be implemented, the number of PLWs and children U5 is as follows according to HNO 2022.Dana (PLWs 90123, CU5 283211)Harim (PLWs 5714, CU5 17956)Afrin (PLWs 15524, CU5 48587)Bulbul (PLWs 2849, CU5 8953)Raju (PLWs 3948, CU5 12407)Sharan (PLWs 4205, CU5 13216)Azaz (PLWs 24104, CU5 75746)Suran (PLWs 7678, CU5 8638)Al Bab (PLWs 15243, CU5 47902)Jarablus (PLWs 2431, CU5 7639)Ghandoura (PLWs 6564, CU5 20629)Atareb (PLWs 14694, CU5 46177)

5 - Implementation of activities

Screen thetarget communitiesand IDP campsthrough CHWs toidentify MAM/SAM cases(using MUAC screening)and refer them to nearby facilities. Monitor cases and referthem to higherlevel management facilities.Implement three programs (OTP, SFP, and SC).Distribute supplies to the MAM/SAM children and PLWs(multi-vitamins, iron syrups etc), to support the nutritional status and prevent severe malnutrition and complications. Develop multi-sectoral awareness key messages including protection, health, WaSH and IYCF advice and distribute them through sessions and IEC materials. This will help reduce under-nutrition and break the intergenerational cycle of hunger by raising awareness of malnutrition's main causes, which will help reduce bad dietary habits. Establish a referral pathway towards medical, nutritional and protection higher management facilities.The MEAL team will conduct frequent field visits to collect data and monitor the activities implementation using checklists, observation and interviews with beneficiaries and staff and share notes with projectmanagement for corrective action and follow-up.Mainlyscaling up integrated nutrition response support through Rapid Response teams which will include strengthening early case finding, referral, and treatment of acute malnutrition, and the target each RRT is expected to reach 30-70children and mothers per day. RRTs includeCMAM,IYCF,health,CP and WaSH integration.All statistical data will be shared monthly using the nutrition 4ws tools

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Maintaining a gender balance in the organization staff to ensure better inclusion and mitigate the optional risk of PSEA and in order to ensure a comfortable atmosphere for the beneficiaries. The organization has an established community-based feedback and response mechanism to include a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases and no tolerance towards SEA. The organization's staff, partners, and sup-partners are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children staff, and children.1- Active PSEA reporting system available in all the community centers and have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback.2- BNFs has access to reporting phone number and feedback boxes3- Training the staff and providing awareness to the communities 4- Mixed-gender follow-up committee 5- Coordination and communication with PSEA working group6- IDA PSEA and safeguarding policy is applied.7-accessible environment and CBFMs for PWDs including CWDs.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Raising awareness through messages about PSEA topics in addition to having a complaint mechanism in place and sensitizing beneficiaries on how to use it. Also, service providers will be periodically trained on such topics.Risk 2: safe closure of facilities and protecting data. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Electronic data and information are properly protected and backed up according to IDA SOPs - IDA has developed its own exit strategy guidance to exit in emergencies.Risk 3: Spread of COVID-19 in the project locations (Likelihood: high, impact: high)Mitigation: COVID-19 mitigation procedure will be in place during the project and will include social distance between beneficiaries and the regulation of the patients flow in a way that keeps all of them safe where the crowding will not be allowed at all at the entry of the facility or within the waiting hall.
