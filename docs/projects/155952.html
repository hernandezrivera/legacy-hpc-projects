Needs

- Mental Health Hospital "Asylum Hospital in Azaz" The area of Azaz, Al Bab and Jerablus under the Euphrashield area has been facing so many challenges in providing quality MHPSS services to both the IDPs and the host communities. From the latest morbidity data on mental health consultation done by 25 active mhGAP practitioners, from the 9,056 mental health consultations they did from August 2017 until June 2018, epilepsy is highest at 28%, followed by depression at 25%, OTH ( psychosomatic symptoms) at 24.7%, and stress at 8.7% only. It is very essential that specialized mental health care support be provided to this area to ensure that proper diagnosis, management, treatment and follow-ups must be done to these patients and provide that proper referral mechanism from communities to PHCs and to specialized mental health care. There is this Azaz Asylum Hospital where there are around 120 chronic mental patients. - Kidney dialysis centre in Rural Aleppo:A study of the need for dialysis services in the northern Aleppo countryside and the eastern Aleppo countryside and the information provided by the World Health Organization through the evaluation of resources (HeRAMS) and the Aleppo Health Directorate showed that more than 100 patients with chronic renal failure need permanent dialysis ,which means the need for more than 20 dialysis devices In the region at least - Surgical Hospital in Shumareen. The study by PAC showed a huge gap in the following areas in northern of Aleppo:• Advanced orthopaedic surgery and spinal surgery.• Intensive cardiac care.• General Internal Diseases.• Intensive surgical care.• Diagnostic services. - Prosthetic Prosthesis center in Azaz.• The estimated number of Syrians who lost at least one of their limbs are 60,000. As well as Thousands of Syrians are suffering from disabilities caused by severe nerve injuries, which caused partial or full limb paralysis.• The disability of an amputee or neurological injury is an important cause of depression for the person who sees himself as dependent on his family and society because of the need to the assistant in his daily activities, not to mention his inability to work. - Surgical Unites in Northern Rural Hama.The regions in northern rural Hama showed severe needs in the areas of trauma management and significant gaps in surgical interventions. In addition, a high percentage of injured people were transferred to Idleb due to lack of nearby surgical health facilities.PAC will establish three surgical units to deliver the primary surgical interventions and stabilization care prior to referral of emergency cases to advanced surgical hospital in northern of rural Hama. -Referral System in Northern Rural Hama.large number of injured civilians in northern Rural Hama and the surrounding areas in ability to provide adequate services for them due to :• shortage of resources • dangerous situation in the region • closure of frontlines by warring parties limiting movements for both medical staff and referred cases -Vaccines Cold Chain management .A big challenge for NGOs today is to maintain the cold chain for refrigeration of vaccines. The result of failing to monitor vaccine temperatures accurately "even for short periods" may result in:• Formula degradation.• Loss of potency.• Waste of product and money.• Substandard immunologic responses.• Need to re-immunize patients. - Vaccine Campaigns.the campaigns are vital to eliminate the polio outbreak and to strengthen immunity status among Syrian children and decrease morbidity and mortality from Measles & Rubella. - Supporting the kidney dialysis centers with medications and consumables .In the survey conducted by the World Health Organization in the north of Syria and targeted the dialysis centers , there were 20 centers providing service and the number of patients in need of hemodialysis 840 patients, including about 70 children and the number, the number of the dialysis machines is 133 .

Protection Risk Analysis

1- In all locations of the activities , environmental risks still has a fatal effect on implementation of the 1- 1-activities in the case that a major disaster happen in one of the areas of implementation.The Probability of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on activities will be very high.2- Changing in the front lines between the warring parties may compromises the safety for the staff and the beneficiaries .3- Gender-based violence (GBV) and violence against women and girls (VAWG)4- Acts of sexual exploitation and abuse either carried out by staff or not prevented by them.5- access constraints will continue to be the most significant threat to humanitarian operations. 6- Humanitarian operations continue to be in a high risk of corruption and aid diversion or beneficiaries exploitation and discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance.7- The IDPs caseload fluctuations in the targeted regions will remains one of the biggest risks to all humanitarian operations.8- projects may adversely affected by the external incidents " field staff or beneficiaries or physical damages on the properties" Monitoring and evaluation mechanism:M&E Coordinator is member in the technical committee and will revise all steps of implementationHR department will monitor employment process according to selection criteria, and then will issue a report about the whole process.The reports shall be submitted to the donors according to the agreement. the operations indicators shall be submitted through 4Ws The M&E Coordinator will develop the monitoring and evaluation framework and tools and will be responsible for all M&E activities.M&E plan will be set to give an overview of all M&E activities that will be conducted within the project cycle.M&E staff will routinely monitor all phases and activities of the project implementation; field staff will do regular field visits to ensure the process is being implemented accurately. Interviews with beneficiaries, stakeholders, meetings and data exchanged with local authorities will be conducted and data will be collected for monitoring purposes. The collected data will be analyzed to assess the achievement of the project and measure the impact of the interventions.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- For this risk , the SOPs applied in all projects activities should be activated once this risk happened. 2- safety policy which prioritizes the staff and beneficiaries3- regularly convened a wide range of stakeholders to share knowledge and build evidence on what works to address violence against women and girls and to take actions4- ensuring that governing bodies are made fully aware of any serious incidents and are accountable for ensuring appropriate investigation, reporting and response.5- changing the locations according the donor's requirements.6- Accountability to affected populations (AAP) will be strengthened, as well as due diligence and remote monitoring mechanisms.7- maximizes the responses capacity ,Resource Mobilization and advocacy for the case.8- Incident Reports to donors and other and activating the SOPs which related to safety and evacuation During implementation of the proposed project activities, ensure community interests are respected, and project objectives and activities are transparent and understood by the local population. The beneficiaries and affected population will have direct access to share their feedback and complaints. In addition, PAC staff will conduct regular visits and monitor project activities in the target communities to ensure smooth implementation and liaise with community members. The beneficiary committees and PAC staff together will provide adequate information to the beneficiaries and affected population, and ensure that the project objectives are well explained, and activities are carried out according to agreed plans.PAC staff will ensure compliance with five key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Feedback mechanisms will also be established and communicated with beneficiaries. Gender and age dis-aggregated data from programme coverage and impact will be collected, analyzed and routinely reported on, while programme activities are monitored for improvements in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction and resilience of both women and men.Furthermore, host community and IDP households (beneficiaries or otherwise) will have a direct channel to PAC, and they may provide feedback, positive or negative, which PAC will review and take into consideration.
