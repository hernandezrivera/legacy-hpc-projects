Project Summary

Afrin district in NWS, grappling with aftermath of conflict and displacement, faces severe health and nutrition challenges. Afrin operated under complex governance. The region has been a sanctuary for displaced populations from Damascus, Homs, and Idlib. The area has suffered from a dire lack of basic services, such as healthcare, water, sanitation, and education, due to damaged infrastructure and limited resources. The humanitarian context in Afrin district, further complicated by the significant earthquake in February 2023, necessitates an urgent and adaptive response. The earthquake resulted in over 1,000 fatalities, with substantial damage to buildings and infrastructure, amplifying the need for humanitarian aid and recovery efforts. According to the WoS 2024 HNO, Afrin sub-district is estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary healthcare assistance in 2024, with 207,152 PiN. About 145 thousand children U5–including 33.550 live births expected, 52.639women of reproductive age, more than20,000 older persons, and about33.345 persons with disabilities.
According to HNO, 5.7 million require urgent nutrition assistance. Compared to 2023, there has been a significant rise in the number of people requiring nutritional support, particularly among PLW, adolescent girls, and children U5 years old. The #of children in need of preventive interventions has surged by12%from the last year (from 812,858 in 2023 to 933,177 in 2024) Integration with other sectors.
BAHAR's intervention in Afrin subdistrict aims to improve healthcare access, and enhance nutrition practices, among the conflict-affected population. The project addresses the unique needs of women, girls, men, boys, and individuals with disabilities.The health component focuses on delivering quality care to at-risk groups with limited access. Afrin Hospital (Bahar Hospital !B0203001) provides essential services for diseases, emergencies, reproductive health, and child health. Special attention is given to reproductive health, pediatric care, and the health needs of men and boys. The project targets 42,00 individuals, including 8,698 men, 10,281 women, 12,423 boys, and 11,438 girls from host communities and displaced populations. Bahar Hospital, is fully equipped to address the community's healthcare needs. The hospital encompasses various departments with the following capacities: 37 beds for hospital and 30 for SC.
BAHAR is committed to improved access and quality to lifesaving preventive nutrition specific and sensitive interventions and aims to enhance its nutrition-related outreach efforts by integrating them into the healthcare facilities it presently operates. These facilities include four Nutrition outreach teams(RRTs), one OTP (Outpatient Therapeutic Program), and one MBA, all of which are closely linked with BAHAR Maternity Hospital. The aim of the nutrition program is to provide nutrition-sensitive preventive and curative services across various sectors, targeting children (both boys and girls) aged 6 to 59 months, adolescent girls, and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). The program operates by screening all children under 5 years old and PLWs who visit BAHAR maternity and pediatric hospital clinics for either nutrition services or other healthcare needs (such as RH or IMCI clinics).Beneficiaries are identified through MUAC (Mid-Upper Arm Circumference) screening. For children with uncomplicated Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), treatment is provided using Plumpy Nut, while children under five and PLWs with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) receive treatment with Plumpy Sup. Micronutrient supplementation is offered to pregnant women and children.In terms of securing nutrition supplies, BAHAR will not procure any, instead BAHAR will communicate Nutrition cluster and request the needed supplies as usual through submission (EoI) every 3 months to secure the necessary supplies from UNICEF warehouse stock under the Nutrition cluster and co-lead partner.

Risk of GBV on service staff during home visits: Likelihood: M Impact: H Mitigation Measures: service staff selected from targeted communities, staff trained in first-line support and GBV identification. 4. Risk of discrimination in service provision: Likelihood: L Impact: H Mitigation Measures: Ensure project design includes accessibility for vulnerable groups, implement effective community-based complaints mechanisms, and adhere to humanitarian principles and a "Do No Harm" strategy. Regard PSEA: Considering the high vulnerability of the project beneficiaries, BAHAR provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per facility. In addition, the services will be provided on a free-of-charge basis for all those in need regardless of their individual gender groupings.Assumption 1: The security situation in the intervention area is stable. Risk 1: Implementation of the project activities is prevented/stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality, including theft or kidnapping. Mitigation1:BAHAR will monitor the security developments and report criminal instances/ violence in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, the project activities will be suspended, and alternative areas/activities might be considered under the planned intervention with the approval from Partner and Health Cluster, as well as, Bahar will develop of safety and security plan and training, identify of a security focal point in each project team, and establishment of a communication plan. Assumption 2: Bahar has a valid official permit from the health authorities in Hatay and local authorities in Afrin to operate the target health center. Risk 2: change in legalizations of work. Mitigation Measure Bahar conducts regular coordination meetings with all stakeholders, including Turkish and Local health authorities, to ensure the smooth implementation of the activities. Assumption 3: COVID-19 cholera and other pandemics and infections are controlled and limited. Risk 3: staff and patients are at risk due to a potential outbreak, in addition to the sudden spread of disease and the lack of capacity to respond to a high number of patients.
