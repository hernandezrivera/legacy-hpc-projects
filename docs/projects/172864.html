Project Summary

The aim of the project is to enhance the capacity of health-care service providers to address the protracted essential health needs and enhance equitable access to primary healthcare services for the most vulnerable displaced people and local communities affected by conflicts, disease outbreak and natural emergencies. The response plans under the project are in line with Health Sector Objective 2021: Objective 1- Increase equitable access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need, Objective 2- Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to affected population by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks; and Objective 3- Strengthening health system capacity to enhance vulnerable communities resilience to respond to shocks.
IOM will focus on eleven localities and this includes: Garb El Galabat, Gadarif State, Wad Almahi, Blue Nile State, Tawilla, El Fasher, North Darfur State, El Radoom, Rahd El Berdi, Um Dafug, South Darfur State, Geneina, West Darfur State, Abyei-Muglad, Al Sunut, West Kordofan State and Abyei PCA areas. High rates of internal displacement or return have been reported in these localities. The most vulnerable people, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under-five (U5) living inside or outside of IDPs camps as well as their host communities are at high risk of morbidity and mortality due to unavailability and inaccessibility of sustained health services.
IOM will provide a basic package of primary health services (BPPHS) at the targeted locations through supporting existing health services facilities. The BPPHS will include: outpatient consultation of communicable and noncommunicable diseases; ensuring the availability of essential medicine, supporting maternal and child health services, facilitated referrals and health promotion activities to ensure aid reaches people affected by crises such as conflicts, outbreaks of disease and natural disasters; supporting child vaccination services and supporting mental and psychological consultation. IOM will focus on enhancing early screening of malnutrition among U5 children and PLWs as well as supporting referral and follow-up treatments and management. These activities will be implemented in close coordination with the nutrition sector and State Ministry of Health (SMOH).
IOM will promote the continuity of the BPPHS and this will be achieved through strengthening capacity of health-care service providers including community health-care workers (CHWs) and Community Midwives (CMs) to build their capacity to provide essential health services on prevention, enhancing risk mitigation means and close monitoring of outbreak disease trend. It will also promote the reduction and prevention of maternal and child mobility and mortality by focusing on the specific needs of U5 children and PLWs. IOM will encourage a direct participation and interaction of community members to tackle issues of malnutrition and negative health seeking behaviour and practices. As such, mothers and community caregivers will be trained on topics of the promotion of good Infant Young Child Feeding (IYFC) practices and supporting community-based nutrition screening in strong coordination with the partners in nutrition sector. In addition, a minor rehabilitation of health centers will be implemented to ensure the continuity of services and the rehabilitation works will focus on improving water facilities and screening points to prepare future diseases outbreak and control like COVID-19 pandemic. As part of essential infection prevention control, the COVID-19 mitigation measures will be integrated within the planned capacity building activities with emphasis on strengthening reporting systems, infection prevention, case management, and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE).

1 - Needs

The health needs continue to be significant, especially among the most vulnerable displaced populations, due to the worsening economic crisis, floods and disease outbreaks that has disrupted the ability of the public health sector to provide adequate healthcare. In 2020, seasonal rain resulted in floods was the main factor creating internal displacement and hazards that can affect hundreds of thousands of people who were suffering from cholera outbreaks. Chance of disease outbreaks associated with the flooding such as cholera, dengue, chikungunya, rift valley fevers and malaria was increased due to the limited access to WASH services, lack of vector control strategies, low coverage of vaccines and unavailability of essential medicines. In addition, Sudan has been affected by the COVID-19 pandemic that further strained the access to essential health services. COVID-19 movement restriction together with the deteriorating economy has a significant impact on the already fragile health systems’ capacity to respond to the needs of the people. For instance, the coverage of DPT vaccination was declined at 63 % due to COVID-19 contamination measure and reallocation of health services to direct response to COVID-19. According to the Central Bureau of Statistics, during 2020 the cost of healthcare was increased at 90% and this is further creating the 70 per cent of out-of-pocket expenditures (OOP) of current health expenditures (CHE) in comparison with previous years due to inflation with shortage of essential medicines. The BPPHS is very limited as the most Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities are only partially functional and many are in remote areas and can only provide limited services due to lack of supplies and dilapidated infrastructure. Furthermore, their capacities concerning surveillance and early warning systems are very limited and there are very few or no community-based surveillance officers trained. Among crisis-affected populations, women and children are especially at risk of malnutrition, and gender-based violence (GBV) and high maternal mortality rates due to limited supports on maternal and child health. To improve delivery of health assistance and strengthen the overall health systems, resources need to be allocated to establish functional PHC facilities that offer BPPHS, referral systems and support health worker training. Since 2014, IOM has been providing life-saving health assistance to support affected communities in Darfur states, West Kordofan, South Kordofan and Abyei PCA area. IOM interventions have contributed to enhancing access to primary health services. However, further efforts are required for ensuring the continuity of services to strengthen community resilience. Indeed, the BPPHS in northern Abyei and West Kordofan state remain extremely limited. There are only four clinics in northern Abyei to response to more 150,000 population. Availability of essential medicine is the key issue in these areas. In South Darfur, urgent health interventions are needed, particularly for communities in two localities of Um Dafug and El Radoom where there have been high number of returnees or refugees from neighboring countries. According to IOM’s assessment conducted in June 2020, there was only one primary clinic in UmDafug town, which was available but suffering from a shortage of health care staff, the minimum required medical equipment, safe water facilities and absence of reliable ambulance for referral services. In North Darfur, since 2018, IOM has been providing health services to four localities, Um Baru, Tawilla, Mellit and El Fasher, and plans to continue its interventions in these localities as many of displaced are still in need of timely health service assistance. IOM will continue supporting the target localities to increase community resilience through increasing access to the BPPHS as well as strengthening capacity of health-care providers and communities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Conduct needs assessment per locality in coordination with SMOH and the sector leading agencies to assess needs and gaps: Community meetings per locality will be held and the management committees will be established. 2. Provide a complete range of the BPPHS encompassing: a. General outpatient consultation; b. Child health, including immunization (routine and campaigns); c. Management of communicable diseases (such as influenza, malaria, cholera) and non-communicable diseases (such as diabetes, high blood pressure), including support to the prevailing disease surveillance, early warning and response system; d. Sexual and reproductive health, including maternal and new-born health, management of Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs); e. Management of mental health and trauma care; f. Environmental health, including waste management and vector control; and malnutrition screening (a mass mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screening sessions) and support to management and treatment of malnutrition in nutrition sector. The project will facilitate referrals from community to primary, primary to secondary/tertiary emphasizing adequate continuity of care. IOM will support the SMoH and target clinics for operational cost to deliver the BPPHS and this will include incentive (in line with the guideline issued by MoH) for health-care service provider and community service providers. 3. Organize and conduct capacity building activities: 1) Train/re-train medical and health-care staff at the target clinics to enhance their skills and knowledge to provide a quality of the BPPHS. 2) Capacity building workshop for community health workers (CHWs) and the training will cover various topics on community- based nutrition and health services such as MUAC screening. The project will provide incentive to the service providers, and this will be in line with the guideline issued by MoH. The training will be designed as training of trainers (ToT) aimed at building their capacity to support community members such as mothers and community caregivers. At least 10 peer learning group/mother to mother support groups per locality will be formed at the project catchment areas to work as key messenger and mentor to promote community health and nutrition status. The trained CHWs will educate the group members on community based nutrition screening, the promotion of good IYCF and hygiene practice as part of education to prevent malnutrition and common diseases. The COVID-19 mitigation measures will be integrated within the planned capacity building activities with emphasis on strengthening surveillance and reporting systems, infection prevention and control, case management, and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE).These capacity building supports can be extended to the local clinics at Point of Entry (PoEs)- especially cross border areas as needed.4. Rehabilitation and/or refurbishment of selected PHC facilities including the rehabilitation and/or installation of hand washing facilities and screening points to prepare future infection disease outbreak and control. Essential medicine and medical equipment will be provided as needed. 5. Conduct a regular monitoring (monthly base) and post assessment. 6. Ensure a regular attendance in the sector meeting at field and Khartoum: IOM will share key outputs and challenges including lessons learnt and good practices to the sector leads, SMOH and other sector partners such as local Non-Governmental Organization (NGOs/ Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) for further programming.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will address the protection needs of IDPs, women, and other vulnerable groups such as IDPs, returnees and other affected communities through strengthening the capacity of community to create protective environment. The project will pay a special attention to women of reproductive age, PLW, U5 children and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health services and vulnerable to large scale of epidemiological event and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on the development of women’s capacities, strengthening women’s participation and direct interaction with communities as well as health providers and addressing negative health seeking behaviours to promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. Protection against gender-based violence such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities will be strengthened.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase -immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants as well as local communities to the implementation of the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage in particular women, PLWs, children and youth. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community- based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, the peer learning groups/mother to mother support groups will be formed and trained as mentor and key messenger addressing negative traditional practice as well as protection threats such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers as well as NGOs/CSOs in the health sectors as duty bearer to inform to the sector partners in health and protection if protection threats were reported. Applying IOM's experiences enhancing the protection of migrants through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psycho social support, counselling and medical assistance to victim in strong coordination with the sector partners as needed.
