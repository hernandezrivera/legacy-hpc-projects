Project Summary

The situation in Sudan has significantly worsened since the last update on humanitarian needs was released in November 2022. Starting on 15 April 2023, fighting between the SAF and the RSF has killed hundreds of people, forced more than 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 730,000 people inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict. Resulting in tremendous human suffering and exacerbating significant pre-existing humanitarian needs amongst the civilian population in Sudan. To act with this emergency, the initial 2023 HRP has been revised, to the scale-up of lifesaving multisectoral assistance and protection services in line with HRP Strategic Objectives (SO) 1 and 2, including related to implementation approaches and costs.
This project plan will work in line with the WASH sector objectives of the revised 2023 HRP to bring sustainable gender-sensitive access to water supplies, improved hygiene, and better sanitation practices to 180,000 women, men IDPs, returnees, people with disability and other vulnerable residents in ND, BN and Kassala states. The project main interventions & activities will design to support construction of new solar-powered water yards (mini and large), rehabilitate, and replace diesel-powered water yards with solar equipment. The project will also support the maintenance of nonfunctional hand pumps and the establishment of new in coordination with states level Water and Environmental Sanitation departments. Haffirs will be maintained where they are the main source of drinking water and livestock watering. Water supply quality will be monitored and tested for disinfection, particularly in flood-affected areas or where surface water is used and as required, while ensuring separate sites for livestock water where sources are shared. Water trucking and storage will be an option in acute emergencies. The project will support 180,000 crisis-affected people to have gender-sensitive sanitation services.
New communal and institutional (e.g. clinics and schools) latrines construction, support maintenance of existing and provide training in maintenance and management and provision of hand washing kits. Communal and institutional latrines construction or rehabilitation will ensure structures to maintain gender segregation and enable access for women and men with disability. Jerricans will be distributed with awareness on safe storage and drinking water use. Awareness-raising campaigns will be led by trained women and men. The project will promote hygiene services by raising awareness campaigns to communicate poor hygiene practice-related risks and promoting hygiene behaviours and practices. Women and girls will be supported with the distribution of reproduction menstrual hygiene management kits. Target community women and men will be encouraged and mobilised to construct latrines coupled with awareness about the risks associated with open defecation practice. Vector and related disease outbreak control activities and waste management will be targeted, particularly in areas affected by floods. Communities will be supported to ensure women’s involvement (as they are the leading collectors and most involved by poor sanitation and hygiene-related services) in WASH interventions planning and implementation (e.g. site selection) and their effective participation in all project interventions. Training of community project management committees (women & men), wil conducted to sustain maintenance, operation, and sanitary and hygienic conditions. Implementation of interventions will be in consultation with women and men and in partnership with the WASH sector and related authorities.

1 - Needs

Sudan’s growing humanitarian crisis is set against a protracted economic crisis and pre-existing conflicts in some parts of the country, with an estimated third of the population already needing humanitarian assistance. In addition to large-scale displacement, the conflict has shattered access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care and other essential services for millions of people as a result of the current crisis. Reflect in the number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increase. The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicates that the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Cluster's 2023 PiN is 11 million (26% women, 48% children, 15% with disability). Displaced people (2.2M), returnees (899K), vulnerable residents (7.1M) and refugees (2.2M) are all affected by WASH constraints. Many of these affected groups either do not have access to WASH or cannot afford essential WASH services. About 22 per cent of refugees use shared latrines, and 51 per cent use communal latrines. Many of the refugee camps and refugee settlements, including open areas, are located in flood-prone areas resulting in damage to WASH facilities. Approximately 36 per cent of displaced people report they cannot afford water. Lack of gender-segregated latrines and undignified, unlit, and insecure sanitation facilities pose a risk of GBV for girls and women. Menstrual hygiene management is often not prioritized, negatively impacting girls' school attendance and enrollment. Diarrhoea kills one in ten children. Over 3 million children suffer from acute malnutrition, 50 per cent of which is associated with repeated diarrhoea or worm infections related to poor WASH conditions. Deteriorating economic conditions and inflation constrain access to WASH services in Blue Nile, Kassala and North Darfur. Communities unable to support the costly operation and maintenance of water supply sources lack government investment in the WASH sector, leaving most water supply sources nonfunctional. Tribal conflicts at Blue Nile and North Darfur have rendered more people to displacement. At the same time, heavy rains and floods have affected many with water supply infrastructure destruction, displacement, and vector-related diseases outbreak across the three states. The tribal conflict in Blue Nile assumed hundreds of lives and left thousands displaced. Reported sporadic tribal conflicts, car hijacking, robberies and crime continued in North Darfur, with over 900,000 displaced across the state, according to IOM. Flooding and poor waste management or vector control capacity of targeted state governments have led to spreading of vector-related diseases (including cholera, malaria, dysentery, and Hemorrhagic typhoid fever), common causes of mortality and morbidity. WASH sector update on WASH humanitarian needs indicates that out of 1.3 million people, 27%, 33%, and 52% of the total population need humanitarian assistance to provide essential water supply, sanitation and hygiene service respectively in Blue Nile. In North Darfur, 13%, 21% and 24% of a total population of 2.9 million need assistance to access water supply, sanitation services and hygiene promotion. In Kassala state and out of a total population of 2.8 million, 9%, 22%, and 24% need humanitarian assistance to access basic water supply, sanitation services and hygiene services, respectively. Across the three states, poor access to basic services, mainly WASH services, reflects on the ability to prevent malnutrition. Poor access to WASH service and high food insecurity rates have contributed to high malnutrition rates in the three states’ populations of displacement, returnees, and vulnerable people. OCHA profiles for states during August and September 2022 indicate that the prevalence of Acute global malnutrition among children under five has reached 18.5% in Kassala and 28% in North Darfur,

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Practical Action has a long experience in adopting Integrated Water Resource management approach that ensures equal access to water for different users while maintaining ecosystem balances and enhance environmental restoration, where possible, PA will apply IWRM in its interventions (especially for multi-sectoral actions). That will be ensuring access to drinking water as well as water for sanitation, farming and livestock in the catchment area. • The project will also emphasis in upgrading number of diesel operated water yards into solar-operated systems to reduce hazardous gases emission from burning fuel for water pumping. Solar lanterns will also be introduced to light public latrines for eliminating the use of Kerosene and other unclear energy sources. • Communities' women and men committees will be trained and sensitized to establish forest nurseries utilizing wastewater to start community forests around water supply points (That also apply to women group farms pilots). •Conditioning user fees pay (Tariff) will -in addition- to providing for maintenance and sustainability, encourage water use conservation. • Increase in water supply amounts and points will reduce concentration of livestock at few points and cause environmental degradation by overgrazing.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Practical Action ensures understanding and commitment to the safeguarding policies package by all employees, partners and contractors, which states Zero tolerance to all types of sexual exploitation and abuse especially against children, women and vulnerable adults. Complaints system will be put in place to ensure safe and effective communication channel is active for all beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The system includes different tools including hotlines, emails, complaints boxes and others. As communities will be well-oriented and engaged in initial consultations and need assessments, Practical Action will ensure inclusion of the needs of different community groups including women and disabled people to assure that no one is left behind! Doing No harm principle will also be maintained to ensure no negative implications will arise from the project interventions that violate people safety, security, dignity or create damage to the resources. The main group of the project beneficiaries is the crisis-affected people those are in urgent need for WASH services for protection from the implications of lack of clean water and limited access to sanitation services. The project will enhance communities’ protection through provision of these essential services and further focus on COVID 19 protection with organizing awareness raising and provision of hygiene kits and protection supplies. Establishment and rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure in health facilities will enhance the capacity of local health system and thus better access to lifesaving health services. Also, gender segregated schools’ latrines will enable girls and boys’ safe access to sanitation facilities and protect them from the different types of physical and psychological hazards. Moreover, well-designed public latrines that are gender and disabled sensitive will ensure protection, safety and dignity of women and people with disabilities. Solar lights used to equip the public latrines will further enhance women safety (against GBV) as it protects women from the hazards associated with open defection especially in the evening hours.
