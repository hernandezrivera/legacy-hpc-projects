Project Summary

The Project Enhancing Food Ssecurity and Resilient livelihood to Conflict and Drought affected Population in Warrap, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria aims at saving life and building resilient livelihood of 43,800 vulnerable Population (11,826 men, 15,330 women, 5,694 Boys and 10,950 Girls) at Warrap state (Tonj North)/ Central equatorial (Yei, Kajokeji and Terekeka) and Eastern Equatoria(Kapoeta East and North). The project shall contribute to providing food assistance and emergency food production to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations by employing Accountability to the affected population (AAP) approach and making sure that activities are implemented in line with core humanitarian standards (CHS). All the respond shall be integrated with strategies that bridges gender in equality gaps and addresses issues of GBV that happens to be a major contributing factor for food insecurity and compromise livelihood of men, women and girls. The respond shall aim at addressing risk factors of covid-19 transmission and employ risk mitigation measures during activities implementation. The respond plan shall save life in Tonj North, yei, KajoKeji, Terekeka, Kapoeta East and North Counties by provision of Food packs to 7, 300 Households. Provide agricultural inputs to 3,700 Households. Provision of fishing kit to 1500House holds. The project shall train 25 farmers groups of 20 members each on Agribusiness skills and best agronomic practices. The farmers group shall be provided 125 ox ploughs to boost food production and strengthen their Agri business skills. The programme shall train 35 agricultural extension workers (CAEWs) to carry on the extension work. To address the issues of animal health and prevent zoonotic diseases. The project shall train community animal health workers (CAHWs) 25 in Tonj north and 25 in Kapoeta East) and procure veterinary drugs to carry on livestock treatment and mass vaccination Campaign targeting 40,000 livestock in Tonj North and 35,00 livestock in Kapoeta East. 300 House hold capacity shall be built on animal products hygiene and handling. As part of Monitoring Evaluation and learning. IRSS shall conduct Project progress and Midterm review workshop to serve both learning and accountability ppurpose and will seek to identify any problems and constraints encountered by the project to date and formulate appropriate recommendations for corrective actions for effective implementation of the remaining period of the project implementation. Also ensure the project indicators that are monitored are on track and lesson learnt are drawn for future improvement. The workshop shall involve key stake holders of the projects especially representatives of women, people living with disability, youth, government line ministries, representatives of implementing partners, FSL cluster members, etc.

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan HNO 2022, an estimated two-third of the total South Sudan population 8.9 million people (26% Girls, 27% boys, 24% women and 23% men) are in need humanitarian Assistance and out it 15% are people living with Disability (PLWD). An increase of 600,000 since 2021. These 8.9 million in need include 8.54 South Sudanese men, women, boys and girls and 328,000 Refuges and asylum seekers. 2million are internally displaced (IDPs). According to interpectoral priority needs ranked food insecurity remain the top primary priority need. According to IRSS rapid needs assessment, 78,000 people are reported displaced across the county and lacks most of the basic necessities (IRSS, RNA 2021); 26,000 people are living in IPC 5; and more than 130,000 in IPC 4 in the county. The most critical needs identified by assessments in Tonj North was need for an emergency food supply. Kapoeta East and North County Eastern Equatoria are counties of Concerns to localized seasonal, intercommunal conflict dry spell and flood. According to IRW independent assessment conducted 2021 indicated that the situation remained unchanged as most households were experiencing hunger and needed food assistance from WFP and other partners. 214 (98.5%) households reported experiencing food shortage and only 25 (10.5%) reported not having experienced food shortage over the past 12 months. Rainfall is a key challenge that is affecting crop and livestock production. The rainfall pattern has become unpredictable due to climate change and it is becoming increasingly hard to rely entirely on rain-fed agriculture. Majority of the population in Kapoeta are dependent on relief and emergency. Livestock is main livelihood options for 93.9 % of the population due to the arid nature of Kapoeta. However, 34.2% are engaged in some form of agricultural food production; 79.7 % of the population rely on humanitarian food assistance. The market functionality is a big challenge in the proposed project area due to poor road network in most of the places. The market is very far with 58.2% spending more than one hour to reach the next market. Being a pastoral community, 31.3% of the respondents have small ruminants, 28.4% large ruminants and 80% keep poultry. 90% of the population rely on pastoralism as their main source of income while 61.8% of the population are engaged in some IGAs. 54.5% of bread winners are women as they are the main work force within Toposa society, the main tribe in the region. Gender equality and GBV profile of Kapoeta is similar to that of Tonj north because these are both pastoral communities. Therefore, is a major concern in tackling protections issues in order to improve food security and livelihood.In the proposed action area high levels of food insecurity, violence and cattle raids affected vulnerable people. The violence severely impacted response operations in the area. People’s homes and humanitarian facilities were looted and burnt down, this included warehouses of relief commodities and vehicles. These incidences have caused constant displacement among the civilian population. The restriction in movement by security forces affect access to farm land by the civil population and compromise on delivery of humanitarian services. All these have contributed negatively to the IPC levels as indicated in the paragraph above. Therefore, it’s within this humanitarian Respond plan that IRSS shall contribute in addressing the needs of 43,800 food insecure vulnerable populations through FSL cluster objectives in Tonj Nortn, Kajokeji, Terekeka, Kapoeta East and North.

2 - Project activities

The proposed actions aims to enhance resilience capacity of the drought and conflict affected people through creating alternation income generation, multiplication and diversification of business portfolio and sustain livelihoods . The vulnerable communities will be in a position to meet their basic needs and reducing vulnerability. As a result, they will be better placed to be self-reliant and will ultimately be less attracted and vulnerable to economic incentives for violence. However, the project shall address emergency needs in the areas of IPC4 and IPC5.. The proposed project includes the following activities:(i) Distribution of Food packs to 7, 300 Households/43,800 people mainly for women, disabled, orphan, child, elderly headed, households(ii) Provide agricultural inputs to 3700 Households (iii) Provision of fishing kit to 1500House holds 4). The project shall train 25 farmers groups of 20 members each on Agribusiness skills and best agronomic practices. (iv) The farmers group shall be provided 125 ox ploughs to boost food production and strengthen their Agri business skills. (v) The programme shall train 35 agricultural extension workers (CAEWs) to carry on the extension work. To address the issues of animal health and prevent zoonotic diseases,(vi) The project shall train community animal health workers (CAHWs) and conduct mass vaccination Campaign targeting 40,000 livestock shall be built on animal products hygiene and handling.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will have a risk mitigation plan with several alternatives to mitigate risksand implement timely. The following measures will be taken to mitigate the risks: (i)Regular follow-up of the security information with the UN and International NGOSafety Organizations, NGO Forum, UN OCHA regularly; (ii) Follow-up with the SecurityFocal Point Persons of the Islamic Relief South Sudan Juba office and Field Offices;(iv) Revision of Risk Mitigation Plan regularly in consultation with the UN OCHA andUN Clusters. IRSS has a risk management policy in place. For this project, IRSSconsiders the risk of political insecurity and general insecurity (particularly due to theon-going war) to be the largest risk factor. Major roads for transporting projectmaterials, the supply of which, if disrupted, could negatively impact projectimplementation. These conflicts could also badly affect local and national markets.While structural solutions to these problems are largely beyond the scope of theproject, IRSS has put in place a system that will monitor the situation if these risksunfold and will put in place mitigation measures to address them. IRSS is a fullydeveloped country office with appropriate, effective, and efficient systems. The countryoffice has written accounting policies and procedures. The country finance managerimplements these procedures with oversight being provided by IR East Africa RegionalFinance Manager. IRSS uses IRW’s policy, records and documents keeping procedures;all records are safely kept under key and lock within the IRSS office. Finalizeddocuments are scanned and stored electronically in the IRSS document archivalsystem. IRSS Finance, Programmes and Country Management Teams operateindependently to ensure that they are providing controls and oversight to each otherbut at the same ensuring that they are working for the common goal according to theIR values. Additionally, IR East Africa office provides strong managerial and technicaloversight to ensure quality programme delivery and management. IRSS is licensed towork in the proposed project location and has an office in project locations.
