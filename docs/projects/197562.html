Project Summary

The intervention is developed along the following three axes:
- Strengthening the capacities of local authorities to ensure the separation, transport and treatment of health waste. To make this possible, work is being carried out along two lines: on the one hand, the system is being expanded in the western zone, the number of health centers receiving support is being increased and procedures are being monitored. On the other hand, work is being done to develop the capacities of the local authorities and the coordination mechanism between them. A gradual transfer of functions is also being managed in the Jazeera Region, where the system was initially introduced.
- Support to supra-municipal authorities (DoH and DoLAE) for the development and implementation of Standard Operational Procedures (SoP) for the disposal of expired pharmaceuticals.
- Consolidation and improvement of the KRC Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Department capacities to manage and monitor Health Waste Management and Infection Prevention and Control activities.
The designed processes incorporate a rights-based approach, with a focus on gender, diversity, sustainability and democratic governance, which are key elements to ensure a transformative impact on the territory.
The duty bearers are the local authorities of the municipalities and public institutions (DoH,CoH, DoLAE) since they are in charge of respecting, protecting and promoting environmental health to foster healthy and safe communities. The 1,491,661 inhabitants of Qamishli, Amuda, Derek, Hasakeh, Tell Tamer, Derbasiya, Raqqa and Menbij are the right holders, while civil society, in particular local NGOs, associations and the media are the responsibility bearers.
The intervention contributes to the 2030 Agenda, especially SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being), 5 (Gender Equality), 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) and 15 (Life on Land).

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Indirect- border closure due to growing political instability, hindering the capacity of international staff to travel to NES and carry out relevant activities in the field;- escalation of the military conflict which might impact the capacity of the staff members to safely move across the regions to implement the activities;- decrease in international funding for UPP-KRC organizations with consequent impact on the capacities of the respective Departments to maintain the current structure and allocate the same resources to the activities;- diversion of the LA’s resources to respond to emergency situations or to cope with the economic crisis, undermining the capacity to consolidate their interventions and further expanding it, both in terms of human resources and equipment.Direct- Staff turnover, both of UPP-KRC and of LAs, resulting in the risk of losing know-how and slowing down the implementation process due to the need to roll-out refresher training;- in case of turn-over, UPP-KRC might face difficulties in hiring in a timely manner specialized staff with knowledgeable background , especially on a specific and delicate topic such as the disposal of expired pharmaceuticals;- decreased commitment and attention of relevant stakeholder in HWM/IPC topics due to perception of lower covid-19 risks.
