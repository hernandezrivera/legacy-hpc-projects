Project Summary

The project will strengthen lifesaving gender based violence (GBV) prevention and response in Akobo, Uror, Pochalla, Fangak, Pigi/Canal Counties in Jonglei state, Leer of Unity State, Nasir of Upper Nile State and Wau County of Western Bahrel ghazel. The proposed project will ensure provision of multi-sectoral coordinated GBV prevention and response services to women and girls including addressing the psycho social needs of women and girls including survivors of GBV and to mitigate and prevent the risks faced by women and girls in the humanitarian crisis. Men and boys will be an integral part of the Programme as allies in GBV prevention and response.
The project will target an estimated 23437 persons (9100 male, 14337 female) comprised of IDPs and host community members affected by conflict to;
- Increase access to lifesaving GBV specialized services including case management and psycho social support (PSS) services for women and girls including adolescent girls.
- Strengthen capacities of frontline staff to ensure quality and comprehensive services to women and girls. To ensure GBV survivors’ recovery and healing process is realized, Nile Hope will work closely with implementing partners and service providers to ensure survivors of GBV have timely access to comprehensive and quality services and increase awareness on availability of services to ensure timely access of available services.
-Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment. Nile Hope will make sure to provide support to the already existing Women and girls friendly spaces in Akobo, Pochalla, Uror, Leer, Nasir and Fangak Counties where Women and girls have been conducting recreational activities. The WGFS will offer specialized GBV case management and psychosocial support basic counseling, age-appropriate, individual and group-based support, information sessions, psychosocial support/recreational activities and skills building/livelihoods courses and activities for women and girls to gain knowledge and skills for positive coping including making healthy life-choices. Nile Hope will also provide tailored PSS activities for adolescent girls to mitigate their vulnerability to multiple forms of GBV and offer support systems and access to information. This will be pivotal in reducing women and girls’ isolation and boosting their self-confidence.
Livelihood opportunities including income generation activities have greatly contributed to improving gender equality and reducing GBV risks particularly among women and adolescent girls. In this project, women, youth and adolescent girls in internally displaced persons (IDP) camps and in the host communities will be supported to generate income and assets through a range of potential avenues.
Establish and / strengthen support to safe house to provide immediate security,
temporary refuge, and support to GBV survivors who are escaping violent or abusive situations. Nile Hope through SSHF’s First allocation funds of 2020, have been supporting and running a safe house in Wau County of Western Bahrelghazel. Nile Hope is proposing to continue with this service provision to the women and girls that have been affected through GBV cases. The safe shelter will provide accommodation to the GBV survivors that have are affected and being tracked by the perpetrators.

1 - Needs

Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19.Overall food security worsened and some communities were facing catastrophic needs especially in Akobo, Fangak, Leer and Uror Counties. More children were acutely malnourished than in the past three years. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psycho-social distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour and child marriage; weakened institutional capacity for provision of basic social services; has aggravated gender inequalities and sustained traditional gender roles, social norms and negative cultural practices, such as child and forced marriages; has reinforced deep seated grievances that underpin many inter-communal conflicts, perpetuating revenge killings (retaliations) and violence, including gender-based violence (GBV). As much destruction goes on at community level through inter-communal conflict during cattle raid, fighting over land and water resources, accompanying abductions, forced marriage and gender-based violence. Even in displacement settings, threats and risks of all forms of GBV against women and girls persist, particularly sexual violence, growing levels of intimate partner violence (IPV), sexual exploitation, harassment and child and forced marriage.Around 52% of girls in South Sudan marry before the age of 18 and community members describe the practice of marrying girls as young as 13 as common. Protection assessments including the consultative process that led to development of Strategic National Action Plan (SNAP) to end child marriage in South Sudan identified Jonglei state as one of the leading state in child marriage and the practice is linked to social norms. The ongoing conflict also worsened the situation due to break down of social structures and poverty that increased practices of child marriage, a practice related to high demand for cattle by the girl’s family in terms of bride price. However, in most locations, available actors are specialized only in general protection and the capacity for quality GBV prevention and response Programmes in Jonglei remains limited to a few international actors, despite several efforts to build capacity of state and non-state actors,Addressing gender-based violence in Jonglei, Western Bahrelghazel and Unity State requires measures to improve protective environment, through prevention efforts that include community mobilization and advocacy by community leaders, men, boys, women and girls that promote positive social norms and strengthen community-based prevention mechanisms and structures to mitigate risks of GBV including vulnerabilities related to child marriages.This project will be using Communities Care model designed to empower people in conflict-affected and other humanitarian settings to create safer and healthier communities by addressing harmful norms that promote violence against women and girls, and fostering dignity, equality, and non-violence. The project will aim at addressing harmful social norms to promote women’s and girl’s rights as well as provide knowledge and information on available response services and mechanisms where survivors of gender based violence including survivors or potential survivors of child marriage can access services.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-streamprotection at every level. Nile Hope is mainstreaming protection in all its programming byensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such asPWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage ofthe project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. Nile Hope with other partners has developed a referral pathway in Wau, Akobo, Fangak, Pochalla and Uror and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools Nile Hope will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats byanalyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in-charge ofdetermining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. Nile Hope regularly revises and updates safeguarding policies. Beneficiaries and community members are familiar with Nile Hope’s ‘zero-tolerance’ standards on SEA and complaint mechanism is in place. Staff turnover: Nile Hope has the reputation of the respectable employer and among the lowest level of staff turnover in South Sudan. In the situation of staff turnover, the positions will be covered to ensure smooth project activities. The project has also considered that the protection of the target beneficiaries will be prioritized to reduce/ mitigate any form of potential harm. COVID-19 has also presented new forms of challenges, the project staff and the beneficiaries are going to be enlightened on the better forms of prevention and mitigation of measures to ensure that the project implementation is not affected. Some of the COVD-19 mitigation measures have also been adopted as part of the project activities.
