Needs

During the 2014 conflict, nearly 485,000 people were internally displaced. During the conflict, some of 285,000 IDPs were hosted in 93 UNRWA schools and around 48,000 IDPs in 24 PA schools However, Gaza lacks well-equipped and safe formal shelters. Based on the after action review of the 2014 Emergency Operations Center and further lessons learned discussions by (ICCG) and all clusters, a number of alternative shelter solutions have been explored. The conclusion was – in coordination with MoEHE and MoSA – that schools are the most feasible option for IDPs in Gaza.1- Hard component: Identifying and upgrading schools to be used as designated emergency shelters (DES)2- Soft component: Develop sector-specific SoPs & management tools for the operation of DES in a gender sensitive manner, integrating GBV prevention & protection standards and provide training to shelter managers. Engagement with IDP in the shelter management and decision-making to ensure equal access to humanitarian assistance, registration and access to information. The proposed project is to upgrade 14 PA schools to serve as DES during emergencies; targeting people fleeing their homes due to security concerns or due to a lack of services in their original residence. After the emergency phase, the schools will be converted into Collective Centers if needed. HNO 2018 addresses the needs of IDPs as a priority, including safe shelters affording them enhanced protection during a crisis and meeting their basic humanitarian needs. Led by OCHA/IDP working group in cooperation with UNRWA, MoSD, MoEHE and the relevant clusters, a selection criterion were set and 32 schools were selected, based on five additional criteria Location, Capacity, Access, Existing facilities/infrastructure and Security/safety. A multi-sectorial technical taskforce (TTF), including Oxfam, ACF, NRC, PUI, UNICEF, GVC, UNDP and others, in cooperation with the relevant authorities assessed each school in April-May 2016. Key informant interviews with the relevant stakeholders were also held. Recently, the cost estimate have reviewed and updated of the remaining proposed DES shelter. The TTF developed two tools: 1. A technical assessment tool to assess the security, capacity and suitability of the nominated schools. 2. SoPs considering all sectors’ needs, were developed in consultation with all clusters, mainstreaming protection, gender and GBV in line with IASC guidelines. This resulted in the inclusion of specific considerations such as assigning separate showers and toilet units, lighting, safe spaces for women & children, cooking spaces. Privacy and culture concerns were considered (for example: pre-fabricated partitions inside shelter rooms). The needs of persons with disabilities were considered through accessibility measures, such as ramps. The assessment analysis by the TTF was reflected in the scope of the work to ensure that all the requirements to enable the schools to be used as DES in an emergency were met. The final scope of work was shared with all clusters for final review. The nominated schools were prioritized according to agreed-upon criteria. The final outputs (scope of work, budget, design & project time frame) were presented to the ICCG and shared with stakeholders, including PWA, CMWU, MoSA & MoEHE. The total number of beneficiaries is estimated at 22,500 IDPs in the 32 schools with an average of 703 IDPs per school. It’s worth to mention that all the targeted schools students and teachers will benefit from some elements of the project inside the schools. The total number of students who will benefit from this elements of schools upgrades project are 34,885 students and teachers. Currently 18 schools are upgraded by UNICEF, NRC, Oxfam, ACF,GVC and PUI while KFW has agreed to add the DES shelter components to an ongoing school building project. The partners are looking for funding to upgrade the 14 schools remaining and to complete the components in the ongoing projects.

Activities

In close coordination among the partners (ACF, Oxfam, UNICEF, PUI, NRC,GVC and UNDP), the proposed intervention will cover WASH, Shelter, food security, and energy components for 14 schools to meet the needs as outlined in the assessment by OCHA/IDP working group, clusters and humanitarian agencies (Handicap international, Islamic Relief, GVC, NRC, Oxfam, ACF and PUI) in cooperation with UN agencies (UNRWA, UNICEF, UN-Women, UNDP and WFP) who provided technical advice to ensure that the design of the DES guarantees the safety of displaced children, boys, girls, women and men. • The WASH components of the intervention are designed to ensure access to clean and safe water, sanitation and solid waste facilities in each shelter which will include but not be limited to (rehabilitation of existing sanitation facilities, establishment of additional sanitation & hygiene facilities (construction of water wells and brackish water desalination plants with a capacity off 10 to 20 m3/day; procuring and deploying standby generators, domestic and drinking water storage tanks, solid waste containers, and laundry facilities). Toilets and showers will be provided in sufficient numbers for both females and males.• The SHELTER components will include maintenance works for the existing buildings, lightning, external ramps, extinguishers, installation of laminated film on windows for safety, and fabricated temporary partitioning to ensure privacy for women and girls.• The Food security sector intervention will include provision of stoves and protected gas cylinders to allow for preparation of light meals for babies and children. All shelter key equipment and hardware, including backup generators, should be protected by security fencing for children safety and to protect against unauthorized access. Climate conditions summer vs winter will be addressed during the upgrading as much as applicable. UNDP is proposing to rehabilitate 2 schools (Yafa secondary School and Abdelfattah Hmoud secondary school) in Gaza out of unfunded 14 schools. The total number of beneficiaries in the two schools is estimated at 1,466 IDPs with an average of 733 IDPs per school.

Indicators

An M&E system will be developed for the project, involve the application of the performance measurement framework (PMF), key performance indicators, and data collection mechanism. Following the frequency cited in the monitoring plan, progress data against the results indicators will be collected and analysed to assess the progress of the project in achieving the agreed outputs and outcomes. In addition, a complaint mechanism will be put in place with follow up procedure. In addition, community engagement will ensured through the participation of key community figures in managing the DES, awareness of beneficiaries and addressing any received complaints.The M&E plan will include indicators, roles and responsibilities, forms and tools to be used in data collection, and organizational data processing procedures both for UNDP and for its local partner, Alata'a Charitable Society (ACS). Regular field monitoring visits will be conducted by UNDP M&E officers on a weekly basis to follow up on the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are implemented in accordance with the work plan. UNDP M&E officer will issue a written report summarizing the main findings after each monitoring visit. The reporting schedule will be identified in the contract and will include updates on the progress of activities and the achievements of different indicators. Regarding accountability, UNDP will maintain ongoing discussions with all concerned stakeholders, including the potential beneficiary households, the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA), the Palestinian Civil Defense, and Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE), as well as the relevant water authorities, to ensure that the proposed activities are implemented properly taking into consideration the needs of the affected population. Consultations are also held with the management teams of the designated emergency shelters. Furthermore, UNDP will implement its own feedback and complaint mechanism to collect any complaints or suggestions regarding the implemented activities. Feedback tools will be incorporated to allow for information sharing and UNDP subsequent follow up with the potential IDPs and community members. ACS will ensure community coordination throughout the implementation process and will lead community awareness activities. Partners will keep the affected populations updated through official meetings with their representative who attended different bilateral meetings or in the IDP WG meetings. Communication with neighboring communities adjacent to the DES will be consulted and their concerns should be addressed during the design and upgrading of DES. ACS will receive CwC training carried out by BBC Media Action in coordination with OCHA. The training will help ACS to develop/improve communication strategies and tools during project activities. UNDP aims through this intervention to facilitate an adequate settlement space to accommodate 1,466 IDPs in the two schools (females and males), and provide access to basic facilities and services taking into consideration the special needs, privacy and dignity of women during the design and implementation of all facilities.
