Project Summary

The action, based on the WC vulnerability map, the WC comprehensive assessment in HCFs that involved CESVI and ACTED, consultations carried out with key stakeholders (WC, CMWU, PWA, MoE, MoH, JSC GNG), and assessments conducted by the partners in the target areas, targets the HRP2023 WASH specific objective 2 “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians” and focuses on:
WASH and Protection support in HHs
The project targets 500 vulnerable HHs (2,850 individuals) in North Gaza, Deir Al Balah, Khan Younis, Rafah Governorates. Thanks to the consolidated experience from recent projects and based on assessments carried out at HH level in the proposed areas, ACTED and CESVI in collaboration with YEC propose to scale up activities for vulnerable HHs and enlarge access to water, by ensuring safe water, increasing water storage capacity and improving sanitation facilities. Improving access to safe water for drinking and hygienic use prevents further deterioration of HHs’ living and health conditions, enhances protection against inadequate WASH facilities and exposure to nutrition risks, limits negative coping mechanisms (e.g. water purchase from unreliable vendors, using open spaces as toilets). CESVI has developed strong expertise in WASH (previous/ongoing projects in Hebron and Gaza in partnership with ACTED for adequate access to water quantity and quality). YEC has proven experience in implementing WASH programs (access to services for fragile families, human and protection assistance, hygiene kits distribution, awareness raising). The project has been coordinated with relevant stakeholders (WC, PWA, MoSD) and simultaneously addresses urgent needs of vulnerable populations while endowing them with proper WASH systems.
WASH support in public institutions (HCFs and schools)
CESVI and ACTED have direct knowledge about WASH-related risks and needs affecting these institutions and thanks to previous projects in Hebron in partnership with UNICEF, gained relevant experience in improving WASH facilities in HCFs and schools. The project proposes rehabilitation interventions of WASH facilities in 10 schools (Gaza, Deir Al Balah and Khan Younis Governorates) and 3 HCFs (Deir Al Balah and Rafah Governorates). This component is deemed extremely important in view of the worsened impact due to lack of improved WASH facilities and related health risks in the frame of the C19 pandemic. Public places like HCFs and schools are strongly exposed to contamination risks due to users' and visitors' high turnover, if not equipped with proper WASH and sanitation facilities, also considering that they serve brackets of population already vulnerable per se (sick people, children).
The activity is implemented directly by CESVI and ACTED with the approach used for interventions at HH level. The specific expertise combined with close relationships with relevant authorities (MoE and MoH) results in assessments pre-identifying necessary and specific interventions in each facility.
Support for Waste service providers
The project supports 8 Municipalities in North Gaza Governorate supplying 200 containers to improve the capacity and the environmental situation of collection points, and 300 kits with PPE and working tools for the workers to improve working conditions.
WASH and protection are combined replicating positive modalities of previous projects and benefit from coordination consolidated between partners’ team, also strenghtening YEC on project management. The action is based on the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach IPA with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, PwDs and the elderly. Gender is mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBPCs are trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with particular focus on women and children. Accountability to Affected Populations AAP is core to the action.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian consequences of the illegal Israeli blockade are huge: Gaza’s economy is devastated, most people are unable to leave Gaza, with no access to food, water, essential health and education services, and Palestinians are cut off from each other. WASH infrastructure destructed, weak resource management, and rapid population growth all contribute to the increasingly dire shortages of safe drinking water and access to sanitation. 96% of water from Gaza's sole aquifer is unfit for human consumption due to over-pumping and wastewater contamination, therefore desalination of seawater is the preferred strategic solution to provide people with safe, clean water. 40% of the domestic water supply is lost along outdated network infrastructure. Due to irregularities in supply, residents have to stock water in containers on their rooftops, but the chronic energy crisis in Gaza results in just a few hours of electricity each day limiting residents’ ability to operate pumps to fill up the containers Who does not get a chance to pump water to the roof find themselves with no running water and have to purchase water from private vendors, much of which is polluted. This crisis affecting all aspects of life, including daily need of drinking water, cooking, bathing and laundry, is also worsened by the lack of compliance to minimum shelter standards for kitchens and bathrooms: as per recent assessments carried out in the target areas, 38% of HHs have kitchens with no solid cleanable preparation counter and sink and adequate space; 55% have weak, not sealed, cracked, or no doors for the bathroom; 46% face issues in toilet flushing systems; 35% do not meet minimum standards of toilet/shower floors tiled with non-slip tiles, shower walls tiled to minimum 160cm height, other non-tiled walls plastered with at least 1 layer of plaster or Tyrolean coat; in 34% cases bathroom is in bad condition and needs repair; 23% don’t have at least 1 functioning 1000L water tank; 27% face problems in the source of drinking water; issues all exposing to high sanitation and hygiene vulnerability.Adequate access to safe WASH facilities and practices are prerequisites for health facilities to function properly and for healthy and safe schools. Unsanitary and inadequate facilities for adolescent girls in particular may discourage them from attending school. The overall status of WASH facilities in schools does not meet international guidelines, with a less than adequate ratio of toilets to students. While prioritising needs to be addressed, it must be noted that women and girls for istance are disproportionally impacted by water quantity and quality limitations affecting substandard sanitation facilities, for their own health including menstrual health and hygiene. This includes bottle-feeding using water that may not be clean, instead of exclusive breastfeeding, which increases the risk of contamination and infants contracting infections and waterborne diseases. More specifically, inaccesibility of WASH facilities in schools prevents female students from accessing education during their menstrual cycle every month. Regarding the situation in healthcare facilities, it is urgent to address the issue that 72% of PwDs don’t have access to toilet units, which leads to difficulties in meeting their basic needs and hampers their access to health services.Gaza Strip is a disaster and conflict-prone area due to severe flooding and frequent conflict. These recurrent events not only cause damage and losses to the infrastructures but also result in huge volumes of piled waste and debris in the aftermath. In post-conflict situations, affected communities produce large volumes of waste, often overwhelming the existing, albeit weak, solid waste management systems in Gaza. This results in an accumulation of waste, which often aggravates the affected communities and runs the risk of disease outbreaks and exacerbating flooding.Delivering efficient waste management services during emergency and post-conflict situations, however, is very challenging in Gaza largely due to the following factors: power disruption and shortage of electricity supply to operate landfills and transfer stations; difficulty collecting and transferring normal waste and overloaded debris from damaged homes, businesses and factories; inability to access landfills due to restriction in mobility and road blockage from debris of damaged infrastructure and/or security measures; shortage of fuel and replacement spare parts for garbage trucks; damage to truck repair facilities, community trash containers, and bulldozers; and lack of effective communication platform/system.Conflicts in Gaza revealed that municipalities are poorly equipped to respond to emergency situations and are often caught off guard to provide crucial SWM services in times of urgency. SWM institutions in Gaza have challenges in identifying and adopting appropriate contingency waste management response systems due to the lack of resources such as infrastructure, knowledge and skills, and capacity in planning for waste management services. Given the frequency and imminent nature of conflict-related emergencies, municipalities in collaboration with local authorities, emergency management personnel and solid waste managers in Gaza need to preemptively develop contingency plan and associated procedures to effectively respond to urgent situations. Finally, through the improvement of inadequate WASH facilities both at community and HH level, the proposed action aims to contribute to gender equality in the targeted communities, with a specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, PwDs and the elderly.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 – Strengthening and capacity-building of WASH committees. 3 Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPCs) are reinforced. They are involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at the community level and to guarantee better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups. CBWPCs provide support in the distribution of hygiene kits, upgrade of basic water and sanitation facilities. ACTED provides capacity-building to the committee members on WASH, SPHERE and protection standards including key PSEA messages. They also raise awareness of the project’s complaint response mechanism amongst beneficiaries. Activity 2 – Provision of PE tanks to vulnerable HHs. Based on assessments conducted by CESVI during 2022 on water condition and storage capacity in North Gaza, in order to enhance vulnerable HH’s adequate availability and storage capacity of water for drinking and hygienic use, CESVI and YEC will supply and distribute 250L- and 1000L- PE tanks to the 300 HHs targeted by activity 4 in North Gaza to enhance their storage capacity in combination with the improvement of their sanitation conditions with the rehabilitation of WASH facilities. Activity 3 – WASH in schools and Health Care Facilities (HCFs) Findings from the response framework for WASH in HCFs and Schools Vulnerability Mapping conducted in 2021 by the WC, with the active support of ACTED and CESVI, highlighting a pressing need to enhance WASH facilities in such institutions in Gaza, are still in need of being addressed as confirmed by the coordination set by the partners with institutions’ representatives (MoH and MoE). Following the technical assessment of selected schools and HCFs, in collaboration with MoE and MoH and with specific focus on needs of people with disabilities (PWDs) age- and gender- sensitive , Bills of Quantities (BOQs) are developed for 13 institutions, outlining rehabilitation and maintenance WASH needs and related action plans. CESVI is in charge of rehabilitating 6 schools in Gaza Governorate while ACTED is for 4 schools and 3 HCFs in Deir al-Balah, Rafah, and Gaza Governorates. Contractors are selected through a transparent tender process. ACTED, CESVI and YEC conduct regular follow-up and monitoring visits to ensure respect of the plan and of the WASH and SPHERE standards, as well as to provide corrective actions where needed. Activity 4- Rehabilitation and upgrade of sanitation facilities at households and level. Based on assessments carried out by the partners in 2022, thanks also to data collected during the implementation of projects recently completed (Cesvi in North Gaza, Acted in Middle and South Gaza), 500 HHs receive conditional cash assistance to upgrade sanitation facilities (upgrade of kitchen and bathroom/toilets, connection of drinking water tanks to a sink equipped with water-saving supply, and connection of their domestic water tank to the water network inside the house). A technical manual to support the rehabiliation works along with PSEA and environmental awareness flyers will be distributed to all households. CESVI supports the rehabilitation of 300 HHs in North Gaza Governorate while ACTED supports 200 HHs in Rafah, Khan Yunis, and Deir al-Balah Governorates. Work implementation is monitored through visits conducted by CESVI and ACTED to each HH to review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel instalments. Monitoring visits are also used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could face and solve them on the spot through complementary technical training and guidance. Referrals to other clusters will be made for additional needs identified that are unable to be met by the project, thus ensuring a clear exit strategy. Activity 5 – Distribution of standardized Hygiene Kits to 500 HH. Under this activity, partners provide assistance to the 500 HHs supported under activity 4 with the distribution of hygiene kits to complement the rehabilitation of HH sanitation facilities, therefore CESVI follows up the 300 HHs targeted in North Gaza Governorate, while ACTED conducts the same activity with the 200 HHs targeted in Rafah, Khan Yunis, and Deir al-Balah Governorates. CESVI and ACTED monitor the activity by visiting each HH in parallel with assistance provided for Activity 4. Also for this activity, vulnerable HHs’ needs were assessed by the partners in the frame of projects recently completed. Activity 6 – Support to Joint Service Council for SWM. In coordination with the JSC of Gaza-North Gaza (JSC GNG), CESVI addresses 8 municipalities’ urgent needs for the improvement of the waste collection service, by way of community containers to improve capacity and environmental conditions of waste collection points, and working tools and PPE kits to improve sanitation workers’ conditions. CESVI provides 200 1-m3 containers and 300 kits for sanitation workers, including protective suits, rain safety suits, safety gloves, boots, and working tools like hand-brooms, shovels, and brakes. The activity includes the procurement process and quality control to ensure meeting the service provider’s standards, and thus ensuring the population of the target municipalities the access to safe waste disposal system, and the sanitation workers proper safe working conditions. In this case, Cesvi takes advantages of its close relationships with JSC GNG established to plan environmental protection activities by supporting the operation of the solid waste management system.

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. Particular attention will be given to PwDs and female-headed HHs with young children and children with special needs. The CESVI Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) officer and ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. MEAL and AME activities will enable the gathering and reporting of detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and to measure the project outcomes of different target groups. Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the MEAL officer and AMEU, will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP and CHS standards and SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBWPC members, Protection Officer and independent monitoring staff from YEC, to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiaries’ complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that the team learns from feedbacks provided through this process. During the project, the Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPC) will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the Protection Officer. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to CESVI as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, the Protection Officer, with the support of the CBWPC, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. Were any issues to be identified, the Protection officer will offer guidance and complimentary training to beneficiaries.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the project Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM), implemented in all CESVI projects. It will be adapted to ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and considers the harmonisation with PSEA complaints and whistle-blower. The system is overseen by MEAL and AMU officer, which is independent of the delivery of activities. All beneficiaries will be explained their right to provide feedback and complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance and will be provided with small pamphlets summarizing the process and the different avenues/channels available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CFM and CRM rely on existing and solid ToRs, SoPs, field paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (<15 days) to the complainants. This CFM/CRM system will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits by staff to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is a key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of projects, and is reinforced through the CBWPCs. Beneficiaries' satisfaction is continuously monitored through regular visits and interviews regarding ongoing activities. The MEAL officer and AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. CESVI and ACTED have been implementing projects in the targeted governorates since 2010 and thus benefit from a high level of acceptance in local communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with CESVI and ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

A community-based protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at the community level. The CBWPCs (Community-based WASH Protection Committees) of the project target areas will be engaged in order to guarantee compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households, households with people with disabilities).Do No Harm: members of CBWPCs will also be actively involved in the activity of distribution of hygiene kits, in order that all beneficiaries are informed on their correct usage, in particular on items which could expose beneficiaries to risks if misused. All team members involved in the project implementation will undergo extensive training in PSEA, Protection and WASH Sphere standards to ensure they engage with the beneficiaries and take into account the specific needs of all household members. PSEA awareness materials, validated by the PSEA network, will also be distributed to all target beneficiaries. The Protection Officer together with CBWPCs’ members, will conduct additional visits to ensure that the responses to specific needs, regarding gender, age and disability, are integrated and the needs of female members are prioritized in the rehabilitation plans. Inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanisms such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance (for example items to be included in the kits), and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. A self-help approach will be adopted to implement rehabilitation and improvement of sanitation facilities at HH level (planned through cash modality) having several added values as it assigns an active role to the beneficiary in carrying on rehabilitation works of their own house, thus increasing ownership and personal satisfaction, and tends to support economic recovery in the Gaza Strip by involving the local market contractors which is more cost-efficient.Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by CBPWC members on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practices. CBWPCs will be responsible to refer to the project team any acutely vulnerable households that could have been neglected, or who would present significant vulnerabilities despite not fitting the criteria mentioned before for further investigation.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have different needs. CBWPCs will receive training on the specific needs of various groups, in order to ensure they factor them in throughout project implementation and that assistance can be adapted to beneficiaries’ needs. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, using and maintaining WASH facilities at the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanisms such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Community-based WASH Protection Committees and ACTED Protection Officer will ensure that the rehabilitation work of the WASH facilities will include adequate lighting, internal lock and privacy fencing in order to ensure women safety, security and privacy inside their house. Women will be highly encouraged to be part of the Committees, follow all the implementation and join partners team during the field visits to the households. During the awareness sessions ACTED, CESVI and YEC teams will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as “women issues” In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actively engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between females and males of proper hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. Specific sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers and children.In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected populations, partners are engaging beneficiaries in the intervention design for construction or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, both in households (families) and in public institutions (staff managing HCFs and schools). Prior to the procurement of assistance, each household and public institution will be informed on the planned assistance in order to determine if needs have significantly changed since the original needs assessment. If outstanding needs are identified through this process, the material assistance will be adapted to meet those needs, as long as it fits within the budgeted amount for the location. Community-based WASH Protection Committees will represent communities’ interests throughout the implementation process. Indeed, they will ensure that the repair to be undertaken by the household is decided in collaboration with the household members (women and male) and is in line with their priorities and needs. They will also ensure that all protection and privacy concerns faced by the household due to the substandard WASH facilities (toilets and kitchens) are addressed in priority. A similar approach will be adopted while implementing activities in public institutions (HCFs and schools), according to the needs represented by the authorities in charge (MoH and MoE) and assessed during the development of the proposal, that will be assessed again at the time of the project start.
