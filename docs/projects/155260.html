Project Summary

Although not explicitly stated as SO 6 regarding multi-sectoral programming, the program does take a multi-sectoral approach by building relationships and offering Protection awareness/trainings to sectors not traditionally included in the Protection conversation such as WASH, shelter, nutrition, etc. Community Based Protection is the core of our program through a serious of workshops with the community, facilitated by trained community volunteers, to improve understanding around Protection issues, alternatives to negative coping strategies, and self-identification/mitigation of risks. This project contract has already been signed by the (non UN) donor so major alterations are not possible.

Needs

The proposed project is in line with the strategy adopted by the protection coordination structures, taking into account the needs and beginning to move into more inclusive solutions in recognition of the protracted nature of the refugee crisis, and the need to strengthen the role of the community and community-based protection initiatives and mechanisms. As per the protection sector needs analysis, the need to strengthen the knowledge and awareness among the community on protection and gender issues as well as better include boys and men in GBV prevention and response remain as one of the key protection gaps in the camps and therefore as one of the points of the JRP sector strategy. As per the site management service monitoring (Monthly camp profile: Camp 27, September 2018), only 2% of persons benefitted from CBP activities in the targeted camp, which is less than sector standards. There is also clear need to improve camp governance based on inclusion, representation, participation and accountability. Considering the above described needs and the second sector objective, this proposal aims to support the community with capacity building aiming to activate community members that be able to spread knowledge in their camp area, raise awareness via community outreach and refer people to relevant services, where needed. With the community support and as part of the community workshops, using small cash grants, the project also aims to allow the participating community members to fix safety issues they themselves identify in the camp as potential risk to the community. Interested community members will be selected with regards to their profile, taking into consideration also their shelter locations to provide with well distributed representation from different blocks within the camp. Key for the selection of the participants is their interest and balanced targeting based the AGD approach to reach diverse gender and age groups within the community: one quarter of the community participants are adolescent boys, one quarter adolescent girls, one quarter adult men and one quarter adult women. There are direct and indirect beneficiaries targeted under the protection part of the project, where workshops, trainings target the selected and participating community members but wider awareness activities spread using the project resources and effort of the direct beneficiaries aim to reach wider community, defined as indirect beneficiaries (entire camp population). CRM: All beneficiaries will have an opportunity to report any problems including misuse of funds, quality of the trainings. The synergy of our internal CRM and existing mechanisms with in the camp will be in detail discussed during the initial phase of the project. While the general camp CRM is considered the basis for interlinked camp CRM mechanism that is simple and clear to the refugees, CRM as approached internally within the project aims to get project specific feedback to allow us to improve the activities accordingly. The system is based on regular “feedback recorder” records. PSEA and DNH policy is part of our Code of Conduct and organisational culture, it is always part of induction of all employees.DRR: Her Safety methodology allows to community to self-identify a variety of risks (with emphasis on protection issues) through risk mapping that then can be supported by very small quick impact projects to improve risks in their environment (such as better lighting on the way to latrines to decrease chance of sexual harassment/assault). Activities or outputs

1.1 Intensive training and continuous capacity building of Community Protection Trainers (CPTs) to lead Health, Safety, and Empowerment Workshops1.2. Formation of Safety Committees (community members) led by CPTs trained through participation in Her Safety Workshops1.3. Community driven risk mapping and engaging community leaders and camp management1.4. Conditional grants to Safety Committees for small-scale projects addressing self-identified protection needs1.5. Information campaigns on community self-identified topics1.6. Intensive training and capacity building of Women and Youth Empowerment Team Leaders to lead occupational therapy workshops for women and youth empowerment1.7. Formation and mentoring of WYE teams led by WYETLs and trained in occupational therapy1.8. Conditional grants to the WYETs for community-based project related to occupation The project activities will be mainly conducted with in existing facilities in the camp. The trainings are usually conducted in the trainers houses or in the safe spaces which exist in the camp. The exact location will be identified during the initial phase of the project. Social-cohesion: is a major emphasis of the Her Safety curriculum and is addressed throughout the workshops with a special emphasis on improving participation of women and girls in the social environment. The curriculum highlights active community role in the community matters and prior professional services are needed (men supporting their pregnant wives, ensuring women can walk safely outside their shelters). Workshop participants are given space to implement their ideas on how to improve safety in the camps. These small community projects aim to bring the community together to develop and work towards shared target.
