Project Summary

The current socio-economic situation is causing some of the most challenging humanitarian conditions experienced in the past ten years of crisis.
Women and girls are particularly vulnerable to protection risks, mainly GBV and sexual and reproductive health, because of gender inequality and discrimination in Syria. In the households, women and girls have taken on additional roles traditionally associated with men, including heads of households and principal breadwinners. Although they have new responsibilities, this does not automatically translate into decision-making power but contributes to increased pressure and stress for women and girls to provide for their families, often lacking any support from family members. Consequently, they are exposed to increased risks of violence within the family and at their workplace, to name a few. (HNO 2021)
Negative coping mechanisms have notably increased during 2021 due to the ongoing conflict and COVID-19 impact. The ongoing conflict has particularly prevented girls from attending schools. It is reported that 2.4 million children are out of school inside Syria, 40% of whom are girls (Syria 10 Fast Facts, UNICEF-March 2021). Also, child labour and child marriage reported as frequently occurring in 22% and 18% of assessed communities, respectively (HNO 2021).
As a result of the prolonged crisis, schools and hospitals were wrecked by weakened protection, security and justice systems. Thus, women and girls lack access to safe spaces and services where they can learn about their sexual and reproductive health rights (SRHR), discuss concerns and issues around GBV, early marriage, harassment and other topics without the fear of social and peer stigma, and receive psychosocial support as needed.
Taking into consideration the Above trends the project aims to contribute to the protection and empowerment of women and girls affected by the Syrian crisis and improve the mental health of approximately 700 women and girls. The project will achieve its goal by establishing (7) girl clubs in target areas as a safe space for women and girls. The term ‘safe,’ in the present context, refers to the absence of trauma, excessive stress, violence (or fear of violence), or abuse. It is a space where women and girls, being the intended beneficiaries, feel comfortable and enjoy the freedom to express themselves without the fear of judgment or harm. In these clubs, women and girls can learn about healthy lifestyles, improve their life skills, receive information on and discuss issues related to SRHR, GBV and mental health, access safe and non-stigmatizing multi-sectorial GBV response services and socialize or re-build their social networks. Two trained female facilitators will facilitate activities in each club and identify and prioritize approximately 300 women and girls requiring psychosocial support to refer them to mental health services, namely AKF’s Health Counseling Center (HCC) – the only facility in Salamieh District delivering services for mental health. The project will also educate the local community members, leaders and parents about the gender-specific risk factors of women and girls and the impact on mental health through awareness-raising campaigns. (5) Community Management Groups (CMG; 122 members) in Salamieh sub-districts consisting of members of NGOs, Community Based Organizations (CBOs), Mental Health experts, and community leaders will support the campaigns and provide feedback on the project. Sensitizing boys and reducing stigma about mental health, SRHR and GBV subjects is also a priority which will be achieved through the establishment of (4) boys clubs in target areas where 300 boys can learn about and discuss these issues in a stigma-free and youth-friendly space.

4 - Needs

Salamieh District has a population of 223,935 people, 20.2% of whom are youth (46.3% female youth). According to a study conducted by AKF in 2019, 80% of the population in Salamieh live below the national poverty line, 94% of households (HH) are food insecure, and vulnerable IDPs and residents also report high reliance on assistance to meet basic needs. The unemployment rate is particularly high among females (85% compared to 54% males). The imbalance between HH income and expenses contributes to negative coping mechanisms adopted by households.About 36% of children (age:0-18) in Salamieh are out of school due to the high cost of education (SYP 13,900/month/person), while 6.6% of all children in Salamieh are engaged in child labour. In 2019, AKF consulted with girls and female youth, parents, NGOs and community leaders to identify the social, cultural, economic, and structural barriers to women and girl’s empowerment. The evaluation determined the following: lack of decision-making ability; high risk of SGVB; personal safety in and around the school; lack of safe spaces and networks where they can share their concerns without the fear of social and peer stigma; the absence of trusted resources about sexual and reproductive health; the need of community awareness, including parents, regarding the transitions adolescents experience during puberty and how to address them. Adolescent girls reported feeling vulnerable to early marriage, as they face family pressure to marry due to financial burdens. The challenges and barriers facing adolescent girls and female youth are numerous with an impact on individual mental health, emotional distress, life stage transitions, aspirations, independence, and self-confidence.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

● Female adolescents and youth might face discrimination/abuse from their peers and/or the wider community such as bullying or negative judgements.Likelihood: medium.Impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will mitigate this risk by equipping facilitators with proper training on how to deal with such cases and establish clear session rules to create an atmosphere of respect, empathy, solid and cooperative relationships among girls. AKF will also mobilize community organizations to expand awareness of project objectives and the significance of such initiatives. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of abuse/exploitations and take actions against them.● Facilitators might be criticized, accused, or even show bias in their relations with girls; favouring some over others.Likelihood: low.Impact: medium.Mitigation measures: AKF will create a code of conduct that will be explained and signed by facilitators. AKF will also closely monitor facilitators adherence to the code and interfere when needed.● Leakage of beneficiaries’ information outside the sessions to the wider community. Likelihood: medium.Impact: high. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure facilitators sign a confidentiality agreement and hold private sessions for special cases.● Safety and Security concerns to find community places that are safe and easily accessible to all female adolescents and youth. Likelihood: low.Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure a safe and secure environment along with the availability of WASH facilities where sessions are delivered.COVID-19 spread concerns: AKF will apply all COVID-19 preventive measures in all planned activities.
