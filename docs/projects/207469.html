Project Summary

DRC's objective is to prevent and respond to protection threats identified in camps 6, 10, 8W, 8E, 11, and 12 Palongkhali, and Rajapalong host communities. The organization will offer Protection services, including case management, provision of Individual Protection Assistance, protection information and support, community-based protection/ community engagement, protection monitoring, awareness raising, capacity building in camps 6, 8E, 8W, 10, 11, and 12 with a focus on avoiding service overlap. The project aims to enhance synergies by integrating with other protection and non-protection actors, providing case management services, and making individual protection assistance available wherever possible and appropriate.
Building on past successes, DRC plans to increase funding for community-based safety and security initiatives. The organization will facilitate community-led initiatives, awareness and sensitization programs, case management, protection information and support, referrals and follow-ups, protection monitoring, and community-led initiatives. These efforts will strengthen existing community groups to better detect, prevent, and respond to protection risks.

DRC will conduct disaggregated age, gender, and diversity analysis to inform and enhance its programming response across all protection initiatives. The organization will expand protection monitoring initiatives to contribute to the Joint Protection Monitoring platform and advocacy efforts. Improved sampling methodologies and enhanced capacity of staff and volunteers will ensure systematic data collection on specific vulnerabilities such as Persons with Disabilities (PwDs), single-headed households, and minorities.

DRC is committed to making all centers accessible to persons with disabilities and setting targets focusing on their needs. Increasing the inclusion of women in the community's volunteer force is also a priority. To avoid duplication of effort, DRC caseworkers will maintain close communication with existing complaint and feedback procedures, collaborating with the CFM and CFP to ensure clients are not already receiving assistance and forwarding non-protection situations appropriately.

DRC will provide Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training to its employees, with volunteers actively contributing to the network's SEA data. Active participation in the Protection Sector and related sub-sectors is part of DRC's strategy.

The organization plans to increase the frequency of sharing protection monitoring data with the sector and sub-sectors, providing summarized overviews of observed risks in the camps while complying with routine reporting requirements.

To ensure refugees have access to assistance without harm, DRC will continue providing protection mainstreaming training to non-protection actors, aligning with the resources and guidance provided by the protection sector.

Ongoing coordination efforts with RRRC and CiCs will prioritize timely access and avoid resource gaps and duplications. Regular briefings on DRC's operations and advocacy efforts are shared with CiCs to ensure alignment and sustainability, fostering an integrated approach with other actors and partners.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Based on the recent assessments (MSNA' 2023 drat) and findings, DRC protection monitors and case workers in the camps have observed an alarming rate of Protection challenges and increased protection risks and threats in the camps and host communities. According to the MSNA 2023, refugee households expressed higher concern (60%) about the protection risks compared to host community households (33%), where concerns include crime, Violence, and potential affiliation with criminal groups. Additionally, 37% of refugees reported feeling unsafe to move alone at night. As of the Joint Protection Monitoring and DRC Protection Monitoring Reports September 2022, the primary protection is Abduction/kidnapping, Killing, Human trafficking, Forced recruitment and violation of rights by criminal groups and influential individuals. Based on the MSNA report 2023, abduction for ransom and forced disappearance in the camps rose alarmingly in comparison to the same reporting period for 2022, representing a 490% overall increase. A total of 693 protection incidents were documented in this quarter (July to September), where 607 protection incidents were recorded in camps (camps 6, 8E, 8W, 10, 11 and 12) and 86 protection incidents were recorded in host communities (Rajapalong and Palongkhali). Most of the incidents documented this quarter were safety and security incidents of the violent type (424 incidents out of 613) in camps, representing more than half of the total incidents for this reporting period. Concerns involving violations of housing, land, and property (HLP) rights (75 out of 613) and restrictions to accessing essential services (9 out of 613) have been identified as a common trend in camps.Furthermore, concerns about sexual and gender-based Violence (SGBV) (67 out of 613) and population movements (32 out of 433) have been constantly growing throughout all camps. The existence of criminal and armed groups in the camps has worsened security, increasing the dangers women and girls face while not at home. Volunteers and armed group members frequently try to break into the shelters of widows and women-headed households where male family members are absent, and women and girls often report being physically or sexually assaulted by male members of the community, particularly while visiting the latrines at night or during night patrolling (Source: DRC protection monitoring, July 2023). Anonymized data from DRC's case management suggests an increase in reports of child physical abuse, child marriage, child labor, and neglect and findings from the Combined MSNA'2023 support this. According to MSNA 2023, Child Marriage is prevalent due to a few reasons, such as cultural practice, lack of education, economic pressure and protection concerns. Protection monitoring in DRC ensures systematic data collection on various concerns, including Safety and security, SGBV, Population movements, HLP violations, Violence against children and access to humanitarian/protection assistance, allowing for the identification of protection requirements. Protection sector, GBV, and CP Sub-Sectors share reports by contributing to the needs assessment. According to DRC protection monitoring reports, recent months have seen a decline in the protection environment in the camps. Increases in physical abuse, forced night patrolling, forced relocation, killing, torture, and enforced disappearances committed by criminal gangs against the camp population. Increases in forced recruitment by armed organizations. The monitoring results show that many people living in the camp are experiencing high levels of anxiety, mistrust, and uncertainty. Using Violence and coercion, armed players are not only expanding their influence and control in the camps but are also progressively fulfilling the responsibilities previously played by humanitarian organizations.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project aligns with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) and recognizes the importance of addressing critical protection needs for individuals at heightened protection risks. In adherence to Rationalization Principle #3, advocating for an integrated approach, DRC aims to fill gaps in protection services for women, men, adolescent girls/boys, and persons with disabilities. This involves incorporating components such as protection monitoring, case management, community-based protection, and gender-based violence within an integrated framework. DRC also seeks to enhance inter-sectoral camp-based coordination through CCCM, ensuring coordinated service delivery to refugees and host communities while maintaining collaborative relations with the Government/CiCs and other sector agencies. Following the Do No Harm Principle, DRC continues to involve beneficiaries in the planning and implementation stages, identifying the needs and vulnerabilities of refugees and host communities. Adhering to Principle #4 for ensuring predictability and continuity, DRC ensures program stability alongside other donor-funded projects by DANIDA, ECHO, FCDO, etc. The EcRec component integrates with protection and livelihood/economic recovery, promoting interventions in both camp and host community settings to strengthen relations with refugees, the Government, and camp partners. Additionally, DRC engages regularly with the District Commissioner, RRRC, sector, and other government agencies. Conducting a comprehensive local partner mapping and selection exercise, DRC expands its program with national and local partners, extending valued services beyond the project period. The project prioritizes safety, dignity, and meaningful access and participation principles, ensuring the active involvement of refugees.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

DRC has been strategically integrating protection across various programs within the organization, including CCCM and economic recovery initiatives. This integration extends to encompass Gender-Based Violence (GBV) alongside other critical protection components such as case management, community-based protection, protection monitoring, and economic recovery efforts.The DRC protection team is dedicated to ensuring the seamless integration of these aspects into all the aforementioned sectors and at the camp level where DRC operates. This involves conducting thorough needs assessments and delivering comprehensive training sessions for both protection and non-protection staff. The goal is to enhance awareness and understanding of protection issues across diverse sectors.Furthermore, the DRC protection team is actively engaged in producing information management products tailored to the protection sector. These products serve to streamline and optimize the collection and dissemination of crucial protection-related information.To fortify the capacity of sectors at the camp level, the protection focal points receive Training of Trainers (ToT) facilitated by trained protection experts. This approach not only empowers the focal points but also contributes to a cascading effect, fostering a more knowledgeable and capable workforce within the camp-level sectors.In essence, DRC's commitment to mainstreaming protection extends beyond theoretical integration to practical implementation, leveraging assessments, training initiatives, and information management strategies to create a robust and comprehensive approach across diverse programs and at the camp level.

3.3 - Age & Disability

DRC prioritizes age and diversity to ensure effective interventions. Actively enhancing service accessibility, particularly for individuals with disabilities and minority groups, DRC seeks technical support from Handicap International (HI). HI conducts regular assessments in DRC centers, offering insights that guide DRC in refining its services. DRC Bangladesh has a dedicated action plan for disability inclusion, showcasing its commitment to creating an inclusive environment. Guided by the Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) policy, DRC ensures interventions are responsive to diverse needs. A key strategy involves continuous efforts to increase service accessibility for all age groups and genders, aligning with the broader goal of fostering a diverse-friendly environment. Central to DRC's success is the active involvement of beneficiaries, ensuring accurate program alignment with community needs. Emphasizing data collection in line with AGDM guidance, DRC stays well-informed for informed decision-making and adaptive programming.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

DRC is committed to engaging Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) principles in its protection intervention, placing a strong emphasis on the Community-Based Feedback and Mechanisms (CFM) framework. Recognizing the paramount importance of open and efficient communication, DRC will establish a robust CFM system within the camps and host communities. This system empowers refugees and host communities to voice their concerns, provide feedback, and actively participate in decision-making processes. Utilizing accessible channels like phone hotlines, community focus group discussions, and feedback boxes, DRC ensures that the voices of these communities are not only heard but also integral to shaping interventions. The CFM not only enhances transparency and accountability but also fosters a sense of ownership among community members. Regular feedback loops enable DRC to continuously adapt and reshape its protection interventions, ensuring they remain relevant and responsive to the evolving needs and concerns of the affected populations.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

DRC teams conduct comprehensive awareness-raising sessions for refugees and local communities, emphasizing the importance of preventing and addressing exploitation, harassment, and abuse. These sessions focus on recognizing warning signs and identifying appropriate reporting channels.DRC has established clear and confidential reporting systems, enabling individuals to express concerns or report incidents without fear of retaliation. Dedicated response teams, including PSEAH focal points in each camp and host community, ensure swift reporting to the national focal point, who then involves HQ for timely and suitable responses. In cases where necessary, thorough investigations are conducted to ensure accountability.All team members and focal points undergo training on their safeguarding responsibilities. This includes recognizing signs of exploitation, harassment, and abuse, and understanding the necessary steps when such incidents are identified.DRC has developed comprehensive PSEAH guidance and a robust code of conduct, clearly outlining expected behaviors and prohibiting any form of exploitation, harassment, or abuse. These guidelines serve as a transparent framework for all stakeholders.DRC collaborates with the PSEA Network within the country, facilitated by the UN, to ensure compliance with international standards and meaningful information sharing that strengthens the system.DRC's commitment to safeguarding surpasses mere compliance—it is a fundamental principle ingrained in DRC's global operations. Fostering a safe environment is a shared responsibility. Through ongoing training, transparent communication, and proactive measures, we aim to uphold the protection of all involved parties.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) is seamlessly integrated into the activities implemented through community-based protection programming. Furthermore, DRC teams play an active role as part of PERU, the emergency response teams coordinated by the sector and mobilized in the event of natural hazards. This integration ensures that DRR measures are embedded within community-based protection activities, enhancing the overall resilience and preparedness of the communities served by DRC. The active participation of DRC teams in PERU showcases a proactive approach to emergency response, enabling swift and effective interventions in the face of natural disasters to mitigate their impact on the affected populations.
