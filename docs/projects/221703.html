Project Summary

The Project Aims to create and provide essential life-saving integrated primary Health Care Services and emergency response to affected vulnerable IDPS, Returnees and residents of Abyei Administrative Areas, Aweil South (IPC-4)of Northern Bhar Al Ghazal state and Bor South(IPC-3) ,Twic East(ipc-4) , Duk(IPC-4) ,Canal Pigi(IPC-4) and Uror (IPC-4)Counties of Jonglei state. The Project seeks to bridge significant gaps to the most affected vulnerable populations in conflict- and flood-affected areas, prioritizing women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities.
The Project Target 62,974 People 54% children and 46% adults, segregated as: Males 49.% (30,857), females 51%(32,117), 7%(4,408 ) elderly persons and people with disabilities represent 15%(9,446) .Women 24%, Men 23%, Girls 27.3% and Boys 26%. By Locations; Uror has 24,382 (39%) segregated as follows host Communities(17,067),IDPs(3901,) and returnees (3,413);Duk County 13,558(21%) host Communities(9,491),IDPs(2,169) and returnees (1,898), Abyei administrative Area 9,104 (14%) host Communities(6,373) IDPs(1,457) and returnees (1,275), Aweil South 7,368 (12%) host Communities(5,158) IDPs(825) and returnees (116) Bor South4,280 (7%) host Communities(2,961) IDPs(677); Canal/Pigi 2,652 (4%) host Communities(1.856) IDPs(424) and returnees (371) and Twic East 1.680 (3 %) host Communities(1,176) IDPs(269) and returnees (235).
The Project will focus on integrated life-saving interventions, reducing morbidity and Mortality by timely detection and response to any outbreak and coordinated health, Nutrition WatSan and Protection services in our targeted locations. Through the supported chain of health facilities, the project will provide the following activities: curative consultation, preventive and promotional and capacity building of health workers, screening, treatment and referral of MAM/SAM cases with medical complications. Training of health workers on MIYCN best practices, conduct WASH-IPCs, distribution of safe reproductive kits and sanitary items and provision of sanitary facilities for both gender. GVB oriented and mental health services at the both level static and MMUs. The project will support 18 chain of health facilities in form of 10 static sites (PHCCs &PHCUs)4 PHCCs, 6 PHCUs and 8 MMUs across the three areas we targeting. Abyei administrative areas will have 2 static sites (1PHCC &1PHCU) and 1MMU,Aweil South ,2 PHCC and 2 MMUs, Uror with 1PHCC,2PHCU and 1MMU,Duk with 1PHCC,1PHCU and 1 MMU, Canal/Pigi 1phcu and 1MMU,Twic East with 1MMU and Bor South 1PHCU respectively
SMC project activities budget estimate is 1,200,000 USD for a population of 62,974 beneficiaries.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, characterized by a protracted humanitarian crisis, widespread displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to basic services, including healthcare [OCHA], 2023).In 2024, an estimated 9 million people needed humanitarian assistance. Out of these, 6.3 million need health services, with the health cluster targeting 3.2 million in 2025.South Sudan still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide essential lifesaving intervention in all the sectors and the people suffer from Hunger, violence and poor health. Women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities are the most vulnerable groups, having limited access to health care and face high risks of death and illness. SMC’s HNRP for 2025 targets, Abyei, Aweil south,Uror,Duk,Bor South, Twic East and Canal/Pigi targeting returnees, IDPs & vulnerable host communities. These areas are mainly inhabited by pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, land disputes, interclan/revenge attacks resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility & humanitarian crisis. Abyei and Northern Bhar Al Ghazal are receiving people from Sudan due to ongoing war since April 2023. The floods have destroyed homes, crops and infrastructure, disrupting health and education services and heightening disease risks. IOM reports 76,230 displaced people. Abyei (7,500), Uror ( 22,290) and Twic East with (55,349). The Health needs are enormous, complicated with contracted economy, Flooding, conflicts, damaged infrastructure, IDPs, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, IPC measures, food insecurity, threat of disease outbreaks, limited health facility functionality, Inadequate Human resource and stockout of essential medicines. The Cholera outbreak in Renk, for the last few Months, is a great threat to our operational areas in, Canal/Pigi, Uror, Duk, Twic East and Bor South, where river access and roads are connecting easily. SMC Interventions will focus on the most affected vulnerable populations in conflict- and flood-affected areas, prioritizing women, children, and the elderly, people with disabilities and IDPs & returnees from neighboring counties. SMC will focus on an integrated interventions to prevent and respond to disease outbreaks and other public emergencies, with support from other sectors, such as nutrition, water, Sanitation, Hygiene and Food security. As of 2023, only 44% of South Sudan’s population is within walking distance of a functional health facility, and in Abyei, Jonglei and Northern Bhar Al Ghazal, Persistent attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, cattle raids and revenge attacks resulted in the displacement of at least 10,000 people, several killings, increased incidents of SGBV, child abductions and looting/destruction of property and resources. Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are acute, particularly among displaced populations who have experienced conflict and trauma. Despite this, access to MHPSS services remains negligible. Similarly, gender-based violence (GBV) is a pervasive issue, particularly for women and girls in vulnerable settings such as IDP camps, where services for survivors are extremely limited. The July 2024 GBV health analysis revealed only 28 per cent of health facilities provide GBV care, with 43 per cent offering services. Lower-level facilities struggle with staffing and infrastructure, limiting early intervention to referrals. The Quality of health care services is also compromise by inadequate Infrastructure, limited supplies, long waiting hours, safe water scarcity, poor maternal &Child health services and lack of follow up. The targeted locations are priority 1&2 with severity levels of IPC 3-4 Next Year. The vast majority of the targeted population are host communities (44,082), IDPs (10,076) and returnees (8,816) Population.

2. Project activities

The activities will be focusing on delivering life-saving health services contributing to the reduction of preventable morbidity and mortality from infectious, non- communicable and Epidemic-prone diseases related to acute food insecurity, floods and subnational violence in the targeted areas of Abyei Administrative areas, Aweil South in Northern Bhar Al Ghazal state And Bor south, Duk,Twic East, Uror and Canal/Pigi counties of Jonglei state.1. Support 18 static and Medical Mobiles clinic to improve access to life-saving essential health services including maternal, child and adolescent health),sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, and provision of GBV and MPHSS services to the vulnerable population including those living with disability. 2. Conduct EPI services through Routine/campaigns Immunizations activities against vaccines preventable Diseases targeting children Under 5 Years and mothers in their Child bearing ages.3. Conduct screening, treatment and referral of all under 5 year’s children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) for SAM/MAM2 cases at our facilities and surrounding villages.4. Training of Health workers on case management, integrated disease surveillance and response & EWARS, CMR, MHPHSS, WASH-IPC, Hygiene promotion health education and MIYCN best practices at the facilities. 5. Provision of basic medical Equipment’s & Supplies and minor construction of 4 PHCCs and 4 PHCUs in Duk, Bor South, Aweil South, Uror, Canal/Pigi and Abyei Areas. 6. Provide technical and Operational assistance to the health facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure service Quality and compliance to the project standards at our operational locations.
