Project Summary

The main objective of WASH response is to improve access to safe drinking water, adequate sanitation and hygiene practices for refugee population, returnees, displaced persons and host communities in South West and North West region of Cameroon.
NRC will build new water points and rehabilitate dilapidated water points to address the gaps associated with access to adequate water supply. In the establishment of new or improved water point/sources, NRC will use consultative ways together with baseline survey to gather information in order to prioritise based on urgency of the needs. The overall activities shall involve rehabilitation and or upgrading of water systems and provision of new water sources.
NRC will ensure that water is safe for drinking through provision of the adequate water treatment equipment, regular monitoring, and continuous engagement of the community in the activities. Water trucking will be explored where appropriate during initial phases of the response but with a clear exit strategy. NRC will form and train WASH Committees on water quality management through the management of chlorination points. This will be blended with trainings on the use of water quality treatment materials, monitoring of safe levels of free residual chlorine.
NRC will facilitate the construction of latrines in schools and intervene in locations requiring emergency latrines. In addition, NRC will promote construction of family/household latrines as opposed to communal latrines that have proven difficult to clean and maintain. While latrine use, cleanliness and maintenance will be the responsibility of the beneficiaries, NRC will support the latter with training, sanitation cleaning/construction tools, and related hygiene promotion activities.
NRC will consult with women and girls before agreeing on appropriate latrine locations while also considering important factors. In order to ensure that interventions are gender responsive, latrines will be located in safe locations within reach of the households, while ensuring that adequate privacy is provided. NRC will also guide on the design, building materials, size, while also ensuring that the target beneficiaries will feel safe while using those sanitation facilities. If those women and girls deem that security measures such as provision of flashlights are necessary, NRC will meet their needs to the extent possible.
In hygiene promotion, the main focus for NRC will be to address problems and needs revolving around behaviour change practices. Hygiene promotion activities with focus on cholera prevention will be characterized by household visits for hygiene sensitization. Hygiene promotion will also be through the use of information, education and communication posters with messaging on proper hygiene practices like handwashing at critical times, safe excreta and waste disposal, safe water chain and safe water management, with the aim of preventing WASH related diseases.
NRC will target vulnerable populations affected by displacement, IDP and Host communities, in North West and South West regions of Cameroon.

Besoins / Needs

Access to water for drinking, cooking and washing :The major WASH problems and needs revolve around old and dilapidated infrastructure, limited and damaged water points, polluted water sources due to proximity to areas where open defecation is rampant, overcrowding at sanitation facilities due to higher latrine user versus latrine availability ratio, poor water quality from water sources and limited community ownership and participation in service delivery. In addition to limited access to livelihoods, unemployment rates remain high and low income amongst the IDPs and their host / surrounding communities. Findings from the WASH KAP survey undertaken by NRC in January 2019 indicated that 50% of the population had limited access to water, with 33% of the displaced population accessing their drinking water from the streams which were evidently turbid. Only 20% have access to improved water sources that include taps. Water quality was also a concern with findings indicating that 93% of the population do not treat their drinking water. Water per capita was approximately at 7 liters per person per day as defined by the number of storage containers, available water sources, number of people living in the household and frequency of water collection. Access to gender sensitive latrines and Improve hygiene practice : Baseline assessments conducted by NRC in October 2019 in the North West and South West ￼revealed that 16% of the IDP population practiced open defecation. Amongst the population who reported that they had latrines, 32% of the latrines had unlockable doors making the latrines uncomfortable to use. Children under 5 years were also reported to be practicing open defecation due to lack of potties and child friendly latrines. Women and girls living in the bushes lack menstrual hygiene products due to lack of resources and access to the products, which affects their dignity and reproductive health.
