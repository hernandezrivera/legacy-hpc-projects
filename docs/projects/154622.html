Project Summary

Project) Installation of solar panel systems for 6 boreholes in Daka mountain in Sharia complex in Semel District
Summary of those interventions:
The civil war of Syria since 2012 and the security deterioration caused by ISIS in Mosul in June 2014, followed by more severe displacement from Sinjar same year in August; forced thousands of civilians to seek safety in Duhok Governorate and other parts of KRI. This mass influx caused sharp increase in the population in Duhok governorate especially Sumel district both in camps and non-camp settlements. According to IOM and BRHA, the number of IDPs in Duhok governorate is 58,320 families, 329,920 individuals, more than half of them, 31,637 families, 189,822 individuals live in Sumel district. Sharia complex is located in the east of Sumel which will be planned to installation of solar panel by this project , now is 4,450 IDP families, 25,775 individuals, living in either 1,170 tents, 2,692 unfinished buildings or rent houses according to Faida Mayor office. The Kurdistan Regional Government, especially Duhok governorate has responded to the daily needs of increasing population of refugees and IDPs, especially tackling for shortage and infrequently supply of electricity and water. Currently in Sharia complex in Faida sub-district, water is provided from 3 sources below.
A) 6 boreholes in Daka Mountain (Khrshenya Boreholes)
B) 2 boreholes in Daka area
C) Zawa 10,000m3 storage
A) 6 boreholes in Daka Mountain (Khrshenya BH) and B) 2 boreholes in Daka area feed to Sharia complex. (C) Zawa 10,000 m3 storage feeds to Sharia complex and also the other settlement for IDPs, refugees and host communities in Faida sub-district (Domiz compound, Domiz1, 2 refugee camps, Kabarto1, 2 IDP camps, Warveen and War city)
Out of 6 boreholes in Daka mountain (Khrshenya BH), 2 of them were constructed during 1988 to 1989 while the other 4 were constructed in 2012 after IDP’s influx in order to meet the needs of increasing population. All of six boreholes are operated by national grid electricity and generators.
SInce PWJ has been working for IDPs, refugees and host communities in KRI for more than 20 years and has an experience in installation of solar panel system for boreholes in Gawilan refugee camp in Duhok governorate, Basirma and Darashakran refugee camp in Erbil governorate, through implementation of two projects, installation of solar panel system for boreholes will provide a sustainable and environment-friendly solution for water supply. Solar panel system, as a clean source of energy, can overcome the shortage of national grid, and also prevent the pollution caused by generators as well as ensuring the stable water supply. Since the Iraq is rich in sunlight, the solar panel system will contribute to the cost-effective operation of boreholes compared to purchasing the huge amount of diesel for generators.
Objectives:
1. To ensure the sustainable and stable water supply for IDPs and host communities in Sharia complex
2. To improve cost-effectiveness of water system operation and to reduce the household expenditure on water
Expected results:
1. Total of 6 boreholes will gain a sustainable source of energy, and beneficiaries will be able to access to safe water more regularly.
2. Financial burden on local government and beneficiaries will be reduced.
Targets:
Sharia Complex in Faida sub-district (non-camp): IDPs: 4,450 families, 25,772 individuals, Host Communities: 8,230 families, 66,511 individuals,

Needs

- Problems: 6 boreholes (Khrshenya BH) in Daka Mountain are operated by national grid electricity and by 3 generators in case of national grid cut. National grid electricity frequently faces the cut off especially in summer because of high demand of use of electricity rather than other seasons. However, Faida water department reported that irregular supply of diesel from the government for those generators due to the economic and political situation in KRI has been leading to the less working hours of boreholes and consequently making water supply unstable in this area. The purchase of diesel, which is about 9,000 litres per month on average by local authorities, is a financial burden for them. In addition, use of generators have caused pollution of smoke and noises in the area. Due to such unstable water supply to Sharia complex, people in this area have reported that they can only access to water for 2 hours every 4 days, and 47 liters per person per day. This water is supplied by 3 sources mentioned in Project Summary (A)(B) and (C); (A) can supply 8 liters, and (B) and (C) can supply 39 liters. Therefore, since this amount of water is not enough and water supply is irregular, they need to buy water from water trucking, posing financial burden to them. - Needs: Following coordination with DoW of Duhok proposed location and need and DoW of Faida gave us to this project more information and planning for future, in order to ensure the stable water supply from 6 boreholes (Khrshenya BH) to Sharia complex, PWJ will install solar panel system consisting from 72 solar panels to each borehole. This will ensure at least 9 hours of borehole operation during the day so as to increase the amount of water supply to 28 liters per person per day, and total amount will increase to 67 liters by 3 sources. It could be expected that beneficiaries in Sharia complex will gain more access to water from every 4 days to every 2 days at least. Thus, ensuring the water supply from six boreholes in Daka mountain (KhrshenyaBH) will benefit 12,680 families.

Activities

This project will installing the solar panel for the 6 boreholes in Daka mountain in Sharia complex. It will fill the gap of national grid electricity cut during the daytime and will lead to the less operation of generators thus less consumption of diesel. Planned activities and methodologies of implementations are below.1. Coordination and communication with stakeholders. 2. Invitation to bid (tender distribution), Tender opening and evaluation process, Contract award and site handover. 3. Installation of Solar Panel System (According to the approved BoQ).4. On the job training for technical staff selected by DoW on installation, repair, replacement, operation and maintenance of solar panel system.5. Personnel, transportation, and communication supported by PWJ6. Supplies (Materials and equipment) provided by the selected contractor.7. Documentation of the project and a sign board on the site provided.8. Operating and testing of the system.9. Initial handover of the project at completion of construction.10. Final handover after 6-month maintenance period.11. Daily monitoring during construction terms.12. Post monitoring after handover of the project. Methodologies of implementations are below.1. Coordination with local authorities and cluster- PWJ will coordinate with local authorities and other stakeholders throughout the project duration and will update its progress at coordination meeting and on Activity Info. 2. Selection of contractor- Company specialized in solar panel system will be selected through invitation to bid (ITB) or Request for Proposal (RFP) following PWJ’s procurement procedure. After the selection of company, the contractor is responsible for the procurement and transportation of materials to the site with close coordination with PWJ’s supervising engineer.3. Construction- Construction will be undertaken following the approved Bill of Quantities (BoQ), design and layout with all technical specifications. It will be supervised by PWJ’s supervising engineer with close coordination with Directorate of Water (DoW). During the construction, PWJ’s supervising engineer and the contractor will give training to technical staffs from DoW on daily basis on installment, repair, replacement, maintenance and operation of solar panel system. 4. Handover- Solar panel systems will be handed over to DoW on completion of construction after testing of the system. The contractor will provide 6 months of maintenance period and it will be responsible for the repair and maintenance of the system in case required during this period. Final handover will be conducted after the maintenance period and DoW will be responsible for maintenance, repair, replacement and operation. 5. Monitoring- For each site, a construction assistant and two field monitors will conduct the daily monitoring, which will be shared to a supervising engineer on daily basis during construction terms. Post monitoring will be conducted 1 month after initial handover. PWJ’s monitoring team will interview the beneficiaries and local concerned authorities to identify, collect and analyze key indicators of each projects, and evaluate the impact of this project.

Protection Risk Analysis

Projects will bring mainly construction work on the site. It is crucial to take consider into possibility of occurring any accidents during contraction works on the project site; how to avoid from affecting people around the project sites, how to mitigate any risk of accidents.

Mitigation Measures

For any accidents on contraction works on the project site, PWJ will plan to implement all construction activities under supervising by PWJ an engineers and two contraction- assistants. Through the procurement process following its procurement guideline, PWJ will do the best procedure on the selection of qualified construction companies who has not only the best technical capacities but also risk management skills. Construction work will be taken consider into avoiding from any accidents that affecting people in the camp and host communities, especially for children, women and elderly people who are mainly staying around project site during day time.

How is your project accountable to affected population

For shortage of water supply in the project site, PWJ will take consider into reaching to longer time to operate water pumping so as to supply much water. This will bring big impact to enable to mitigate people's suffering from shortage water. Enough amount of water per each household will contribute women's house works and make people proper hygiene activities. For disease control, proper amount of water supply will enable people to keep clean and healthy daily life. In summer, especially for children and elderly people, water cooler usage can prevent from heat syndrome caused from staying overheating room. To involve affected populations in the project, before implementing projects, PWJ program and technical team will communicate with local authority, muctars (village focal person) and people in communities where covering water supply so as to get to know their problems and needs. For designing of project site, PWJ will welcome to local authority for participation in so as to try to reflect their idea on the design. During implementing project, PWJ engineer will supervise all construction works by contractors and continue to communicate with local authorities and muctars. To do feedback, PWJ will have post monitoring after completed implementation each project after one month later. PWJ monitoring team will arrange Focus group discussions (FGDs) and key-informant interviews among local authorities and people staying in the project site of host communities. Women and vulnerable people also necessary to be involved through not only the FDGs but also household visiting to monitor their satisfaction of water supplying, get recommendation from beneficiaries to reflect on the next project. After handover to DoW on completion of construction after testing of the system, PWJ technical team will also invite engineers from local authorities and solar panel caretakers to technical training so as to get skills for maintenance that how to use and maintain more sustainable and properly.
