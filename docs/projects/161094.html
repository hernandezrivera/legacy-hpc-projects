Project Summary

South Sudan has been afflicted by protracted conflict, emergent crises, and harsh environmental conditions notably seasonal flooding. These have contributed to weakening of the health system over the years resulting in reduced access to and utilization of quality primary health care services. South Sudan has some of the worst indicators globally including a maternal mortality rate of 789 per 100,000 live births. The most vulnerable groups in the society including women and girls, children under five years of age, people living with disability (PWDs), mental health illness, and the elderly among other vulnerable groups continue to be among the most affected. The proposed project targets 17,247 (8,968 F; 8,279 M) people in need as follows; 10,845 (5,639 F; 5,205 M) in Aweil East County, 1,594 (829 F; 765 M) in Koch County and 4,809 (2,501 F; 2,308 M) in Panyijar County. This will be inclusive of 5,174 IDPs spread out as follows in Aweil East County (20%), Koch County (8%) and Panyijar County (72%) and 1,897 IDP returnees as follows in Aweil East County (2.6%), Koch County (72%) and Panyijar County (25.4%).
The proposed project will target 2 static health facilities in Aweil East; Majokaken PHCU and Malualdit PHCU. In Koch, the project will target Patit PHCU and in Panyijar County, the project will operate 1 mobile health team which will offer gap filling services in villages which are cut off from essential health services by seasonal flooding and the swamps. In Nyal Payam, Panyijar County the IRC will run one Stabilisation Center based at the Nyal PHCC which will serve as a referral nutrition facility for the County. The SC will support the continuum of care for children under 5 diagnosed with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications before discharging them to outpatient treatment facilities. IRC will leverage on existing linkages to support clients accessing services in supported health facilities to reduce missed opportunities and ensure holistic care. Referral for tertiary level health care will be supported using existing referral pathways through the County Health Departments. Vunerable populations including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) will be linked to existing IRC child protection programs.
The IRC through recruitment of qualified health workers in particular to support maternal and health services will result in improved maternal and child health outcomes. The IRC will integrate and strengthen the prevention of mother to child transmission PMTCT of HIV/AIDS and screening of pregnant mothers of Syphilis during Antenatal visits. All complicated new cases identified will be referred for appropriate care at the PHCC. The project will leverage on existing IRC projects in the counties covered to provide integrated holistic care for vulnerable populations including women and girls. Children screened positive for acute malnutrition will be referred to IRC static and mobile nutrition sites for continuity of care whereas women and girls will be subjected to a voluntary GBV screening at the consultation clinics and referred to the nearest GBV one stop center for psychosocial support and other services.
The support will further include training staff on IDSR, EWARS, IPC, Disability inclusion in health care and CCSAS training to ensure quality health services are provided. The project will support the procurement of drugs and medical supplies inclusive of psychotropic drugs for the treatment of mental health disorders. Regular supportive supervision will ensure quality of care is maintained by conducting regular QOC assessments. The proposed project will conduct a rapid WASH assessment of supported health facilities and through consultation with key stakeholders including the health committees, the project will seek to rehabilitate solid waste management facilities. Medical waste generated will be disposed according to internationally acceptable standards.

1 - Needs

Decades of protracted conflicts, seasonal flooding, economic and political instability continue to negatively impact access to essential health services in South Sudan. Recurrent disease outbreak, poor immunization structures, inadequate qualified health personnel, and high malnutrition rate contribute to high morbidity and mortality rates reported for children under 5 years across the country. Since July 2019, heavy rainfall has affected parts of the country with 32 counties (including counties in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity States) affected by flooding. This has exacerbated the acute food insecurity and continues to impact the humanitarian situation. 60% of the affected counties are currently classified as facing extreme levels of acute malnutrition. This has had an impact on access to clean water and sanitation with resultant heightened risk of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea and cholera. According to the August 2019 Integrated Food security phase classification (IPC), an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Aweil East, Panyijar and Koch Counties in Emergency (IPC phase 4) and Critical (GAM prevalence of 15.0 – 29.9%, IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4). These continuous and emergent crises have contributed to South Sudan’s poor health indicators including an immunization coverage rate of 48%, under five mortality rate is 90.7 per 1000 live births and a maternal mortality rate of 789 deaths per 1000 persons. Majority of the population (about 56%) reside more than a 5km walking distance from the nearest health facility.An estimated 46% of the women in South Sudan deliver at home without accessing skilled birth attendants, this is further coupled to inadequate qualified health care practitioners, inadequate access to supplies and medical equipment, poor health workers competencies and morale. Sociocultural barriers have also limited uptake and utilization of primary health care services. In 2019, Measles outbreaks have been reported in 17 counties including Aweil East where a mop up was conducted with 91% coverage, cholera preparedness activities continue across high priority counties including Panyijar County (Category 1) where two rounds of Oral cholera vaccinations were conducted in 2018, and high malaria caseloads noted during the 2019 rainy season with 7 counties exceeding the malaria threshold compared to the same period from 2013-2017.The revitalized agreement on the resolution of the conflict in the republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) and the tension in Sudan has resulted in increased returnees population. According to the IOM DTM April-September 2019, most of the returnees are women (60%) and <18 years (54%). This increase in affected population is likely to strech the existing health systems in Aweil East and Rubkona/Koch.

2 - Activities

The IRC will procure and distribute essential drugs including psychotropics for the PHCC, equipment, medical and laboratory supplies to all target health facilities and the Mobile health team. This will also include costs for last mile distribution of the equipment and commodities.The IRC will also recruit essential health staff for the running of mobile health teams including (1 clinical officers, 1 nurse, 1 dispenser, 1 CHWs, 1 MCHWs, 1 card registers and 1 EPI vaccinators) and for operation of three static facilities including (3 Clinical Officers, 4 Nurses, 3 dispensers, 2 midwives, 3 MCHW’s, 3 Medical Waste operators and 3 registration clerks). These staff will provide basic essential health services including consultations, skilled deliveries, immunization, and provision of essential medicines to the beneficiaries as well as triage and management of children with severe acute malnutrition with medical ailments admitted in the stabilization center.The staff at the health facilities will be provided with the capacity to conduct nutritional assessments and screening in particular for children under 5 years. Children screened positive for moderate or severe acute malnutrition will be referred promptly to nutrition sites to be initiated on treatment. Staff will be trained on Maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) practices and Infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) so that they can cascade this to all clients during the health education sessions.The IRC will provide capacity building of key essential staff on monitoring, prevention, detection and response to epidemic-prone diseases by conducting a training IDSR and EWARS. Staff will also be trained by IRC staff on screening for people living with disability (PWDs) using the Washington group set of short questions, on disability mainstreaming and inclusion, MHPSS and WHO mhGAP training will also be conducted to provide staff with essential skills in managing mental health disorders in treatment and psychological care. With a good institutional capacity in GBV, the IRC will provide training to health workers on Clinical Management of Rape in Clinical Care of Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS). Through existing conflict resolution mechanisms, Boma health committees and community leadership, IRC will be document and escalate any reported cases of attacks on health care workers.The IRC will support the referral of emergency medical and obstetric cases to the nearest Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (CEmONC) facilities for prompt responses as well as support referral of PWDs, including clients on treatment for mental health illnesses for further rehabilitation services.To improve the status of WASH in the health facilities, the project will support the rehabilitation of placenta pits, incinerators while ensuring the availability of safe water for human consumption is available at the health facilities. The staff and care givers will also be trained in Infection Prevention and Control procedures to strengthen the outcomes.The project will support the provision of health education during mobile outreaches and in the health facilities on common causes of mortality and morbidity for men, women, and children aged five years or less including on vaccine-preventable illnesses, WASH, hygiene and sanitation, IYCF and MIYCN practices. To further improve and increase health education, the project will support the design, printing, and dissemination of IEC materials on various health topics including maternal health, hygiene practices disease prevention measures.The existing reporting network will be strengthened through the county health departments and state ministry of health to support prompt detection, identification, investigation and reporting of all epidemic prone diseases during the project.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiaries rights will be recognized through the project implementation through IRC's client responsiveness strategies. Clients will be provided with mechanisms to provide real time feedback during the implementation of the proposed project through various means including the use of client exit interviews, quarterly community review meetings to assess program performance and through the use of inclusive and representative leadership in the Facility health committees. Through this mechanisms clients will be able to provide timely and anonymous feedback to the health facility through the health facility in charge who will be the secretary of the committee. Corrective action will be taken to improve on client centered services delivery in the health facilities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The proposed project will make use of existing community channels to capture the perspectives of the displacement affected people. In the proposed project locations most displaced people don't often self identify and instead integrate into the host community, therefore the IRC will provide tailored client exit interviews to ensure that the perspectives, opinions and view points of displaced persons as well as the vulnerable (PWDs), mental health illness, elderly, women and girls) are captured during the implementation and inform project direction during implementation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The proposed project will aim to provide integrated health services to displaced populations by leveraging existing services provided by IRC women and protection, nutrition and child protection programs. Referral linkages will be provided to ensure that all clients receive holistic services at the service delivery points. The IRC has greater acceptance in the proposed project location and has worked with the local authorities, County Health Departments and community leadership structures. This existing relationships will be beneficial in ensuring IDPs and returnees benefit from existing services and reduce potential sources of conflict between the host communities and IDPs. Most IDPs do not self-identify and integrate with host communities and share existing health and nutrition services.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed project seeks to align with chapter 3 of the Revitalized Agreement of the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) which provides for upholding of the right of return and prioritizing reintegration during the transitional period, however sustainable conditions are not in place for the safe and dignified return of IDPs in South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

During the lifetime of the proposed project, the IRC will engage the local authorities, community leadership, the CHD and other community stakeholders to identify safe and secluded areas of the health facilities, where solid waste management will be conducted. The IRC will support the rehabilitation of placenta pits and incinerators in supported health facilities. Health care staff working in supported health facilities will receive on job coaching on infection prevention and control to ensure that staff and clients are protected from potential harm. As much as possible, the IRC will utilize renewable energy sources to power up the health facilities including solar power for maternity and in patient facilities. IRC will employ its environmental policy to as much as possible reduce the carbon footprint in supported health facilities.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

At the onset of the project, the IRC will conduct community dialogue sessions which will aim to introduce the objectives of the proposed project and the time frame for the project, this will ensure community awareness of the time frame of the project and through local community leadership and the CHD, the affected communities can lobby for MOH support to supported health facilities to be handed over to the CHD through MOH and other funding mechanism support to ensure continuity of the services delivered by the health facilities.
