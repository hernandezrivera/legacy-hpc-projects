Project Summary

According to Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2023/ Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024, statistics showed that around 2.1 million individuals in Northwest Syria (NWS) (Idleb and Aleppo) are considered People in need of shelter assistance, 65% of the shelters in NWS are damaged, and 75% of the shelters are inadequate to live.

Based on the HNO, across NWS, 1.9 million people reside in 1,525 camps, and of these around 1,400 are unplanned, self-settled IDP sites, and 86% of sites are critically overcrowded.

Inadequate Living Conditions: With 2.1 million people in need of shelter assistance, and over 800,000 people living in tents and makeshift shelters (90% of which are more than 1 year old), the severe winter months pose a significant humanitarian challenge in NWS. The majority of people reside in old tents in IDP sites that lack proper sewage, drainage, electricity, and water supply. Among the camp population, 80% are women and children who have experienced multiple displacements over several years and are subjected to safety, security, and protection risks (in part) due to the temporary and undignified nature of shelter and settlement conditions. High rates of GBV, and the impact of poor living conditions on children, the impact of climate change and natural disasters make traditional approaches to shelter less effective.

• Earthquake: The earthquake that struck Syria and Turkiye in February 2023 worsened the situation for an already vulnerable population living in overcrowded and substandard shelter conditions.
• Winter Severity: The long, extremely cold, and unpredictable winters in
NWS results in acute suffering for 2 million individuals who lack adequate shelter. During winter, there is an increase in respiratory infection outbreaks, leading to a rise in hospitalizations, morbidity, and mortality. The absence of warm clothing, insulation, and heating further increases the risk of respiratory infections, hypothermia, and preventable deaths among children and the elderly.
⦁ Intervention, Implementation of activities:
In this project, NGO X is submitting a multiple solution approach to Shelter intervention to address the multiple vulnerabilities that households are facing.
Shelter activities target beneficiaries at a HH level and create links between multi-donor funded emergency response, winterization and shelter programming, to complement food assistance programming.
NGO X will rehabilitate shelter units using self-help modality providing CASH grants to HHs to carry out the required work themselves. The shelter rehabilitation activity will target 1439 HHs. Moreover, NGO X will target several IDP sites (400 HHs) with the construction of dignified shelter units, and complete site planning and infrastructure rehabilitation and upgrades in addition to providing basic WASH services. The HH-level interventions are done in tandem with the distribution of MPCA and winterization cash where feasible.
Winters in NWS can be long, cold, and unpredictable, causing acute suffering for almost 2 million people living without adequate shelter. This has been compounded by the devastating earthquakes in February 2023 and recent intensifications of hostilities NGO X will provide winter MPCA to vulnerable households using various modalities, including cash in hand or cash disbursements facilitated by an e-card system. This approach ensures comprehensive, real-time oversight of all cash transfers to beneficiaries. Distribution points will be established in each village, minimizing travel for beneficiaries to access the distribution point and return home with their cash.
On the settlement level, the program will focus on flood-prone IDP sites, aiming to provide preventive measures to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter to ensure uninterrupted access to assistance and services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NGOx will carry out IDP site improvement activities including road graveling, construction and rehabilitation of rainwater drainage systems, tents levelling, and installation of solar lights targeting 26,860 HHs. In total, the unique target for 2024 is 7,211 HHs/36,055 individuals under the Shelter sector, and 15,043 HHs/75,215 under NFI.NGO X emphasizes the safety and well-being of program participants with robust measures integrating comprehensive PSEAH (Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment) strategies into all activities. This includes prevention, awareness, responsive reporting, and risk mitigation, reinforced by training for staff, partners, and leaders to enhance SEAH incident management.Assumption, Risks, and Mitigation Measures:Assumptions: Assumes community cooperation and a stable security environment for project success. Operational Risks:• Implementation Delays: Mitigated by improved coordination, streamlined logistics, and staff training.• Quality Control: Ensured through regular monitoring, skilled engineers, and beneficiary feedback. Security Risks:• Conflict and Instability: Managed with security monitoring, safety protocols, and local coordination.• Resource Theft or Diversion: Prevented through secure storage and local community and security collaboration. Protection Risks:• Physical or sexual harm at cash distribution points: Can be mitigated by choosing Safe and accessible locations, organizing gender-separate queues with trained female staff, offering home-delivery for groups with special needs, and expanding the number of distribution sites to minimize beneficiary’s travel.• Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse by distribution actors or GOAL Staff: Prevented by ensuring that all staff are aware of and have received training on PSEA policy as well as signed code of conduct. Also, female Protection/GBV staff are present during distributions. Moreover, contractors understood and signed the code of conduct, PSEAH, and safeguarding policy.• Tensions between host communities, IDPs, and returnees: Managed by ensuring the response is needs-based, promoting inclusive participation, and raising awareness on selection criteria.• Children at risk of being directly or indirectly engaged child labour by contracted construction contractors and vendors: This is avoided by including clauses against Child Labor in vendors contracts, raising awareness among contractors and vendors on GOAL CP Minimum Standards, closely monitoring the implementation to identify any possible cases. Other Risks:• Risk of eviction from shelter after the completion of rehabilitation: Can be mitigated by Carrying out HLP due diligence and ensuring consensus before proceeding with constructions. Signing agreements with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction, raising the awareness of beneficiaries about their rights.
