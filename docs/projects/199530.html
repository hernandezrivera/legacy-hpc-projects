Project Summary

Since 2018, the security situation in Burkina Faso drastically deteriorated with the expansion and intensification of activities of non-state armed groups (NSAGs), exploiting pre-existing inter-communal tensions. A further escalation was observed in 2020 and 2021, with a greater toll on human lives and on vulnerable populations. The non-state armed groups (NSAGs) are fragmented, with multiple internal players; and there are increased external players linked with the regional dynamics (i.e. Central Sahel countries). This has led to massive population displacements and has had a severe impact on already vulnerable populations.
As of October 2021, over 1.4 million persons have been displaced and new displacements are reported daily. The security situation has had repercussions on the overall food and nutritional security of the country. Food security and nutritional situation has been impacted by the growing crisis affecting vulnerable populations. The unprecedented humanitarian emergency left 2.6 million persons in need of humanitarian assistance during the 2022 lean season – according to the last Cadre Harmonisé.
As of 30 November 2022, the number of registered internally displaced persons (IDPs) is 1,810,105 hosted in 299 communes spread over all 13 regions of the country. In addition, according to the Analysis of the Cadre Harmonisé of November 2022, 3,533,220 people, or 16% of the total population, would be food insecure. This includes 572,382 children under 5 years of age (RGPH 2019). These people would be concentrated in the regions of East (19%), Centre-North (18%), Sahel (17%), North and Boucle du Mouhoun (respectively 10%). These areas also correspond to localities with a high security challenge.
Humanitarian access to vulnerable populations in need of assistance is restricted by threats posed by non-state armed groups, counterterrorism operations, the presence of Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) planted on roads and the damage or destruction of key road infrastructure (bridges targeted in attacks in the Sahel region for instance). In order to support the planned humanitarian response and enable access to the population in need and project implementation sites, UNHAS aims to provide a safe, effective and efficient passenger air service to the entire humanitarian community and their implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, red cross/crescent movement, donor organizations and diplomatic missions. UNHAS will be used to deliver assistance to hard-to-reach areas were people are the most vulnerable.

2 - BESOINS, ACTIVITÉS ET RÉSULTATS ATTENDUS
2.1. Décrivez les besoins, activités et résultats attendus

The WFP-managed United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) in Burkina Faso was launched its operation back in 2020. In 2023, UNHAS will continue to provide safe, reliable, cost-efficient and timely passenger and light cargo air transport services in Burkina Faso ensuring access to the most remote locations for to the entire humanitarian community and their implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, red cross/crescent movement, donor organizations and diplomatic missions. Crucially, this service represents the only viable solution to access hard-to-reach areas due to rampant insecurity, and as such remains the backbone of the humanitarian response. Moreover, UNHAS provides a critical medical and security evacuation services to a field-based partners.Given security limitations and growing demand, UNHAS fleet has increased from one helicopter in 2021 three helicopters in 2023, capable to transport both passengers and cargo.UNHAS flights serve 14 regular and 16 ad-hoc destinations through out the country facilitating access of over 100 user organisations, among which 64 percent were international NGOs, 11 percent national NGOs, 23 percent UN agencies, and 2 percent diplomatic missions and donors. While it received no requests for security evacuations, UNHAS performed thirteen medical evacuations of humanitarian personnel in 2022. Two governance platforms have been put in place to manage the service:• UNHAS User Group Committee (UGC) meetings composed of NGOs, UN agencies and Donor Representatives. The role of the UGC is administrative, provides feedback to UNHAS' Chief Air Transport Officer (CATO) to help ensure high service quality and maintains a service-oriented approach to supporting the partners. The UGC are used as a forum where the users of the service can voice their views on operational matters, including suggestions with regard to destinations or other changes to the weekly flights schedule.• UNHAS Steering Committee meetings chaired by the Humanitarian Coordinator and comprised of some representatives from INGOs, UN Agencies and Donors to define operational requirements and air transport priorities, also to discuss topics of a strategic nature such as funding and give guidance on UNHAS policies. Upon the need and decision of the Steering Committee, UNHAS currently offers different weekly flights rotations and special flights all departing from Ouagadougou airport. The service is accessible to all humanitarian actors upon registration. Furthermore, partnership is ensured at local, regional and national level through the different platforms. For each serviced location, a partner has been identified locally to serve as a focal point for security-related aspects. Relevant analysis and consultations are ensured in advance and during flights to ensure that a location is accessible. In 2022, UNHAS transported four times more passengers and cargo as in 2021: 15,320 passengers and more than 800 metric tons of light cargo, including specialized nutritious foods, medical supplies, medicines, and IT equipment. To collect users’ feedback on the quality of its services, UNHAS conducted two satisfaction surveys over the course of the year. The results for Passenger Satisfaction and Provision of Access Satisfaction showed a satisfaction rate of 84 percent and 76 percent respectively.
