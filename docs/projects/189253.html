Project Summary

This project will provide Integrated, life-saving, emergency health interventions for populations affected conflict, disease outbreak and displacement, targeting a total of 91,594 people (44,881 Males and 46,713 Females). Out of these, 19,235 will be under the age of 5 years (9,425 Males and 9,810 Females) and 72,354 above the age of 5 years (35,456 Males and 36,903 Females). 4,580 will be Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). 1,150 of those targeted will be people living with disability. The project will focus on women, girls, boys and men among the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), Returnees and host populations with limited or no access to health services. The project will ensure improved access to integrated emergency lifesaving healthcare services focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality for conflict-affected and vulnerable IDP, host community and returnee households in Bor South, Twic East, Rubkona, Pariang, and Juba (Mangalla), Counties.

In addition, due to its geographical presence and health programming in Wau, Pibor and Duk Counties under the Health Pooled Fund and World Bank (UNICEF) funding, CARE will have the capacity to provide emergency health interventions in the 3 counties (Wau, Duk and Pibor) depending on the need and availability of funding
To address the identified health needs within the 5 counties, CARE will deliver a package of emergency and essential health services in line with the three 2022 Health Cluster Objectives as follows:
1. Improve access to lifesaving essential health services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, provision of GBV and MPHSS services to the vulnerable population including those living with disability.
2. Reduce mortality and morbidity by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases
3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

The health system in South Sudan remains weak, with multiple shocks experienced in the past year having worsened the situation further. Flooding, which has devastated many locations where CARE works (Pariang, Rubkona, Twic East and Duk) has heavily affected the capacity of the health systems and its ability to function well. Of the 809,000 people that have been affected by flooding, 498,000 (61%) are in Jonglei and Unity , where CARE implements its health programmes (OCHA, 2021). The current flooding has destroyed WASH facilities (Boreholes, Water Points and Latrines leading to widespread contamination of water sources and loss of water storage containers as well as long lasting insecticide treated nets (LLIN). As a result, Malaria and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) related morbidity and mortality trends in the last Quarter of 2021 were above those of 2020 within the affected counties, particularly in Rubkona, Twic East and Pariang. (IDSR week 27, 2022). In addition, and more worryingly, flooding has hugely impaired access to health services in Rubkona, Twic East, Duk and parts of Pariang. Roads have been flooded or destroyed, making transportation difficult and expensive. Canoes and speed boats, which are the only means of transport available, are expensive and not easily available, further negatively impacting access to health facilities for critical maternal and child health services. Flooding has also led to the destruction of crops, cut off markets and led to deaths of domestic animals which are the lifeline of the population in Unity and Jonglei. This is projected to lead to severe food insecurity and the attendant malnutrition among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women. Combined with the high burden of infectious diseases, this is likely to lead to high morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable populations. Poor access to health services (Only 28.6% of the population living within 5 km of a health facility, Macharia et al, 2017), combined with population displacement due to flooding provide a conducive environment for disease outbreaks. Indeed, cholera, measles, meningitis, Hepatitis E and malaria have been flagged by the Ministry of Health and WHO as likely to cause outbreaks in 2022. In response, vaccination efforts for measles, Meningitis, and Cholera are ongoing in various counties. The impact of COVID-19 continues to be felt particularly in Pariang, where positive cases have continued to be reported among the refugees and host community members. CARE has had to recruit and train additional staff and provide the required supplies for preparedness and response activities. In addition, infections among health care workers and the need for isolation and quarantine have continued to disrupt delivery of health services.

2 - Project activities

CARE will provide Emergency Life-saving Health services in a total of 40 Static Health Facilities (under the World Bank and Health Pooled Fund) funding and 10 Mobile Clinics across 4 States of Jonglei, Unity Western Bar el Ghazal and Central Equitoria in line with the South Sudan’s Ministry of Health Standards and Guidelines as well as the WHO and SPHERE Standards and guidelines. The response activities implemented by CARE will be based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden and seasonality. CARE will work with other clusters and partners to ensure integrated response across the field locations. These Clusters include Nutrition, Protection and FSL. CARE will also put in place a robust system of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints from the communities that we serve. Analysis and results of the feedback and complaints provided will be shared monthly and this will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence based decision making in order to improve programme quality and performance. In the 5 Counties where CARE proposes to work, it is also the partner that is implementing development related health funding from HPF and UNICEF. CARE will therefore ensure that the activities carried out under the HRP will be linked to the Development /longer term funding. To address the identified health needs within the identified counties, CARE will:1. Improve access to lifesaving essential health services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, provision of GBV and MPHSS services to the vulnerable population including those living with disability. 2. Reduce mortality and morbidity by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. CARE will train staff on emergency preparedness and response for diseases of outbreak potential. This will ensure early detection and response. In addition, CARE will strengthen its existing surveillance systems through the IDSR and EWARS. CARE will provide health services that are adapted to the COVID-19 context in South Sudan. This will be in addition to ensuring COVID-19 prevention measures are taken into account while continuing to deliver health services. Specifically, in response to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) CARE will carry out the following activities. - Ensuring epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response across all its health projects. - Providing immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities for the health programmes. - Provision of BeMONC/ CeMONC services including procurement of essential RH commodities and building the capacity of skilled birth attendants- Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. - Integrating MHPSS and SGBV services in health facilities and provision of supplies for MHPSS services. - IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities - Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the provision of health services - Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services –- Community health service provision linking with facility- based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives - Provision of rehabilitation services and assertive devices for the for disabled.

3 - Project indicators

A participatory gender sensitive monitoring and reporting plan will be developed and established for this project. The M&E Coordinator in collaboration with the Health Team will develop the M&E plan. Routine monitoring will be undertaken by CARE M&E and Health teams and will involve relevant stakeholders including the State Ministries of Health and the County Health Departments, as well as other partners working in the 5 counties. Monitoring results will be shared monthly and quarterly and will inform the extent to which inputs were appropriately used, the extent to which planned activities were carried out as per the detailed 2022 HRP implementation plan, and the quantity and quality of outputs resulting from the activities. During project implementation, all approved South Sudan Ministry of Health tools for data collection will be used supplemented by internal project monitoring framework ensuring weekly analysis and sharing of data. The data collected will be fed into the MoH surveillance system to support surveillance, health planning and program management. IDSR data collected will be compiled weekly and submitted to relevant offices. The data on morbidity, maternal, child health and immunization will be compiled monthly and entered into DHIS with a copy submitted to the SMoH. In addition, monthly health cluster 5w reports and contributions towards health cluster bulletin will be provided to showcase activities in the field. Quarterly data quality audits will be undertaken by a joint team consisting of; the SMOH, CHD, community representative and CARE technical staff to ensure integrity and credibility of information reported. The analysis of the data on a monthly basis will inform program decision making as it pertains to progress made in the implementation process. Analysis on a quarterly basis will inform decision making on program strategies in terms of best practice and review of lessons learned in the course of implementation. Data generated from GBV cases treated will be handled with confidentiality adhering to privacy standards required.The window of opportunity during the dry spell towards end of year will be fully utilized to provide 3 monthly technical monitoring visits done jointly by the program staff, community representatives and the CHD to supported health facilities ensuring that services are in line with positive local norms, national treatment protocols, quality standards are upheld and a proper application of the skills and concepts covered during in service trainings conducted. CARE will use its supervision checklist in addition to the QSC (Quantified Supervision Checklist) for the joint supervision visits as a tool to monitor progress. CARE will also put in place suggestion boxes at places of service delivery in order to get community views on services provided and provide feedback to promote accountability to the beneficiaries.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

CARE will roll out a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV by providing high quality health services, including mental health and psychosocial support to children, women men and boys either at risk of GBV or GBV survivors. This, CARE will do by ensuring that staff are trained and provided with the supplies and tools required for management of GBV related complications and provision of support. CARE will also work with other partners and clusters to improve access to Clinical Management of Rape services by strengthening referral pathways in the community and educating members of the community, especially the vulnerable groups, on available services and how to access them. CARE will also provide a reliable and confidential system of reporting GBV incidents including PSEA

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

CARE will work with the relevant partners (Government departments, UN Agencies, Non Governmental Organizations etc) to improve preparedness and response towards the perennial flooding witnessed in South Sudan. This will include working with communities to develop time tested Early Warning Signs that can be used by communities to take precautionary measures including moving to higher ground or areas not normally affected y flooding and prepositioning food and other supplies. Other strategies will include working with communities to develop trenches and waterways to reduce the chances and impact of flooding.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

CARE will ensure decreased proportion of the population facing IPC 3, 4 and 5 by: 1. Improving access to health services in all its operational areas. this will be done by addressing the challenges affecting access to health services in conflict, flood and food insecurity affected locations. Some of the measures to be undertaken will include: addressing the issue of distance by running mobile clinics; providing medical supplies and training staff working in the IPC 3, 4 and 5 locations. 2. Establish Early Waring Systems to ensure impact of emergencies (flooding, disease outbreaks etc) are reduced.
