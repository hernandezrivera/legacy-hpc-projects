Project Summary

Needs: According to HNO 2024 summery an HPC health sector package, over 14.9 million people in Syria are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary care health assistance in 2024.The already exhausted Health System in NWS cannot cope with this increased number of people in need.
After earthquake, Out of the total 585 health facilities assessed by the health cluster in NWS, 15% (97) health facilities were reported damaged [23 fully damaged and 71 partially damaged],which affects the access to health services for the population of NWS. Additionally, 4.2 million women of reproductive age in the health sector PIN require health care assistance to access essential reproductive health services, including antenatal care, safe deliveries, postnatal care, family planning, and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care. Of the 501,783 live births expected163 in 2023, an estimated 75,267 of those women will need assistance to access emergency cesarean sections to save their lives and that of their newborns.
Intervention, Implementation of Activities: CARE will continue to address health and nutrition needs including SRH, general consultations, pediatric consultations, awareness raising on health and nutrition, nutrition services and contributing to strengthening the health systems by operating ambulance networks facilitating transport of high-risk cases. Additionally, the project will focus on integration between SRH and GBV through implementing AMAL initiative and ensuring safe referral in place. To support localization, CARE will implement the proposed activities through local organization and will provide the needed technical and administrative support. The activities are monitored via regular monitoring, and evaluation by implementing partners as well as by CARE's health and protection technical consultants. The locations and facilities will be as following: Amal hospital for maternity and pediatric in Azaz, Amal PHC for primary health care in Azaz, Ambulance centers in Armanaz,DaretEzzah and JisAlshugur.three BEmONC centers in Baba Alsalam,Aghtrin,and Albab, with integration of SRH within three CARE WGSSs in azaz including RH consultation, FP and SRH awareness raising session ,Kelly and Bennish.Nutrition teams will be integrated in the HFs.They will deliver nutrition services, including MUAC screening, distribution of micronutrients, IYCF awareness sessions, and referrals to CMAM centers. One RRT will be established in the Aghtrin sub-district.
PRA: CARE aims to address community needs, particularly those of women, boys, and girls, while considering local norms. Facilities will be located for accessibility, including for PWDS. Transportation will be provided as needed, prioritizing women, girls, and boys, to improve access to health services. CHWs, both male and female, will be mobilized to reach the most vulnerable, with a focus on outreach services. Integrating SRH clinics into CARE WGSS will create additional safe spaces for women, girls, and GBV survivors to access essential health support.
PSEA strategy: CARE Safeguarding Policy applies to all Employees and Related Personnel. CARE conducts programmatic safeguarding risk assessments for each project, conducts GBV and PSEA Field Check List quarterly and mitigates risks accordingly with the risks identified. All feedback and complaints channels are shared with the community members on regular basis. CARE carefully examine allegations and investigate, and take appropriate disciplinary action where needed, considering the rights and interests of the survivor.
Risks, and Mitigating measures: CARE prioritizes security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments. Project activities are directly monitored at various levels. CARE has drafted a contingency plan detailing planning efforts, scenario forecasting, and options to adapt programming. Contingency plans are in place to manage significant outbreaks in NWS while safeguarding field staff.

Ziadiyah PHC (Shafak).The primary focus of CARE's efforts will be on reproductive health and child health, encompassing nutrition services, diagnostic services, and the treatment of communicable and noncommunicable diseases. Essential services, particularly for children experiencing severe acute malnutrition with complications, will be provided throughout the supported health facilities.CARE's goal is to deliver primary health care services in line with the WHO Essential Primary Health Care Package including BeMONC services, consisting of five pillars: child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, noncommunicable disease, and nutrition. Secondary health care services, including specialized reproductive health care services including CEmONC, will also be provided, adhering to international guidelines and the (UNFPA) standards. This encompasses neonatal care, vaccination, reproductive health care during pregnancy and childbirth, and both normal and C-section deliveries, particularly in maternity and SRH facilities.Nutrition services will involve screening for SAM and MAM cases in children under 5 PLWs, along with IYCF counseling and the provision of nutrition supplies for those in need.CARE will cover staff salaries, operational costs, and medical commodities, including medicines, consumables, and equipment for health facilities. It will also offer recruitment support, capacity building, and ongoing medical supervision to ensure qualified medical personnel are present and adhere to standard case management protocols.Furthermore, CARE will continue training health staff on various health and nutrition topics through training sessions or collaborative efforts with other agencies. This includes the management of CD and NCD, pharmaceutical supply management, patient data management, SRH treatment protocols, and gender sensitivity and protection.CARE will gather and disseminate health data from the health facilities monthly. These facilities play a vital role in the referral pathways organized by WHO, the health cluster, and partners to ensure the quality of healthcare services. CHWs will conduct outreach activities in the communities surrounding the HFs, to enhance community health and align knowledge with the messages distributed to the communities
