Project Summary

To respond to the needs of the most vulnerable households across NES, this project will focus on providing temporary employment opportunities using CfW method as well as livelihoods restoration support to small and medium enterprises (SME) and Vocational Training using the Graduation Approach. All activities will target some of the most vulnerable households left with no means of income generation to meet their most basic needs as a result of the conflict and COVID-19. This includes families engaging in child labor, families with only one or few potential wage earners, persons with disabilities, and households without an existing vocation or source of reliable income. Whilst in rural areas, this project will focus on supporting agricultural production (please see Agricultural sector), in urban settings the NGO will support SMEs through cash grants and business training. Meanwhile, CfW activities in urban and semi-urban settings, will comprise public works and services which benefit the whole community such as small rehabilitation, cleaning and cosmetic improvements of public spaces. The NGO will also continue to implement CfW activities in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. CfW participants will support COVID-19 mitigation measures in Menbij camps by sanitizing surfaces and cleaning WASH facilities.
Many small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) have been severely affected by the consequences of COVID-19 including the imposition of a curfew, non-essential market closures, travel restrictions and border closures and other supply chain-related disruptions. The restoration grant to SMEs is designed to support skilled and semi-skilled workers to restore their productive assets and resume income-generating activities. This endeavour will also target women and persons with disabilities who have skills but lack the tools or equipment to expand into a viable income-generating activity without initial investment. The NGO will target the same communities where the NGO is providing food assistance and conducting shelter rehabilitation with a view to supporting the revitalization of the local economy and, increasing work and livelihood opportunities. At the same time, where possible this project will directly support households who are receiving food and/or shelter assistance under this grant. This will improve the resilience of the most at-risk households by giving them the means to generate their own income and eventually transition from depending on humanitarian assistance.
This project will also provide to individuals that have been unable to practice their trade for a long period of time, a tailored Graduation programme that will comprise technical courses where existing gaps in the worker’s skills have been identified, a business grant to restart their Income Generating Activity (IGA) with support in creating a business plan to drive the use of the grant, and coaching sessions to support the household through their recovery. A comprehensive micro-business management training will allow them to develop their business management skills and create a business plan, enabling them to start their own business or home-based business. The mentoring and coaching sessions will take place in each individual household as well as in groups with other participants. Individual mentoring sessions and life skills training will aim to strengthen the resilience of the households, reduce their exposure to protection risks and boost the individuals’ confidence in their own ability to succeed in the establishment of their IGA; while group sessions with other participants will allow for sharing difficulties and initiatives between participants. During the training period, the households will receive a monthly cash payment equivalent to the value of the SMEB to ensure that they are able to stabilise their living conditions and fully commit to the training and time needed to build the necessary skills.

4 - Needs

The median SMEB cost in North East Syria continues to increase with devastating effects on household purchasing power in turn decreasing resilience and exacerbating vulnerabilities, particularly for those residing to informal settlements and hard-to-reach communities. According to REACH, the median daily wage for residents and IDPs in NES, as of October 2021 was 5,000 SYP. This means a household of six whose sole income is from an unskilled daily labourer would need the equivalent of 84 days of work to cover the price of the NES SMEB. 84% of IDPs assessed by HNAP report that monthly income is insufficient to meet basic needs. Due to the economic situation, households struggle to access employment opportunities and, more and more Syrians are dependent on humanitarian aid to survive. Lack of livelihood opportunities is evident across NES with REACH reporting a lack of employment opportunities that match people's skills as barriers to meeting basic needs in 64% of communities. The activities will focus on addressing those needs. Temporary employment opportunities provide a source of income and fosters the development of technical skills of CfW participants; restoration grants to local SMEs will increase available opportunities in the labour market; vocational training will allow the most vulnerable households to develop their technical and entrepreneurial skills while also supporting them to develop their life skills and become more resilient.

5 - Implementation of activities

Temporary employment activities will be provided to vulnerable and poverty-stricken IDP and returnee households through a Cash for Work (CfW) scheme. Beneficiaries will receive 5 USD per day (20 days per month) for a maximum of 5 hours of work per day, as per WG recommendation and in line with NGOs , adopting where relevant. Skilled supervisors will work alongside the CfW participants providing technical guidance. This activity will apply do no harm principle with consultations of all relevant stakeholders to ensure there are no tensions including the use of a clear targeting approach. Many small-to-medium enterprises have been severely affected. The restoration grant to SMEs is designed to support skilled workers to restore their productive assets and resume income-generating activities. This endeavour will also target women and persons with disabilities who have skills but lack the tools or equipment to expand into a viable income-generating activity without initial investment. This project will provide vocational trainings specifically designed to allow participants, including adolescents to acquire or reinforce skills to undertake technical vocations and to develop their business management skills, enabling them to start their own business, their home business or to find a stable employment in the labour market. This project will deliver micro-business management training which will help the trainee to create and monitor basic household/small business budgets.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Among CfW teams, women will be encouraged to work in smaller teams of 3-5. Older people will also be selected, and the range of work activities will be tailored as per their capacity. People with disability (PwD) will also be encouraged to participate according to their capacities and specific needs. The NGO's system of internal referrals allows for extremely vulnerable households identified by the NGO's Protection team to be included in the beneficiaries list even when they haven not applied to be considered CfW scheme. This project will provide proper work gear/equipment (e.g. helmet, boots, gloves, googles, facemasks, etc.) to all CfW participants. They will also receive proper health and safety training prior to start of CfW activities, as it’s necessary to ensure that participants do not come to harm at work. For vocational training and livelihoods restoration activities, for those with difficulty leaving the home due to childcare responsibilities (in the case of women and female-headed households) or disability, the NGO will support home-based businesses tailored to the skills and capacities which the participant may already have and which the person can utilize at home. As with all the NGO's programming, this project will adhere to “do no harm” approach to ensure that any support provided will not put the participants at risk. Livelihoods staff will be trained on protection and identify protection risks and make appropriate referals to Concern's protection programme.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.
