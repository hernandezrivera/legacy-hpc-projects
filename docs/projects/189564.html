Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes the Provision of emergency health assistance to IDPs and host communities affected conflict and floods in Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria, and Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal, Ezo and Nagero Western Equatoria, South Sudan in the year 2022. This project will provide essential life-saving health care services including maternal, child, adolescent health provision through static health facilities, Medical mobile teams and Community health Volunteers in Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria, Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal, Ezo and Nagero Western Equatoria, South Sudan by focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality.
Following conflict between farmers and herders in Magwi (UNMISS Mar 2022) More than 14,000 displaced people are now sheltering at Magwi central payam [administrative division] while another estimated 1,000 are facing dire humanitarian shortages in Ayii and sexual violence has been reported.
IHO conducted a needs assessment in Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal, Ezo and Nagero Western Equatoria, and concluded that the health systems in the five locations were not only challenged with limited access to human resource medicine and other supplies but also limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services but also limited access to WASH services but the referral system is hampered by poor roads. The community has limited access to health education, Immunization coverage is still low. The leading causes of morbidity include Malaria, Pneumonia, Watery Diarrhea among children, HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis. The IPC Acute Food Insecurity & Acute Malnutrition Analysis October 2020 – July 2021 Issued: 18 December 2020 classified Ezo and Nagero Crisis (IPC Phase 3), Aweil South Emergency (IPC Phase 4 ) and Magwi classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3)
In this project, IHO will provide lifesaving quality health service to 40000 people (Male children 10147 Femal Children 9366 , Adult Male 9762 and Female Adult 10725) reach 2000 people with disabilities (Male children 507 Female Children 468 , Adult Male 488 and Female Adult 536). The targeted beneficiaries are part of health cluster target in Magwi, Aweil South, Ezo and Nagero Counties by undertaking 4 mobile team, 4 PHCU Activities. IHO will work will increase coverage of PENTA3 in under one-year-old, deliveries assisted by a skilled birth attendant, pregnant women who attended 4 ANC visits, persons receiving MHPSS services, care for SGBV survivors receiving CMR services, OPD consultations treatment of malaria, community health events for behavioral change communication, health education and promotion messages and shall promote facilities- community feedback mechanism and as well conduct community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected populations and people with disability) and conduct quarterly supportive supervision to health facilities.
IHO will work close with other cluster such as protection to address GBV cases, Food security to fight malnutrition, WASH to prevent diseases and Finally, IHO will work closely with WHO, health cluster and MoH in ensuring provision of timely live saving health care services.

1 - Needs

Following the outbreak of conflict, the December 2013, the Republic of South Sudan continues to face considerable humanitarian problems, which have increased substantially. Among the counties affected include Magwi, Aweil South, Ezo, and Nagero Counties. In addition, a major crisis erupted in July 2016 with subsequent intercommunal fights which further aggravated the humanitarian situation, producing secondary or third displacement with populations under stress lacking access to basic services and constraining humanitarian access. The conflict situation and floods in the targeted counties has impact greatly on the social services and as well the health care system is challenged with inadequate human resource and as well insecurity and attacks on health care and workers have limited movement and access limiting documentation of cluster response. limited access to safe water, poor waste management and infection prevention and control measures, poor infrastructure, potential risks of disease outbreak ie cholera, Ebola etc. The IPC Acute Food Insecurity & Acute Malnutrition Analysis October 2020 – July 2021 Issued: 18 December 2020 classified Magwi, Ezo and Nagero Crisis (IPC Phase 3) Aweil East and Aweil Emergency (IPC Phase 4). and is expected to raise due to worsening food insecurity is primarily driven by influx of conflict and destruction of crops due to floods hence insufficient crop production and disruptions to livelihoods and persistent macroeconomic deterioration. The County has been affected disease outbreak as WHO confirmed measles outbreaks in Aweil South (WHO Humanitarian Situation Report Issue # 12 18 – 24 MARCH2019) and Aweil East South Sudan Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) Epidemiological Bulletin Week 10, 2020 (March 02 – March 08). Following conflict between farmers and herders in Magwi (UNMISS Mar 2022) More than 14,000 displaced people are now sheltering at Magwi central payam [administrative division] while another estimated 1,000 are facing dire humanitarian shortages in Ayii and sexual violence has been reported.IHO Assessments in Magwi, Aweil South, Nagero and Ezo Counties showed that the main cause of morbidity included Malaria, Pneumonia and Watery Diarrhea among children. The high prevalence is attribute to limited access to primary health care services as the available health facilities are far and faced with limited human resource and shortage of drugs. As a result, there is limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services, as well as treatment of SAM cases with medical complications. The referral system is hampered by poor roads and lack of transport means. The community has limited access to health education and Immunization coverage is still low. There is a need to provide emergency health care services through static primary health care unit, alongside a Medical Mobile Unit to support other health partners in management of common illnesses to reduce excess mortality and morbidity. In this regard, IHO plans to reach 40000 individuals (Male children 10147 Female Children 9366 , Adult Male 9762 and Female Adult 10725) reach 2000 people with disabilities (Male children 507 Female Children 468 , Adult Male 488 and Female Adult 536 with health service in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations and Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. This project will provide lifesaving quality health service through the 4 mobile unit and 4 PHCU by working closely with the Nutrition, FLS, WASH and Protection clusters partners. In the same regard the project will monitor disease outbreaks in the intervention locations to inform the health cluster and undertake immediate response.

2 - Project activities

In order to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses, such as measles, among male children under 5, female children under 5, male children aged 5 - 17 years, female children aged 5 - 17 years, male adults aged 18 - 60, female adults aged 18 - 60, male adults aged over 60 and female adults aged over 60; IHO will; 1. Promote epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response through IDRS and Submission EWAR reports.2. Undertake Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases including Measles,3. Undertake IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities4. Provide Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM To provide access to life-saving essential healthcare, including mental health to women, men, girls and boys, including ability challenged persons and ensuring vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action. In addition, Reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls and boys at increased risk of mortality and morbidity and protection incidents/threats in priority areas through protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness-raising and risk mitigation, IHO will provide life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (PHCU and mobile healthcare services), procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers.To rnsure that population groups targeted to receive assistance are consulted throughout the entire cycle of the response, and their needs are taken into account in decision-making by humanitarians. IHO will promote community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM. To ensure vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions to displacement with respect to their rights, IHO will conduct community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance. To strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs at national and sub-national levels, IHO will provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IHO will use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance to promote accountability, IHO will promote transparency during the project implementation by directly involving the community in every stage of the project to ensure clear understanding of objectives of the project, expectations and stakeholders. IHO has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions.The locations of the Primary health care unit and Mobile team services targeted for this project have been identified in consultation of the CHD and state ministry of Health. In addition, the Mobile Teams placement shall be based on the community prioritization and community leader consultations. IHO has as conducted initial needs analysis to identify the needs of the community targeted by the project. During service provision, IHO shall ensures facilitation feedback mechanism from affected people on the services by holding monthly feedback meeting and on Monthly basis IHO will administer a perception questionnaire to 10 beneficiaries. Team will ask beneficiaries to give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary. All project activities will engage local authorities to oversee their implementation. The implementation teams are recruited with attention to a balance of women and men, cultural diversity and age. Communities will be educated on the services provided to enable them make informed decision on service utilization and as well as IHO will promote community dialogue to continuously incorporate the needs of the community. Finally, IHO will also conduct evaluation exercises involving men, women, girls and boys. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible. this will allows beneficiaries to hold IHO and its staff to account and reduces the possibility of mistakes, abuse and corruption. IHO will also be able to assess and improve the quality of work, which benefits both the IHO and the people it shall reaches. IHO will also use beneficiaries’ feedback to respond to their issues in real time and act upon it before the project ends and lastly IHO will use use beneficiaries’ feedback Empower communities and promote project sustainability.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IHO will ensure the project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) by creating different channels that allows them to share concerns anonymously. Examples of feedback and reporting channels may include telephone hotlines, satisfaction focus group discussions, community feedbackcommittees, voice recorders and feedback boxesWhile conducting discussions with general topics and IHO move towards more sensitive topics as the participants become more comfortable. Guide group discussions towards general, rather than personal conversation, so people do not feel pressure to disclose their own experiences of violence.IHO will ensure that staff member are trained to speak privately with participants who require additional psychosocial/emotional support and/or referrals to other services and will have experience working with survivors of GBV. IHO will be flexible when and where consultations take place.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors .All IHO staff members are trained on PSEA Awareness-Raising. The organization staff or contractors or suppliers are prohibited from exploiting beneficiaries for exchange of services for sex or any form of abuse. All IHO staff members have a duty to report in good faith any SEA allegations, concerns or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA and SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result indisciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate. IHO will ensure all service provision points are accessed in safe and secure place.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IHO will facilitate the provision of feedback from affected people on the services provided through feedback meeting which will be held Monthly basis and upon completion of the project. IHO will administer a perception questionnaire to beneficiaries. Team will ask beneficiaries to give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary. Finally, IHO will also conduct evaluation exercise involving men, women, girls and boys. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible.
