Project Summary

South Sudan has experienced continued civil conflict and armed violence that has resulted in many areas being contaminated with UXOs and mines, despite annual clearance by UNMAS, TDI MAG, and Danish Refugees Councils the detonation of the explosive remnants of war (ERW) leftover, explosive ordinances (UXOs), mines bullets and bomb still rampant in South Sudan. This project will target, 24,000 (6,000 women, 6,000 girls 6,000Mand 6,000 boys) men, women, boys, and girls who are affected by conflict and flood, to the conflict and flood-affected populations living in Fangak, Canal/Pigi county of Jonglei state and Nasir County of Upper Nile. The above-targeted counties have been facing explosive hazards that impact negatively on the ability of civilians to safely access basic services which include; education/TLS, WGFS CFS, health facilities centers, and natural resources such as water and farmlands as most of these people are depending on agriculture and small scale farming which is reducing their coping mechanisms. Contributing to the restoration of a safe and secure environment can improve the living condition of the rural population especially in the most contaminated areas and improve outcomes across almost all sectors.
The project will be implemented in a period of 12 months and it will cost $300,000. CAO will implement this project directly in the target locations in collaboration with the target population, the local administration, line ministry, and partners on the ground to achieve maximum results. The project activities are designed to prevent and respond to “explosive ordnance risk education (EORE)” It is expected that by the end of the project period, the lives of the vulnerable population at risk of ERW (explosive remnants of war) have increased access to EORE awareness and safe behavior. Women, men, girls, and boys will be better informed on the dangers and safe behaviors regarding ERW in emergencies that will make a meaningful contribution to the population's needs for safe behaviors.
CAO being a strong protection partner in the above-mentioned locations, we envisage working closely with other partners on the ground who deliver services in Nutrition, Health, WAsH, and Education during EORE awareness campaigns aimed at passing key messages of safe behaviors and use of referral pathways whenever they came across EORE incidence in line with the sub-clusters 2021 strategic objective.

1 - Needs

Since 2006 reports state that 15% of incidents involving mine and explosive remnants of war(ERW) have occurred while children are playing, making boys and girls to susceptible to risk, injury, and even death. According to the most recent assessment which was conducted in Jan 2021 by CAO and the local authority in Nasir, many UXOS were found in Dhorguongni and Wangbub in Nasir, and the CAO EORE team are planning to go back to conduct the marking and taking coordinates to share with UNMAS. It was also reported by the County ROSS in Mandeng that 3 incidents happened in Keatbek leaving 2 people dead and 1 injured.UNMAS reported over 75 victims of mine explosives half were fatalities and this is due to a lack of knowledge about the mines and the UXOs. CAO also conducted an IRNA assessment in Fangak and Canal/Pigi and the report showed that the areas contaminated with mines / ERW following several accidents that have happened to women, girls, men, boys, and also animals. During the assessment, several incidences were reported involving 3 women, 3 children, and 3 animals in Fangak, and 1 child in korwai in Pigi/canal who were confirmed dead due to the explosion of land mines. There is a great need to conduct more EORE awareness since women and children are ignorant and not aware of the types, dangers, and effects of mines and UXOsThere is widespread trauma among women, men, girls, and boys because of the existing protection threats of the mines/ERW and UXOS in Fangak, Pigi/Canal, and Nasir. In the community in these areas, many are farmers and cattle keepers such contaminated areas prevent access to their farms and the grazing field. CAO as a partner on the ground was trained and accredited by Danish Church Aid (DCA) in 2018,2021 was trained by NMA on how to conduct awareness and taking coordinates of hazard areas on EORE in the community structures, and at schools/TLS, CFS, WGFS, CAO has received one GPS from UNICEF to take coordinates of the contaminated areas, CAO has the painted stones and the tapes for marking the hazard areas. CAO will provide EORE awareness on the safe behavior and will reach 24,000 (6,000 women, 6,000 girls 6,000Mand 6,000 boys) to the conflict and flood-affected populations living in Fangak, Canal/Pigi county of Jonglei state, and Nasir County to the Host communities, returnee, and IDP. There is a need for community liaison with partners to ensure girls, women, boys men, and animals are protected from mines and ERW
