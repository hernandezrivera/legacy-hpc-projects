Project Summary

This project aims to improve the food security in NW Syria via supporting IDPs in camps through life saving intervention to enhance their access to food. This project consists of processing 320 MT of wheat flour into 360 tons of fresh Syrian bread and distributing it as fresh bundles to 2500 IDPs households in camps for six months via contracting local bakeries. Fund is secured.

4 - Needs

An estimate of 3.3 million people, in NW Syria, as 80% of total population is put under food insecurity. 86% of population in Idleb governorate and 59% of population in Aleppo governorate need food assistance. In NW Syria, 2.8 million people up to 65% of total population is IDP. Among these IDPs, 1.5 million people is forced to live in camps, as the most vulnerable. Among them, 80% population is female and children. Since they do not have stable income source, they have to rely on humanitarian assistance. Among food needs, bread is in high demand. According to a survey conducted at northwest Syria, 77% of IDPs and 70% of host communities answered that bread was the most needed food. In Syrian eating habits, breads are inevitable. However, bread price tripled and only 58% of population satisfied their minimum needs in 2020. Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) Cluster points out the serious gap between demands and supplies of breads in the region. It calls for assistance especially towards the IDPs living in the camps where no bread distribution is conducted.

5 - Implementation of activities

1st Selection of target areas: AAR Japan coordinates with FSL cluster CCCM Cluster regarding needs to avoid the overlaps. On the field level, our partner Hand in Hand for Aid and Development (HIH) coordinates with local councils, camp managements, and other organizations working inside Syria. AAR Japan and HIH integrate collected information to make potential target areas. 2nd Selection of bakeries: To ensure the quality of bread, bakeries must be within 15 km from the target camps. AAR Japan conducts a bidding from several suppliers. A selection committee consisted of both AAR Japan and HIH decides bakeries based on submitted information from suppliers such as price, quality, hygiene environment, and delivery to the camps. 3rd Procurement of flour: Flours will be procured in Turkey by AAR Japan. After the shipment from the supplier’s warehouse, flours will be transferred onto trucks of a Syrian transportation company at the crossing border area, and will be handed over to HIH at its warehouse. 4th Baking and Distribution of Breads: Bakeries will come to HIH warehouse to receive flour once a week. Bread will be baked 6 days a week except Friday. Distribution is conducted and recorded by the camp management based on the beneficiary list. 5th Monitoring: HIH monitoring team visits bakeries every day and confirms appropriate baking in accordance with the contract in terms of quality and quantity. It also conducts a spot check to verify the quality of distributed breads.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

AAR Japan and its partner uphold the 9 commitments of the CHS throughout the project, putting the Code of Conduct in practice and fulfilling its essential responsibilities as humanitarian organizations. To avoid conflict with communities due to misinformation of the services, pre-coordination with local councils shall take place to provide detailed information of the services to the beneficiaries, to ensure that beneficiaries understand their rights. Paying particular attention to women, children, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups, measures such as allocating a female staff in the field team will be taken to facilitate communication with communities and people affected, and to ensure that their feedbacks are properly reflected. Measures will be in place to ensure beneficiaries have safe access to handle their complaints and requests. Leaflets specifying the contact point for complaints will also be handed to each beneficiary receiving the service. All complaints will be addressed in a confidential and appropriate manner.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAR has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEAH (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment) and a responsibility to ensure that all people are treated with dignity and respect. Every staff member working for AAR and our partner organizations must always uphold the highest standards of behavior and accountability with everyone we encounter at all times. AAR ensures that all AAR staff and associates are aware of the high standards of behavior and adopt exemplary behavior to create, strengthen, and maintain healthy environments and minimize the risks of SEAH. This includes, but is not limited to, promoting awareness among AAR staff and associates and people in the communities, conducting safe staff recruitment, building safe projects and programs and ensuring good practice when using media. In case complaints and tip-offs regarding sexual abuse or exploitations are received, the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Officer shall intervene and respond to the case; an investigation team, under the direct chain of command of the Chief Executive Officer, shall report to the PSEA committee, and verify the facts on the case. HIH MEAL team shall report misconducts in the field in accordance with policies on preventing such acts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As a precondition, HIH staff members must be able to operate safely in the target areas. The assumed risks are deterioration in security situation, intervention by armed groups, closure of border between Turkey and Syria, restriction of humanitarian activities within Syria by Turkish or Syrian governments, and the outbreak of COVID-19. Depending on the situation in the areas of operations, the project will be suspended till safe operations can be ensured. If no improvement in security situation can be seen, AAR Japan will consider changing access routes and target areas, or ceasing the project. Security assessment will take place before any field activity. To mitigate the risk of the spread of covid-19 in NW Syria, both AAR Japan and HIH have adopted their own policies to prevent Covid-19 infections among the staff members and beneficiaries. All staff members are required to abide by polices and restrictions imposed by the organizations and the local authorities, wear masks and gloves, wash hands on regular basis, measure body temperature, and take other precautious measures as necessary. At distribution points, appropriate behaviors will be prompted by using banners in addition to handwashing and temperature measuring areas will be set, and appropriate social distance among beneficiaries will be kept through the cooperation with camp management. For the safety of the staff members and beneficiaries, field activities will be postponed and rescheduled in case security concerns
