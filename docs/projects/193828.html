Project Summary

The project aims to provide urgently needed essential maternal, newborn, and child health and nutrition interventions serving vulnerable communities, particularly pregnant and lactating women, neonates, and children under the age of ﬁve, both boys and girls.
The project responds to two of the Health Cluster's strategic objectives:
- Strategic objective 2: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt
- Strategic Objective 3: Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises
The project will provide an urgent response to immediate health needs by ensuring access to essential health and nutrition services for the most vulnerable in the target population. This will be achieved through the implementation and provision of health and nutrition interventions including:
- Support the Ministry of Health to maintain the provision of essential maternal, neonatal, child health, and nutrition services for high-risk women and young children through the provision of medical supplies including essential drugs, equipment, and consumables.
- Prevention and control of micronutrient deficiencies in children and pregnant lactating women. Prevention and Management of acute malnutrition in children under five
- Provision of postnatal home visiting services after delivery to high-risk pregnancies and vulnerable mothers and babies. These services are important for mother in terms of providing postnatal care, including medical checks, assessment of health status, provision of medication and supplements, ultrasound images, health education, and counselling
- Capacity building of service providers particularly in relation to the implementation of the national Maternal and Child Nutrition Protocol
- Additionally, the project will provide essential drugs and consumables for prepositioning purposes, which will contribute to the timely and effective response to emergencies.
The project will be implemented in close coordination with Health Cluster partners including WHO, UNFPA, and international and local NGOs to ensure the complementarity of the interventions for the improvement of maternal, newborn, and child healthcare.
The number of women and children benefitting from the project will be 196,155 across the ﬁve Gaza governorates over a twelve-month period. This includes 36,000 high-risk women and caregivers and 60,000 children under the age of ﬁve (30,000 boys and 30,000 girls), 10,000 neonates (5,000 boys and 5,000 girls). 20,000 people (18,000 women and 2,000 men) will benefit from awareness sessions and health education. 155 health professionals will be trained (35 males and 120 females). 60,000 persons (30,000 women, 24,000 children, 6,000 men) will benefit from prepositioned drugs and consumables.

1 - Needs

In the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) published by OCHA in July 2022, 89.6% of Gaza households reported a member of their household having a healthcare need requiring care - with 99.8% of these households reporting a barrier to accessing care. The most commonly reported barrier to care was cost of services being too high, followed by unavailability of treatment and unavailability of medicines. .The context and key drivers of the humanitarian health needs in the State of Palestine attributable to several and multiple factors and conditions including the longstanding Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian political divides, and recurrent escalations of violence. The humanitarian health and nutrition needs of the Gaza population (more than 2 million residents) were further exacerbated by the continuing COVID-19 pandemic and, more recently, by the escalation of hostilities in August 2022, which negatively impacted livelihoods and access to essential health services in Gaza.The MoH 2021 Annual Report showed that the maternal mortality rate in Gaza has increased in 2021 reaching 60 deaths per 100,000 live births, compared to 24.2 deaths per 100,000 live births in 2020. This negative trend would require immediate action to reverse the situation. The situation in the maternity and neonatal wards in Gaza remains complicated due to overstretched capacity of health services leading to the early discharge of mothers and babies within 2-3 hours from birth with no access to post-natal services which reduces opportunities for detection of potential medical complications and timely provision of lifesaving interventions. Access to quality maternal and newborn health care is further deteriorated due to shortages of medical supplies and consumables. Hence, the life of 30,000 newborns requiring neonatal health care interventions annually could be severely jeopardized with intermittent and insuﬃcient provision of services. UNICEF interventions supporting the provision of post-natal home visits (PNHV) have proven their effectiveness in providing adequate care, particularly for the most vulnerable and hard-to-reach pregnant and lactating women and newborns in Gaza. The intervention fills important gaps, especially in provision of postnatal care. According to the Sep 2022 MOH Gaza Central Drugs Store report on drugs and disposables, 215 items (14 %) out of the essential medicines list were reported at zero stock levels in the Central Drug Store, while 411 items (17%) out of the medical disposables in the essential list, were reported as zero stock in the Central Medical Disposable Store. This severely impacts the service provision for newborns, young children, and high-risk pregnant and lactating women. UNICEF will continue to work in close coordination with the Ministry of Health, WHO, and other health and nutrition partners on strengthening the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of emergencies through provision of medical supplies including but not limited drugs and consumables. With the repeated escalations and outbreak of emergencies in Gaza, there is a continuous need to support local authorities in emergency preparedness and response. The project will contribute to enhancing the government emergency response through prepositioning of essential lifesaving drugs and consumables. This project is designed to contribute signiﬁcantly to the gender equality and is following a gender-transformative approach focusing on reducing gender-related discriminations and social norms faced by high-risk pregnant and lactating women and their new-borns and boys and girls under the age of ﬁve. The project will ensure engagement of fathers with targeting 2000 men to benefit from awareness sessions and health education.

2 - Activities

The project aims to provide urgently needed essential maternal, newborn, and child health and nutrition interventions serving the vulnerable communities particularly pregnant and lactating women, neonates, and children under the age of ﬁve, both boys and girls. The project will ensure complementarity with the WHO’s, UNFPA’s and an international NGO partner’s HRP projects. The project will achieve its desired impact through the following activities:1- Provide access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring availability of preconception care, clean and safe delivery, essential newborn care, safe spaces, and emergency obstetric services are available at all times - this will be achieved through:• The procurement of the necessary medical equipment; drugs and consumables and other medical supplies to support the provision of services for neonate children, children under-five and women• Support the provision of postnatal home visits to high-risk pregnancies and vulnerable mothers and babies. These services are important for mothers in terms of providing postnatal care including medical checkup, assessment of health status, provision of medication and supplements, ultrasound images, health education and counselling, and mental and wellbeing counselling. • Support the prevention and control of micronutrient deficiencies in pregnant lactating women and and under five children. • Support optimal infant and young child feeding through sensitization of care givers and communities. • Support the Management of acute malnutrition among under five children (detection and treatment) • Capacity building of service providers particularly in relation to the implementation of the national Maternal and Child Nutrition Protocol2- Enhance preparedness within the health sector to better deal with sudden onset events, such as heightened conflict and disease outbreak, through prepositioning of medical and nutrition supplies, capacity building, enhanced coordination of the nutrition sub cluster of which UNICEF is responsible, and communication. In terms of monitoring the implementation of the project, UNICEF will oversee project monitoring and will ensure timely reporting by taking the following actions:• Programmatic visits and regular monitoring field visits to project sites and implementing partners will be conducted by UNICEF responsible Programme oﬃcers • Review of submitted reports packages from implementing partners including review of progress against agreed indicators, verification of the validity of reported data, and verification of financial data, • Through continuous monitoring, barriers and challenges will be captured and proposed solutions and lessons learned will be identified and shared with all relevant stakeholders if required• Collected and reported data will be analyzed and disaggregated by gender and by age group. Additionally, and throughout the implementation of this project, UNICEF will ensure the following considerations:• Gender will be mainstreamed by working with other clusters to ensure that clinical management of GBV is improved. • Disability will be mainstreamed by ensuring the inclusion of persons with disability in all project stages• Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. • Ensure the involvement of target beneﬁciaries throughout the interventions • Channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction survey and recording goods of services delivered to the end user, so that assistance is delivered in accordance with the needs and in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.• PSEA awareness raising material including information on rights and safe complaint channels will be available to beneficiaries at all project sites.• UNICEF will work on leveraging resources through other partners to address issues related to maternal care such as GBV and referral of cases to relevant services.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The feedback received from affected populations will help in the timely identification of challenges, problems and issues. This will inform decision making, corrective procedures, and measures and re-design of interventions capturing lessons learnt and received feedback

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Across all projects of UNICEF in the SoP, feedback mechanisms have strengthened and replicated whether in the form of a hotline or an email address. A new email address (sop_report@unicef.org) has been created to establish a direct line of communication between beneficiaries and the UNICEF CO. It is a case management system under which beneficiaries’ feedback and/or complaints are handled on a case-by-case system through referrals and response. UNICEF has engaged with WFP on Inter-Agency hotline services, the IA Hotline is in the form of a toll-free, country-wide number that affected populations can call to obtain information on humanitarian and development assistance programmes; submit their feedback; and, be referred to the humanitarian or development organizations that are best-suited to handle their issues and address their need. UNICEF SoP has piloted the cash transfer programme to collect feedback from direct beneficiaries via the IA hotline services. UNICEF is planning to scale up the coverage with the IA to include other programmes including part of the interventions implemented under this project Keeping in mind partnership building and project sustainability, UNICEF SoP involved and worked with local implementing partners on capacity development and training them on the core humanitarian standards and AAP and PSEA principles. The partners’ commitments were also strengthened in terms of the IASC principles of AAP through project activities.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNICEF has strengthened Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in programmes through the development of AAP Context Analysis, capacity building, and AAP system strengthening. With the Inter-Agency hotline services, UNCIEF provides a free platform that affected populations can use. The platform provides a space to beneficiaries and population to inform UNICEF of their feedback, concerns, and any issues that they face that are related to UNICEF programmes and interventions. UNICEF is planning to scale up the coverage of the hotline services to include other programmes including part of the interventions implemented under this project

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards, and WHO standards for medical equipment and medical supplies procured. UNICEF ensures implementation of feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end users. UNICEF also conducts end user monitoring field visits to ensure safety and quality of services and supplies provided.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNICEF ensures the verification of needs working with WHO and health cluster members to ensure reaching the most vulnerable communities. UNICEF ensures that independent needs verification is completed prior to distribution of supplies, in addition UNICEF verifies the completion of the delivery of supplies to their intended locations/beneficiaries. UNICEF ensures that a multi-vulnerability targeting is considered to guarantee equity and impartial interventions are designed.
