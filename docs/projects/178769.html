Project Summary

Given the humanitarian needs in Syria, St. Ephrem Patriarchal Development Committee (EPDC) is planning to conduct a project with a total budget of 567,110 USD (1 USD = 2500 SYP according to the Central Bank of Syria) for a period of six months from June 1st to December 31st, 2021. The proposed response will be in Rural Damascus in the following locations: East Ghouta (Kafr Batna, Nashabieh, Al Maliha, Haran Al Awameed), West Ghouta (Kisweh, Babela, Daryya, Sehnaya), Qatna, and Jaramana. The response will response to the needs enlisted under the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) in line with priorities of protection, environment, and cross cutting issues. The intervention will respond to the estimated 939,306 individuals (206,647 W, 169,075 M, 309,971 G, 253,613 B) through solid waste management activities using cash for work modality, and conducting hygiene promotion including awareness on COVID-19 protection measures in accordance with the Health sector and Ministry of Health instructions and publications using IPE materials, e.g. brochures, and at the end of each session the beneficiaries will receive family hygiene kits including gender sensitive items, i.e. sanitary pads.
According to HNO 2021, the number of people in need increased from 11.06 in 2020 to reach 13.4 million with 6.76 million in severe need and 4.51 million in extreme need of humanitarian assistance. 12.2 million are in need of WASH services. The conflict has left the infrastructure destroyed, impacting the provision of basic services to people and their ability to cope. This has led to the reduction of resources, including basic services, and increased use of negative coping strategies. Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), communication and participation and complaints/feedback mechanisms will be inclusive in the project cycle. Responses to WASH is aligned with the HNO overview 2021, as well as with updates shared by the relevant Whole of Syria (WoS) Clusters and Working groups, encouraging revitalization of Syria’s social, economic and environmental space, while simultaneously mainstreaming key cross-cutting issues (human rights, conflict sensitivity and Do-No-Harm (DNH), women’s rights and gender equality, climate and environment and anti-corruption), as well as putting particular focus on COVID-19 prevention and its effects on the population of concern.
EPDC also foster this two-way communication with beneficiaries and local populations through: extensive and systematic community sensitization at the start of activities and throughout implementation, regular monitoring of the project implementation through both the MEAL and Program teams, and CRM. EPDC will ensure transparency with the targeted community in the provided services especially regarding the selection criteria and project objectives.
EPDC also will report on the progress of the activities under this project to the relevant sector, i.e., WASH sector, using the 5Ws tool and will participate effectively in the coordination meetings to ensure high coordination with other humanitarian actors and will share lesson learnt and experience. It is worth mentioning that EPDC has experience in the above-mentioned activities and in the above-mentioned locations given its good reputation and easy access. Also, EPDC has long experience in SWM activities that involved recruiting women and people with disabilities using CFW modality.

4 - Needs

This crisis has been compounded by both the COVD-19 pandemic and the deterioration of the socio-economic situation, which saw the rapid devaluation of the Syrian currency during the summer of 2020 and early 2021. This has had significant impacts on all communities, increasing households’ vulnerabilities, and limiting access to basic items and services. According to HNO2021, about 3.2 million people are in need of SWM and about 7.5 million people are in need of hygiene supplies. The cumulative effects of a pre-existing fragile economy and the subsequent restriction measures applied during the initial phase of the pandemic have heavily impacted employment opportunities, increased prices and negatively affected households’ coping mechanisms. This resulted in more than 90% of Syrian population live below poverty line and prices of goods increased 236% (HNO 2021) adding challenges to vulnerable people to access safe WASH services or combat the spread of COVID-19. As the services were highly affected, the WASH services need support as about 29% of garbage is inappropriately disposed. It is anticipated that C-19 cases will continue in 2021 especially with the low awareness, increase in prices cleaning materials and density of population in some areas like Jaramana and East &West Ghouta. Thus, the targeted community are in need of comprehensive approach to address solid waste problem, availability of hygiene materials and information, IPC measures including sterilization of streets and places of crowding, e.g., bread plants. EPDC has conducted a needs assessment in the targeted locations and 75% are in need of SWM.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project plans to hire 225 workers to clean the streets using cash for work modality and 30 supervisors to supervise and monitor the workers (Each supervisor monitors 10 - 12 workers).The site selection for the CFW will be through geo-target assessment, recommendation from WASH sector, avoid overlapping, and feedback from communities to EPDC.An advertisement for recruitment will be published in the targeted locations with transparent selection criteria. Then, the registered will be verified according to eligibility and vulnerability criteria will be score-based. The people of the targeted communities will be included without discrimination, encouraged of all its parts to participate in the program including females.The workers will be assigned to sectors in the targeted locations and a supervisor is assigned to each 10-12 workers. The workers will receive their salaries (250,000SYP).The accumulated garbage will be removed safely to official disposal lands in the targeted communities.The MEAL team will conduct monitoring visits to the project sites, monitor salary distribution, and conduct end line.The project will also contain conducting 100 COVID19 awareness sessions to beneficiaries in the targeted location and distributing 2000 family hygiene kits and promotion materials (200 family hygiene kits per location) with strict application of COVID19 preventive measures.The MEAL team will verify potential BNFs names, monitor distribution and COVID19 preventive measures, and conduct PDM.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Security level does not deteriorate to an extent that prevents EPDC teams from conducting the activities in the field - No major change of the current balance between parties at conflict leads to major upheavals in the targeted areas - No major security incident targeting EPDC personnel or other I/NNGOs or civilians - Field access remains granted by Syrian authorities - No major rise in the exchange rate and prices of materials remain stable COVID19 situation do not escalate and vaccination proceed to all populationRisks: - Planned activities pose environmental/ health risks to staff, stakeholders, or targeted communities and beneficiaries including the unintentional spread of COVID-19 - The value of the Syrian Pound deteriorates to a level that activities cannot be implemented within the planned budget - Potential corruption or fraud - Planned activities pose negative impact on groundwater, land and biodiversity- Increased COVID19 cases and lockdown Mitigation Measures: - All activities will be planned and monitored closely in coordination with the local community to ensure no harm to beneficiaries. All EPDC staff use adequate PPE while in the field, practice social distancing and receive awareness training on COVID-19 and prevention measures - Maintain strict controls and application of Anti-Fraud policy and procurement and finance procedures - cumulated garbage transferred to official disposal lands, all waste resulting from the activities are disposed in allocated locations- Activities modified according to the COVID19 situation, e.g. hygiene kits become door-to-door and promotion become online, coordination with official authority to sterilize most infected and heavily populated locations.
