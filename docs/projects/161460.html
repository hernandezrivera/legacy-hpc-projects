Project Summary

To help CCCM partners to implement the CCCM 2020-2021 strategy, REACH will work in partnership with the CCCM Cluster to provide actionable information about displaced populations. The project will support three key aspects of the strategy. Firstly, to support static responses REACH will produce maps of camp infrastructure and services, enabling camp management to identify and any address gaps in service provision. Secondly, REACH will support the mobile response through the provision of data and analysis on hard-to-reach areas. This will help mobile teams select areas of intervention based on need. Finally, REACH will assist both static and mobile teams in improving their evidence-base for decision making through technical support on assessment methodology, including developing indicators on accountability to affected populations.

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the conflict in 2013, more than 1 million South Sudanese are displaced within South Sudan. In order to continue to meet the needs of internally displaced people (IDPs) in South Sudan seeking temporary assistance, CCCM actors require information in order to inform needs-based prioritisation and identify areas of improvement in service provision. In particular, it is critical that the perspectives of the affected population are understood and fed into CCCM decision making. The target group is humanitarian organisations who support IDPs within South Sudan.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Support static responses by producing maps of camp infrastructure and services. To support planning and programmatic activities in the POC sites and other static responses, REACH support resulting in the production of general infrastructure and thematic maps i.e. spatial analytical maps such as, access to key services, shelter density, PoC address system. These maps can help CCCM partners evaluate service coverage, identify and address gaps in service provision, and can facilitate operational planning. Activity 2: Support the mobile response through the provision of data and analysis on hard-to-reach areas. Information on the presence of displaced populations and their needs is critical to ensuring mobile teams respond in areas where they will have the greatest impact. REACH will support this process by providing regular updates on the needs of hard-to-reach areas, including monthly factsheets on CCCM needs and other sectors using the area-of-knowledge (AoK) remote needs monitoring data. In addition, REACH will mobilise its field teams and networks when information about specific locations is required, and feed this information back to decision-makers in the cluster. Activity 3: Provide technical support in data collection. Both static and mobile responses require data to inform and improve service delivery. For static teams, CCCM partners conduct assessments on the needs and intentions of the camp population. For these partners, REACH can provide technical support to optimise the accuracy and relevance of the information gathered. This can include capacity building on how to create and use smart surveys (ODK), guidance on sampling and input on analysis of the results. In addition, REACH can draw upon our expertise in accountability to affected populations (AAP) assessments to provide AAP indicators for partners hoping to increase the input of the camp population in their service planning. For mobile responses, mobile response scoping is a key part of deciding if and how to respond in a location. As data collection specialists, REACH can provide guidance on how to adapt the tool depending on level of access and time on the ground. Coordination with the cluster: In order to ensure these activities are coordinated with the CCCM cluster REACH will attend the CCCM cluster meetings and provide updates when relevant. Maps will be produced on the request of CCCM partners, and designed in consultation with camp management. Technical support will be available to all partners.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Given the sensitive nature of the data REACH collects, and the vulnerability of many of potential interviewees, REACH recognizes the risk of assessment activities to expose interviewees to unintended physical or psychosocial risk. To mitigate this; REACH tightly control the release of identifying information that may expose interviewees and through its data protection policies. REACH staff received on trauma-sensitive interviewing techniques as part of a training of trainers’ session focused on avoiding re-traumatization of research participants during data collection. The training was provided by a protection expert from an international protection agency and REACH will roll out this training across its 14 field bases through these newly trained staff. REACH is currently in the process of creating awareness of this new system amongst protection agencies in field locations to ensure agencies are prepared for potential referrals before the system is rolled out across field locations. Once fully functional and with lessons learned incorporated, this mechanism could serve as a best practice for other data collection agencies / activities in South Sudan to ensure an ethical data collection approach

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The assessments tools REACH will develop for CCCM cluster will be handed over to the implementing partners, providing them with the technical support and capacity building sessions on how best to role them out.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Data collection is carried out in a gender-sensitive manner, i.e. conducting FGDs separated by sex when discussing potentially sensitive topics. All REACH data collection field teams have been trained on this and SOPs on gender-sensitive data collection practices are in place and followed by all teams. Women are represented as key informants / research participants to ensure their needs are adequately recorded. Gender disaggregated data, specifically for protection concerns, will be shared with the relevant clusters and sub-clusters (protection, SGBV and Child Protection) to ensure a gender-sensitive understanding of protection issues in South Sudan and to encourage gender mainstreaming in the broader humanitarian response.
