Project Summary

The recent drought in Somali Region has affected 9 zones out 11, of which Dawa zone is one of the most significantly affected Zone in the region. According to Regional Drought Response Plan, prepared in December 2021 showed that 2,279,606 people in the region are reported to be currently facing water shortage and need of water trucking support. The drought also affected the schooling of more than 99,000 students due to school closures and lack of school feeding programs.
The 2021 draught situation seems an era in the history of shocks at Dawa zone due to widespread and several precipitating factors, The 2021 Deyr rain in many parts of Dawa zone was totally not satisfactory in comparison to previous GU rains in terms of onset, intensity, coverage, frequency, and distribution. Based on the joint assessment made in October 2021, only Mubarak woreda with seven sub villages received little rain out 31 villages, whereas the rest of the three woredas received none. The pasture and peruse have in all woredas of the Dawa zone been exhausted, with migration of livestock and human from different dimension leading to scarce pasture and water leading degradation of resources. Both the 2020 short rains (October to December) and the 2021 long rains (March to May) were poor across Dawa zone.
The crisis led to displacement and continues to threaten a humanitarian crisis. Drought has led to mass harvest losses in the zones. As Dawa Zone Rapid Draught Assessment Report October 2021 indicated around 49 kebeles in four woredas have a total 62,960 host communities and 41,745 Internal Displaced People (IDPs) requiring urgent response on food and water. (Source: Dawa zone rapid needs assessment). The emergency shelter and non-food items Kit for the IDPs and host communities project is planned to be implemented in Hudet in Dawa Zone of Somali Regional State. Due to the drought and high needs of IDPs and host communities, Hudet woreda is prioritized by the ES/NFI cluster for HRP2022. The woreda is extreme priority. The overall goal of the project is aligned to CLSHL/CO1 and CLSHL/CO2; ensure contextualized access to lifesaving shelters and NFIs for displacement affected populations with a focus on the most vulnerable and at-risk population group to safeguard their health security, privacy, and dignity and Contribute to improving protection outcomes by ensuring the conflict/displacement affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions. To achieve the project goal, ASDEPO will propose the following activities; Provision of in-kind or cash for emergency shelter assistance for physical protection and to reduce overcrowding, Provision of Non-food items to reduce the likelihood of health and protection consequences , Provision of NFI Kits to the persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness, Provision of Emergency Shelter and NFI that consider the needs of women, children, people with disabilities and improve the beneficiaries safety and security , Provision of ESNFIs to the persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness and provide PSEA training for its frontline staff as well mainstreaming gender. During the selection of beneficiaries, women, girls, and people with disabilities will be given a priority. The required budget for this proposes project is 420,123 USD for the consecutive six months. 70% of the project will be IDPs and 15% for host communities as well as 15% will be pwds’ affected by conflict.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

ASDEPO will work closely with partners – INGOs, NNGOs, clusters, and the government who have also long experience in the sector implementations and have grounded and proven expertise in implementing humanitarian response to reinforce the long-term impact. There are currently limited organizations providing emergency shelter and non-food items kit responses for IDPs and host communities in the area. When developingASDEPO live-saving activities, prioritization of activities was done to ensure the complementarity of emergency shelter and non-food items kit activities with other operating partners and ensure geographical complementarity. The deployment of the humanitarian personnel will enhance the coordination forums both at the zonal andworeda level for better response services to IDPs and host communities are found in the targeted woreda. The emergency ES/NFI Cluster Strategy Plan tracks the ongoing and planned responses of cluster members and is specifically designed to support complementarity and coordination. Throughout this project, ASDEPO will take into account the continued and active involvement and participation of the community, particularly women, and stakeholders. ASDEPO will advocate at the zone level to link efforts by organizing monthly cluster coordination meetings to evaluate the sector activity implementations and humanitarian situation of the zone with other partners and UN agencies. ASDEPO will actively participate in national and sub-national ES/NFIclusters and HLP WG.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

.To further ensure accountability of the project and project staff, a feedback mechanism will be put in place (suggestion box, suggestion registration book and hotline calls, as well as cases, can report anonymously. This will primarily consist of community meetings, segregated by sex and age and with an emphasis on persons with specific vulnerabilities such as persons with disabilities, in which beneficiaries are encouraged to give feedback or voice complaints/in person at the field office. This method is proposed given the low level of literacy which rules out written feedback. Moreover, ASDEPO M&E staff, not involved in project delivery, will also be involved, benefit ciaries feel confident to report problems. Feedback collected from these meetings will be reviewed by program staff, who will adjust interventions accordingly, where appropriate. Notes will be taken, and actions taken reported back to the next community meeting.
