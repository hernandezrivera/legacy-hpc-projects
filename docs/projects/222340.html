Project Summary

South Sudan's health systems are weakened due to prolonged conflict, climate change impacts, and limited government investment, resulting in poor infrastructure, insufficient human resources, and insufficient financing, limiting access to quality health services.
The Project will aim to provide quality lifesaving integrated health care to crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system targeting communities in remote and underserved areas of Panyijar, Koch, Rubkona, and Aweil East, counties in two states (Unity and Northern Bahr El Ghazal) targeting approximately 170,882 people, including a prioritized focus on women, children, youth and individuals living with disabilities.
The proposed health intervention will be aligned to Health cluster objectives and Humanitarian Needs and response Plan 2025 by seeking to reduce mortality and morbidity by ensuring access to essential health services, improving community resilience, and strengthening local health infrastructure to respond to immediate and future health crises.
The proposed project will utilize a multi-channel approach to ensure healthcare reaches the most vulnerable populations, including those in remote and conflict-affected areas. The IRC aims to support 3 Static health facilities (1 Rubkona, 1 Koch, 1 Panyijar county) and 11 Rapid response (2 in Koch, 3 In Rubkona, 3Panyijar, 3 in Aweil East) teams and 342 Boma health workers (22 Koch;146 Rubkona, 30 in Panyijar; and 144 in Aweil East) to provide essential integrated lifesaving health interventions. Communities and County Health Departments will be engaged to map out underserved populations and prioritize displaced populations with limited access to health services.
The proposed initiative will focus on the provision of primary and emergency healthcare, maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). The response will also improve sexual reproductive health services of EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape for vulnerable women and girls. The response will utilize referral mechanism to enhance efficiency, integration, and quality of care.
To strengthen the local response capacity, the IRC will work with county health departments, health service providers and community health workers to enhance their capacity to manage health emergencies through training of health care workers and community health workers. Furthermore, to ensure communities are more resilient and better prepared for future disasters, risk communication and community engagement focusing on avoiding hazards, risk of waterborne diseases, and health promotion will be prioritized during the implementation.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces significant health challenges due to conflict, political instability, and humanitarian crises. The underdeveloped health system has a high maternal mortality rate of 1,223 per 100,000 live births according to World bank MMR Estimation 2022 and under-five mortality rate (98.8 per 1,000 live births-UNICEF 2023). Food insecurity, displacement, and disease outbreaks further strain the healthcare system, with only 44% (ISNA 2023) of the population living within 5 kilometers of a functional facility. Frequent flooding, protracted conflict, and population displacement have worsened health access, leading to disease outbreaks, including cholera, malaria, and measles. The situation is worsened by a critical shortage of healthcare workers, essential medical supplies, and inadequate infrastructure and impact of Sudan crises further straining the fragile health system.The health cluster’s People in Need (PiN) severity target prioritizes Aweil East, Koch, Panyijar and Rubkona counties, due to critical healthcare gaps and systemic vulnerabilities. The targeted counties exhibit some of the worst health outcomes, recurrent shocks, and exacerbating humanitarian challenges with an estimated 874,804 people (Aweil East: 346,435, Koch: 95,176; Panyijar 103,117 and Rubkona:330,076) in need of health services (PiN target 2025) including women and children, who make up over 60% of the targeted population.Population displacement in targeted counties due to internal conflict and recurrent flooding has destroyed infrastructure, displaced communities, and disrupted access to essential health services. In Panyijar, 50% of health facilities become inaccessible during the rainy season and 8% of the facilities provide full package of BPHNS, leaving populations without life-saving care. Displacement places immense pressure on host communities and overstretched health systems, with IDPs living in overcrowded conditions and increasing disease transmission risk. The Health Sector Transformation Project supports only 50% of functional health facilities, with 18% being non-functional.Immunization coverage remains below the national average, with less than 40% of children receiving the recommended three doses of DPT vaccine. This has led to vaccine preventable disease outbreaks, such as measles and Hepatitis E in Aweil East, Panyijar, and Rubkona. Cholera outbreaks have been reported in Aweil East due to the Sudan crises and poor WASH conditions, while Rubkona and Panyijar are prioritized locations for cholera outbreaks. According to IRNA 2024, less than 10% of households in Panyijar county have access to toilets, with open defecation being a norm. Limited access to skilled birth attendants and emergency obstetric care in rural areas affects 20% of women, leading to high under-five mortality rates due to preventable diseases like pneumonia, diarrhea, and measles, and malaria accounting for over 50% of outpatient consultations and 75% of childhood mortality. Targeted counties are experiencing severe acute food insecurity, often hovering between IPC phase 3 (crisis) and IPC phase 4 (emergency), leading to poor health outcomesThe Sudan crisis has significantly impacted Aweil East, with 36,760 refugees and returnees (IOM-DTM Nov 2024) causing strain on the already fragile health system. Overwhelmed health facilities, shortages of essential medicines, inadequate staffing, and overburdened infrastructure have led to deteriorating health outcomes, especially for women and children. Women and girls face increased risks of SGBV due to displacement and overcrowded living condition with only 28% of health facilities providing GBV care (GBV Health analysis July 2024)The highlighted needs reflect the severe and multifaceted challenges necessitating urgent humanitarian action, while strengthening health systems to improve resilience and equitable access to services.

2. Project activities

The proposed project seeks to improve the health status of vulnerable populations in need across 4 Counties (Aweil East, Koch, Panyijar, and Rubkona Counties), targeting 170,882 persons (35,790 Men, 39,109 Women, 46,664 Boys, and 49,321 Girls including 10,961 people living with disability). All intervention will be provided in alignment with the health cluster strategic focus. 1.Provision of equitable access to life-saving essential health care services: The project will employ a multi-channel approach including support to 3 static facilities, 11 rapid response mobile (RRM) teams and 342 Boma health workers (BHWs) to deliver services to underserved population in need. Qualified health workers will be recruited, and equipped with essential medical supplies, to provide timely and quality health care services in the static and mobile teams including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, and treatment of common illnesses. BHWs will be selected from the targeted communities and trained on screening and diagnosis for common illnesses affecting children under 5 years (malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea) including community nutrition screening and referral. Treatment of non-communicable diseases, mental health, and trauma clients will be provided while strengthening referral links to existing secondary care facilities.2. Strengthen disease surveillance and response to reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases- The project will ensure timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases through a strengthened community system, and coordination with local authorities, boma health committees, and the health facility in charge. Response activities will be coordinated, looping in all actors to ensure zero duplication of effort. Data will be stored in DHIS2 and Integrated disease surveillance and response (IDSR) and Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS). The project will develop and update integrated health and nutrition micro-plans in collaboration with local authorities and the Ministry of Health, aiming at comprehensive service coordination and enhancing Emergency Preparedness and Response. 3. GBV health response and Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS)Integration: the project will aim to strengthen provision of comprehensive GBV services, including clinical care, psychosocial support, and referrals, to survivors of sexual violence. Healthcare workers will be trained to offer MHPSS, clinical management of rape (CMR) and integrate psychosocial care into primary health services. Community awareness targeting women, girls, and other vulnerable groups will be conducted to generate demand for services, create awareness on GBV preventions, and existence of referral mechanisms.4. Support community health engagement and health promotion interventions- to empower the affected population in targeted counties, particularly in remote and hard-to-reach areas, the project will engage BHWs and other community structures to conduct community awareness campaigns, behavior change and communication, disease prevention and health promotion. BHWs through door-to-door visits, group discussions, radio messaging, and visual aids to disseminate information in culturally appropriate ways.5. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. The project will enhance resilience and promote humanitarian development linkages through engaging communities in the delivery of health care. Community members will be selected to join the facility health committees to strengthen accountability. Technical and operational oversight through monthly support supervision visits to health facilities will be conducted. This will result in decreased disease prevalence, improved health-seeking behavior, and ultimately a more robust and resilient community health ecosystem.
