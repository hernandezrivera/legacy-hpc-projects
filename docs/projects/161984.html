Project Summary

Through this proposed intervention, DCA will provide support to women and girls through provision of services and continue to engage men and boys in transformational behaviours to work as allies with women. The project will therefore enhance the wellbeing and dignity of the host communities, empower women and girls to access services, and make positive life choices. The project will furthermore mitigate negative coping mechanisms, enhance wellbeing in families, and support women and girls living in host communities to better lives in an intermediary situation. Individual health, psychological and social consequences of GBV will be reduced, ensuring that survivors will have improved their psychosocial abilities and reduce health impacts.

Needs

In August 2017 a surge of violence in Myanmar’s Rakhine State saw 700,000 Rohingya flee across the border to neighboring Bangladesh. They settled in Cox’s Bazar District, joining around 200,000 Rohingya already seeking shelter in what is one of Bangladesh’s poorest and most disaster-prone districts. The total number of people in need is estimated at 1.2 million people, and two years on, protection risks are paramount in this protracted crisis. 52% of the refugee population in Cox’s Bazar are women and girls. Many of them were exposed to sexual violence in Myanmar, and during their current displacement they continue to be at disproportionate risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV). Key areas of concern are increased incidents of intimate partner violence (IPV); 74% of reported GBV cases are IPV, with 98% of those reported affecting women, underreporting of GBV due to stigma, and restriction of female movement. In March 2019, DCA conducted a baseline survey in camp 21 and surrounding host communities including Whykong, it was evident that there are gaps in service delivery when it comes to GBV services. Alongside this high risk of GBV, the majority of Rohingya women do not have a reliable income source, and report that they are unable to meet their basic needs because of a lack of job opportunities (60%), and limitations in access to markets (68%). In June 2019, DCA’s survivor satisfaction survey identified that of the closed cases, 71% were very satisfied with the case management services they received, and particularly happy with the fast referrals to other actors in health, shelter, legal support and security. Survivors liked that DCA caseworkers accompanied them to service providers as it gave them confidence and sped up the process. Strong case management practices encouraged survivors for visit the WGSS more often, so they benefited from a wider range of support including PSS group empowerment activities, and livelihood training. From the facility mapping conducted by the GBV sub-sector members by mid 2019, also shows there is greater need to increase the capacity of GBV staffs in the field as well as reach more host communities in GBV interventions.

Activities or outputs

Community participation is one of DCA’s key operating principles and regarded as intrinsic to good programming and accountability. DCA is a certified member of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) and is committed to sharing information, participation and handling complaints during the project cycle. The design is informed by DCA’s experience in GBV programming in Cox Bazar and across the world. The following activities will be implemented at the host communities.1. Women and girls at risk and GBV survivors receiving PSS support including GBV case management- Provision of focused individualized PSS intervention and case management for GBV. GBV survivors disclosing their incidents are provided with timely services through the case management process where they will be offered psychosocial support and referred to other service providers through the existing referral pathway. Referral pathways and mechanisms has been developed and updated to facilitate safe and ethical referrals and to cover the needs of the survivors responding to survivors wishes in the project location. - Hot spot mapping to identify the risk of GBV in the project location - Recreational activities such as dancing, drama, arts and crafts, story-telling for women and girls- Tailored sessions with adolescent girls using different adolescent girls’ toolkits - Organize monthly staff wellbeing and staff care sessions to provide the better services to community members.- Provision of material assistance such as Dignity Kits 2. Engaging the community in GBV prevention and strengthening the community resilience.- Interactive sessions with the community members using "Walk in her shoes tool kit" as well as Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP)- FGDs with men and boys including religious/ camp leaders on gender, Human Rights etc.- Promote survivors’ access to IGA based on their own needs and wishes3. Strengthening the capacity of services providers internally and from other actors on the GBV guiding principles and survivor centered approach- GBV training of duty bearers and humanitarian staff on GBV prevention and response, GBV guiding principles, Case Management, Human Rights and IASC guidelines for integrating GBV in humanitarian settings- Training DCA staff on comprehensive psychosocial well-being. Sector coordinationDCA engages with a range of stakeholders during the project planning, design and implementation phases. These overall include the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Committee (RRRC), UN agencies (IOM, UNHCR, UNICEF, UNFPA, WFP) and other I/NGOs, and local authorities (DC and UNO). DCA is an active member of the GBV sub Sector in Cox Bazar and attends all relevant meetings related to protection in the camps. DCA contributes to bi-weekly 5W matrix and GBVIMS to the sub sector focal point according to information sharing protocols. DCA emphasizes the coordination and discussion with the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) representatives at all levels considering frequent changes on allowed activities, decisions taken by the different departments of the Government through a time evolving bureaucratic process. Inter Sectoral linkages/cooperation The GBV sector closely works with DRR unit, Education and livelihoods sectors within DCA and with other actors working in the camps. Activities are guided by protection priorities and assessments on how for example local site or shelter improvements can help mitigate GBV risks or other risks for women and girls (e.g. installation of solar lamps in dark areas, separate bathing spaces for women) as well as focus on improving access to essential, life-saving services by constructing pathways, stairs and bamboo bridges to water points, health points or distribution areas. Street solar lights will be installed in high-risk areas where women, girls and children face risks during night due to lack of lighting.
