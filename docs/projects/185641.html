Project Summary

UNICEF-C4D (Communications 4 Development), as a member of Communication with Communities (CwC) Working Group, have been playing a critical role in engaging communities to enhance life-saving behaviours, promote the uptake of services and foster a structured accountability and social cohesion through community multi-level, evidence-based communication, and community engagement with a view to reach and productively engage Rohingya refugees and host communities.
C4D community resources include 18 Information and Feedback Centres (IFCs) as service points for communities to register grievances, gather information, and obtain referrals on humanitarian services. In addition, over 700 community volunteers including Religious Leaders as key influencers are engaged in community mobilization, including establishing strategic partnerships with 2 radio stations to amplify and reinforce key messages on behaviour change communication interventions and support mass mobilization campaigns. C4D collaborates with broad range of partners from Local Government Authorities, NGOs (Non-Governmental Organisations), CSOs (Civil Society Organisations), Media and communities in host community and Rohingya camps in enhancing social and behaviour change.
Within the framework of building back better and drawing on from the success of 2021 in Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) for Covid-19 response, where 97% in host community and 100% in Camps (JMSNA (Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment 2021) reported being able to access clear information on Covid-19 preventive practices, the focus in 2022 will be to sustain the gains achieved in RCCE promoting maintenance of Covid-19 prevention practices and other emerging diseases, promote demand for COVID-19 vaccine across age cohorts and generating vaccine acceptance through intensified community engagement through various approaches and platforms. Furthermore, community feedback will be strengthened and reinforced ensuring strategic linkages with health, nutrition, education, water and sanitation and Child -Protection services for Rohingya and Bangladesh host communities. The role of RCCE is increasingly being recognized by key sectors as critical and underpinning component enabling rapid uptake of services, containment of disease transmission, as in the case of Covid-19 pandemic and in promoting continuity of services due to Covid-19 pandemic.
On Accountability to Affected Population and Do No Harm, C4D will enhance the capacity through updating the IFCs to digital platforms and working with CwC to provide support to other sectoral information hubs, updating common Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), common data sharing platform and support training tools for inter-agency referrals and meta-data analysis to guide data collection and utilization.
In enhancing CwC humanitarian responsiveness, C4D will support evidence generation through behaviour monitoring of real-time data on changing knowledge, attitude, perceptions and practices on COVID-19 preventions, vaccination trends and other related services including health, nutrition, education, and WASH. The information gathered will be used in differentiated programming, strategic alignment of key messages to the context, and assessing the effectiveness approaches in social and behaviour change interventions.
Within the framework of strengthening disaster risk management, C4D through CwC will promote disaster resilience amongst the Rohingya Refugees particularly the boys, girls, youths and adolescents in climate change resilience and adaptation activities to improve their awareness and engagement in protection and conservation of the ecosystem and environment.

1 - Identified Needs

The preliminary findings of Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (2021) provided evidence that RCCE significantly contributed in containing disease transmission particularly Covid-19 pandemic, AWD/Cholera, and enabled the high coverage of vaccination campaigns (Covid-19 and OCV - Oral Cholera Vaccination)) and enhanced reach to scale of efforts towards disaster preparedness through early warning in camps and host communities in 2021. The assessment shows that 97% in host, 100% in camps households reported having been able to access enough clear information on COVID-19. This is similar to the findings in 2020 JMSNA. Similarly, 97 per cent of households in camps and 83 per cent in host community reported having got enough clear information on cyclones, meaning families received actionable information for preparedness and response. The findings perfectly resonate with the RCCE strategy response in 2021 which mostly focused on raising awareness and promoting actionable messages to facilitate the adoption of preventive practices. The demand driven interventions underpinned by mixed communication approaches reached a wide range of population with Covid-19 messages, specifically the symptoms, and preventative actions for self and family protection and referral services if infected with COVID 19. However, as Covid-19 pandemic becomes protracted, risk perception is significantly declining, which largely necessitates the need to strengthen RCCE in 2022, to facilitate and sustain COVID-19 preventative behaviours and promote vaccinations towards halting unforeseen future outbreaks.In host community - Teknaf and Ukhia upazilas, large proportions of households reported not able to access sufficient clear information on the types of assistance available to them, face challenges providing feedback or complaints and do not feel consulted or heard by humanitarian actors. C4D will strengthen community engagement in host community to enhance community participation, ownership, and adaptation of essential family care practices. Emphasis will also be on promoting social cohesion and resilience building among the host community and Rohingya refugees, overarchingly to sustainably promote peaceful coexistence.
