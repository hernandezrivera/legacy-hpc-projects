Project Summary

Since 2014, Cameroon’s Far North region has been the scene of a brutal war in which civilians have done most of the suffering and dying. Boko Haram attacks and harsh wartime measures taken by local authorities have devastated the local economy, restricted basic services, and thrown both refugees and host communities into a climate of destitution and fear. Over 330,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) have been driven from their homes, and a total of 2.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance.
In the ongoing conflict, civilians remain vulnerable to insecurity and serious violations of their human rights. Women and girls are the group most exposed to violence, including gender-based violence (GBV), and to serious human rights violations. They continue to be at risk of rape, sexual and physical abuse, early and forced marriage, emotional abuse, and lack of resources and opportunities.There were 1,649 reported cases of GBV in the Far North in 2017, and it is likely that many more cases went unreported. Gender inequality severely limits opportunities for women and girls, as cultural practices favour men’s and boys’ economic and educational advancement. Services for GBV survivors are limited, and survivors often lack access to appropriate assistance.
Vulnerable individuals face a number of threats to their safety and dignity. Protection monitoring conducted by IRC in July 2018 showed that more than 20 villages in Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga departments, near the border with Nigeria, have many displaced persons and asylum seekers from Nigeria, but lack adequate access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity.
Affected populations in the Far North continue to bear the economic burden of this conflict. Most of the displaced lost their means of livelihood and almost all of their material possessions during their flight. Host communities have seen the local economy shattered by conflict and restrictive wartime measures. These factors have compounded the chronic poverty of the Far North. Three-quarters (74.3%) of Far Northerners live below the national poverty line, double the national average (37.5%). In the border areas most affected by the Boko Haram crisis, the poverty rate exceeds 80%. A baseline survey conducted by IRC in July 2018 in Mayo Sava shows that 75% of households surveyed reported resorting to negative coping strategies to meet their basic needs, potentially threatening their safety, health, and resilience to future shocks. For women, girls and vulnerable individuals, economic deprivation further exacerbates protection risks, potentially exposing them to sexual exploitation and other forms of harm
