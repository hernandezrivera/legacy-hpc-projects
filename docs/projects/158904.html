Project Summary

The project objective is to strengthen the psychosocial resilience affected children in the GMR and their families in the Gaza Strip. The project is in line with the protection cluster’s strategic objective that aims at providing protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. The project is composed of a series of structured responsive and remedial interventions that respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of affected Children in the GMR. The capacity building of services providers on child protection and psychosocial interventions will guarantee the quality of services provided to children, thus improving the well-being of children. The family and community members will be sensitized on child protection mechanisms and concerns; therefore, this will contribute to better understanding the needs and rights of affected children in the GMR. The project will contribute towards building a protective environment for children with and without disabilities that will prevent and respond to violence, abuse and exploitation through strengthening the capacity to fulfil children’s right to protection; addressing harmful attitudes, customs and practices; developing children’s life skills, knowledge and participation; raising awareness of families and communities; providing essential services for prevention, recovery and reintegration.
The project aims at improving the community engagement in the project through evolving the target group in the project cycle. The children disabilities and their families who are the main target groups are part of the planning, implementation, and evaluation processes of the project. The project undertakes address needs expressed by the target groups themselves throughout the focus groups discussions and the course of years of interaction with them, their families and communities.
To identify CWDs, the project will use the already established referral pathways, and it will mainly target affected children during the GMR and especially those who have acquired permanent disabilities due to their injury and orphaned children who have lost a parent in the GMR, and who are a target in the CPMHPSS WG, and provide them structured rehabilitation and psychosocial support
During the planning phase of the project ASDC has held coordination with PSS actors and CBOs who are members in the CPMHPSS WG and they were part from the needs assessment process, and agreed on the establishment of the PSS clinics distributed across the Gaza Strip

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip witnesses a dramatic process of de-development due to recurring cycles of violence, demographic pressures, and the tightened siege. Since March 2018, victims among the Palestinians have significantly increased during GMR. In addition to the losses and traumas of recent deaths and injuries, the growing frustration and despair among the people of Gaza have increased. In Gaza there is 900,000 children, who are exposed to high mental stress due to the ongoing conflict and its interaction with economic factors. According to a study by Save the Children (2018), 95% of children (boys and girls) are mentally ill and suffer from depression and/or are aggressive Due to the financial distress, medical treatments or mental and psychosocial services are often not affordable. more specific mental illnesses of children were identified in early 2018 in an assessment conducted by Terre des Hommes, this can be seen in changes in emotions or behavior eg withdrawal, violence, attachment to parents. CWDs are particularly affected as they are particularly vulnerable to violence and crises and service providers often do not recognize and treat their specific needs. Social exclusion, cultural stigmatization and poverty further complicate the living conditions of children with disabilities. According to needs assessment conducted by Atfaluna(2018-2019) in with CWDs (boys and girls) aged between 5-17 and their parents about 94% interviewed parents (20-40) (men and women) of CWDs reported that they suffer from poor livelihood conditions and they cannot provide their families basic needs, which causes the high stress on the breadwinners, where 64% families reported that there is an increase of conflict and tension between family members. Families reported that their CWDs formulated the feeling of deprivation, as their parents cannot provide them with needs. About 23.7% of the interviewed families stated that their children went to occasional theft, and 57.5% of families reported that the children tell lies. In focus groups with CWDs from both genders, they reported that the current escalations revived the terrifying memories of 2014 war, 80% of interviewed CWDs 44%boys, 55% girls suffer from stress, fears, anxiety. About 84% of interviewed CWDs (52% boys, 48% girls) reported that they became more dispersed with less concentration. Where 72% (48% boys, 52% girls) of CWDs reported that they are confused, 78% (48% boys, 52% girls) nervous easy to lose their temper, 87.5 % (53 % boys, 47% girls) of children expressed their need to cry, where 57 % of families reported that they noticed their children crying in some times without reason. About 82.6 % of interviewed parents reported that they become angry quickly which is negatively reflected on their children, and 90% of parents feeling distressed. A total of 3 out of 29 interviewed mothers cried during the focus group. Approx. 86% families reported that they become less attentive and caring to their children, where 66% (45% m, 55%f) reported decrease of academic achievement of their kids. 76 % of families reported their children become more aggressive. A total of 24 out of 299 of surveyed people reported that one of their family members has been injured during Great Marches of Return. The project responds to the needs of the target group through direct psychological interventions (promotion, prevention, treatment), supporting families of CWDs, strengthening and improving the capacities of CBOs to ensure long-term, institutional support for CWDs, and the individual psychological support of the service providers of the CBOs reduce their double burden and strengthen their capacity to provide help in emergency.In 2019 Atfaluna conducted needs assessment to identify the needs of with 1169 CWDs and children without disabilities (584 m , 585 f, from 5-17 years) according to the assessment analysis, children (boys and girls) suffer from low psychosocial resilience trauma symptoms, child protection

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Participatory assessments are conducted during the design phase of the project life cycle to identify locations for interventions, and to assess the beneficiary’s views, needs and expectations. Atfaluna strategic plan, projects and intervention are built on a sustainable approach to strengthen the interventions and make sure that women and girls with disabilities have access to services across the Gaza Strip. Regular consultations are conducted with the target group to ensure all programming responses are informed by their voices, experiences and recommendations. Atfaluna uses a number of different strategies in place that allow women to openly voice their opinions on specific issues, and make sure that these strategies are sensitive to local cultural norms and don’t inadvertently encourage a deepening of power imbalances. These strategies cannot exclude men and discourage their support. Atfaluan will periodically report the indicators to the CPWG/MHPSS and through a4 and FTS system

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The critical content analysis of the project as well as project planning and monitoring are based on the prin-ciples and methods of the Do No Harm approach. As part of a context analysis, a divider and connector analysis (DCA) was conducted. Likewise, a critical detail mapping was carried out during the project planning in order to evaluate the respective project activities and measures. A Action and Behavior Analysis (ABC) analyzed the resource distribution and the associated messages in a conflict context in order to strengthen positive effects and to reduce negative effects of the project.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Atfaluna adopts the principle of "Leave no One Behind" within all its interventions, which gives an equal access of all categories. All interventions are built on an inclusive approach where services are accessible to persons with and without disabilities (men, women, girls, boys).
