Project Summary

Helpcode's experience in protecting humanitarian response in Mozambique has led to the creation of a response and prevention program that can include GP, GBV response and CP in its strategy. This is because community protection needs were found to be multi-sector, complex, and impactful at the household level but also at the community level. For this reason, while we try to respond through case management to the support needs of the individual and families, we also try to have a community approach, aimed at building strong community protection networks and going through institutional accompaniment and strengthening the public response system.
The protection program is concentrated in five main districts where a coordinated response and more comprehensive response to individual cases can be achieved: Palma, Nangade, Mocimboa da Praia, Macomia, and Mueda (with a specific interest for Child protection in Muidumbe (prioritized as underserved and because of the severity rate). The choice of territories is based on the prioritization of the HNO study and the interest of notching a key component of the protection response, which is the reintegration of women and girls survivors of abduction experience by NSAG.
Helpcode's collaboration (at provincial and district level) and intervention efforts in protection will also always be aimed in parallel to strengthen PSEA prevention and information sharing, and for other part to ensure support for SEA survivors.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project is part of a protection program that connects GP, GBV and CP components in 5 prioritized districts: Mocimboa da Praia, Mueda, Nangade, Macomia and Palma. The prioritized GP activities for this intervention are: group and one-to-one MHPSS activities inside of safe spaces or multi-functional spaces with emphasis on prioritization of interest groups; utilizing the case management component of GBV and CP to also give support and referral to cases/families presenting GP cases and using the protection space as an entry point for access to services always prioritizing utilization and support in updating RPs at the Cabo Delgado level. CBP will be other key pillar of the intervention through specific activities to strengthen community protection structures and relevant stakeholders identified and a push of community spaces to be used for community reconciliation activities. Some cases, considered most vulnerable according to the organization's assessment, will also be accompanied in accessing services and benefiting from their rights through direct delivery of specific kits to meet needs (NFIs).

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

5 main activities’ blocks in 5 districts: MdP, Mueda, Nangade, Macomia, Palma. - MHPSS: group and one-to-one support with psychologists and CBP officers in Protection houses or multifunctional spaces. - Civil documentation: support targeted communities with birth certificate support in those districts where the institutional system is struggling to reach remote communities. - CBP activities: prioritizing strengthening community based protection group and safe networks; community reconciliations in displacement area and returning ones; capacity building for institutional and community relevant stakeholders with the objective of enabling a protection environment to support the community structure to create resilient protection networks and support groups/communities. - Case management: GBV and CP specialized case management to ensure services access with specialized MHPSS.- Awareness raising with GBV risk mitigation strategies: sharing information, referral pathways and reporting channel with the community regarding several protection issues (GBV,PSEA, Child rights, Human rights, SRH, etc). Women economic empowerment activities will also be connected with awareness raising and strengthen capacity about GBV and PSEA. - Protection monitoring

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Assumptions/risks identified: - Sub-national institutions involved in the action are not committed across its whole duration and do not support surveys and data collection realization. Access to population and permits to enter areas of interest are not released and so the data are not collected to start working on protection activities. Expected solution: MoUs are signed with the relevant District Services clearly explaining the project and their role. District technicians are involved in the interventions and given responsibilities and incentives. - Sub-national institutions are not able to provide protection services Referral system fail so the population is not properly assisted. Expected solution: All the activities are carried out in full collaboration and coordination, in order to enhance the District Services capacity to deliver and to prepare for future steps following the end of the intervention.- Attacks occur in the intervention area so access is not possible and activities cannot be implemented. Expected solution: A security plan is drafted and updated regularly by project core team, including based on the information received from District Authorities / local intelligence and cluster actors. Beneficiaries are reassessed in the case of attacks.- Population is not inclined to provide information and be involved as protection focal point so the community based protection system cannot be built and protection network fails. Expected solution: specific attention will be devoted to communicating properly with village/community leaders andkey community stakeholders with regards to the project specific objectives, and their collaboration will be requested to minimize risks of conflict linked to expectations.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Helpcode AAP’s strategy abides to IASC 5 commitments, according to the following: Leadership - Promote a culture among staff of acceptance of failure and negative feedback from affected people. - Ensure that relevant staff and managers collect feedback from affected populations in appropriate age groups (sensitive to gender & age); respond to this feedback; and report back. - Work with local and national partners, so that there is a coordinated plan and capacity which is sensitive to gender, age, disability and ethnicity. Transparency - Provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that may impact communities in order to support informed decisions, and engage communities in a dialogue as part of information provision Participation - Work with relevant local and national partners and actors to support and influence effective design, assessment, implementation and monitoring of the response. - Engage all parts of a community, including those who are vulnerable or at risk to draft robust, contextual vulnerability and capacity analyses. Inclusivity is ensured through a thorough analysis of local social dynamics, mobility capacity of beneficiaries, differences in the use of time by women and men, identification of “role models” among community stakeholders that can promote social change. Feedback and complaints - Provide information to all in affected communities about the organisation, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave (including commitments made on PSEA), the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Communicate in languages, formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the community, especially vulnerable and marginalised groups. - Establish and maintain effective feedback systems, using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. The feedback is disaggregated by age and sex at minimum. Design, Monitoring and evaluation - Assess the implementation of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and other collective standards including the IASC AAP commitments through assessments of organizational performance on accountability to measure and improve accountability to all persons of concern. - Include persons of concern as partners throughout the operation's management cycle and report the results of assessments and follow up actions to them. -

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The focus for Helpcode's project on inclusion of people with disabilities will be addressed on several fronts: coordination with local district organizations working in the field for a joint training effort during capacity building processes with institutions; awareness raising materials and key messages on inclusion shared with the community; effective use of RPs to ensure access to services of people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Among its internal procedure, Helpcode has developed an environmental policy aiming to avoid, minimize and mitigate the environmental impact of its activities in the field, in line with Sphere Humanitarian Standards and global environmental standard, guidelines and tools. In this respect, each project is assessed since design in terms of environmental impact. For the nature of the activities proposed, the project does not entails specific environmental concerns (as the project will work in spaces that already exist, therefore no construction activities are foreseen, nor distribution of materials). In any case, a quick environmental assessment will be carried out again at project kick off and at the end. It is also critical to highlight that the overall implementation context is affected by severe vulnerability to climate change and extreme climate events. In terms of mitigation / preparation, the project foresees to provide ad hoc information during awareness / training activities.
