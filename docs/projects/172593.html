Project Summary

The NERP program will provide approximately 7, 723 children under 5 with life-saving nutrition treatment and 40,457 Pregnant & Lactating Women (PLW) with optimal nutrition behaviour promotion and counselling through IYCFe activities, while improving the quality of care provided at health facilities for children and PLWs. A projected 7,723 children diagnosed with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) will receive life-saving treatment for acute malnutrition as guided by the National Guidelines for SAM and MAM treatment, with logistical, financial, coordination and technical support from Goal. The children will also receive a protection ration of Super Cereal Plus to ensure that the malnourished child’s RUTF is protected and a discharge ration of super cereal plus to prevent relapse. Approximately 40,457 PLWs will receive behaviour promotion and counselling on optimal maternal and child nutrition behaviour through group and individual nutrition sessions at the health facilities and in the communities through Care Groups/NIPP circles.
Each district will receive a unique set of interventions that correspond to their distinct needs, an approach that is cost effective and minimises duplication. A package of interventions will be implemented at the District, Health Facility and the Community Level. At the district level, coordination activities will be implemented in collaboration with Ministry of Health and Child Care and existing structures. At the health facility level, NERP will improve the technical capacity of health workers, provide inputs, and support regular and effective oversight linked to timely reporting. At the community level, NERP will promote optimal nutrition behaviours amongst PLW and households with SAM and MAM cases, extensive screening to ensure early case detection via screening campaigns at public gatherings, regular outreach visits to underserved and remote communities, and the introduction and expansion of the family MUAC.

1 - Activities

The NERP programme will support implementation at district, health facilities and community levels. Volunteers will be supported to screen and refer children under 5 for treatment of acute malnutrition by providing equipment, material, stationery, logistical support and training. Health workers at health facilities will be supported with protection and discharge rations, technical trainings, logistical and equipment so that they deliver quality treatment to referred children. The programme will also support the MIYCN and IYCFe activities in a similar manner, so as to complement the IMAM and contribute to reduction in relapse and new malnutrition cases in targeted districts. A crisis modifier will be deployed to provide non-food items (NFIs) to villages experiencing an outbreak of water-borne disease to further protect nutrition gains.At district level, the team will facilitate that a coordinated nutrition emergency response is implemented through support for cluster coordination meetings and District Food and Nutrition Security Committee (DFNSC) Meetings. Th district team will facilitate data quality monitoring at health facilities to improve performance of indicators and activity coverage.The program will be implemented in Harare, Masvingo and Manicaland provinces (Highfields, Chivi, Masvingo urban, Mutasa, Nyanga and Mutare Rural). According to the Nutrition Cluster HNO for 2021, these districts are in Severe to Extreme categories of need for nutrition interventions (2021 Nutrition Cluster Severity Map and Priority Districts List). The NERP program design is informed by the 2021 HNO, an analysis of available secondary data, a household assessment conducted by GOAL in December 2019, and a needs assessment conducted by GOAL in mid-January 2020. It is consistent with the Humanitarian Response Plan’s Nutrition Strategy as developed by the Cluster and incorporates suggestions, ideas, and feedback shared by provincial and district officials.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Goal is currently implementing the Nutrition Emergency Response for Early detection and Treatment (NERET) in Manicaland and Masvingo provinces, and have been engaging project beneficiaries through MoHCC health facility staff and and engaging with senior MoHCC staff at district, provincial and national level. This has allowed GOAL to receive feedback and recommendations from both programme beneficiaries, as well as project partners at different levels of implementation and coordination. This feedback and recommendations have resulted in refining and re-designing of the current NERET project to NERP, with improved effectiveness, coverage and programme delivery quality mechanisms being set up in the two provinces. However due to limited interface by GOAL with programme beneficiaries as direct implementation is being done by MoHCC and that there were limited handouts in the intervention the feedback was low if compared to a food aid or cash transfers intervention. Goal implements the Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) across all projects, including the current NERET intervention, whereby beneficiaries and community members can access communication channels, register their feedback to the programme and receive responses fromGoal programme staff, donor agent staff and the Goal M&E department. The CFM mechanism has been working well and will be replicated in the NERP project, with continuous improvement from lessons learnt across all projects and in the current nutrition project.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The NERP project will encourage and promote environmentally complaint and safe disposal of RUTF and protection ration Super Cereal Plus sachets by beneficiary caregivers and communities, with enforcement by community health volunteers participating in the programme.
