Project Summary

The project will contribute to the health cluster objective 1, related to strategic objective 2.
SO2: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in Palestine
UNICEF, WFP and Save the Children will aim to reach the following 76,000 people through the different targeted interventions in West Bank and Gaza Strip as listed:
- 36,000 pregnant and lactating women with micronutrients, including 1,400 will benefit from counselling sessions, community awareness, and restricted cash-based transfer (e-vouchers)
- 40,000 children under 5 including 1,000 children with acute malnutrition (moderate and severe) (50% female)
- 35 Non-Breast Fed infants under 6 months
- 200 men
- 70 health and community workers (70% females).
UNICEF, WFP, and SCI have inclusive policies; accordingly, the targets above are inclusive of children, women, and men with disabilities. The three agencies will consider the needs of boys, girls, women, and men with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups. Through additional agency funding sources, the needs of persons with disabilities to access services are being addressed. Agencies are consulting people with disabilities through focus group discussions to define responses, including services location, design, and time.

1 - Needs

The Health Cluster has identified a total of 1.5 million Palestinians, two-thirds of whom are in the Gaza Strip, as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2022. Over a year of responding to the COVID-19 pandemic has had a significant impact on the delivery of other essential healthcare services, which have been intermittently disrupted whenever there have been surges in COVID-19 cases. According to the Ministry of Health (MoH) 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women and 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates in need of sexual and reproductive healthcare, gender-based violence, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services. About 110,000 people with non-communicable diseases and some in need of palliative care remain with minimal available support, especially those with cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. The July 2021 MSNA found that 47% of surveyed households in Gaza and 42% in the WB have at least one member with chronic disease.The May escalation in Gaza, East Jerusalem and West Bank combined with the effects of the COVID-19 highlighted the continuous need to provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). According to a 2021 study3 involving 1,800 adolescents (aged 13 to19 years) conducted by WHO and a local NGO, Juzoor, on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the mental health of this group, there are significantly elevated rates of psychological distress compared to before the pandemic and lockdown. The Multisectoral Need Assessment (MSNA) results showed that 20-38 per cent of surveyed households have at least one member showing signs of psychological distress in the 30 days before data collection in July 2021. The UN Women Gender Alert (2021) highlighted that women are disproportionately more affected by the mental health situation.Across the State of Palestine (SoP), health needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by stock out of medical supplies, insufficient human resources, and imposed access restrictions, including for patients referred outside of the Gaza Strip and the West Bank. Meanwhile, the constant surge of conflict-related casualties has created a double burden of disease. The current situation in Gaza has further deteriorated the nutritional status of the most vulnerable groups, including children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women. In 2018, 18% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers who live in access-restricted areas (ARA) were undernourished (Multisectoral Nutrition Assessment, 2019).The 2019 assessment conducted by UNICEF, World Food Programme (WFP) and Save the Children (SC) outlined a deterioration in the nutritional status of the most vulnerable children under 5, pregnant and lactating women in Gaza. 43% of infants are exclusively breastfed, and just 45.7% of infants are still breastfed at one year of age. 18.5% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers in the most deprived communities in Gaza are malnourished, with around 36% of PLW anaemic; more than 30% of children 6-59 months are anaemic, and around 22% of adolescent girls (10-19 years) are also classified as anaemic. 8.7% of children under five years in Palestine suffer from moderate and severe stunting (compared to 7.4% in 2014). Seven out of ten children under the age of five years suffer from at least one kind of micronutrient deficiency. There is a pocket of 36,400 children under five living in Access Restricted Areas who are particularly vulnerable and prone to incidences of watery or bloody diarrheal disease and respiratory infections, exacerbated by the deteriorating access to safe drinking water and health services. The combined effect of the poor maternal nutrition status, sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices, child morbidity, and inadequate water and sanitation may lead to an increased risk of acute malnutrition, impaired physical growth and cognitive development. The World Bank expects that poverty levels will further deteriorate, reaching around 64% post-COVID-19 pandemic. Unemployment in the Gaza strip is three times higher than that in the WB. It is expected to increase drastically due to the COVID-19 effects. More than 120,000 Palestinian lost their jobs in 2020. An additional 81,000 HHs need assistance with food distribution due to COVID 19. Accordingly, this joint project aims to address the critical gap in health and nutrition services in the West Bank (Hebron H2, Area C and East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip. It will address life-threatening micronutrient malnutrition among the most vulnerable women of reproductive age and U5 children, including moderate and severe wasting through the provision of appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling, food and non-food items, including essential micronutrients and treatment jointly delivered by UNICEF, WFP and Save the Children. UNICEF with Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics jointly conducted the MICS6 in 2019-2020, and from the gathered data, disaggregated information on boys, girls, with disabilities was collected, and this information will be the baseline data for this project interventions to ensure the needs of children with disabilities are addressed.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will continue supporting the MoH to coordinate the Nutrition technical working group in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. Jointly with partners, UNICEF will ensure the implementation of the updated National Maternal, Neonatal and Child Health (MNCH) Nutrition Protocol.Through this joint Humanitarian project response, UNICEF will utilize its international procurement mechanism and channels to procure the annual requirement of essential micronutrients for at least 36,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) (Gaza: 24,000; WB:12,000) and for 40,000 children aged 6 to 59 months (girls: 19,600; boys: 20,400) (Gaza: 30,000; WB: 10,000) who are attending the Ministry of Health (MoH) and NGO facilities. All micronutrients will be predefined jointly with relevant authorities to ensure specifications meet the updated national nutrition maternal child and adolescent protocol. SCI, through its technical experts, will train 70 health workers (50 females and 20 males) from INGOs and NGOs in the 5 Gaza governorate and West Bank on: 1) Detection, management and treatment of acute malnutrition, micronutrient deficiencies, anaemia and common childhood illnesses2) Provision of quality IYCF counselling 3) Management of NBF infants under six months through the provision of quality counselling of IYCF practices and practical re-lactation4) Maternal reproductive and sexual health, including antenatal SCI, in partnership with local NGOs, will provide supplies (safer BMS kit and generically labelled infant formula) for the safe and appropriate management of 35 non-breastfed infants <6 months living in the most vulnerable families (boys:17, girls:18). This will be done through strict criteria of selection of NBF according to the WHO protocol and Palestinian MOH protocol. SCI and its partner will screen 3,000 under five years children to detect malnutrition, anaemia and common childhood illnesses and admit 1,000. SCI will also support the provision of individual counselling on maternal nutrition and IYCF counselling to 1,000 PLW of children under two years of age (600 PL women and mothers in Gaza and 400 PL women and mothers in the West Bank, including 50 disabled PLW women) that are receiving the WFP voucher support /cash support. Through community awareness interventions, SCI and its partner will reach 400 women of reproductive age, including 100 adolescent females on maternal reproductive and sexual health and nutrition. Adolescent females will have hygiene and dignity kits distributed to them as part of self-empowerment and improving their hygiene status. It will also support the creation of 40 community-based support groups with lead mothers to PLW of children under the age of 2 years of age that are receiving the WFP food voucher/cash support. SCI will directly procure needed anthropometric equipment for the clinics of our local partner in order to distribute the best quality of screening, management, treatment, and follow up for the malnourished and anaemic children with minor WASH rehabilitation for the clinics in order to sustain better hygiene. WFP will provide U$ 20 restricted cash transfers (e-voucher) on a monthly basis for most vulnerable pregnant, lactating and mothers of children under two years old) with high anaemia and micronutrient deficiencies. WFP will target the same 1,000 (600 PL women and mothers in Gaza and 400 PL women and mothers in the West Bank) who are targeted by SCI with IYCF counselling sessions. The voucher value will be restricted to iron-rich foods. WFP will coordinate with relevant authorities on selected hardship cases for the e-voucher and identify the targeted groups for IYCF counselling. UNICEF, WFP and SC will leverage resources through their networks to ensure the referral of suspected victims of GBV to relevant service providers and coordinate with UNFPA to ensure local partners receive capacity building on detection and referral of women subject to violence. The partners will contribute to the improved nutritional status of children under the age of five through building the capacity of service providers' staff, provision of case management and treatment services to children, and supporting caregivers to have improved knowledge to provide adequate knowledge IYCF to their children. All capacity building and counselling activities will ensure building the capacity of health professionals and caregivers to enhance their skills to address severe and moderate malnutrition, which includes IYCF management in emergencies. The agencies will target malnourished children, focusing on children with developmental delays and disabilities, as early detection of malnutrition is essential for early intervention to reverse potential complications of diseases. The agencies through their partnerships with specialized Community Based Organizations ensure to target and address the needs of persons with disabilities identified in the intervention design phase. The agencies conduct focus group discussions to design interventions, and people with disabilities are among the vulnerable groups that will be consulted.

3 - Indicators

In line with the intervention logic and strategic objective, a set of indicators will be assigned to measure the level of success in achieving intended changes on target children and women. UNICEF, WFP and SCI will be contributing to improved nutritional status of children under the age of 2 years including children with developmental delays and disabilities. Through, building the capacity of staff at service providers, provision of case management and treatment services to children, in addition to supporting caregivers to have improved knowledge to provide adequate IYCF to their children. All 3 agencies will be reporting against the following cluster indicators. # of < 5 children receiving targeted health and nutrition interventions and management of acute malnutrition (Target 40,000 including 1,000 with acute malnutrition and childhood illnesses of which 50% are females) # of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women including disabled women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services (36,000 PLW, including 50 disabled women) # of healthcare workers trained on the prevention and management of malnutrition and improved quality IYCF practices (Target 70 health, community and protection workers of which 70% are females) # of people directly benefitting from awareness sessions and health education on IYCF practices, and maternal reproductive health (Target 2,635 of which 90% are females) UNICEF, WFP and SCI will be developing different pre and post surveys with targeted beneficiaries mainly the health and community workers. In addition to a KAP survey by end of the project, to measure actual changes on caregivers’ knowledge and practices will be conducted to identify the percentage of targeted pregnant women, mothers and caregivers (men, women) including persons with disabilities who demonstrate improved knowledge and adoption of recommended maternal and IYCF practices. For the e-voucher intervention, WFP has a process monitoring that is conducted at all contracted retail shops on a regular basis for a representative sample. WFP conducts post-distribution household monitoring (PDM) visits with a view to monitor the improvement or deterioration in the food security status of assisted people and assess the effectiveness of its interventions.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability mechanism and feedback channels will be established and disseminated at the start of the project period, those include the toll-free hotline, compliant boxes and community sessions. SCI, WFP and UNICEF pay special attention to ensure that beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any concerns and make use of their preferred communication channel. The instalment of a robust accountability system will be used by children and communities to raise their voices and provide their feedback, which will be reflected in the activities, and contribute to the fulfilment of their rights. When receiving feedback, the relevant monitoring teams will categorize inputs and act according to the agencies rules and procedures on complaints and accountability toward affected people

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

All 3 agencies possess different mechanisms to reach out to their direct beneficiaries. All 3 agencies will work with implementing partners to inform direct beneficiaries through the relevant field visits, monitoring and focus groups activities. UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. Awareness and communication actions have started to progressively publicize the PSEA system and the right of affected populations to the complaint.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

All 3 agencies protect and uphold the rights of their beneficiaries, to receive safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries and their guardians in case of children is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies, joining training and community interventions. All 3 agencies will ensure applying international standards, and WHO standards for micronutrient supplements and other supplies. All 3 agencies have developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end-users.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All 3 agencies conduct independence need assessment prior distribution of supplies or materials. This is verified through different cluster members to ensure targeting the most vulnerable. The 3 agencies agreed to target common communities in both Gaza and West Bank that have been identified as the most vulnerable by the cluster and where all 3 agencies have been operating previously and have conducted several assessments last year and earlier this year. The relevant assessment will ensure an equal and impartial interventions are designed.
