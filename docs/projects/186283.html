Project Summary

Project Summary
LABDI will implement an 8-month protection intervention focusing on GBV prevention and response to improve access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated protection of children, adolescents and caregivers at risk of GBV, through comprehensive case management and enhanced psychosocial support mechanisms in Mubi North and Mubi South LGAs in Adamawa States. At project start-up, LABDI will prioritize access to life-saving services for survivors of GBV including those vulnerable and at risk of GBV, and will seek to address the risks of Gender-Based Violence including sexual violence and Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SHEA) faced by women and girls including men and boys. We will strengthen capacity of case workers including community capacity to prevent and respond to incidents of GBV and will work closely with communities and government to establish safe and confidential spaces taking into regard WHO COVID-19 guidelines where survivors as well as individuals at risk of GBV can access safety and psychosocial services. Strengthening referral pathways, clinical management of survivors including remote case management on child helplines and coordination. Returnees and host community members including community-based protection mechanisms and local leaders will be engaged to identify and provide appropriate referrals to psychosocial services. LABDI will hire and train a team of Case Workers with gender consideration (male and female) who will be deployed at the community level to provide case management and psychosocial services for survivors of GBV and to support individuals at risk of GBV especially women and girls. The approach to recruit and build the capacity of Case Workers from the Local Government Area (LGA) will ensure that project beneficiaries have access to Case Workers as well as sustainability of support by fostering ownership and involvement of community-based structures in all relevant processes and discussions. LABDI will integrate inclusive service delivery within this project using our mobile protection approach to reach out to hard to reach communities and mobile population.

1 - Needs

According to 2nd Multi-Risk Needs Assessment conducted by Inter Sector Working Group 2021, the protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria progressed in its eleventh year, and despite a sustained number of humanitarian actors responding to the crisis, humanitarian needs in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States remained dire and multi-faceted in 2019. The conflict has resulted in an estimated 7.9 million individuals in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 – more than 50% of the entire estimated population of the three affected States. Women and children constitute about 81% of the affected population while 87% comprised of new displacements. Moreover, over 80% of internally displaced persons (IDPs) were located in Borno State only, the epicenter of the protracted crisis, with a majority living in urban host communities, making it difficult for actors to reach them and to plan responses appropriate to urban contexts. In addition to this humanitarian landscape in accessible areas, for 2020 the humanitarian community has identified approximately 1.2 million individuals staying in hard-to-reach areas with limited to no access to humanitarian assistance according to the humanitarian needs overview report published at the fall of 2019. Coupled with this, the risk of SHEA and other forms of GBV continue to be a reality and remains one of the major consequences of the conflict. IDPs, women, single female headed households, adolescents, girls with disabilities, girl hawkers and those who are experiencing food insecurity are among the most vulnerable to experience GBV and are more likely to engage in transactional/survival sex, a form of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Survivors are also exposed to abuse and continue to experience stigma and shame. The conflict and its consequences is maintaining vicious cycle of GBV that are even made worse with some social norms that legitimate violence against women, lack of laws and policies to protect women and children from violence, weak or ineffective protection and response systems and with the current COVID-19 pandemic. According to a recent assessment by the GBV Sub Sector in May 2020 on child help line management, the threat of the spread of COVID-19 to communities affected by conflict in North East Nigeria is high and heightens the already existing vulnerabilities for gender-based violence. In different parts of Nigeria including in the north east, activists and service providers are already recording increases in GBV incidents reported to them since the COVID-19 outbreak. Yet reporting and seeking services remain challenging due to the lack of available, safe, ethical and quality response services as well as fears of stigmatization, reprisal, and lack of access to appropriate information on seeking help in the context of the pandemic. It is estimated that 2.4 million people especially women and girls are at risk of GBV including SEA and the GBV sub sector is targeting 0.96 million people at high risk of GBV and SEA in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe The economic effect on both men and women especially turning upside down the traditional structure nets has been eluded to by International Alert in its assessment of conflict sensitivity on project implementation .According to Alert, the number of women becoming family heads increased especially in the IDP camps with the implication of their children including girls and adolescents becoming more vulnerable to risks of exploitation including forced marriage, recruitment and child labor including professional counseling and psycho-education on self-protection as well as report cases of abuse and violence for appropriate services. culturally appropriate safety and dignity kits. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

LABDI shall consider differentiated needs women, men, girls and boys may have and give attention to persons with special needs, such as elderly, disabled or chronically ill people. Disaggregated data is key. LABDI shall work with GBV sector to put in place a standardized dignity kit in close collaboration with the Protection sector during pre/post distribution exercise. Protection considerations, notably the principles of do no harm and non-discrimination will be adhered to during assessments, programme design, while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluating the project. Thus LABDI will prioritize (1) Safety & security, including land tenure security, and dignity; (2) Meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs; (3) Accountability, for example through community-based feedback mechanism; and (4) Information, consultation, participation and community empowerment.
