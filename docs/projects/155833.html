Project Summary

The project is designed to respond to the needs of IDPs (from Eastern Ghouta, northern Homs, south Syria, Damascus and other areas) and hosted people, The prioritization of the targeted beneficiaries would be based on cluster coordinated vulnerability and selection criteria, to the protracted needs of the existing IDPs and the hosting community by providing a reliable sources of drinking water and sanitation service and men, women, boys, and girls will benefit from the activities which aim to increase access to sustainable water, sanitation, and environmental hygiene solutions. Sustainability will be aimed at through the re-establishment of a cost recovery system. This project aims to cover the WASH services need of 615090 persons (246036 IDPs, 369054hosted people) at Idleb and Aleppo governorate.
The planned quantity of water that will be provided to each person in the targeted communities that will benefit of water sector intervention is about 45-50 liter/day.
During the project SECD will perform the following activities:
-Rehabilitation of the four water stations in the targeted communities.
- The rehabilitation sewer system in the targeted communities.
-Establish a cost recovery system
- 10000 family Hygiene kits will distribute to the most valuable people in the targeted communities.
- Garbage bins 0.1m3 size for will be Provided and install.
-Fuel and chlorine will be supported by water stations.
-Solid waste management (collection and disposal) will be supported.
-Capacity building of the water unit in the civil council so as to ensure that they have adequate operations and maintenance teams. These teams will be responsible for ensuring sustainable water services by activating a tariff system.
-Rehabilitation of the sewer system and wastewater treatment will be will be supported and rehabilitated.

Needs

Based on WASH assessments there are an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need (displaced people in camps, informal settlements, and host communities). Prior to the crisis, nearly 100 percent of the population in Syria was served by well-developed, state-owned, centrally-managed water systems. Results of 2018 household assessment indicate a slight increase in water share received from the water distribution networks. Piped water systems, though dysfunctional in some areas, continue to operate at varying levels of efficiency, with urban centers generally better served. Limitations in regular operations, maintenance and repair have had a significant impact on the system's efficiency, leading to a higher risk of contamination. The lack of a regular power supply still constitutes a primary bottleneck for the WASH sector and explains the sub-standard performance of the water distribution systems in many areas, in particular NSAG-controlled. In northeast Syria. Garbage collection services are primarily provided by municipalities/local authorities in most parts of the country and usually provide a basic level of service. However, due to the lack of comprehensive disposal strategies and operational challenges, additional efforts are needed in specific rural and urban communities to strengthen and upgrade the quality and regularity of solid waste management. Idleb Governorate, as well as surrounding NSAG controlled-areas, have become extremely crowded amidst the significant displacement to and within those areas. Increased pressure on already overstretched WASH services has resulted in increased dependency on alternative and often unsafe water sources that significantly increase the risk of waterborne diseases. In addition, the continuous drilling of wells in search for water has negatively affected groundwater resources, with a significant depletion of water aquifers observed especially around camps near the Turkish border. The extremely high levels of displacement combined with the increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites has presented significant challenges to WASH sector partners, in view of the increased cost and complexity to deliver in IDP site settings than in host communities. Cross-border and cross-line access have a particular importance for WASH sector, especially for people with acute WASH needs as this type of programming constitutes bulk of WASH emergency response.

Protection Risk Analysis

Activity 1: Emergency water supplyProtection Risk/Threat: Movements of water trucks in congested, camp-like settings Likelihood: High.Impact: lowMitigating Measure : Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that uses wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators. Activity 2: Solid Waste Management (SWM)Protection Risk/Threat: Movements of garbage trucks in congested, camp-like settings; Reliability of services.Likelihood: HighImpact: low.Mitigating Measure: Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes, and schedule; Provide household level solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease disposal of waste from H/H to the public SW collection points. Establish clear routes and schedule for garbage trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school. Activity 3: Support to water supply systems.Protection Risk/Threat: Unequal distribution of servicesinadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organise joint planning culture and agree on the minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care. Activity 4: Support to wastewater systemsProtection Risk/Threat: Potential exposure to the explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructure (WWTP, sewer networks).Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with staff and contractors; Add explosive hazard awareness material to your programming.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Security situation allows access to the targeted communities.- The project is accepted by the communities and key stakeholders- Local markets are able to provide needed construction suppliesRisks and mitigation :- SECD has a clear policy for risk management, all negatively identified risk has mitigated, transformed and avoid. The following table shows SECD's risk management plan for the project:Risk 1: Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations.Likelihood of Risk Occurring: low.Effect on Expected Results: Very HighRisk Management Strategy: Experience from 2013 operations found that while conflict occurred, access could be restored within a few days. A cautious approach that plans for these type of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Risk 2: Armed groups ban specific types of activities such as information-sharing.Likelihood of Risk Occurring: LowEffect on Expected Results: Very HighRisk Management Strategy: In 2014, ISIS banned NGOS from activities related to education, information messages, and any activities with children. As such, the proposed project locations are in non-ISIS controlled areas. Thus far, the armed groups in the project target locations have not expressed an issue with these type of interventions; SECD will continue to, liaise with these groups’ representatives through the NGO Forum and local partners. The project design considers this limit to be a possibility and is as such designed with built-in alternatives to direct engagement or messaging so that project results may still be achieved. Risk 3: Airplane shellingLikelihood of Risk Occurring: mediumEffect on Expected Results: Very HighRisk Management Strategy: The risk of Security situation (only heavy shelling) and the risk of the local council buildings being targeted by airstrikes are a very low probability according to the historical information for 1 years ago, also SECD will be linked with the de -conflict mechanism which provided by OCHA.
