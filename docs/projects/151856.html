Project Summary

The project aims to enhance IOM’s ongoing efforts to support the humanitarian community by providing a common operating picture concerning the movement of populations in Libya, allowing humanitarian actors to provide timely assistance to those in need. This includes monitoring migratory trends, tracking migration dynamics, capturing data on migrant demographics, profiles and vulnerabilities, monitoring displacements, profiling detention centres , and building capacities of national authorities on information collection and database management. The data and results produced by DTM help to implement evidence-based humanitarian assistance, advocacy and identify locations in need for further in-depth sectoral assessments.

Needs

IOM’s DTM programme has been used as one of the primary sources of information for providing targeted assistance by humanitarian organizations in incidences of sudden-displacement in Libya, such as the August-September 2018 crisis in Tripoli. Furthermore, DTM has been one of the primary sources for the past two years of Libya’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). DTM coordinates its activities closely with the Ministry of Displacement and is also used by national and local entities. DTM is used by all Sectors in planning interventions, proposal writing, and advocacy. It is a recognized information management system used by the Inter-sector Working Group and Information Management and Assessment Working Group (IMWAG). The DTM has also fed into IOM’s broader regional efforts and captured the number of people crossing the Central Mediterranean route towards Europe, and quantified deaths during the transiting period. DTM’s regular migrant report also provides an overview of migrant numbers, dynamics and conditions across the country. It acts as a reliable guiding system to measure the impact of the Libya crisis and contributes towards delivering sustainable solutions. Many agencies responding to the Libya crisis face challenges in data collection, management, and dissemination. Accurate information allows responders to identify needs and gaps and inform strategic planning in the continuously evolving situation of Libya, where a prompt operational response is often difficult. This project aims to enhance IOM’s ongoing efforts to strengthen the coordination of humanitarian assistance by providing evidence-based information on displacement and migration patterns through DTM activities.

Activities

1. DTM’s Mobility Tracking activities continues to provide regular evidence-based reports on number and location of mobile populations. 2. Flow Monitoring delivers regular updates on Libya’s migration dynamics including information on countries of origin, migrant routes, intentions, and vulnerabilities. 3. Detention Centre Profiles are produced to capture the number and demographic breakdown of populations and conditions. 4. Build capacity of national authorities and local partners in information management and delivering up-to-date information on displacements.

Indicate any study/assessment that has been used, if any

Data collection in humanitarian operations is often undertaken with a technical focus on sectors or specific needs. However, this process can often de politicize the underlying problem at hand and convert it into a technical problem that masks the power relations and patterns of inequality and exploitation. IOM will apply a protection lens to the overall process allowing a clearer identification of underlying causes through effective data collection. Attainment of informed consent from the surveyed population is a basic ethical requirement. Accordingly, data collectors will explain who they are, the agency they represent and its mandate, the purpose of collecting data and how it will be collected, stored and used, and that participation in the process is voluntary and in confidence.. IOM will strive to employ women enumerators to achieve gender balanced teams. Where safety or protection concerns are raised during the data collection, IOM will encourage enumerators to guide beneficiaries to related service providers so they can undertake further assessments.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

DTM’s data collection includes availability and accessibility of services at community-level (e.g. access to health care, availability of electricity, WASH facilities, etc.) for returnees, IDPs and migrants, as well as security risks (freedom of movement, UXOs, etc.). Access to services is further disaggregated for migrants to capture their (in)ability to access existing services in many municipalities.
