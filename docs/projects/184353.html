Project Summary

Somalia, with an estimated population of15.8 million people, is suffering one of the world’s longest running humanitarian crises, lasting for over 3 decades. The combination of conflict, insecurity, mass displacement, recurrent prolong droughts, flooding, COVID-19, Locust and extreme poverty, coupled with very low basic social services coverage, has seriously affected food security and livelihoods and greatly increased the population’s vulnerability to disease and malnutrition.
The ongoing drought led to severe food and water shortages, massive population displacement, and an alarming rate of acute malnutrition and mortality among children from common preventable causes such as pneumonia, diarrhoea and malaria.
With one of the highest maternal and child mortality rates in the world, Somalia is one of the worst places to be born in with a 76.6 & 121.5 infant & child mortality per 1000 respectively-, according to “Statista-2020”. Morbidity reports from previous project of SFH in Lower Juba indicates respiratory tract infections, Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) and Malaria account for over 65% of the cases treated at outpatient clinics. Similarly, sporadic upsurge of measles and COVID-19 has continuously been reported in Kismayo and other districts in Lower juba. Due to overcrowding, poor sanitary conditions and low immunization coverage, the risk to increasing morbidity and mortality related to these diseases remains high. Children are of particular concern as they are particularly vulnerable to the high levels of malnutrition and disease. Though maternal mortality rates have dropped from 732 in 2015 to 692 maternal death per 100,000 live birth in 2020, maternal mortality remains high in Somalia and at least 1 in every 1000 women aged 15-49 dies due to pregnancy or birth related complications (SHDS-2020). The SHDS further indicates, at least 5% of women would die from pregnancy related complications during their reproductive lifetime. This is attributed to post-partum haemorrhage, pre-eclampsia, obstructed labor, low uptake of antenatal care, postnatal care and low level of delivery at health facilities and no support from skilled health care providers. Additionally, available reports indicate, 78% of birth are delivered at home, only 24% of women had at least 4 ANC visits and ONLY 32% of birth are delivered with assistance of skilled health professional. Neonatal mortality rate stands at 38% for both sexes.
In the proposed project area of Badhadhe and Kismayo district, there is a high risk of measles outbreaks, acute watery diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera outbreaks and COVID-19 risk due to limited access to primary health care as well as basic sanitation and hygiene services. According to SHDS ONLY 11% of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated in the country (received all the basic Vaccinations-One dose BCG, one dose measles, 3 doses of pentavalent and polio vaccines) and 89% did not receive full vaccination. While DPT3 immunization coverage stands 52% in Somalia, only 48% of children were vaccinated against Measles. Immunization coverage in Badhadhe district stand at 54% as of now. SFH will use its previous area experience and relations with local communities and authorities to establish and roll out equitable and accessible life-saving curative and preventive health services in Lower Juba. The propose action will particularly focus predominant health related morbidities as, poor reproductive health services, low vitamin A & immunization coverage, AWD/Cholera, Malaria and COVID-19 risk mitigation measures. Due to the high incidence of GBV &MH related cases in the drought displaced population and hard to reach rural areas, SFH will mitigate the protection concerns including Clinical management of rape, strong referral pathway, survivor assistance and linkage with available services will be strengthened. 60,500 beneficiary (Men, women, boys and Girls) including marginalized and persons with disability will be reached.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed action aimed to provide 60,500 consultations (31,474 women and girls and 29,026 men & boys) including 9,206 maternal consultations and 1,705 new-born consultations (886 girls). 4000 Pregnant women will be focused as ante-natal care (ANC), 1,300 assisted by skilled birth deliveries, 1,300 women receiving PNC within 48hrs of child birth, 4,500 PLW’s receiving Iron supplement and 2,100 PLW’s tested for HIV, Syphilis. Further, at least 2,400 (1,446 girls and 954 boys) of 0-12 months old infants will be targeted for routine immunization including measles, BCG, penta 1 and penta 3 at the facility and outreach services. At least 13000 U5’s (7723 girls, 5277 boys) will be treated for common child hood illnesses like respiratory infections, diarrhoea and malaria at facility and community level. Additionally, the same number of <5 (boys & girls) will be targeted for De-worming and Vitamin A supplementation both at the fixed health facilities and outreach.The proposed intervention will be carried out in 4 fixed health facility and 4 outreach teams in Badhadhe and Kismayo districts serving over 150,000 populaces indirectly through health education, hygiene awareness and Social mobilizations while directly reaching 60,500 beneficiaries (Men, women, boys & girls) in the 2 districts. 60,500 persons will also have direct to access COVID 19 risk mitigation measures both in our outreach services and fixed facilities including awareness raising and provision of PPE’s, set up hand washing points, whilst enhancing surveillance systems to boost reporting of notifiable diseases. SFH will Conduct Training of 36 gender balanced technical staff (20 Women and 16 men) 6 Qualified nurses, 6 Auxiliary nurses, 6 vaccinators, 8 health educators, 6 pharmacists and 2 clinical officer to undertake quality health care services and 2 doctors on the management of childhood illnesses (IMCI) and prevention. The training will take a period of 5 days in 2 separate arrangement of 20 person per shift to avoid large congregation that precipitate the spread of COVID-19.In Summary, the following activities will be prioritized and undertaken:(1) Provide outpatient consultations, diagnosis and treatment of common illnesses through 4 static and 4 outreach teams (2) Sensitize women to make 4 antenatal care visits during pregnancy and during these visits provide: at least 2 doses of tetanus toxoid vaccination; insecticide treated bed nets (ITN); screening and treatment of common pregnancy related disorders including anemia, pre-eclampsia, hemorrhage among others.(3) Provide skilled birth attendance and basic emergency obstetric care services at the 4 fixed and 4 mobile facilities(4) Develop and implement a patient referral mechanism between the primary health care facilities and Secondary level care facilities. (5) Implement routine immunization services and participate in EPI scale up campaigns in collaboration with UNICEF and WHO.(6) Conduct refresher training for healthcare workers on standard case management protocols, Reproductive Health, EMONC and EPI. Provide ongoing on-job-training and mentorship of clinical staff to enhance adherence to treatment guidelines and quality of care.(7) Support communicable diseases surveillance and timely reporting to the cluster/MOH/WHO Early Warning and Response Network (EW&RN). (8) Implement timely investigation and appropriate control measures in case of disease upsurge and outbreaks(9) Design and disseminate health and hygiene promotion messages to encourage the affected population to adopt health promoting practices and seek early treatment for illnesses, especially in children. Utilizing appropriate local communication channels, promote appropriate newborn, infant and young child care practices including early and exclusive breastfeeding; hygiene, warmth, immunization and early recognition of illness and danger signs.(10) Provide LLITNs and educate pregnant women on correct and consistent use

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

RISK: 4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance Mitigation Measure 4.1-SFH considers exclusion based on ethnicity, race, disability, gender or social status as strong protection concern and will be mitigated through the following approach: a) In the mobilization phase of the program, SFH will inform the targeted stakeholders, community that persons affected with disability and those marginalized are by default the prioritized groups. b) SFH will undertake direct implementation of the program without any intermediary or third party contractor to ensure inclusive service delivery with the support of the district health committees and AAP committees representing persons with disabilities, marginalized groups, men and women. c) Through the mobile or the outreach teams SFH will ensure person with mobility impairment are reached at their place of residency. d) SFH will facilitate installation of appropriate hand bars and disability friendly/movable seat at the health facility latrines. e) To accommodate persons with impaired hearing, vision or suffering mental health, SFH will provide information about the availability of services through both verbal or written forms. f) Using Washington group disability questions, SFH will collect disability data as well gender, age and diversity sensitive data to ensure all age groups, minority, persons living with disability can access inclusive and quality health care services. g) Where applicable SFH will coordinate with other actors in Lower Juba such as Humanity & Inclusion (Handicap International) who are specialized in working with persons with impaired mobility and disability to provide data of such individual to improve service delivery. 4.2 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.Mitigation Measure 4.2: a) SFH owes allegiance to the beneficiaries whom they serve, and as such, they are accountable to them and consider their feedback as crucial in improving services rendered to them. Project beneficiaries just like all other stakeholders in this project will be consulted throughout the implementation. Recommendations and lessons learnt from previous project on feedback and complaints on accountability to beneficiary, helped to feed into this project. This project directly benefits disaster and conflict affected women, men and children (boys and girls) from the IDP population and host communities. Women and children are particularly included as target beneficiaries. b) a) SFH has strict policy on Prevention of Sexual exploitation and abuse that guides our humanitarian programming and assistance. In this policy, every new staff upon recruitment is required to sign PSEA policy, organizational code of conducts and Child protection statement to ensure strict adherence/compliance and its violation will lead to disciplinary actions including termination of employment contract. b) No 1 to 1 engagement will children and both fixed and mobile staff are gender dis-aggregated and women with special cases attended by female staff. c) Waiting areas will be separate for male and female at the health centers. d) Separate examination rooms with female nurse or doctors will be established. e) Consent will be sought before any operation or examination is included by any male doctor/Nurse in case a female doctor is not around.
