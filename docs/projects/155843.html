Project Summary

Bonyan designed its 2019 SNFI program; to meet the Shelter and NFI needs of total targeted 38,370 individuals (18,054 male/ 20,316 Female – 10% are Young children (<5) – 40% are Children (5-17) – 48% are Adults (18-59) – 2% are Elderly (>59)) including provision of 150 short-term livelihood opportunities; and in line with the strategic Objectives of both the HRP and SNFI Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance to assessment findings and key indicators.
the intervention of the program will be in case of urgent necessity situations such as poor shelter-conditions, new arrival waves, natural hazards or sudden front-line shifting especially in hard-to-reach areas, last resorts, and unformal settlements - in coordination with related authorities (Turkish authority, Local Councils) in NSAG controlled areas in Aleppo gov. and Idleb gov. targeting 32,520 individuals (15,285 male, 17,235 female). In such situations, Bonyan will conduct RNA to update data of IDPs, returnees and host community and assess the situation whether it is NFI need or shelter need. Then proceed with; procurement and distribution of 2,000 NFI kits, 2,000 seasonal and supplementary winter kits including heating fuel and 1,000 sealing-off kits (needed items of age and gender segments will be included in the kits) to the people in need with considering vulnerability criteria to ensure that the most effected targeted people have their core and essential NFI support and they are protected from harsh seasonally conditions; and rehabilitation and repairing of 12 collective centers and 300 unfinished buildings by Cash for Work modality (70 workers) to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelters for short or medium-term purposes to the most vulnerable people in coordination with stakeholders to shortlist beneficiaries and verify HLP documents.
on the other hand the program will contribute towards increase the resilience and cohesion of targeted communities and households through improve housing and shelter conditions for IDPs, returnees and host communities in NSAG controlled areas in Aleppo gov. and Idleb gov. targeting 5,850 individuals (2,769 male, 3,081 female). To achieve this, Bonyan will provide needed support through Cash for Work modality (80 workers) to rehabilitate damaged houses of the most vulnerable owners, tenants or host families to increase adequacy of available shelter. Selection of damaged houses for rehabilitation will be in coordination with LCs to ensure the availability of all required HLP documents with considering vulnerability criteria when shortlisting beneficiaries. Along with the rehabilitation, Bonyan will provide essential trainings to local councils of targeted locations and program staff of Bonyan targeting 150 persons (100 men, 50 women) aiming to build capacities and increase skills on resilience and quality-oriented shelter and HLP verification guidance and basic of legal contract guidance.
Finally, Bonyan will address needs of different age and gender segments, during designing and implementing activities of the program, to increase safety and dignity, and to decrease any protentional protection risks, or GBV practices. The layouts of the suggested buildings for rehabilitation will be created by Bonyan engineers to fulfil gaps of women’ and girls’ needs, then the engineers will follow up and supervise implementation processes to ensure qualities and quantities during rehabilitation.

Needs

The political landscape in Syria is likely to remain unpredictable in 2019. the severe humanitarian situation is anticipated to persist. Hostilities and insecurity are likely in some areas, notably in Idleb and surrounding areas of north-western Syria and in the north-east. In NW Syria. The last political announcements may surge conflict, Localized tensions, including over control of resources is expected. although high levels of vulnerability persist, and protection issues will need to be closely identified and followed up. Aleppo:Returnees 161,762IDPs 990,494Shelter PiN 1,130,815: 883,834 in sever needNFI PiN 789,974: 704,140 in major need Idleb: IDPs: 1,125,234shelter PiN 847,579: 750,931 in sever need NFI PiN 1,028,750: 570,103 in sever need The number of IDP is likely to remain high. it is estimated to be up to 1.2M in 2019. most population movements are expected to occur as a result of ongoing hostilities. The scale of displacement is expected to be largely determined by events in Idleb and other areas of north-western Syria. 4.7% of the IDP households are female headed, among IDP children between 0-19 years, 48.7%male and 46.4%female. Each of these groups have had a unique experience of the crisis and are often exposed to different risks and vulnerabilities. Women becoming the primary family breadwinners. Women and girls as well as persons with disabilities face distinct protection risks, including gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse. Need assessment of Bonyan in western Aleppo shows that 38% of the population are IDPs. in Azaz and Al-Bab districts shows that 42% and 47% of population are IDPs. Our findings, HNO2019 and REACH assessments show: Shelter needs: 4.7 million people need shelter support due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters. Against the backdrop of increased self-organized returnees in 2018, there are increased needs related to the rehabilitation of damaged shelters as people seek more durable solutions. Significant proportion of population are living with at least one shelter adequacy issue. There is significant overcrowding in Idleb and north-west Aleppo. Shelter capacity has been exhausted with no additional capacity to absorb further arrivals. Common issues of shelter adequacy are lack of lighting inside or around the shelter, lack of heating or insulation from cold, lack of privacy and inability to securely lock their homes. However, shelter and repair materials are too expensive; repairs require professionals but cannot afford the costs.Top shelter needs according to age and gender- Boys and Girls: lighting inside and around more space inside the shelter- Women and adolescent girls: insulation, increase privacy ability to lock houses securely - Men and adolescent boys: doors, windows, electricity water pipelines- Elderly men and women: heating, insulation locks NFI needs: 4.4M require NFI assistance. The needs remain substantial particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance, as well as longer-term needs, among those communities requiring resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance the ability of families to withstand future shocks and stresses. While market access appears to be gradually improving still items are unaffordable such as Hygiene kits, cooking fuel & utensils, heating, clothing, lighting sources, mattresses and blankets. Top needs of NFI differs to age and gender as follows- Boys, Girls: diapers of children, clothing shoes- Boys adolescent: clothing, shoes, hygiene items- Girls adolescent clothing, shoes, sanitary pads - Men: heating, cooking, fuel,- Women: hygiene kit, cooking fuel & utensils- Men elderly: heating, bedding, clothing- Women elderly: hygiene kits, heating fuel bedding - People with disability: clothing, diapers, bedding - Female headed households: cooking, fuel, hygiene kit, heating

Implementation of activities

Bonyan will coordinate with Cluster, LCs, and active actors in the targeted communities to initiate SNFI program activities, Bonyan will conduct RNA to update IDP, returnees, and host community data and needs. Beneficiaries will be selected and provided with the services. If the intervention is targeting limited number of people Bonyan will announce the registration and selection criteria through several ways to ensure availability of distribution information for all age and gender groups. And share final beneficiary list publicly. MEAL team will follow up the Program and conduct PDM. Protection Risk Analysis

B. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Likelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs4. Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process D. Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety)Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or cancelled2. Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding3. Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management.4. Application of checklist at distribution sites5. Ensure that NFIs distribution are completed in time for beneficiariesto reach their homes during daylight6. Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion onhow to best avoid and mitigate risk7. Communicate with national and local authorities,stakeholders and community leaders8. Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women9. Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs R. Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. Likelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites.2. Provision of gender segregated mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit.3. Provision of gender segregated sanitation facilities sufficiently close to dwellings.4. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities.5. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them.6. Extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD. AC. Selection criteria is influence by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out.Likelihood: High Impact: MediumMitigation measure: 1. Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners.2. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and is clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. 3. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. 4. Establish complaint mechanism at community level.5. Training to humanitarian staffs.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Project’s locations are safe, secure and accessible for both genders of staff and beneficiaries while implementing activities. - Availability of requested HLP documents and accessibility with unfinished buildings’ owners.- Owners provide written consent for repairs and tenancy duration of at least one year from completion of rehabilitation.- Local council are cooperative with Bonyan team. Risk and Mitigation: H. Access restrictions to geo area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to the affected population.Likelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground2. Develop contingency planning3. Set-up multiple back-up distribution centres4. Liaison with the local authorities5. Explore different distribution modalities6. Elaborate different plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible. AA. Risk of conducting rehabilitation of building with poor structural integrity and/or not fully cleared from land-mines and ERWsLikelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Conduct a thorough shelter assessment and infrastructural technical assessment of the buildings that are aimed to be rehabilitated2. Coordinate the interventions with local authorities that might be aware of security issues in the neighborhood3. Ensure that ERWs clearance is conducted if needs be4. Check with other actors working in the same area to understand what kind of issues they encountered Z. Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitatedLikelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines.3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed4. Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form
