Project Summary


This project is in line with WHO’s strategic shift to the GPW 13 goals. This will take an integrated approach towards ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being and the three strategic priorities of achieving UHC, addressing health emergencies and promoting healthier populations, while optimizing synergies between one another. This project will focus on strengthening the capacity of management of severe acute malnutrition across the BAY states. It will strengthen the capacity of existing government health workers and partner organizations in provision of quality treatment to 20,670 SAM children out of the estimated SAM children expected to develop medical complications across the accessible LGAs in the BAY states. WHO will also support the government in preposition essential medicines for the smooth running of the Stabilization Centres to ensure uninterrupted supply of commodities to the implementing partners. This will be achieved via procurement and distribution of 200 SAM/Paediatric kits(upgraded 2020 module) to the various SCs and support partners and government in the establishment of new ones. Also over 250 health care staff will be trained on inpatient management of SAM with complications and supportive supervision and mentoring. The limited capacity of health care workers which is worsened by high staff turnovers (attrition rate) in especially the security compromised areas is an additional justification to sustain these training sessions. The training in 2021 will focus on T.O.T(training of trainers) which will focus more on resident health care professionals to ensure sustainability. Some of these professionals will also be rotated to SCs with low capacity for regular on-site sessions. The project will also facilitate access to life-saving nutrition services through early detection and referral of malnourished children especially from Hard-To-Reach areas to nutrition treatment centres for timely intervention. It will complement the humanitarian nutrition response of the sector in general and WHO’s efforts in particular by addressing unmet nutrition and health needs. It will also support the State MoHs by providing integrated life-saving nutrition services to the under-5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the priority areas. The services will be provided through 113 mobile health teams positioned in hard-to-Reach communities in the states by enhancing their capacity to strengthen community mobilization activities for timely detection of SAM children through routine screening for malnutrition and subsequent referral to the nearest treatment centres at the same time promoting IYCF practices. It will also integrate the screening for acute malnutrion in the WHO Early Warning and Response System (EWARS) to the targeted areas – EWARS is a simple and robust system to detect disease outbreaks. EWARS deployment consists in the restoration and stimulation of existing disease surveillance through a network of focal points and key informants to collect weekly report on a subset of 17 key diseases including measles, malaria, cholera and Sever Acute Malnutrition (SAM). This will improve timely detection of change of trend in nutrition status with its associated medical determinants.

1 - Needs

According to the 2020 HRP, 1.5 million people, including over 1 million children under five and half a million women of reproductive age within the BAY states, need life-saving nutrition services. Of these, 15 per cent are IDPs; 15 per cent returnees and 70 per cent are in host communities. Women and children are the most affected and those living at the reception centers are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed nutrition services prior to their arrival at the reception sites. Children who are severely malnourished are 12 times more likely to die (all-cause of mortality) compared to a well-nourished child. A non-breastfed infant under 6 months is 14 times more likely to die from pneumonia/diarrhea than an exclusively breastfed infant <6months. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno and 7.5% in Adamawa. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, approximately 809,814 children under five will suffer from acute malnutrition including 295,288 with SAM and 514,526 with MAM. Approximately only 54% of infants less than 6 months are exclusively breastfed in the BAY States. A total of 720,739 PLW women will require contextualized and skilled IYCF counselling both at facility and community levels with the aim of improving the exclusive breastfeeding rates. Children and women in hard-to-reach areas e.g. Magumeri, Kala/Balge, Gubio, Mafa, host community areas with low humanitarian activities/presence and poor infrastructure development e.g. Yobe State and newly arrivals from inaccessible areas are more likely to suffer from acute malnutrition. Women in rural setting are less likely to exclusively breastfeed their infants less 6 months due to increased workload/labour and lack of access to skilled IYCF counselling at health facilities. To reduce morbidity and mortality associated with global acute malnutrition, there is need to urgently increase the capacity to treat SAM cases with medical complications by establishing additional inpatient care facilities in Yobe and Adamawa States and in LGAs near to inaccessible areas in Borno State. There is also need to scale-up the identification, referral and treatment of children from hard to reach areas and in the newly liberated or return locations and other areas planned for the returns. The main thrust of WHO in emergencies is to strengthen, through information dissemination and training; the national, sub-national and international capacities of partners to meet the nutritional needs of populations in emergencies but with the BAY states emergency becoming protracted there is need to build a resilient system that will adequately respond to the acute nutrition shocks and at the same time have a strong system in place to ensure continuity of services. WHO’s unique status as a science- and evidence-based organization that sets globally applicable norms and standards makes it vital in a rapidly changing world especially in the context of emerging pandemics e.g COVID-19

2 - Activities

Since 2016, the provision of life-saving services to the affected communities is a joint effort between the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community. This project will be implemented in line with the World Health Assembly’s Global Programme of Work 13 goals which is focused on 3 interconnected strategic priorities to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages: achieving universal health coverage (UHC), addressing health emergencies and promoting healthier populations.The proposed activities will include mainly supporting government and partners in-patient management of SAM with medical complications with supplies and capacity building, provision of effective technical support through the production and dissemination of scientifically validated and up-to-date guidelines, norms, criteria and methodologies relating to nutritional standards. Close collaboration with sector partners including SMoH, Hospital management board, nutrition sector, health sector, and partner organizations will be maintained to review needs, assess progress and devise a strategy for strengthening. The key activities are: 1) Strengthen government and partner capacity to provide quality preventative and curative nutrition services. WHO will progressively integrate those health service delivery platforms into stronger local health systems through the setting up of a referral mechanism and increasing the health system capacity to cope with life-saving interventions and needs. ViaI. Organize a TOT for a pool of MoH master trainers on In-Patient Management of SAM with Medical Complications in the BAY states with the COVID-19 updates integrated. II. Promote an integrated approach for the management of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM,CM, OTP, SC, TSFP) as a minimum package of MNCH services; this will be achieved through the procurement and distribution of 200 SAM kits to all treatment centres for complicated SAM cases. The 2020 WHO SAM kits have been updated to include all the live-saving drugs and medical consumables needed to treat all complications of severe acute malnutrition and paediatric emergencies. This will go a long way in facilitating the smooth integration of SAM treatment int primary, secondary and tertiary facilities in which no sick child will be left behind including MAM cases with medical complications. The present collaboration with government and partners will be strengthened. Strengthen Government capacity on regular joint supportive supervision/monitoring of nutrition services; III. Support the systematic implementation of the Nurturing Care Framework in Health Facilities to ensure that children are developmentally on track.2) Providing access to comprehensive and high impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the 113 Hard-to-Reach Mobile Health Teams (H2R MHT) and Community Resource Persons (CORPs) delivery platforms. Under this approach, WHO will deliver extended basic and essential health packages in areas with low/no access. I. This will be through systematic screening for malnutrition and referral of all identified cases to treatment centres.II. While those at risk will be counselled through IYCF in addition to treatment for minor illnesss, vaccination, vitamin A supplementation and deworming to children.3) Enhance nutrition surveillance, prevention and control across newly accessible areas and the conflict affected LGAs in the BAY states. This will be through the expansion of the WHO Early Warning and Response System (EWARS). EWARS deployment consists in the restoration and stimulation of existing disease surveillance through a network of focal points and key informants to collect weekly report on a subset of 17 key diseases including measles, malaria, cholera and Sever Acute Malnutrition (SAM).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project is designed to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of accessing health interventions. In addition, WHO treatment standards will be followed to ensure interventions achieve the desired effect and not cause harm. There will be no discrimination and anyone within the targeted areas can access the services. Patients seeking treatment will be treated with respect and dignity. Also, training on GBV and PSEA policy/standards of conduct will be integrated into all WHO trainings sessions

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WHO’s Community health champions and Community Resource Persons(CORPs) will be engaged to continuously interact with the community members to inform them about the projects and services obtainable at the SCs, they will also support community mobilization as well as nutrition screening and referrals. Since the CORPs reside 24/7 at the communities, they will also update us on the feedback on the quality of service delivery. These will be analyzed with the MoH focal persons during the monthly CORPS supervisors review meetings. And feedback shared with the community leaders.Also the Ureport system already establishedin several locations will be utilized for feedback and complaints. The complaint desk of WHO through its dedicated toll-free lines that is stationed at the public health EOC will also closely monitor the activity by closely analysis the complaints sent and it will be followed by visit to the field to verify and provide feedback to the community.This project will ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed.WHO will periodically solicit opinions and thoughts of beneficiaries (including of the vulnerable groups and those with special needs- including people living with disability) through community engagement sessions. Feedbacks from the community will serve to guide the project implementation. • Through established network of LGA facilitators , the beneficiaries will be able to give feedback on the quality of service delivered and how well the activities have addressed their needs. This feedback will be used to modify services and activities implementation to the vulnerable population.
