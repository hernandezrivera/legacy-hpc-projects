Project Summary

The project aims to distribute free bread daily to about 4000 displaced families in more than 21 camps in Maarat Tmisrin, especially newly displaced families who have been displaced from the Maarat al-Numan countryside, Saraqib and the surrounding areas.
Where flour is provided free of charge by IHH while Insan secures the operating cost of baking the flour and distribute free bread to the beneficiaries at a bundle rate for each family, taking into account the most vulnerable groups, such as people with special needs, the elderly, and families headed by a woman or child. Every ton of flour produces 1150 bundles of bread weight (1 kg), contains (10 loaves per bundle).
About 4000 bread bundles will be distributed daily to 4000 newly displaced families during weekdays for one year, except Friday and Saturday. The project started on 10/2/2020 and will end on 31/12/2020.
The main goals of the project :
1- improving the food security status of food insecure people in northwest Syria.
2- provide life-saving and sustaining food assistance to IDP’s living in camps.
3- By providing the above-mentioned assistance, the IDPs will be less vulnerable to negative coping mechanisms resulting from insecurity about the immediate future, income, and social instability.

4 - Needs

Based on results from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. ” Some 70 percent of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65 percent of their expenditure. More than 37 percent of food-insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57 percent of food-insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. Of the displaced population, more than 760,889 people living in last resort camps are of particular concern to the sector as they have limited access to a diversified diet or income opportunities. With at least 69 per of the people living below $1.90 a day, more than double 2011, and with an estimated cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 and 2016, the relationship between poverty and food security remains particularly strong. Wheat production estimated to be at 2.2 million tons remains lower than the pre-crisis level of 4.1 million tons (2002-2011) and that of the domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tons.Humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2020 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through life-saving, life-sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. on 8/2/2020 INSAN CHARITY team conducted need assessment to 4000 H.Hs( 20653 individuals ) in maaret masrin camps . According to the need assessments :85 % of breadwinners are males while 15 % of breadwinners are females from whom 89 % are widowed.69 % of H.Hs displaced in less than 3 months. 96% of the H.Hs stated that the most important needs for them are bread while 3% stated that food basket is a priority for them. %100 of surveyed households stated that they need to buy bread daily, and the bread bundle costs between 300 to 500 S.P.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: No more IDPs displaced Risk: Increasing in the waves of displacement. Mitigation: Regularly update beneficiary lists and increasing bread quantity. Assumption: Distribution will be implemented smoothly.Risk: a conflict between IDPs and the host community because the host community will not receive humanitarian assistance. Mitigation: explain clearly the mechanism for selecting the beneficiary list, also we will rely on local authorities, civil society leaders to explain the criteria for selection and resolve any dispute. provide information written, verbal and illustrated like banners, beneficiaries cards .... etc to inform people who are qualified for assistance. assumption: fuel price is stable. Risk: Increasing fuel price due to security deterioration. Mitigation: 1 - sign a contract with the bakery based on stable prices throughout the project and ask the bakery to buy and store the fuel dedicated to this project. 2-decrease the number of the bread bundles produced from the ton of flour or decrease the weight of bread bundles to be suitable to compensate for the loss resulted from increasing fuel prices. Assumption: Beneficiaries will use bread as it is intended for. Risk: Beneficiaries selling bread.Mitigation: Applying distribution criteria (one bread bundle) for each family. assumption: The security situation is stable in project locations. Risk: The area gets exposed to security or military crisis. Mitigation: The area that we will work the project at, is considered secure and no major security issues were encountered.However, we will monitor the situation closely to secure our team and the material planned for distribution. In the case of security issues arising, the operation site will be changed after consultation with the donor.6- Assumption: Flour is available. risk: lack or shortage of ﬂour. mitigation: storing flour in our warehouses. IHH provides flour through cross border gate if it is suspended, finding local sources.
