Project Summary

The overall objective of the intervention is to ensure that emergency affected girls and boys as well as youth continually access quality learning opportunities relevant for their psycho-social well-being, physical and cognitive development. The project targets children and youth aged between 6 - 17 from IDP, returnees and host community families with age appropriate interventions that respond to their education needs. Primary focus for this project is displacement affected out-of-school children and youth affected by conflict and climate emergencies. NRC anticipates to reach 38,873 children (52% girls); 875 adults (400 men ; 475 women [375 teachers & 500 parents/caregivers and other non-teaching professionals) as direct beneficiaries. The project will focus on strengthening and improving quality and delivery of education in four strategic response areas which are 1) First Phase response - through NRC's mobile response operations and targeting newly displaced families, with short term education activities lasting from 6-9 months. 2) Formal education - conducted through NRC's static program operations across the country in prioritized counties where NRC has existing physical presence; 3) Non Formal education through ALP classes for over-aged children who missed years of schooling due to conflict and displacement and 4) Youth Education and training with an emphasis on vocational skills training, peace-building and life-skills training. In close collaboration with parents, caregivers and local actors, NRC will construct and repair learning spaces/classrooms and gender segregated school WASH facilities; provide school hardware assets (desks/benches), essential learning materials, teacher aids, recreational & hygiene kits; girls dignity kits for MHM; support mgt of schools through teacher selection, recruitment, motivation, training on child centered pedagogical methods, curriculum planning, classroom mgt & conflict sensitive education; support teacher learning cycles (TLCs) for continuous professional dvpt; facilitate community engagement in education through establishment/strengthening of PTAs & SMCs through training in roles and responsibilities, school development planning and awarding school improvement grants; conduct awareness raising community dialogue sessions to mobilize enrollment, promote attendance and support retention especially for girls who face multiple barriers to education including harmful and social practices such as forced early marriages and boys who also face protection risks that include child labour and forced recruitment into armed groups; conduct life skills training & support structured recreational activities for physical and psycho-social development; provide technical and material support non-teaching professionals (county education directors, supervisors) to effectively conduct their school supervision and support duties; training of teachers, facilitators, on Mental Health and psycho-social support (MHPSS), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), child protection, child safeguarding and PSEA; provide cash and vouchers assistance to the most vulnerable for examination registration.
Key approaches that NRC will adopt in this project include integration, partnerships and community based programming. NRC will collaborate local structures as well as with other clusters including protection, WASH, shelter, LFS, Nutrition and Health to deliver comprehensive package that addresses the holistic needs of targeted population. Furthermore, for durable solutions, NRC will work with local actors, parents and care-givers building their capacity for self reliance and also to increase coverage in hard to reach areas with periodic access challenges and bureaucratic impediments. For effective and efficient use of resources and for learning purposes, NRC will continue to participate in coordination mechanisms at both the national and sub-national levels to avoid overlaps and duplication with other education players.

1 - Needs

Families in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian situation. Their condition is exacerbated by widespread violence, climatic shocks, economic challenges, impacts of COVID-19. The dire situation has negatively affected the capacity of vulnerable households to access essential social services such as education, health, water and sanitation. According to the 2022 HNO, more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, will need of humanitarian assistance. This represents an increase of 600,000 since 2021. On one hand continued conflict and instability combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. While on the other, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns. Compounding shocks have resulted in cyclical displacements in various locations across the country. At least 90,000 returnees from neighboring countries remained displaced within the country. From 2021 to 2022, the number of counties with extreme food insecurity levels has more than doubled from 6 to 13. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (HNO; 2022). This poses a serious threat to education especially in view of the fact that the education cluster posits that hunger is one of the main reasons why children drop out of school. This not only increases associated protection risks but also negatively affects long term physical and cognitive development of affected children.The protracted conflict and climate-related crisis coupled with COVID-19 pandemic in 2020/21 resulted prolonged school closures. Thereby increasing the number of OOSC from 2.2million in 2018 to 2.8 million in 2022 and reducing gross enrollment by 7% between 2020 and 2021. The increase in the number of OOSC has also been reflected in the number of candidates registering for P8 examinations 2022 in early - which is 53,220 as opposed to 64, 200 in 2021 (SRTWG , 2022). With concerted efforts of humanitarian actors, there has been a steady increase in school enrollment post Covid-19. UNICEF asserts that voluntary returns from Uganda where schools were closed for 2 years increased pressure on already overstretched education facilities with negative effects on quality learning. Due to excessive flooding, 40% or more than 190 schools mostly in Jonglei and Upper Nile States were non-functional in 2021. Some suffered from infrastructure destruction while others were occupied as shelter by climate displaced families. According to the EMIS data of 2018, some schools either partially or completely damaged, were forced to close down due to insecurity in 2019. These schools had not yet reopened when the government closed schools in 2020 due to Covid_19 and due to low levels of government investment in education infrastructure most of these schools are still in a state of disrepair. Among the 20% of schools that were non-functional, insecurity is the main cause for closure. Despite calls by the UN to limit hostilities during Covid crisis, insecurity continued to escalate. Active conflict and regular communal violence throughout 2021 led to school closures in Jonglei, Pibor, Lakes, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria, Warrap, Unity and Western Equatoria among others. Learners in the 2021 ENA cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. At estimate of 63% of teachers lack formal qualifications UNICEF (2021). This, coupled with low and delayed salaries demotivates teachers and lead to absenteeism and ultimate high levels of drop-out amongst learners. The project will address the identified needs through interlinked activities that contribute to life saving goals and address immediate education needs while strengthening community capacity and building on their resilience to withstand future shocks .

2 - Project activities

In line with cluster objectives, this project's specific objectives are; 1) Children and youth affected by conflict and displacement continue to access quality learning opportunities in safe, inclusive and protected environments, 2) Children and youth who have missed years of school due to conflict and displacement have access to age appropriate education opportunities. These two project objectives align with cluster objective #1 and #2 which seek a). to improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for emergency affected children including children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) from IDP returnees and host community families in the priority counties and b) improve the quality of education services at all levels and protect the well-being of all children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of crisis on learning environments respectively. 3) Local education structures and systems are strengthened and are effective for building back better in recovery phases and beyond. The third project objective also aligns with cluster objective #3 which aims to "Strengthen the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities by 2022/23 in priority counties." NRC will implement the following activities; *community mobilization sessions/back to learning campaigns to promote enrollment of girls, boys and youth including children with disabilities *enroll 38,873 children (52% girls) in supported learning centers incl. 790 children with disabilities *establish 20 new TLS and repair 25 existing TLS *provide TLMs to teachers and learners *provide assistive devices to children with disabilities *train teachers in child -centered teaching methods, MHPSS, GBV, CP, CSG, Code of Conduct, referral pathways * conduct ALP classes * distribute TLM * Train 375 PTA members and other non-teaching professionals on school management, supervision & support DRR, school safety planing * provide technical and material support to county education offices* sensitization sessions on disease prevention life saving messages_for Covid_19, HIV and other water borne diseases * provide IEC materials *payment of teachers incentives * facilitate continuous teacher professional development through peer to peer support in teacher learning cycles *establish and repair gender segregated WASH facilities * distribute dignity kits for girls, hygiene materials and recreational kits * promote children participation through child-led clubs *conduct conflict sensitive education & peace building training *provide school feeding in IPC 4 &5 areas * establish school gardens in collaboration with LFS cluster * conduct staff training on safe and inclusive education, child safeguarding and PSEA * set up and support community feedback mechanisms inclusive of school help desks and trusted community advocacy processes to address children's concerns

3 - Project indicators

Project Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) will be undertaken in line with NRC's guidelines that emphasize participatory approaches, relevance, accountability and timeliness of information and data. The focus will be on results monitoring which will measure the contribution of this assistance in changing or improving the lives of beneficiaries. NRC will also use gender analysis to inform the development of monitoring, reporting and evaluation frameworks for quality programming to ensure outputs deliver for the needs of women and men. The M&E system will consist of following key tools: a) An M&E Work plan/matrix outlining plans for data collection, analysis, and use and reporting premised on the log frame indicators. The plan will be a living document that will be reviewed periodically based on emerging needs. NRC will use KOBO mobile data collection tools to conduct survey including multi-sector rapid assessments, PDM and baseline/end line surveys for measuring outcome indicators. b) An Indicator Definition and Analysis will provide a detailed description of each indicator, sources of data, the measurement parameters and beneficiary dis-aggregation by sex, age, displacement and ability status. c) An Intervention Tracking Tool will track the progress against a target on monthly basis and measure if a result is achieved based on the prescribed activities/outputs in the log frame. The tool will be shared during quarterly reviews of the project. d) A Detailed Activity Implementation Plan will outline key activities and tasks to be undertaken to achieve the planned results. This tool will track activity implementation in order to ensure planned activities are implemented on schedule and where appropriate adjustments are made. e) A simple beneficiary compliant/feedback response mechanism will be implemented to enhance accountability and transparency in the delivery of the project activities. This will also consider child-friendly feedback and consultation methods. NRC will carry out an integrated Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) that cover all distributions to assess beneficiary satisfaction and usage of the facilities provided. In addition, bi- weekly monitoring system used for tracking project outputs will be used. NRC will continue to provide monthly updates to the cluster. Focus Group Discussions to integrate qualitative information in the monitoring system and internal Project Reviews will also be carried out. Results of the monitoring processes will be shared with stakeholders, MoGEI, local leadership, PTAs, school administrators and other relevant parties for accountabilities purposes.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NRC implements it project using integrated approach. Under this project, NRC will coordinate with protection CC and cluster and conduct training, awareness sessions on PSEA. The training will target a variety of stakeholders who include local chiefs, law enforcement agencies, child protection committees, PTAs, school communities and education authorities. The training will also ensure that participants' capacity to identify, prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse is build. Referral pathways within and outside of the project locations will be made available to the beneficiaries. NRC will print and distribute IEC materials on PSEA. Furthermore, the PTA, and teachers training will also have a session dedicated to teachers’ code of conduct which largely addresses PSEA issues. Under teacher, PTA training, NRC will ensure that a session on PSEA is included. Additional mechanisms to provide information about PSEA include community sensitization and awareness sessions, use of IEC materials, training of program volunteers, providing accessible information for girls, boys, men and women of different abilities, utilization of school mothers and child-led child protection committees NRC. In this complaints, feedbacks and recommendation will be taken into consideration.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability lies at the core of this project. Prior to activity implementation, the education team will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders are involved in all stages of project development and informed of the feedback mechanisms available to them. Beneficiaries will be able to make confidential suggestions for improving the project, lodge complaints where they disagree with implementation modalities, or seek feedback. During and After implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation teams will collect data on the quality of information received by beneficiaries and stakeholders about the feedback mechanism and their satisfaction with the mechanism. The modalities of the mechanism will be based as much as possible on traditional. NRC has policy and standards to enable all external stakeholders, especially the people we serve, to provide feedback and issue complaints on NRC’s organizational performance, and for NRC to address such feedback and complaints in a safe and responsive manner. NRC is committed to accountability and transparency in all activities. This Policy is framed within that commitment and provides broad guidance on complying to the Core Humanitarian Standard. The project will work with the communities and people affected by crisis to ensure complaints are raised, received and addressed timely. NRC will ensure that the project systematically engages with all beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, including the most marginalized, to deliver information about assistance to be provided in such a way as to maximize meaningful participation and consultation. Community dialogues, sensitization meetings are some of the platforms that the project will use to relay information to beneficiaries. Information shared will include the scope of the proposed interventions, entitlements of those involved; clarity on targeting of vulnerable groups, and information essential to accessing complementary services, as well as other relevant information. NRC will consult with communities about the optimal way to share information, and provide information in forms accessible to those with limited literacy and in local languages, to the extent that it is possible. In 2021 NRC will roll out a digital community hub accountability platform where program beneficiaries can access information via bulk messaging, sms, whatsapp, a call centre and and other channels deemed appropriate in the local context. Program beneficiaries will raise them complains through the same media channel and also receive feedback in similar ways. NRC will establish a dedicated complaint response and feedback mechanism which will be used throughout the intervention period from assessment to post distribution or intervention monitoring. Before the establishment of the complaint and feedback mechanism NRC will raise awareness so that affected community get to know where and how to address their complaints, give feedbacks and receive feedbacks as well. The Complaint mechanism will be put in a way that encourages beneficiaries to freely address their concerns and get feedbacks. Specifically, NRC will use the following ways to receive complaints and feedback; 1. Help desk will be established and managed by NRC staff and a representative from the affected community will be included in the process of receiving complaints and feedbacks as well as responding to concerns raised. 2. Before, during and after interventions, NRC will organize meetings involving the community leaders from the affected population, teachers, education authorities, PTA/SMC members, religious leaders, representatives of persons with disabilities, women representatives, youth representative and others to sensitize them on the project. 3. NRC will conduct a focus group discussion with specific group from the affected population including children with disabilities to get their views on the activities carried out by NRC. In this complaints, feedbacks and recommendation will be taken into consideration.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

NRC has robust Policy frame work that guides its operation especially on ensuring protection of persons in Need we serve. For example, to address the risk of beneficiaries’ exposure to violence during registration/ distribution as a result of distance to or location of humanitarian assistance services, NRC ensures that the staff moves closure to the beneficiaries and staff are trained to observe protection and safeguarding issues throughout the project at all times. Further to deal with risks of women, girls , boys without and with disabilities exposure to sexual violence including sexual exploitation and abuse ( SEA) when accessing humanitarian services ; NRC plans to deal with this both internally and externally, Internally NRC has clear policy being enforced and apart from having policy and enforcement of policy which safe guard against this also within this project NRC will coordinate with protection CC within the organization and cluster and conduct training, awareness sessions on SEA to target beneficiaries so that they are responsible , be at watch and talk against it and encourage people at risk to report such incidents before it culminate into serious situation. Further the project has sufficiently planned mechanism to combat and ensure child Safe guard and PSEA, this is within NRC policy. Internally NRC trains its implementing staff on these and ensure any person collaborating with NRC in the project is sensitized on and signs the policy documents to adhere by when serving beneficiaries. To deal the risk of lack of access to information on referral pathways; NRC will work with protection partners in the project locations, conduct trainings, hold sensitization sessions for community leaders, girls, boys, PTAs, teachers and other education professionals. Information will be provided through community meetings, electronic media - radio programs, IEC materials (posters, flyers, banners and leaflets). Further information will also be relayed through child led activities that will promote peer-to- peer learning among girls and boys from affected communities. To minimize the risk of low levels/ non participation in feedback processes, NRC will use multiple channels to allow boys, girls, women, men and youth of different ages, abilities and displacement status to participate in the feedback processes. The channels to be used include suggestion boxes, the digital hub which encompasses a call center where beneficiaries can send feedback via sms, whatapp or voice calls. Moreover, NRC will conduct community meetings targeted at specific groups such as children with disabilities and provide them with an opportunity to participate in a safe environment. This is in addition to child feedback and consultation meetings that NRC will conduct in participating communities using child friendly approaches - drawings, art, games, and other artistic tools. To protect beneficiaries data the following mitigation measures will be taken: • Beneficiaries information is only mentioned in registration sheets, which are archived in the NRC archiving system for which the access is limited to few key staff for reporting and auditing purposes only. • Beneficiary including children's pictures are only taken after having consent and the concerned individuals are well informed on how NRC will utilize the information collected. • Project Reports are only shared with the clusters, key partners and donors (the reports do not include any personal information). • NRC staff's use tablets for collection of data during assessment and PDMs phases without any personal individual information.

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This project seeks to ensure that children and youth affected by emergencies access quality and inclusive education in safe and protected environments that is relevant to their cognitive development and psycho-social well-being. Therefore, conflict sensitivity is an over-arching principle to all project activities. This intervention will support children and adults to conduct risk assessments at local levels. Community and child-led risk assessments will inform safety measures that are appropriate for each project location. The project will also advocate for services that are inclusive, safe, socially appropriate and protect the dignity of girls and boys as well as the whole school communities. The project will work closely with protection cluster partners and contribute to Collective Outcome 2 through activities to be implemented via multiple channels such as school help desks, community and children's clubs in learning centers. Further more, there will be focus on strengthening family and community structures supportive of safe learning environments. Representatives of community and school based children protection committees as well as teachers, PTA members, facilitators, girls, boys and youth will receive basic training in mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS), child protection, GBV prevention and referral pathways. The project will support interventions that raise awareness on protection issues, child safeguarding and mitigation of protection risks for all project participants regardless of their age, sex or ethnic background. The project will ensure that all contracted facilitators and volunteer teachers are trained on the South Sudanese code of conduct and that they sign a copy of it. Community leaders, parents and children will also be trained on the teachers code of conduct to raise their awareness on the same. Equipped with the requisite knowledge, parents and children will be in a better place to prevent, identify and take appropriate action including reporting cases of abuse. The target groups will also be in a better position to seek specialized services timely. Safe and secure learning environments will contribute to improved learning outcomes for emergency affected children and youth.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will conduct DRR training for the whole school communities. It is envisaged that as a result of this training, community leaders, teachers, parents, youth, girls and boys will be in a better place to assess climate risks and develop mitigation measures that will reduce the risk of further displacements in future crisis.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

In close collaboration with the LFS cluster partners, this project will implement school and community gardens where PTA members, children in senior classes and youth will receive basic training on nutrition gardens, setting up of seedbeds, food preservation preparation of nutritious meals and dietary diversification. Material support, inclusive of seeds and inputs, will be provided to the trained individuals who will receive continual support on managing their gardens. Parents will also be supported to set up nutrition gardens in their communities and supply vegetables to schools to supplement school feeding commodities made available to participating schools. Skills acquired through nutrition gardens will enable learners to contribute to food production at household level, which in turn increases the available food thereby reduce the number of people facing critical food shortages.
