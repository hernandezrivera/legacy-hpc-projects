Project Summary

In 2023, Tdh will scale up its WASH response through gender-responsive, rights-based, adolescent and youth-friendly inclusive services in camp 26, camp 27 and the surrounding host communities. WASH activities will offer knowledge and awareness on how to maintain personal hygiene and WASH facilities in appropriate ways. Tdh will also address adequate and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene, which Tdh continuously advocates for. All activities will ensure the do-no harm principle by prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls. Tdh will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures before and during natural disasters, including disaster risk reduction, AWD/dengue outbreak, COVID issues etc. WASH operations and maintenance committee and key stakeholders were included throughout the project development and community feedback mechanism and response will be maintained during the implementation period.
Tdh WASH activities will focus on Rohingya refugees residing in camps as well as the surrounding host communities, according to the gaps and needs at different community levels, being inclusive to age (05-17 years boy and girls, 18-59 years men and women, and 60 years plus elderly men and women), gender and diversity (If there are any transgender families living there, the project will also support them). Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the Feacal Sludge Management plant (desludging, treatment, and disposal) that is already built (including an incinerator) in camp 27 as well as waste management activities (collection, transportation, segregation, and disposal) including organic composting. Tdh-operated FSM and waste management services will continue to receive, sludge, treat and dispose (dry sludge and waste) from the camps 26 and 27, the two Primary Health Care Centers run by Tdh and the host communities, as well as the Tdh Multi-Purpose Community Centres. The two PHCs run by Tdh have an incinerator but given the PHC’s current production of large quantities of contaminated waste which cannot be treated at the PHC that’s why an autoclave will also be installed in one PHC to sterilize plastic parts of infectious waste then all waste will be managed at waste management plant, where crusher machine, pits (ash and sharp) and incinerator is already built-in accordance with proper standards. Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the WASH facilities in the PHCs, Camps and surrounding host community as well as construct/rehabilitate/decommission WASH facilities (latrine, bathing cubicle, garbage bin, and environmental cleaning area) in the Camps based upon their condition and needs. Additionally plastic recycling plant will be installed in camp 27 (to make toys, ring, slab, alphabet cube, and bricks/tiles). Due to the scarcity of water in Teknaf, 03 water recycling hand washing station will be installed in camp 26 and 27 (02 at PHC and 01 in camp), to ensure access to hand washing. Tdh will conduct continued management and improvements of all facilities to ensure adequate Infection Prevention and Control (IPC)/disinfection.
Awareness raising activities will be delivered to both refugees and host communities through household sessions/meeting, campaigns, day celebrations and the development and distribution of IEC/BCC materials. These activities will cover the topics of waste management, sanitation, handwashing, COVID-19/AWD/Dengue, and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) among adolescent and women. This will be complimented by the distribution of NFI/WASH kits (during emergency Tdh will distribute NFI items as contingency plan, as like; handwashing device, WASH kit, MHM kits at HH’s level) to ensure beneficiaries’ adequate WASH support.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In camps 26 and 27, lack of access to proper waste disposal services continues to be a major challenge, with the 2021 J-MSNAs reporting 27% (refugee) and 11% (HC) HHs often finding visible waste in the vicinity of their house. Tdh-PRM funded implementation in 2020-2022 has already addressed this through the operation of Faecal Sludge Management (FSM) and Solid Waste Management (SWM) plants. While continuing the SWM and FSM services, further improvements still need to be made such as solution to recycle LDPE which are not sellable in the recycling market. Most of the HHs reported arranging their own waste and reported a designated waste pit as the main source of waste disposal, close to a quarter referred to disposing of waste in a water source while another quarter cited undesignated pits. Due to density of the population, latrines quickly filling up and inadequate desludging services can lead to pits overflowing and contaminating surrounding water sources. Block cleaning services needed, as adequate wastewater disposal from households and WASH facilities is mostly lacking. As per 2022 JRP and 2021 J-MSNAs, only 23% of refugee have access to safe and functional latrines, while only 26% of HC respondents reported having access to clean drinking water. In the host communities, most of the HHs reported not having access to soap and water after utilizing latrines. At camp level, 6% of HHs reported loss or diminished access to clean water and sanitation as an impact of COVID-19, while 17% of the host community recorded loss of diminished access to basic services.As per the May'2021 J-MSNA, ongoing WASH needs in camp 26, 27 and surrounding HC include the lack of access to functional sanitation (now overall functional 23%), waste disposal services, FSM, and the potential for water source contamination and health and hygiene issues. According to the J-MSNA 2020, access to safe and functional latrines for males is 20%, female is 33% whilst overall access is reported to be 23%. FST facilities need to be upgraded to improve efficiency and coverage. Need to strengthen SWM systems, promotion of waste segregation, recycling, and composting. There is a need of harmonizing SWM systems and to engage WASH agencies in sustainable, and female participation engagement.Uptake of appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviours remains low. For example, only 32% of HHs reported washing their hands after handling children’s faeces (REACH, 2019). Safe water handling practices at HH level are also not widely observed, with the reported levels of contamination of stored water exceeding 90% (WHO/UNICEF -2019). In respect to MHM, 24% of women and girls reported not receiving MHM materials within the last 3 months (REACH, July 2019).After 5years of implementation of WASH activities in Cox’s Bazaar, WASH agencies now need to focus on carrying out continuous improvements to existing latrines and BC to increase durability, quality, privacy while addressing gender, disability, age factors. The construction of new latrines following the unified designs, will address coverage gaps, and the upgrading of FST facilities to improve efficiency through full chain treatment processes. Another priority area is to strengthen the SWM systems, in conjunction with assessing the factors that motivate people to segregate waste. There is a need to follow the hygiene promotion strategy and focus on strengthening participatory approaches for Rohingya refugees to contribute in change high-risk behaviours. Community mobilization will be reinforced to ensure participation-especially of women and girls during design, feedback mechanisms and implementation of WASH services. As Teknaf is a water scarce area in Bangladesh, there is a need to focus more on the development of systems for water resource management, and safe and appropriate SWM that will contribute to preservation of the environment, as well as innovative and new interventions that can save more water.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project is designed to address WASH strategies in consultation and coordination with the WASH and other relevant sectors in the response of Rohingya and host communities in Cox's Bazar to implement quality, inclusive WASH interventions, and also supporting to the Government of Bangladesh in contributing to reach SDG-Sustainable Development Goals (Goal 3: Good health and wellbeing, Goal 4: Quality education, Goal 5: Gender equality, and Goal 10: Reduced inequalities). The main objective of the project is to increase access to quality WASH including waste management services, strengthen inclusiveness of facilities and community participation, empowerment, and awareness. This is an integrated project where the main components are Child Protection, Health, and WASH. In Tdh working areas, targeted beneficiaries will get all mentioned component's services from the project, and as well as outreach team will enhance community people’s knowledge on all components that will contribute to increase in meaningful participation of the beneficiaries/stakeholders. This will also increase in knowledge of community people about their rights/responsibilities which will support to stabilise the maintenance of WASH facilities in the community. The project will be implemented following the prevailing guidelines of RRRC and respectice CiC through approval of projects from NGOAB and regular consultation with the authorities for implementation of different activities. All these activities will directly contribute for the WASH facilities functionality and sustainability which will bring positive changes in their lives (between Rohingyas in host communities peoples) and will establish gender equality, age and disability inclusion appropriateness and environmental protection in the area of Water Sanitation and Hygiene needs. During implementation period project will follow core Humanitarian Standard and the ‘do no harm’ approach.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

This project is part of an integrated project that focuses on CP, Health and WASH. Targeted beneficiaries will receive services from all the mentioned components from the project, as well as the outreach team will engage both communities (Rohingya and host) to implement all the components and increase the meaningful participation of the beneficiaries/stakeholders which will help them in decision making during project activity and facility designing, planning, and implementation, for example; The same beneficiaries will get WASH, CP and health services, WASH project we will construct gender sensitive, age and disability friendly structures, hygiene promotion (including NFI), messaging (including handwashing, sanitation, MHM, waste management, water safety plan, AWD, Dengue and related to COVID-19) and will receive their feedback and complain from CFRM system and address them, and will follow do no harm and CHS, all service directly contribute to protection and to save lives.

3.2 - Gender

In the proposed 2023 project Tdh will address gender issue equally, especially during the all-project activities implementation period, such as; participation in meetings/sessions, gender sensitive WASH facilities (toilets, bathing cubicles) design and construction, involvement of male and female volunteers/staff members in hygiene message dissemination. During staff and volunteer hiring time, Tdh will give more importance to hire equal numbers (male and female) of staff member. As well, project will ensure equitable gender sensitive WASH access at both community (Rohingya and host).

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

In regular WASH activities (Including Hygiene Promotion session/meeting), will include different age, gender and person with disabilities in decision making time, and also project will ensure their smooth access to the WASH facilities and will ensure child safeguarding issues during implementing WASH activities and will follow do no harm and CHS standards. To reduce open defecation, the project will provide children under 5 years of age with plastic potty to promote defecation habits, which will help them develop toilet habits. Also, any child protection issue detected will be immediately reported to the protection team which will ensure child protection.

3.4 - Diversity

If there is a transgender community/family living in the project area, the project will ensure access to WASH support to them as they are still not easily accepted by the community, so they face a lot of harassment and humiliating situations when they want to use any male/female/community toilets. Ensuring this diversification activities among the targeted beneficiaries, it will help them to reduce social inequality. Another aspect is lack/absence of plastic recycling possibilities that will be considered producing different types of toys, alphabets, rings, slabs, drain covers, alternative bricks and tiles from the waste plastics thorough installation of plastic recycling machine. All items produced will be used at camp and host community level. On pilot basis, recycling hand washing station will be installed at certain locations which will be assessed to scale up in the following years.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP IASC and Tdh framework, Tdh will implement activities following three main phases. (I) Taking account: the project team will engage with beneficiaries from the very beginning to update the baseline data and share all relevant project information and to involve them in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: Tdh will organize formal and informal meetings at key moments of the project (start, annually and end line evaluation) to provide communities with the opportunity to assess the project and to give feedback on the project performances. Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism that is already established at the camp level will continue getting direct information from the beneficiaries and addressing them through proper channel.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In part, the project will contribute to disaster risk reduction during floods/flush floods, and AWD outbreaks, and as well as the project will manage all types of waste in the community to reduce flood situations, the project will provide portable toilets to meets basic needs of the beneficiaries, and the project will provide emergency assistance in case of WASH-related outbreaks that will mitigate the situation.
