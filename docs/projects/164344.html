Project Summary

Basma for relief and development (BRD) will establish a planned camp for the 5,400 newly displaced populations affected by the ongoing conflict, including forced evacuations, and provide multi sector assistance to the IDPs including temporary shelter, non-food items, WASH, Food, Protection and Health in Idleb Governorate.

4 - Needs

According to CCCM Cluster trucked displacement of 520K to Jan 2020 which the Snapshot indicate, Idleb especially has experienced a significant IDP influxThis inflow of IDPs is tied to the south Idleb, which led to the movement of up to 520K individuals to north of Idleb.the total number of displaced people in the governorate of Idleb increased to 920,000, and further displacement is expected towards north of Idleb in the coming weeksIDP sites are approached only as a measure of last resort after IDPs have exhausted all other financial and social assets. Thus, residents in such camp-like settings are amongst the most vulnerablethe continuous influx of evacuees from South of Idleb has raised the demand for shelter, food, and non-food items. Additionally, the influx has led to a shortage of drinking water across Idleb, forcing people to rely on trucked waterThe humanitarian situation is further exacerbated by an ongoing shortage of basic food and non-food items, in particular for the evacuees from South of IdlebGBV and CP issues are present in most areas in North Syria and seem to worsen within IDPs communities. According to the 2019 HNO, child recruitment is a prominent concern in 42 per cent of 262 sub-districts and children are often the main breadwinners.As such, the CCCM cluster highlights the urgent need to provide coordinated, comprehensive assistance to the new IDPs in critical need of shelter, NFIs, food, hygiene, health, protection, and sanitation services. As hostilities persist, more IDPs will likely exhaust their household resources and take refuge in camps, informal tented settlementsIDP sites have reached capacity or already have exceeded optimal population density conditions. In light of this scenario and the evacuation of families from South of Idleb, the CCCM Cluster has determined the need to establish a planned camp in Idleb, and approached BRD to undertake its initial set-up and coordinate a multisectoral response therein

5 - Implementation of activities

BRD will serve as the camp management agency, and set in place proper staffing/management personnel to conduct CCCM activities, including registration/data collection, vulnerability mapping, verification, establishment of gender-balanced camp governance/ participation management structures, and accountability mechanisms, routine site renovationsProcedure of Reception of IDP’s in the camp Once IDP families arrive at the camp, the following procedures will be followed:The families arriving to the camp will pass through the registration reception tent to be registered, and undertake a profiling questionnaire with focus on identifying vulnerable and special cases. Registered families will also be given a registration card Vulnerable cases (disabilities, elderly) will be given priority during the processThey and their family will be allocated a family tent near to access points. BRD will conduct workshop to start project.BRD will organize monthly planning and progress meetings and monitor activities through periodic field visits by MEAL team. The methods used for data collection and observing changes will be beneficiary interviews PDM after MEAL team will ensure community participation during the project implementation through activated feedback mechanism, where many channels will be available includes but mot limited, WhatsApp, email, skype, suggestion boxes and field team. Feedback mechanism will support the accountability within the project staff and targeted camps.Field coordinator will always be willing to listen from the beneficiaries, Monitoring will focus on, but not limited to, cycle of activities, and how BRD is achieving the criteria of CCCM project.• Regular locations supervision carried out by Field Coordinator• Weekly locations visits by MEAL assistants,• Weekly reports prepared by Field Coordinator specifying work completed, work planned..etc

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Intentions surveys and/or needs assessments will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs living in camps and contribute to assessment fatigue. Distribution of tents will lead to more spontaneous and informal settlements, which itself poses risks associated with the absence of official site management and coordinated services. Targeting of communities and beneficiary selection will fuel unintended inter or intra community tensions. The management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities. Construction contractors could create some tensions or harm based on their salary payments. Mitigating measures:i) Ensure intentions of IDPs arriving to planned camp ii) Ensure comprehensive service mapping of IDPs sites in the region is undertaken, intentions results are shared with CCCM partners and service providers to better coordinate services at intended destinationsMitigating measure i) Ensure that the IDPs in camp are the most vulnerable people. All IDPs based and that camp residents are amongst the most vulnerable ii) Ensure healthy communication with the people of concern to avoid raising unrealistic expectation iii) BRD will strive to employ women enumerators to achieve gender-balanced teams. Mitigating measurei) Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on the camp establishment and camp support ensuring that camps are the last-resortTo mitigate against tension and project disruption, BRD will engage the target community through regular meetings, transparent information management and beneficiary selection, and throughout implementation.
