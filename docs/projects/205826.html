Project Summary

This project will target 14,000 persons (2,800 men, 3,920 women, 3,500 boys and 3,780 girls), which is further broken down into 7,000 IDPs, 2,800 host communities and 4,200 returnees, with a main focus on protection analysis and advocacy and reducing the sufferings of gender-based violence (GBV) survivors. For the purposes of ensuring an in-depth understanding of protection environment, allowing for appropriate prevention and response activities, Nile Hope (NH) will focus on protection data collection and analysis. This is to say that, NH will ensure that protection concerns are captured and highlighted in toolkits such as assessment reports, monitoring reports and protection snapshots, therefore, allowing for appropriate and relevant advocacy with the national cluster, humanitarian partners and other relevant stakeholders.
In order to ensure that appropriate and principled actions are taken to prevent and respond to protection risks or concerns, NH will ensure that this project is centered on both individual and community level by ensuring evidence-based interventions. During the implementation of this project, NH will promote partnership with the affected communities through already established and function community structures including vulnerable groups and individuals, with the aim of identifying protection risks and coping strategies, community and individual intentions and resilience amongst the affected population. Notably, these activities will enhance communities’ and at risk individuals’ preparedness and resilience. Moreover, NH will work closely with communities’ protection watch groups/committees, facilitate focus group discussions, awareness raising and training on basic human rights and protection mainstreaming, with an aim of preventing and mitigating identified protection risks.
Needless to say, NH will provide timely, coordinated, and complimentary protection responses based on the identified protection needs. Most important to realize, is that protection will be central to all interventions which will include service mapping, safe referrals, individual protection assistance for at-risk individuals, information sharing, awareness raising on fundamental human rights and protection mainstreaming. To ensure that this objective is achieved, the project will integrate with other sectors including S/NFIs, FSL, WASH, Education, Nutrition, Health and Peace Building Programs. Furthermore, the project will primarily focus on ensure that the safety and dignity persons and communities affected by displacement are prioritized and a do-no-harm approach is mainstreamed during all aspects of the implementation.
Out of the 14,000 persons that this intervention seeks, 1000 persons will be people with disabilities. The project will target most vulnerable Households which includes old people and persons with disabilities. An overall percentage of 10% is allocated to catering for persons living with disabilities. Older persons and persons with disabilities (PWD) will be considered at the decision-making level to ensure their needs and issues affecting them are brought forward. The registration and distribution sites will be chosen considering the challenges affecting PWD and older people. They will be near, safe, and accessible for all. Localized methods and approaches will be developed to ensure that the old and disabled people are considered not to miss out on any topic. The PDM tools, other data collection tools and sample size will also consider the inclusion of the marginalized groups and 3% out of the 10% sample size will be allocated to them when collecting data. This will also ensure their feedback and or complaint is included.

1. Needs

Protracted conflict and other emergencies such as famine and floods have continued to weaken the socio-economic foundation of South Sudan for years. These continued man-made and natural disasters have affected the populations to a point of no return, with women and children mostly affected, and the Government of South Sudan unable to provide basic protection services. Consequently, since the beginning of December 2013, grave human rights violations have been committed including but not limited to rape and other forms of sexual violence against women and children, abduction of children and denial of humanitarian assistance. As per GBV and Protection cluster reports, the population in need for GBV interventions have increased as of 2023 compared to 2022. This increment is arguably because of alarming reports returnees from Sudan reporting grave human violations and degradation caused by conflict in the area. GBV, abuse and exploitation remain the greatest protection risks to women and girls, due to a deeply patriarchal society which sustains gender inequalities and discrimination. Unfortunately, females aged 15-25 have experienced some forms of GBV, and shockingly cases are underreported by survivors due fear of stigma, re-victimization and reliance on traditional justice structures. Despite the emergence of gender equality legislation and increased women engagement in public and private spaces, men continue to lead power, and decision-making processes due to deeply entrenched patriarchal norms and rigid gender rules. Notably, the widespread acceptance of violence against women by both women and men, is indicative of its socialized tolerance. Intimate partner violence regularly comprises at least half of all the GBV incidents. The most pervasive risks that women and girls face, regardless of displacement status and contributing factors, is the prevalence of violence, rape culture, and perceptions of inferiority of women and girls. A staggering 82% of respondents said that they would report an incident of GBV, however, only 15% could specifically identify to whom or where they would report. Only 20% of at-risk women and girls have access to services related to GBV prevention and response. The provision of medical care, MHPSS services, safety, legal aid, and strengthening of referral pathways remain critical interventions for women and girls. Furthermore, there is a need to continuously monitor and analyse the situation and needs of the affected population, to inform responses and adhere to the ‘do-no-harm’ and protection principles. By the same token, there is also a high need to continuously advocate for improved access to services, particularly for PSNs, the most vulnerable and displaced persons, focusing on remote locations. Community Protection Committees, will be encouraged to include men and women with disabilities to determine accessibility of protection services. Similarly, volunteers will be tasked with the duty of championing for needs and safeguards of households, in which the caretaker is a person with disability. This project will put an emphasis on working closely with affected communities/groups to identify needs and empower them to become more resilient, based on their protection risks. Lastly. accountability to the targeted beneficiaries will be adhered to through appropriate information dissemination alongside beneficiary feedback sought during post-intervention monitoring. Nile Hope will ensure accountability and full participation of the affected population with formation of AAP committee that works with us throughout the implementation period. We shall actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.
