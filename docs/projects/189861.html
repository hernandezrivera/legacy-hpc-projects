Project Summary

The WASH Emergency Response in Afder Zone will target populations affected by a prolonged drought after three consecutive failed rainy seasons since late 2020 affecting 6.8 million people living in Oromia, SNNP, Southwest and Somali. An estimated 175,000 people have now been displaced from their homes due to the drought. The conditions continued to worsen with the successive failed seasons in 2021. The next season in March/April 2022 might also be well below normal, making it the fourth consecutive failed rainy season thus leading more people into an alarming situation. According to the Ethiopia HRP 2022 Cluster case load Across the targeted three woredas of (13,948 Barey, 11,378 Qohle, and 5,121 God-God of Afder Zone (drought) IDPs populations have been displaced while the 3,087 people are non-displaced in the three woredas who need humanitarian lifesaving A Total 33,534 are in need of WASH Emergency lifesaving support. With the following gender, age and disability dissegregate.
As in the document of the HRP 2022 location and activities prioritization Guide for the Clusters, the project will target 47,907 vulnerable IDP and host communities in 3 woredas of Afder Zone (Barey, Qohle and God-God) locations where ADRA used to implement multi-years’ food security programs and also Emergency projects of WASH and livelihoods Funded by ADRA’s internal Funds and UN-OCHA–EHF.
The main objective of the project will be to ensure vulnerable Drought displaced affected people have increased equal and sustained access to reliable safe water, appropriate sanitation and hygiene services. This will not only provide access to humanitarian assistance but also contribute to durable solutions.
To achieve this objective, ADRA will focus lifesaving interventions for 7 months with possible Cluster decision of flexibility to adjust in case of new life threatening crises occurs. This will be done through safe water rationing, water systems rehabilitation while ensuring proper testing and functionality before handover to the regional water bureaus and local communities, basic WASH NFIs and hygiene items like soaps, construction of gender sensitive communal latrines with hand washing facilities and promotion of adoption of positive hygiene practices and measures to prevent and also reduce possible disease outbreaks. To ensure smooth implementation and complementarity ADRA will closely coordinate with the WASH Cluster, regional and zonal authorities, and other operational partners in the targeted communities as the needs are massive and will require concerted efforts. Continuous monitoring will be undertaken in the implementation period to ensure all emerging trends are captured and reported to stakeholders. Community participation will be incorporated during the project design with women and children needs emphasis who mostly form a great percentage of the displaced populations. This approach will continue in the project implementation and lessons learnt incorporated in future responses.
In addition, ADRA will establish a protection committees involving men, women, a community leader, Religious leaders, a representative from the local authority and the police aimed to raise awareness on sexual violence and its impacts in the community; connect GBV and HIV; denounce any abuses of human rights; and share knowledge on how people could protect themselves.
Humanitarian crises increase the risk that GBV will occur—compounding the challenges already faced by people. Girls and women are disproportionally affected by WASH issues that arise during humanitarian emergencies. Forced to travel long distances to water points or latrines—with existing sanitation facilities often lacking privacy, lighting, or locks—they are at increased risk of sexual assault and harassment. Water scarcity can create tension in the home and heighten the possibility of domestic violence. and a shortage of culturally appropriate and accessible WASH supplies, services and other support.
