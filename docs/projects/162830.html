Project Summary

Project Site: 4 public primary schools in agricultural remote areas in White Nile
Direct Target: 2,000 students
Indirect Target: 60 teachers
Project Activities: school gardening setting and capacity building training

1 - Needs

Public primary schools in rural areas cannot provide with child protective activities (school meals, recreation activities, child friendly school management, etc.) due to their scarce budget. Although in Sudan primary education should be free, each school collects money from each student to cover the administrative expense. Especially in rural areas families cannot afford to pay for this school fee, then schools themselves are suffering from poverty. Government subsidies and breakthroughs for public primary schools have not been implemented and proposed, and generally it takes a considerable amount of time to take it into account and implement each measure, and it is hard to expect any improvement of this condition within five years. In Kosti and Rabak, White Nile, 35,000 children are in need of physical and mental wellbeing, and around 9,000 children are suffering from the low living standard, and public primary schools can be the central child protective place to improve children’s physical/psychological health and their living condition. School building repairs, supply of goods, or food distribution that depend on external support can be temporal solution, however it cannot be the school's own sustainable problem-solving strategy, and even there is a risk to reduce their motivation for active community participation. Although each public school considers their quality of education and facility as issues to tackle for children’s healthier development, they are reluctant to make an action due to lack of necessary funds to propose, plan and start up the method. In order to solve the school's poverty problem, and to manage and develop the community itself in the future, it is effective to introduce activities utilizing the resources of the local community even a small-scale. Any rural areas have community members (villagers) with practical knowledge and skills in agriculture and abandoned land not maintained by the schools. These schools and communities themselves can effectively use the environmental and human resources to improve their own self-efficacy and promote inclusive active participation regardless generation as aiming at systematic function of child protection in the communities. In solving problems, it is suitable for activities that can comprehensively set practical goals and educational (child developmental) goals, and it is effective to use independent and sustainable community resources through school gardens. Centering on local public facilities such as schools, the entire community can be involved, and by utilizing the resources that the local community has, the problem-solving model of the community that does not rely on external support can be shared with the residents. Rather than relying on external specialists or directly targeting beneficiaries with poor households, students or teachers, this approach encourages the participation of people with different attributes in the community.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental protection is effectively improved through planting trees, growing garden products and practicing activities after environmental education training in the schools.School garden establishment is based on effective environmental resource use. Planting/maintaining trees and growing fruit trees/vegetable provide with richer soil quality in the area and invite insect and birds. Capacity building training strengthens the culture of environmental protection through school gardening as getting interest in school environment, raising environmental awareness and understanding, respecting nature and learning the balance of natural resource use and protection.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project realizes Child Rights through the activities."Provision": To improve adequate standard of living, health care, education and recreation."Protection": To improve the child protective environment and system in communities as encouraging schools to fulfill the central child protective function."Participation": To improve children's participation in communities as decision makers and introduce programs and services for themselves. School garden establishment (designing of the garden, selecting fruits/vegetable to grow, recruiting volunteers to plant trees and sow seeds, etc.) and management (building gardening management team, selecting team members/leaders, coordinating gardening activities, etc.) are run by students’ opinion and participation under protective watching by teachers and community members. HT staffs encourage child-friendly culture in the schools and communities through practicing activities with all the stakeholders in the communities in order to accelerate their participation and cooperation to realize Child Rights.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in other venues/locations) through aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As any activities of HT is community-based, all staff regardless his/her position is encouraged to build reliable relation with community members (beneficiaries) by field visit and direct communication in order to conduct effective and/or sustainable program, project and/or activities. Feedback procedure is conducted by mainly experienced staffs based on this relation of mutual trust, and staffs under training assist them. The feedback mechanism consists of four phases; designing feedback system, applying the system, collecting feedback and sharing/analyzing feedback. Feedback mechanism is driven by demand from beneficiaries, request from donors, internal needs of project quality improvement, etc. Feedback is collected mainly by suggestion boxes and focus group meeting set in the community, hearing from beneficiaries or other appropriate methods built for each case. Collected feedback is shared and analyzed according to the each purpose.
