Project Summary

The conflict in Sudan, over nine months now, resulted severe deterioration in the protection environment and leaving almost the entire populations in dire humanitarian needs. This project contributes to achieving HRP SO1 ensuring provision of safe, timely, principled and gender-responsive life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable groups to reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with high severity of need or at risk of a further deterioration of critical humanitarian needs; SO2 to directly respond to the conflict-driven protection needs of crisis-affected people through principled and conflict-sensitive assistance and advocacy as well as prevent and mitigate emerging protection risks, according to international norms and standards.
WR protection interventions are founded on bodies of laws such as human rights, international humanitarian law and refugee law, and actions are guided by humanitarian principles, and international standards in humanitarian actions. WR ensures conflict sensitivity and inclusiveness using age, gender, disability and diversity approach to create and support a peaceful co-existence between IDPS, nondisplaced and host communities and provide case management, lifesaving services to persons with specific protection needs through provision of in kind or cash assistance and MHPSS to adults and timely response to mitigate protection risks, human rights violations and other forms of violence. WR will build relationships with and among affected populations, IDPs, nondisplaced and host communities with the goal of enabling them to identify and respond to their own protection needs through establishment and support of community-based protections mechanisms. With the support of the community-based structures and other key stakeholders, WR will engage in protection monitoring to inform timely response and prevent exposure of women, girls and other vulnerable populations to harm, as well as identify needs and gaps in service provision. WR will collaborate and enhance capacities of partners and authorities to prevent and mitigate risk of evictions of affected population.
WR will provide life-saving timely and quality GBV and child protection services, including case management of GBV survivors and vulnerable children particularly UASC and with disabilities, psychosocial support, legal assistance and reinforce the referral pathways. WR will support establishment of women centers and rehabilitate existing ones, while also provide running cost. To economically empower GBV survivors, they will be provided with livelihood opportunities and vocational skills. To ensure coordination, collaboration, advocacy and information sharing, WR with actively support state and national level protection, child protection and GBV working groups, conduct awareness among all stakeholders and ensuring participation of men and boys on the prevention and response to GBV risk including safety audits. WR will establish child friendly spaces where children will receive structured and sustained psychosocial support activities. Caregivers and partners will also receive MHPSS and training on child protection. WR will conduct awareness raising activities and information dissemination on child protection and PSEA to IDPs, host communities and nondisplaced communities.
WR will ensure conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all actions that will further expose the populations from harm and ensuring all staff, volunteers, contractors and workers are equipped with knowledge and adhere to the organizations policy on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as ensuring confidential and accessible feedback mechanism. WR will closely collaborate with protection clusters and sub-clusters, and partners for effective and impactful response and advocacy to enhance well-being of affected populations.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be at the center of the response, and all assistance will be planned and implemented so as to promote the safety, dignity, and rights of affected people. The Child-friendly space and the women-friendly spaces will be guided by the safety audit. In addition, the community will be consulted in the establishment of the center and later provide feedback on the ways the centers are safe for the community. WR will consult the Protection Cluster within in the specific area of intervention to have access to the Protection Analysis which identifies threats, vulnerable groups, and existing capacities. In addition, training will be provided to the child protection, women support committees and WR protection staff on protection mainstreaming. To create a proper understanding of identified threats, the most vulnerable groups, and the existing capacities of the affected populations as well as services and resources already available. This will help us to identify real and potential risks that may arise in protection programs. Will confirm the threats, vulnerabilities, and negative coping strategies identified in the Protection Analysis Report by consulting with the affected population of each area of intervention. In addition, through analysis, WR identifies with beneficiary’s preventive and mitigation measures with the objective of reducing the threats and the vulnerabilities and increasing the existing capacities. In addition, the community through the community-based protection structures will have full participation in the designing and implementation of the program. Furthermore, the community will have full participation including a decision-making role in the program. AGD approach will be used to ensure the inclusiveness of the program to all of the community in the area. Active and effective participation of affected people both in planning, response, and monitoring processes will be ensured through community discussion. WR will promote peace by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups and hiring diverse staff. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area, and international staff.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

WR will create a number of reporting mechanisms including a hotline, comment boxes, trained community volunteers, and mechanisms at project sites through which community members can report feedback and ensures lessons learned are adapted to improve delivery of services to affected populations. Through M&E team will evaluate and adapt interventions to mitigate SEA/GBV risks and ensure that SEA/GBVrisks are not being created or exacerbated through the implementation of response activities. WR complaints and feedback mechanisms so that they are appropriate and inclusive for different groups of the affected communities, feedback is analyzed to be used in adapting activities. WR will ensure that staff appropriately contextualize and share information with communities about the expected standards of staff behavior and commitments on PSEA, staff code of conduct, affected populations’ rights, and selection criteria of assistance implemented.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WR will strengthen the existing complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFMs) internal to the organization. In addition, through the Gender focal persons in each field office will ensure that there are safe and accessible reporting channels for survivors of sexual exploitation and abuse and link with UNFPA established interagency complaints mechanisms. Ensure personnel is informed on a regular basis on how to file a complaint/ report and the procedures for handling these. Ensure all service delivery points have clear information on how to prevent and report incidents of SEA. WR has integrated AAP and PSEA into regional and national capacity-building curricula. The protection team in collaboration with the gender focal persons will develop community-based and participatory methodologies and tools for interagency community consultation and systematic participation that allows for designing and/or adopting the response across all platforms. In addition, WR will ensure that these practices are harmonized and implemented in the different field offices of the response in a manner that recognizes the preferences of different groups of the affected population and with special emphasis on the action without harm.
