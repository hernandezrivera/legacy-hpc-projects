Project Summary

The primary target group were the new arrivals who have been screened and registered at various reception centers in Borno State. Mercy Corps provided emergency assistance to Using Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) as the assistance modality, providing 1 monthly tranche of cash transfer at the rate of $118 per household (NGN45,000) to enable newly displaced beneficiary households to meet 100% of their basic needs. MC used the MEB from the Cash Working Group, however it has revised the transfer value based on the increased Food basket. The cash assistance will target persons registered at the screening centers as head of households. In line with the recommendations of the Cash Working Group, “basic needs” is defined as: food, hygiene items (soap and reusable sanitary pads), water (purchased from water vendor), cooking fuel, transportation, education, communication, and rent. The transfer value is different for each location, as the prices in the different locations differ. However, Mercy Corps expects the average transfer value to be 45,000 NGN per household (US $118.4 per household). Under this intervention MC will target 2500 vulnerable displaced families (17,500 population). Mercy corps align the MEB with Cash Working Group standards – including information from all sectors. Mercy Corps also conducts bi-monthly price monitoring to ensure that the transfer value provide does indeed cover what is should.
The secondary target groups under MPCA will be those households that are not newly displaced, but facing assistance challenges due to any gap left from partner organization. The transfer value for such households will remain tailored (less than 100%) based on a quick needs assessment to identify the income vs expenditure gap and gap of assistance identified (food or non-food based basic needs) through partners on ground. The overarching intention of the transfers is to enable a household to cover 100% of their emergency basic needs – so the amount will be tailored so as to ensure that this 100% basic-needs threshold can be met.

1 - Needs

The project will address basic needs of newly displaced participants and also vulnerable host community meet their basic needs. The cash grant will also reduce negative coping strategy and improve food consumption score of both new arrivals and vulnerable household in host communities. The MPCA will increase household income security and standard of living among many poor households,

2 - Activities

The MPCA provides dignity and flexibility to vulnerable household to meet their needs. It increases economic and markets development in the locations program implementation. The used of cash/POS agent to deliver cash also increase capacity and attracts more players in terms finance service provider (such commercial banks) resume/start operation in the target location. The MPCA also provides safety and dignity of participants and minimizing protection concerns amongst participants.The cash disbursement is based on the recommendations provided in the recent Financial Service Providers Assessment, Mercy Corps engage Financial Services providers (electronic cash-out services) with a possible network of local cash out agents in remote sites for redemptions through e-transfers through a tendering process. The purpose of engaging these larger FSPs, which have wider outreach of local Cash out agents at LGA level, is to minimize the risk of physical cash transportation from main city to LGA level and strengthen the local financial services market. This will make implementation faster, convenient and traceable, and possibly ensure the financial inclusion of disadvantaged group. Once a participant redeems the voucher for cash, the FSP will receive electronic payment reimbursement from Mercy Corps. In case this does not work out owing to supply chain challenges with these electronic/digital FSPs then to distribute the cash Mercy Corps will continue to engage a cash-out agent (also through a tendering process).Mercy Corps will also be present, alongside the CRCs, in order to assist and to monitor the transaction process. Most of our target population does not have access to formal banking services.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Prioritizing safety and dignity of participants and minimizing harm: Mercy Corps will also work with Protection Master Trainers stationed in each community as a focal point, ensuring protection issues are monitored regularly and beneficiaries’ concerns are voiced. Mercy Corps will also ensure distribution cash redemption sites are set up in communities so that beneficiaries do not have to travel long distances to collect cash, thereby reducing the risk of attacks on women, men, girls and boys. Where it is safe to do so, Mercy Corps will utilize the IDP reception centers for the cash disbursement, though other safe locations may be considered as an alternative in order to safeguard the beneficiaries. Identified households will be registered using Mercy Corps’ registration tools, which includes biometric data. At point of identification until the finalization of participant’s engagement in the process, beneficiaries will be verbally sensitized on their rights and entitlements. Mercy Corps sets-up help desks at all program activities, allowing beneficiaries to report and have addressed any complaints they might have.
