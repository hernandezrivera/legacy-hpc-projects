Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will operate an integrated child protection program in Cox’s Bazar and Its aim is to strengthen protective factors and reduce risk factors at every level of the socio-ecological framework. All of the activities will be adhered to protection principle like Do No Harm, non-discrimination, protect from abuse and violence and ensure participation and access to rights. In 2022, through the partnership with four local NGOs, SC will maintain critical interventions and services including case management, adolescents, and PSS programming, with a focus on the most vulnerable children including UASC, child headed households, child marriage and pregnancy, children with disabilities, domestic violence, and child labor. Based on evidence and input from affected communities, interventions will seek to address the ongoing risks posed by COVID-19 and target vulnerable boys and girls and their families. Child participation and eliciting the views of boys and girls across age, ability, and protection concerns will be an integral part of designing, implementing, and monitoring program components. As earlier, SC will work to mainstream an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes for the interventions. For different age of children SC has different time slot in MPCAC, activities designed considering the age range of the children, materials procure and distribution with age sensitive specification and size. SC Volunteers are oriented to identify children with disability and prioritize them to include in child protection programming of SC. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) will be included into the plan of SC. The respective staffs of SC will represent in the PERU of respective camps. They will be trained and will be involved in different responses and awareness raising initiatives for community people like disseminating awareness raising messages, making the community people being alert before cyclone, ensuring safety security from any other natural disasters. SC will also continue to build the capacity of parents and caregivers in parenting without violence, as well as strengthen the community-based child protection mechanism in both camps and host communities, to create an enabling and protective environment for children. It will include scaling up capacity building of community-based groups and volunteers through training and coaching on CP concerns, increasing their engagement in prevention and response activities. In both Rohingya and host community, the engagement of influential leaders, parents/caregivers, and children will be continued to enhance the participation, ownership, and sustainability of the program. Through maintaining and establishing services in both refugee camps and host communities, critical gaps in service delivery will be addressed and social cohesion will be strengthened, by targeting inequity in access to services, which is a driver of conflict. In 2022, SC will emphasis on supporting the host community, especially with a focus on strengthening the CP systems. This will include partnerships to strengthen technical capacity and work closely and collectively with duty bearers and service providers in camps and host communities. SC will give importance to the protect from and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). SC will conduct periodical trainings, awareness sessions to the staffs, volunteers and other related stakeholder on PSEA policy. SC will also work to strengthen the coordination among different stakeholders to engage them in the child protection system to improve the accessibility of CP services. SC will continue coordination with CPSS, PWG in a regular basis and participate in different committees and working groups. Multiple accountability mechanisms will be applied in line with the AAP principles to ensure that feedback is actively collected, whilst also ensuring the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries.

1 - Identified Needs

More than four years since the influx, the Rohingya refugee crisis remains a protection emergency at its core. Children represent 52% of the Rohingya refugee population in Cox’s Bazar (UNHCR October 2021) and 53% in the host community. Prior to the displacement, Rohingya children experienced protection risks that were then further exacerbated by the humanitarian crisis that exposed them to physical violence, psychosocial trauma, sexual violence, forced labor, child marriage, and other forms of abuse and violence. The host community and refugee populations experience several shared protection challenges and risks. Both are vulnerable to the growing impacts of climate change and the number of disasters the district experiences, including monsoons, flooding, landslides, and fires. Children in both communities are struggling to cope with the current situation, which includes decreased access to services, stressors in the home caused by income loss, and increased anxiety.Child protection concerns also keep changing based on newly emerged crises, and it is critical that their needs are understood and met. According to J-MSNA (2021), child labor and children going missing were both reported to have increased in communities by 16% of households. Based on the child protection assessment findings of 2021, the most reported child protection concerns perceived by respondents as existing in the camps were child labour (64%), neglect (59%), child marriage (57%) and risks of trafficking (32%). These were followed closely by depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse of children (23%). Household poverty was the most given reason for the increase of child labor, and other reasons given were lack of access to education and sickness of elderly caregivers.Interestingly, caregivers and adolescents reported being against child marriage. However, an exception to this belief was made if the safety of the girl was believed to be at stake. The key causes of child marriage that emerged from the assessment included economic reasons, family pressure/force, and cultural acceptability. (CPSS Child Protection Assessment-2021).At the same time, secondary data indicates a general increasing of protection issues in camps, especially in crime and theft, as well as an increased presence of criminal groups with heightened levels of control. Households further reported a number of protection related incidents, most commonly robbery, but also kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment or fear of sexual harassment preventing especially adolescent girls from moving freely, unofficial demands for rent payment and resulting fear of eviction, threats by local people or armed groups, demanding money from Mahjees and/or using violence against those reporting issues, challenges related to registration, resulting in difficulties accessing assistance. At the same time, the limited presence of protection actors in camps in line with COVID-19 containment measures, including child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) actors, posed challenges in monitoring activities and effectively referring people. (J-MSNA 2021)Inevitably, the gender implications of these risks vary for girls and boys. For girls, girls have increasingly resorted to staying at home for fear of sexual harassment (REACH, May 2020). While staying in their shelter is cited as a coping mechanism, 38% of adolescents reported that they were concerned about feeling unsafe in their shelter.Although the long-term impacts of both the disease and the implications of COVID-19 are unknown, assessments point to a grim situation for children with significant long-lasting impacts on their development and wellbeing. Now, more than ever, there is a critical and urgent need for life-saving child protection services in both the host community and refugee populations.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Comprehensive case management service for the children and adolescents at risk considering their age, gender, disability, and diversity. Activities include support to alternative care arrangements for UASC (including foster families), basic counselling support to children and their families, provision of family tracing and reunification service, and referrals to the needed services. Activity 2: Support the parents and caregivers to create a safe and violence-free home environment and address the unique risks faced due to COVID-19 through 1) the Parenting without Violence program; 2) training and sessions supporting foster caregivers, 3) peer support in mothers’ and fathers’ groups, and 4) through the provision of self-reliance opportunities.Activity3: Curriculum-based, structured, semi structured and focused psychosocial support for children, including children with disabilities, will be provided through multi-purpose centers for adolescents and children (MPCAC). Activity4: Targeted adolescent girls and boys, including disabilities will be identified and engaged in gender specific life skills curriculums, resilience sessions, MHM, SRHR and protective behavior sessionsActivity5: Community led initiatives: Continue to support a network of community-based child protection committees (adults with adolescent representatives), adolescent boys' and girls' clubs, and children’s clubs in and around our protection spaces and targeted host communities and support them to initiate and deliver their own initiatives.Activity6: Child-friendly DRR messages and awareness-raising will be mainstreamed; community-led responses to family separation will be strengthened and staff/volunteers will be trained to activate a safe and timely response during an emergency. Activity7: Capacity building of law enforcement, CiCs, military and border officials, and key stakeholders on child rights and child protection (during and after COVID-19)Activity 8: Awareness raising activities among the children, caregivers and community people on key child protection issues, and mitigation. "Activity9: Children are engaged in project design through FGDs and participatory approaches; the findings of assessments and consultations are shared, and reasons are given for decisions made. SCI have maintained child friendly F &C mechanism (in addition to a toll-free hotline, helpdesks, voice recorders, group discussions, and door to door collection of feedback and complaints).Mandatory training of staff/volunteers/partners, on PSEA and CSG. "Activity 10: Establish and/or support Child protection community-level child protection mechanisms / structures led by women and girlsActivity11: Capacity building of Para Social Worker, Child Protection volunteer and CBCPC members on child protection focusing on identification of child protection concerns, and maintain appropriate safe referrals when needed. Activity 12: Distribute Menstrual health management (MHM) and dignity kits to adolescent girls and female caregivers. Activity 13: SCI will take several initiatives for child protection system strengthening which includes 1) coach and mentor DSS social workers on best practices in GBV/CP case management and supportive supervision; 2) provide technical support to enable Child Friendly Police Desk; 3) coordination with Child welfare board and police personnel to ensure justice for children in conflict or contact with the law in host communities;
