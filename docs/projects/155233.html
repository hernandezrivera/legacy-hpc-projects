Project Summary

The project ensures lifesaving services delivered through 20 WFS, including case management, clinical management of rape, family planning, STI prevention and response, psychosocial support and access to services through functional referral pathways, with continuous service evaluation through Client Satisfaction surveys. It ensures Advocacy and engagement with communities, including men and boys for GBV prevention and response. It also Ensure GBV mainstreaming in all 20 UNFPA-supported Health Facilities, including direct GBV service linkage, outreach and information dissemination about available services for women and girls, and safe and ethical referrals of GBV survivors by health staff. 10 UNFPA-supported Women Led Community Centres (WLCC), providing life-skills training, psychosocial support and a community mobilization intervention for the prevention of GBV, through the engagement of men and boys and empowerment of women and girls, with an ongoing process of consultation with participants to assess and improve WLCC classes and services. It Continue training GBV service providers on GBV case management and psychosocial support services, and ensure specialized MHPSS mentoring and supervision for case management service provision. Train health personnel on GBV guiding principles and referral pathways. Promote sustainability by working with local IPs and community volunteers to build their capacity for GBV prevention, response and referrals. Engage in capacity development opportunities for national governmental institutions. Include specific information on do no harm that is tailored to training types.

Objectives

SO1: Monitor and advocate for access to territory, prevention of refoulement and promotion of respect for refugees’ rights, enhancing continued registration and documentation of all refugee women, men, girls and boysSO2: Promote a community- based approach to the response and provide protection services to persons at heightened risk (including girls, boys, men and women of all ages and diversity determinants)SO3: Support the protective system strengthening, by promoting peaceful coexistence and enhancing social cohesion within and between refugee and host communities

Needs

•Refugees and host communities, especially women and girls are being disproportionately affected by forms and patterns of GBV including rape, conflict-related sexual violence, intimate partner violence, sex trafficking and commercial sexual exploitation, child and forced marriages, sexual exploitation, survival sex, and forced engagement in drug and sex trade. The cur rent response calls for high quality case management services, that offer appropriate and trained MHPSS interventions.•There are significant safety and security concerns for women and girls in the camps due to the challenges they face accessing water points, latrines, bathing areas and safe spaces. Women and girls lack adequate clothing and personal dignity items, highly restricting their mobility including during menstruation.•Increased overcrowding and limited privacy across all refugee sites are elevating safety and security risks for women and girls. Many Rohingya refugee family units are female-headed (up to one in five), exhibiting even greater vulnerability. The burden of care women assume for children and others can make it difficult for them to take proper care of themselves, women often neglect their own needs as they care for their families and neighbours. There is a need for safe women-centered healing spaces that are accessible to all women and girls, especially including the most vulnerable. •Lack of community understanding of causes and consequences of GBV highlights the need for specialized engagement, targeted to specific community groups and levels. •There are lack of opportunities and capacity building activities around existing skills among the community, especially for women and girls. Women's empowerment and life-skills training, linked with awareness raising campaigns engaging and sensitizing men on the benefits of allowing women to participate, are highly needed.

Activities or outputs

1. Ensure continuity of the 19 UNFPA-supported WFS and finalize the set up of one more WFS. Seek opportunities to expand use of WFS to foster social cohesion through mixed host/refugee beneficiaries as is current at some WFS locations. 2. Quality assurance of all lifesaving services delivered through the WFS, including case management, clinical management of rape, family planning, STI prevention and response, psychosocial support and access to services through funtional referral pathways, with continuous service evaluation through Client Satisfaction surveys. 3. Ensure GBV mainstreaming in all 20 UNFPA-supported Health Facilities, including direct GBV service linkage, outreach and information dissemination about available services for women and girls, and safe and ethical referrals of GBV survivors by health staff. 4. Continue training GBV service providers on GBV case management and psychosocial support services, and ensure specialized MHPSS mentoring and supervision for case management service provision. Train health personnel on GBV guiding principles and referral pathways. Promote sustainability by working with local IPs and community volunteers to build their capacity for GBV prevention, response and referrals. Engage in capacity development opportunities for national governmental institutions. Include specific information on do no harm that is tailored to training type. Extend UNFPA's internal PSEA policy to IPs and provide training and support to equip IPs to properly address PSEA using esablished guidelines, as well as to protect survivors of PSEA. 5. Advocacy and engagement with communities, including men and boys for GBV prevention and response, including appropriate referrals for male survivors. 6. Distribute Dignity Kits and activate Mobile Case Management services, as an emergency response following natural distasters on affected communities. 7. Set up and operationalize 10 UNFPA-supported Women Led Community Centres (WLCC), providing life-skills training, psychosocial support and a community mobilization intervention for the prevention of GBV, through the engagement of men and boys and empowerment of women and girls, with an ongoing process of consultation with participants to assess and improve WLCC classes and services. 8. Maintain strong intersectoral coordination as well as community level coordination. UNFPA leads GBVSS, GBViMS, GBV Case management Task Force, and the Dignity Kits taskforce, in addition to active participation in PWG, GiHA, SRH working group, MHPSS WG , CWC working group , and PSEA network. UNFPA also ensures dedicated staff for Ukhiya based GBVSS representation, and its partner has dedicated staffs in PERU team and as well as camp level focal persons for GBVSS.
