Project Summary

Goal: Contribute to enhance resilience of vulnerable hurricane Mathew affected households in the Grand-Anse.
Objective: Reduce shelter related vulnerability of the hurricane affected population in the municipalities of Jeremie, Abricot and Roseaux through access to safe, healthy, and appropriate living spaces, and safe building skills.
Results: R1: Vulnerable families from the rural areas in Grand-Anse have increased access to adequate and disaster resilient housing. Result 2: knowledge and competences of community members and Construction professionals from Grand-Anse on safe and disaster resilient constructions is Increased. Under this result, the project will respond to housing need of most vulnerable families through the repair of damaged houses and the construction of new chore houses for the families whose house were destroyed. Under this result HFHH will also work with family to increase their understand about safe construction method and therefore increase their and community resilience to future shocks such as Hurricane, earthquake and flooding’s. The selection criteria to identify the most vulnerable ones will be guided by (5) majors’ phases: 1. Mapping of most affected localities (number of houses damaged or destroyed) – 2. Census of households -3. Preselection – 4. Community validation – 5. Final verification 6. Publication. Based on the data collected on the households related to their socio-demographic status, assets and income, HFHH will rank the most vulnerable families affected by combining, Technical assessment, qualitative and socio-economic criteria. Key selection criteria are as follow: Head of family/member Handicap; Woman headed family; Minor headed family; Large family more than 5 people; Breastfeeding family leader; Pregnant women Elderly people without support; Family income. The assessment will be guided by the approved Template by UCLBP
The draft list will be submitted to both Mayor office and community representative for validation. After local validation, Habitat for Humanity Haiti will submit the full beneficiary’s dossier including the assessment, BOQs and other relevant documents for UCLBP approval.
To ensure transparency in the project implementation as well as active participation of community in the monitoring, a complaint Mechanism will be established where beneficiaries and other community member can report their concern in regard to the project implementation. . Cross-cutting issues: Protection / (do no harm): HFHH will place emphasis on community engagement and participation to minimize potential tensions.
Result 2: knowledge and competences of community members and Construction professionals from Grand-Anse on safe and disaster resilient constructions is Increased: Under this result, HFHH will implement action to improve the skills and competencies of construction professional on safe and resilient construction and therefore promote safer construction culture in the project intervention areas. Theoretical and practical training will be giving to Masons and carpenters. Trainings will be facilitated by HFHH engineers. The trained construction professionals who successful complete the training program and pass the final test, will be used in the construction and repairs of household under this project. the list of trained masons and carpenters will also be shared with Mayor offices and community representatives who can refer them to community members in case of house construction/ repair need. Additionally, the list will be shared with UCLBP and shelter working group
Target: the project will target 350 most vulnerable household affected by Hurricane Mathew in Grand-Anse. (250 new construction and 100 house repairs. The beneficiaries will be selected based on transparent and participative process guided by Humanitarian standards

1 - Needs

Haiti remains the poorest country in the Americas with 59% of the Haitian population living under the national poverty line of US$ 2.42 per day. Haiti is located in the hurricane corridor, and annually exposed to a long hurricane period (June to November), and subsequently exposed to high risks of recurrent natural disasters. On October 2016 the category 5 hurricane Matthew hit the southwestern part of Haiti, leaving a death toll of 546. 2.1 million persons were affected by hurricane Matthew, and it nearly or destroyed around 200,000 homes. Overall damage and losses related to the hurricane are estimated at US $ 2.8 billion. With at least 75 percent of the affected population relying on agriculture, livestock and fishery for their livelihoods in the affected department (FAO), the effects of Hurricane Matthew left the households with no or limited means for income generation or crops production to be able to repair or reconstruct their home. Amongst the 2.1 million people who were affected, nearly 1.4 million still need some type of humanitarian assistance in various sectors, particularly shelters. One year after the hurricane, durables repairs have been providing to an estimated of 20.500 HH covering 30% of the need at national Level and 3.500 HH benefitted of a new construction out the 103.000 identified by the PDNA (3%of the estimated need) The communes of Jeremie, Abricots and Roseaux are located in the department of Grand’Anse, which has been severely affected by the hurricane Matthew and where 80% of the population were in need of humanitarian aid. over 6000 houses (UNITAR-UNOSAT ) have been severely damaged by the hurricane Matthew. Needs assessments highlight shelter as one of the key priorities needs of the hurricane Matthew affected households in these 3 communes, which had severe impacts on housing in both municipalities. Preliminary assessment data indicates that damage caused by the Hurricane combined with existing challenges such as low income, and limited resources has resulted in drastically reducing households’ physical protection and further exacerbated the likelihood of exposure to weather conditions such as cold temperatures, rain and storms and mosquito born-diseases, and outbreak of diseases. This problem affects particularly the most vulnerable ones, including young children, pregnant women, elderly, people living with disabilities or serious specific medical needs. The assessment of the need has been conducted through a combination of methods including desk review, individual interview with potential beneficiaries and local authorities and observation. HFHH and CESVI are working in Grand-Anse to help families rebuilding their home. The project team regularly meet with the local authorities and the community members on project activities and challenges related to housing in their communities. Need assessment also took into consideration the results of the housing assessments from HFHH, OCHA’s Situation reports and the PDNA.

2 - Activities

Activities: R1 Land assessment: to mitigate the risk of future housing problems related to flooding, land erosion and hurricane, Habitat for humanity Haiti will conduct a thorough analysis of land appropriateness for safe and disaster resilient construction Construction and repairs of houses: Homeowner-driven construction approach will be used for constructions following TCLA model, the project will support 1290 Households from Grand-Anse to build new Core house of 27.88 m2, comprised of a bed room, a multi-functional room, a veranda, rain harvesting system and a toilet. Home owners will be involved and take the lead in the construction process to define the locations for construction, design and technical characteristics, selection of the local trained masons, negotiations of the labor cost, supervision of the work of the masons etc. each core house will cost as follow: Construction Materials funded by the project 3,048.88 and Beneficiary contribution = 384,26 USD a total of 3,469.14 USD. Labor funded by the project 912.43 USD and beneficiary contribution 35.74. total house cost to be funded by the project is 3,997.03 USD. The repairs of the damaged houses are estimated at 100 dollars per house . Supervision of houses’ Constructions: To ensure strict application of technical guidelines, HFHH engineers will be based in the community to guide the households and trained local masons in safe construction process to ensure houses fully meet safety and security standards and they are disaster resilient Activities: R2 Safe construction Training: to fill up the existing skills gap on safe construction, the project will support to increase local technicians’ construction skills and knowledge on construction techniques, incorporating disaster risk reduction practices, while respecting local/traditional norms (and culture). Technical Construction training for masons will be conducted through a mix of theoretical and practical sessions, facilitated by HFHH civil engineers. Safer construction awareness campaign: Building prevention culture and resources represent the starting point to develop vulnerability reduction strategies strongly rooted into local conditions and practices. Housing construction in rural areas of Haiti is by default owner-driven combined with the use of local masons. door-to-door awareness as well as community gathering approach will be used. Coordination and Collaboration with local authorities and Shelter working group: HFHH will continue attending the shelter working group meetings and shared lessons learned with other shelter focused actors. The project will also work in close collaboration with local authorities will jointly with habitat propose the list of potential beneficiary communities and households based upon which Habitat will conduct the need assessment to identify the final project beneficiaries validated by community-based structures and local authorities. Habitat for Humanity Haiti will closely coordinate with UCLBP. To ensure UCLBP oversight of the project, a full package with all beneficiaries’ information, including assessments, BOQs and other relevant data will be shared with UCLBP for approval. Joint visit between Habitat and UCLBP team be organized to the project sites Duration: 12 months
