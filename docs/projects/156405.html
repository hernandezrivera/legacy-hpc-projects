Project Summary

Contributing to the reduction of morbidity and mortality, and improve overall health services for conflict-affected Syrians. The intervention will focus mainly on provision and improvement of primary health care services, secondary health care and specialized services.
The scope of QC 2019 is to support the core health services represented by the primary health care services (the plan is to support 19 PHCs so far in line with the essential health package, these centers are distributed between Aleppo, Idlib and Hama)
Operating four specialized hospitals (two surgical and two gynecological and pediatric) as follows: 2 surgical and 1 women's hospital in Aleppo, 1 women's hospital in Idleb.
All projects are planned through self-implementation by QC field teams in target areas; partners may be involved only in exceptional circumstances and will be decided as and when required.
So far $3M USD are secured from different sources. This fund of $3M will be utilized for the procurement of drugs and consumables AND for fully supporting 4 PHCs (salaries, operations, drugs and consumables) for one year.

Needs

According to the 2019 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 13.2 million people inside Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance (including 1.8 million in acute need). The conflict induced damage of infrastructure and depletion of human resources inside Syria, resulting in poor quality of primary and secondary health care, including reproductive health, and mental health support services for the conflict affected and displaced communities within Syria’s borders. Destruction to infrastructure, including roads, water pipelines, and electricity networks, continues to isolate many populations in Syria and reduce their access to clean water and adequate sanitation. Closely related to the deterioration of preventive care, the increase in instances of communicable disease continues to be a major concern. There is an ongoing need for timely and effective interventions that respond to the direct costs and impacts of war, and that shore up existing capacity among the civilian population. QC intervention and because of its comprehensive nature, will target the whole community groups including (Women, girls, men and boys) with different ages. The assessment conducted by QC through contacts with local health directorate, communities and other stakeholders indicated the need for continuity of the support to health facilities by staffing, provision of medical supplies and equipment and improvement of the facilities' WASH systems and covering the running costs. Need assessment conducted in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates for QC health projects indicated that drugs is a priority in 68.8% of total facilities followed by medical equipment with around 18.8% while medical consumables come at least with around 12.5% of total. The inadequacy of health services in Northern Syria expose women to risks of morbidity and mortality due to lack of maternal health services, including the ANC, safe delivery and BEmOC services. The assessment targeted the whole community (women, men, girls and boys) with different ages because the services will be provided for all.

Implementation of activities

• Project Key activities are:- Increasing access to primary health care through provision of the Essential Package of Primary Health Services, including reproductive health services and child health care (both for boys and girls)- Rehabilitation and reinforcing health facilities, including physical structure, equipment/supplies to provide safe and secure environments for health service delivery for men, women, boys and girls.- Procurement and supply of medicines, consumables and medical equipment for trauma, obstetric care for women, infectious diseases, and chronic conditions and strengthen the medical supply chains, including emergency supply stocks at districts warehouses and health facility levels.- Increasing access to treatment for people who live with non-communicable disease (NCD)- Training of staff on key essential services such as: management and primary and secondary prevention of non-communicable diseases, treatment of NCDs, basic surgical and obstetric care, task shifting of basic services to staff to provide care in their home communities when mobility restrictions or health facility destruction occur.- Cover the cost of salaries of medical staff and provide incentives to attract and retain qualified medical staff from both sexes (males and females)- Support Health information systems (HIS) at the health facility levels to track morbidity and mortality data for males and females and age specific, including epidemiological surveillance and capacity to detect, investigate, communicate and contain threats to public health security.- Provide running costs of the hospitals and health centers- Monitoring and evaluation of the health program activities QC has a MEAL system in place both at country level, Gaziantep and inside Syria to monitor and evaluate the planned activities. In addition to the health facility based information that will be collected as part of the HIS, QC will organize regular submission of 4Ws reports to cluster, internal reporting on monthly and quarterly basis through its field teams. - Monitoring and evaluation of project activities will take special consideration to seek feedback from the beneficiaries and other stakeholders. QC will report to the cluster at Gaziantep level on the implementation of the health project activities using the recommended reporting format. - QC will ensure accountability to the target communities through strengthened complaint response mechanism and will document and share lessons learned at cluster and other relevant stakeholders

Protection Risk Analysis

The Whole of Syria (WoS) Health sector conducted a review to identify the most pressing health related protection risks facing healthcare providers and the affected population. A set of recommended mitigating measures was identified to guide health agencies and operational partners during the process of program design of the 2019 Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC). * Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:The attacks resulted in 97 fatalities (of which 17 health care providers and 11 patients), and 169 injuries (of which 44 health care providers and 41 patients).Mitigating measures:- Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities.- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare. * Gender Based Violence GBV:Mitigating measures:- Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. * Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:Mitigating measures:- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. * Duty of care:Mitigating measures:- Staff Care programs which provide psychosocial support services and resources to staff.- Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights. * Provision of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services:Mitigating measures:- Implementation of standard essential services package across the sector interventions, including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs.- Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated health subgroups groups (trauma, physical and mental rehabilitation, sexual and reproductive health, etc.).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks (floods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happen in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff, will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff and schools’ principals. In this intervention, QC assume that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. 1) The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide sufficient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm.* These can be mitigated by:- putting in place task shifting guidelines as part of care protocols- providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency- decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care2) Increased morbidity and mortality rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention.* Mitigating measure:- Hire a qualified medical staff- Distribute medical supplies and consumables.- Choose a safe hospital (underground, fortified). After Sushi treaty between Russia and Turkey, QC assumes that areas out of Syrian government control in north-west of Syria will be stable for the whole next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs some areas suddenly increases.
