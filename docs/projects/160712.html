Project Summary

The signing of the “Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan,” in October last year has led to relative peace in formerly insecure areas in the country. This has prompted a continuous inflow of voluntary returnees to their areas of origin. A regional publication puts the returnees at 140, 000 by March this year. (Kamoga. J. 140,000 South Sudanese refugees return home. The East African. Nairobi. Kenya). The paper cites South Sudan’s Ministry for Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management as saying the returnees as coming from Uganda, Ethiopia, Central African Republic and Sudan. MTT has continued to provide risk education to returnees, host communities and IDPs in Twic East, Melut, Renk and Canal counties. Our project in Renk and Melut in which we partnered with UNICEF ended in May 2019. With the onset of returnees, much remains to be done. This project targets communities in Twic East, Canal and Melut counties. Communities will actively participate in the MRE activities. These include awareness-raising on the risks posed by explosive remnants of war (ERW); how to avoid them and report all suspicious objects. As the returnees continue to settle back into their previous homesteads, they are mostly unaware of the risks posed. Using our developed and proven primary and secondary approaches, MRE teams will disseminate awareness messages to most-at-risk members, disaggregated by gender, age and (dis)abilities. Primary approach refers to raising awareness messages through direct presentations; while secondary approach refers to using community members able to pass on the same messages to others in a simplified form. The influential community members include local authorities, religious leaders, teachers and peer group representatives. They will reach out to other members of the population who are unable to be reached directly by MTT teams. As carriers, they will own of the messages – thus add an element of sustainability at the end of the project. This project will increase the capacity of the communities to identify and report any suspected hazardous areas and share the information with the NMAA and UNMAS. To ensure an integrated approach addressing the priority needs of targeted people, our team will coordinate with other sectors and sub sectors such as Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health and GBV, Child Protection as they deliver other key life-saving messages. The aim is to reach a majority of the population with key MRE information. have any questions.

1 - Needs

The signing of the “Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan,” in October last year has led to relative peace in formerly insecure areas in the country. This has prompted a continuous inflow of voluntary returnees to their areas of origin. Returnees are now coming to a land heavily infested with UXOs and ERWs. The returnees continue to settle back into their previous homesteads, mostly unaware of the risks posed. There is need for awareness-raising on the risks posed by explosive remnants of war (ERW); how to avoid them and report all suspicious objects. The teams will focus on reaching target groups with key EORE messages. Vulnerable groups will be identified and be provided with direct presentations and other participatory activities. The activities aim to promote safe behavior to populations living in or close to contaminated areas or other suspected hazardous areas. Secondly, most influential stakeholders, regarded as very close to their population will be approached, trained and will become agents to disseminate careful behavior in their communities. These are mainly religious leaders, teachers, group leaders or representatives. Other humanitarian workers will also be alerted about the risks of MRE in their geographical areas of operation. Expected to become agents of change, they will also be provided with receive clear messages of what, where and how to avoid risks. Our current project in Twic East and Canal/Pigi ends in February 2019. This project targets 56,160 returnees, hosts and IDPs disaggregated as follows: In Twic East, 6-12 years (3,640 Girls, Boys 3,640), 13-17 (4,460 Girls, Boys 3,954), 18-60 (5,561Women, 2,430Men), Over 60 and disabled (600 men and 779 women). 9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

EORE will draw attention of host community, IDPs and returnees to be observant to signs in the environment such as dead animals and unusual objects. Influential community members such as local authorities, teachers, religious leaders and gender peer groups will pass contextualized information to the most vulnerable categories of the community such as children, women, the old, and people with disability (PWD). Their attention will be drawn to ground signs that indicate the presence of EOREs. The colour of vegetation that shows patches of dried plants. In sandy areas the soil showing patches not typical soil formation. If a minefield has been in existence for some time, there might be visible pattern of depression over the mine where the once loosened soil has settled after rainfall. Returnees constructing their new habitats, eager to find scrap metal like barbed wires for fencing off domestic or wild animals will be warned of the risks as these may be dangerous places where EOREs were concealed. The project will instruct the target group to avoid occupying houses abandoned by soldiers where mines or booby traps may be left behind. They should avoid; a log or branch placed across a road or path (possible ambush), greener patch in a dry land. They should avoid unused or overgrown paths, roads or fields. Pastoralists will be told to avoid getting close to animal carcasses or skeletons. Opinion leaders will reach out to other members of the population who are unable to be reached directly by EORE teams. As carriers, they will own of the messages – thus add an element of sustainability at the end of the project.
