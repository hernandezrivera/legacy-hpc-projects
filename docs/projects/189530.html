Project Summary

Islamic Relief Worldwide is a humanitarian organization has been working in South Sudan since 2004 and providing humanitarian support for the flood drought and conflict affected people in need including provision of WASH services at Warrap state (Tonj north county) Central equatoria (Kajokeji and Yei counties) Eastern equatoria (Kapoeta East and Kapoeta north counties) Islamic Relief South Sudan (IRSS) has developed “Enhancing resilience for equitable WASH services for IDPs, returnees and host communities project to respond and increase resilience capacity of the flood, drought and conflict affected communities to achieve the following objectives:
(i) increase access to clean and safe water;
(ii) Improve the access to safe and dignified sanitation services;
(iii) Increase access to improved hygiene services and products;
The proposed action intended to provide lifesaving inclusive WASH services to 111, 606 people in need in Warrap state (Tonj north counties) Central equatoria (Kajokeji and Yei counties) Eastern equatoria (Kapoeta East and Kapoeta north counties). This project is anticipated to positively impact on the lives of people through increasing access to improved water supplies systems/facilities, restoring dignity in women and girls through provision of gender sensitive sanitation infrastructure and hygiene services/products for women and girls, boys and men, elderly people, people with disability including people suffering chronic illnesses.
To achieve the project objectives, Islamic Relief will be focusing on the Right Based Approach linked with the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) approach to address the pressing WASH needs and gender/protection related concerns in order to minimize or mitigate the common causes of vulnerabilities.
This project strives to provide timely and equitable access to clean and safe water supply, gender and vulnerability sensitive water supply and sanitation infrastructure and hygiene services/products through construction of new water facilities/sources (Hand pump boreholes, solarized water yards, water dams, and surface water improvement. Conduct rehabilitation activities for the nonfunctional/defunct water sources/schemes, and sanitation facilities such as institutional latrines, incinerators, hand washing facilities. The project shall as well integrate the aspect community capacity building strategy through awareness campaigns, formation and training of Community based WASH structures such as; Hygiene promoters, leaders, Water Management Committees (WMC) and Hand Pump Mechanics to improve on operation and maintenance for sustainability purposes.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian situation in South Sudan continuous to deteriorate due to endemic violence, conflicts, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in to the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and health services including the proposed project intervention areas. Peoples access to safe and improved water and sanitation is low. Communities reported issues in accessing WASH facilities, including long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tension. Women and girls frequently faces physical and sexual assault and harassments at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Lack of latrines and/ or overfull latrines leads to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) South Sudan 2022, approximately 41% of the total population have access to safe water while 10% have access to improved sanitation. 61% of the population practice open defecation. The impact of floods, linked to the effect of climate change, and high level of food insecurity has increased water and sanitation needs in Tonj north county, Kapoeta east and north, yei and Kajokeji counties respectively.The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding (2021) contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities across the country particularly counties such as Tonj North in Warrap are the most affected with estimated number of 161 boreholes destroyed, leaving vulnerable people in an urgent need of Water Sanitation and Hygiene services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people, which include high poverty rates, limited access to basic WASH services. The WASH Severity Classification (WSC) conducted in 2021 classified three counties in phase 5 which includes; Tonj, Maiwut and Pibor. An estimated population of more than 5.9 million people (half of the country’s population) continues to suffer due to inadequate access to clean water and sanitation and poor hygiene practices in 2021. This population continuous to lack access to essential WASH services such as clean water supply and provision of valuable WASH NFIs, dignity/MHM kits, and Household latrine construction materials) remain in a dire need of WASH services across the country.According to the needs assessment report (2021) conducted by Islamic Relief, open defecation stands at Kajo Keji 45%, Warrap (Tonj North) 42%, & and Yei 34% respectively. Therefore, the limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and handwashing locations in the public places and at households increases threats of water borne diseases. In the IDP and returnees’ settings, for example Tonj North in Warrap, Kapoeta North in Eastern Equatoria, WASH needs remains very high due to limited access to hygiene and sanitation facilities including WASH NFIs such as; jerry cans, washing basins, soaps, water treatment tabs and are at higher risk of diseases outbreaks in the congested conditions

2 - Project activities

This project intends to provide timely and equitable access to safe and clean water supply, safe sanitation and improved hygiene services to 111,606 vulnerable people affected by the conflicts, flooding and drought in the targeted project intervention locations which includes IDPs, returnees and host communities. Cognizant of the needs highlighted, IRSS will undertake the following activities:- Drilling of 26 new boreholes installed with Indian MKII/III hand pumps (10 Tonj North, 03 Kapoeta North, 5 Kapoeta East, 04 Kajokeji and 4 Yei).- Rehabilitation of 03 water pans in most difficult areas in eastern equatoria (Kapoeta east 02, Kapoeta North 01).- Rehabilitation of 50 nonfunctional boreholes (20 Tonj North, 10 Kapoeta North, 08 Kapoeta East, 06 Kajokeji and 6 Yei).- Rehabilitation of 03 defunct water yards (01 Tonj North, 1 Kajokeji and 1 Kapoeta North).- Construction of 8 blocks institutional latrines in health facilities (2 in Yei) and schools (4 blocks in Tonj, 02 blocks Kapoeta east) piloting 01 biogas latrine to support St. Bakita boarding secondary and school.- Pilot construction of 4 fossa alterna latrine (alternating latrines) for households in Yei. - Training of 85 water management committee (WMCs) with 30% women representation for proper operation and maintenance of the various water sources (33 Tonj North, 17 Kapoeta North, 13 Kapoeta East, 10Kajokeji and 10 Yei)- Training of 70 community based hygiene promoters 20 Tonj North, 20 Kapoeta North, 10 Kapoeta East, 10 Kajokeji and 10 Yei) - Training of 55 community hand pump mechanics 15 Tonj North, 10 Kapoeta North, 10 Kapoeta East, 10 Kajokeji and 10 Yei) - Provision of 7,000 kit of WASH NFIs to 6,120 HHs.- Provision of 8,000 dignity kits (MHM kits) to the most vulnerable women and girls. - Conduct 25 CLTS triggering sessions across the project intervention areas.- Distribution of 225 sets of latrine digging materials (45 Tonj North, 45 Kapoeta North, 45 Kapoeta East, 45 Kajokeji and 45 Yei)- 10 sessions on GBV mitigation and prevention will be conducted across all targeted areas.- Conduct GBV risk analysis in all target areas - 34 sessions of hygiene promotion, awareness and campaigns will be conducted in public places, health facilities, nutrition centers and schools.- Conduct one (01) KAP survey across the project intervention areas.

The following measures will be taken to mitigate the risks: (i)Regular follow-up of the security information with the UN and International NGOSafety Organizations, NGO Forum, UN OCHA regularly; (ii) Follow-up with the SecurityFocal Point Persons of the Islamic Relief South Sudan Juba office and Field Offices;(iv) Revision of Risk Mitigation Plan regularly in consultation with the UN OCHA andUN Clusters.
