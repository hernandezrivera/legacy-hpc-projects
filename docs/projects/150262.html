Project Summary

Project’s Multiyear Objective: Build safe, protective, inclusive and enabling learning environments, including during winter and summer, in order to improve the accessibility of school and preschool learning provided to particularly vulnerable 5 to 12 years old girls and boys in the Gaza Strip.
2018 2020 HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
Education Cluster Objective 1: Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children.
Education Cluster activities: Provide essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are safe for students; provision of emergency education supplies to vulnerable and unserved students and teachers; support students with disabilities with adaptations to school infrastructure.

Needs

The recurrent outbreaks of violent conflicts and ongoing blockade continue to pose a serious threat to life, liberty and security of affected all480,013basic, secondary and kindergarten students in the Gaza Strip. As per HNO2019, armed conflict, military incursions and violent manifestations like Great March of Return and the resulting damage and destruction of facilities and property(including use for IDPshelters) disrupted educational services and impacted psychosocial wellbeing of children and teachers, in addition to deteriorating living conditions, unpaid teacher salaries, lack of school budgets and uncertainty about UNRWA ability to continue providing education services. The closure combined with the trauma of wars2008,2009,2012and2014add to continuous power outages and electricity rationing, poor shelter and living conditions, economic deprivation, psychosocial impacts from the escalation, overcrowding, limited hours of instruction due to schools working on double or triple shifts, and overloaded and unpaid teachers-which all affect educational completion rates and increase dropout.Overcrowding due to severe restrictions on the construction, rehabilitation and upgrading of educational infrastructure was already a serious challenge for Gaza’s schools prior to2014conflict. The already overstretched capacity of the education sector greatly suffering from a shortage of schools (running on double and triple shifts classes) and kindergartens, both with limited resources and overwhelmed staff, was further exacerbated by51day conflict in the Gaza Strip that affected462,770students at that time(basic,secondary,KGs),including62,516KG girls and boys. The effects continue till today when of particular concern are students in the North Directorate, East Gaza Directorate and East Khan Younes Directorate, as well as5,239students attending the schools in ARAs. 67localities have no schools and46localities have no kindergartens,76.7%lack access to education support services,93%lack access to children friendly spaces(IRPAL Vulnerability2018). The Education Cluster identified 36,474girls&32,474boys and2,478teachers in need of emergency rehabilitation(renovations, adaptation, seasonalisation) of educational premises, 241,554girls&238,459boys in need of emergency education supplies, 2,261girls&2,306boys with disabilities in need of assistive devices, and 12,121girls&12,268boys need remedial education to prevent dropout and ensure access to education. KGs lack age, gender and disability suitable facilities, equipment and tools, including bathrooms(SYFS2018,IRPAL2016) which raise protection concerns(some not repaired since2014war). Schools face similar challenges, in addition to damage resulted from converting schools into temporary shelters for IDPs in2014; MoEHE assessment revealed a need for86new school buildings and1081new additional classrooms in2017-2021(Education Under Siege2017). Need for rehabilitation, disability adaptation and learning materials support, for both UNRWA and MoEHE schools was confirmed by teachers, principals&counselors(SYFS2018).During SYFS field work, women, men, girls and boys from different Gaza Strip’s communities, especially in East Gaza, East Khan Younes and North Gaza, asked SYFS to act as intermediary towards the donors to fund the improvement of infrastructure, equipment, materials, assistive devices and remedial education to prevent dropout especially of children from poorer families, CwD, with distress symptoms, IDP. Schools and KGs that require the necessary infrastructure rehabilitation (renovation, seasonalisation, adaptation for children with disabilities), and equipment and materials support serve an estimated number of11,250children in the most vulnerable age group of5to12years old, 5,625girls and5,625boys, including500girls and500boys with disabilities(incl.GMRaffected), all in need and with right to access the safe and quality school and preschool learning, appropriate to gender, age, disability.

Activities

Selection of the target group is based on cross checking SYFS schools and KGs information with assessments of other organizations and in coordination with Education Cluster to avoid duplication, as well as through community engagement of women and men caregivers, teachers and relevant education staff. Coordination with UNRWA and MoEHE to determine the specific needs at that time in terms of assistive devices for different impairments(movement, hearing, visual) to focus on the most needed devices at that time and avoid duplication with devices already provided or pledged by other funding. The assistive devices component will also be coordinated with HI and Atfaluna for technical support. According to all assessments the priority for intervention are children in border areas as mostly affected by the crisis, particularly East Gaza, East Khan Younes and North Gaza, including ARAs (on case by case basis, other schools and KGs underserved in terms of similar services are also identified and targeted), and GMR affected children. In case that the activities will identify cases in need of further specialized assistance (e.g. psychological, GBV, risk of child labor or child marriage), they will be referred for specialized assistance to existing programs providing such help, based on existing cooperation with other organizations specialized in this domain, including through CPWG. The project benefits from multiyear planning to expand the outreach to the most vulnerable beneficiaries in the subsequent years. SYFS uses an integrated, systematic approach to monitoring and evaluation: identifying, capturing, evaluating, managing and sharing all program knowledge through periodic tracking of outputs and outcomes and dissemination of regular reports. In the starting phase of the project the baseline data will be collected against which progress will be tracked, as well as specify data sources and collection methods and schedules, explain limitations and quality protocols, and describe evaluation methods. Indicators will be quantitative measures that can be tracked and standardized. The project staff will also employ qualitative methods as appropriate in order to capture the impact of the project. Sex and gender disaggregated data collection includes review and analysis of project documents, structured and semi structured surveys, focus groups, interviews with beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders (women, men, girls, boys), on site visits and observation. Ongoing feedback from beneficiaries is regularly reviewed during weekly staff meetings to address any arising issues, including protection, and to assure the accountability to the affected and targeted population. Following the baseline, activity/output data are collected monthly, entered into monitoring database, internally assessed against relevant benchmarks and compiled into reports used to inform project management and donor. At the end of the project external evaluation is conducted to explore the extent to which results are achieved and the underlying reasons. It assesses the achievements of the project, as well as implementation process and management. It is also used to identify best practices and lessons learned for future relevant programming. Findings from such evaluation are shared with donor and relevant stakeholders.
