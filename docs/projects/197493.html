Project Summary

Providing NFI emergency assistance for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families in conﬂict-affected areas of North East Syria, through the provision of winterization and NFI assistance to IDPs in informal camps and settlements as well as host community. NFIs and Winterization support will comprise three related but distinct components. First, supporting IDPs living in camps, informal settlements and collective centers to survive the harsh winter months. This includes the provision of kerosene or diesel heater kits with three months cash for fuel for areas not covered 2022 and the distribution of 2,520 cash for winter grants supporting such as 15,120 individuals in Abu Kashab Camp. Furthermore, winterization NFIs will be provided in both camps and informal settlements through cash and voucher assistance targeting 61,200 individuals. The sites targeted include Menbij camps, and informal settlements in Raqqa and Deir ez Zor. Winter NFI assistance will improve the safety and wellbeing of households by reducing exposure to harsh climatic conditions while also reducing the burden on the families’ limited income assistance. Thirdly, inkind core NFI kits will be distributed to the populations in Deir-Ez Zoir informal sites and other vulnerable informal site populations, targeting 17,640 individuals. The NFI assistance will be provided to emergency-affected populations to ensure that households have access to life-sustaining basic NFI support.

3 - Needs

The whole of Syria MSNA of 2022 estimates that in 2023, around 5.1 million people to be in need of NFI support in Syria, an increase of 4.7% compared to 2022. The collapse of the Syrian pound combined with the secondary and tertiary impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic has had a devastating impact on households across Syria with many struggling to meet their basic needs in turn leading to continuous displacement trends as households move to camps and sites due to the inability to pay rent in urban centre. As the economy deteriorates and prices rises, the majority of basic NFIs continue become increasingly unaffordable to vulnerable IDP and host families.

4 - Implementation of activities

This project comprises 3 core activities. First, for Sector Objective 1, this project will provide (Sector Activity 1.1) core NFI kits to 2,940 IDP, returnee and vulnerable host community households in need (17,640 individuals). This kit will be provided either in-kind or through a voucher modality. The second activity also under Objective 1 (Sector Activity 1.2), comprises seasonal support as winterization kits (heaters, jerry can, fire extinguisher), cash for heating fuel, and cash and vouchers for winter for three months. This support will be provided to 10,200 vulnerable households (61,200 individuals). Kits (heaters, jerry can, fire extinguisher) will be distributed in mid November and cash for fuel starting December. The cash and vouchers for winter NFIs will be distributed in December. Sites targeted for both activities will be Menbij camps, Abu Khashab camp, and informal settlements in Raqqa and Deir-Ez Zoir.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The SNFI cluster Protection Risk Analysis identifies over 50 key protection risks specific to the sector. Based on this, and the NGO's own internal protection analysis, identifies the key risks as follows: inter-community tension as a result of inclusion/exclusion, unclear selection criteria or varied assistance packages by different actors; increased tension between host and IDP communities; and risks particularly associated with distributions. To mitigate the first risk, the NGO adopts SNFI cluster guidance in its vulnerability assessments and its assistance packages to ensure harmonization with other actors. Similarly, the NGO hires local staff to carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive information networks and good community acceptance. This ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness of the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. In terms of tension between host and IDP communities, the NGO conducts extensive consultations with both groups before commencing any activities. The NGO does not target on the basis of status, and thus supports IDP and host communities. In order to mitigate for protection risks for all beneﬁciaries during SNFI distributions, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneﬁciaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female ﬁeld workers.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: -Escalation in the ongoing conflict between SDF and Government of Turkey or GoS, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence with extension of attacks and activities to Raqqa and surrounding areas. -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. -Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations. -COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access. Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally. Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. -Flexibly shifting modalities as required depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. -COVID-19 SOPs, activity level SOPs. -Build local capacity and staff.
