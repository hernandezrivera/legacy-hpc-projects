Project Summary

The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels, the nutrition sector has estimated that 3.9 million children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will need humanitarian assistance to meet their nutritional needs. Through this project, UNICEF aims to increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition curative and preventative services for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 6-59 months) and PLWs among highly vulnerable populations including internally displaced people (IDPs) and vulnerable residents. In 2022, UNICEF will continue to support the provision of nutrition curative interventions through strengthening the quality of services and scaling up Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) while working on securing pipeline for essential nutrition supplies such as Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF), therapeutic milk, Resomal, OTPs drugs and anthropometric measurements. Through this UNICEF will target around 330,000 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and will procure 396,000 RUTF cartons. To ensure early identification and referral of children with acute malnutrition, UNICEF will work on strengthening outreach activities. UNICEF will also support the provision of preventative nutrition interventions through the promotion of infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. In 2022, UNICEF will continue to exert efforts in scaling up the IYCF program to ensure the counselling of 990,000 pregnant women and mothers and caregivers of children less than two years. To alleviate the risk of micronutrient deficiency in women and children, UNICEF will continue to provide vitamin A supplementation targeting 6,520,335 children (6-59 months) and iron foliate supplementation targeting 990,000 PLWs.
Through this project, UNICEF will continue to support health system strengthening through efforts to strengthen supply chain management of nutrition commodities, besides building the capacity of front-line health workers and other CMAM related cadres on CMAM program and CMAM monitoring to ensure the delivery of high-quality nutrition services.

1 - Needs

Over the past decades, Sudan has faced a series of shocks including civil wars, tribal conflicts, natural disasters and disease outbreaks. The situation has been further exacerbated by political instability and deteriorating economic situation marked by soaring inflation rates (over 422% in 2021) , a spike in the average price of local food basket by nearly 206% compared to 2020 and overall shortages in fuel, food and medicine. These shocks triggered a decline in the overall nutrition trends across the country. Approximately half of the population is at or below the poverty line, and food insecurity remains alarmingly high. 7.3 million people in Sudan are in high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), which is 16 percent of Sudan’s total population. The situation has been further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic which continues to exacerbate food insecurities among vulnerable groups. Food insecure populations (IPC 3 and above) are projected to increase by 34% from 7.3 in 2021 to 9.8 million in 2022 ; meaning that children under five and PLWs will continue to face high levels of food insecurity and are considered as the most vulnerable groups at risk of malnutrition. Malnutrition in Sudan remains a major problem over the past three decades. The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) stands at 13.6 percent meaning that 3 million children under five suffer from acute malnutrition out of whom 619,000 suffer from severe acute malnutrition using HAC/OCHA projected population . 73 localities have acute malnutrition rates that exceed the 15 percent WHO emergency threshold. Moreover, chronic malnutrition is a major public health concern with the prevalence of 36.6 percent which is above WHO critical level . More than one third of children in Sudan are unable to develop to their full physical and cognitive potential. The other contributing factors to malnutrition in Sudan include limited access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and health services, sub-optimal infant and young feeding practices, high prevalence of co-morbidities (malaria, diarrhoea, respiratory infections among children under five years), conflict-induced displacements and natural disasters; floods, droughts. Appropriate infant and young child feeding practices are critical for children's growth and development. Despite the progress, IYCF practices among children under two years are suboptimal in Sudan, which increases the risk of malnutrition and micronutrient deficiency. The rate of exclusive breastfeeding at six months is 61.5 percent, reaching its lowest (20 percent) in Central Darfur (Um Dukhun locality). The timely introduction of adequate complementary feeding is limited with an estimated 74.6 percent of children 6-23 months do not consume the minimum dietary diversity recommended by the WHO and UNICEF. Additionally, children consumption of protein and vitamin-A rich food is as low as 28 and 23.4 percent respectively. With reliable scientific evidence, the interdependency between WASH conditions and nutrition status among children under five is becoming more acknowledged. Hence, it is crucial to have access to safe drinking water, sanitation and be aware of good hygiene practices, especially among carers of children under two years. In Sudan, three-quarters of the population have access to basic drinking water services and only 39 percent have access to limited sanitation services. Nearly a third of households practice open defecation which, poses grave public health risks. The prevalence of diarrhea among children under five years is 24 percent. Handwashing with soap is one of a range of hygiene promotion interventions that can interrupt the transmission of diarrhea-causing pathogens and only 7.37 per cent of carers are aware of the key handwashing events.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The nutrition sector has estimated that 3.9 million children under five and PLWs will need humanitarian assistance to meet their nutritional needs. UNICEF’S nutrition response will be in line with the nutrition sector main objectives: 1. Scale up access to quality lifesaving treatment and preventive nutrition services.2. Contribute to the reduction of malnutrition among girls, boys and PLW in prioritized localities through preventive multi-sectoral responses. 3. Improve understanding of the nutrition situation in prioritized localities with critical levels of acute malnutrition through evidence generating assessments and inter-sectoral analysis to guide planning and decision making. To reach these objectives, UNICEF will continue to work with the Federal/State Ministry of Health and partners on enhancing its nutrition response and support an effective scale-up of nutrition treatment and preventative activities targeting the most vulnerable children under five and PLWs among IDPs and venerable residents. This will be achieved through four main activities: Activity 1: Expand the geographical coverage of the OTPs to upsurge the accessibility of life-saving treatment services and ensure timely procurement of 396,000 cartons of RUTF (1 carton per child plus 20% buffer) to reach 330,000 SAM children. Based on the nutrition sector’s severity mapping, UNICEF will scale up the OTPs site from 1,753 to reach the target of 2,000 OTPs and ensure complementarity with Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP). Additionally, UNICEF will adopt innovative approaches, such as family MUAC and use of digital initiatives to strengthen and scale up outreach activities to ensure early identification and referral of cases and improve the coverage of treatment services. Also, community outreach volunteers and mother support groups (MSG) will continue to play a critical role in the outreach activities. Activity 2: Scale-up preventative nutrition interventions targeting “1000- day window”- as a critical opportunity to prevent acute malnutrition and stunting among children under two years by promoting IYCF practices and age-appropriate micro-nutrient supplementations. Around 990,000 pregnant women and mothers and caregivers of children, less than two years will be targeted for IYCF counselling at the community level through MSGs and at the facility level through IYCF counsellors. Additionally, 6,520,335 children (6- 59 months) will be targeted for vitamin A supplementation out of which 4 million children will be reached with two doses. and 990,000 PLWs will be targeted for iron and folate supplementation. Activity 3: Evidence generation through high-quality MICS & SMART surveys to provide more recent information for understanding the evolving nutrition situation. Activity 4: Strengthen the capacity of front-line health workers on nutrition information system, CMAM, IYCF & IYCF-E programming and supply chain management to ensure the delivery of high quality of nutrition services. In 2022, UNICEF will work on capitalizing on the triple nexus approach to building resilience among crises-affected people. UNICEF will continue to place emphasis on a number of key initiatives that contribute to the immediate, medium- and long-term nutrition solutions. This includes scaling up lifesaving nutrition interventions, strengthening systems and investing in preventative nutrition interventions such as promotion of optimal IYCF practices and micro-nutrient supplementations. NOTE: UNICEF is supporting many activities to ensure the continuity and scaling up of essential nutrition services at the facility & community level, including capacity development, strengthening referral system, strengthening supply chain management and information management system. UNICEF is reflecting those activities in detail in its annual work plan and will provide an update on the progress to the nutrition sector & key stakeholders regularly.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Beneficiary's safety and protection are one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. It has zero-tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. For the last couple of years, UNICEF has been training and sensitizing its staff and implementing partners to raise awareness on the Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence, not only those that may be happening within its staff but also with its beneficiaries. Because of its focus on this, UNICEF’s highest level (representative) is held accountable at the organizational level to put in place this mechanism. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners for their sensitivity to the matter, including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy: - The children, women, men and communities that UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse; -UNICEF has zero-tolerance for SEA and SH. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors; -All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment; -All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone, or tolerate behaviour that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing SEA and SH - An Organizational Culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organizational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero-tolerance for SEA and SH, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented. - Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights. - Swift and Credible Investigation and Sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and sanctions for violations by UNICEF personnel, and actively promote swift and fair investigations and sanctions by (as appropriate) Government partners, implementing partners, commercial vendors, and other UN System organizations. - Survivor-Centred Response: Provide survivor-centred assistance and support that is timely, predictable, sustainable and adequately resourced. - Engaging Partners in the fight against SEA and SH: Engage and equip.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is one of UNICEF core commitments in humanitarian actions. UNICEF is consistently encouraging its partners at the national and sub-national level to design and implement beneficiaries centred interventions. Efforts will be made to advocate for establishing efficient feedback and complaint mechanisms at implementing partners project sites to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have suitable means and methods to raise their voice and reflect their ownership of the services provided. Different modalities will be used, such as establishing hotlines, dissemination of a mobile number of focal points for feedback and complaints, provision of complaint boxes at health facilities, community feedback logbook and patient exit interviews. UNICEF is also an active member of the interagency platforms and is committed to making significant-quality contributions to it. At the Global level, the UNICEF Executive Director has assumed the role of Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Champion for the prevention of SEA and SH for one year. As part of this role, UNICEF has led the development and adoption of an IASC Champions Strategy on the protection from and response to SEA and Sexual Harassment.
