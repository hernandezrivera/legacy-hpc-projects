Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Southern localities of White Nile in ElSalam, ElJableen, Kosti and Tendalti localities and Zalingi, and Wadi Salih localities of Central Darfur. It will target a total of 6000 women and men and their families, of which 70% are female, through an FSL intervention aimed at integrating resilience in the relief and recovery actions. Action Against Hunger-Spain FSL activities (as a component of our strategic objective to prevent undernutrition and promote resilience of nutritionally most vulnerable communities in Sudan) are designed to save lives of the most vulnerable communities and communities host refugees, while improving food security and protecting and restoring livelihoods (nexus).
Farmers both men and women in the targeted localities will receive extension services support from ACH as well benefit from the Cash or provision for Assets as the project is aimed at establishing community assets and saving groups.The modalities will be identified on later stage of the project.Farmers will also receive appropriate skills training. Selection of beneficiaries will be done based on the criteria of vulnerability and capacity, in catchment communities of ACH.A Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA) expert will provide VSLA training after establishing the groups.ACH will train our nutrition staff and FSL staff in White Nile as well as the partner’s staff and other INGO/NGOs working interested to ensure a similar support to the groups in the localities.The first component of the project will target the mothers and caregivers of children admitted into the CMAM,OTP and TFC. They will receive immediate support in the form of urgent agricultural inputs and basic training, which will protect and restore their basic livelihood assets. This is expected to lead to increased production of food, increased food access and dietary diversity at the household level.ACH will establish twenty working groups of active mothers of children suffer of malnutrition to form Mother to Mother Support groups.These groups a total of 300 women will be trained on kitchen gardening, and the training will focus on transforming these leading mothers into an examples and agents of change for good nutrition sensitive cultivation practices.These groups will also be able to sell any surplus vegetables/food produced within the program and use the collective income for any other activities of thee choice of the group. Groups will a good performance working together and that are generating income will receive training in VSLA methodology. Some of the income generated from the gardens will go into personal savings for these women.The project will also work with men in the community, by way of engaging farmers in restoring community production and equally promoting the establishment of VSLA schemes among the male farmers.This will create more value for the communities and ensure the do-no-harm principle (avoid that women are perceived as having support or assets and therefore exposed to GBV / control of assets by the male members of the family).Finally, the cash or vouchers for asset component will directly contribute to the improvement of the food security and nutritional status of vulnerable households in the project area, and to enhance rural development through the creation of productive community based assets.
Moreover, the project helps to meet the immediate food needs of vulnerable people by having them build or boost assets that benefit the whole community, thus building the resiliency of individuals and communities in the face of climatic and/ or conflict shocks. In addition to that, it consist of the creation of community assets: roads, dykes, multipurpose ponds, planting trees and cultivation of arable land. Principal target groups are 50 Household of the most vulnerable groups,70%female-headed households.

Needs

Despite efforts of partners, the food and nutrition security situation remains critical in Sudan. Moreover, the situation is getting worsen due to the limited access to cash as well as fuel crisis and occurrence of regular demonstration The elected areas these areas were not directly affected by conflict but are hosting huge number of refugees from South Sudan.The inflation rate and the devaluation of the Sudanese Pound has caused an increase in the prices of food supplies and the rural areas are more affected due to continuous fuel crisis that lead to some shortage as well. In White Nile, all localities will remain classified as food insecure . By December 2018, Aslam will remain in the Critical phase for acute malnutrition. On December, 2018, ACH has conducted an assessment that indicated that food insecurity level is alarming and it indicated that there is little support from external actors to public health and nutrition services in host communities. This trend will be difficult to reverse in the coming years, and it requires a concerted efforts of all partners and donors. As of December, 2018, HNO estimated that (IPC Phase 3 and Phase 4) or approximately 396,494 people in the four localities. IPC Phase 4 outcomes continue due the economic crisis and high levels of food insecurity, limited income-earning opportunities, high morbidity, poor infant and young feeding practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services. Access to safe and reliable water also exacerbates the nutrition situation. HNO indicated that the total number of people in need in these four localities is 283,262 persons and the total target is 219,188 persons. Generally, in Sudan men have control over all HH income, including that generated by women. While, the women responsibility is cater for all HH expenses (including food) based on the allocation the man provides. In most cases, women’s lack of decision-making power and control over assets combined with her responsibility for household affairs during times of severe physical insecurity poses a significant threat to household food security and nutrition status: a situation which reflects the vicious cycle of gender inequality, conflict, poverty, and malnutrition. Therefore, in direct response to this reality, and in line with HNO Outcomes 2 and 3 for Sudan, the proposed project will increase access to food and diversity levels by providing opportunities for women and men in at risk communities to strengthen their agricultural knowledge and capacity, as well as it will help to reduce any tension between local communities and refugees served in the camps. Additionally, access to sources of food will improve through the enhancement of community infrastructure, providing for increased supply of nutritious foods. Activities

This project will work in While Nile States and Centeral Drafur State with 6000 beneficiaries (1000 HH). Women targeted for the project will receive extension services support and VSLA training from ACH and the partner organization. Groups will work for 12 months and cash out their savings at the end. The project will identify and register group members (Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and Farmer Groups), verify arable plots and HH garden space that will be cultivated. We will procure vegetable seeds and proceed to train the groups and commence the cultivation practices. A committee from each locality will select farmers farming sites. Assets will be identified through Community Based Participatory Planning sessions. Through community-based MtMSG, lead mothers and community volunteers, home health gardens will be put in place. Lead mothers will act as ‘demonstration plot’ owners and thereby influence others within support groups. Next step will be to introduce women groups’ idea and initiate self-organization into cooperatives, based on women’s interest, availability and cultural aspects to ensure the do-no-harm principle. Currently, there are very few or no female cooperatives in the target areas; this could be an impulse to women’s economic empowerment in a way that supports and uses the nutrition sensitive agriculture, water resource management and sustainable cultivation techniques to produce vegetable crops for own consumption as well as for sale in local markets. Training on vegetable marketing skills and record keeping will accompany the cultivation work. Formation of VSLA groups will follow this initial stage. We will include intercropping techniques, climate sensitive cultivation practices and disaster risk reduction planning based on the priority risks. Farmers will work on climate change adaptation through resilient production approaches, learning about water and soil management. The program will consider offering smallholders cash protection.
