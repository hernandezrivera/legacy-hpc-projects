Project Summary

Save the Children proposes to support primary and community health care services, including the prevention and control of epidemic prone diseases in vulnerable communities in Central Equatoria (Yei, Morobo and Lainya), Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Budi, Lafon and Torit), Jonglei (Akobo and Nyirol) and Warrap states (Twic and Abyei) tomeet the following objectives:
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases.
2. Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
Save the Children will respond to the most urgent needs of the beneficiaries, including emergency clinical service provision, community health care and maternity (supporting FP services, SGBV, ANC, PNC, normal delivery, BEMONC services and newborn care), health and protection services to support MHPSS services, WASH in health facilities, outbreak investigation and response for epidemic prone diseases and the provision of essential medical supplies.
The proposed activities contribute to CO1: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, CO2: Improve timely access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations and CO3: Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and monitoring health insecurities. With an overall financial requirement of $2.5 Million, Save the Children intends to provide essential health care services to 41,667 beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

With the on-going outbreak of Ebola in North Kivu and Ituri Provinces in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), high priority neighbouring countries, including South Sudan, are accelerating preparedness and prevention initiatives. Especially in the light of the fact that South Sudan due its border with DRC and Uganda has been declared a ‘high risk countries’. World Health Organization (WHO) increased the risk classification of Ebola in South Sudan to be ‘Very High Risk’ as of late September 2018. SC has been supporting the national response since July 2019 in Yei River State, in Yei, Lainya, Morobo and Kajo Keji. Through this project, these activities will be continued in the respective counties, with possible expansion to other areas. In March 2017, Save the Children conducted a rapid assessment of the Greater Kapoeta area, which revealed that only 40% of the population has access to basic primary health care services. Very few communities are within reach of health facilities. Long distances and security conditions impede access and facilities are unequally distributed. Kapoeta South has 2 hospitals, 4/5 PHCCs and 0/2 PHCUs functional, Kapoeta East has 7/11 PHCU functional and 12/14 PHCCs. This does not mean they are fully stocked and staffed. The 2018 SARA found that Kapoeta had 0.95 qualified health staff / 10 population, the global standard is 23/10,000. SARA also found 84 patient beds per 10,000 populations, 32% of assessed functional facilities had basic amenities, 51% had basic equipment, 21% had basic diagnostic equipment and only 15% of facilities had basic drug supplies. 19% had family planning supplies, 43% standard ANC services, 24% basic obstetric services, 19% offer adolescent health services but 40% staff are trained, just not resourced. (Preliminary Findings SARA 2018). Primary health care facilities in the programme catchment area are to be managed by a new as yet unnamed implementing partner. Save the Children will work in partnership with the partner and MoH to support RH services in these facilities. In remote and rural areas such as the Abyei Special Administrative Area (ASAA), most births occur at home, with unskilled birth attendants such as Traditional Birth Attendants. Many of these providers are non-literate and have received no formal education or training. Instead, they must rely on traditions and limited knowledge and skills passed down from previous generations. However, multiple studies have shown that many of these traditional practices are not effective and are unsafe . A Baseline Assessment by Save the Children in ASAA highlighted key gaps in community knowledge of danger signs during antenatal, labour and post-natal periods, as well as newborn care. This is particularly concerning as it illustrates that the local community is currently unaware of when they should access services. Fluid populations movements not only affect ASAA, but also prevalent in Warrap State, where severe acute malnutrition is also prevalent. During the IPC Analysis in August 2019, Twic County was classified as IPC AMN Phase 4. The situation is similar in Jonglei, where both Akobo and Nyirol counties fall under PIC 4 - Emergency category.

2 - Activities

Prevention and control of common communicable diseases: SCI will ensure health promotion, vaccination, active surveillance and case management for notifiable communicable diseases, AWD, Dysentery, Typhoid, Measles ensuring case fatality rates (CFR) in any outbreak remains below 2%. SCI will support mitigation for disease outbreaks including mosquito net distribution, ORS corners and DTU support, disease surveillance and deployment of a rapid response team for outbreak response including diarrhea and malaria and to increase surveillance and IEC through community outreach. Vaccination/immunization: SC will support EPI through fixed facilities with outreach and deployment of a rapid response team for mass vaccination and outbreak response. SC will refer children for vaccination from OPD, delivery and postnatal care and nutrition services and ensure vaccinations for pregnant women and SGBV survivors. WASH and waste management: SCI will make sure that standard precautions and essential WASH and waste management are in place in health facilities, following WHO guidelines. All health facilities will be provided with sufficient water for IPC, drinking water, latrines for staff OPD and labour rooms, 3 pit waste system and incinerator. Outpatient care and clinical service provision: SCI will support quality clinical outpatient services, ensuring a utilization rate at least 1, 1 visit per person per year, children under five will account for at least 20% of consultations. SCI will train health workers on management of key communicable diseases namely respiratory tract infection, malaria, diarrhea including STIs and referral, for TBHIV; using IMNCI guidelines for children under five and South Sudan and standard treatment guidelines for adults and children over five ensuring staff are trained on standard treatment guidelines and management of referrals. Facilities will provide services for management of chronic disease focused on asthma, heart disease, hypertension and diabetes referring suspected cases, especially HIV TB and caring for treatment continuation at clinic level. All PHCUs and PHCCs supporting active listening psychological first aid (PFA) and referral. All PHCCs supporting primary health care treatment of anxiety, depression, epilepsy and psychosis, referral for specialist health services and protection services and follow up. SCI will support capacity building and supplies to provide MHPSS services. Safe deliveries: SCI will ensure at least 20% of deliveries are attended by skilled birth attendants, provision of skilled human resources for providing reproductive health services supporting 1 enrolled midwife 1 community midwife in each PHCC and 1 community midwife in each PHCU. SC will ensure training of all staff on safe and clean delivery, normal delivery, BEMNOC including management of rape and newborn care Clinical officers will be trained on MHPSS, all staff will be trained on PFA. SC will ensure all pregnant and lactating women and child under five are screened for malnutrition and referred and vulnerable children and adults are referred to protection services. ASRHR: Reproductive health services will be provided at community, PHCU and PHCC including normal delivery at PHCU and PHCC level, normal delivery and BEMNOC services at PHCC level, primary and community based newborn care and family planning services. SC will support care of survivors of sexual and gender based violence including community awareness, CMR, MHPSS support and referral to protection services. SC will ensure all services are adolescent friendly and monitor utilization in age groups 10-15 and 16-19 years. Community outreach:SC will support community outreach following the Boma Health Initiative guidelines where possible supporting ICCM, hygiene promotion of EPI services, skilled birth attendance, identifying and referring SGBV survivors, conducting active surveillance and reporting on notifiable diseases.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project focuses on delivering health care services to the most vulnerable communities in South Sudan, which includes host communities, internally displaced people, returnees and other populations affected by conflict and/or natural disasters. Therefore, the primary beneficiaries of the proposed activities are affected by displacement. All vulnerable groups including children, women, elderly, disabled, hosts, IDPs and returnees in the catchment areas will be targeted will a holistic assistance package.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Save the Children has already played a significant role in MoH system strengthening and capacity building at the various levels of the South Sudan health system. We will continue building the capacity of County Health Departments in disease surveillance, data collection analysis and utilization of data for decision-making. Save the Children will also support the health facility teams through supportive supervision, on the job training and technical support. Joint monitoring with CHD will be conducted to ensure sustainability and ownership.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Save the Children delivers health assistance in strong collaboration with the Ministry of Health and the County Health Departments. Therefore, working in alignment and contributing to national policies is at the center of SCI's health programming. Save the Children also co-leads the Health Cluster with WHO, and coordinates closely with other clusters, too. This proposed project will contribute to CO1, CO2 and CO3 for HRP 2020.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The current (relative) stability in the region and the country at large will provide an enabling and supportive atmosphere for a successful project implementation. It is also assumed that the positive working relationship between the government and Save the Children will endure, where government will continue supporting access to health services in public health facilities, hence reducing barriers to access and utilization of services by poor and vulnerable children and women.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Save the Children will support technical capacity building for the professional health teams in existing public health facilities. Such services will be delivered by the MoH technical staff, who are already recruited and will be deployed in the health facilities alongside Save the Children medical and non-medical staff. When the project ends, this structure will remain behind providing the services to the community. Sustainability will be enhanced by capacity building of community structures, including TBAs to support community-based activities of the project. These are believed to be more sustainable since they are identified and selected from the community members, and reside within the community and will continue to exist and serve even after completion of this project. Hence, the use of the already existing institutional structures such as the Government health system and the Community Health team is likely to enhance the sustainability of the project. We assume that the government will continue recruiting and retaining more qualified health workers to improve the staffing levels in health facilities; as well as set-up and operationalize new community health centers where they are currently non-existent to increase access to services.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Through the SCI Child Safeguarding Policy, we commit to safeguard children to the maximum extent possible and protect them from deliberate or inadvertent actions and failings that place them at risk of child abuse, sexual exploitation, injury and any other harm. These key principles guide the implementation team in their daily work, and ensure that the activities on the ground are consistent with the “Do no harm” principle. The direct and indirect beneficiaries of our interventions are informed about the breach of accountability e.g. sexual exploitation and abuse, and how to report these misuse of power through the complaints and feedback mechanism.We are committed to giving account to the people we intend to serve, therefore, multiple communication channels are in place during the implementation period. We aim at sharing information in an effective and transparent manner; therefore, the project team utilizes multiple forums, such as boma-level meetings, community focal points, posters, leaflets, signboards etc. to communicate with the beneficiaries. The information package describes the mandate of Save the Children, the scope and scale of the project, clear beneficiary targeting criteria and includes a comprehensive summary of their entitlements. The implementation team will choose the most effective channel(s) tailored to the specific context, and adjusted to the preferences of the target communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

At Save the Children, we actively seek feedback from our beneficiaries to tailor our programming to their most urgent needs. A robust MEAL framework is part of the project design, including a multi-channel two-way Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM). From the early stages of the project, the target population will be informed about the available communication channels to share their views and concerns, and will be encouraged to do so. The feedback and complaint channels are child-friendly, gender-sensitive and accessible for all, including people living with disabilities. These will be adjusted to the context and the preferences of the local community. In each of our program areas, a resident MEAL officer is in place to ensure continuous monitoring of the deliverables and to strengthen accountability. The MEAL officer facilitates the collection of feedback and complaints through forms, focus group discussions and surveys; and measures satisfaction levels at the same time. The implementation team takes lead on giving timely feedback to the beneficiaries and openly discuss their concerns through community meetings. The complaints and feedback received in the intervention area will feed in to the countrywide database; and will influence future programming.
