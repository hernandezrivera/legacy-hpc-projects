Project Summary

The project is about Resilient Reconstruction of Classroom, Administrative Blocks and Latrines/toilets in conflict zones of Mozambique. These areas are located in the north of country. According with Education Cluster prioritization and aligned with UN-Habitat plan were selected Chiúre and Metuge disctricts and Pemba City for intervention in Cabo Delgado Province.
One of the objectives of sustainable development is education with quality, inclusion of everyone, in addition to the need to make communities safe, resilient and sustainable. It is essential to restore damaged school infrastructures, as they had a significant impact on the lives of a large number of children. With reconstruction interventions, UN-Habitat intends for the education sector to restore appropriate learning conditions and dignity to children affected by armed conflict.
Forced displacement is the main reason for these children not attending school, and those in host communities also face challenges due to the deterioration of temporary learning spaces and pressure on existing basic services and infrastructure. Another point to take into consideration is the poor sanitation systems and sometimes inadequate management in schools that contribute to putting students at risk, with the possibility of causing diseases such as cholera due to poor hygiene.
The implementation of the project will follow the following steps: identification of priority schools for intervention; approval of schools chosen by authorities; process of finding contractors and inspectors; execution of works, completion and delivery of buildings. Monitoring of the project during the implementation phase will be done in coordination with local authorities. To this end, UN-Habitat makes use of various monitoring mechanisms such visit and progress reports, control matrices, as well as inspection instruments and devices.
The reference population was identified through an analysis of the Education Management Information System (SIGE) database of the Ministry of Education. The project is going to benefit 5,670 children aged 6 to 17, with the target being composed by those from host community, as well as internally displaced people (IDPs) in approximate proportion of 50% for each group.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The possible risks were identified:- The exposure of workers to possible accidents during the execution of works. To prevent these from occurring, the contractor and the inspection team must ensure that all workers always have personal protective equipment to ensure safety and health at work.- Weak ability of technicians to apply properly technical resilience measures in reconstruction. As a solution, these technicians will be trained by UN-Habitat through induction workshops and training on construction sites. The inspection must follow a monitoring and supervision plan, together with the government and UN-Habitat throughout the reconstruction process. To ensure that all works have quality materials, technical specifications of the materials to be used in the work and detailed bill of quantities will be provided before implementation phase starts.- Weak government involvement during the course of the project could represent a risk. To prevent this situation from occurring, multisectoral visits will be established from the beginning involving SDPI, SDEJT, DPE's in addition to bilateral meetings, sending reports and involving this institutions in trainings.- Any type of harassment or abuse of power by construction team to gain advantage where schools are located. For cases like these, there will be reporting mechanisms such as complaint boxes, where any report about a negative situation can be deposited. Complaints may be made by community members, as well as by workers themselves due to work-related situations.It is important to note that MINEDH is oriented by a Code of Conduct, which aims to guarantee ethics in relations with contracted companies. This manual aims to ensure adequate environmental and social management in the reconstruction of school infrastructures. In addition to this instrument, legislation on environmental and social safeguards is applied too.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

It is projected that 53% of the children will be female, which is composed with children from community and internally displaced people. Girls have specific needs compared to boys, but they continue to have disproportionate access to adequate sanitation. The reconstruction of gender-sensitive toilets is an important step towards ensuring that girls and boys have access to an improved learning environment, as well as encouraging and providing the same educational opportunity for all.Regarding the construction team, there has been awareness about the inclusion of women in the construction team as a way of demonstrating their skills and being trained, and in this way reducing socio-cultural inequality in access to work opportunities. With the knowledge obtained in project, the women covered will be able to implement the lessons learned during and after the end of job.On the other hand, women belonging to the community can be a target in cases of harassment and abuse by builders during the project implementation period. Cases like this are discouraged, either through awareness raising, as well as through reporting through the existing reporting mechanisms described above.By ensuring that women are included as direct beneficiaries of the project, as well as encouraging the inclusion of women as workers, these are ways of guaranteeing their empowerment.With gender participation in activities that guarantee income and financial independence, the family structure is also indirectly influenced, such as dependent members who are out of working age, such as children and elderly familiars.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The lack of appropriate access for children with special needs can limit their professional future, making them dependent on other people. In order to guarantee the inclusion of boys and girls belonging to this group of people, who are often discriminated, all classroom and administrative blocks, including toilets, will be equipped with access ramps. These elements must have the minimum width that guarantees comfort for users, handrails on both sides, an adequate percentage of inclination and an alert floor. In case of people with reduced vision, MINEDH plans for schools to be painted with color hat ensure contrast between walls and doors.The distribution of students in this situation, which is estimated to benefit from the ramps that will be built in each of the districts, is presented below:Chiúre District – 119 children with disabilities;Pemba City – 119 children with disabilities;Metuge District – 70 children with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Reconstruction activities can have some adverse effects on the construction site and its surroundings, such as polluting of soil, air and water resources, altering their initial state. The possible impacts are as follows:- Deposits of materials and other waste on the construction site. In order for this situation to be avoided, the contractor must present an environmental action plan to be analyzed by environmental and social safeguards technicians, in order to mitigate or prevent such impacts from happening.- Illegal removal of fibre cement sheets, a material with the potential to cause health problems. If this material exists, it must be handled carefully and sent to a landfill designated by municipal authorities, and its use for temporary rooms must be discouraged.- Bad decision regarding the area of implementation of sanitation pipes in latrines/toilets. To do this, there must be a prior survey of the existing buildings on the land and the school's history, which includes knowledge of the existing water sources that are intended to be installed to make sure that there is no contamination. The bottom of the latrine pit should not be too close to groundwater, due to the contamination that can occur.- Use of toxic wood treatment products handled by workers. In this case, products must be used with adequate protection, provided by the contractor.- Use of wood to manufacture roofing elements of illegal origin. For this reason, wood must have a certificate of quality and origin and local material must be prioritized in order to discourage land transport, which also contributes to environmental pollution.
