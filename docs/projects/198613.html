Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the delivery of quality protection services, improving the protection environment through protection monitoring, and supporting durable solutions for IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees and vulnerable host community members in Northeast Nigeria. UNHCR interventions will strengthen the protection environment by building the capacity of government institutions to better protect the affected population, identifying, and minimizing protection risks through protection by presence and urban outreach Programme in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. To this end, the key activities to be implemented include:
1. Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring using enhanced and harmonised tools, including through community-based mechanisms, and protection by presence;
2. Quality of registration and profiling of the affected population for an enhance targeting and prioritization using innovations such as the ProGres V4;
3. Enhanced access to legal assistance and legal remedies;
4. Enhanced access to Civil registration, including civil status documentation;
5. Advocacy and capacity building in support of the development of Law and policy, including the domestication of the KC;
6. Promote Self-reliance through access to protection safety-net activities;
7. Support the discussions and the processes for durable solutions; 8. Provision of material assistance including cash targeting the most vulnerable; 9. Referral for specialized services including Mental health and psychosocial support, GBV and CP services.

1 - Needs

The prevailing security situation in the BAY states remains volatile, characterized by non-state armed groups (NSAG) attacks, the killing and abduction of civilians, destruction of property and widespread human rights abuses. The ongoing military counterinsurgency operations have also restricted the affected populations' freedom of movement. Given the pervasive insecurity, killings, abductions, illegal vehicular checkpoints, complex suicide attacks using improvised explosive devices (IEDs), incidents of detonation of unexploded ordnances (UXO) and explosive remnants of war (ERW), widespread human rights violations continue. NSAGs have destroyed essential utilities and public infrastructure, which has aggravated poverty and worsened the economic conditions of people in the BAY states.The crisis coupled with the COVID pandemic and climate change resulting in floods and poor harvest, has compounded the vulnerabilities of the displaced men, women, girls, and boys resulting in massive unmet needs. A combination of the above factors has triggered new displacements. According to UNHCR, 338,000 people have sought refuge in neighbouring Cameroon (131,000), Chad (20,000), and Niger (187,000). In the BAY states, a total of 8.4 million people is in need of protection and humanitarian assistance according to the Northeast 2022 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview. This includes 2.2 million IDPs, 1.5 million IDPs, 3.9 million host community members, and 700,000 individuals in areas not accessible to humanitarian actors. Of the total persons in need, 80% are women and children. The global percentage of persons with disabilities is 15% and is expected to be higher in locations with conflict. Despite COVID-19 and related restrictions, movements across the borders continue to be recorded. From January 2022 to June 2022, over 13,000 spontaneous Nigerian refugee returnees were registered with 63% returning from Niger, 36% from Cameroon and 1% from Chad. The closure of official camps in Maiduguri municipality by the Borno State Government has resulted in secondary and tertiary displacement which an upwards of 150,000 IDPs in Maiduguri will be affected.To respond to the needs of vulnerable populations, UNHCR aims to ensure that protection monitoring services that identify and refer the most vulnerable populations, are in place and increase the affected populations’ access to leal assistance and civil documentation through partnerships. The activities will be concentrated in 11 LGAs in Borno state (Bama (and Banki), Damboa, Dikwa, Ngala, Gwoza (and Pulka), Jere, Kala-Balge, Konduga, MMC/Jere, Mobbar, Monguno); 9 LGAs in Adamawa state (Madagali, Fufore, Maiha, Michika, Mubi-North, Mubi-South, Yola North, Yola South, Girei); and 5 LGAs in Yobe state (Damaturu, Bade, Machina, Geidam, Gujba).

2 - Activities

In line with UNHCR’s protection mandate, UNHCR will implement key activities to achieve a favourable protection environment, to create a conducive environment to enable access to legal and civil status documents to prevent human rights violations in line with the northeast Nigeria protection strategy, support material assistance including cash, maintain protection by presence and advocacy for the domestication of legal instruments that protects the affected population. • Regarding Protection Monitoring, the existing protection monitoring tools are developed using Kobo toolbox without integration across the tools and because of the absence of a registration/enrolment tool to manage the data properly, problems of analysis emerge. UNHCR will deploy the P21 protection monitoring tool for an enhance data collection, identification of persons at heightened risks, ease of analysis and uniformity of information with other protection actors. In addition, The ProGressV4 will be deployed as the tool to be used for the ease of identification of the persons in need. This is in addition to the harmonized protection monitoring tool which can also be used at the point of arrival to identify persons with specific needs before the ProGress V4 can be deployed. The individual or incident level protection monitoring will seek to document human rights abuses and violations suffered by the survivors/victims and collect confidential and sensitive information from victims, survivors, or witnesses for immediate targeted response in shelter, Nonfood Items (NFIs), Cash-Based Intervention or documentation response, and referrals for other services such as nutrition and medical assistance. It will also serve as the basis for case management. . • UNHCR through its access to justice program will register and issue birth and indigene certificates to IDPs and returnees. The PRIMES tool rolled out in 2022 will be used for population enrolment, registration, assistance monitoring, and returnees’ management. Efforts will continue to domesticate the Kampala convention through advocacy with different international and local partners, including government institutions.• UNHCR, in line with its protection mandate to create a conducive environment to enable access to legal and civil status documents to prevent human rights violations and in line with the northeast Nigeria protection strategy, UNHCR through its access to justice program will register and issue birth and indigene certificates to IDPs and returnees. The project seeks to provide the affected population and host community members with certificates of indigene and birth. UNHCR also plans to expand its partnership to provide National Identity Number (NIN) and National ID Cards targeting the affected population. • UNHCR intends to maintain access to justice program through partnership. UNHCR will support legal assistance in court representation, legal counselling for individuals and groups and adjudication of probate matters. In locations where courts are unable to return, UNHCR will support in establishing and organizing mobile court sittings. In the same vein, UNHCR will also implement detention monitoring in detention facilities and implement human rights monitoring in camps and host communities. UNHCR will strengthen alternative dispute resolution mechanisms through the training of lawyers, paralegals and community leaders on ADR. Government officials and security personnel including CJTFs will be trained on human rights and humanitarian law.• UNHCR will adopt a two-prong approach in pursing the domestication of the Kampala Convention at the Federal Level and the passage/ adoption of an IDP State Laws in the North-East Geo-political zone. • The target population will include IDPs, Returnees (Refugee Returnees and IDP Returnees) and a percentage of the host community.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. • All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
