Project Summary

This intervention aims to provide comprehensive health and nutrition care for the nutritional rehabilitation of vulnerable populations, including children under five, adolescents, and pregnant and lactating women, in the districts of Palma and Mocimboa da Praia, Cabo Delgado Province. The project focuses on combating intergenerational malnutrition and saving lives by addressing micronutrient deficiencies through targeted supplementation and promoting good eating practices. By improving access to essential nutrients and fostering healthy nutrition habits, the intervention seeks to reduce malnutrition rates, enhance health outcomes, and break the cycle of malnutrition across generations.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project in Cabo Delgado Province, specifically targeting the districts of Palma and Mocimboa da Praia, is designed to tackle the pressing issue of malnutrition and improve the overall health and nutritional status of vulnerable populations. The primary focus is on addressing intergenerational malnutrition by providing essential health and nutrition care to key groups, including children under five, adolescents, and pregnant and lactating women. These groups are particularly susceptible to the long-term impacts of malnutrition, which not only affects their immediate well-being but also put at risk future generations.The initiative's central goal is to reduce malnutrition rates in these communities by offering micronutrient supplementation to combat deficiencies that are prevalent among the target groups. Micronutrient deficiencies, such as a lack of iron, vitamin A, and iodine, can lead to severe health issues, including stunted growth in children, compromised immune systems, and complications during pregnancy. By addressing these deficiencies, the project aims to prevent these health consequences and improve the long-term health outcomes of individuals in the affected regions.In addition to supplementation, the project seeks to promote healthy eating practices within these communities. Education campaigns will focus on teaching families how to incorporate more nutritious foods into their diets, even with limited resources. The emphasis will be on locally available foods that are rich in essential vitamins and minerals, helping to foster a sustainable approach to nutrition. Community-based interventions, such as nutrition workshops, will encourage the development of healthier eating habits and the adoption of practices that reduce the risk of malnutrition.Furthermore, the project aims to enhance nutrition education. The project will help reduce the reliance on external food aid and promote sustainable, community-driven solutions to malnutrition. This holistic approach is designed not only to address immediate nutritional needs but also to build resilience in the community, ensuring that they can better cope with future challenges.Overall, the project's long-term impact will be seen in improved health outcomes for children, adolescents, and women, particularly those who are pregnant or lactating. The reduction in malnutrition rates, better food security, and the promotion of healthier eating habits will contribute to the overall well-being of these vulnerable populations, breaking the cycle of malnutrition and fostering a healthier, more resilient future for the communities in Palma and Mocimboa da Praia.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Based on the project’s objectives, the priority activities will be tailored to meet the specific needs of vulnerable groups in the districts of Palma and Mocimboa da Praia, Cabo Delgado Province. These activities will focus on reducing malnutrition, promoting healthy eating practices, while considering the unique challenges of each district.In Palma, the project will focus on micronutrient supplementation for children under five, pregnant women, and lactating mothers to address common nutrient deficiencies. Mobile integrated brigades teams will distribute supplements and provide regular health check-ups, ensuring ongoing access to essential nutrients. Additionally, nutrition education campaigns will be conducted in local communities to raise awareness about healthy eating, focusing on the importance of locally available, nutrient-rich foods. These sessions will target families, with special emphasis on mothers and caregivers of young children.Capacity building for local health providers is another priority, with training programs to improve the ability of health workers to assess and address malnutrition. This will include using tools like the Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) for better monitoring of malnutrition rates. Through these efforts, the project will enhance the quality of health services and ensure that health workers are equipped to effectively combat malnutrition in the community.Overall, the combination of supplementation, nutrition education, food security programs, and strengthened health services will contribute to long-term improvements in health and nutrition outcomes for vulnerable populations in Palma and Mocimboa da Praia, particularly children, pregnant women, and lactating mothers.In Mocimboa da Praia, the project will focus on emergency nutrition support for severely malnourished children and women, providing micronutrient supplements and ready-to-use therapeutic foods (RUTF). Mobile clinics will target remote areas to deliver these interventions, ensuring that vulnerable populations receive timely nutrition support. In addition to supplementation, regular nutrition screenings will be conducted to monitor malnutrition levels and provide immediate interventions when necessary.The project will also focus on promoting healthy eating habits by organizing community outreach programs that educate families on incorporating more diverse, nutrient-rich foods into their diets. These programs will be adapted to local food preferences and availability, ensuring that the information is both practical and culturally appropriate. Special attention will be given to mothers, caregivers, and adolescents, empowering them to make informed choices about nutrition for their families.Additionally, maternal and child health support will be prioritized, with targeted interventions for pregnant women and mothers in the first 1,000 days of their child’s life.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project recognizes that interventions in vulnerable communities, such as those in Palma and Mocímboa da Praia, must be carefully designed and implemented to minimize potential risks and avoid harm to target populations. The project takes a comprehensive approach to identifying and mitigating risks, ensuring that the needs of the community are met responsibly and sustainably.One of the main risks identified is the potential lack of access to health services, especially in remote or conflict-affected areas. Limited infrastructure, transportation barriers, and safety concerns can make it difficult to distribute supplements, implement nutrition programs, and provide health services. The project addresses this risk by utilizing mobile health clinics and community-based outreach teams that can reach remote locations. Health services will be decentralized to ensure that people in hard-to-reach areas can still access interventions. Collaboration with local health authorities and community leaders will also ensure better coordination and safety during implementation.There is a risk that the project will inadvertently disrupt local customs or practices, especially in terms of eating habits. Excessive intervention or the imposition of external solutions without considering the local context can create resistance or confusion in communities. To address this problem, the project focuses on strengthening local capacities, especially in terms of nutrition education and health services. Health professionals will be equipped with the knowledge and tools to continue to monitor and support nutrition efforts independently. In addition, community-based nutrition education will help establish long-term healthy eating habits that can be maintained beyond the duration of the project.The main targets are vulnerable groups, such as pregnant women, children under five and breastfeeding mothers, but there is a risk that not all individuals in these groups will benefit equally from the project, particularly in areas with higher poverty or marginalisation. The project will ensure that outreach efforts are inclusive and equitable, targeting the most vulnerable through targeted interventions. Community health workers will be trained to identify and address the needs of marginalized groups, ensuring that interventions reach all members of the target populations. Regular monitoring will monitor progress and identify any gaps in service delivery.The project will focus on promoting health and nutrition, aiming to improve the well-being of communities in a sustainable way, without causing negative impacts on natural resources or the environment.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project ensures meaningful access to care and empowers vulnerable groups, with a focus on women and the elderly, by actively involving them in all phases of implementation. Women will be encouraged to take leadership roles within community health committees and programs, promoting decision-making and ownership of interventions. The project will also strengthen women's economic empowerment by engaging them in activities that increase food security and income generation.Older people, as key pillars of education in families, will be empowered to play a central role in transmitting knowledge about health and nutrition to younger generations. They will be encouraged to get involved in promoting healthy habits and nutrition education within their communities and families, becoming references of wisdom and support for the youngest. This intergenerational learning model will promote social inclusion and reduce isolation, allowing older people to become active agents in improving collective well-being. The participatory approach of the project ensures that both women and older people are not only beneficiaries but also active contributors to the well-being of communities, strengthening local ownership and creating long-term sustainable impacts.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify individuals with disabilities through community outreach and collaboration with local organizations, disability advocacy groups, and community health workers. These groups will play a key role in identifying persons with disabilities, especially those who may be more hidden or marginalized. Health workers will be trained to recognize different types of disabilities and conduct home visits to assess needs and ensure that individuals with disabilities are not overlooked. Additionally, the project will use inclusive data collection methods to capture information on the number and specific needs of individuals with disabilities in the target areas.To remove barriers to access, the project will implement various strategies, such as providing accessible transportation for those with mobility challenges and ensuring that health services and education are offered in accessible formats. The project will also engage people with disabilities and their families in planning and decision-making processes to ensure that interventions are designed to meet their specific needs, fostering an inclusive approach that promotes equal access to all services and activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project has been designed with environmental sustainability in mind. The promotion of community gardens and agricultural training of other projects, while aimed at improving food security, may lead to overuse of local natural resources if not carefully managed. Furthermore, increased agricultural activity may cause environmental manipulation of soil or harm local ecosystems if ecological agricultural practices are not properly approved. The project will therefore take steps to ensure intersectoral support that all agricultural activities follow ecological methods, such as climate-smart agricultural techniques, crop diversification and organic farming practices, to minimize negative environmental impacts.To mitigate these risks, the project will conduct environmental assessments at the outset to assess the potential environmental impacts of its activities, particularly in agriculture. These assessments will identify areas where intervention is needed to prevent soil erosion, water depletion or damage to biodiversity. In consultation with local environmental experts, the project will develop guidelines for sustainable agricultural practices that promote soil health, water conservation and biodiversity preservation. In addition to agricultural activities, the project will also focus on reducing any waste or negative impacts related to the distribution of supplements and other resources. The project will promote the use of biodegradable packaging and ensure that any waste generated by the distribution of supplements or medical supplies is managed responsibly. In addition, the project will incorporate environmental education into its broader community outreach, teaching the importance of environmental conservation alongside nutrition and health education. Through these measures, the project aims to create a positive and sustainable impact on community health and the local environment.
