Project Summary

Due to its position over major tectonic faults, and being situated within the "Hurricane Alley", Haiti is exposed to extreme weather conditions and frequent natural disasters. Between 2004 and 2018, there has been two (2) earthquakes, twelve (12) notable hurricanes, four (4) tropical storms as well as major floodings. Most recently, on October 6th 2018, a 5.9 magnitude earthquake struck 20 km north of Port-de-Paix (North-West department). This earthquake was the strongest to hit Haiti since the devastating earthquake that struck the country on January 12th2010.
IOM, in its collaboration with DPC in disaster risk management and preparedness, was able to identify many lessons learnt and unresolved issues which are likely to worsen the effect of the upcoming tropical cyclone on the Haitian Population if not improved. Building upon solid results achieved in the previous interventions and based upon IOM’s lessons learned, as well as the key strategic priorities of the DPC 2018 Contingency Plan, IOM’s proposed Action aims to strengthen the capacities of the disaster preparedness and response DPC local authorities through a multifaceted intervention.
IOM co-leads the Shelter and NFI Working Group (WG) with the Unité de construction de logements et bâtiments publics (UCLBP) and with the DPC, the Ministry of Social Affairs, the Ministry of Interior, the Entreprise Publique de Promotion de Logements Sociaux (EPPLS) and local authorities in the targeted areas. As such, the proposed activities are designed in line with the Government’s response strategy and build upon IOM’s on-going activities in the North West, North and Artibonite Departments, Nippes and Grand’Anse . Since the 2018 Hurricane season, a platform to manage the NFI response in case of emergency is available.
The overall objective of this project is to ensure emergency preparedness and response capacity of government authorities and communities through the availability of rapid NFI response, and to reinforce capacity of people managing collective shelters.

Needs

Due to its position over major tectonic faults, and being situated within the "Hurricane Alley", Haiti is exposed to extreme weather conditions and frequent natural disasters. In addition, climate change is expected to further aggravate Haiti’s geographical vulnerabilities, by increasing the intensity and duration of the hurricane season while also increasing both drought and rainfall intensity year-round. Between 2004 and 2018, there has been two (2) earthquakes, twelve (12) notable hurricanes, four (4) tropical storms as well as major floodings. Most recently, on October 6th 2018, a 5.9 magnitude earthquake struck 20 km north of Port-de-Paix (North-West department). According to the Directorate of Civil Protection (DPC) latest Situation Report, 17 people were killed and 427 were wounded across the North West, North and Artibonite Departments. The earthquake caused significant damage to infrastructures and the DPC estimates that 261 institutional buildings and 29,433 homes were severely damaged, and that 2,668 homes were completely destroyed, with most of the damage being recorded in Saint Louis du Nord, Port de Paix et Gros Morne. This earthquake was the strongest to hit Haiti since the devastating earthquake that struck the country on January 12th2010. The overall coordination of the response is under the authority of the Prime Minister in close liaison with the President, while the operational coordination is led by the DPC with the support of national and international entities. While it is clear that the disaster response capacities of the DPC at national and departmental level especially with the Emergency Operation Centers (EOC) have been improved, the massive consequences of the above-mentioned shocks demonstrated that there remain considerable gaps in disaster preparedness and response capacities of DPC local actors. Since 1994, IOM Haiti's Disaster Risk Reduction and Disaster Management program has been designed to prepare for and respond to potential disasters in the near term as well as reduce the risk faced by the Haitian population to potential disasters in the future. To achieve this objective of preparing and responding to potential disasters, the IOM DRR Unit has implemented diverse activities that include Emergency preparedness and response, Evacuation shelters-related activities, Community-Based Disaster Risk Reduction, and Reduction of Physical Risk/Mitigation. To ensure the sustainability of IOM's actions, all activities are conducted in the context of capacity building of state authorities, including the DPC at the national level and local authorities at the commune and neighborhood level. The DPC 2018 National Contingency Plan forecast that 65,000 families would require immediate NFI/shelter assistance if a disaster were to occurs in the southern departments, as it continues to be the most vulnerable zone prone to natural disasters. Today, if there was an emergency in Haiti or within the Caribbean, the total available NFI stockpile (including IOM, and other actors) could assistance an estimated 25,000 families. It is due to this significant gap in available resources for emergency preparedness and response that IOM aims to increase the availability of prepositioned NFIs as well as maintaining and managing warehousing throughout Haiti IOM, in its collaboration with DPC in disaster risk management and preparedness, was able to identify many lessons learnt and unresolved issues which are likely to worsen the effect of the upcoming tropical cyclone on the Haitian Population if not improved. Building upon solid results achieved in the previous interventions and based upon IOM’s lessons learned, as well as the key strategic priorities of the DPC 2018 Contingency Plan, IOM’s proposed Action aims to strengthen the capacities of the disaster preparedness and response DPC local authorities through a multifaceted intervention.

Activities or outputs

Project Activities include:1. Shelter Working Group Coordination: As co-lead of the Shelter/NFI WG, IOM aims to maintain and continue on going activities through close coordination and collaboration with the partners of the Shelter/NFI WG in promoting emergency response and providing technical advice and guidelines to partners. The Coordination will ensure information gathering and analysis on ongoing shelter and NFI response activities. Coordination will be decentralized at departmental level. North West, North, Grand’Anse and Artibonite departments will be considered priority areas. 2. Warehousing Maintenance and Management for 4 warehouses locations.- Equipment maintenance- Warehousing Security and Safety- Quality control and Pest control- Documentation flow- Stock handling and stock rotation- Inventory management - Legal consideration (Leasing)- TransportationThese activities mentioned above, aim to ensure the warehousing management of NFI stockpile for 12,000 households, prepositioned in 4 warehouses, Port-au-Prince, Gonaives, Cap Haitien and Les Cayes. These NFIs will be made available for emergency responses in coordination with the DPC and other partners.The NFI stockpile for 12,000 households is composed of:- Plastics sheeting, 4mX6m: 12,000 units- Blankets, Wool (BW01): 12,000 units- Kitchen sets (KS05-60L): 8,000 units- Water Container Collapsible, 10L: 12000 units- Hygiene Kits – Family: 12,000 units3. Capacity building support to warehouse staff in order to enhance their knowledge on NFI and warehouse management.Warehouse management plays a crucial role in the successful operation of IOM. In an effort to strengthen the level of response in NFI and Warehouse management, IOM aims to provide the warehouse staff with capacity building and training activities especially on the latest warehouse management techniques. Once the advanced capacities of the warehouse staff have been build, they will be integrated into the NFI Warehousing and NFI distribution network response during a disaster event. 4. Rehabilitate/repair priority Emergency Evacuation SheltersIOM seeks to expand its programming in the most prone disaster risk areas in order to assist the vulnerable population to better prepare for future disasters as well the 2019 hurricane season. Thus, based on the existing evacuation shelter master list, IOM along with DPC authorities at deparmental level will identify priority Emergency Evacuation Shelters that are in need of repair/rehabilitation. Bearing in mind the multitude of needs among Emergency Evacuation Shelters, in consultation with the DPC, IOM aims to complete minor structural roof repairs and rehabilitation but also some WASH facility rehabilitation when needed in cooperation with WASH sector. 5. Support the creation and the training of evacuation shelter management committees for the targeted key priority Emergency Evacuation shelters.IOM proposes to support the CTEGAP Committees in the development of a database for the existing trained shelter managers in the targeted departments, reinforce their capacities, constitute the different committees and assign them to the existing key priority shelters. Moreover, as recommended by the shelter management guidelines, the responsible for the buildings used as shelters will integrate the shelter management committee. In addition, the project team will ensure that the assigned Shelter management committees are incorporated within the departmental contingency plan. During the training, the referral mechanism system on how to manage protection cases is highlighted. 6. Support to the DPC: IOM proposes to continue supporting DPC and other partners in the functioning of the thematic committee including the drafting of meeting minutes, agenda, administrative details. IOM will support the thematic committee in its work in order to ensure policy coherence at the central level and to provide support to the regions as needs are identified.
