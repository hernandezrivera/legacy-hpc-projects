Project Summary

According to the HNO 2021 draft issued by the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), there are 13.4 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian assistance - an increase of 21% compared to 2020 - with needs increasingly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy. .
Statistics indicate an increased exposure to ongoing hostilities, particularly in northwest and northeast Syria. Limited access to services; Displacement, which often separates children from family members, including primary caregivers, disrupting education. 2.45 million children between 5 and 17 years of age are out of school, which increases the risks of reported protection, cases of torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, denial of and lack of access to humanitarian assistance due to lack of civil documentation, among other factors. Wars and the obstacles arising from them
. The project aims to reduce the risks of the remnants of war and enhance community awareness of dealing with them by expanding the services provided at the Al-Haramain Foundation Center for Soldiers by increasing the following protection activities interventions: 1. Educating women, men and children. With regard to the remnants of war through the mobile teams. 2. Implement case management services that address the vulnerabilities of the injured and primary care providers associated with immediate risks. These services will be provided by workers specialized in case management through Al-Haramain Foundation 3. Executing surveys and identifying places of waste contamination.
4- Building the capacity of workers in the field of case management. Protection activities will be implemented in the Al-Junudiya area of ​​Jisr Al-Shughour and the surrounding area, and the project will target host communities, IDPs, returnees, community centers and camps. The center will serve the most vulnerable people. All activities align with the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan as well as the Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives and Funds Allocation Sheet. Services will be provided for one year. The project will coordinate with affected populations and stakeholders and is designed to promote gender balance. The project aims to include children with special needs, and all activities will be implemented according to the recommendations of the Protection Cluster and the World Health Organization in order to deal with Covid-19 and the necessary preventive measures to protect the beneficiaries and humanitarian workers. The project will be monitored by the M&E team, in order to monitor the progress of the project and ensure that the activities are implemented in accordance with the agreed work plan and indicators. The project will ensure accountability of affected populations and stakeholders through transparent accountability, complaints mechanism, feedback mechanism, and workers' competency monitoring.

4 - Needs

In parts of the country, the civilian population is still exposed to ongoing and new hostilities, resulting in civilian casualties and forced displacements as people seek safety. Country- wide, the prolonged and widespread use of weaponry has left behind a range of explosive ordnance, each posing their own risks worsened with each subsequent year of the crisis.▪ One explosive incident every 10 minutes was recorded in the past five years (2015-2020). Trend shows an increased use of improvised explosive devices, notably in the Northwest and Northeast of the country, makes responding to the threat more difficult.▪ One third of populated communities is estimated to be contaminated by explosive ordnance.▪ 10.3 million people, approximately 1 in 2 Syrians, live in areas affected by explosive ordnance▪ Men and boys are considered to be the most exposed to the direct, immediate threat of explosive incidents.▪ Nearly 1 out of 4 recorded victims is a child. 61% of recorded child casualties were killed or maimed while playing in a housing yard, building or in the vicinity ofresidential areas. Among all recorded direct child victims, on average 4 out of 10 are killed and 6 out of 10 are injured.▪ Based on recorded data, for each known explosive accident, an average of 1.5 people are killed and 2 people are physically injured. Among direct victims of explosive ordnance of have lost a body part or function, almost 50% suffered a limb amputation, and 20% lost a sensorial function (eyesight, hearing). 2 of 3 survivors will sustain lifelong impairment.▪ Contamination by explosive ordnances continues to cause catastrophic physical and mental impacts for the population living or returning to contaminated areas, but also reduces peo

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: COVID-19 pandemic and its impact on awareness activities, case management and training probability: high Mitigation: - Maintaining social distance between beneficiaries and the project staff - Use methods of protection, including masks, gloves and sterilizers - Avoiding meetings that contain many beneficiaries - Spreading awareness on ways of protection around the project implementation locations and on transportation means of the project - Transfer project personnel in batches within transportation means to reduce congestion - If necessary, implementation will be terminated in the field and follow up on cases that have a high-risk caseRisk 2: activities are suspended due to the conflict. probability: medium. Mitigation: in the event of attacks or invasion of the region, the service center will be moved to the Jisr Al-Shughour area in coordination with the local authorities to ensure the safety of project personnel and beneficiaries and after acquiring the required approval
