Project Summary

The context in Syria remains critical, with a complex set of issues affecting the population, particularly in NW. The number of people in need has increased from 14.6 million in 2022 to 15.3 million in 2023 (HNO 2023). Over 27% of Syrian families are psychologically distressed due to conflicts, hostilities, displacement, or death. The earthquake in NWS, along with attacks on Idleb and Aleppo since October 2023, has caused a significant increase in displacement, compounding existing problems and heightening protection risks. Conflict-related needs persist in NWS, with inadequate basic services, destruction and degradation of infrastructure, leaving almost half of the population without access to healthcare and worsening economic conditions. Women and girls in NWS face various forms of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), including forced marriage, forced labor, workplace exploitation, beatings, and domestic violence, with vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, and widows being disproportionately affected. Safety audits conducted by the IRC revealed a 100% increase in security concerns specifically affecting women and girls, with a surge in reported cases of rape and sexual violence, often met with extreme measures, perpetuating a distressing cycle of trauma.
The proposed project is designed to address gender inequality by supporting women and girls' access to protection services and empowering them to challenge social norms perpetuating gender-based violence (GBV). It targets vulnerable individuals, including those with disabilities, GBV survivors, and female-headed households, while considering their distinct needs through gender-sensitive approaches based on needs assessment data and research. Under the project, IRC WPE teams will provide specialized GBV case management services, cash assistance, psychosocial support (PSS) individually and in groups, life skills programs for women and adolescent girls, awareness-raising campaigns and sessions, safety audits, and formation of women committees. Protection risks such as GBV, limited-service access, and entrenched social norms are addressed through comprehensive interventions, with protection mainstreamed across target locations and referrals made to internal and external providers. Additionally, the project implements a Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) strategy, engaging community members as key implementers and conducting various monitoring and evaluation initiatives, including satisfaction surveys, focus group discussions, post-distribution monitoring, safety audits, learning activities, knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) surveys, training evaluations, and regular collection of complaints and feedback. Mitigating measures are in place to address risks such as accessing vulnerable populations, inadequate community engagement, and resource limitations, ensuring the project remains adaptive and responsive while upholding stringent PSEA measures to prevent any form of exploitation or abuse.
