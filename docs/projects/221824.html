Project Summary

The proposed project envisages ensuring access to inclusive quality emergency education for 11,600 children affected by conflict in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panykang, Bor South and Pigi/Canal in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. The project also aims to mainstream protection of children from physical dangers, abuse, exploitation, recruitment and psychological distress as part of proposed education activities. Key proposed activities included: Training of 175 teachers on child friendly teaching methodology; Strengthen the capacity of Parents teacher associations ( PTA ) in 16 target schools; Support back to school campaigns to promote school enrolment and retentions of children in schools; Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 10,000 emergency-affected IDPs, returnees and host community children including children with disabilities ; Support enrolment and retentions of 11,600 learners (5000 boys, 6600 girls ) at Early Childhood Development (ECD), Primary and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) in 16 schools; Distribute teachers' kits for 175 teachers and student kits for 10,000 children; Distribute 32 recreational kits for 16 schools. Renovate 16 Temporary Learning Space (TLS) with segregated WASH facilities in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panykang, Bor South and Pigi/Canal to create access, quality and enabling environment for 10,000 children and 175 teachers; Provide monthly incentives for 175 Volunteer Teachers (110 Male, 65 Female) including training on child protection and Psychosocial Support (PSS); Train 88 (48 male and 40 female) Members of Parent Teachers Association on their roles and responsibilities ; Support 39 Community mobilizations/back to school sessions conducted involving Parent Teacher Association (PTA) and School Management Committee (SMC) and community representatives ; Support quarterly joint monitoring and technical support visit along with local education office in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panykang, Bor South and Pigi/Canal counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile States.

1. Needs

About 2 million children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years of age (53 per cent are girls), along with 1 million children between 3 and 5 years of age, encounter significant obstacles in accessing education including early marriage and GBV, especially for girls (HRNP 2024). Jonglie and Upper Nile are among the states affected by recurrent inter-communal fighting, floods and also receiving large number of returnees and refugees from Sudan. These contributed to broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. In addition, about 139,000 refugee children also need education support. About 900,000 returnees and refugees arrived to South Sudan since the advert of conflict in Sudan in April 2023. Upper Nile is the major entry route and destination area for significant number of returnees and refugees (IOM/UNHCR 2024) . As a result of new arrivals, the schools in Renk, Malakal and Fashoda are over stretched. Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. In Jonglei and Upper Nile active conflict and ,recurring communal violence and floods negatively impacted education activities. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities, as well ass destruction or occupation was in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021 also mentioned as barriers to access education facilities. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as impediment for learner's access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile have been the worst impacted by floods. Malakal continued to host children and communities displaced following armed clash in Tonga in 2022. An estimated 18,000- 27,000 people reported displaced from Tonga, Panykang County and its surroundings (OCHA , 2022). Prior consultation on education needs and gaps in Malakal Town and PoC highlighted that the schools and learning spaces in both locations have been over stretched and facing funding constraints to expand education access to the needy children. With an increasing arrival of IDPs to Malakal Town and PoC, the current learning spaces/schools do not have the capacity to absorb additional children. Key issues affecting child education include lack of payment for teachers, limited technical capacity of teachers on teaching methodology, shortage of school facilities and educational supplies and low value for girls education and protection risks including child marriage and GBV. Hence, this project is envisaged to provide education in emergency services to 10,000 children affected by conflict and flood induced displacement in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panyikang, Bor South and Pigi Canal Counties. The project will strengthen the capacity of teachers on teaching methodology and focuses on enhancing the capacity of Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) in education in emergency and school management in line with humanitarian needs overview and education cluster priorities .
