Project Summary

Child protection mainstreaming project aims to empower child protection system within local communities of Northern Western Syria, by ensuring that child protection concerns and considerations are taken into all other sectors of the humanitarian response to maximize the impact of the child protection services in these communities and minimize the risks to children which may exacerbate from programming without children’s safety and will being taken into considerations.
Hurras will operate on many aspects to reach the goal of the project, such as:
- Integrating child friendly spaces with school activities: Hurras is targeting to engage 1,500 children in structured, sustained child protection programmers, within 60 schools libraries. And 300 children through the community centers.
- Case management: Hurras has will equipped and professional team in each office who can handle 100 cases per time through case management system and children’s emergency fund. Hurras will manage 500 children through this project.
- Child protection training; to ensure adequate programming, 3 school staff of each of the 180 schools that Hurras works with will receive training in regarding child protection or child save guarding. In addition, as Hurras is committed to raise the capacity of all partners who is working with Hurras, Hurras is planning to train all 20 suppliers on child protection and child safeguarding policies.
- Provide microgrants attached to the 28 established community-based child protection committees, to encourage local initiatives of raising the level of child protection within their communities.
- Awareness raising; Hurras has developed multi- staged raising awareness program which targets 100 health pediatric clinics, 60 schools, and 28 communities. Health pediatric clinics would have a stand in the waiting room which will contain child protection messages in brochures and awareness raising children magazine, this magazine (Tayara Warak) will also be distributed in 60 schools, to raise awareness of children about their rights and how to protect themselves in child friendly language. In addition, Hurras will provide raising awareness campaigns through its 28 child protection local committees and the community centers for child protection awareness raising and parenting programs to reach out for 5600 adults and 6000 children.
- Establish and support the running of school-level child protection committees, HRS has established 168 committees in schools and could scale up to include another 162 schools. Each committee gender balanced representation of 3 parents, 3 school staff and the head teacher. Parents are elected during parent/teacher meetings.
- Youths programs, conduct cycles of training for youths and adolescents, through established 3 community centers about civil rights and engagement, life skills, and leadership along with vocational trainings, to support initiatives led by this age groups and provide them options which are limited for them. Each centre can hold up to 20 youths per cycle and Hurras would support 2 youth initiatives per centre.

Needs

Entering its eighth year, the Syrian conflict has killed at least 470,000, injured more than a million and displaced 6.5 million people within Syria and over 4 million in Turkey, Lebanon, Jordan, Europe and elsewhere, making it the largest displacement crisis globally.According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for Syria, “Negative coping mechanisms have increased in use due to eroding community cohesion and exhausted resources, including economic assets necessary to survive.” The same report highlights that a greater number of people in need for protection in the targeted communities of this action is alarmingly high for instance: (Daret Azza, Al Atareb, Al Dana). According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) Hurras has participated in this year, the most vulnerable category in the Dana, Atareb, and Daret Ezzeh subdistricts is children and youths as per interviewed persons. And 86% of evaluations stated the existence of early marriages within the communities, most of them are females between 12 and 17 years. Also, 51% of the interviewers stated, although of sensitivity of the subject, that there’s child recruitment within their communities, 17% of them are children below 12 years old. All the answers regarding child labor except for two, identified child labor within their communities as problem preventing children from attending schools. All of the above-mentioned problem can be managed through child protection case management system.Adolescents are forced to assume adult responsibilities, especially when their parents die or become ill, or family networks break down; girls find themselves bearing and raising children; boys and girls must care for siblings and family members or even head households and earn income. Based on Hurras experience since 2012 in the same context and other recommendations by key protection service providers in Syria the following issues have been identified for this project: lack of positive coping mechanisms for adolescents that might lead to recruitment, child labor and child marriage. Hurras was successful in establishing core case management teams in Idleb and Aleppo province, and mapped services in both provinces and is working to establish a referral pathway that links CP services to other sectors. In cases of forced displacement, HRS has also identified the issue of family separation and harm from explosive remnants as key threats. HRS will be working with communities to share protection messages via committees imbedded in those communities to ensure safe identification and referral for children facing protection threats to Hurras center in the area. Also, Hurras will ensure that the recruitment, child labor and child marriage are reduced by providing opportunities for children to participate in recreational and PSS activities through schools and community centers, in awareness raising sessions and information sharing about their rights, in youth empowerment programs for children of older ages and through case management system which will detect vulnerable children and support them with their needs, refer them to the needed services while accompanying them, and advocate for the cases in the communities. Finally, HRS will work to develop an integrated humanitarian child protection response plan that includes child protection, case management, and mainstreaming of child protection minimum standards into other sectors.

Implementation of activities

Community-based child protection for girls and boys is available in targeted locations- CFS’s will offer PSS sessions 1 session per day for each class followed by reading sessions. Each CFS will have a PSS facilitator trained by HRS to deliver these sessions. The space will offer 5 sessions per day to children in grades 1-6. The space will also offer after school extracurricular clubs for 1 hour immediately after the end of school day.- PSS programs will be integrated into schools using the Guidelines for Integrating PSS and CP into Education adopted by the CP and Education clusters in Turkey, by providing PSS sessions in school’s libraries.- 3 standalone CFS’s will act as a community centre and provide parenting programmes for mothers and ECD activities for children of 0-5 years old.- Awareness raising:Option 1 :CP key cluster messages placed on stands in health clinics that includes Tayara Warak (children’s magazine with child protection messages written in child friendly language) in pediatric clinics Option 2: Tayara Warak (Children’s magazine) distribution with key messages to schools Option 3: campaigns run by community-based committees.- Provide microgrants for the local child protection committees which will be used for awareness raising and local initiatives within the communities.- Establish and support the running of school-level child protection committees, HRS has established 168 committees in schools and could scale up to include another 162 schools. Each committee gender balanced representation of 3 parents, 3 school staff and the head teacher. Parents are elected during parent/teacher meetings.- Conduct cycles of training for youths and adolescents, through established CFS’s about civil rights and engagement, life skills, and leadership along with vocational trainings, to support initiatives led by this age groups and provide them options which are limited for them.Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations- in addition to Hurras’s case management system in which HRS’s team has been working over the last year to streamline the case management system and has been successful in improving the referral pathway and managing the expectations of both the community and the team with regard to the agreed vulnerability criteria for cases accepted on case management (the same agreed on in CP sub cluster SOPs), Hurras developed new case management program to include children with disability and/or serious medical needs that are not eligible for case management identified through assessment using UNICEF/Washington group child functioning screening to 59000 children, by providing one -time cash assistant or medical supplies to improve their condition.Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns in Syria- training to frontline workers, education actors, health workers, suppliers, and key members of the communities, about child protection in emergencies, child protection minimum standards, safe identification of children and referrals, child safeguarding, … etc.HRS has an established M&E department they are mainly in charge of evaluating the output and outcome level. A detailed M&E plan is developed with the log frame and has clearly defined roles and responsibilities that include all the implementation team to ensure real-time monitoring, evaluation and learning for activities. All the M&E tools are already developed as those activities were practiced before. HRS experience proves that external M&E is also a very useful activity for learning and would usually do lessons learnt session to inform programs team. HRS has developed the M&E policy during the year 2017 to include FARM in its mechanisms: Complaint boxes, M&E WhatsApp Hotline linked with the HQ in Turkey, Direct interview with the M&E field officer. where it is mandatory to respond to a complaint, and it is not for the feedback only.

Protection Risk Analysis

Establish CFS’s and community centers - Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.- Likelihood: High. Impact: High- Mitigating measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and CSG policies- Protection risk: Girls, boys, women and men's physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision.- Likelihood: High. Impact: High- Mitigating measures: Same as above and 1. Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision. 2. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues). 3. Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community. 4. Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered). 5. Ensure consultation and mobilisation of key persons in the community. Provide culturally adapted information on key child protection issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth and development of children. Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations- Protection risk: Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately.- Likelihood: Medium. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• A safe environment allows the staff to implement the activities (low airstrike bombing).• No delay in the provision of financial resources.• Cooperation of local authority.• Engagement of local community with the committees.• Commitment of partner groups to Hurrass SOP.• Availability of services providers or services required as part of case management plan. Risks:Safeguarding incidents (Medium)HRS has a well-established safeguarding allegation system that is accessible by children, families and community members. Each activity is assigned a safeguarding officer and stakeholder are made aware of how to report incidents through multiple ways: online system, verbal communication with safeguarding staff and complaint boxes.Community and local council acceptance (Medium)Before engaging in any new community HRS outreach and community relation teams establish formal relationships with independent local councils and civil entities. The long history and positive reputation of HRS helped in establishing strong relations. Designated staff are assigned the role of dealing with any community conflict and all programs are reviewed prior to implementation to ensure they are both conflict sensitive and safe.The risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium)HRS has a duty of care policy and all staff are trained on safety measures including emergency locatorsSecurity incidents (Medium to High)HRs has a strong security department with qualified staff that monitors the security situation and put in place prevention and response plans. All staff receive safety training and all facilities are screened and provided with first aid and fire extinguishers in addition to a selected safe room and evacuation planInterference or influence by armed groups (Medium)HRs has strong relationships with the community and independent civil entities such as the health directorate, ED, and local councils and has been successful in the last 4 years in mitigating the risk and using such entities as a buffer. In addition, HRS has a clear SOP on how to behave in such incidents and clear disengagement criteria in case the risk could not be mitigated. Poor staff capacity (Medium)HRS invests in 4 weeks training of staff when they join, and ongoing coaching and supervision processes are in place. HRs work closely with partners to identify the capacity building gaps and put in place training programs to fill the gaps.Poor evidence-based and knowledge management due to remote management (Low)HRS has a strong M&E team that is independent of the program team. HRS uses staff from different offices and projects to cross-check its programming, in addition, the area managers who worked for 4 years with HRS are empowered to manage the day to day implementation.A potential loss of money due to theft or airstrikes (Medium)HRS’s finance team limit the cash on hand to a certain ceiling to minimize the risk and rely on an established network of cash transfer offices.
