Project Summary

The project aims to improve access of food for the food insecure, vulnerable households in across Somalia, with the focus of the most food insecure districts, through conditional and unconditional voucher distribution, depending on communities capacity. It also intends to support rehabilitation and/ or restoration of households community productive assets to create enabling environment for communities to recover from the shock of past droughts,displacements,adverse weather conditions and build resilience toward possible challenges in the future. A total 65,524 most vulnerable and food insecure population in Somalia/ Somaliland will be targeted.

Needs

The 2018 Gu rains were well above average except in the northeast which received slightly below average rainfall. River and flash floods occurred in many low lying areas, while cyclone Sagar brought torrential rainfalls and flooding to the northwest in May 2018. The 2018 Gu rains was the wettest season in nearly two seasons in nearly two decades in several areas that ended drought conditions that had persisted since the 2016 Gu. Despite the recent improvements the levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition will remain high across Somalia, with conditions worsening in northern and central Somalia with some areas moving from Crisis (IPC 3) and Emergency (IPC 4). A high level of acute malnutrition persists in several population groups due to underlying structural causes, an estimated 294,000 children will likely face acute malnutrition through December 2018, including 55,200 who are likely to be severely malnourished. According to the September 2018 FSNAU technical release, an estimated 1.5 million people are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December. These location require immediate cash and in kind assistance to arrest further deterioration. At the same time, an additional 3.1 million people in Somalia will remain in Stress (IPC 2), which brings the total number of people facing acute food insecurity in Somalia through December 2018 to 4.6 million. The report further states that livelihoods support is required throughout 2018, as worst affected populations in northern and central pastoral areas are highly likely to face larger gaps in their basic food needs. WV proposes to leverage its existing multi sector footprint to implement food security activities within its operational areas. In areas of IPC 3 and IPC 4, WV will implement mainly unconditional assistance with only a small fraction going to IPC 2 to support agricultural production. In areas of IPC 2 and IPC 3, the program will implement mainly conditional works programs.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention is aimed at bringing both immediate relief to human suffering and to help those affected to recover from the impact of the drought. In order to achieve full spectrum and shift from an initial survival and livelihood protection thresholds. WV aims to utilize the cash for work mechanism to rehabilitate community production assets, WV will seek to develop a follow-up recovery project to change or improve the household ability to enhance their productivity and, in the longer term, increase and/or diversify their income. The team will work on seeking funds to develop integrated resilience programming particularly in the thematic sectors of Food Security and Livelihoods, Protection, Nutrition, Disaster Risk Reduction-DRR and WASH within the targeted communities in the proposed regions. The vision is to aim for a holistic program implementation approach where-by targeted households will benefit from an array of sector activities to graduate from the cyclical poverty cycle and hence making them more resilient to withstand and recover from shocks. WV will also work closely with the local authorities and other partners in the region to see how the coordination will be upheld to support the transit from emergency to full recovery and sustainability of the project. WV will include a training component targeting vendors under the proposed food voucher approach so as to ensure market sustainability and also include a nutrition awareness training which will target the selected households to inform their future choice of food, this will help to enhance market sustainability and diversity of food consumption at household level. WV will engage beneficiaries during selection of durable solution/ resilience activities so as to ensure that only community-owned structures are prioritised and targeted to ensure sustainability and continued ownership of the structures after rehabilitation. In order to ensure that the assets rehabilitated are well maintained and remain operational beyond the project period, the project will put in place the following measures. Both direct and indirect beneficiaries will be involved in the selection of these activities in order to ensure full ownership and that the outputs will benefit all. The project will put in place community project management committees and train them on their roles and responsibilities, technical and managerial skills and operations and maintenance of rehabilitated assets to ensure they continue applying the same beyond the project life. WV will communicate clearly with all stakeholders the scale and scope of the proposed programme, including the time frame, to manage their expectations. The project will be horizontally linked with relevant government institutions and line ministries where they exist to provide technical support and oversight for selected activities. This will ensure continuation of support from the government and other prospective donors beyond the project.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Inaccessibility; Inaccessibility of project locations due to security concerns. WV operates in both secure and potentially insecure areas, this means even if situation gets insecure in some areas, the program may not come to a complete halt. In case of displacements, WV will conduct an assessment on the security situation to establish the feasibility of operation. During implementation, WV will conduct regular reviews of the security situation of the project locations together with local authorities hoping to identify red flags as early as possible and develop ways to mitigation them.During project designs and implementation, WV programmes will ensure that only the most vulnerable households and therefore staff will be provided with necessary skills to identify protection issues as well as consider social and gender relations, religion and culture. Therefore, in collaboration with communities, our FSL staff will undertake assessments that specifically identify child protection issues and ensure that their needs are taken care of and ensure broader FSL interventions ''do no harm'' to children e.g.children will not be allowed to participate in cash for work activities,and when when distributing food,pregnant women,people with disability receive their reactions first and women are nor exposed to any work norms that can expose them to risks

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Child Protection: Children from child headed households may end up being employed in the project's cash for work activities. To mitigate this risk, WV will ensure all activities targeting household members for cash for work have a criteria for beneficiary selection that ensures children are not be selected to engage in cash/ voucher for work interventions. Instead, child-head household will be supported with unconditional or other more appropriate intervention. This will prevent children being abused as child labor. All implementing staff and relevant stakeholders will be trained on WV’s child protection policies and will be required to adhere to them. Monitoring visit (planned or unplanned) will also be regularly done as another mechanism to identify possible child protection issue. Sexual gender based violence (SGBV): WV will aim to reduce distances covered by beneficiaries especially women to project sites under the cash for work project or food distribution sites so as to ensure that women participants can return home before dark to minimize risks of theft, physical or sexual abuse. At the minimum, voucher distribution time and location will be assessed to ensure these two are appropriate to the needs of women and children and that they don’t create protection concerns. Under the voucher program, traders will be trained on the voucher redeeming process to ensure beneficiaries do not spend too much time at the distribution point thus increasing opportunities for GBV against women beneficiaries or cases of neglect, abuse or exploitation of children due to absence of their care givers. WV will ensure safety through strict adherence and commitment to uphold the principle of “Do No Harm” in all its activities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WV has in place a safety and security structure, an area specific security plan and dedicated Safety and security department to monitor and advice on safety ensure. WV’s professional security advisor continuously monitors the security situation in each area of operation in close collaboration with UN Department for Safety and Security. WV’s security protocols are based on best practices in Security Management according to the prevailing security risk levels in the area. In addition, to reduce the risk and impact of violent attacks, full-time static guards are a necessity in all locations in Somalia, as are armed security escorts when traveling to accessible field sites. In the North, WV uses local security providers that are sanctioned by the regional governments and are part of the United Nations’ framework agreement, but in South Central districts, NGOs must make their own arrangements. WV’s Civil-Military Engagement Policy informs WV Somalia’s engagement with armed escorts for staff movements.
