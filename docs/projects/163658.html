Project Summary

The project aims to provide urgently needed WASH assistance to affected by clashes and displacement and vulnerable population in Libya including detained or stranded migrants and refugees in detention centers and urban areas, in line with two WASH strategic objectives focusing on the most vulnerable and affected population. Approximately 5000 migrants and 4000 affected Libyan families (Provision of WASH NFIs only) The proposed interventions include:
1. Rehabilitation of WASH facilities;
2. Provision of essential hygiene items to Libyans who have been affected by clashes and displacement and non-Libyans, including migrants and refugees at the DCs;
3. Awareness raising on hygiene practices.
Through those activities implemented in urban settings, IOM will also address the needs of a people requiring assistance within affected Libyan communities, as is practically not feasible to separate potential beneficiaries. In addition, IOM will abide by the division of labour agreed with other humanitarian stakeholders and approved by the sector coordinator in terms of locations for implementation, to avoid potential overlapping and encourage synergies. There shall be effort to enhance the resilience of local communities through awareness and knowledge sharing activities alongside the provision of material supports such as instillation of pumps and expansion of water networks to improve the overall WASH conditions.

1 - Needs

There are between 5,000 and 10,000 migrants and refugees detained in the government-held detention centres in Libya at any given time. The conditions of water and sanitation facilities at all detention centers remain dire and inadequate. As a result of overcrowding, the conditions are exacerbated with all government-run detention centres for migrants and refugees requiring improvements to the water, sanitation, garbage collection and hygiene situation. The sewage tanks are also insufficient for the needs, resulting in flooding of the holding cells and leading to alarming and life-threatening sanitary conditions for the detained migrants. Needs for distribution of the hygiene kits have been also observed among the migrants and refugees at the detention centres (DC) and disembarkation points (DP) including for the Libyan population during the displacement. The migrants disembarked after rescue from sea normally have no clothing or personal belongings with them.Similarly, the WASH conditions for the affected Libyans are also challenging, and the government capacity to address the situation remains limited. The existing facilities like water networks, delivery systems, etc. are in need of rehabilitation and also not adequate to meet the actual needs of the population.

4 - Protection risk analysis

IOM continues to advocate for the alternatives to detention and tries to reduce the sufferings of the migrants in DCs and at disembarkation points (DPs), while fully focusing on the protection needs of the vulnerable migrants, specially women and children. Migrants risk exploitation and smuggling from the DCs and DPs and during transfers from DPs to DCs and also between DCs. The DCs are temporary centres lacking very basic WASH facilities, which fail to address the needs of the migrants and refugees. Moreover, when adequate WASH facilities are missing, the risk of gender-based violence is increased. Lack of access and inadequate facilities contributes to the competitions over the resource which has the potential to fuel tension among the communities.

5 - Mitigation measures

While directly implementing various activities to address migrant and refugee needs, the project will mainstream protection as an integral part of its overall intervention. IOM will ensure that access for these particularly vulnerable groups is prioritized, that women and children have separate latrines/showers in DCs, have lighting, there is the possibility to always lock doors, and that they are safe and located at culturally appropriate areas. . Hygiene Kitss. HKs that will be distributed will be age and gender sensitive hygiene kits will have menstrual hygiene management (MHM) items and other items to address the needs of women and girls with dignity, diapers for children, diapers for elderly people). IOM will prioritize assistance to most vulnerable persons, including unaccompanied/separated children and youth, women at risk, persons with disabilities, persons with severe medical conditions, victims or persons at risk of trafficking/smuggling, and survivors of GBV. The project will identify specific protection needs of vulnerable groups, utilizing the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mobility Tracking with protection enhanced indicators. The findings will be taken into account throughout the project cycle including beneficiary selection, service modality selection, and delivery of assistance. Whenever cases with protection concerns are identified, they will be referred to IOM’s Protection Team for further assistance or referral. Additionally, the project will be implemented in line with the international humanitarian principles and protection values, including ‘do no harm’, non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation, empowerment and accountability. IOM will raise awareness and enhance capacities of national actors through various capacity building activities on rights and protection of migrants as well as alternatives to detention throughout the programming.
