Project Summary

Project Summary
The main humanitarian needs in Sudan result from different underlying root causes and drivers including instability and multiple conflicts, recurrent natural disasters including droughts and floods as well as disease outbreaks. women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from GBV due to violations of basic rights, insecurity, low economic status, denial of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women's rights and GBV as well as due to negative cultural and societal norms. Rape, physical violence, FGM, early/ forced marriages, denial of resources opportunities, and verbal abuse are some of the common violations perpetrated against women and girls. The 2023 HNO indicated that there are 3.1million People in need of GBV services, out of which the GBV Sub Sector is targeting 1.3 million.
Network of paralegals (NoP) will contribute to the three GBV Sub -Sector objectives:
1- Strengthen the provision of life- saving specialized quality GBV services, for survivors of GBV and vulnerable women and girls. (with a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 1), through the provision of different services to vulnerable women & girls including GBV survivors (Case management, PSS, referrals), assessments, community awareness-raising, and programming, provision of dignity kits, and capacity building of GBV frontline actors.
2-Enhance strategies for community resilience through the empowerment of women and girls and strengthened community-based interventions (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 2), by supporting women centers, community protection networks, and training of women & girls on vocational skills,
3- Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 3), by training on GBV for Non-GBV actors.
Overall target : 534,181 (15% of GBV SS Target).
The project will seek to face challenges in cooperation with the donors to reach the target groups due to the fact that the majority are victims of the civil war in Darfur who were forced to live in camps for internally displaced people (Jebel Marra, and Al Salam locality).
Some are born and raised in these camps where traditional social safety nets, previously provided in their villages of origin, no longer exist and medical services including sexual and reproductive health services, psychosocial and stigmatization services, legal services, and immediate physical care as necessary, such as safe food clothing in cases of Emergency, shelter, education, medicines and basic services are not available, including the destruction of women and children centers that were hit by the fall 2022 floods. Necessary consultations have been conducted with relevant sector leaders and government institutions to receive the required guidance and data. Consulted includes the United Nations Population Fund, UNICEF, and UNHCR, OCHA, Council for Humanitarian Aid, Council for Child Welfare, Police Sexual and Gender-Based Violence Unit, Maine. Health, Maine. Social Welfare. Issues, data on SEA cases and related concerns are discussed and collected.
The project is aligned with the main strategic objectives of the HR plan accordingly,.

1 - Needs

IDPs, returnees and vulnerable women and girls face challenges in South Darfur, especially Kass localities (South Jebel Marra areas) and El Salam localities. Gender-based violence (GBV) is a major concern within different communities affected by various emergencies, such as conflicts, tribal clashes/tensions, and floods. drought and disease outbreaks. Women and girls in displaced and refugee communities are at particular risk of gender-based violence. Therefore NoP plans to reach 2,900 in South Jebel Marra and Al-Salam localities, targeting 1,900 IDPs, 500 returnees and 500 host communities. The needs of GBV survivors are to access quality specialized life saving services, reporting mechanisms, and legal/judicial interventions, as the majority of communities in South Darfur lack access to GBV services (such as clinical management of rape, psychological support , and advice). legal aid, case management) List of specific project objectives to be achieved (in bullet points)(a) Improving means of protection and coordinating with government agencies working in the field of gender-based violence, with attention to the issues of those who do not have sponsorship or parental care (issuing identity documents and legal advice). (B) Establish, train and support mobile teams to provide psychological support to victims of sexual exploitation and abuse and provide psychological first aid, counseling and referral to specialized health clinics. And providing victims of gender-based violence with quality services at all levels (health - legal - psychosocial support). (c)Establish complaints mechanisms to report pressing societal issues to take action to address them. This will be through building Community Complaint Networks (CBCN) which are crucial in encouraging the reporting of complaints while also providing assistance and support services to victims/survivors. (D) Awareness campaigns as an essential part of the project. Educating victims/survivors and their communities of their rights as well as the support available for their realization and empowering women by raising awareness of the importance of their participation in decision-making at the village level on public affairs. (e) Providing small projects to support victims of sexual exploitation and abuse (education - primary health care and livelihoods). Provide income-generating projects to provide victims/survivors with knowledge and tools that enable them to become economically active and self-sufficient, help them regain their dignity and self-respect and enhance social support to facilitate their reintegration into their communities. Increased access to markets, transferable skills, and income-generating opportunities that support self-reliance, economic recovery, and women's empowerment and participation in decision-making positions (F)Provide medical care and treatment, including reproductive and sexual health care services, and provide immediate needs for hygiene items and other sanitary items (g)environmental protection and rehabilitation interventions to mitigate the impact of climate change and harmful human practices on the natural resource base; protection risks, human rights violations, and gaps in service delivery; Strengthen community protection mechanisms to address the needs of women and girls who have experienced GBV, strengthen preparedness and early warning capacities, and prevent or mitigate risks of GBV (h) Address immediate and acute GBV risks while identifying the needs of vulnerable IDPs, IDPs and vulnerable host communities affected by the crisis through the timely provision of life-saving GBV-related legal services (I) Strengthening the protection environment through robust GBV situation monitoring and analysis, and defining project objectives

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The activities and implementation design considered the impact on the environment with a mitigation and enhancement process, the planned women centers – if supported - will be rehabilitated using to the extent possible the no environmentally harmful materials. The promotion of fuel-efficient stoves - if supported - will contribute to the improved livelihood of vulnerable households while reducing environmental impact as fewer trees are cut for cooking fuel. In addition, during the dignity kits distribution, women and girls will be sensitized to environmentally friendly ways of disposing of the used sanitary wear. Through the women and girls’ empowerment program, the GBV AoR partners will explore the production of reusable sanitary pads as a replacement for disposable ones.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is core in the Network of paralegals (NoP) response and will be strengthened through continuous consultation with the affected population on the response modalities as well as their satisfaction with services provided. In addition(NoP) is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of SEA (NoP) will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. (NoP) partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regards to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities.This is done by conducting focus group discussions with women and girls in women centers. The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV.
