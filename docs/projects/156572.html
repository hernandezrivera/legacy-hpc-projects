Project Summary

IR is designing this NFI& shelter project to respond to those who are in need in this sector, in additional to the regular winter program that IR is doing every year , this year we are planning to shift our response from emergency response toward durable shelter solution mainly shelter rehabilitation in the IDP settlement and host communities. The target beneficiaries will be the most vulnerable beneficiaries by mainly targeting IDPs, female-headed households, and beneficiaries with disabilities, low income families. If one of the criteria mentioned above - and in accordance with an agreement with the local community - is fulfilled the beneficiaries are selected for the distribution process. The project will be directly implemented by IR team in Northern Syria in coordination with all the relevant stakeholders. The project will be implemented in the accessible location in Aleppo and Idleb governorate. We are planned to reach 150000 individuals. The project is designed based on Humanitarian Need overview 2019. The activities included in this proposal are in line with the NFI & shelter cluster objective and within the humanitarian response plan.
Below will be the project activities:
1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, Jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps).
1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, blanket, winter-specific shelter upgrades).
1.3 1.3 Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter mainly tent installation in camps.
1.4 2.1 Support to repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public

Needs

•4.2 million People remain in need of shelter support in Syria. Shelter and infrastructure damage has taken place on a massive scale, especially after the heavy bombardment of densely populated urban environments.. On the other hand, a significant number of IDPs were able or would like to return to their communities of origin, increasing the need to repair and rehabilitate damaged shelters to move toward solutions for those able to return. In terms of shelter, 5.3 million people live in shelters with multiple inadequacy issues, including lack of necessary bathing or cooking facilities, inadequate space, lack of privacy, lack of heating, lack of insulation, the inability to securely lock their home, and others. According to a recent study by the World Bank covering eight governorates, a conservative estimate is that 32 per cent of all housing stock in the governorates has been impacted by the conflict, with nine per cent destroyed and 23 per cent partially damaged. The damage and destruction varies across locations and between rural/urban areas, with some neighborhoods completely destroyed and beyond repair, and others with the potential to be salvaged. 3.7 million People living in Syria cannot carry out necessary repairs to their homes, principally because they cannot afford the materials and/or professional assistance required to carry out the rehabilitation. The overall impact of lack of income and high prices is illustrated by the data, which shows that up to 1.2 million families cannot afford rent costs, leading to increased vulnerability and use of harmful coping mechanisms. Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) issues remain a challenge on many fronts mainly in the overpopulated areas in North West Syria in Idleb and northern Aleppo. A lot of formal and non-formal IDPs settlements which were established on the lands of host community which cause many problems. Repair and rehabilitation of housing is only pursued following confirmation of HLP rights, however, many displaced people do not have sufficient documentation (many never had proper or formal documentation or lost them during displacement), and the processes and infrastructure to formalize or recuperate documents is not uniformly functional or accessible. There is increased risk of eviction and/or exploitation with major protection implications (especially for particularly vulnerable groups), while there are also the risks of rights violations (either of owners or occupants) and future disputes due to the difficulties in demonstrating ownership.• The accessibility of NFIs is more stable than the previous year, with 24 per cent of communities reporting a deteriorating situation, compared with 72 per cent in 2016.However, needs remain substantial, both in terms of life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs in North West Syria requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance; as well as longer-term needs, particularly among high severity of need communities requiring resilience oriented support to help reduce dependence on harmful coping strategies. Findings show a decreased need for standard/core NFI items and a significant increase in requests for tailored “supplementary” (i.e. non-core/basic) items specific to the situation faced by certain communities or population groups.

Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, Jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps). This will be implemented by IR field team inside Syria in coordination with the local community and other relevant stakeholders, procurement of essential nonfood items during the emergency situation. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable cases mainly female headed households, elderly people, people with special needs like disabilities or chronic diseases.1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, blanket, winter-specific shelter upgrades). This will be implemented by IR field team inside Syria in coordination with the local community and other relevant stakeholders, procurement of mattress, winter clothes, and thermal blankets plastic sheet, heaters... Priority will be given to the most vulnerable cases mainly female headed households, elderly people, people with special needs like disabilities or chronic diseases.1.3 1.3 Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter mainly tent installation in camps.This will be implemented by IR field team inside Syria in coordination with the local community and other relevant stakeholders, procurement of tents, plastic sheet during the emergency situation. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable cases mainly female headed households, elderly people, people with special needs like disabilities or chronic diseases.1.4 2.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labor, etc.) This will be implemented by IR field team inside Syria in coordination with the local community and other relevant stakeholders. IR team will target partially damaged houses, pathways and roads in the camps. To achieve the above activities will go through the below process:1. Procurement Process: the procurement exercise for this project followed the IRW procurement policy and procedures. IRW Syria Operation procurement and logistics department conducts the tender exercise in close coordination and under the supervision of the procurement department at IRW HQ level.2. Coordination: On the cluster level, IR coordinated with camp coordination cluster management (CCCM) and NFI/shelter working group to avoid duplication and fill the gap of need which occurred during project implementation. For the internal actors, IR also attends coordination meetings with local communities of different areas inside Syria 3. Community involvement: the communities on the ground have been involved in supporting IR team in finding the need assessment, identifying the number of the targeted beneficiaries, arranging the beneficiaries during the distribution process. The local communities also play a good role in the safety and security of IR team, we coordinate with them during the movement of the team and the items.4. Distribution Process: distribution was done in coordination with local councils and local communities in the targeted locations. The process including the final list of beneficiaries and their location, informing the beneficiaries about the date of distribution and the items that will be distributed , beneficiaries arrangement during distribution. IR distribute the items according to the list that they have collected prior distribution, they call beneficiaries by names, check their IDs and let them sign the beneficiary list. All collected beneficiaries will be shared later with Gaziantep office to be checked and archived.5. Monitoring Process: monitoring is taking place during all project phases through a direct follow up of all project activities by the program department based in Gaziantep and by the related project team. Monitoring is done by follow up meeting with team based in Syria and field visits of senior staff to the distribution sites.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members (IR will use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. IR will make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs )2. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion) ( IR will ensure that recruited staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct.IR will develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly.IR have a complaint mechanism in place.)3. Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated (Will follow HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster.IR will not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines.)

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Takeover of the trucks moving the IR supplies: IR team will make continuous assessment on the accessible safe routes before the movement of the goods. The team will choose appropriate time of truck movement. The movement will take place only on the main and frequently used roads during daylight. Additionally, journeys planned beforehand and based on the latest security and accessibility information • Diversion of aid by the belligerent parties: IR field team assesses any area before commencing any type of activities. If there is any probability of aid diversion in a certain area, IR refrains from working in it. Additionally, IR has good networks with local communities and stakeholders, which enables IR to know about any potential risks of such type and take the necessary precautions. • No access to certain target area: Islamic Relief can cancel the inaccessible target areas from the distribution plan. Islamic Relief will reallocate the goods to more secure and accessible areas. On certain occasions, delaying the distribution for a limited time until accessibility is again possible.• Closure of the Turkish border: IR will coordinate with Turkish authorities to enter the goods through relief cross points and to have exceptional border passing permissions.
