Project Summary

Peace Corps proposes an emergency education response programme focused on provision of life saving safe and quality protective education interventions to school going children(3-17 years) in Jonglei (Pibor), Lake (Rumbek East) and Warrap (Tonj North ) of South Sudan. This project targets 9,140 beneficiaries of which 8580 will be school going children aged 3-17 years (3783 boys, 4797 girls ), and 560 adults (316 Male, 244 Female) This project will run for a period of 12 months covering the three terms and the targeted 18 schools in selected counties. This education project will incorporate and complement life-saving components such as psycho-social support, disaster risk reduction and COVID-19 prevention and mitigation measures. positive disciplining to prevent corporal punishment, referral mechanism, and availability of gender-sensitive WASH in schools is essential for keeping children in the classroom and ensuring their safety and well-being, with clean and secure separate toilets for girls and boys and hand-washing facilities to both students and teachers. PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Health and stakeholders to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed education needs of affected populations. PCO will also synergize with health and nutrition cluster partners who manage the health facilities and OTPs centers in the counties of operation, by offering outreach services to supported schools.
The Key activities of this project will include setting up/rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLSs), providing psycho-social support, providing learning materials, providing WASH facilities, provision of MHM to Adolescent girls to maintain their dignity, training PTA members and teachers on psycho-social support, provision of ECD services to 3-5 years children, training of teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies, training of community education committee members and Education officials in school management, monitoring and record keeping and providing, training of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) in life-skills and basic vocational courses. PCO will also set-up a simple, safe and accessible Community Based Complaints Mechanism for SEA and protection of school children to add efficiency to strengthen reporting mechanism by incorporating local solutions into the overall SEA reporting system.
The organization will involve SMCs, PTAs and Community leaders in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of SEA with the aim to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will also be involved in the design of TLS, identification of a safe location for construction of TLS and monitoring teachers and pupil attendance through the PTA.
The project will also mainstream gender, GBV, conflict sensitivity and protection with hopes of contributing towards protection of children from risks, GBV and other protection challenges that have a potential of disrupting their education, enhance their psycho-social development, improve hygiene and sanitation and alleviate strains on their academic performance.
PCO is currently implementing education project in 42 schools in Wau and Jur River counties supported by WFP running up to December 2021, implementing integrated Education and Protection project in 8 schools in Pibor GPAA supported by SSHF 1st Allocation and Multi Year Response Education Programme project in 20 schools in Gogrial West and Tonj North Counties funded by ECW and so is familiar with Jonglei and Warrap States. PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG, WASH Cluster Co-Lead and WASH/UNICEF Core Pipeline Manager in Pibor GPAA and has established presence across the entire 2 States and PCO is best placed to implement this project.

1 - Needs

The persisting humanitarian crisis, displacement, protracted conflict emergency, poverty, famine, outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic and livelihood insecurity have been the main drivers leading to interruption of education and absence of protection for children 3-17 years old, leaving 3.4 million children in need. According to a recent Humanitarian Needs Overview Report 2021 the number of people in need has increased by 400,000 in 2021, compared to 3 million people in 2020. In addition, the recent impact of COVID-19 pandemic, the influx of IDPs, spontaneous returnees’ refugees from neighbouring countries and the IDPs returning to their original residential areas are demanding educational access and protection for their children that put pressure on education sector. All the years of conflict, famine, floods and general instability have taken their toll on South Sudan’s education system. Many schools have been destroyed/ stopped functioning due to conflicts/ unfavourable conditions, and or stand empty because children and teachers are too scared to attend, while some parents are no longer able to pay for uniforms and books as they deal with unemployment and the effects of a chronically devalued currency. Considering these challenging predicaments in the operating environment and limited access to education for the targeted host community children and their demand for education, the majority of these children are out of schools due to insecure stances and destruction and closure of schools. The closure of schools and all learning institutions followed by COVID-19 had devastating effects on the education of all children including refugees and jeopardized critical gains made over the years. It also exposed them to different forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies while some parents are no longer able to pay for uniforms and books as they deal with unemployment and the effects of a chronically devalued currency, lack of qualified teachers; a damaged education infrastructure; overcrowded classrooms; inconsistencies in the provision of teacher incentives; a shortage of learning materials; lack of hand washing, lack changing rooms for adolescent girls during menstrual period poor sanitation and water facilities in schools; the long distance a child needs to travel to school; and little to no support services in schools for children with disabilities. 68% of schools in South Sudan lack proper WASH facilities according to Nation Wide Education Cluster Assessment (November 2018), lack of proper WASH in schools exposes learners to risks of diseases, coupled with high level of malnutrition, and weak immune system. A total of 2700 incidents of grave violations affecting 81,000 children and 2300 GBV cases reported to service providers by the UN since 2014. According to the (IRNA) joint assessment mission in Lekuangole, Gumuruk, Verthet/Doren and GPAA between in September 2020 in which PCO was involved, Conflict and floods destroyed School supplies including furnitures, Tempory Learning spaces were destroyed and Children of school going age are left to survive on their own. The floods also affected a total of about 26,000 in Lekuangole, 29,000 plus in Gumuruk, 7,000 plus in Verthet and in Doren was 5,000 plus. Many trained and qualified teachers fled during the years of hostilities, many of whom have not yet returned and the existing teachers came forward as volunteers from within their communities. Top of the concerns include lack of clean water and latrines in most of these operational schools in Pibor Tonj North and Rumbek East Counties, no school is benefiting from WFP school feeding programme. A number of community members mentioned the need for school girls’ dignity kits, which has prevented girls from at times attending classes.

Peace Corps Organization’s activities will focus on three (3) cluster objectives; Cluster Objective 1, 2 and 3 as follows: 1.Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services to emergency and COVID-19 affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) by 2021 in counties under (IPC 4 and 5). .Project Inception workshops in selected counties to share project document with stakeholders. This will be done at the start of the project as a sign of accountability and transparency. • Social mobilization and meetings with respective schools on implementation modalities. This will be conducted to gather perspectives from various stakeholders on the roll out modalities on ground and as well agree on the schools to support. Improve enrollment of children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers. This will be done through Sensitizing the community on back to school to improve on enrolment of school going children aged (3-17). • Setting up/rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLSs). In selected schools TLSs will be rehabilitated to offer protective learning environment to the pupils. • Providing learning materials and supplies pupils aged(3-5) children with ECD kits which will include pupils learning materials like school-in a box and also teachers training guidebooks • Providing gender-sensitive WASH facilities both hardware and software components of WASH in schools. This will include things like disability and gender sensitive latrines construction/rehabilitation, hand washing facilities installation, boreholes repairs and hygiene and sanitation awareness and messaging etc. Procure and supply sanitary and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, masks, and sanitizers etc) to both teachers and students. 2. Improve the quality of education services of at all levels education system and protect the wellbeing of all children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of the COVID-19 crisis on learning environments by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5. • Training of teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and Child-Centered methodologies and on impact of COVID and its protection mechanisms. Training of community education committee members and Local Education officials in school management, monitoring and record keeping. Train and update teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms. • Training of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) in life-skills and basic vocational courses. • Conduct awareness raising on school-related gender-based violence (SRGBV) among students, teachers, communities and local officials about sexual & physical violence and implementing prevention strategies. • Conduct training of staff members on PSEA • Conduct routine monitoring of project activities and reporting. 3. Enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergency and the transmission of COVID-19 pandemic on learners, teachers, and communities by 2021 in counties under (IPC 4, and 5). • Training PTA /SMCs members and teachers on psycho-social support and Disaster Risk Reduction. Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures. Organize back to learning campaigns to all the community and stakeholders. This will be done through sensitization of parents, children and the school community at large. There will be routine monitoring of all supported schools and the entire project by both PCO and also externally by stakeholders to ensure activities are in line with the project design document. Reporting will also be done as required and 5Ws will as well be filled and shared monthly.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PCO will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. During signing of contracts, all staff will sign a PSEA code of conduct. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training to the beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available for them to report PSEA and contact persons.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the project lifecycle. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee comprises of diverse representatives such as pupils representative, PTA/SMC, Teachers and with support from project staff. The regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) will be a core component of beneficiary communication and Accountability mechanism. Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and reiterating implementation strategies and work plans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

PCO has presence in the locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. PCO will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project creating an awareness of the project and its goal including how to report complains and feedback and how they will be addressed. PCO will then set up an implementation committee composed of the same beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and other agencies on ground will be involved in monitoring to continuously review and gaps will be identified and discussed jointly and addressed in order to meet the main goal of the project. Key community members including individuals living with disabilities, women and girls will be involved during joint monitoring of the project and from the different constituency groups. A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage beneficiaries, listen to their stories and give upward feedback. For those who can write, a complains box will be put around distribution locations for upward complains and feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions will be held at project site with beneficiaries and community leaders to provide feedback. Sampling for FGDs will be representative for all affected groups of people. Where distribution is involved, a PDM will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

While PCO sets up a feedback and complains mechanism at the project location among the beneficiaries, at Juba level, a committee composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedbacks directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiaries advise and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. PCO is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. PCO is a very active national NGO on GBV in the three States and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. PCO, together with other agencies will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools PCO will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. PCO staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. PCO is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.

All projects activities will be implemented with a protection lens, ensuring that safety of women and girls, boys and men and taken into consideration through safety audits without compromising on quality and time.
