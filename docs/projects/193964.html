Project Summary

In this project, AORD will provide multi-Sectoral Protection (GP, CP and GBV), WASH, Education and Food Security & Livelihoods (FSL) interventions for Refugees in Blue Nile, West Kordofan and South Kordofan States. The total targets are 16,950 beneficiaries (3,322 Men, 3,458 Women, 4,983 Boys and 5,187 Girls).
PROTECATION intervention (GP, CP and GBV):
AORD will raise awareness on general protection topics for 3000 beneficiaries, Establish 12 Community-Based Structures (51 % from the females), Capacity building for 120 service providers on general protection topics and Provision 140 individual protection services (cash or in-kind) to increase the income for the targeted beneficiaries.
AORD will support 4130 individuals through the distribution of direct material Support (dignity kit). 1500 kits for identified SGBV survivors assisted with appropriate support. AORD will conduct 12-community awareness raising to raise awareness about GBV, and support 50 GBV survivors by receiving individual case management and Capacity building on GBV topics for 180 people.
AORD will support 1560 children through the provision of Case Management and referral services for children. Run and Maintain six child-friendly spaces with furniture, stationaries and equipment ( Solar Power System, computer) and assistance to 30 children with disability by reaching with direct services to improve their care and well-being.
The project will support WASH intervention for 16,950 beneficiaries (3,322 Men, 3,458 Women, 4,983 Boys and 5,187 Girls) through rehabilitating hand pumps, conducting vector control (spraying, draining standing water) hygiene promotion in communal setups accompanied by the Provision of Personal Hygiene Materials.
AORD will support the sanitation facilities by Constructing 20 household latrines and rehabilitating 100 household latrines and conducting Dispose of Solid waste and supporting its management.
AORD will improve the education environment by implementing educational activities for 1,960 children, AORD support 180 Teachers by providing training, 300 School furniture sets will be provided to primary schools and 20 classrooms will be constructed in primary schools supported by Hand washing facilities inside the targeted schools.
AORD will support 630 beneficiaries through FSL interventions through the provision of agricultural inputs for 200 persons and livestock support for 150 persons. AORD will conduct vocational and skills training for 140 people and provide support activities for 140 youth.

1 - Needs

Due to current political developments, continuing economic degradation, and reoccurring conflict, sector needs are expected to increase in 2023.The Government of Sudan’s inability to fully implement the Juba Peace Agreement and the National Plan for the Protection of Civilians (NPPOC) has been accompanied by increased displacement and conflict. Accountability of armed actors for violations of international humanitarian and human rights law remains elusive. In the context of Darfur, the withdrawal of UNAMID in Darfur created space for the greater localized and cyclical conflict to occur, increasing violence in Darfur to levels unseen since the mid-2000s. There is also an increase in violence in South Kordofan, West Kordofan and Blue Nile State with IDPs also fleeing to surrounding states and Khartoum. Durable solutions for IDPs and returnees remain largely unachieved. Most refugees live in out-of-camp settlements, host communities and urban areas, while others stay in 24 camps (10 in East Sudan, 1 in Blue Nile State 10 in White Nile State, 2 in East Darfur State and 1 in Central Darfur State). Sudan continues to generously host and receive additional asylum-seekers.The BN region has been in conflict for decades with the ensuing humanitarian crisis, causing widespread insecurity and destruction. Over the years, violence has caused the displacement of civilians from the affected areas internally and to neighbouring countries. The conflict ceased temporarily in 2005 with the Comprehensive Peace Agreement, but in September 2011, conflict erupted again between the Government of Sudan and the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N). In 2017, the split of SPLM N into two factors resulted in fighting and further internal displacement. Protracted since then and divided into government and non-government-controlled areas, civilians have received limited attention and support. The political landscape and operational context in Sudan changed substantially since August 2019 following the establishment of the Transitional Government. This triggered a new phase of re-engagement, implementation of the Juba Peace Agreement (JPA) of October 2020, which made the Blue Nile an autonomous region, and is a critical pointer to initiate development and long-term support. The events after the 25 Oct 2021 military takeover continue to shape the political landscape and context in the Blue Nile. A total of 4,445 refugees have arrived in three localities of the Blue Nile as of September 2022 (3600 are South Sudanese)Kordofan operation is hosting 125,907 refugees of which 66,166 are biometrically registered whilst the remaining have been registered at level one by COR as of 2022. All documented refugees in the Kordofan operation are from South Sudan.There are 1,284 new arrivals in 7 South Kordofan localities as of September 2022, 1,296 SSRs in At-Tadamon and 2,736 SSRs in Kadugli. The total number of new arrivals refugees in West Kordofan is 3,719 SSR. There are three locations in Ghubaish 11560 SSR and Babanusa 6421 SSR( Babanusa, Babanusa town, and El Tibbun). At Tadamon locality is a priority area identified as in need of emergency assistance in South Kordofan state. It is made of four administrative areas namely: Al-Terter, Wakara, Al’Omkheirate, and Balula. At Tadamon a total of 191461 individuals (Locality department of Health planning figures, Ministry of Health 2021). and host an estimated 1,025 refugees registered from South Sudan (UNHCR-2022) and 780 IDPs from different parts of Sudan including Darfur. Despite over 90% of the community drawing livelihoods from subsistence farming (90%) and 10% accounting for mining /others (TGH Assessment Report -2021), an estimated 25% of the households in the locality have significant food energy gaps or livelihood change strategies with potential consequence to endanger lives or livelihoods (Sudan HNO-2022).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program; AORD will abide by Do No Harm principles. In terms of staffing, AORD will strive for gender balance in the recruitment of staff at all levels. Project staff and community leaders will be trained on gender issues PSEA and gender-sensitive approaches Protection will be incorporated across all sector interventions to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity during the implementation of the project. The approach will be based on Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS) including prioritization of safety & dignity, and avoiding causing harm by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of the interventions which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks.Targeted people will be granted access to assistance and services based on identified needs and without any barriers or discrimination. The project will provide special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable to accessing assistance and services such as disabled individuals, women and children. Community groups and targeted individuals including children will be supported to develop their self-protection capacities and ability to claim their rights to all services. In this regard, children and adolescents will be engaged in the planning, implementation and monitoring of this project through child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanisms, as well as conducting consultation with children during the implementation on issues in implementation, monitoring or evaluation targeting girls and boys collectively or individually to inform decision-making processes concerning their care, protection and other rights.AORD will also mainstream protection concerns in activities like men and women need privacy during a consultation, gender designated latrine sites inside the Schools to ensure that girls are protected and considered private.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AORD will ensure Accountability to Affected Persons (AAP) through a joint design of accountability in line with humanitarian standards. Consultations of Line ministries, target communities’ men and women, as well as girls and boys, will be actively involved in the process. To ensure “Do No Harm”, methods of receiving feedback confidentiality will be identified and communicated to community members appropriately to avoid exposure to further risks from sensitive issues. Mechanisms and approaches will be tailored to both the refugees and host communities as appropriate, ensuring the participation and inclusion of both throughout implementation. AORD is committed to aligning to the Core Humanitarian Standards focusing on standard # 5 Community feedback mechanism and response. To ensure the quality and accountability of the project, AORD will promote transparency, meaningful participation and inclusion through the following steps: 1. Enable the targeted populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence 2. Share project information at an early stage and on an ongoing basis with girls and boys, parents and caregivers, community leaders, and government stakeholders; 2. Host regular consultations/ listening forums with established groups (such as Water management committees), including women, boys and girls; 3. Ensure partner communities are fully aware of PSEA, Protection and Safeguarding Policy including how to make complaints or raise issues. 4. Share information on progress and lessons in the relevant coordination groups and key messages on child participation 5. Ensure a robust and accessible complaints mechanism and response procedure is put in place and appropriate for different community groups. The mechanism and timelines for expecting responses and how complaints and feedback are managed to inform programming; 6. Ensure that all staff, and stakeholders, are familiar with existing referral pathways both to service providers for supporting identified cases and referring to other agencies. In WASH/protection facilities, posters and leaflets will be posted and shared on program objectives, key activities, and ways in which beneficiaries can provide feedback to the implementing partner. Feedback will be collected where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, boxes, feedback, and a dedicated call-in number and meetings. Compliance boxes at the community level, community volunteers, women groups, and Community-based Networks are the mechanism put in place by AORD for suggestions and complaints and reporting on SGBV or any other type of complaints, ensuring their proper periodic review, analysis, reporting feedback and documentation. This intervention will strengthen the protective environment of the vulnerable population.
