Project Summary

The project seeks to provide comprehensive, responsive protection services for at-risk conflict and displacement affected people, to address protection risks, human rights violations, gaps in available service in relations to IDPs, returnees and the host community in five counties of Rejaf (Juba), Panyijiar, Mayendit, Koch and Rubkona. The project will target the most vulnerable people at risk, including returnees from Sudan and flood affected people. The objective of the project is to strengthen the protection of most vulnerable girls, boys, women, and men affected by conflict, displacement, and climatic shocks by reducing risks and vulnerabilities and increasing community capacities. Key to this action will be a focus on improving capacity, including community protection networks, for immediate response to violations and remedial action as well as strengthening the protective environment in target locations. The project will have a 60% protection mitigation and response component and a 40% access to justice, through legal information services, safe referrals on HLP monitoring and response (including eviction monitoring) and civil service documentation support - reinforcing sustainable community-based protection activities at community level. The project aims to build and strengthen local capacities within the community and support them to establish a protection framework to prevent and respond to violations. It will focus on improving capacity for immediate response to violations and remedial action as well as strengthening the protective environment in target locations. On immediate response, the action will aim to address acute protection needs of women, girls, boys, and men, stemming from violence, displacement, climatic shocks including rights violations, as well as coercion, and abuse. In terms of delivery modalities and outputs, the overarching objectives of the project will be achieved through a combination of complementarily reinforcing set of actions that are framed around the following core pillars; monitor protection trends (legal, HLP and rights violations) in targeted areas, in the framework of the Protection Monitoring System (PROMOS) of the Protection Cluster and informed by IRC's ongoing protection and legal analysis initiatives at target locations; promote inclusive identification and screening through assessment and analysis of persons at risk to receive protection services and basic needs; provision of psychosocial activities plus structured self-help to enable PSNs to manage distress; protection case management targeting persons with special needs to complement GBV and CP case management services; service mapping and review of referral pathways to promote and coordinate safe referral of cases; provide information services designed to assist displaced and conflict-affected people (including returnees and flood victims) to access services, navigate complex procedures, and utilize existing remedies in areas of displacement to enhance their own protection; awareness raising, information services and outreach on rights, access to justice, housing land and property issues (HLP) and civil documentation; provision of targeted individual assistance including cash for protection and NFIs targeting the most vulnerable individuals – that will include civil service documentation support; Legal aid support will include provision of specialized counselling, legal assistance and information services through fixed centers and mobile clinics. Access to legal aid services will be unrestricted due to the nature of the service. The project takes PWDs as among the most vulnerable in target communities. On advocacy, Action will strengthen coordination of protection services and enhance advocacy on protection with relevant stakeholders in the four locations. Key advocacy activities will include strengthening of referral pathways, mapping of service providers and Protection Monitoring of rights violations

1. Needs

Rubkona, Panyijiar, Juba and Mayendit are some of the conflict and displacement-affected counties of South Sudan. South Sudan remains a protection case, irrespective of some milestones achieved in the implementation of the R-ARCSS. Conflict, insecurity, fueled by sub-national and intercommunal violence, crime, and wide-scale impunity, continue to be among the main drivers of humanitarian needs in South Sudan. Populations in Unity state bear the brand of intermittent conflicts and extreme climate conditions, as evidenced by protracted floods that have hit Rubkona, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties in the past four years. They Affected populations encountered in target locations are IDPs plus returning and host communities. PWD, women, and elderly individuals are the most vulnerable people in the community. These vulnerable groups of people experience challenges in accessing support and services due to physical access barriers in travelling to these locations. Protection monitoring data and risk analysis, in target areas, shows that priority needs for the most vulnerable people differ somewhat by location, but general the needs include physical protection, security and safety from criminals and militia, access to basic services, food security, access to health, access to NFI and emergency shelter, freedom of movement and security, psychosocial support services, access to justice, civil status documents (affecting population across the board due to displacement, and conflicts), responsive and two-way information services, and community-based social safety nets and mechanisms to support preventative and early response measures. Now, South Sudan does not have a strategic plan for civil registration. And as such the facility does not cope with the needs, especially in the most remote areas of the country which are receiving returnees. Delivery of assistance continues to be expensive and difficult due to road access challenges associated with floods, poor roads, and insecurity along main roads. Women, children, and other at-risk people require critical protection services including cash for protection, dignity kits and safe spaces. Psychosocial support services through community-based mechanisms are critical, an action that is always affected by vicious displacement and conflicts. Limited or lack of access to NFIs items such as dignity kits, clothes, sleeping mats, etc., increases vulnerability of women and girls to information and support services. Protection risks related to HLP remain unpredictable and widespread in the target areas, owing to lack of rule of law, weak government and community systems, unprecedented inflow of returnees and other displaced people back to their place of origin and flooding. The risks include physical aggression (forced evictions, and theft of property), movement affecting access to housing, HLP rights creating specific protection risks facing women including GBV, psychosocial distress and deterioration in mental wellbeing associated with loss of HLP rights, and conflicts related to grievances over HLP access, use and access. Peaceful coexistence between communities and population groups (IDPs, host community, returnees etc) is key to sustaining humanitarian responses recovery of communities from the crisis. Advocacy with Government and authorities to protect the returnees from robbery and theft, is a priority in the four target locations. Lack of information sharing on services is a challenge owing to humanitarian access challenges, affecting people.

2. Project activities

Key response priorities and activities are as follows: 1) Community based protection and legal programming – To ensure sustainability of protection interventions, the action strengthens and leverages community networks (harmonizing community action networks for protection) to promote meaningful access and participation in services during the project life cycle. The action engages and strengthens community structures, through community risk reduction and mitigation sessions, to identify risks, prioritize solutions, mobilize existing community resources, and inform protection interventions at community level. IRC will promote integrated CBP networks that will cover a wide spectrum of protection issues including CP, GP and GBV to enhance access to protection services for at risk groups including PWD 2) Protection, HLP and Return Monitoring: Protection monitoring, through mobile and static approaches, will focus on the protection environment (including rights violations, safety and security, housing land and property, DRR concerns) and protection risks providing recommendations for and ensure responsiveness and efficacy of protection services and response. It will inform PSN profiling, and protection analysis. IRC will prioritize individuals at risk for ensuring safety and receiving emergency support and trauma-informed care they need. 3) The action strengthens the mobile protection response capacity to enhance the reach of the protection response as well as to enable early action and a flexible responses 4) Protection Risk and Legal Analysis and Assessments . Using PAF and LAF tools, the team conducts ongoing protection risk and legal analysis of the crisis environment, producing products including actionable incent and protection trends, access to justice and information needs assessment reports etc to inform response. 5) Protection Case Management (PCM): CM will target at risk persons facing heightened protection risks (outside child or GBV CM service provider’s mandate). Targeted PSNs includes PWD, the elderly, etc. PCM will include linkages to services, PSS, and development of individual action plans to mitigate the protection risks of the affected individuals. 6) Targeted Individual Assistance: Based on assessed needs and risks, one-off response (cash-based assistance or in-kind) will be implemented for at-risk people to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity and reduce their vulnerability and exposure to risks and promote meaningful access and participation to services. 7) PSS Activities – PSS activities will reinforce PSS wellbeing of adults to manage stress and distress at safe spaces, through group wellness activities and structured Self-Help Plus (SH+) sessions for people both with and without mental disorders in hard-to-reach conflict areas. PSS will support safe referrals to specialist service providers, within and without IRC. 8) Access to Justice (A2J) for women and HLP interventions: A2J and HLP activities includes legal counseling, responsive legal information services, customized legal assistance, support to community paralegal interventions, eviction monitoring, and civil documentation awareness and support 9) Integrated Responsive Information Services in Emergencies: Responsive information services will and center on affected people as drivers of information production. IRC will use two-way information services to provide vulnerable communities, including IPDs, returnees and other people living in crisis, with the knowledge and resources on rights, HLP and access to justice to protect and inform their decisions. 10) Peaceful Coexistence activities: IRC will promote peaceful coexistence through linking community protection network with existing peace networks in the target areas. 9) Protection Mainstreaming: GP will lead and promote protection mainstreaming and safe programming practice across multi-sectoral areas through training and client action planning and working with community structures.
