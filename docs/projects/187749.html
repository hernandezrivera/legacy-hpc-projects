Project Summary

Through this project, SARD intends to support a number of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and children at risk of separation through continuation and extension of its specialized and unspecialized protection services which specially target sensitive child protection issues such as family separation, early child marriage, child labor and recruitment to armed groups and forces. Through its emergency shelter (ES) services and staff and Case management and Psychosocial Support mobile teams.
Over the course of 2022-2023 SARD aim at expanding its specialized and unspecialized protection services to Dana with much emphasize on ES and supporting child labor cases. Corresponding with the local legislation, SARD will have ESs segregated by gender. 2 ESs will be in A’zaz and 2 will be in Dana. Likewise, to address the needs of the foster families and families of children at risk of separation, SARD adapted a multi-sectorial approach by providing SNF and referral to other department and actors in the area.
SARD provides UASC and children at risk of separation with critical life-saving protection services including shelter, referral to education, PSS, Special Needs Fund, and foster placement. Children will be identified through SARD’s referral system in coordination with the Child Protection AoR and its members, and SARD’s Mobile Case Management team.
SARD will provide specialized and unspecialized protection services to its targeted population through the Case Management, PSS, and the ES units. In harmonization, the three units will strive to provide protection services. SARD will support the UASC through providing temporary accommodation and identifying suitable foster families, referring to (accelerated) education courses and enroll them in schools, providing specialized and unspecialized psychosocial support along with individual psychological sessions. SARD will also work with the foster families by providing trainings and engage them in a parenting programs to ensure their ability to foster children.
As for families of children at risk of separation (such as child promised to marry, child labour, possibility to join armed group/forces), with an integrated livelihood restoration approach as a way to promote family kinship and strengthen the family unit as a whole. With the programming grounded in creating sustainability for the family, the likelihood of dependence on the aid in the future is profoundly reduced. In addition, SARD will work to raise awareness on child protection risk and child rights among families and communities overall.
To monitor and evaluate the project, SARD has established multiple approaches to pin-down this matter. First, internally, program team organize weekly meeting to monitor the progress of the activities and provide technical support for the team. As well the weekly and monthly reports are used to monitor the progress. Second, M&E team has its own monitoring and evaluation tools to track the progress and quality of the CP activities. They carry out random field visits to monitor the activities at the ES. As for monitoring the CM work, they conduct random phone calls to check the clients' satisfaction. Program team utilize the results from the M&E team and internal report for learning purposes.
Reporting, in addition to the above mentioned reporting tools such as weekly, monthly reports, SARD reports on the 4Ws, CM inputs of the CP AoR.

4 - Needs

The needs were identified based on three resources; data from SARD’s current child protection project, and a conducted rapid needs assessment on child protection issues in its operating areas in November 2021 as well as the MSNA 2022. The triangulated data is used to determine the scale of the needs and protection risks for children affected by the conflict in NWS and the existing capacity to respond to the needs. Based on the data from SARD’s current CP project, (38%) of the identified cases are UASC, (35%) child labor (50%) of the child labor cases are engaged in the worst form of child labor such as scavenging, (85%) of the cases experienced at least one form of child abuse, (71%) of the cases are out of school with around third of them never attended school. Findings and field experience from SARD's team continuous to show that there is a significant gap in the child protection system, mainly due to the lack of a system responding to child separation. Furthermore, the MSNA 2022 highlighted that in NWS, (68.4%) of KIs reported that child labor prevents children to attend schools, (21.6%) of HHs reported that children between 3 and 17 years of age do not go to school because they are working to support their homes, in Aleppo governorate 26.4% of HHs confirmed this statement. Additionally, according to the MSNA, in NWS, 24.4% of KIs reported that children had stopped living with their families, 21.6% of KIs in Aleppo governorate confirmed this statement.

5 - Implementation of activities

Over 2022 and 2023, the project will support 4400 individuals (including men, women, boys and girls) across North-West Syria by meeting their protection needs by mitigating protection risks, in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action. The project will specifically target unaccompanied and separated and children at risk of separation. SARD identifies/receives referral of protection cases through various channels including: SARD's Mobile Team, the Protection cluster, local authorities, other humanitarian actors, and self-referrals. Following referral/case identification, SARD's Case Management Team studies each case and set a tailored plan for it. The activities that will be provided for the cases are; (5%) family tracing and reunification or alternative care arrangements/foster placement; (5%) emergency shelter for UASC, SARD provides full-time caregiver support, and accelerated education; (70%) psychosocial support (PSS) structured and unstructured; (40%) Special Needs Fund; (70%) Income Generating Activity; (50%) school enrolment; (30%) issuing documentation. This program utilizes an integrated approach to strengthen the family unit which includes education enrolment and PSS support for the children and a parenting program and livelihood support for the caregivers. In situations where protection services are outside SARD's capacity or expertise, such cases are referred to other humanitarian actors for proper management.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Breaching the confidentiality of the UASC at the ES. There is a risk of exposing the privacy of the children in the ES as other actors repeatedly visits the ES.The likelihood: high, Impact: highMitigation Measures: To minimize the risk, SARD developed (regularly review) the accessibility to and confidentiality policy of the ES and constantly brief partner on themProtection Risk: The socio-cultural context is a barrier to identify and work with unaccompanied and separated girls as most case are behind closed doors and the community self-referral is very limited.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: SARD conduct awareness raising activity to increase self-referral, and coordinate with key stakeholder to identify casesProtection Risk: Foster families exploit the fact of fostering a child to get the highest amount of humanitarian aid.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: SARD engages the foster families in training/awareness raising activities prior foster placement, Monitor and regularly evaluate the family life style, and conduct a families’ relationships program for the foster families and the child.Protection Risk: Community leaders do not see the value of SARD’s protection program and remove permission to operate in the area.Likelihood: lowMitigation Measures: SARD field management regularly communicates with local interlocutors to ensure understanding of our programs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Due the nature of the ES service, where employees and unaccompanied and separated children live in the same building, then there is a possibility of any form of abused to occurred. There is also the possibility that the UASC do not trust the reporting system. Likelihood: low, Impact: highMitigation: SARD established complaint mechanisms to facilitate reporting of abuse, including referral pathways, constantly check the efficiency of the reporting pathways; Employs staff aware of the PSEA policies and run regular refreshing training; conduct awareness raising activities for the UASC.Risk: Beneficiaries of the case management service may be targeted for sexual exploitation/abuse by a staff member.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation: Through MEL team, and established accountability and complaint mechanism, SARD insure a quick response in case on any incident. constantly check the efficiency of the reporting pathways; Employs staff aware of the PSEA policies and run regular refreshing training; conduct awareness raising activities for the UASC.Risk: The socio-cultural context in the area may impact the relationship between SARD employees which may results in abuse of power or sexual harassment.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation: ensure all staff are aware of the PSEA and CoC; review the policies on regular basis and updates them accordingly; review the complaint mechanism and ensure all staff are aware of them by running refreshing training on regular basis
