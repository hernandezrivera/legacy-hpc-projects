Project Summary

The Christian Mission for Development (CMD) intends to support access to safe, conducive, inclusive and quality learning opportunities for 9, 083 (4, 087 girls & 4, 996 boys) vulnerable children and youth aged 3-17, particularly IDPs, returnees & their hosts, in Ayod, Uror & Duk Counties of Jonglei State; and Ulang and Longechuk Counties of Upper Nile State. 5% or 454 (204 girls & 250 boys) of the total target will be children with disabilities (CWDs). This action will cover establishing Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) and water & sanitation facilities, providing teaching & learning materials and school supplies, early childhood education (ECE) kits, recreational kits, teacher training, providing psychosocial support to students, teachers and parents, and compiling lessons learned from the emergency period. South Sudan’s school children urgently need safe learning spaces to provide structure, stability & continuity to their education and prevent exposure to other vulnerabilities such as child labor, human trafficking, child marriage, and gender-based violence. Additionally, the psychological impact on children, parents, teachers, and the community need to be addressed as potential disruptions from floods, insecurity, lack of teachers and hunger could lead to increased dropout rates. Girls and boys experience emergencies differently and are exposed to a variety of risks if they do not have immediate access to education. Another critical issue is the scarcity of qualified teachers, resulting in high pupil-teacher ratios in hard-to-reach areas. 25 target schools (5 ECD, 15 Primary & 5 Secondary) with 150 (67 female teachers & 83 male) teachers and 36 (15 female & 21 male) PTA members will provide the platform for stability and continuity of education. They will be supported by 5 County Education Directors (CEDs) and 50 (22 female & 28 male) local education leaders in 5 Counties under this emergency response plan. Unless there are lifesaving incentives to ensure quality education, hundreds of crisis-affected children and youth aged 3-17 will continue to encounter significant obstacles in accessing education, including early marriage and GBV, especially for girls.

1. Needs

For years, South Sudan has been affected by a humanitarian crisis, exacerbated by floods and sporadic internal communal conflicts in a context where access to education is already severely limited as 2.8 million school-aged children are yet to enrol (MoGEI, 2024; Education Cluster, 2023). Access to education has thus been challenged due to the large displacement of communities, schools that have been damaged in the conflicts and there are inadequate classrooms for the number of children due to surges and population movements. Some schools are conducting classes outside and or do not have roofs on the classrooms. This leads to significant learning loss, weakened social skills and a heightened risk of permanently dropping out, all with lifelong impacts. Many parents are unable to continue working while balancing their children's care and learning needs. Added to this are health risks, such as diarrhoea, dengue, and malaria, which threaten the health of thousands of children and worsen their already precarious living conditions, marked by a lack of clean water and adequate sanitation facilities. Although a great many schools are open, have children attending, not every child within the community is in attendance. This is due to a wide range of reasons such as child marriages, early pregnancy, domestic jobs, and displacement. The urgency for providing support and services at school for the physical and mental well-being of children more acute than ever. This plan is to ensure that every child can receive safe, quality education, even in the most challenging situations. Investing in education means investing in the future of these children, giving them the chance to build a better life.

2. Project activities

Prime activities will cover rehabilitation of learning; equipping them with essential teaching & learning materials; training teachers to adapt their teaching methods to the emergency context and respond to the specific needs of the children; integrating psychosocial support into educational activities; and providing stress management strategies and building resilience for community members and local education leaders. The envisaged activities are as follows:1. Establishing 2 TLS2. Rehabilitating 3 TLS3. Rehabilitating 3 WASH facilities4. Providing recreational kits to 9, 083 learners5. Providing teaching & learning materials (TLMs) to 9, 083 learners & 150 teachers6. Training 150 teachers on EiE pedagogy and class management, PSS, CP & Climate Change Response7. Providing life skills and mentorship to 1, 000 youth8. Strengthening 36 PTA members to provide community oversight and support9. Supporting 5 CEDs & 50 local education officials to build local resilience at the school level10. Engaging in systems strengthening with 2 State Ministries of Education
