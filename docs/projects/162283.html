1 - Needs

The North-East region of Nigeria was one of the economically promising regions of the country from the 1960s to the late 1970s. At its peak, it was the bastion of commerce and trade with prominent local enterprises thriving in the region. Prior to the discovery of crude oil in Nigeria, cash crops from the zone contributed to the economic viability of the country, especially from the 1960 to the late 1970s. The production of agricultural products engages millions of small-scale farmers in productive agriculture with decent income in many states across the region. The abundant natural resource base of the region - agricultural produce, fisheries among others - drove the investment and industrial potentials in the region for several decades. In those decades, the region enjoyed religious, cultural and ethnic harmony, as well as, relative prosperity and peace. However, the fortunes of the region were heavily affected by poverty, unemployment, neglect in governance, and environmental degradation paved the way for idleness and radicalization, and violent extremism. Following the neglect and decline in productive investment, the north-east region has become adversely affected by the menace of insecurity and terrorism in Nigeria. The Boko Haram insurgency erupted in 2009 and exacerbated an already declining situation. The insurgent group conducted multiple attacks on towns and military bases and took control of significant territories in Borno state, as well as, some Local Government Areas in Adamawa and Yobe states. Since 2009, more than 27,000 people have been killed and hundreds of women and girls abducted. The insecurity due to the conflict has severely restricted freedom of movement, affecting access to basic services, farmlands, markets, and other sources of livelihoods, leading to high unemployment rates and low economic participation. The unemployment rate in the states are: 20.8% (Adamawa), 31.4% (Borno) and 29.0% (Yobe) in 2018, A large percentage of agricultural lands have been destroyed and are inaccessible due to military restrictions. Prior to the insurgency, the north-east Nigeria did more trade across national borders than it did with the rest of Nigeria. Border closures, insecure transport routes and the destruction of marketplaces have had a significant dampening effect on trade. According to the REACH multi-sector needs assessment of northern Nigeria in 2018, almost half the population is borrowing money to buy food (Borno 43%, Adamawa 46%, Yobe 44%). The livelihood and economic opportunities need to be improved for recovery of the lives of people in the area.This conflict has triggered massive destruction of basic infrastructure, health and educational facilities, commercial buildings, private houses and agricultural assets. In the three target States of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe, the total damage is estimated at USD 8.93 billion with the bulk of the losses (79%) attributed to agriculture (USD 3.7 billion) and private housing (USD 3.32 billion). Damage to private enterprises is also significant at USD 0.9 billion or 10 percent of total damages. The conflict also created high insecurity, which has affected the access to basic services, farmlands, markets, and other sources of livelihoods leading to high unemployment rates and low economic participation. For many individual households, the conflict and displacement has resulted in loss of housing, livelihoods, productive assets, and business networks. Those access to basic services should be restored urgently.This year, 2020 marks the eleventh year since Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) launched a campaign of violence in north-east Nigeria, but the crisis in the region remains one of the most severe crises in the world, with large-scale displacement of people continuing in the region. As of October 2019, the estimated number of IDPs in the region was 2 million individuals.

2 - Activities

The activity areas are as follows: 1) Restoration of basic services through the repair and reconstruction of infrastructure essential for the pursuit of a dignified life in communities that were devastated by conflict, and the strengthening of basic services provision. The restoration of basic services will be achieved through partnerships between the government and international actors for the provision of basic services. Priority needs will be confirmed through consultations with communities, security groups, and local governments (both traditional and formal representatives). Key activities include: -Reconstruction/rehabilitation of housing and shelter for returnees - Construction/ rehabilitation of key infrastructure (e.g., water, schools, clinic, markets) - Promotion of renewable technologies such as solar power for basic services and environment protection, such as waste management - Coordination of service provision and capacity building, as well as the provision of effective maintenance systems Priority Area 2) Livelihoods Stabilization infused with opportunities for immediate employment and income generation including, but not limited to, cash for work (CfW) programmes. Interventions will focus on both emergency income generation measures (e.g, CfW, agriinput provision) and sustainable job creation. Key activities include: - Cash for work for construction/waste management labour - Provision of crop production inputs including tools, seeds, and fertilizers – Support for micro and small businesses both in agri- and non-agricultural areas - Re-establishment of market structures and community saving schemes - Grants for women and youth led small and micro-enterprise recovery. -Vocational training for vulnerable people, especially female-headed households. Priority Area 3) Rebuilding social cohesion and peaceful coexistence among IDPs in Host Communities, Returnees, and Host communities through systematic social engagement and community security initiatives. Restoring community stability, social cohesion and trust is the most critical precondition to recovery and peace building, given the deep impact of the conflict. The programme will strengthen community cohesion through building the sense of shared responsibility for and ownership of the reconstruction process and well as inclusive dialogues among different population groups in the communities Key activities include: - Strengthen the existing community mobilization mechanisms, in particular with traditional conflict resolution mechanisms, and access to the formal legal chain - Strengthening community-level peace-building activities, including cultural activities, peace dialogues, joint communal activities and trust-building between the community members and security/police actors. – Conduct stakeholder dialogue on counter narratives on radicalization - Conduct sensitization for religious and traditional leaders on radicalization - Research and knowledge development for prevention of violent extremism (PVE) Priority Area 4) Reinstating effective and accountable local governance systems through, community participation and capacity building activities. The programme will aim at building capacity of the local government to support for effective coordination and monitoring of existing Recovery Plans and Programs and gradually assume the responsibility of basic services provision and managing recovery efforts. Key activities include: - Mapping and profiling of local government structures and communities in places of both displacement and origin - Establishment of community platforms to serve as a forum to discuss community needs, grievances and security; and building a bottom-up planning model - Capacity building for state and local governments on budget planning and execution, and monitoring and reporting - Development of a transparency and accountability model through community participation (social accountability mechanisms)
