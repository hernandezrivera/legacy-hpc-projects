Project Summary

" Under the ECW MYRP Phase II Top-up Funding, UNESCO aims to enhance the quality and accountability of education services for Rohingya refugee children and host community children in Cox’s Bazar. The intervention directly supports JRP objectives 2 and 3, focusing on capacity strengthening for implementing the Myanmar Curriculum and fostering community engagement to ensure sustainable, high-quality education services.
1) Burmese Language Training for Teachers and Volunteers (Obj_02):
A key barrier to implementing the Myanmar Curriculum is the limited Burmese language proficiency of teachers. UNESCO, in collaboration with the Green Tara Language Institute and Dhaka University Institute of Modern Languages, has developed a 10-week Burmese Language Training course tailored to meet the needs of volunteer teachers, and NGO staff at varying levels of proficiency. The training will be implemented in at least 18 training facilities and two language labs by Myanmar nationals, who will stay in Cox's Bazar for at least six months of project implementation whilst capacitating local trainers. Over time local trainers will be capacitated and provided digital resources so they will eventually take on full responsibility. This training equips educators with the necessary skills to use Burmese as the language of instruction, access teacher guides, and teach subject-specific content. By 2025, UNESCO aims to train at least 1,800 individuals (60% female), contributing to the broader roll-out of the Myanmar Curriculum. These efforts directly align with Objective 2, ensuring that educators are equipped to provide quality learning. The initial investment of international experts will develop a pool of local resource persons with the adequate Burmese language skills to drive up standards in learning centres.
2) Teacher Wellbeing and Self-Care (Obj_02):
The Teacher Wellbeing and Self-Care initiative addresses the professional and personal stresses that educators face. This training focuses on Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), equipping teachers with emotional regulation and self-care strategies. Teachers with better mental health are more likely to create safe and nurturing classroom environments for students, which is critical for both the quality of instruction (Obj_02)
3) Parenting Education Course for Teachers (Obj_03):
UNESCO will train teachers to deliver Parenting Education Courses that empower parents and caregivers with skills to support their children’s holistic development. Teachers will receive resources and capacity-building to lead community sessions that address child protection, nutrition, and interactive caregiving practices. This approach fosters stronger community engagement (Obj_03). In 2025, UNESCO will further contextualize the parenting education materials to ensure accessibility for illiterate and neo-literate parents, ensuring that the intervention is inclusive and effective.
4) System Strengthening (Obj_03):
To address systemic challenges in education provision, UNESCO will deliver targeted capacity-building sessions for 500 government officials, local administrators, education partners, and School Management Committees (SMCs). Training will focus on gender- and crisis-sensitive education planning, management, and coordination, fostering a cohesive education framework. This component directly supports Objective 3 by enhancing accountability and implementation quality across education services in both refugee camps and host communities.
Together, these interventions aim to strengthen teacher capacity, enhance community engagement, and ensure quality education delivery in line with the JRP objectives. Resources will be deployed strategically to address identified needs, including targeted support for contextualizing and scaling interventions in 2025."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"1) Burmese Language Training Needs: With the Learning Competency Framework and Approach (LCFA) replaced by the Myanmar curriculum, endorsed by the Government of Bangladesh, there is a pressing need for Burmese language training for volunteer teachers. While many of these teachers are committed, they often lack the necessary language skills to effectively teach core subjects such as mathematics and science using Burmese as the medium of instruction. The 2024 year-end assessment data reveals that children are underperforming in these subjects, and this underachievement is partly attributed to the teachers’ limited Burmese proficiency, which hampers their ability to explain concepts clearly and engage students. Strengthening teachers’ language skills through targeted training is essential to improve instructional quality, foster better student comprehension, and enhance learning outcomes in these critical subjects.2) Teacher Wellbeing and Self-Care Needs: The Cox’s Bazar context presents a stressful and demanding environment for educators, many of whom are faced with both the challenges of teaching in overcrowded, under-resourced settings and the personal stress of living in crisis-affected communities. Teachers often struggle to balance their professional responsibilities with the emotional and mental toll of their personal lives, leading to burnout and decreased effectiveness in the classroom. Without adequate support for their own wellbeing, teachers may find it difficult to provide the academic and emotional support that children require to thrive in such a complex environment.3) Parenting Education Needs: In both the Rohingya refugee and host communities of Cox’s Bazar, caregivers often lack the knowledge and resources to provide the early childhood care and stimulation essential for children's cognitive, emotional, and social development. Displacement, poverty, and the ongoing crisis have compounded these challenges, leaving many parents and caregivers without proper guidance on how to support their children’s development. As a result, children miss out on key learning opportunities in their early years, which can affect their long-term educational readiness and overall wellbeing.4) System Strengthening Needs: The education system in Cox’s Bazar faces multiple challenges, including poor coordination, limited resources, and fragmented communication between stakeholders. Both refugee and host communities are affected by high levels of poverty, gender inequality, and exposure to climatic hazards, all of which complicate the provision of consistent, high-quality education. Furthermore, the lack of a unified, government-approved education framework has hindered efforts to deliver structured, certified learning programs in the camps, creating a gap in educational services that affects the most vulnerable children and youth. Strengthening coordination and building capacity within the system is essential to address these gaps and ensure inclusive, quality education for all."

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"This project embeds protection principles across all activities, strengthening community mechanisms to safeguard the dignity and rights of both refugee and host populations. By engaging young women and men, community leaders, teachers, School Management Committees (SMCs), faith leaders, and local government officials, the project promotes inclusive participation and reinforces protection at all levels. Parenting Education equips caregivers with essential skills for supportive child-rearing, fostering protective family environments that mitigate harmful practices and promote educational readiness. Additionally, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) for teachers enhances their ability to manage stress, enabling them to provide emotional support to students and maintain a safe, nurturing learning atmosphere. To ensure sustainability and crisis-resilience, capacity-building training for 500 government officials and education partners includes gender- and crisis-sensitive planning, strengthening the system’s ability to address protection risks and maintain safe learning spaces during emergencies. Guided by a “Do No Harm” approach, the project minimizes unintended harm by delivering services inclusively, fostering social cohesion and peaceful coexistence between communities."

2.3 - Age & Disability

"The project integrates age and disability inclusion across all components, ensuring that quality education benefits everyone, especially children with disabilities. Following the principle that good inclusive education is good education, the project equips teachers, caregivers, and officials with the skills to meet the diverse needs of all learners.Burmese Language Training strengthens teachers’ ability to deliver effective instruction, benefiting all students, especially those requiring additional learning support. The training also includes lesson topics that teach vocabulary related to disability inclusion and deepen volunteer teachers' understanding of disability and inclusion. This helps them create more inclusive learning environments. Parenting Education promotes caregiving practices tailored to individual developmental needs, fostering a positive home environment for children with disabilities.Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) helps teachers manage stress and create emotionally supportive classrooms, essential for students with additional needs. Meanwhile, system strengthening training equips government officials with the skills for inclusive, gender- and crisis-sensitive planning and management, ensuring education systems can address the specific needs of children with disabilities and maintain continuity during crises. Through these efforts, the project promotes inclusive education, ensuring all children, regardless of ability, have the opportunity to learn, thrive, and participate meaningfully."

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"This project integrates Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by ensuring that the people it serves are meaningfully involved throughout planning, implementation, and evaluation. AAP principles are embedded across all components through transparent processes, inclusive participation, and responsive feedback mechanisms.Participatory Planning and Evaluation: Materials used in capacity building have been developed in consultation with the community and feedback has been incorporated throughout the development process. During the implementation stage, the project uses findings from assessments, including the ISNAs, programme monitoring, KIIs, FGDs and lesson observations, to shape its interventions and ensure community involvement in evaluations. Clear communication channels will share results and feedback with communities in accessible formats, fostering transparency and trust.Inclusive Participation and Transparency: Beneficiary selection processes for training, education, and support programs are transparent, with regular community consultations to explain criteria. The project engages individuals across demographic groups—including children, women, persons with disabilities, and older persons—ensuring that age, gender, and disability considerations are integrated throughout.Feedback and Adaptation: UNESCO is taking measures to establish a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) to monitor community perceptions of the project, ensuring concerns are addressed and guiding adaptive management. Feedback from CFRMs will inform adjustments in services, which will be communicated through multiple accessible channels.Language Accessibility and Social Inclusion: Burmese language training not only enhances teaching quality but ensures that messaging and materials are provided in languages accessible to the communities. This inclusive communication approach strengthens community trust and encourages engagement in decision-making processes."

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"The project contributes to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) by working closely with implementing partners to ensure that all staff and personnel understand and actively minimize PSEA risks. PSEA is embedded in the project’s operations through mandatory PSEA training for all personnel, including government officials, teachers, and volunteers. Regular monitoring, reporting, and dialogue ensure SEA risks are actively assessed and mitigated. PSEA is a standing agenda item in coordination meetings, and all stakeholders are accountable for maintaining a safe, respectful, and abuse-free environment throughout the project lifecycle.Both the refugee and host communities face vulnerabilities that increase the risk of SEA, and the project addresses these risks by mainstreaming PSEA principles and strategies into all components, including parenting education, psycho-social support (PSS) training for teachers, and system strengthening with gender-responsive planning for crisis situations.UNESCO follows a robust PSEA policy, with the Cox’s Bazar office supported by global expertise to align activities with international standards. Through collaboration with the PSEA Network, the project ensures that all partners, staff, and contractors are trained to prevent, identify, and respond to SEA incidents safely and confidentially. This includes awareness-raising among communities, with PSEA materials shared widely and is in the process of strengthening its own complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFRMs) to compliment existing implementing partner systems. "

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"The project integrates climate adaptation strategies to strengthen the resilience of refugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, addressing the region’s high exposure to climate shocks such as monsoon rains, flooding, and cyclones. Through system strengthening training, local officials and education sector partners develop gender- and crisis-sensitive planning and management skills, enabling them to ensure education continuity and child protection during environmental crises and in response to climate induced disruptions to education.Burmese language training enhances teachers' ability to effectively communicate climate-related risks and adaptation strategies to students and communities, fostering awareness and preparedness. This includes improved capacity to deliver science education, which is taught using Burmese in the language of instruction. Lack of language competency has been suggested as one of the limiting factors to effective teaching of science under the Myanmar curriculum.Parenting education further builds community resilience by promoting positive caregiving practices that help families respond constructively to environmental stressors, reducing the risks of harmful coping mechanisms. Families are encouraged to foster safe, adaptive environments for children during crises, ensuring continuity in learning and emotional well-being.Finally, mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS) training for teachers strengthens the emotional resilience of educators, allowing them to provide steady support to children during climate-related disruptions. "

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

"The project strengthens emergency preparedness and response (EPR) in Cox’s Bazar by building capacity at multiple levels to ensure that refugee and host communities are better equipped to respond to climatic hazards and civil disruptions. Given the region’s vulnerability to natural disasters, including cyclones, flooding, and monsoon rains, the continued risk of civil unrest—as seen during the July 2024 disruption leading to the change in government— and the raised threats posed by violence in the camps, the project emphasizes proactive planning and preparedness across the components.The system strengthening component equips government officials and education sector partners with skills in gender- and crisis-sensitive planning, management, and coordination, ensuring alignment with the ISCG Multi-Hazard Response Plan, national disaster protocols, and relevant sectoral SOPs. This training enables the development of resilient education systems that can maintain learning continuity and protect children even during emergencies. Officials will also be trained to address emerging risks to ensure that children remain safe and can continue their education in secure environments.The Burmese language training component enhances teachers' ability to communicate life-saving messages and disaster risk reduction (DRR) strategies to children and parents, fostering community preparedness. Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) training ensures that teachers can provide emotional support to children affected by disasters or civil unrest. The parenting education component mobilizes caregivers to support learning continuity during disruptions, reinforcing positive coping strategies at home and preparing families to respond constructively during crises."

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

"The project addresses the challenges youth face in accessing quality education and achieving positive learning outcomes through targeted interventions that respond to the significant decline in education participation among 15-18-year-olds. ISNA data reveals that only 8% of girls and 30% of boys in this age group are enrolled in education facilities, compared to 75% participation for younger children aged 5-14. This gap poses risks, including early marriage, child labor, and the likelihood of youth becoming involved in or victims of criminal activities, particularly for vulnerable girls who experience intersectional barriers.The system strengthening component ensures education systems are equipped to address these challenges by training government officials and partners in gender-sensitive, crisis-responsive planning and management. This improves the capacity of local education planners and managers to address the challenges for young people and to do so with a gender lens. This component strengthens capacity of planners to provide youth with access to safe, inclusive learning environments, ensuring education continuity even during crises."

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees through targeted education, capacity building, and community engagement. By equipping teachers with Burmese language skills children will have greater learning outcomes, allowing them to take advantage of future opportunities such as income generation, further education and access to services.The parenting education component empowers caregivers with the knowledge and skills to support children’s development and learning, fostering environments that encourage education continuity. This reduces the reliance on negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage and child labor.Additionally, mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS) training helps teachers manage stress and provide emotional support to students, promoting well-being and engagement in education.Through the mobilisation of refugee volunteers for Burmese language training, and the capacity development of a pool of Burmese language experts, the UNESCO activities present income generation opportunities for adequately qualified refugees.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

"Yes, the project activities in the host community broadly align with development priorities in Cox’s Bazar by supporting education, capacity building, and social cohesion, which are key focus areas of the District Development and Growth Priorities (Dec 2022). In particular, system strengthening builds the capacity of local government officials and education partners to implement gender-sensitive, crisis-responsive planning and management, ensuring continuity of education and protection during emergencies. These efforts complement district-level priorities for education and resilience-building by strengthening local systems to manage both environmental and social challenges, fostering sustainable development in the host community. That said, there is a need to strengthen UNESCO’s alignment with the district and upazila level education plans and policies, which will be a component of the 2025 activities."
