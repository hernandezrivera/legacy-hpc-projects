Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. An operational base was opened in Kukawa, Kukawa LGA in 2018 to deploy health, nutrition and protection activities in Kukawa LGA, and more recently in Monguno. This presence enhanced PUI capacity to provide a rapid health and nutrition response following the influxes of IDPs which have occurred in late December.
PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.
Given the persisting humanitarian needs within Borno State, and the increased security threats towards the civilian resulting in afflux of IDPs in Maiduguri, Monguno and other perceived safe LGA, the proposed project aims to improve access to basic services through an integrated health and nutrition response for conflict affected populations, based on a community approach.
PUI has been supporting a 24/7 reproductive health care services in two PHCC and ANC/PNC in three outreach sites in Bolori II MMC. PUI has plans to start sexual reproductive health in Monguno in 2020. Beside the 24/7 reproductive health care, PUI is also running ANC, PNC and under-five years immunization in the 5 health facilities in Bolori II in MMC.
PUI rolled out MHPSS activities in December 2018 in MMC and in February 2019 in Monguno. This was after PUI identified that IDPs seen at its supported health facilities are among the most vulnerable group to mental health issues. Over 62% of all consultations were delivered to IDP populations presenting with symptoms like somatic pains, sleeping difficulties and low mood.
Following the high burden of malnutrition in Bolori II, PUI is providing life-saving nutrition interventions since 2016 integrated with Primary Healthcare; Food Security and Livelihoods; Protection and Community Outreach; and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). PUI implemented OTP activities in collaboration with MoH at the PHCC Kukawa from August to December 2018. Following attacks and instability in Kukawa with health facility hosting Outpatient Treatment Program for SAM cases affected, PUI nutrition activities were relocated to Monguno. In Monguno, nutrition activities are and will be limited to Infant and Young Child Feeding intervention because there are several partners currently implementing nutrition activities including 5 OTP and 1 stabilization centre.

1 - Needs

Health service delivery continues to be hampered by the conflict, impeding movements of health workers, drugs and other medical supplies, and general breakdown of adequate health facilities and infrastructure (Humanitarin Needs Overview OCHA 2019). With an estimated 5.3 Million individuals in need of health assistance, and with only half reached as of July 2019. There is also lack of local capacity and resources to address health needs within poor host communities with high IDP concentrations especially in remote LGAs. Around 42% of medical facilities in North East Nigeria have been damaged either partially or completely with humanitarian aid partners offering health services either under trees, temporal mark shits or in the tents. Treatable communicable diseases are highly endemic especially in areas with high concentration of IDPs due to high congestion, poor environmental and hygiene conditions from originating areas, a weakened health system, and poor sanitation among other important infrastructure. Malaria has been one of the highest cause of morbidity and mortality in North-Eastern states with children under 5 years and pregnant women being the most affected. In Monguno, 82 % of the Key Informants surveyed reported malaria as a top health concerns in their community (Multi-sectoral Rapid Needs Assessment of New arrivals, REACH, February 2019). Malaria cases in Borno state are seasonal with peak being witnessed during the rainy seasons, hence the need to have appropriate preventive and curative measure to handle the malaria upsurge during the rainy season. In 2018, 10,571 cases of Cholera were recorded in 18 LGAs within the BAY region. The second half of 2018 showed the highest rates recorded since 2016 for Malaria, Measles, AWD, and ARIs . While in early 2019, Measles outbreak was reported within multiple LGAs in Borno State. Given the frequent diseases outbreaks in Borno, there is need to strengthen surveillance system and ensure robust emergency preparedness and respond is put in place ready to respond to any diseases outbreaks. Gender-Based Violence is widespread in the region. Urgent medical care is often required as GBV seriously affects all aspects of women’s health, especially for an estimated 700,000 women and adolescent girls of reproductive health age who might be at risk of sexual violence and exploitation (Humanitarin Needs Overview OCHA 2019). Essential, often life-saving, medical services for GBV survivors are not available in many affected areas. In instances where health facilities exist, these often lack the necessary equipment, drugs and trained personnel to provide timely adequate clinical management of rape survivors. Survivors of rape require post exposure prophylaxis for the prevention of HIV/AIDS to be administered within 72 hours of the incident, but clinical management of rape capacity is only available in eight out of 27 LGAs in Borno State (Humanitarin Needs Overview OCHA 2019). Mental health and psycho-social support remain extremely limited within the BAY region for example, mental health treatment services are only available at the Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital in Maiduguri. In PUI health facilities 60% of referred patients are IDPs, with 80% requiring an additional session or referral to psychiatric services. It’s estimated that only 48% of estimated needs are covered due to limited coverage coupled with cultural attitudes and stigma towards mental health.

2 - Activities

To reduce the morbidity and mortality rates, PUI will continue providing free-of-charge life-saving health care services and support to primary health care facilities and health outreach sites, promoting sustainability through its support to the Ministry of Health (MoH) situated in both Maiduguri and Monguno. PUI will provide diagnosis, treatment, and referrals when required of communicable diseases through PHCs and Health Outposts through the delivery of curative services and case management. For the treatment of common illnesses and diseases (e.g malaria, ARIs, diarrheal disease, measles, Urinary tract infection) PUI will use both international and national validated guidelines to ensure best practices. As part of the Primary Health Care Package, immunization will be implemented in each health facility supported by PUI. Each PUI facility follows the Nigerian immunization routine program which targets mainly children <1 year old and will ensure a follow-up of the children. PUI will obtain all vaccines used in the supported health facilities for routine immunization activities from the MoH, which is being supported and provided with vaccines by UNICEF. PUI will also support Mass Immunization Campaigns (organized by the MoH or UNICEF) through logistics support and community mobilization.To prevent and mitigate the communicable disease outbreak, outreach teams will be responsible for raising community awareness through community health education around hygiene promotion, communicable disease, and immunization.Public health surveillance of epidemic prone communicable diseases will be conducted with reporting to the Health Working Group through EWARS on a weekly basis and regular updates in coordination meetings. This will cover all EWARS identified diseases such as measles, cholera, meningitis, viral haemorrhagic fever, yellow fever, Lassa fever, and poliomyelitis. The EWARS will be implemented through door to door visits carried out by Community outreach workers and through case self-identified at the facilities, referral of identified suspected cases to the health facility, proper diagnosis and sending of alert to the Disease Surveillance Officer (DSO), information sending to the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and other national and international bodies. PUI has modelled its reproductive health interventions on the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP). PUI will provide 24/7 maternal health care including comprehensive antenatal neonatal, and postnatal services to Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). This includes performing deliveries through trained health-care providers and midwives within PHCs. PUI will provide free-of-charge SRH consultation within health facilities, including BEmONC (according to MISP) and family planning. Following MISP standards and recommendations, medical care for survivors of GBV - in particular sexual violence - will be available at the PHC level. Medical staff will be trained on the identification of sexual violence and appropriate response. PUI is collaborating with UNFPA for the provision of Reproductive Health Kit. The care will be provided by the midwives and the clinicians.PUI will integrate some Mental Health and Psychosocial support in the health activities as well as in protection and nutrition. The MHPSS activities will focus on training of health, protection and nutrition field staff. The trained staff will be able to recognizing psycho-social problems, warning signs of stress, PFA, and self-care. PUI will put a referral system in place where field workers can refer protection, and nutrition cases to the MHPSS team. Patients reporting with mental/psychosocial problems at the health facilities will be attended by PUI qualified PSW who will provide counselling, PFA and follow up. Patients who will require mental consultation will be referred to the psychiatrist specialist/nearest psychiatrist hospital.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through:(1) Community Committees Meetings that are organised once a month with community leaders, elders, women and PUI program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; (2) Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link PUI to targeted communities; and (3) PUI Complaint Mechanism which consist of helpdesks in the community and a toll-free helpline managed by dedicated trained staff.
