Project Summary

The SCALE project will respond to the vulnerable needs of vulnerable Syrian communities in Deir AL Zor, Homs, Idlib, Hama and Lattakia. These areas have been considered to have many people with needs where services, resources and assistance are limited. As a result, substantial responses are needed to reduce the suffering of those who have lived through considerable hardship, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic.
The objective of the SCALE project is to improve the access of men, women, boys and girls, including people with disabilities, to essential services in the governorates mentioned above.
The SCALE project will benefit 435,000 highly vulnerable individuals, in 2 years, including 121,800 men, 121,800 women, 95,700 boys and 95,700 girls, by supporting to maintenance and improvement of equitable access to basic services through rehabilitation of electricity networks, water networks, solar street lights, and increase in access to Livelihood and job opportunities through providing livelihood grants and repair of shops.
The proposed activities are under objective 1 of the ERL Sector log frame (Support the Maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services) activity 1.1 (Rehabilitation of basic electricity), indicator 1.1.2 (# Km electrical networks rehabilitated), activity 1.2 (Promote/support the use of renewable energy), indicator 1.2.1 (# KWA generated from solar panel per month), activity 1.9 (Rehabilitation water stations), indicator 1.9.1 (# of water stations rehabilitated) and objective 2 (Increase access to Livelihood and job opportunities), activity 2.3 (Create long and short-term work opportunities), indicator:2.3.1 (# # of longer-term jobs created), and indicator 2.3.3 (# of jobs created during the rehabilitation of public and private infrastructure).
ADRA will prioritize areas that include the most significant number of children under five years, women of reproductive age (15 – 49 years), older persons (age 60 and above), displaced persons –including refugees, IDPs and returnees, and persons living with disabilities.
ADRA will further prioritize sub-districts with the highest severity scale (3 and above), dense urban settings, areas with lower/ limited functional facilities.
Where possible, ADRA will select the areas where ADRA is already present to provide an integrated approach, including the sectors of WASH, Livelihood, food security or education.

4 - Needs

A combination of conflict in Syria and deteriorating economic conditions, combined with the COVID-19 pandemic, resulted in lack of access to basic services infrastructure which is also driving worsening humanitarian conditions (2022 Syria HNO). In 2022, ERL estimated People In Need with 13.8 million across Syria, including 6.9 million women, 6.9 million children and 3.2 million people with disabilities.Access to electricity, a priority needs for Syrian returnees and residents in particular, effects are directly felt in Syrian life and impede access to education, livelihood and security (2022 Syria HNO). 76% of HHs are unable to meet basic needs of life for their families, people are less able to cover their expenses using their salary or income due to a growing income deficit – a severe regression recorded since mid-2019 and highlighting the growing class of the ‘working poor’ among the crisis-affected in Syria.As a result, 13.8 million Syrians are estimated to require some form of income and livelihoods support in 2022, an increase from 11.6 million in 2021 and 9.9 million in 2020. (2022 HNO Syria).Syrians do not have access to basic services because of the lack or limited functionality of the infrastructure needed to provide these services, including educational institutions (26%). 5 - Implementation of activities

During the selection of locations, ADRA will conduct community-based needs assessments. ADRA will meet with men, women, boys, girls, (children, adolescents, young adults, adults and older adults), and people with disabilities and pre-implementation surveys will include community’s feedback. Based on this feedback, ADRA will ensure the needs of the local population are taken into consideration. ADRA will share the contact number of the complaint mechanism making sure the beneficiaries and stakeholders are aware of it and are able to provide anonymous complaints and to report any feedback to the organization.ADRA, in coordination with relevant local authorities in the targeted areas, (such as MoLAE, MoE, MoWR and SARC), and sectors (ERL) will conduct a detailed technical assessment of the planned infrastructure projects (electricity networks, water stations, schools, shops rehabilitation and solar street lights). The technical assessment will be the basis of the bill of quantity (BoQ) needed for the tender process to identify a qualified and experienced contractor. A number of stakeholders will be consulted ensuring that the needs of the community will be met, including men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities and the elderly, and appropriate approvals obtained.ADRA will ensure that rehabilitation work will include installation of ramps for the access of people with disabilities and gender-segregated disability friendly WASH facilities are also installed. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk:1. Beneficiaries may be placed at risk due to project interventions including the possibility to tensions between beneficiaries and community members.2. COVID-19 continues to spread and impact on project staff / beneficiaries.3. Sensitive data is leaked / released to unauthorized sources4. Discrimination of people with disabilities5. An increase in GBV if the abuser finds out about the reported cases.Mitigation measure:1. ADRA will work closely with local partners to ensure that the planned interventions are culturally appropriate, sensitive to local customs and traditions and harmonized with other forms of assistance to ‘do no harm’ and minimize the risks to beneficiaries.2. ADRA will assure the social distancing is respected during all stages of the project, PPEs will be available for ADRA team, partners, stakeholders as well.3. ADRA has a data protection policy that will be implemented to ensure the protection of sensitive beneficiary information.4. During all project stages, ADRA will consult with the management of the health centers to ensure protection mainstreaming component is included. 5. ADRA will ensure that the ADRA staff present at the implementation locations are aware of the risk and know the safe referral pathways in place in the area. The staff will also be trained on communication with persons (male, female, boys and girls) with diverse disabilities.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/ assumption:1. Delays in obtaining the necessary access and implementation approvals. 2. Changes in security and access especially with the COVID-19 status in Syria. 3. Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or devaluation of SYP may place project activities outside the planned budget.4. Qualified staff may not be available / Local staff may not have the existing capacity or skills needed.5. The procurement of supplies and materials could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest.6. Planned activities pose protection risks to staff, stakeholders or beneficiaries.7. Undue pressure placed on ADRA staff by stakeholders to prioritize specific locations or activities outside of the selection criteria.Mitigation measures:1. ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow up on necessary permissions and approvals.2. Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. 3. Closely monitor exchange rates, use competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding. 4. Staff are recruited through a clear and transparent recruitment process and provided with the necessary training.5. ADRA Syria has developed and follows a clear procurement policy. 6. Temporarily pause activities when necessary and utilize staff from the target area for monitoring. 7. Work closely with stakeholders and explain criteria and red lines in the early stages of the target area.
