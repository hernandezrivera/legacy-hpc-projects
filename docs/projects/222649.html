Project Summary

Many areas in South Sudan have been affected by multiple challenges simultaneously, including armed conflict, violence, natural disasters, food insecurity, and poverty. This has led to multiple displacements, and human rights violations, eroding people's resilience and ability to cope. According to the Global Protection Cluster, 5.5 million people are in need of protection services across the country. Given the high levels of sexual violence in South Sudan, the project also aims to address GBV, mitigate sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), and provide support for GBV survivors. The needs are also increased by the migration of returnees and refugees from Sudan who are often vulnerable and become targeted by perpetrators.
The project's ultimate goal is to provide a comprehensive response to the complex and multi-layered protection challenges in South Sudan. PAH’s approach to assistance is integrated, multi-sectoral responses meeting the priority needs of the beneficiaries and mainstreaming protection operating both in mobile and static modalities depending on the context. PAH is planning to provide protection services to vulnerable communities in South Sudan. These services include General Protection (GP), and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) activities. Priorities are selected based on Cluster priorities and already submitted multiyear proposals for 2025 by PAH that hasn’t yet been confirmed.
-Protection Monitoring and Assessments: PAH aims to strengthen monthly protection monitoring, conduct needs assessments, and safety audits at the beginning of each intervention to ensure that they address the priority needs of beneficiaries effectively.
-Cash and In-Kind Support: The project will include cash and in-kind support, especially for returnees and GBV survivors, to enhance their resilience. This support will be coupled with psychosocial support (PSS) activities for the most vulnerable community members.
-Women and Girls-Friendly Spaces: PAH plans to establish safe spaces for women and girls, including PWDs. Activities in these spaces will include PSS, training, and awareness-raising, along with prevention activities involving men, boys, and youth. Capacity building on protection topics along with income-generating training (IGA) for GBV survivors and persons at high risk, including fuel-efficient stoves. Dignity kits will be provided to women and girls. For GBV survivors PAH will ensure access to information, effective referral, and transport services to access health care and psycho-social services. Case Management services will be provided to both GBV survivors and persons at high risk and CVA or in-kind support will be granted.
PAH recognizes the cross-cutting nature of protection and integrates protection activities with other sectors and partners, including Education, S/NFI, FSL, nutrition and WASH. The project will be promoting long-term resilience and safety for vulnerable populations. PAH will ensure meaningful access to services for all ages, genders and PWD, encourageing community participation, establishment of safe and accessible feedback mechanisms, and promote resilient approaches.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1. Needs

According to the latest ISNA, an estimated 8.5 million people will require humanitarian assistance in 2024. In addition, the ongoing Sudan crisis and the planned 2024 presidential elections will likely influence the scale and type of violence and displacement patterns. According to the latest HNO - 2024, more than 8.5 million people need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance in South Sudan with locations in 5 states being classified as catastrophic inter-sectoral severity level. 5.53 million people require protection assistance. 3.15 million children need critical child protection support and 2.7 million people risk gender-based violence. The protection crisis in South Sudan is multi-layered with armed conflict, heavy flooding, sub-national violence, migration from neighbouring countries and others. Child protection and general protection needs have significantly increased since 2023. It is estimated that 13 counties are at catastrophic severity 5. The limited capacity of local governments, including lack of access to resources, further exacerbates the situation of vulnerable communities as violence is widespread. Forced displacement, killings, injury, sexual violence, GBV, child recruitment and abduction, forced marriage, family separation, loss and destruction of civil documentation, HLP, forced eviction, and ERW contamination are some of the ongoing human rights violations. Livelihoods are disrupted or destroyed. Education and health care remain at risk, with schools and hospitals occupied, looted, or damaged by conflict and floods. Food insecurity exacerbates protection and exploitation risks, resulting to the adoption of negative coping mechanisms, including school dropouts, child labor, child marriage, and human trafficking. Public health risks further aggravate the protection situation. Women are at particular risk of sexual violence. Children’s constant exposure to violence, exploitation, neglect, and abuse takes a toll on their wellbeing and development. Young people are subject to instrumentalization, such as for gang violence and other forms of negative coping strategies, in the absence of viable alternatives. Persons with specific needs, including the elderly, PWD, or single-headed households are among the most vulnerable and at high risk of exploitation. Many IDPs have suffered multiple displacements, while thousands live in protracted displacement, unable to find a durable solution. Multiple and protracted displacement has undermined people’s resilience and ability to cope, as a result of the loss of assets, livelihoods, homes, properties, family members and communal networks. Many barriers to durable solutions exist, including the extensive destruction of property. The number of child survivors of GBV, indicates a strong need for integrated approach of GBV and CP. Around half of the girls get married before reaching adulthood. GBV is one of the most critical threats, especially to women and girls, however, man are also affected, especially by forced recruitment, revenge killing or abductions. Almost half of the households expressed concern about GBV and the needs are reported. Collecting firewood in the bush, conducting livelihood activities outside the settlement or accessing WASH facilities were reported among the most common situations in which people felt unsafe. Studies indicate that ca. 65% of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, and some 51% have suffered IPV. The majority of girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18. Flooding, armed conflicts and sub-national violence are escalating the risk of GBV. Yet only 6% of the community members are aware of GBV response services available. The need for more reporting mechanisms, medical support, and other services was expressed by the communities. Exact needs and existing protection gaps will be identified at the needs assessment level prior to each intervention.

2. Project activities

PAH proposes a response strategy that encompasses both static and mobile approaches. General Protection: - Monthly protection monitoring involving Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) per payam, followed by Focus Group Discussions (FGD) and referrals when necessary. - Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) services for communities. - Conducting protection awareness campaigns and prevention messaging. - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) for returning individuals to enhance resilience. - Psychosocial support through the establishment of Child-Friendly Spaces (CFSs), parenting sessions, and provision of recreational materials to educational institutions. - Capacity building and outreach activities, including training for various groups on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE), Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Gender-based Violence (GBV). Gender-Based Violence (GBV): - Case management and referrals, offering Psychological First Aid (PFA), financial or in-kind support for GBV survivors, and updates to referral pathways. - Providing Psychosocial Support (PSS) through Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (W/GFS) and age-appropriate support group sessions. - Conducting awareness campaigns, including outreach activities, school-based GBV education, and the distribution of Dignity Kits. - Fostering prevention through engagement with youth, safety audits, and workshops on risk reduction. - Capacity building through training on GBV, human rights, and response services for frontline actors, teachers, healthcare personnel, community and youth leaders, and authorities. Protection Mainstreaming: - Conducting protection needs assessments and safety audits, both as dedicated protection activities and across all sectors. - Ensuring equal access to services for individuals of all ages and abilities. - Encouraging community involvement in project design and empowerment, with a focus on building resilience. - Establishing safe and accessible protection desks, particularly in IDP (Internally Displaced Persons) and returnee settlements. - Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries in all interventions.PAH or the IP will ensure to regularly report the implementation status of GBV interventions on 5W's and resources mobilized on Financial Tracking Services
