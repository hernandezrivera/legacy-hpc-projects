Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide emergency and sustainable WASH services to the crisis-affected IDPs and host communities in Puntland, Hirshabelle, and Southwest states of Somalia. The target population of the project is 40,000 crisis-affected communities who have not safe WASH services. At the inception of the project, AADSOM will hold a workshop to the prominent community stakeholders including women, disabled persons, marginalized communities, and IDPs to familiarize them the modalities/objective and roles such as project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement, and roles. Side meetings with girls, boys, men and women will also be convened to ensure their different needs are addressed. AADSOM will present the project documents to cluster members to avoid overlaps, following the cluster discussions. The project will reduce people’s needs, risks, and vulnerabilities and increase their resilience, to achieve over a period of 3-5 years. They are designed to strengthen coherence and complementarity between humanitarian and development efforts, and ultimately aim to reduce needs and vulnerabilities to a point where humanitarian action is no longer required. In this project,
Water:
AADSOM will provide lifesaving safe water trucking to 2,000 IDP households in Bari, Hiran, Lower Shabelle, and Bakool regions, benefiting 12,000 men, women, girls and boys who have no access to safe water for a period of one month. As an exit strategy, the project will rehabilitate 3 boreholes, 12 berkeds and 20 shallow wells. In order to keep the sustainability of the water source and increase the ownership of the rehabilitated water sources, AADSOM will conduct training to 35 WASH committees and 70 well operators. As cross cutting, AADSOM will conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms. AADSOM will conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases. AADSOM will conduct rehabilitated/constructed water source treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases.
Sanitation and hygiene:
In order to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls and discourage open defecation, AADSOM will construct 250 gender sensitive and flood proof IDP latrines including 40 latrines with disabled people, and 12 twin blocks latrines complete with hand washing basins in 6 schools and 6 health facilities with equal access to men, women, boys and girls including disabled persons and marginalized communities.
AADSOM will carry out hygiene promotion and COVID-19 prevention and protection campaigns participating women, men, boys and girls in all groups, which is planned to reach 40,000 people. The HP and COVID-19 awareness campaigns will carry out by 300 CHPs who are trained through this project. Finally, to encourage men, women and children to wash their hands with soap during the use of latrines and to fetch and store the water safely, AADSOM will distribute 3,000 HP kits to 3,000 IDP HHs who are at risk of AWD and/or HHs with malnourished children and pregnant & lactating women. During the project implementation, AADSOM will encourage women and disadvantaged people to take part in the decision making of the community decisions. AADSOM will make continuous monitoring during the project implementation and will hand over the rehabilitated WASH infrastructures to the community leaders with the presence of the local authorities. The project is complementarity with the existing AADSOM food security and WASH projects in the target locations.

Project Activities

I. Water:1. Conduct two workshops to 200 (100 men and 100 men) community stakeholders including women, disabled people, and minorities to consult and familiarize them with the project objectives, activities, feedback, and complaint mechanisms for accountability & transparency; 2. Provide safe drinking water to 2,000 households for a period of 30 days, representing 12,000 men, women, and children in flood-affected vulnerable and underserved communities including people with disabilities and minorities through water voucher, each household receives 45 liters/day (7.5 liters/day/person) in accordance to SPHERE standard.3. The project will rehabilitate 2 boreholes equipped with hybrid systems, 5 berkeds, and 20 flood-proof shallow wells equipped with hand pumps; The rehabilitated WASH facilities will benefit equally 30,000 (4500 men, 6000 women, 9000 girls, 9000 boys, and 1500 disabilities) including IDPs, marginalized communities living in crisis-affected vulnerable and underserved locations in Hirshabelle; Southwest and Puntland states of Somalia. 4. In order to keep the sustainability of the WASH facilities and increase the ownership of WASH facilities to the communities, AADSOM will conduct series of capacity building training to 27 WASH committees shared equally men and women, each committee is composed (4 men and 3 women) and 54 well operators. As cross-cutting, AADSOM will also conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms. 5. Conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination, and routine water quality testing to 27 rehabilitated/constructed water sources to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases. II. Sanitation:1. Construct 12 twin block flood-proof and gender-sensitive institutional latrines with lockable doors and handwashing points at 6 schools and 6 health facilities benefiting around 3000 school and health facilities, benefiting around 3,000 (450 men, 600 women, 900 girls, 900 boys, and 150 disabilities) attending the health facilities and the schools on a daily basis. 15% of the constructed twin block latrines will be accessible to disabilities. 2. Construct 200 flood-proof and gender-sensitive IDP latrines with lockable doors and handwashing points including 30 latrines designed to be accessible for disabled people, benefiting around 6,000 (1380 women, 900 men, 1800 girls, 1800 boys, and 300 disabilities). All WASH facilities will be constructed in a way to protect from destruction during floods as a durable solution and will be accessible to women, children, disabled people, and minorities. The locations of the latrines will be consulted with women and girls to avoid the risk of GBV against them. III. Hygiene: 1. Conduct hygiene promotion campaigns and protection & preventive methods of COVID-19 to reach 30,000 (4500 men, 6000 women, 9000 girls, 9000 boys, and 1500 disabilities) including IDPs, marginalized communities living in crisis-affected vulnerable and underserved locations in Hirshabelle; Southwest and Puntland states of Somalia. 40 CHPs (20 women and 20 men) will carry out community sensitization and awareness hygiene promotion and COVID-19 awareness campaigns including handwashing with soap, masks, and social distancing practices. Two KAP surveys will be done at the inception and completion of the project to evaluate the knowledge of the community to the spread of WASH-related diseases and COVID-19 prevention methodologies and the impact of the awareness campaigns respectively; 2. Conduct training to 40 CHPs (20 women and 20 men) on hygiene promotion practices and protection & prevention methods of COVID-19 using WASH cluster and UNICEF manuals. The CHWs will develop action plans to disseminate gender and age-segregated audiences; 10. Distribute 3,000 HP kits to 3,000 IDPs who are at risk of AWD/cholera diseases and malnutrition. Each HP kit contains: 200 aqua tabs, 5 bars of 800mg soaps, 1 Jerry can (20lt), 1 Bucket (20lt), and 3 female sanitation sets).

Mitigation Measures

(1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services:Mitigation measures:During the registration of the project beneficiaries, the local authorities, community leaders, elders, women groups, and minorities will select the project beneficiaries with the guidance of AADSOM through the agreed selection criterion; the distribution location will be near the beneficiaries' settlements; the distribution will be done in the presence of the community stakeholders.2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures:AADSOM will ensure that the marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes during the project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. To meet the specific needs of women separate meetings will be held with the women and girls respectively facilitated by female staff. Targeting is a joint process involving communities representatives. During the sensitization meeting, action plans will be drawn with a clear division of roles and responsibilities. The capacity building of the beneficiaries is planned to empower them to participate fully in the implementation process. Local vendors will be given priority for contracted works and supplies and the project will promote participatory monitoring and monthly feedback to all stakeholders to enhance accountability. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanismsMitigation measures:AADSOM will ensure that the project implementation process is accountable and transparent to the community and to the stakeholders including women, people with disabilities, and underserved communities. One of the ways of achieving this is through the establishment of a feedback and complaint mechanism to record and act on expression of satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the quality of the project services. The key objectives of AADSOM’s feedback and complaints mechanism include: Recognition and protection of the beneficiaries’ rights; Preventing and mitigating risks before they escalate; Building and maintaining good relations and dialogue with the community; Promoting accountability towards the stakeholders (beneficiaries, local authorities, donors and partners); Identifying weaknesses and areas for improvement in the service delivery. The complaint mechanism contributes towards creating a safer environment for the most vulnerable members of the community as well as increasing their voice during the implementations of the project. AADSOM complaint mechanism is accessible to everyone and beneficiaries are mobilized regularly. In addition to using the feedback and complaint mechanism, the project shall deliberately involve the beneficiaries in the implementation cycle. IDPs and host community leaders, elders, women, men, religious leaders. Local stakeholders, local authorities have specifically been consulted during the project implementation cycle. Sensitization and coordination meetings will be held before and during the project implementation with the local authority and communities in targeted locations to create awareness on project objectives, activities, target locations, expected outcomes, scope, and type and selection criteria for beneficiaries and beneficiary entitlements.

Please provide further explanation

During the project design: (1) All the stakeholders including the government representatives were consulted and agreed that the land for constructing the WASH facilities should be secure from the eviction of the IDPs. (2) Disabled people are not considered in their communities, excluded from the communities' committees, and have no role in the consultation meetings during the design and implementation of the project. Thus, they are at risk using the constructed WASH facilities. AADSOM will consult the disabled people during the design of the WASH facilities to make the facilities' disabilities friendly facilities. (3) To keep the sustainability of the constructed/rehabilitated WASH facilities, AADSOM will establish through community consultations WASH committees and well operators including people with disabilities, and women who will be given training on management and maintenance of the facilities. (4) AADSOM will also handover the facilities to the WASH committees with the presence of government representatives.
