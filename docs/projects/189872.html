Project Summary

UNHCR’s 2022 protection programme builds on the scale up of 2021 response and will be implemented in nine regions. The programme, builds on evidence gathered from ongoing protection monitoring using the revamped Ethiopia Protection Monitoring Tool (E-PMT) in the operational areas to ensure the ongoing identification of protection and specific needs and changes in the protection landscape for IDPs, IDP returnees, host communities and other persons with specific needs requiring protection services.
As the Protection Cluster-lead agency, UNHCR ensures the coordination of its activities, implemented directly or through partnerships, through the Protection Cluster at national and sub-national level as appropriate.
Based on the protection monitoring, UNHCR will implement an integrated set of protection services and interventions tailored to the needs and priorities of the affected, which include:
• the provision of information, counselling, identification of protection and specific needs, referrals to services/assistance,
• direct provision of services and assistance to persons at risk or with specific needs;
• GBV awareness, prevention and response activities;
• child protection awareness raising and case management;
• strengthening of MHPSS,
• emergency EORE as required by the Mine Action AOR,
• provision of legal awareness and legal aid for GBV case management, HLP, legal identity and civil document restoration,
• and strengthen social cohesion and community-based peacebuilding.
Through its CCCM intervention, UNHCR will provide care and maintenance service of site infrastructure for IDPs living in 63 sites in Tigray, 4 sites in Afar and 11 sites in Amhara for at least 230,000 vulnerable IDP children of under 18 (120,000 girls, 110,000 boys) and assisting more than 230,000 adult IDP population (103,000 Adult Men, 107,000 Adult Women(18-59 years), 11,000 Elderly Men and 10,000 Elderly Women (60 and above) which include 32,000 people with disabilities to ensure protection and assistance while striving for durable solutions.

1. - Projects activities

Based on the protection monitoring, UNHCR will implement an integrated set of protection services and interventions tailored to the needs and priorities of the affected population, which include: 1. Provision of protection information and counselling, identification of protection and specific needs incl. referral, notably through the growing protection desk network. 2. Provision of direct services and assistance to persons at risk or with specific needs identified through protection desks or protection monitoring, including in particular to persons with disabilities or other specific needs using UNHCR’s established specific need categories. 3. Provision of GBV awareness, and implementation of prevention and response activities, including by strengthening and expanding the capacity of GBV response services, such as one-stop centers, as well as the legal aid services (may include a cash for protection element), as well as addressing needs for dignity kits. 4. Child protection case management for girls and boys with identified child protection needs and community-based awareness raising to reduce child protection risks. 5. Strengthening of mental health and psycho-social care services 6. Based on a needs-gap analysis by the Mine Action AoR (MA AOR), UNHCR will also continue to utilize its protection desk network to provide emergency Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) in contaminated areas in line with the guidance note prepared in 2021 and otherwise facilitate EORE as advised by the MA AoR. 7. Provision of legal awareness, legal aid and legal case management to prevent housing, land and property (HLP) rights violations (e.g. forced evictions), restore HLP documentations and rights. (can include a cash for protection element) 8. Provision of legal awareness, legal aid and legal case management to restore legal identity and civil documentation(can include a cash for protection element) 9. Strengthening social cohesion and supporting community- driven peacebuilding initiatives to prevent or reduce risks of inter-communal tension and conflict and resolve disputes through a community-based protection approach. Under the CCCM cluster, UNHCR will be implementing the below activities: 1. Capacity building including capacity sharing. 2. Coordination and Information Management: UNHCR will build on a well- established coordination forum to ensure a coordinated multi-sectoral response in sites that addresses the assistance and protection needs of displaced and affected populations, while advocating for durable solutions with different stake holders. 3. Community participation and self-governance through establishment of camp committees, camp wide complaints, feedback and response mechanism, establishment of information hubs and carrying out volunteer outreach. 4. Site Development and Site Improvement works will be conducted as required including rehabilitation or construction of communal infrastructures, disaster risk reduction assessments, and small site maintenance toolkits distribution. In addition, UNHCR will work to ensure that roads, pathways, drainages are installed or rehabilitated. 5. UNHCR will work to enhance durable solutions (Relocation and Return) for IDPs this includes engagement of authorities, partners and other clusters to ensure relocation and/or returns is conducted in line with minimum agreed humanitarian standards

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

UNHCR works primarily with the communities directly, including through the establishment of community-led structures, such as community-based protection committees. UNHCR further is increasingly anchoring its programme in the local communities by identifying suitable local actors, (e.g. Universities) that are respected and rooted in the community in a given operational location to enhance the programme’s impact as well as sustainability through a more tailored and effective design and implementation. As appropriate, UNHCR also works with faith-based organizations for the same purpose. UNHCR as a partner in CCCM will follow the localization strategy through deliberate efforts to increase the number of national NGOs working within the cluster as evidenced by the recently concluded Request for Proposal specifically targeting recruitment of Ethiopian national NGOs. UNHCR will also continue to advocate for continued leadership and chairing of cluster coordination meetings at national and subnational level by national NGOs. In addition, UNHCR will continue to enhance National NGOs capacity by organizing various training sessions. UNHCR also works with National NGO for shelter and NFI program intervention as per the cluster’s localization strategy. These organizations plays critical role by bringing in the indigenous knowledge and local capacity, increasing direct access to local communities and hard-to-reach areas

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

UNHCR applies its corporate age, gender and diversity policy in all its programming, in addition to its strong data protection policy. UNHCR’s experience in applying these policies in practice will ensure the appropriate, safe and confidential channels required for the implementation of its integrated protection programme. In addition, UNHCR Ethiopia’s protection monitoring system as well as its approach to information provision, counselling and identification of needs (e.g. through protection desks and outreach capacity) is designed to ensure such input and feedback.The project will create and adapt information and messages to local languages of the IDP population so as to maximize impact. Through its CCCM interventions, UNHCR will be working with service providers and clusters to ensure referral pathways are in place and linked to the UNHCR Protection Desk, carryout safety mapping/safety audits and organize focus group discussions with vulnerable community members. UNHCR will put a system in place to ensure the referrals to service providers is tracked and thereinto monitored to ensure on time response is provided for PoCs and address the complaints.
