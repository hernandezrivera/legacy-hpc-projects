Project Summary

This project is aimed to increase access to clean and safe water, improve sanitation and hygiene with focus on protection and dignity of women and girls to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities across the counties with the objective of decreasing mortality and morbidity rates caused by poor WASH services which have been amplified by the pandemic. Total target population: 29,536 (Men 4,430, Women 7,384, Boys 7,975 and Girls 9,747) The beneficiaries will mostly constitute IDPs, returnees and the host communities while considerate of other vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities by15% and the elderly. HFO will encourage and support infection prevention and control (IPC) The beneficiaries will mostly constitute IDPs, returnees and the host communities while considerate of other vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities and the elderly. HFO will encourage and support infection prevention and control (IPC) and WASH in health facilities including COVID-19 appropriate prevention and management activities. HFO will use integrated approach with health, nutrition and protection 4 payams of2 priority counties (Fangak) as per the targeting guidance Guidance 2022. SAM children and their care takers and preganant and lactating women (PLWs) are targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services to reduce disease transmission at household levels. HFO will target nutrition facilities in providing a basic water and sanitation package to reduce risk of patients and health workers cross infectious disease transmission due to poor WASH conditions. In addition, communities showing high malnutrition prevalence combined with limited access to safe water. HFO will rehabilitate boreholes at health, nutrition sites and catchment areas. Selected water points will be monitored for quality before and after rehabilitation. This will improve sanitation and reduce prevalence of water and sanitation related diseases which is one of the top causes of morbidity and mortality among children under five. Local capacity will be enhanced through selection and training of hand pump mechanics, hygiene promoters and water management committees. The project will support operation and maintenance, hygiene promotion and organize equal access to water source to mitigate conflict. Community members will be encouraged to use wastewater for vegetable growing and animal watering to prevent stagnation that will provide breeding site for mosquitoes. Distribution of menstrual hygiene kits, general hygiene kit items, water purification tablets to caregiver families with consideration of both gender and age-specific hygiene. Water User Committees will influence hand pump selection for rehabilitation and consist of women and man members to enhance inclusiveness, as women are considered as responsible for collection of water for household needs. Sanitation committees will be identified and trained to oversee the latrine construction. In order to ensure women have a voice all committees will be female to male 50:50 and women will be in leadership roles.
HFO invests in environmental protection activities and in the project locations, Manajang, Mareang and Paguir in Fangak County, Diel/Atar 3 Kaldak and Atar 1 in Canal/Pigi County of Jonglei, Unity Leer Town, Mayendit Tutnyang Luom payams, Gogrial East County in Toch North and South payams, Gogrial West County Alek West and East repectively.

1 - Needs

The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are in Upper Nile and Jonglei states. Every fifth person is unable to collect enough water for drinking, and every third reports insufficient access to water for handwashing. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a negative impact on the health of children, women and elderly in Fangak and Canal/Pigi, Gogrial East and West (IPC 2022) through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases like acute water diarrhea cholera and water wash disease eyes and skin infections. Fangak and Canal/Pigi are completely flooded, and more flooding is threatening. Above mentioned Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs and are prioritized by the WASH Cluster for multi-sectoral response for famine prevention. Displacement, insecurity, flood and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure in these counties as it is in other parts of the country. The economic crisis has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. In areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water to the people, with the situation worsening when additional people are displaced with no water point at all. Insecurity is also another factor that has affected water access. In South Sudan, acute food insecurity is driving millions of people into marginal livelihoods resulting in poor health outcomes. An estimated 8.9 million South Sudanese currently need humanitarian assistance (HRP 2022). According to the World Bank Development Report 2022, South Sudan is among the world’s poorest nations with a poverty level of 82.30%, (WB, Feb 2022). The productivity of the economy is low with 80% or more people survive on wages labor which has little sustainability to the families. Government funding towards the health sector is low at 7.9% of the budget most health system services are supported through humanitarian fundings, there is general deficiency in social services available such as water, sanitation, and hygiene along with a shortage of household food resources and income that increases the risk of malnutrition and its associated cases of infectious, non-communicable, and epidemic-prone diseases. In 2022, there has been a general spike in food insecurity, the Famine Early Warning Network (FEWSNET) report published in 2022, estimates with up 7-8 million people are at higher risk of clean water gaps, Secondly, using a latrine is considered a taboo in many parts of the country, especially in states with a larger rural and/or pastoral population like Jonglei and Upper Nile (IPC, 2022). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.8 million people in 2022. Extreme weather events have led to the degradation of natural resources, a reduction in agricultural production, food insecurity, and a loss in livelihoods. In addition, armed conflict has had a devastating impact on the food security situation across the country, including through asset stripping, restrictions in mobility, displacement, and impediments to food assistance.Climate change has affected the variability of weather, exposing the country to torrential rains, seasonal flooding, and drought. Extreme weather events have had direct repercussions on peace and security. Flooding has been a major driver of displacement, followed by conflict. In 2021-2022, about 2,835,000 people were affected by floods in 33 counties across eight states, with Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states being the worst impacted. Climate change has altered the routes and periods of livestock transhumance, while the depletion of resources due to natural disasters, particularly grazing land, and access to water sources.
