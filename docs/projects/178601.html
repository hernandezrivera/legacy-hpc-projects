Project Summary

As part of IOM’s contribution to effective coordination and the UN's localization efforts, IOM aims to support capacity-building initiatives to strengthen the quality of humanitarian programming in Syria. Strengthening local and national partners' ability to respond to humanitarian needs effectively and efficiently in Syria is also in-line with its commitments in the Grand Bargain. With Clusters and UN agencies depending heavily on the network of NGOs to implement activities, it is vital to support these partners to continue to grow and improve in the delivery of humanitarian assistance to people in need. Furthermore, in the event of non-renewal of the UN Security Council resolution, INGOs and NGOs would be required to scale up their operations further in order to continue supporting the affected communities in northwest Syria. INGOs and NGOs will be expected to maintain the delivery of quality humanitarian response activities and ensure effective coordination. As such, it is paramount to continue building the capacity of INGO and NGO partners across technical and cross-cutting topics. This past year IOM supported its partners with training on project development, report writing, governance and legal compliance with Turkish law. In addition, IOM supported the S/NFI cluster with NFI distribution guidance document in the NWS context and also developed the e-learning course for humanitarian in-kind distribution that guides the humanitarian field staff responding in NWS. In-line with commitments under the Grand Bargain, IOM continued to support and invest in the institutional capacities of local and national responders across preparedness, response, and coordination efforts. IOM also supported its IPs by building their institutional capacity by providing HR coaching, working with IPs to improving their Human Resource unit policies and procedures based on their individual needs.
Based on the organizational capacity assessment (OCA) that IOM conducted in late 2020 with its implementing partners, humanitarian organizations continue to require capacity building in sector-specific technical thematic areas, but also need broader support to maintain the high standard of the humanitarian response in project management and administrative areas. Furthermore, IOM aims to build the capacity of implementing partners and Cluster partners on cross-cutting topics such as humanitarian principles, access negotiation, community engagement, and disability inclusion. Building on its capacity development activities in 2020, IOM will organize 39 trainings and coaching sessions on project cycle management, finance, and procurement topics. IOM will also organize community engagement training of trainers (TOTs) for CCCM cluster partners in Turkey, and training sessions for field staff in Syria on community engagement topics. In addition to the above trainings, IOM will also support the partners’ development of organizational and operational policy and procedures. All activities will be coordinated with relevant Clusters, Technical Working Groups, and other coordination fora.

4 - Needs

According to the UNDP data from 2021, there are around 165 local NGOs responding to humanitarian needs in northwest Syria. Among these, 56 LNGOs are actively participating in the Partnership Working Group (PWG). According to the training needs assessment conducted by the PWG in late 2020, both organizational and operational capacity development needs have been highlighted by the majority of the implementing partners. Training needs in humanitarian principles, humanitarian access negotiation, community mobilization, logistics, and finance were some of the frequently requested thematic areas, among others. Based on the perception-based survey by Ground Truth Solution (GTS) on IOM partnership with IPs, 56% of the IPs requested for more support to develop their financial planning and management skills- an area where IOM’s partner Capacity assessment showed the most room for improvement. Recently, IOM, together with NGO implementing partners, prepared joint capacity development plans to identify partners’ capacity needs. In this plan, IPs consistently highlighted the need for more capacity-building sessions in administrative areas such as project management, finance, and procurement to support their organizations’ implementation. Given the reliance on local and international NGOs in the NWS context, IOM proposes to continue building partners’ technical and organizational capacity, which will contribute to the improved quality of humanitarian assistance, as well as a more sustainable and localized response. IOM will continue to coordinate capacity-building efforts with the PWG and relevant Clusters.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM aims to develop humanitarian partners’ organizational capacity in cross-cutting areas by providing training and coaching sessions for IPs on project cycle management, procurement, and finance topics. To improve partners community engagement (CE) mechanisms, IOM proposes to conduct a socio-economic and social dynamic formative study to gain a broader understanding of different communication needs of different groups, identify preferred channel(s) of communication, and find trusted sources of information and channels for sharing feedback and complaints. IOM will also conduct 4 TOTs on CE for CCCM cluster partners in Turkey targeting 100 participants. Participants from this training will then conduct CE trainings in NWS targeting 100 CCCM field staff. IOM will also hold 2 learning exchanges for CBI TWG members targeting 80 participants. IOM will also hold 2 TOTs for partners on humanitarian principles and access negotiation targeting 50 access working group members and partners. IOM will organize a learning exchange on procurement-related topics in coordination with the Logistics Cluster and procurement working group. IOM will also support partners with guidance in procurement and logistics. To mitigate against the spread of COVID-19, IOM will continue to deliver virtual trainings, only hosting face-to-face sessions when it is safe to do so. IOM has years of in-house expertise from working in the cross-border context and will recruit technical experts to deliver specific trainings when needed. IOM will use existing local capacities to deliver its Capacity-building activities to contribute to the localization effort, which will in-turn strengthen participation, representation, and leadership of local and national actors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In its capacity development efforts IOM will ensure that protection is mainstreamed. Protection topics will be integrated into the training curriculums across all trainings and coaching sessions. More specifically, protection risks include the limited participation and representation of females in the training conducted and hence limited inputs on protection mainstreaming from participants. IOM aims to increase the meaningful participation of women, and continuously encourage women facilitators to deliver capacity development activities. Each training aims to have gender-balanced participants, with 50% females and 50% males.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As COVID-19 continues to be a threat, trainings will be adapted to meet the needs while mitigating the spread of the virus. IOM will continue web-based capacity-building activities using online platforms, videos, and e-learning courses to reduce the risk of spreading COVID-19. Whenever safe and feasible to do so, in-person training and support will be considered in line with national regulations, WHO and Health Cluster’s COVID-19 guidance. The identification and recruitment of qualified service providers for capacity development needs can be challenging. However, IOM, through a transparent and clear recruitment process, will ensure that the most qualified candidates are selected to deliver capacity-building activities. To mitigate against rapid changes in security which may impact the ability to deliver capacity development activities, IOM will ensure that trainings are organized within a sufficiently flexible timeframe so that activities can be conducted when there are relatively stable conditions. To avoid duplication efforts from different organizations, IOM will liaise with the PWG and the relevant Clusters to capitalize on existing resources. To ensure that priority needs are being met, IOM, in coordination with the NGO forum and the relevant clusters, will continue to assess the capacity development needs of IPs through regular needs assessments and will prioritize training needs based on the highest impact. IOM will strive for 50/50 gender-balanced capacity development teams to ensure equal involvement of both male and female participants in all capacity development activities. IOM will conduct pre and post-surveys to confirm that humanitarian partners have increased their knowledge and identify lessons learned.
