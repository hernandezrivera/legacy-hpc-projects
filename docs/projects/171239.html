Project Summary

Overall Project Objective is vulnerable households protection and enhanced their coping capacity to meet basic needs. and resilience to reduce the spread of the COVID 19pandemic. And the two main outputs from the project upgrading, rehabilitation and repair for 200 substandard houses in Gaza City.
And provision of shelter assistance NFIs (Sealing Off Kit, Kitchen Set, COVID 19 family Hygiene and cleaning kit) to vulnerable groups exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns for 1000HH. Thus, the total number of beneficiaries expected from the project around 7,200 beneficiaries.
According to The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020(HNO) an estimated 307,000 highly vulnerable people across the OPt (263,000 from Gaza) face severe gaps in accessing adequate shelter and essential shelter non-food items. These vulnerable groups include IDPs, host families; Palestine refugees, communities and families affected by demolitions including Bedouin communities, in which a majority are refugees and poor, people with disabilities and widowed, separated or abandoned women.
In the Gaza Strip, one of the most densely packed territories in the world, destruction of housing, impoverishment of families and other factors have contributed to overcrowding among and between families. Women attribute the increased crowding, “often citing living in extended-family households of their spouses as compounding their abuse.”
The objectives of our project alignment with goal of humanitarian assistance in the oPt is to protect the rights of Palestinians under occupation, provide access to basic services for those who are acutely vulnerable, and support the ability of households to cope with prolonged stresses to prevent a further deterioration in their situation until more sustainable solutions are found.
Shelter Cluster Strategy 2020- Strategic Objectives 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
AIOCP and local partner ROWAD will implement project a line with PSEA network recommendation, furthermore will be strengthened through refresher training, including on PSEA for all project staff.
All activities that will be implemented will take into account the security and safety measures recommended by health authorities to deal with the Corona pandemic. Among these procedures, for example: Work in areas where government agencies are permitted to work and that do not pose a threat to the spread of infection (green areas), and temporarily stop work in areas where the epidemic is spreading.
M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. AIOCP conducts a post project lessons learnt exercise to continuously improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation.
AIOCP engages male and female beneficiaries in its regular monitoring visits throughout project implementation to ensure the quality of the intervention. AIOCP conducts post-distribution monitoring and post-project surveys with beneficiary households to measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance and the satisfaction of male and female beneficiaries of all ages.
Project information is communicated to both male and female family members in direct communication and through information leaflets which also informs them about AIOCPs feedback and complaint mechanism with available complaint box, email, field visit and phone line.

1 - Needs

According to The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020(HNO) an estimated 307,000 highly vulnerable people across the OPt (263,000 from Gaza) face severe gaps in accessing adequate shelter and essential shelter non-food items. These vulnerable groups include IDPs, host families; Palestine refugees, communities and families affected by demolitions including Bedouin communities, in which a majority are refugees and poor, people with disabilities and widowed, separated or abandoned women.The main affected groups are the 9,356 families (53,000 persons) which the Ministry of Social Development (MOSD) has registered in the Palestinian social protection programme in Gaza, who are living in rented accommodation and are at risk of eviction, due to judicial cases involving rental arrears. Over 8,500 people are still displaced from previous hostilities in Gaza, including the November 2018, March, May and September 2019 escalations.In the Gaza Strip, one of the most densely packed territories in the world, destruction of housing, impoverishment of families and other factors have contributed to overcrowding among and between families. Women attribute the increased crowding, “often citing living in extended-family households of their spouses as compounding their abuse.”In Gaza, the continuous deterioration in the economic situation, combined with the restrictions on the entry of construction materials, hinders new construction as well as the maintenance of existing housing stock. This has resulted in a severe shortage in housing units needed for natural population growth, about 120,000 units, as estimated by the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MOPWH) in 2017. This has led to an increase in substandard living conditions at household level, and unhygienic, overcrowded and unprotected living space in existing shelters. The demand for new housing units and the need for rehabilitation and maintenance of existing shelter, is increasing, with a shortage of resources to cover these needs.In June 2018, the Shelter Cluster published the findings of the inter-agency shelter survey on substandard housing conditions in Gaza, which revealed that 70 per cent of households (found as substandard) suffered damage or destruction from the 2014 conflict, and 51 per cent from previous conflicts or floods and winter storms. These conditions will require minimum shelter interventions for the protection of the vulnerable families exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns.According to the field assessments conducted by MoPWH in 2019 which included approximately 47,000 families living in Gaza strip showed that 16,985 shelters in Gaza Strip are in poor condition and in need for urgent interventions and rehabilitation assistance, 4,772 shelter from them in Gaza and North Governorate. Also 4,994 shelter in Gaza Strip need finishing for unfinishing shelter intervention, 1,766 them in Gaza and North Governorate. Based on the field survey carried out by MoPWH, the Corporation will coordinate with the MoPWH and AIOCP to obtain the vulnerable families in need of urgent intervention included upgrading and rehabilitation of their shelters. The AIOCP will visit these families and prepare a new social and technical assessment for them and determine their needs according to the Shelter Cluster standards. Accordingly, 200 substandard houses in Gaza City and Gaza North will be selected to urgent intervention. And provision of shelter assistance NFIs (Sealing Off Kit, Kitchen Set, COVID 19 family Hygiene and cleaning kit) to vulnerable groups exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns for 1000HH. Thus, the total number of beneficiaries expected from the project around 7200 beneficiaries from Gaza City. The list of names nominated to benefit from the project was obtained from the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH) and will be approved by the project's steering committee, excluding names that do not meet the conditions for benefiting from the project.

2 - Activities

This project aims to Substandard shelter rehabilitation in Gaza to create adequate living space with integrated hygienic WASH facilities for the most vulnerable household, with particular focus on families at higher risk of being affected from COVID 19crisis and harsh weather conditions. AIOCP will be implemented project through two main activities, Activity 1 will target upgrading and rehabilitation around 200 household (1400 beneficiaries) in Gaza City and North of Gaza through Cash modality. Activity 2 will provide essential household NFIs for 1000HH (7000 beneficiaries) such as water storage, household assets and equipment, bedding set, kitchen set, hygiene kit and COVID 19family Hygiene and cleaning kit, enough for 1month (At household level). A steering committee will be established to support all project activities from all stakeholders such as Arab and International Organization to Construct Palestine (AIOCP), Rowad Association for Society Development (ROWAD) as a local partner, Ministry of Public Work and Housing (MoPWH), Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), and Shelter Cluster (SC).Works will be carried out according to SPHERE and (IASC) minimum shelter standards. This will provide safe and dignified shelter to targeted HHs and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children and mitigate the transmission of COVID-19 outbreak. The Beneficiaries will be selected considering a combination of protection, economic and shelter technical vulnerability criteria. Factors reflecting protection vulnerability include age, disability, families with high numbers of dependents, women-headed households and families suffering from harsh living conditions including protection related problems while economic factors will include main income, source of income, depts, and the status of household assets. The expected beneficiaries HH will be selected from the previous AIOCP, ROWAD and MoPWH database, and the beneficiaries’ name will be shared with the stakeholders (SC, MoPWH, MoSD) to ensure that they do not benefit from previous projects and that there is no duplication in the expected beneficiaries.An orientation session will be held for all project team members (social researchers, civil engineers, protection officer and M&E officer) to review all work procedures and ensure that each member of the work team knows the tasks required of him for the project to succeed.The work team will start visiting HH to update the social and technical assessment for each household. During this visit, a meeting will be held with all family members (father, mother elderly, sons, daughters...) to identify their needs and sort out their requirements in accordance with the minimum shelter standards. As well as discussing the legal issues regarding house possessive and rental issues. the negotiation with both HHs (women and men). The family members and in focus of female and children are to participate in identifying the priorities of their shelter needs of upgrading and protection and privacy concerns.A final beneficiaries list from the project will be presented explaining the intervention required for each beneficiary, and the financial value for each beneficiary according to the need assessment and the priories of minimum shelter standard.The steering committee will approve this list after reviewing and verifying its compliance with the approved project outputs and indicators.AIOCP will Sign an agreement with all beneficiaries, this agreement clarifying the activity that will be implemented to improve the family home, in addition to the conditions, duties, obligations, financial payments and work mechanism, in the presence of the legal advisor.Using a self-repair approach, 200 of the most vulnerable HHs will receive cash assistance (average 3,000 USD) conditional on the adherence to BoQs. Cash will be disbursed in 3 instalments (35%, 35%, 30%) via bank transfer/cheque. The workplan will be monitored through 4 visits to each HH by local partner ROWAD engineers to review progress and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. The visits will also help identify technical challenges and provide tailored remedial action, complementary guidance and/or special assistance to ensure safe and quality shelter solutions, compliant with SPHERE and Shelter Cluster minimum standards.AIOCP and ROWAD will provide shelter adaptation support NFIs for most vulnerable 1000 families in Gaza City. It includes items that ensure physical & climate protection, privacy & dignity, safety & security and reflect gender & protection concerns & special needs like those of people with physical disabilities and access-concerns, such as (Sealing Off Kit, Kitchen Set, COVID 19 family Hygiene and cleaning kit).AIOCP will ensure that information on reporting fraud and PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is available to the project and the partner, and they are in line with PSEA network recommendation, furthermore will be strengthened through refresher training, including on PSEA for all project staff. AIOCP main implementing partner, ROWAD, will be responsible for the selection and follow up of the civil engineers, the participation in the beneﬁciary selection process, the reporting on the progress of activities, the updating AIOCP on the challenges faced and lessons learnt during the implementation of activities and the meaningful contributing to the M&E processes.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection committees represent communities’ interests throughout the implementation process. Indeed, they ensure that the repair to be undertaken by the household was decided in collaboration with the household members (women and men) and was in line with their priorities. They also make sure that all protection concerns faced by the household due to their substandard home are addressed in priority. Finally, the committees perform protection monitoring visits of their own, and participate to technical monitoring visits performed by AIOCP technical staff in order to make sure that all beneficiaries, including women, are consulted. Their inclusion throughout the implementation process ensures that the information is conveyed properly to each affected household, notably in case where a household initially assessed will not receive assistance.The monitoring and supervision staff in the AIOCP continuously monitor the satisfaction of the beneficiaries, as the staff visits the beneficiaries and interviews them about the ongoing activities, then the staff informs AIOCP management about the cases that they are presented with, to properly address them.The AIOCP takes active steps to be aware of and take into due consideration local culture, customs, beliefs, capacity and strategies to survive with dignity. In addition, where relevant, an understanding of the “Do No Harm” concepts of dividers, tensions, connectors and local capacities for peace is sought, including through the participation of communities in this analysis, and integrated within planning and design of projects and the humanitarian response.The organization has a means by which it can continuously review its interventions against a “Do No Harm” framework, to ensure that its projects, programmes and interventions are not exacerbating conflict or causing harm to the people it seeks to assist.
