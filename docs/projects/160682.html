Project Summary

This project aims at building resilience in livelihoods by supporting vulnerable populations in Koch County, Unity State and Fangak County, Jonglei state. To achieve this, the project will be implemented in line with the food security and livelihoods cluster objectives. The project will enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support to populations in stressed, crisis or emergency levels in the IPC classification. To achieve this, the project will directly distribute livelihood support kits to 154,180 beneficiaries in both Koch and Fangak counties. These beneficiaries will be provided with crop, vegetable and fishing kits.
Secondly, the project will strive to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb shocks. To achieve this objective, the project will train 23,130 beneficiaries on improved agronomic practices. The training will focus on three key areas; appropriate agronomic practices, pest and disease control, and post-harvest handing and preservation. Fishing training will focus on appropriate net making, preservation of breeding grounds as well as fish preservation to avoid post-harvest losses.
The project will also support 3,000 beneficiaries with small ruminants (sheep or goats) or poultry (chicken) to give them an opportunity to re-establish their stocks after losing their livestock during the years of protracted fighting. These livestock beneficiaries will be organised into small groups for group management of the provided livestock but also for training and follow-up. The project will also support these livestock beneficiaries with training. Livestock training will focus on livestock routine management practices and disease control.
The project will have a strong coordination and integration component. Coordination with other stakeholders will be done at both the Juba level and the county level to avoid duplication and reach out to as many beneficiaries as possible. Integration will involve working with Nutrition, WASH, and Protection programs to ensure beneficiaries are served more effectively. This will involve working with caregivers of patients admitted in the OTP and TSFP programs to ensure that they can continue producing enough food and avoid relapses once discharged from the Nutrition programs. It will also involve working with beneficiaries to improve hygiene and sanitation at home to ensure proper food utilization. Integration will also ensure mainstreaming protection concerns in this project to ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse as well as general protection to ensure accountability to affected population. As a Nutrition and WASH partner in the proposed locations, World Relief will be able to integrate sectors easily with existing programs.

1 - Needs

The IPC report of August 2019 indicated that 6.35 Million people in South Sudan will need humanitarian support in form of Food Assistance and Livelihood support. In the former Unity and Jonglei states, 1.6 Million people will face food insecurity and will need humanitarian assistance. It is estimated that in Fangak County, Jonglei State, 146,000 people would be in stressed, crisis or emergency food security needs by December 2019. This number was expected to rise to 164,000 by April 2020. These figures were based on some good harvests that were expected by November 2019 from the main season cropping which was supported by humanitarian partners. However, Fangak County has continued to experience floods particularly in October 2019. The floods affected crops just before maturity and are expected to adversely affect crop production. The percentage of population affected by food insecurity is therefore expected to be higher than projected by the August 2019 IPC findings. Most of the population in Fangak is expected to remain in IPC level 3. Some specific locations may tend towards IPC Phase 4. Some of the key drivers of food insecurity in Fangak include Flooding, limited food sources among the vulnerable populations and non-functional Markets. Additionally, inter-communal fighting and revenge killings is expected to disrupt livelihoods and restrict access by humanitarian actors. In Koch County of Unity state, a large population expected to be in stress, crisis or emergency levels was 90,000 by December 2019 and 95,000 by April 2020. The IPC report of August 2019 notes that crisis acute food insecurity is expected in Koch County by April 2020. The protracted fighting in Koch made farmers to completely lose their productive capacity. Seeds were burnt in stores and farming tools lost when communities had to evacuate for safety. Markets in both counties have remained largely non-functional because of poor road network and low demand for goods. The FSNMS round 23 reports a significant reduction in number of livestock owned per household after the protracted fighting in the Greater Upper Nile region. Assets were depleted either from theft of livestock or sale to access cash to purchase essential needs. Coupled with lack of employment opportunities, the terms of trade have also deteriorated. The Joint Market Monitoring Initiative report of October 2019 notes that the cost of Minimum Expenditure Basket has continued increase but, the purchasing power of large segment of the population has remained poor. Lack of demand and insecurity along the tread routes has discouraged traders from providing commodities in these markets. The cessation of hostilities in both Koch and Fangak is expected to provide an opportunity for building resilience in livelihoods. However, this is also expected to come with an influx of IDPs returning back home to re-establish their livelihoods. Returnees are expected to continue flocking into these counties given the unpredictable situation within the refugee camps in Sudan and the desire to vacate Protection of Civilian sites. IDPs and Returnees will increase pressure on already strained resources, especially food availability and utilization. Environmental conditions, especially flooding which is seasonal but also chronic increases incidences of water borne diseases. This situation is further complicated by poor hygiene and sanitation conditions, as well as limited access to health services which not only affects personal nutrition and health but also reduces labour force required to engage in agricultural activities, especially during the productive season that experiences rains and flooding. World Relief therefore proposes this FSL intervention to immediately curb and gradually start to reverse effects of food insecurity by building resilient livelihoods able to respond to unexpected shocks and stresses that keep the community depended on humanitarian support.

2 - Activities

The project will have three key activities: distribution of livelihood kits; distribution of small ruminants; and poultry and training. The markets in Koch and Fangak will remain largely non-functional. Distribution of inputs through market systems will therefore not be feasible in 2020. The project will adopt direct distribution of agricultural inputs particularly to the most vulnerable and neediest. This will target returnees and IDPs, Women/Child Headed Households, and those households headed by the elderly. Distribution process will ensure accountability to affected population. The project will also locally procure and distribute small ruminants and poultry. This activity is in recognition that massive cattle raids experienced particularly in Koch between 2015 and 2019 has left many of the households without livestock. Livestock is a major source of food and income to communities in this region. Small ruminants are of particular interest to this project because they can be owned even by women. Similarly, the decision to slaughter for food or sell for cash does not require extensive family consultations as would be in the case of cattle. Small ruminants are also a ready source of milk and meat required by young children to enhance their nutritional value. The third key activity will be training. Training will be offered in three key areas. The crop and vegetable training will focus on appropriate agronomic practices, pest and disease control, harvesting, post harvest handling and utilization. To facilitate these trainings, farmers will be organised into groups of maximum 25 persons from neighboring homes. Each group will select one leader who will also be the lead farmer. The lead farmer will be supported through this project to establish a demo farm that will be used as training ground for all the other farmers. Livestock training will be in two parts. Fish training will focus on appropriate fish net making, preservation of spawning grounds and fish preservation to reduce post-harvest losses due to rotting. The training on shoats and poultry will focus on livestock routine management practices, simple breeding techniques to avoid inbreeding as well as pest and disease control. The initial project phase will start with community mobilization and sensitization to will discuss project content with beneficiaries and stakeholders. This will ensure accountability and transparency but also encourage buy-in and up-take of project activities. The project will also establish community structures that will be involved right from inception to completion of the project. These community structures will take the lead on beneficiary selection, providing feedback on project progress and general management of project activities. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will target caregivers of children within the nutrition centres with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH Non-Food Items through partners. Specifically, the project will:• Support 25,697 vulnerable households with crop and vegetable kits • Support 25,697 vulnerable households with fishing kits • Train 3,855 Farmers in appropriate agronomic practices, pest control and post harvest handling• Establish and maintain 18 demo farms • Train 3,855 Fisher folk on appropriate fishing methods and fish preservation• Provide 600 beneficiaries with 5 birds each (5 hens, one cock)• Provide 300 HHs with one Goat/Sheep each and provide 60 Boers for Breeding.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. A key resource in re-establishment of livelihoods is land. Land in the targeted locations is owned by the communities. There has not been any pressure on land in both Koch and Fangak counties. Based on current observations of returnees, it is reasonably expected that land for agricultural use will not become a major issue in these locations.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The Project will also target displaced population. In both counties of Koch and Fangak, the ongoing peace process is expected to provide conducive environment for IDPs and Returnees to come back to their ancestral homes to re-establish their livelihoods. These populations will also be included in this project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Right from inception to implementation, monitoring and reporting, the project will work through the existing government and community systems. Project team will consult government officials at county level and disseminate information about this project, the geographical coverage and targeted beneficiaries. The feedback provided from the meeting at county level on settlement patterns and population numbers per payam will help in apportioning number of beneficiaries per location. At the community level, the project will work with existing community systems for instance in identification of beneficiaries, resolving any arising conflicts and provision of inputs, including training. The project will establish a beneficiary selection committee at Boma level. Membership to this committee will include local authority representatives at Payam and Boma level, but will also include representatives of other vulnerable groups, with a representative each from women, elderly and physically challenged populations. This committee working together with project staff will be charged with responsibility to identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries using an agreed upon criteria. This committee will also be involved in identifying distribution points that would be easily accessible and safe for beneficiaries from those particular localities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is aligned to the National objectives. it is expected that through this intervention, both refuges and returnees will be supported to re-establish their livelihoods. Anticipating additional IDP and returnee beneficiaries during the project period, the project will follow all relevant national strategies for these population groups. In Koch County, the project will link up with partners working under the beyond Bentiu response. This will help the project staff identify IDPs returning from the Benitu PoC back to Koch and provide them with livelihood support kit that will enable them to re-estbalish their livelihoods as they settle in their destinations.In Fangak, UNHCR is partnering with Nile Hope to receive organised IDPs from PoC in Bor and Malakal. IDPs returning voluntarily are being tracked and registered with the local authorities. Project team will work with these agencies so as to ensure IDPs/Returnees as provided with livelihood kits to re-establish their livelihoods.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The ongoing peace process within the country is expected to create a stable environment for implementation of livelihood activities. This has been demonstrated by a substantial number of IDPs who are voluntarily returning back to their original places of settlement as witnessed in both counties of Koch in Unity state and Fangak in Jonglei. Similarly, at the community level, there are already efforts to ensure stability. The different antagonistic parties are already in agreement to ensure that peace is sustained at community level. In Koch for instance, there is free civilian movement in all locations as opposed to the prior situation where civilians living within government controlled areas would not be allowed to move to SPLA-IO controlled areas. The project will also deliberately promote protection especially for women, girls and the physically challenged. In close consultation with the beneficiaries, the project team will ensure all distribution and training grounds are sited in locations that do not expose women and girls to sexual abuse. The project team will also be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and will be expected that program implementation will not at any cost expose women and girls to sexual exploitation and abuse. The project will target and assist the physically challenged beneficiaries to give them fair opportunity in participating in livelihood activities. Distributions and training will be communicated well in advance to allow beneficiaries prepare to come and receive their inputs. During distributions, the elderly, sick, disabled and pregnant or lactating women will be accorded priority. Distributions will also be done in small groups and in locations that be easily accessible to beneficiaries to reduce amount of time spent in distribution sites. Before distribution, beneficiaries will be informed what they should expect. A complaints desk will also be available to register and resolve any arising complains.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Involvement of existing governance systems is expected to instill sustainability within the project. The governance systems will be strengthened overtime and take over implementation and management of subsequent interventions. At County level, the project will include the County Agriculture Department where possible. The County Agriculture Department is expected to continue with these activities after the project life. At the community level, each group of farmers will be lead by a lead farmer who will be a person of high moral standing on the community, who is easily accessible and willing to work with other farmers. The lead farmer will be trained on appropriate agronomic practices. They will also be given incentives to move, train and support another 20-30 farmers within their locality. This extension approach encourages farmers to learn from other farmers and is expected to last even beyond the project life. Farmers will also be encouraged to start seed saving, especially for local vegetable seeds which will be provided through this project. Seeds saved will be used even after the project period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries. Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services minimizes the risk of beneficiaries, especially women and girls being targeted with sexual or physical violence by armed groups on the way to and from accessing services. Gender-based violence has unfortunately been increasing in South Sudan, having been sanctioned by certain armed groups as a tactic of war. WRSS is serious about the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), including the demands for sex in exchange for goods or services. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. Staff are also trained on referral pathways for beneficiaries who identify as survivors of gender-based violence.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

A variety of feedback mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to express concerns and to ensure that vulnerable community members have access to a forum for giving feedback. First, comment boxes and/or complaint desks are made available at implementation sites to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback directly to WRSS in a confidential manner. Secondly, volunteers are able to receive feedback from participants one-on-one during home visits in settings where participants may feel safer sharing their comments in the privacy of their own home. This also ensures that elderly, disabled, or other people confined to their homes or with difficulties visiting project sites have the opportunity to submit concerns. Thirdly, beneficiaries will be informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a World Relief Area Coordinator at a project site.
