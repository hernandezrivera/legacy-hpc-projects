Project Summary

The crisis in the South-West and North-West Regions of Cameroon has increase pre-existing vulnerabilities among the population. Since 2016, political and social instability, aggravated by violence, has had a negative impact on the civilian population of these two regions. With an increase in violence as a result of clashes between the NSAGs and the government, the deteriorating security situation has resulted in multiple population displacements in the NWSW. Across the months incidences including deadly attacks on civilians, burning of houses, kidnapping, arbitrary arrest and other serious violations against the civilian population has been reported. Through various CHRAPA’s monitoring reports in it’s target communities, it could be deduced that, the violence, displacement and breakdown of social cohesion have left communities and families in desperate need. Civilians have faced grave violations of human rights and humanitarian law, including death, injuries, sexual violence and exploitation, detention, forced displacement, attacks on civilian sites and forced recruitment. Children have witness grave violence and displacements. Women on their part have experience forced marriage, rape, abduction and slavery. Detention of men, women and children associated with the armed conflict either directly or due to their connections with NSAGs continues to be an area of significant concern. Furthermore, many of those detainees, have not had access to a legal counsel and their families do not even know where they are and many have already gone for years without trial.
In addition to the issues mentioned above, IDPs lack access to basic legal services to assist them to go about their daily activities. One of the most common issues for IDPs has been loss of civil documentation, the need to register births and deaths, probate, inheritance matters, matrimonial and family disputes requiring conciliation. It is also certain criminal cases not relating to NSAGs or terrorism as deem by the government, to be tried in the state court systems will need legal representation. There is no doubt that many IDPs are in need of legal assistance. There is no gainsaying either that as they return to their places of origin, conflicts and disputes over land, housing, and property will be inevitable and could be a potential cause of imminent conflicts in these communities. It is important to note that access to the state justice system is limited because of the high court cost, long distances to court and long trial periods. Thus several communities, even before the crisis, had relied on the traditional justice system. With the crisis, these traditional structures have collapsed because of disintegration of communities forced to flee their homes. There is therefore need to strengthened these structures to continue to provide these much needed services to community member.
Thus the intervention has as objective to promote access to justice through awareness raising on the importance of civil documentation and human rights law, carry out advocacy amongst regional and national authorities for better adequacy of civil documentation procedure, facilitate the acquisition of Civil documentation for IDPs and vulnerable members of host communities through identification, registration and forwarding of list to competent state authority (BRUNEC) who have the prerogative to issue birth certificates. The project will strengthened the capacities of community members with knowledge on their rights. Furthermore, CHRAPA in partnership with the Cameroon Bar Council and it’s existing paralegal centres will provide legal aid to IDPs and other vulnerable groups. This intervention will take place in Boyo (Belo, Fundong, Bum sub Divisions) and Mezam (Tubah, Bafut, Santa, Bamenda I, II, III, Division Bali ) where CHRAPA already have existing activities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

One of the most common issues for IDPs has been loss of civil documentation. The need to register births and deaths, probate, inheritance matters, matrimonial and family disputes requiring conciliation is also a major challenge. Many IDPs and returnees are unable to access basic services such as education , health care, experience restrictions on their freedom of movement, are exposed to increased risk of arrest and detention, and may be excluded from recovery and reconstruction programmes. According to CHRAPA monitoring reports, IDPs and returnees also mentioned challenges in obtaining civil documentation, such as the high transportation cost to access government offices in their places of origin, lengthy processing times, and difficulties in obtaining security clearance to travel and to obtain documentation. • There is lack of harmonisation and inadequacy in the procedure to acquire civil documentation. Advocacy on the issue will help to make the procedure better. • Access to justice for those detained /whose rights have been violated is limited. Many people have been detained and are awaiting trial. A significant proportion of them have not had access to a lawyer because they don’t have the means. There is also another category whose rights have been grossly violated but they unable to seek redress because they can not afford justice. In this light, legal aid will facilitate those in need to have legal counselling and court representation where necessary. • There is gross ignorance among community members on their human rights and on humanitarian law. This ignorance limits their ability to speak out when there is a violation.• Community structures including traditional and religious institution that had been instrumental to bring peace and order before the crisis have collapse or are weaken. There is need to strengthen their capacities and to ensure that they are at the centre of the programme. CHRAPA would capitalise on strengthening community structures that had been in place before the crisis.• Provide Individual Protection to 700 victims of protection violations or victims at serious risk of violations. IPA could be provided both in cash and in Kind and will be different according to the specific needs of each case. Examples could include: transportation cost to social centre,/medical services, payment of consultation, emergency shelter, provision of wheel chair, clothes shoes, pots amongst others. The Gender and age and diversity will be taken into consideration. Activities will including a) Organise 20 awareness raising sessions targeting 100,000 people on their rights, the importance of civil documentation and where they can access legal servicesb) Identify, register and support 2500 people to acquire birth certificates, and other missing documents- land certificates, marriage certificates, d) Provide legal support to 160 people to regain access or recover lost propertye) Provide legal aid to 50 vulnerable people f) Provide individual protection assistance to 700 victims of protection violations and those at serious risk of violation in target communities . • Greater awareness of statelessness, and internally displaced persons issues • Greater knowledge and awareness of rights amongst IDPs and host communities and Humanitarian law • Increased access to basic rights and services for IDPs in targeted areas• At least 50 paralegals fully trained and supported • Access to justice is improve with an increase in number of
