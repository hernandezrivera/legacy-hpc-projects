Project Summary

The project aims to improve well-being and health through integrated healthcare support to the Rohingya community living in the camps and to host communities in Ukhiya in Cox’s Bazar as a continuation of previous project phases. The project includes the operation of 1 Primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) and the general consultation, dental, eye and limited surgery section of Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU, a secondary Healthcare center). One primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) in Camp 7 will provide services in affiliation with the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS). For the Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU), the maternity part (CEmONC) is funded by IOM and UNFPA, the family planning services are supported by IPAS, and the rest of the services including general consultation services, eye and dental units are funded by the Friendship Luxemburg and provide us the required flexibility to strengthen and consolidate services across the facilities. The Maternity part (CEmONC) of the Friendship Hospital Ukhiya (FHU) is identified by the health sector as a referral point to provide higher-level maternal healthcare services including Caesarean sections for both host communities and Rohingya. The project focuses on protection against illness and equitable access to (Health Sector Indicator 2) health services without any discrimination and especially targeting the most vulnerable groups (women, children, pregnant/lactating women, people with disabilities, the elderly, GBV, and conflict-affected survivors). The project also plans to collaborate with the Ukhia Specialized Hospital (UNHCR) by sharing the expertise, accepting referral points, and receiving maternal patients from USH until the maternity part is started there. Friendship will conduct the following activities following the MESP:
- Provide essential health services to both the host and Rohingya community through 1 primary healthcare center in camp 7 and one 24/7 Secondary level healthcare service named Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU).
- Provide SRH services including safe deliveries (assisting Normal Vaginal Delivery ) and providing basic neonatal care, Ante Natal Care (ANC) and Post Natal Care (PNC) services to both the host and Rohingya communities.
- Conduct normal vaginal delivery through the PHC and C-section through FHU to ensure safe institutional delivery.
- Provide Family Planning, Menstrual Regulation, management of STIs, and post-abortive care services.
- Screen and refer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Medium Acute Malnutrition (MAM) children (6 to 59 months) to Outpatient Therapeutic feeding Program (OTPs).
- Provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support and Gender Based Violence services including Clinical Management of Rape services and clinical management of Gender Based Violence cases (in Camp 7 1 MHPSS Officer and 1 GBV case worker).
- Support Expanded Program on Immunization services in alignment with the national program.
- Provide laboratory investigation facility services for the beneficiaries at the health facility level.
- Provide essential medicine and supplements including Vitamin C and Zinc tablets to increase immunity of the beneficiaries
- Coordinate with District and Upazila level COVID-19 Task Force, Health and Family Planning Department, and other stakeholders.
- Conduct awareness-raising sessions including IPC at facility.
- Strengthen the feedback mechanism by developing and implementing action plan based on feedback received from camp-level health coordination and health committee meetings.
- Community Health Volunteers are identified, trained and supervised to identify community members in need of referral for PHC services, EPI, SRH including ANC/PNC with special attention to term mothers for skilled birth attendant deliveries with the support of outreach Midwives.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya crisis has become one of the largest humanitarian crises in recent times, requiring relief and support at an unprecedented speed and scale and causing immense pressure. After fleeing mass-scale violence in Myanmar in August 2017, as of 31 October 2023, 936,961 Rohingya refugees have been living in the world’s largest and most densely populated refugee camp in Cox’s Bazar– one of the poorest districts of Bangladesh (UNHCR Population Factsheet), which is putting a lot of pressure on the already limited and strained infrastructure, services, and environment currently existing. Host communities and local authorities are struggling to cope with this reality as detrimental social, economic, and environmental effects of the prolonged displacement continue to increase their suffering. The Rohingya population in Cox’s Bazar is highly vulnerable, after generations of statelessness and no access to health or education before the severe traumas inflicted by the Myanmar Army, Police, and local Rakhine Buddhist mobs. Especially, the people who arrived in Bangladesh on 25 August 2017 came with very few possessions and are still almost entirely reliant on humanitarian assistance for food, shelter, healthcare, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and other lifesaving needs while living in difficult conditions. Of the 33 refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar District, 29 of them are in Ukhia Upazila, hosting 86% of the total refugee population (813,176) currently residing in different camps in Cox’s Bazar. Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), led by UNHCR and IOM, continues to work with the Bangladesh government and coordinate Rohingya response efforts of all local, national, and international NGOs and UN agencies in Cox’s Bazar to arrange essential shelter, food, water, medicine, sanitation, and protection. As in 2022, ensuring adherence to quality, and respectful implementation of the minimum essential service package for health services remains a top priority for 2023 According to ISCG’s (Inter Sector Coordination Group) Need Analysis and Response Plan, a substantial gap remains in accessing quality healthcare services in the Rohingya camps and host communities, despite four-year continuous interventions by NGOs and donors. The prioritized sector objectives for health by ISCG JRP 2022 was1. Support equitable access to essential primary and secondary healthcare services for Rohingya refugees/FDMN and the host community. (SO3, SO4) 2. Prepare for, prevent, and respond to outbreaks of communicable disease and other health related hazards, including for periods of increased risk during the monsoon and cyclone seasons. (SO3, SO4, SO5) 3. Promote health and well-being at individual and community level. (SO1, SO3, SO4, SO5)For continued support to the objectives, quality healthcare services through health facilities for Rohingya and host community is still highly required and these facilities are already recommended by the Health Sector’s rationalization process and health facility gap analysis.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As per rationalization principles, Friendship is already providing healthcare services from the mentioned facilities (1 PHC at camp 7 1 secondary hospital FHU) to both Host and Rohingya communities with equal approach. An integrated approach is essential for fulfilment any project goal as it brings on board all the key stakeholders including but not limited to the beneficiaries themselves. Friendship has collaborated with other Health facilities inside the camp and outside the camp including Government Upazila Hospital and Cox’s Bazar Sadar Hospital. And also planning to collaborate with UNHCR- Ukhia Specialized Hospital by sharing skilled medical staff, receiving patients from USH until their maternity center is functional, sending patients to USH as referral points, etc. Friendship is already an important part of the camp coordination and collaboration with other sectors of a camp including WASH, nutrition, site development etc. Also participating in the camp coordination meeting regularly. The question of the representation of beneficiaries in the coordination efforts as a unified entity might also need to be answered. One of the key stakeholders for the smooth running of operations is the local authorities. Strong government liaison at both the RRRC as well is the Upazila level is crucial for the implementation as well as the sustainability of any project in the Rohingya context. While the government is represented in every camp through the CIC office, to oversee the appropriate execution and actual effectiveness of proposed plans and to better understand and address gaps in services. Dedicated manpower to coordinate, discuss, and improve communications for the alignment and development of policy for the benefit of all stakeholders must be ensured. Friendship is providing. Regularly updating the CiC offices and communicating about any kind of problem faced by the facilities are the core part of implementing the project into a camp for Friendship.

3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection issues have been mainstreamed through below considerations:- Health interventions are designed to address the health needs of special age groups and gender groups (male, female, and third gender) - In collaboration with Handicap International, Friendship staff receive training on disability mainstreaming - A special cell comprising facility coordinators at field and headquarter level will be formed to monitor the activities and address any such violations conducted by any service providers across the health service networks like gender issues, protection, misconduct, forgery etc. A direct phone number and email will be provided for recording any such violations. The human resource department of Friendship will take the overall lead in this. - All healthcare facilities of Friendship were made disabled-friendly, and features were added to provide easy access to the vulnerable population- Proper security will be ensured to avoid any kind of violence on the premises. At the same time, frontline workers will receive basic training to recognize and safely refer cases of sexual and gender-based violence.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Incorporating Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into the health project led by Friendship NGO is imperative to uphold ethical standards, ensure the safety of beneficiaries, and maintain the integrity of the humanitarian effort. Friendship has already developed a comprehensive PSEA policy specifically tailored to the Friendship NGO's FDMN health project. Clearly articulate the organization's commitment to zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, outlining preventive measures and consequences for violations. Conducted specialized training sessions for all staff and volunteers involved in the health project. Provided detailed instruction on PSEA principles, the code of conduct, and the importance of maintaining a safe and respectful environment. Ensure that all team members are well-versed in reporting procedures. Established a confidential and easily accessible mechanism for reporting incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. Ensure that beneficiaries and staff are aware of the reporting channels and guarantee a prompt and thorough investigation of all reported cases. Implemented a robust system for holding individuals accountable for PSEA violations. Ensured that disciplinary actions were taken swiftly, and communicated transparently with relevant authorities when necessary. Scheduled regular audits to assess the implementation of PSEA measures. Use audit findings as a basis for continuous improvement, ensuring that the organization remains responsive and adaptive to emerging challenges. By adhering to this comprehensive plan, Friendship can effectively mainstream PSEA for the proposed project, contributing to a secure, respectful, and protective environment for all involved.
