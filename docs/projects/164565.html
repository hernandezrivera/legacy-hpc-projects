Project Summary

Terre des Hommes ( TDH Italy ) proposed nutrition project seeks to address not only the inadequate coverage and quality of life-saving services for the treatment of acute malnutrition in children under five and vulnerable pregnant or lactating women (PLWs) , but also some of the key determinants of malnutrition through promotion of improve infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and healthy behaviors linked to unnecessary deaths amongst vulnerable populations in (Izra, Al Tal, Al Hasakeh city, Qahtaniyyeh, Tal Tamer, Shadadah). By integrating CMAM program into the already established PHC clinics of the implementing partner in or establishing new CMAM centers supported by outreach team to screen and identify cases of malnutrition among boys, girls and women amongst IDPs and host community, so unmet needs of the acutely malnourished boys, girls and women amongst the vulnerable returnees, refugees and IDPs in can be served.
SAM classification and treatment will also be supported and strengthened in referral clinic through training and supervision as needed. Critical IYCF practices as well as promotion of appropriate health seeking and preventive behaviors for the main diseases will be targeted through the establishment of a mothers’ group network.
The project plans to conduct focused trainings for SARC volunteers to build their capacity, to provide structured CMAM and IYCF services and activities to those in need, case management and referral, capacity building activities to local partner SARC through the training of the volunteers and community members, and support facility and community based 1-1 counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices.
The intervention is interested in the framework of the strategic objective 1,2 and 3 of the HRP 2020.
Total beneficiaries include the following:
Children under 5, Pregnant and Lactating Women, Refugee Returnees, Trainers, Promoters, Caretakers, committee members, etc.
Indirect Beneficiaries: Family members of mother group participants who are anticipated to benefit from the healthier practices of the mothers group participants.
Project Beneficiaries:
PLWs: 20,220 pregnant or lactating women who will be screening for malnutrition using MUAC (Middle upper arm circumference) and will receive awareness raising sessions, peer to peer sessions and care group module.
PLWs MAM: 1,415 pregnant or lactating women identified with moderate acute malnutrition who will receive treatment and follow up.
Children under five years: 45,597 who will be screened for malnutrition using MUAC (Middle upper arm circumference).
Children under five years MAM: 1,012 children identified with moderate acute malnutrition who will receive treatment and follow up.
Children under five years SAM: 434 children identified with sever acute malnutrition (both with and without medical complications) who will receive treatment and follow up.
The nutrition sector in the 2020 HRP and other documents has prioritized the treatment of acute malnutrition in the most under-served, conflict affected areas for scale-up of coverage and effectiveness in those provinces with the highest burden and worst access. The targeted beneficiaries will be the IDPs, returnees, refugees and vulnerable host boys and girls under 5 and pregnant and lactating women. The beneficiaries of CMAM services will be identified by their anthropometric classification (MUAC, Weight for height and edema check) of severe and moderate acute malnutrition as per national guidelines. The selection of the intervention areas and the targeting of beneficiaries in informal settlements and other vulnerable areas will be based on a gap analysis from a secondary data review and a screening exploratory phase which to be conducted by the implementation partner.

4 - Needs

Needs related to malnutrition basing on the following: Basing on the needs analysis of 2020 HRP nutrition annex:"Acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers as well as population affected by conflict. Throughout Syria, about 240,000 children under the age of five years and over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers are in need for urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services. At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications, including death. Furthermore, it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants under 6 months of age".Basing on 2020 SMART survey results:GAM (Global Acute Malnutrition) figures in areas of intervention varies from 2.8 1.1 to 2.8 as SAM (Sever acute malnutrition) varies from 0.3 to 0.7 and MAM (Moderate acute malnutrition) varies from 0.9 to 2.1 according to wight for height results.GAM figures in in areas of intervention varies from 1.0 to 1.6 as SAM varies from 0.2 to 0.6 and MAM varies from 0.5 to 1.4 according to middle upper arm circumference results.As the CMAM program in Izra' supported by TDH-IT and implemented by SARC strted with the beginning of January 2020 the following have been reached:Screening: 362 beneficiaries have been reached including 160 PLWs. Out of them 2 cases of SAM within children under five have been identified, both of them are Females. 15 cases of MAM within children under five years have been identified, 7 females and 8 males.TDH-IT aims to start other CMAM and IYCF programs in the mentioned areas of intervention as the expected number of beneficiaries is up to 65,000 beneficiary.list of need assessment sources:Syria SMART surveys 2019.Nutrition Sector Humanitarian Need Overview 2020 5 WHO guidelines for management of severe acute malnutrition.Nutrition Sector Humanitarian Need Overview 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of CMAM/IYCF activities will be by integrating CMAM/IYCF programs into the already existing implementing partners health facilities ( 5 centers )and support it with outreach teams. Conduct a focused needs assessment with the implementation partner to finalize service coverage mapping and selection of locations for CMAM outreach sites (OTP, SFP) and women's groups keeping some flexibility to respond to the changing security environment. Prepare at least 2 outreach teams with ongoing reevaluation of needs and security. Recruit and train nutrition extension workers for MUAC screening, IYCF promotion and default tracing (Volunteers). Recruit and train health and nutrition staff for 1 center of CMAM and IYCF practices. Provide outpatient treatment of SAM and MAM for children under 5 and PLWs at CMAM outreach sites (OTP,SFP). In liaison with MoH and nutrition sector establish a referral system for SAM patients with complications requiring inpatient care. Promote infant and young child feeding in returnee sites and host communities in health facilities and through community outreach and mother’s groups. Support process of selecting lead mothers by their community. With establishment of each new CMAM outreach site, set up mothers group networks with 1 lead mother: 10-15 PLWs or mothers of children under 2 years. Conduct ongoing supervision and training of nutrition extension workers and lead mothers through biweekly meetings. Identify or adapt IEC materials and curriculum for lead mothers, and extension worker/promoters. Coordinate training schedule with UNICEF to conduct training for CMAM outreach sites on CMAM and nutrition in emergencies. Train and support center staff as needed in the use of standardized protocols and guidelines for the management of severe acute malnutrition in potential referral centers for CMAM outreach sites. Initiate Breast-feeding awareness raising campaign in areas of intervention.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities, M1: SARC displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations . Currently SARC already accessing the mentioned locations with funding from various entities for several months. However, if SARC lost the access to project locations, Should SARC, TdH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with local actors, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue. R2: TdH are unable to timely contract technically skilled employee, M2:TdH has solid recruitment/human resources processes and regulations to ensure the best qualified candidates are hired in a timely manner. Early launching of the process is key. Vacancies will be publicly announced soon after the approval is received .R3: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities, M3: TdH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TdH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU, R4: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria, M4: TdH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources. R5: Security risks : TdH and Local partner’s staff are exposed to threats, including kidnapping, detention, intimidation, and serious injury, M5: the security situation has been considered relatively calm but unpredictable and partners are able to conduct their work in relative safety, TdH Security person and Security Focal points in the project locations are regularly analyze the operating environment and have established safeguards to prevent and respond to risks of insecurity which may arise, addressed through location-specific safety and security management plans.
