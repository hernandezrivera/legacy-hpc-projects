Project Summary

In 2019 Tdh will continue to assist highly affected IDPs, returnees and host communities in hard to reach areas and areas of displacement. In line with Tdh capacities and expertise, Tdh response will be seeking systematically an integrated approach of Child Protection and Education and full coordination with Tdh WASH department, as a mean to enhance scope and impact of the action, while maximizing resources.
Tdh programming in Education is strongly oriented towards mainstreaming child safeguarding and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) within the response, as per Tdh’s relevant global policies. Also, specific needs of children boys, girls, women and men, persons living with disabilities and vulnerable beneficiaries are addressed through culturally appropriate processes and dedicated monitoring.
Tdh programming is framed by a Quality and Accountability Framework, which is informed by the Core Humanitarian Standards and actively seeks to establish relevant strategies to link relief, rehabilitation and development.
Tdh action in 2019 aligns with the HRP Strategic Objective 1 and 2, and with Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 and 2. Under the CO1 Tdh will ensure access to quality formal and non-formal learning opportunities for children in areas of displacement and return. The action aims at endorsing transition of children to recognized educational streams, discourage drop out and ensure meaningful access. A set of key actions will be:
• Monitoring of access to quality and inclusive formal and non-formal education. Supporting children in need of remedial education as a mean to endorse effective enrolment to formal education opportunities.
• Supporting schooling and teaching with teaching and learning material.
• Awareness raising and back to learning campaigning. Soft life skills sessions to improve children’s capacity to how to stay motivated, how to focus at school, how to organise your workload, how to grow up with your classmates.
• Structural improvement of facilities with a specific focus on safety and security and accessibility.
• Distribution of furniture and equipment.
• Joint intervention with WASH in school as part of the quality learning framework of Tdh.
Under CO2 Tdh will promote inclusive and protective learning environment able to support reintegration and attendance of children in conflict and displacement affected context. In the targeted areas, indeed, children have experience isolation, lack of access to a structured learning and social environment, exposure to violence and coercion, exposure to negative emotional and behavioral coping mechanisms, frustration for difficulties to adapt to new context or re-adapt to their context of origin. Drawing from this analysis, Tdh will:
• Lead and promote psycho-social mainstreaming within learning environment
• Capacity strengthening of teachers on inclusion, psycho-social support and safe identification and referral of individual protection risks.
• Strengthen capacities of Parents and teachers association in identification of out of school children and support of enrollment.
• Distribution of PSS kits to school
Under the hat of CO3 Tdh will:
• Establish and facilitate PTAs
• Support teachers’ capacity building on class management

Needs

In the targeted areas of intervention, Education needs continue to be priority among IDP population both in camp and out of camp, remainees, returnees and host communities. In a recent Tdh monitoring of education services in West Anbar it difficulties in the proper re-launch of the school year have been observed. Insecurity and shortage in teaching staff limit the capacity of the DoE to launch or to provide continuity to the school year in most remote areas. It is common to have class size up to 70 children and schooling over 2-3 shifts is observed in most of the schools assessed. In the area, numerous schools suffer from damage to premises and environment putting children at risk (such as rubble or unmanaged waste) . Looting and damages of school furniture and school material has been claimed by most of the interviewed headmasters. According to the DoEs consulted, no more than 50% of the needs in textbooks will be covered by MoE funds for the school year 2018-19. In rural villages of Qu’im and Rawa child marriage is largely observed and is particularly affecting girls. Boys are more likely to drop out from school and more affected by child labor practices. The lack of intermediary and secondary schools impacts the incidence of both early marriage cases and child labor. Tdh recently led another education assessment in Tal Afar District (October-November 2018). In all schools a significant lack of teachers has been observed. Volunteers teachers are also missing, as in many areas scattered insecurity and inter-communal tension prevent part of the population from returning to their homes. In the assessed schools, presence of children living with disabilities has been registered. The premises are insufficient to accommodate all children, hampering their capacity to attend regularly classes. In the assessed schools, less than 20% of the needs in textbooks was covered. Most of the schools are running over shifts and up to 4 schools function in the same building. In all present areas of intervention of Tdh, regular consultation with school’s staff, DoEs, caregivers and parents is held. During a recent field visit in Hawija (November 2018), the interviewed headmasters claimed to face issues in ensuring sufficient number of teachers and appropriate teacher-children ratio. The lack of material and the state of premises, including WASH facilities, make learning and teaching conditions unsuitable for proper schooling. Capacity building of teachers is underlined as being crucial in all monitored areas. Lack of teaching and learning material is largely reported in all assessed areas also. In all governorate proposed for the intervention, children have lost up to 4 years of schooling. Many of them have never attended a structured learning and social environment and have been exposed to great uncertainty and traumatic events. Interviewed key stakeholders consider of primary importance to enhance the presence of psychosocial support services within learning spaces and to build capacity of teachers in mainstreaming psychosocial support, inclusion and positive class management. According to 75% of the interviewed teachers and caregivers in Hawija during a recent assessment (October 2018), negative behaviour change of children is observed, resulting in anxiety, fear and aggressive reactions. A better coordination between teachers and parents to identify, prevent and mitigate psychosocial distress among children and ensure access to learning opportunities it is claimed by interviewed teachers and headmasters as key to face constraints that most school are experiencing.

Activities

Tdh in 2019 will support access to quality and inclusive formal and non-formal learning opportunities for children and adolescents in need of education support. The action will be carried out by promoting community-supported education services and by endorsing formal education structures to resume services and ensure equitable and inclusive access. The response will focus on the following key actions: - Distribution of teaching and learning material- Improvement of infrastructures of learning spaces- Distribution of equipment and furniture- Monitoring of attendance of children at risk of drop out- Identification and mapping of out of school children- Support to the enrolment of out of school children- Awareness raising to support reintegration to school and to mitigate the risk of drop out. - Education system strengthening with a specific focus on PTA establishment and facilitation- Capacity strengthening of teachers in safe identification and referral of cases of children at individual risk- Capacity building of teachers on class management, positive discipline, inclusion, gender mainstreaming and psychosocial mainstreaming- Mine risk education for children at risk and training on MRE for teachers- Monitoring of learning outcomes - Catch up classes for children in need of remedial education- Provision of psychosocial activities in learning spaces- Distribution of psychosocial support kit to school as a mean to allow schools to continue child-led and teacher-led psychosocial support activities within learning spaces. - Monitoring of WASH need in schools and advocacy and orientation of WASH partners. Dedicated M&E and information management system are supporting monitoring and assessment of formal and non-formal activities. M&E is designed to be participatory and age and gender disaggregated to monitor equal and inclusive access to proposed activities. Joint WASH-Child Protection and Education Quality Learning Environment monitoring exercises will be carried out all along the response. The Quality and Learning Environment monitoring will identify concerns and priority actions, but also major achievements against the commitment towards each learning space that Tdh undertake. The exercise will inform advocacy efforts and recommendations for further programming and information sharing among actors.Tdh exit strategy from the proposed intervention will be pronged over the following approaches: -strengthening of the education system to promote durability in the scope of the proposed response-promoting community-supported education processes. - mitigating the impact of durable negative effect on the most vulnerable population, in line with the No lost Generation Initiative. To achieve the above expected results and support and consolidate the proposed response, the following key actions will be put in place: -training of teachers on EiE and specifically in inclusive education, class management, PSS mainstreaming, Mine Risk Education. Teachers will be trained (2 per school-1 man and 1 woman) to be CM focal point and being able to refer to Tdh cases eligible for individual protection support.-quality learning environment monitoring and formulation of key recommendations to headmasters and education governmental bodies. -establishment and facilitation of Parents-teachers association to ensure community-supported education processes. It includes PTA-led processes of identification of out of school children and training on community-mobilisation and effective participatory school management processes. - catch up classes for children in need. The action is meant to support durable reintegration of children, avoiding further drop out. - support of vulnerable children reintegration in formal learning opportunities or no formal learning opportunities. The latter to be considered as transitioning to formal education. - improvement of schools and ALP premises infrastructures and equipment, as a mean to mitigate durably the risk of drop out and allow formal education services to restore quickly continuative provision of services. -awareness raising of children, adolescents and caregivers on the importance of education, but also on soft skills able to improve attendance and performance of children (e.g. how to stay motivated, how to organise the workload, how to contribute to improve learning environment..).

Protection Risk Analysis

Continuous risk analysis informs Tdh programming all over the programme cycle. Risk analysis takes into consideration specific needs of boys and girls, women and men, persons living with disabilities. Specific needs of vulnerable groups are also considered and in particular marginalised group, minorities, groups non protected by social floor. The following risks for 2019 within the Education response have been singled out as medium-high ranked: 1 Operational access: security continues to be intermittent in most of the areas of implementation, with a specific focus on Hawija, Tooz, Sherqat and Tal Afar. 2 access of Tdh to most vulnerable marginalised groups: stigma and denial of access to services is observed in many areas of intervention for marginalised groups by the local authorities. The identified patterns is drawn over religious, ethnical and affiliation to armed group-based division and often exercised by the local authorities 3 acceptance by the beneficiaries: prioritization on the basis of needs is challenged by acceptance concerns. The access to most vulnerable population in need of assistance can encounter blame and stigma in particular against beneficiaries perceived as affiliated and marginalised groups. 4)Possible presence of UXOs within schools, which were severely damaged during the fighting, poses a risk to children. 5) Gender based restriction on access to education within certain areas based on the local customs (child labor, early marriage) can impede the enrolment in school. 6) Capacity to reach out of school children might be limited within the school based response and would therefore have a disproportionately smaller positive impact on these children.

Mitigation Measures

The risk analysis takes into consideration protection risks of beneficiaries and staff and it is supported at coordination level and field level by dedicated staff such as: Risk Advisor, Field Coordinators, Liason Officers. Also, a Risk Department at HQ level ensure technical guidance and streamline with internal and inter-agency procedures. Also, dedicated risk analysis are designed to ensure compliance with internal Child Safeguarding policies and PSEA policies. 1) Operational access is monitored by a trained staff through a set of key actions carried out by a dedicated team at coordination and field level, among them: field coordinator, liason officer, risk advisor. Daily monitoring of safe access to the area of intervention it is carried out at base level and through inter-base networking. Access is prepared at the beginning of the operations and all along the programme through the establishing of field-based networking of key informants and by continuous coordination with local authorities, communities national and international actors. Incidents are avoided also thanks to a spread efforts in making Tdh’s mandate and action appropriately known by key stakeholders. Security plan and contingency plan are active to respond to crises and incidents. In order to provide continuity to the activities, Tdh privilege to recruit locally based staff able to access the areas despite checkpoints closure and limitation in the movements. Their security and the security of beneficiary is continuously monitored and assessed. 2) A challenged access to most marginalised groups it is mitigated by continuous risk assessment, that it is designed to take into account expressed needs and concerns of affected communities, local authorities, national and international actors, host communities. Consultations systematically seeks to collect voices from IDPs communities, host communities, returnees, boys and girls, adolescents boys and girls, women and men, marginalised groups and minorities. A conflict-sensitive lens is applied to all proposed activities and screened with key informants, relevant clusters, national and international actors, communities and locally-hired national staff. 3) Acceptance is built on a set a fundamental activities, designed to ensure accountability to affected communities principles to be put into practice. Continuous consultation with communities inform programming. A flexible approach allows Tdh to re-designed activities according to feedbacks and suggestions. Feedback mechanisms are systematically integrated into Monitoring Plan and evaluation plan. Tools and methodologies are expressively designed and contextualised to ensure voice to women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, marginalised groups. Also, Tdh’s approach within the targeted areas of implementation is conceived to build up on existing resources, including community-based resources and governmental resources. Locally hired staff represent a key informant in ensuring that activities and approaches are culturally appropriated and conflict sensitive. 4) Tdh liaises with local security forces as well as IMMAP to ensure that the schools of implementation are safe to work at and construction work would not pose a risk to Tdh staff or the children. 5) Tdh will try to address the risk of children dropping out of school due to local customs with community level back to school campaigns as well as with messages stressing the importance of education for both boys and girls. 6) Tdh will strive to reach as many out of school through backto school campaigns and by working closely with the community level structures as well as with the Parent-Teacher associations.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Tdh is accountable to affected population, whose involvement as a key participant in all phases of the project cycle in crucial to ensure meaningful participation of the beneficiary community. Tdh incorporates and promotes the five IASC Commitments to Accountability to Affected Population and framed its entire response within a Tdh Quality and Accountability framework. 1) Leadership/Governance: Tdh promotes leadership and governance, and ensures that staff is aware that programs and approaches must be in line with accountability to affected population principles. This means a commitment to build on the capacities of local population and strengthen the resilience of people affected, whenever possible. Collaboration and consultation with local actors is crucial to ensure that the implementation of activities meet the needs of the affected population and will encourage beneficiaries to actively participate. Participation of boys and girls, women and men and person with disabilities to decision making is systematically sought. 2) Transparency: Tdh ensures the transparency of communication with the affected population to help both Tdh and beneficiaries make informed decisions. There is a focus on providing clear and transparent information about consequences and risks, particularly related to essential services for beneficiaries. At all stages of the project cycle, continuous and transparent communication on Tdh’s activities and objectives will be conveyed to local authorities all other actors operating in the area, with feedback mechanisms also in place to allow for reciprocal information sharing. Tdh will also observe full coordination and information sharing with relevant cluster and sub-cluster actors both at national and governorate levels. 3) Feedback and Complaints: Feedback and complaint mechanisms that are child friendly and adequate to the age and the gender of beneficiaries are set up. These mechanisms are designed to be inclusive, accessible, confidential and will not expose participants to any harm. Feedback and complaints will be systematically considered, and focus group discussions will be regularly conducted with beneficiaries to gather feedback and report on achievement in integrating feedback in the response. This will allow Tdh to assess the level of beneficiary satisfaction and adapt the design of activities and approach to implementation to meet the current needs of beneficiaries. Tdh will take all necessary measures to ensure operationalization of Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) and PSEA policy, including the identification of a Child Safeguarding focal point, the setup of safe and accessible reporting mechanisms and the adoption of measures to ensure data protection, confidentiality and dignity. All Tdh employees, partners and visitors are carefully briefed on Tdh’s CSP and must read, understand and sign the policy before starting any assignment, collaboration or visit. 4) Participation: Affected populations will play an active role in decision-making processes, ensuring that principles of accountability is incorporated at all stage of the project cycle. To ensure comprehensive representation of beneficiaries, a particular focus will be given to child participation, specifically girls and children with disabilities. During all activities, children will be encouraged to participate and speak out. A wide range of participatory activities will be implemented to make sure that all opinions, and above opinions of those more marginalized, are considered. Community-based design activities are prioritized. 5) Design, Monitoring and Evaluation: A participatory design, monitoring and evaluation of activities is ensured through ongoing consultation with beneficiaries. Focus groups discussion, bilateral and groups informal meetings and other activities will constantly aim at improving the capacity of the project to better meet beneficiaries’ needs and collect best practices and recommendations.
