Project Summary

The principle aim of this project is the implementation of syndromic and event-based surveillance in the cross-border locations and along migrant transit points to strengthen the overall early warning surveillance system. The primary level data collection will be conducted via IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix programme in Libya.
The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) is a system developed by IOM to track and monitor displacement and population mobility, provide critical information to decision-makers and responders during crises and contribute to better understanding of population flows. DTM was first conceptualized in 2004 and has since been adapted for implementation in over 60 countries including in contexts of conflict, natural disaster, complex emergencies and protracted crises.

1 - Needs

The movement of large numbers of people has created new opportunities for the spread and establishment of common or novel infectious diseases. An effective surveillance system to detect emergent and re-emergent infections is needed among other provisions in order to stop the spread of infectious diseases that may occur as a result of mass migration. Migration-inclusive public health systems improve public health as well as global, regional, national and local health outcomes which are critical to achieving universal health coverage. Libya is a country of destination for migrants originating from over 40 countries of origin, and especially cross border migration across Libya's eastern, southern and western borders presents significant public health opportunities and challenges. During 2019 IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix conducted health surveillance along migration routes and identified migrant vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs from public health perspective as presented in the DTM Libya Migrant Vulnerability and Humanitarian Needs Assessment (link: https://migration.iom.int/reports/libya-migrant-vulnerability-and-humanitarian-needs-assessment). A need to continue the health surveillance and to conduct in-depth health sectoral was also identified as a need during this assessment. Therefore under this project, DTM proposes to continue to conduct health surveillance exercises through its Flow Monitoring Surveys (FMS) and implement sector specific assessments to facilitate migrant health programming. The proposed project will pay specific attention to gender perspectives by including gender specific focus in the design and implementation of the health surveillance questionnaires and by conducting gender analysis for reporting. DTM Migrant Vulnerability and Humanitarian Needs Assessment report presents specific gender analysis, and via that highlights the case of female migrants being relatively more vulnerable than their male counterparts. All DTM data analysis includes gender and age disaggregation to lead to programming that contributes to equality across gender and age.
