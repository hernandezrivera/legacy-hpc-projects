Project Summary

The project aims to improve the quality of life for 600 children with type 1 diabetes mellitus in Gaza Strip (56% boys and 44% girls) through improving availability and accessibility to healthcare services. This aim will be reached through providing comprehensive healthcare services, psychosocial support and raising awareness activities for children and their caregivers. The project will be carried out by QRCS, in partnership with Ard El Insan (AEI) covering children living in southern governorates of the Gaza Strip and Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) covering children in Gaza and North Gaza.
The proposed intervention will include the following:
-Children with type 1 diabetes will visit local partners’ clinics monthly to receive the following healthcare services:
- Medical follow up by endocrinologist.
- Required diagnostic tests.
- Necessary medication and micronutrients.
- Glucometer and blood glucose test strips.
-Psychosocial support and awareness activities targeting the children, caregivers and community:
- Awareness and psychosocial support sessions (60 sessions).
- Develop and distribute educational booklets (5000 booklets).
- Conduct advocacy campaign around World Diabetes Day on November 14.
- Cooking demonstration and practice (60 sessions).
- Recreational fun days (8 days).
Other activities include: - Cash assistance for targeted children and caregivers covering their transportation to health clinics in order to reduce financial barriers to access the medical services. – Capacity building for AEI through programing their software to include needed database for children with type 1 diabetes.
QRCS has previously provided support to T1DM children in cooperation with PMRS. The proposed project comes as an extension of those services to the south of Gaza Strip so service would cover the entire Gaza Strip. One of the main outputs of the proposed intervention is a database for children with T1DM that will be at the national level. PMRS is currently in the process of acquiring a software program for this purpose, and through this proposal AEI will be acquiring a similar program in order to provide full geographical coverage.
Target beneficiaries will be reached through project’s announcement and through coordination with United Nations Relief and Work Agency (UNRWA), Ministry of Health (MoH) clinics and local CBOs.
The proposed intervention will run over a period of 12 months and to benefit a total of 106,200 direct beneficiaries (600 children, 600 caregivers, 105,000 community members). The project will lead to improving children’s health conditions and growth patterns. Moreover, its impact will last beyond the project's period as it will ensure avoiding deterioration in health status of children targeted in a safe manner and will minimize the load on their families and MoH health system.
The proposed project is aligned with the national health strategy 2022-2025 Objective 2: Enhancing services for communicable and non-communicable diseases, control of DM and hypertension program. Furthermore, it responds to the Health Cluster Objective No. 1: “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”, and it is linked to Second Strategic Objective: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under international humanitarian law”.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip is still affected by the protracted humanitarian crisis which is tied to prolonged occupation, internal political division, and recurrent escalations of Israeli hostilities along with limited humanitarian and financial resources. The healthcare system of the Gaza Strip is facing a real challenge to deliver essential health care services with limited resources and 16 years of access restrictions. According to the recent 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview “HNO’, more than one million people in the Gaza Strip are in need of health-related humanitarian assistance. In addition, according to MSAN 2022, 53% of households in the Gaza Strip have at least one member with chronic illness.Children with type 1 diabetes living in the Gaza Strip are among the most affected by the current situation. They suffer from limited access to quality healthcare services, lack of medication, and resulting debilitating consequences such as (impaired physical growth, cognitive development, blindness, renal failure, legs amputations, osteoporosis, cancer of the gastrointestinal tract, and others), harsh economic situation and scarcity in community awareness and engagement. According to the Ministry of Health annual report in 2021, the number of children with type 1 diabetes under 18 years old reached 662 boys and girls. In reference to MENA 2019 Palestine fact sheet, the number was 677, while the UNRWA health center (Annual report 2021) recorded the total number of all type 1 diabetic patients registered as 1641. Of the total diabetic population in Palestine, 4.4% of persons are diagnosed with type 1 diabetes, and 95.3% are diagnosed with type 2 diabetes. However, there is insufficient and incomplete national data on the quantity, quality, and scope of the burden of diabetic Mellitus patients. One of the intended outcomes of this project will be the availability of the first nationally accurate data about children with type 1 diabetes in the Gaza Strip.During the evaluation of QRCS's ongoing program with PMRS targeting diabetic children, surveys, and interviews with different groups of beneficiaries were conducted, from which disaggregated information on boys, and girls, with disabilities, was collected. This information will be the baseline data for this project's interventions to ensure the gender differences and children with disabilities' needs are addressed.Based on HNO 2023, Type 1 diabetic patients (less than 18 years old) who need access to essential health services within the Gaza Strip are considered a priority where all of them are vulnerable and in urgent need of health intervention.The proposed project is aligned with the national health strategy 2022-2025 Objective 2: Enhancing services for communicable and non-communicable diseases, control of DM, and hypertension program. Furthermore, it responds to the Health Cluster Objective No. 1: “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”, and it is linked to the Second Strategic Objective: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under international humanitarian law”. The project’s design considered the listed activities in Health Response Plan 2023 targeting children with type 1 diabetes, which include the regular provision of insulin analogs, education and awareness programs for caregivers, provision of lab supplies, and psychosocial support.

2 - Activities

The project will be implemented by QRCS, in partnership with AEI covering children living in southern regions and with PMRS covering children living in northern regions.QRCS has extensive expertise in procurement and follows rigorous system that ensures transparency and quality. QRCS will be in charge of procurement and will oversee the implementation of all other activities by partner organizations as well as be responsible for reporting through a rigorous MEAL plan. AEI and PMRS are community-based Palestinian health organizations, seeking to improve the overall physical, mental, and social wellbeing of people all over the Gaza Strip. They have full capacities to provide the planned intervention and both parties have expertise in providing several services to children with T1DM.Children will be reached through announcements using websites and social media and through coordination with (UNRWA), (MoH) clinics and local CBOs.Children with T1DM living in South Gaza Strip will visit AEI clinic in Khan Younis, while those living in Gaza and the North will visit PMRS clinic in Gaza. 600 children with T1DM (56% boys and 44% girls) will visit the clinics monthly to get comprehensive healthcare services as follows:- Full examination and follow up by an endocrinologist who will manage a proper treatment plan.- Requested diagnostic tests will be carried out in the clinics. The following lab tests will be conducted: (HA1C test every three-months, lipid profile and kidney function test every six-months, C peptide and insulin resistance one-time, anti-tissue-transglutaminase screen for newly discovered cases and vitamin D test one-time for all children and twice for vitamin D deficiency cases).- Necessary medication (insulin analogues) will be provided for each child every month. Refugees’ diabetic children take their insulin analogues pens from UNRWA clinics. Thus, quantities of insulin analogues pens were calculated to cover non-refugees’ diabetic children. Less amount of medication was added for refugees’ children in case of extra quantities needed due to their tests and treatment plan correction and in case of shortage of distribution of medications by UNRWA. Multivitamins supplement and vitamin D prophylactic dose will be provided for all children. Therapeutic dose of vitamin D will be provided for cases of vitamin D deficiency.- A glucometer will be provided for each child. 2 boxes of blood glucose test strips will be provided monthly to ensure continuous control of blood sugar levels.To ensure accessibility to health services and reduce financial barriers, cash assistance for targeted beneficiaries covering their transportation to clinics will be provided. Moreover, scheduling visits and communication with caregivers will be monthly. Defaulters will be contacted by telephone within 48 hours of their absence. If not responding, home visits to assess defaulter’s situation within one week after telephone contact may be conducted. If family insists on defaulting despite all trials of return to clinic, then a discharge against advice is made (if family returns later, the child will be re-admitted once applicable to admission terms). Late complications of diabetes such as Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) will be referred to hospitals. Children with T1DM and their caregivers will receive psychosocial support and awareness activities as following:- 60 awareness and psychosocial support sessions targeting 900 children and caregivers (50% women and 50% children) aiming at increasing their capacities toward good healthy habits including proper nutrition and ensure continuous of care from center to household, each session will include 15-16 persons. Attendance will be divided according to children’s gender and age groups. Some sessions will include children with their mothers. Others will be for groups of boys or girls at the same age. This activity is targeting children’s mothers aiming at enhancing their decision-making role regarding their children’s health status.- 5000 of booklets will be developed and distributed by AEI in southern regions and by PMRS in northern regions, to raise community awareness toward diabetic disease type 1 and enhancing diabetic children engagement in communities. It is expected to benefit 2,550 men and 2,450 women.- Advocacy campaign set to launch on World Diabetes Day on November 14 will be conducted by coordinated work between AEI and PMRS targeting 100,000 beneficiaries from community (51,000 men and 49,000 women) which will raise awareness about the disease resulting in early detection, quick response, and appropriate intervention.- 60 sessions of cooking demonstration and practice will be conducted for 150 mothers of newly diagnosed and uncontrolled glucose level cases, aiming at teaching and preparing variety of dietetic regimen resulting in controlled blood-glucose levels for children with T1DM, each woman will attend 4 sessions either in AEI or PMRS clinic based on her geographical area (each session will include 10 women).- 8 recreational fun days will be conducted for diabetic patients under 12 years old and their caregivers as part of psychosocial support activities, each day will include 70 children with their caregivers.Special awareness activities, health, lifestyle and nutrition topics will be discussed using different education techniques adapted to the audience, including questions-answers, exchange of experiences, audio-visual films and cartoons; beneficiaries' active participation will be encouraged. Gender differences needs will be taken into consideration through selecting men and women staff. Furthermore, both clinics are well adapted for people with disabilities with ramps and elevators.As part of capacity building of NGOs information system, software programing of AEI system will allow for maintaining an updated database for children with T1DM. This activity will ensure national registration and monitoring system for children with T1DM.

3 - Indicators

The implementation of the proposed project will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system enabling us to gather the needed data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects as well as provide corrective measures when needed. QRCS will measure the following indicators:1. Indicators related to health cluster: - Number of NCD patients benefitted from access to medicine: the project is designed to benefit 600 diabetic patients under 18 years old (56% boys and 44% girls) from access to medicines provided through AEI and PMRS living in all Gaza Strip governorates.- Number of people benefited from procured drugs and medical supplies: it is expected to benefit the total number of targeted children (600) from the drugs and medical supplies that will be procured by QRCS and provided by the local parties (56% boys and 44% girls) living in all Gaza Strip governorates.- Number of people directly benefiting from NCD awareness sessions: total of 900 children and their caregivers (50% women and 50% children) from diabetic disease awareness sessions.- Number of people benefitted from cash and voucher assistance for the improvement of health condition: through the proposed intervention, 1200 people (600 adult, 600 children) from all Gaza Strip governorates will benefit from cash assistance to cover their transportation from and to health clinics.2. Other indicators: - Number of people benefited from cooking demonstration and practice: 150 mothers of under 12 years old diabetic children will benefit from cooking demonstration and practice sessions from all Gaza Strip governorates.- 5,000 people will benefit from distribution educational booklets in all Gaza Strip governorates that will be developed by AEI and PMRS (2,550 men and 2,450 women).- 100,000 people benefited from advocacy campaign on World Diabetes Day on November 14 conducted by local parties from all Gaza Strip governorates (51,000 men and 49,000 women).- Number of NGO benefited from capacity building: through this project one NGO (AEI clinic in Khan Younis) will benefit from capacity building for its information system through software programing to have good database and monitoring for children with type 1 diabetes records.- Number of people benefited from psychosocial support activities: 560 people (50% adult and 50% children under 12 years old) from all Gaza Strip governorates will benefit from conducting 8 recreational Fun days.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

QRCS, AEI and PMRS understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian fundamental principle. Throughout the project implementation period, all partners will make sure to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of the activities especially on PWD. The project in its purpose of controlling the disease will lead to stoppage of highly possible disability which might restrain all educational and cognitive process in childhood or chronic vulnerability during adulthood. The free service and transportation ensure “leaving no one behind” promise. The guidelines adopted by QRCS, AEI and PMR in dealing with received complaints and providing the timely response to people’s concern guarantee preventing further harm. Regarding the media coverage of project’s activities (photos, videos and stories), a consent form or parenteral permission form will be signed by beneficiaries. QRCS, AEI and PMRS will ensure the full confidentiality of beneficiaries concerns either during intervention provision or their informational security.
