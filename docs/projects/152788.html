Project Summary

This project aims to respond to protection and psychosocial needs of IDPs, returnees and Host Community in Maban and Yambio. To do so, JRS will use a comprehensive approach, offering different services and support. It will target an estimation of 8,500 (4150M, 4350F) Persons of Concern (POCs), focusing on vulnerable groups such as: people of specific needs (children with disabilities, elderly, widows...), children and youth.
The intervention will be aligned with the 3 Strategic Objectives for the Protection cluster:
S.O.1: "Provide lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach, and priority areas"
Under this objective JRS will contribute with (1) material distribution for POCs to help them improve their living conditions, (2) Strengthening and ensuring Safe referrals pathways between JRS and other organizations to ensure a comprehensive respond to POCs’ needs and (3) protection/support for people with specific needs.
S.O.2: "Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience"
Regarding this objective JRS will focus on (1) promoting psychosocial wellbeing and strengthening resilience mechanisms of POCs. This will be done through psychosocial support activities (group counselling, individual counselling and basic counselling training), (2) protection awareness activities and (3) workshop for youth to help them develop resilience skills.
S.O.3: "Enable durable solution for IDPs and other affected populations"
JRS will carry out (1) peaceful coexistence and conflict mitigation activities and work on (2) profiling IDPs to make sure their needs are collected and heard from the humanitarian community.
This project will be implemented with a community based approach, to strengthen its participatory structure so that refugees and host community members are truly partners in the provision of services. For the psychosocial activities JRS will train and recruits IDPs and host community members to spearhead services to the people of concern. Also, the JRS staff will undergo continued capacity building to enable them develop competencies that are required for service delivery. This will allow IDPs and host community members to take control of the services provided to their communities now and in the future.

Needs

JRS annually conducts a comprehensive needs assessment reaching out to members from host and IDPs community. JRS also participates and relies on the AGDM (Age, Gender, Diversity and Mainstreaming) Assessment coordinated by UNHCR, and other sectoral assessments (education, livelihoods. etc.). Maban:The findings in the needs assessment point that the violence in Sudan, compounded by the recent conflict in South Sudan and worsened still by the interethnic violence, have contributed to the psychological distress of host community members. These have engendered adverse consequences on mental health (e.g. sexual and gender based violence, trauma, stress, frustration, insecurity, depression, suicidal ideation, and anxiety, among others). However, unfortunately, since fully fledged community-based psychosocial services are still limited in the host community, the Persons of Concern (POCs) often cannot consistently access appropriate counselling services that would enable them to cope with their trauma. Most commonly, the limited services that are available are facility-based, offered at clinics and health posts, but most POCs would prefer accessing them at home or within the community. Therefore, this population would greatly benefit from individual home visit counselling. Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) services are often not attended consistently and a personalized, home-based approach is not always prioritized by agencies in charge of camp management. Idleness among youth and lack of knowledge about the risks of substance abuse puts many youth at risk, especially of forced conscription. Yambio: The main needs found in Yambio are: (1) Lack of access to non-food items for Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) in IDP camp (2) Inadequate mechanisms of profiling of IDPs and reporting of protection concerns in the IDP camps (4) large number of conflicts and tensions among the community and lack of peaceful conflict resolution

Activities

All JRS activities will be carried out with a COMPREHENSIVE CASE MANAGEMENT APPROACH, which means that the beneficiaries will have access to more than one service provided by JRS and receive follow up to ensure their needs are being addressed in an holistic way. JRS works with a strong community based approach to ensure sustainability of the services. 1. PSYCHOSOCIAL SUPPORT SERVICES (PSS)Support Groups, formally called Group Therapy, will be the centerpiece of JRS’ counselling services. There will be groups for parents of children with disabilities, youth, widows, women and men with the aim of strengthening family units and equipping attendees with tools to handle stress, conflict, and adversity in healthy and productive ways. Individual counselling will be also offered for the same group of people, to JRS staff and to other NGO’s staff (if needed). Basic Counselling Training will be conducted to JRS staff, incentive workers and other NGO staff. These trainings will provide attendees with a basic understanding of mental health problems, symptoms, and some techniques for managing them. 2. ESTABLISHING AND SUPPORTING OF REFERRAL MECHANISMSWorking closely with other protection, education and health partners, JRS aims to ensure that our beneficiaries receive proper services that can cover their needs in the fields were we are not experts but other NGOs are. Thus, JRS will work on improving the existing referral mechanisms, to coordinate in a better way with other partners. 3. IDPS’ REGISTRATION AND PROFILINGProfiling of IDPs in Yambio will be done through a close collaboration with the government, specifically with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC). The aim of this will be to ascertain the number of IDPs in the camps and know their specific concerns and grievances that will need attention. 4. SUPPORT TO PERSONS OF CONCERN WITH SPECIFIC NEEDSAcknowledging the unique challenges faced by people with special needs, women, and children, JRS psychosocial services will employ a holistic approach inviting men, caregivers, and community leaders to participate in its activities. Incentive staff will conduct Home Visits which are a conduit for several other program activities, including material distribution, referrals, and general psychosocial support. Once a week they will be provided with lentils and soap for their families. 5. MATERIAL SUPPORT TO VULNERABLE PEOPLE (NFI intervention)Solar lamps, torch, security lights, plastic sheets and other materials focused on protection will be distributed through home visits and occasionally support groups, to the most vulnerable households at risk and affected by conflict and displacements (women, widows, PSNs, elderly, children…). This materials will help them to improve the security of their homes during night time, preventing from been robed or attacked. This groups are identified and followed up by the home visitors, to ensure they receive a comprehensive support, accessing other JRS activities, according to their needs. 6. PEACEBUILDING AND PEACEFUL COEXISTENCEJRS will conduct Awareness to community leaders and teachers from various communities on Peaceful co-existence to enhance ways of solving conflicts among the community members. Community leaders will use the knowledge from these trainings to solve minor conflicts in the community. Training on conflict management resolution, will be conducted for the community leaders and teachers to increase their capacity to solve conflicts at community 7. PROTECTION AWARNESS RAISING & PREVENTION MESSAGINGWorkshop focused on youth will be held to promote human rights, prevention of violence and resilience skills development

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Beneficiaries are mobilized through our network of community-based home visitors. Information is communicated orally about the timing of events and the services made available to them.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Mobilizers target specific groups: youth, elderly, women, men, etc. Messaging is therefore adjusted on an individual basis to reach the target audience in the most appropriate way possible.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

JRS has established standards for protection mainstreaming into programs based on inter-agency protection standards. Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and child protection are core concerns of JRS that are reflected in its strategies and programming including in JRS partnership agreements. JRS has developed security protocols at the international and regional levels for staff working in conflict and remote sites. Through the principle of subsidiarity, JRS leadership exercises a participative, accountable and transparent decision-making process.Action: JRS has continued the training of staff on PSEA, Child Safeguarding and Protection Policy. These efforts are led by the Human Resources Department (HR) and is integrated into staff recruitment, orientation and project implementation. Reporting mechanisms regarding these issues and concerns, including assigned focal persons, have been identified at all levels—from the project, country, regional to international level.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The main assessment methods included a 12-day participatory needs assessment exercise with the affected population (refugees, IDPs and Host Community). The main participatory assessment techniques used was free listing technique, Key Informant Interviews and Focus Group Discussions (FDG). Sufficient information regarding the current context and representation across both locations were given due attention in selection of designing the methodology. Different categories of POC were targeted in the assessment: women, men, youth, children, leaders. Persons with special needs for example, persons with disabilities were integrated in the above categories.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

JRS establishes clear inter-departmental mechanisms and structures for receiving and responding to feedback and complaints in its projects. JRS values the engagement of people served in planning and implementing projects and in seeking ways to improve its services. While the HR team leads response to certain types of feedback and complaints that involves JRS staff, the Programs department is responsible for ensuring that the critical engagement of people served is robust in managing and improving the project. Strict confidentiality and privacy for receiving and responding to feedback are maintained for the protection of all people concerned. Through its accompaniment, JRS ensures that refugees are able to express their needs, hopes and concerns and that JRS is able to address them in an appropriate manner based on institutional mechanisms as well as respect for socio-cultural contexts. JRS has established and implemented feedback and complaints mechanisms both for one-time and ongoing services in the project. Weekly meetings with incentive refugee staff is the main fora for refugees to be involved in the program implementation and design of corrections needed. Regular feedback from participants and beneficiaries will be used to adjust and improve trainings and services provided.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

JRS promotes the active participation of the people in the management cycle of the project including monitorung and evaluation. It includes qualified refugees as part of its personnel to provide valuable presence and input to the work and to work closely with refugee communities and organizations in developing and implementing programs. JRS encourages co-responsibility, discernment and participative decision-making. Women has a strong contribution to JRS programs especially in psychosocial related activities. Participation of refugees and host community starts with their involvement in the assessment. Also there are some support groups composed of community members to aid project design and implementation. This project incorporates the service of incentive workers who carry out most of the activities in their respective communities. All of them are trained to ensure that they acquire the skills and competencies needed to make an impact in their communities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project takes into account the access barriers that participants may encounter and seeks to provide the necessary means to ensure that beneficiaries access the services provided by JRS. Activities such as home visits are an example of the project's commitment to reach the most vulnerable people with difficult or reduced mobility. In this way we make sure that our help goes where others can not reach. JRS also has a transportation service to facilitate the beneficiaries in access to the places where some of the activities are carried out.The objective of JRS is to reach those people who have greater needs, ensuring their access to services and accompanying them with a comprehensive approach.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Our databases compile biodata about all the beneficiaries we serve (age, location, gender, vulnerability, etc.) and we are able to periodically conduct an analysis to see gaps in our services. If the data reveal a certain group is being overlooked, or alternately, being saturated, we can adjust who we target for services accordingly.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Project monitoring exposes the project’s potential risks and weaknesses. JRS monitoring tools capture this risks and provides feedback on how best they can be reduced ensuring that the safety of the beneficiaries is increased. Through the weekly home visits and other activities such as counseling services, JRS staff develops weekly reports that are shared with their coordinators. In this reports, the safety of the beneficiaries is one of the aspects that are looked and followed up closely, to put in place any mechanism needed to ensure it.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Through quality assurance activities, we are able to measure the satisfaction and dignity of beneficiaries when they receive services from JRS. For example, in the home visitation program, JRS staff are designated to visit beneficiaries and discuss their experience with home visitors. Questions during this verification process include: Did they receive the services as they were supposed to? Are they well-informed of the service opportunities available to them? How do they feel about the home visitor? If it is found that home visitors are not meeting quality expectations, their performance is reviewed and their contracts are subject to cancellation.
