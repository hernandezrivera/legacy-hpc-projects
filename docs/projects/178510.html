Project Summary

The objective of the project is to provide children and adolescents with the tools and space to improve their mental and psychosocial wellbeing, identify and manage stress, and develop positive coping mechanisms in order for them to better cope with the stress and potential trauma that they are likely facing in their daily lives. Structured and sustained PSS activities targeting children/adolescents will be conducted in DCAs Child Friendly Spaces through art, music, drama, sports, story-telling, games and activities aiming to develop feelings of safety and trust. Adolescents who are unable to attend the CFS will be reached with awareness sessions focusing on stress management in their schools, sports clubs and/or youth centres. Additionally, parents/caregivers of children and adolescents receiving support will be provided with awareness sessions focusing specifically on child protection issues.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, around 94% of households report having no access to Psychosocial Support (PSS). Specifically, amongst IDPs, there appears to be a strong need for PSS (56%), Psychological First Aid (PFA) (43%), and access to information about protection services (30%). In NES, children and adolescents represent over 60% of the total population. With continuous exposure to violence, deepening poverty and persistent lack of access to services there is an increase in the possibility of harmful coping mechanisms. Children/adolescents are especially susceptible as their development can be negatively impacted by prolonged exposure to stress. An additional factor negatively impacting children and adolescents is caregivers’ limited capacity to adjust parenting techniques to handle difficult behavior as a result of stress. A survey conducted on children (6-12 years) attending DCAs Child Friendly Space (CFS) activities in early 2020 showed that over half the children exhibited repeated signs of negative coping skills. Over 60% expressed being worried most of the time, having bad dreams, and feeling angry all the time. Over 65% reported feeling sad all the time. A needs assessment conducted by War Child Holland in January 2021 on psychosocial wellbeing concluded that adolescent also showed several signs of negative coping strategies. According to community leaders’ interviewed, adolescents were more prone to aggressive behavior, including picking fights in school and at home, indulging in excessive smoking, and crying excessively. These findings call for holistic, community-based PSS/Child Protection support targeting children, adolescents as well as their parents/caregivers.

5 - Implementation of activities

Community-based PSS and Child Protection (CP) activities will include: * Structured and sustained PSS and CP activities targeting vulnerable boys and girls (aged 5-12) (following a 4-week curriculum), conducted in DCAs Child Friendly Spaces (CFSs). The activities will promote individual and collective support through art, music, drama, sports, story-telling and games. *Provision of awareness sessions to male and female adolescents in schools (or sports clubs/youth centres) (aged 13-17) focusing on topics such as how to recognize and deal with stress and how to deal with everyday problems caused by living in prolonged conflict. *Provisions of art and music sessions for male and female adolescents (aged 13-17) in the CFS in order provide a safe environment for them to express their feelings in a creative way. *Provision of awareness sessions and parenting programmes to parents and caregivers focusing on topics such as CP, good parenting techniques, and how to deal with children who are displaying negative coping mechanisms. *Provision of specialized CP through case management and referrals, in line with sector standards and guidelines. All activities are carried out after initial participatory sessions conducted with relevant stake holders, such as parents, school administration, and community leaders. Regular M&E will be conducted by the PSS teams. Specifically, in order to assess the impact of PSS activities in the CFS, children will be asked to also participate in pre-post surveys, and adolescents attending awareness sessions in their school or sports club/youth centre will be asked to participate in a post awareness satisfaction survey.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Protection Risk: Protection risk faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community and caregivers about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. Mitigation measures for children against abuse take place in the CFS through storytelling and developing a good rapport with providers. Children who show signs of possible abuse are spoken to by the PSS providers in the CFS in a safe reassuring environment. They are encouraged to share their experiences in indirect ways, such as through drawing and painting (as an alternative to the official complaints mechanism).

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. Should the security and/or access situation deteriorate, the project will prioritize safety of staff and beneficiaries and will shift activities to other locations, as and when possible. Risk: Closure of CFSs due to COVID-19 related lockdowns or curfews, causing delays and decreased access.Mitigation: DCA has developed an online system for delivery of activities to children and caregivers in case of closures. The system is dependent on platforms such as ZOOM and WhatsApp to allow access to beneficiaries. Adjustments are done to the curriculum of sessions to accommodate for the lack of physical presence in the CFS. Risk: lack of access to schools and adolescents due to restrictions by the educational committee.Mitigation: The organization continues to maintain a good relationship with the educational committee, and informs them about possible timing and content of activities. Community leaders and working groups are also approached in case of any disapprovals to mediate.
