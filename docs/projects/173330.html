Project Summary

To address the identified needs of Rohingya refugees related to Shelter, World Concern/Medair will conduct Transitional Shelter Assistance and Emergency Shelter Response in Kutupalong/Balukhali Mega Camps in alignment with the Shelter/NFI sector’s objective. World Concern/Medair will conduct all activities through the IOM pipeline (IOM will provide all materials), with Sector’s strategy and coordination with all stakeholders including beneficiaries, RRRC, CiC, IOM, Sector and other IPs.
Throughout the TSA process, the shelter beneficiary’s family will decide their needs and will be given maximum choice to select materials based on their need. They will be engaged through awareness campaigns, focus group discussions and feedback sessions. Households lead construction, with training on connections/ties, bracing, tie-down, safe cooking space, ventilation, privacy etc. with an emphasis on ensuring safety and DRR features adapted to the context. Engineers will technically counsel and ensure that minimum construction standards are met. Beside this, technical assessment and distribution will take place in emergency response. Intersectoral analysis will take place to identify the most vulnerable, considering age, gender, diversity and specific needs of individuals, and World Concern/Medair will provide full support with porter, carpenter and volunteer. Team are regularly trained on Gender and Diversity and PSEA. Teams will refer any gender, protection issues following referral pathway and will involve women, older people, PwSN and other forms of gender diverse population in Shelter activities identified in SADDD. WC/MEDAIR will ensure priority lines and shaded space are provided in the distribution points for women, older people and PwSN. WC/MEDAIR will involve women, older people, PwSN in small scale, lightweight works during TSA2 implementation. Works will include but will not be limited to cleaning, bamboo lattice making, count materials during unloading/distribution, token writing, thermal scanning, rope distribution etc.
Real-time feedback will be collected by CFRM and in a Complaint Box at each distribution point. Needs-based community meetings will take place to help with decision making and to resolve issues. PDM will allow beneficiaries to report their level of satisfaction with the distribution and distribution items.
To ensure both Rohingya refugee and host actors are shielded from the risk of COVID-19, the latest COVID-19 guidelines will be followed during the 6-month project.

1 - Identified Needs

The J-MSNA assessment (Oct 8, 2020) shows that shelter is still the main priority, as indicated by 63% of Rohingya, even more than Food (60 %). This need is easily understood given the congested and poor shelter conditions that the Rohingya experience: 51% have roof problems, 17% have wall problems, and an additional 10% have damaged materials. Apart from the materials issue, 9% were not able to improve their shelter because they were not able to pay for labour.World Concern worked previously in Camps 8E and Camp 12 in 2019 and 2020. It would be an ideal location for TSA 2, given the significant needs, and also since community relationships are already established and there is good awareness of vulnerable individuals. TSA assistance was provided one year ago, thus, existing shelter materials provided to the displaced population and improvements in camp infrastructure remain insufficient for protection against adverse weather events. The recent monsoon season weakened the shelters. Untreated bamboo has deteriorated due to beetle infestation. In both camp 12 and 8E Female are 51% and Female key informants emphasised the discomfort in existing shelters due to heat and humidity that results from poor ventilation and materials used. Families’ main concerns regarding their current shelters were related to durability of shelters, volume and quality of materials, heat and lack of ventilation & privacy. Furthermore, vulnerable individuals' have added concerns regarding porter and construction support.
