Project Summary

The project aims to cover the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men people living in the close to the conflict line settlements in drinking water (target: 18 000), wastewater disposal systems (target: 2500); solid waste disposal services in accordance with sanitary norms and rules (target: 2500). The aim will be achieved using different intervention strategies, addressing the results of the assessments in each location (drilling new wells, repairing existing water sources, upgrading of existing equipment). The main objectives:
● To improve access to drinking water using assessment based interventions (drilling of new wells for water, repair, modernization of existing or currently closed wells).
● To reduce the risk of spread of infectious diseases through the installation of the wastewater disposal systems.
● To reduce the epidemic risks through the organization of solid waste disposal processes.
● To implement behavior interventions aimed on changing attitude of local population to the usage of water and waste management.

Needs

According to estimates from 435 000 to 600 000 people are still living in so called buffer zone of the Ukrainian conflict. According to Caritas data 59% of its inhabitants are elderly people and only 24% – able-bodied, but because of the conflict, limitation of migration roots and loss of agricultural market in the region they also have no possibility for receiving any income. WASH cluster reports that there are serious problems with water supply for settlements both in GCA and NGCA close to the line of conflict. In the Donbas area water is scarce due to insufficient volume and poor quality of groundwater. The situation is worsened by old water systems which have been subject to damage by shelling, but which are also close to collapse due to lack of maintenance. WASH cluster reports 105 WASH incidents in 12 months. According to HNO2019● 3.2 million people, of 3.6 million whose water supplies are affected by the conflict, need direct water supply and sanitation assistance;● An estimated 100,000 IDPs living in all areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts need WASH support: elderly IDPs will be prioritized taking into consideration their limited mobility;● 850,000 older people need to be prioritized for water and sanitation support;● 450,000 children under 18 years, in the affected area, need assistance including those attending 1800 schools and kindergartens in the conflict-affected areas which need repairs to water supply and toiletsBecause the main water supply facilities are in NGCA the whole area in proximity of contact line is under risk of water supply interruption. Damaged infrastructure cannot be repaired due to ongoing fights. Because of the decreased activity in coal and iron mines the ground water level rises. This is since during mine operation the water draining system works to keep the mines dry and accessible. The rising water level washes out health endangering minerals into the wells that are used by population. Centralized water supply is partially present in some villages, but the situation is very complicated, as most of the pipes have been defeated because of damage from mines and shells. In other villages, the water supply is only from local wells, which, due to the small volume, are quickly devastated and people are forced to either look for another source or wait until the current wells are filled. The quality of such water is often poor but even access to such water is limited. The second and almost the only source of drinking water in villages is the option of buying imported refined water. In 2018 several important water system repairs have been put on hold due to regular ceasefire violations. In the absence of adequate repairs, the 2018 Needs Overview states that the authorities and the humanitarian community are forced to fill the gap in water provision caused by shell strikes and fragile water networks through increased water trucking, which is unsustainable.General information on domestic wastewater (limited project to cover the needs currently):● Only 3% of small settlements (up to 2000 inhabitants) have central drainage systems.● 8.8% of rural residents use centralized wastewater systems, from them in 22% of the wastewater does not pass cleaning.● About 215,000 people in the buffer zone do not have access to wastewater cleaning systems.● With the average consumption of 60 litres/day per person the volume of non-cleaned wastewater in the buffer zone is 12,900 m3/day, or about 5 million m3 per year.Source: National University of Water Management and Environmental ManagementCurrently Caritas provides drinking water to at least 900 beneficiaries in 3 target locations in the buffer zone. Depending on the facilities in place either water is delivered, or wells are repaired to reestablish the water supply. Additionally, Caritas is conducting water analyses to inform target areas about the quality of potable and service water.

Activities & outcomes

Overall objective: The immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision for conflict affected people is ensured .Specific objectives:SO1: To improve access to drinking water using assessment based interventions (drilling of new wells for water, repair, modernization of existing or suspended wells).SO2: To reduce the risk of spread of infectious diseases through the installation of the sewage disposal systems .SO3: To reduce the epidemic risks through the organization of solid waste disposal processes.SO4: To implement behavior interventions aimed on changing attitude of local population to the usage of water and waste management. Activities SO1:● Identification and selection of settlements suffering from water shortage.● Selection of intervention strategies based the assessment results for every single location (drilling of new wells, repairs/improvement of existing water sources, modernization of existing equipment).● Preparation of project documentation and terms of reference for external collaborator.● Conducting of tender procedures for the selection of external collaborator.● Performance of technical tasks by selected external collaborator.● Monitoring and expert examination.● Transfer of the ownership of new/renowated water supply systems to the local communities. Activities SO2: ● Identification and selection of settlements influenced by lack/damage of sewage infrastructure.● Selection of intervention strategies based the assessment results for every single location (construction of individual waste treatment facilities for objects of social sphere, construction of individual waste treatment facilities for certain small settlements or other strategies).● Preparation of project documentation and terms of reference for external collaborator.● Conducting of tender procedures for the selection of external collaborator.● Performance of technical tasks by selected external collaborator.● Monitoring and expert examination.● Transfer of the ownership of new/renowated water supply systems to the local communities. Activities SO3: ● Identification and selection of settlements suffering from inappropriate waste disposal services.● Selection of intervention strategies based the assessment results for every single location (organization of the removal of garbage in the amounts that meet the needs of the residents).● Conducting of tender procedures for the selection of external collaborator.● Performance of technical tasks by selected external collaborator.● Monitoring and expert examination. Activities SO4:● motivating people to support water supply, waste water and household waste disposal systems in accordance with the relevant sanitary norms and regulations.● Provide critical supplies and information related to WASH to prevent water-related and sewage-related diseases. The assistance provided will cover the needs of all genders and ages as in case of implementation the general access to water and sanitation in the settlements will be assured. The accessibility of sources of water will be made taking into account the primary factor that up to 60% of residents are elderly people who may have limited mobility The educational/informational component will take into account special needs of women/girls who are more responsible for water usage (cooking, hygiene, taking care of children). As waste management in traditional approach are more related with men roles in the society it will also taken into account.
