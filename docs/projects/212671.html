Project Summary

The education crisis in Syria tops the agenda of concerns, especially in the coastal areas of Syria, at a time when crises are diverse, problems are multiplying, and directly affecting the outcomes of the educational process on one hand, and the infrastructure on the other.
Many experiences have passed through the education system, but success has not been written for them, due to many considerations, foremost among them the diseases that have spread recently, especially COVID-19, which led educational institutions to close their doors and enter the world of online and virtual education. Here, the fundamental problem appeared, where the educational environment was not equipped with the necessary tools and technological means, creating a gap in the cognitive stock of students at various educational levels, not to mention cases of educational dropout that affected many who are the focus of the educational process.
Crises and disasters continue, and the earthquake strikes the Syrian coast, affecting 140 government schools in Tartous and 470 government schools in Latakia in various educational areas, in addition to 248 government schools going out of service, and many of them being converted into shelters to provide a safe environment for the affected.
A team from AL- Batoul Humanitarian Services Association intervenes urgently in various government schools, providing 213 individual and group consultations that reached a group of teachers and students, in addition to compensatory lessons for 120 students, as well as providing a series of special training in first aid psychological support for 42 teachers and schools, to carry out their tasks in emergency situations whether inside or outside the classroom.
Today, there are 11,096 students from various educational stages in the Jableh area and its surroundings, including 1,013 students who are educational dropouts, and 4,156 children at risk of dropping out of school.
Regular coordination is carried out with the Directorate of Education in Latakia Governorate regarding anticipated developments and interventions. A memorandum of understanding has been signed with the Directorate regarding the expected interventions by us in the targeted areas through the existing schools.
Efforts must be intensified by the relevant authorities to implement reinforcement lessons to compensate for the educational loss of these students, restore their cognitive stock, in preparation for the second semester exams of the academic year 2023/2024, and to prepare an appropriate educational environment for the upcoming academic year 2024/2025.
Monitoring and evaluation of the teacher:
Reviewing the lesson plan before implementation, considering the three educational objectives: cognitive, skill-based, and emotional.
Conducting field visits to ensure the implementation of the lesson plan.
Checking exam questions and periodic tests, both essay and objective, ensuring their coverage of the subject matter.
Conducting a self-assessment of the teacher based on feedback, considering strengths and addressing areas of professional shortcomings, and conducting a series of necessary training exercises based on the educational environment data.
Monitoring and evaluation of the student:
Conducting pre-and post-tests for student information and knowledge in the subject.
Monitoring the student's progress in their academic level through formative assessment methods (stage-wise).
Student self-assessment through immediate feedback.
Working according to different age stages: Implementing a series of tests to determine the student's academic level, taking into account students with special needs. First category: Grades 1-6 Second category: Grades 7-9 Third category: Grades 10-12

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Since 2020 Al-batoul started focusing on PSEA, First there has been a lot of work to train all staff members on PSEA and raising awareness about the expected behaviors and the disciplinary procedures for those who commit it. Trainings have been conducted to WCWs to make sure that the community itself has a main role in fighting this problem and that key persons have the right knowledge and information to deal with this problem since people of the same society may trust each other more than strangers especially in sensitive subjects. Later, there have been a policy and code of conduct for PSEA read and signed by all staff members. The policy includes whole procedures of complaining and what shall be done after a complaint is submitted. Three complaint mechanisms have been established in each of Al-batoul 4 community centers; complaint box, hotline and email address. Also, the policy contains the investigation process and highly stresses the confidentiality and safety of both the survivors and whistleblowers. Awareness sessions targeted different age categories of displaced and host communities. Brochures were distributed, street commercials have been displaced in crowded and common areas in Tartous City, text messages that assured the existence of complaint mechanisms in Al-Batoul centers and encouraged the beneficiaries to use them without hesitation were sent to almost 2500 persons in the areas covered by Al-Batoul centers.
