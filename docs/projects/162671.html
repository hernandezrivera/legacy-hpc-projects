Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in improving access to PHC services for the Vulnerable population in Umm Dukhun Locality of Central Darfur State that will contribute to the three HRP Health sector objectives for 2020.
In this project HOPE will (1) Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referrals for 15,000 individuals disaggregated to 41% (6150) male, 59% (8850) female, 8% (1200) elderly, 63% (9450) children and 29 (4350) adults through conduction of outpatient consultations, trauma consultations, mental health consultations, antenatal care sessions, postnatal sessions and referral for specialized treatment that are not available in PHC facilities. (2) Increase access to health services by supporting referral pathways by supporting mobile medical units. (3) Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services through (a) conduction trainings for health staff on different health topics and, (b) conduction trainings for community health workers/ promoters on different health topics. (4) Increase access to health services by rehabilitating and refurbishing health facilities with the provision of medical equipment the rehabilitation of health facility with the emergency operation and support.
HOPE will focus on Umm Dukhun of Central Darfur based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, Nutrition, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio. The BIN/ targets in Umm Dukhun locality are 76,852 in the needs of health services including 3,074 Pregnant, 13,065 U5, and 1,537 Elderly people (PIN & Severity 2020 HPC).
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through fully community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee as well as do follow up for all benefits targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

According to sector targets and PIN & Severity, 2020 HPC confirmed that 5 million number of people in need of life-saving health intervention overall Sudan, in the target locality of Umm Dukhun there are 76,852 in needs to health services including 3,074 Pregnant, 13,065 U5, and 1,537 Elderly people with considering total of 62,901 are IDPs. About 24% of the PHC facilities in five Darfur states are not fully functional either due to staff shortages or poor physical infrastructure. (FMoH/WHO HeRAMS July 2018).HOPE will focus on Umm Dukhun based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, Nutrition, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio by current service providers. Many health facilities have been destroyed, effectively leaving these places without government health services. Inadequate provision of and access to PHC services especially for women/ girls and children across Umm Dukhun remains the main constraint as these same areas have high mortality rates for infants and mothers, the reasons for this deterioration of health services are lack of equitable access to life-saving services, non-accessibility during rainy seasons, poor transportation infrastructure and lack of funding from the central government. The main communicable diseases and outbreaks in the target locality are Malaria, AWD, and Diphtheria as well as high risk to expand the last outbreaks of Chikungunya, and Dengue Fever that are happening in border state of South and West Darfur, (Cumulative Chikungunya cases reported are 159 in WD and 23 in SD, Cumulative Dengue Fever cases reported are 92 in WD and 8 in SD, source FMoH report 27 Nov 2019).The last inter-communal and inter-tribal conflicts and fighting that is happened last years ago which are affected the safety and security of people exacerbating the risk of sexual violence especially when displaced. There has been an overall increase in GBV in affected areas. There is an increase in other forms of GBV such as domestic violence and early marriage of young girls. Women and girls continue to face the risk of gender-based violence including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. These children will suffer from more long term developmental and health impact resulting in both a shorter stature than non-affected peers as well as a significant impact on the development of the brain resulting in long term impact on IQ, learning potential, educational outcomes as well as poorer health in the immediate and longer-term future. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings, and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country.HOPE, through MoU that is signed with SMoH in Central Darfur, has access to health facilities that are not functional in Umm Dukhun as well as HOPE will fully coordinate with IMC, CRS, Sawa Sudan and UNICEF of Health and Nutrition partners in Umm Dukhun.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design, HOPE assessed the safety and security for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to health and nutrition. All essential drugs at the pharmacy will be handled with the utmost care and storage - warehouses will be clean to avoid contamination. This project will include psycho-social stimulation activities for infants and young children will also be provided with psychosocial support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to health and nutrition outreach and awareness- rising. All health staff will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and government in already supporting the health sector at nearby facility and other secondary facilities. Adequate shelter and waiting areas of all the health centers will be provided with all basic furniture and clean water to be accessed by all seeking services. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy during medical history taking and examination, keeping medical data without unnecessary disclosure. Awareness raising during community mobilization on health and nutrition, education a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
