Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the EIEWG education framework objectives 1 and 2, and strategic objective 1 and 2 within the HRP, with the MOE protection and recovery plan 2020, the cluster objectives related to “increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for the most vulnerable children”, and the protection objective related to provision of school based PSS to school staff and students affected by the education related to violation and COVID19 and legal aid in West Bank. The project aims to provide protection of vulnerable children affected by conflict and other situations of violence, particularly those at risk of denied access to educational services and individual and mass forcible transfer in the West Bank. NRC will work with the MoE and partners to identify new methodologies to reach the most vulnerable children and promote more inclusive education practices, in-person and also while implementing blended and distance education programs. NRC will promote the identification and coordinate inclusive education activities with community-based organizations in Area C and East Jerusalem. NRC will promote parental engagement in the educational program, but also will include a legal and counseling component in the work at the school level and with teachers and non-teaching staff.
The project will target 70 most vulnerable Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE) schools supporting children access to a protective and enabling educational experience and will be four-fold: 1) provision of psycho-educational support through training of teachers on how to manage a classroom with children who have gone through stressful and traumatic events, how to better support under-achievers and training of counsellors to support students with chronic nightmares that severely affect their school functioning thought the NRC flagship ‘Better Learning Program’ (BLP); 2) provision of emergency school kits to support crisis-disaster risk reduction in tandem with capacity building; 3) delivery of legal information sessions through awareness raising of students, school staff and parents about legal rights and protection as well as response to incidents/violations through documentation, including response and referral mechanism to document interferences/impediments to the delivery of education in schools, and strengthen the legal response of the ‘Alert Mechanism’; 4) advocacy pursuing enhanced protection from violations of the right to education. 5) Remadial education to contribute to mitigation to the learning loss and children drop out of school due to COVID 19 restrictions. 6) support in the preparedness of any education disturbance through supporting distance learning modalities and virtual classes including by providing tablets to specific group the most vulnerable children; printing self-learning materials; and rehabilitate the schools’ ICT infrastructure 7) Supporting children with disabilities with the adoption of learning material, developing school staff capacity, and providing provision of devices. Capacity building will be tailored per school, and include the Better Learning Programme, following the International Network of Education in Emergencies (INEE) criteria and standards.

1 - Needs

The coercive environment in the West Bank, presenting itself as the systematic denial of rights, structural barriers to development, conflict and lack of accountability for violations of international law, has led to an ongoing threat of forcible transfer and created a cycle of chronic humanitarian need and aid dependence, which presents a major obstacle to the development effort, even during the Covid 19 crises. In vulnerable communities in the West Bank, the rate of demolitions of Palestinian-owned structures and settler violence remain high, and many Palestinians, particularly in Area C, East Jerusalem, and Hebron city (H2), continue to face the risk of forcible transfer. These areas have has witnessed multiple cycles of violence and increased on Education related violation include attacks on schools, military use of schools, threats of attacks against school staff and students and interferences with access to education. Children continue to face violence and other challenges in accessing schools and the realization of their right to education remains severely compromised due to violence and armed search operations in schools; harassment, intimidation, and violence towards students and teachers on the way to and from school; poor education infrastructure; demolition and stop-work orders on school buildings. Additionally, children suffer from loss of school time due to firing tear gas into the vicinity or inside schools, delays at checkpoints and continuous Israeli military presence and operations around schools. In area C in particular, children have to walk long distances to reach the nearest school, often having to cross one or two checkpoints. Teachers and school personnel also report frequent harassment, searches and humiliation at checkpoints. In East Jerusalem, a discriminatory, restrictive planning and zoning regime has allocated 2.6 per cent of the land for the development of public buildings for Palestinians, resulting in more than 2,557 classroom shortage for Palestinian students within the public-school system, while in terms of infrastructure, many schools are in rented premises. The rented schools/classrooms are normally private residential properties, not adequate to meet education requirements. NRC’s research (2020) showed that between January 2018 and June 2020, 296 attacks against education by Israeli forces or settlers and settlement took place in the West Bank. Inadequate road infrastructure or absence of school transportation also limit access to schools. This environment negatively impacts the learning opportunities, decreasing the learning outcomes and increasing the dropout rates. The gendered impact of COVID-19 has seen women burdened with additional home and caring responsibilities and a possible increase in domestic violence. Learning losses also threaten to extend beyond this generation and eradicates decades of progress. In April 2020, NRC conducted a multi-sector rapid assessment through phone survey with 760 individuals split equally across Gaza and the West Bank. The rapid assessment aimed at gaining a better understanding of the context due to the impact of COVID-19 restrictions on vulnerable households. NRC conducted the rapid assessment with a representative sample of its own beneficiaries across Gaza & WB at household level. The analysis of the data showed that 72% of interviewed households have school-aged children at home of which 55% have at least one child not following any kind of education system. There is an expected increase in inequality among children, with the most vulnerable at increased risk of dropout or reduced academic achievement. In the West Bank, in the 2020-2021 school year, up to 637,762 students attend 1,881 Ministry of Education schools and 192 UNRWA schools. During the 2019 – 2020 school year and the outbreak of C-19, at least 4 months of in-class education was disrupted, and the 2020-2021 school year started with many challenges for thousands of children in Palestine. In March 2021, NRC, in partnership with the University of Auckland and with the support of ECHO, finalised research to explore the impact of Covid 19 on children’s wellbeing, and therefore on education attendance and learning outputs, as part of the ‘Learning in the Margins’ research. The study indicated that in the West Bank, the most vulnerable schools presented more risk factors than the less vulnerable schools. For students attending schools in communities and locales that were already more educationally vulnerable prior to COVID, there is a real risk that educational inequalities will worsen in COVID’s aftermath, particularly as learners in the more vulnerable communities miss out on learning, or experience sub-optimal e-learning experiences, compared to other students. (Ritesh, Zhao and Choo, 2021). The situation is further aggravated for children with disabilities; according to the PCBS, 20 percent of the overall people with disability in Palestine are children of which 46 percent between the ages 6-17 years old are not enrolled in schools. About one-quarter of children aged 16-17 throughout the oPt, and five percent of children between 10 and 15, are out of school (either dropped out or never enrolled) . Dropout rates are higher for boys, who are expected to contribute to household income in poor families. Early marriage, commonly used by families as a coping mechanism to reduce expenses, is the most significant cause of early dropout among girls, followed by protection concerns related to friction with Israeli forces and settlers. According to the HHs interviewed, the main reasons reported behind school children dropping out are: lack of interest in continuing their education, COVID19 related school closure and poverty.

2 - Activities

NRC has in place a MoU with the MoEHE for collaboration and engagement across the West Bank. The activities are clustered in the following main areas; 1) Continue providing Psychosocial support at all levels, by teachers and counsellors at school level: a. Vulnerability/need assessment at school level and selection/confirmation of targeted schools. b. Grant opening meeting with schools, directorates and MoE key staff. c. Define and implement the training package for each school and directorate on BLP 1, BLP 2 and BLP 3. The training package is based on a minimum package but tailored per each school. d. Define and implement the training package for supervisors and key staff in the Ministry of Education, to ensure the continuation of the programme. e. Identify basic supplies, in partnership with the MoE and Directorates, to ensure the schools have basic equipment and supplies to implement the PSS activities. f. Support supervisors, teaching and non-teaching staff to implement BLP 1, BLP 2 and BLP 3 at school level. 2) Promoting the psychosocial wellbeing of students and teaching and non-teaching staff will contribute to promoting a better-quality basic education, which will be enhanced through provision of safe and protective learning environments, in terms of both inclusive physical infrastructure and child protection mechanisms through NRCs school improvement Plan (SIP) approach. Community members will be informed about their roles and responsibilities in protecting children. MoE staff, Parents, teachers, community leaders, religious leaders and women’s committees and associations will be sensitised and trained on a range of subjects including MHPSS through BLP, child protection and services already in place. Community groups and children’s clubs will be established/strengthened and receive training on child protection, child rights, hygiene, PSS and child safeguarding, in order to ensure that children and communities will be empowered to identify, mitigate and respond to risk factors and violations wherever they may manifest. 3) The project will provide support to school teaching and non-teaching staff to have a more inclusive and child-centered education through : a. Identify the specific needs at school level (pedagogical gaps and needs) b. Support training at school and directorate level on distance and inclusive education, following basic INEE and MoE recommendation c. Develop a community engagement component, in order to improve the quality of the education d. Support the adoption of blended plans at school level, ensuring the continuity between in-person and distance education e. Identify the most excluded and at risk children, and ensure they have access to distance education, with tablets and self-learning materials. f. providing the school with emergency educational kits, PSS kitsetc. 4) Ensure access for children and youth (aged 6-17) to quality basic–level education (formal and non- formal) in a safe, inclusive and protective environment focusing on out of school children (OOSC) and those at risk of dropout school readiness and remedial components to ensure learners both go-to school and stay – in school will prioritized. a. provide direct technical support, b. Identify the specific needs at school level (pedagogical gaps and needs), c. Support training at school and directorate level on distance and inclusive education, following basic INEE and MoE recommendation, e. Develop a community engagement/mobilization for OOSC/Y component, in order to improve the quality of the education, f. Support the adoption of blended plans at school level, ensuring the continuity between in-person and distance education, h) Improved capacity of education actors to provide protective quality education, Provision of safe, temporary child friendly learning space to vulnerable children. 5)Youth at risk of dropping out will be provided with services aimed at enhancing the empowerment and skills of vulnerable youth, developing their capacities and resilience and enabling them to play their full and effective role in society by prioritising skills application through exploring opportunities for livelihood initiatives and by adopting a complete youth skills development approach targeting youth in secondary schools. a) a comprehensive situation analysis of adolescents and youths learning needs including those related to the social and educational empowerment of youth b) Pilot a youth leadership projects in target schools c) Early identification of drop out or at high risk, to support their reintegration into the formal education system or transition to vocational education d) A comprehensive situation analysis of out-of-school youths’ skills needs analysis and identify mitigation measures. 6) Through out the project implementation, advocacy efforts using field and desk research will take place to produce materials, briefings and submissions to the international community in oPT, including EU and UN bodies and diplomatic missions, as well as EU and UN bodies based in Brussels, Geneva, and New York, and the U.S. Congress. Development of focused evidence-based content, with rights based legal analysis, on education-related violations and interferences impacting Palestinian children’s right to education. Capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff on a tailored EIE file that may include PSS, DRR (in selected schools), INEE Conflict Sensitive Education and Teachers in Conflict and Crisis context. All NRC staff and local partners staff receive an information on code of conduct, PSEA and CFM. NRC will also include binding conditions in all procurement documents and procedures with suppliers to ensure their commitment. In addition, NRC protection team will develop and disseminate PSEA awareness raising material.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support. The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, all beneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general classroombased intervention approach targeted at all children in target schools, its suitable for all children in any context, as its designed to reach larger numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee the equal chances of access for all children both males and females and their specific needs. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to children who need additional support, to support resilience a mong them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilised. iii) The third dimension is a specialised, clinical one designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptoms of traumatic stress. The equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls, special need or without special need children are affordable based on the real needs, beneficiaries interest and potential. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach.
