Project Summary

AMAN (Association for Mass Advancement Network) has developed the Livelihoods and Skills Development for JRP 2023 with objectives to Provide Livelihoods and Skills Development trainings for refugees based on formally recognized curriculum/ certification programs with sewing machine operation; Tailoring / Sewing/ Cutting/Finishing and handicraft training to reduce physical and protection-related risks and opportunities to become self-sufficient. Livelihoods and Skills Development trainings; handicraft trainings activities are inclusive and responsive to community feedback, promote peaceful coexistence between refugees, enhance resilience and receiving capacity building support through skills development and handicraft training.
AMAN has concentrated at camp 10, 14, 15 and 16 for Livelihoods and Skills Development training where the trainees will receive the training on tailoring, sewing, cutting and finishing. Besides, AMAN will operate handicraft training at camp 16 for EVHHs of Rohingya Refugee. AMAN will implement this project for 2023 in the camp 10 (block F), Camp 14 (block D), Camp 15 (block E), Camp 16 (block A) and total 4 Community Promoter, 13 Trainers will be assigned with the project as its cross-cutting activities along with Health, Shelter/NFI, FSS, GBV, Education and others project activities will be covered during 2023. The proposed project will be implementing by following LSD Sector policy and guidance where Multi-Year Operational Plan till 31 December 2025 has been designed.
Major activities will be sewing machine and handicraft training in line with formally recognized curriculum/ certification programs (Vocational Training). Refugees involving women, older people, people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population will participate in Vocational Training in line with formally recognized curriculum/ certification programs practices and climate sensitive self-reliance interventions. AMAN’s estimated budget is $207,000 for total beneficiary targeted 720 individual (adolescent/women) from age 18-59 years giving priority on PWD inclusion.
AMAN has skilled manpower to run the proposed project. AMAN has necessary policy and guidelines those will be followed for implementation of the project. AMAN always emphasizes on capacity building training and its concerned staff has already attended in different training by the initiatives of concerned sectors and stakeholders. Moreover, AMAN is an active member of PSEA network and its staff will be trained further to ensure proper protection mainstreaming.
All Livelihood and skill development training inclusion Handicraft will support for extremely vulnerable households (EVHHs) ensuring that, training are accessible and appropriate for persons with disabilities and older persons. Before intervention any projects, AMAN always conducts baseline survey, FGD, KII, etc. Collect, analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with women, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Livelihood opportunities available to the Rohingya and host populations differ significantly, as the Government restricts work opportunities for refugees, officially preventing them from accessing formal employment. While the host population almost entirely depends on employment or other income-generating activities, the Rohingya community primarily depends on humanitarian assistance as their primary income source. Of the Rohingya earning income 75–80% were primarily engaged in casual work, the least secure type of income source. By contrast, almost all host community households had an adult engaged in income generating activities. Of this number, just over half (53–58%) relied on casual labour as their primary income source (REVA 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, and 01/2022). [ACAPS - NPM ANALYSIS HUB, 30 August 2022]The limited livelihood opportunities encouraged refugees to adopt more negative coping mechanisms to fulfil their needs. While the REVA in 2019 found that 80% of Rohingya households had some form of income, most were in casual labour with very low income. Another study found that 45% of households reported no income and depended entirely on aid and coping mechanisms to meet their basic needs (The Asia Foundation/CPJ 09/09/2020). During the pandemic years of 2020–2021, the number of Rohingya households with a working adult sharply fell, while the average income of a casual laborer was significantly less than half of those in the host community (REVA 2019, 2020, and 01/2022). One illustration of this is the increased proportion of people unable to afford the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB). The pandemic also appeared to reduce remittance flows, which an estimated 20% of households reported receiving pre-pandemic (The Asia Foundation/CPJ 09/09/2020. Remittances play an important role in economic support among the Rohingya. In 2017, those receiving remittances were found to be less poor and had a 16% lower rate of unacceptable FCS. From 2017–2020, the percentage of the Rohingya receiving remittances decreased from 4% to 1% before increasing to 6% in 2021. Likewise, the percentage of the host population receiving remittances decreased from 7% to 1% until 2020 before increasing to 5% in 2021 (REVA 2017, 2019, and 01/2022). [ACAPS - NPM ANALYSIS HUB, 30 August 2022]The normative framework that governs the lives of Rohingya refugees unequally affects women and girls by limiting their mobility, their ability to make decisions about their lives and access lifesaving services, and deprioritizes their needs and demands compared to those of men. Women are excluded from decision-making systems in the community, and their lack of access to reliable information, consultations and feedback mechanisms limits their ability to influence both prevention and response plans. AMAN has already established Skills development training centers at camp 10, 14, 15, & 16 where it has been providing skills development training on Tailoring / Sewing/ Cutting/Finishing and handicraft training under JRP 2022, and it will be continued in 2023 aiming multi-year planning till 2025, when cross-cutting activities along with Health, Shelter/NFI, FSS, GBV and others stakeholders will be addressed and strengthened. The inclusion of women leaders, networks and volunteers in the planning, implementation and monitoring of response plans will have a strong impact on making these right-based strategies, inclusive and effective. Through the proposed project, AMAN will provide culturally and gender appropriate Vocational training on Livelihood and Skill development as well as Handicraft training, including for those people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population.As per gender age group adolescent and women along with elderly will be provided training on GBV issues and above all social cohesion thus they will be aware of their rights and their roles in the Communities in regards of achieving the training.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

AMAN will ensure that the planned activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation in light of Safeguarding and PSEA policy for effective implementation of the project. There will be SOPs or guidance on the establishment and operation of feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection, GBV, and PSEA issues. In addition, collaboration with other stakeholders, such as peer agencies, sectors, partners, local authorities, and involvement of camps leaders’ and SMC groups to fulfil this commitment will also strengthen the program.

3.2 - Gender

AMAN collect, analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with women, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring. AMAN will apply clearly defined risk management processes and ensure that project design and implementation are aligned with, and clearly mainstream, age, gender and diversity, and to ensure that all refugee groups are taken into account and that protection concerns of persons with specific needs are duly addressed with PSEA Policy. AMAN will follow the policy of gender-based violence towards children, women with disabilities and older women in the camps during the implementation of project. The activities will be performed through involvement of site management team, proper communication with CiC, sector focal and other stake holder who are related to implement the project. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection, GBV, and PSEA issues.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

AMAN will implement the project, with or come into contact with children by following its safeguarding policies and procedures to ensure that every child, regardless of their age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex, or sexual orientation, has a right to equal protection from harm. Setting up and following safeguarding and PSEA policies and procedures by smooth cooperation with site management, CiC, sector focal and other stake holder for children are safe from adults and other children who might pose a risk. This includes voluntary and community organizations, faith groups, private sector providers, as well as schools and hospitals. AMAN Safeguarding and PSEA policy is the action that is taken to promote the welfare of children and protect them from harm.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity program will have followed by AMAN during project implementation period which involves three major steps: (1) Building a corporate culture that values diversity. (2) Changing structures, policies, and systems to support diversity. (3) Providing diversity awareness training to fulfill the project needs. AMAN will follow transparent, fair and equitable hiring practices, establish pay equity, provide support that helps each team member succeed and give all team members a voice. AMAN will follow true diversity—be it of gender, age, race, experience, sexual orientation, thought, nationality or culture. AMAN will focus cultural diversity, racial diversity, gender diversity, physical Disabilities and diversity in interest during implementation of project. The activities will be performed through involvement of site management team, proper communication with CiC, sector focal and other stakeholder who are related to implement the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by AMAN to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people they seek to assist. AMAN will consider following suggestions while designing humanitarian projects for JRP 2023 and beyond: Information, Communication, and Transparency; Participation, Engagement, and Inclusion; Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism; Learning and Adaptation. Information should be routinely provided to the community by both the organization and its partners on the organization’s vision, mission, mandate, accountability commitments, code of conduct, feedback, complaints procedure, and relevant contact details. Community participation should take place during all phases- needs assessment and program planning, program implementation, distribution and service delivery, monitoring and evaluation. The Do No Harm framework or equivalent approach will be employed with the participation.
