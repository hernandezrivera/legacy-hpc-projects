Project Summary

The Housing, Land and Property (HLP) challenges confronting displaced communities in Somalia are protracted and multifaceted limiting access to and impacting the enjoyment of HLP rights. The centrality of Housing, Land and Property (HLP) is a key component of achieving sustainable durable solutions to displacement. Despite increased prevention efforts, over 100,000 individuals have been forcefully evicted across Somalia in 2021 alone and 1,054,306 persons in a span of five years (HLP Sub cluster 2021). The HLP challenges are created by repeated and protracted displacement in urban areas that are exacerbated by the high demand (in the context of rapid urbanisation, investment and development) and rocketing land prices. This has been compounded by competing claims on land coupled with limited legal and policy frameworks around HLP rights, inadequate technical and functional land registries or departments. And yet, evictions, forced or otherwise result in widespread protection risks, impact victims’ dignity, livelihoods, physical safety and reverses incremental gains made in attaining sustainable durable solutions.
Further to this, the shelter situation in Somalia is critical, coupled with limited public land available to establish new settlements. 85% of informal settlements are constructed on private land (CCCM Cluster, 2020). Moreover, tenure insecurity remains widespread, leading to an alarming rate of forced evictions countrywide. Most informal settlements are either without formal documents or written land tenure agreements or have in place some form of short-term arrangements (Shelter Cluster, 2020). In addition to this, IDPs who lack legal identity documents, specifically nationality documents and civil registration such as birth certificates are disproportionately affected. There is increasing evidence that legal identity is linked to HLP rights. Land owners are increasingly demanding for proof of identity as a prerequisite for accessing HLP. The project seeks to facilitate enhanced protection of displaced communities from Housing Land and Property violations including but not limited to forced evictions.
Through holistic and integrated assistance packages, this Project will benefit 500,000 individuals (women, girls, boys and men, including persons with disabilities) in 21 districts across 15 regions around the following seven complementing actions.
1. Strengthening access to documentation for displaced persons including women, girls and persons with disability through a comprehensive analysis and response to documentation needs.
2. Strengthening of ongoing eviction monitoring systems and expanding eviction risk mapping to collect, review and analyze data.
3. Supporting proactive engagements to negotiate improved tenure arrangements and/or facilitating dignified relocations for populations of concern.
4. Mitigating protection risks exacerbated by HLP violations such as inadequate access to housing, and forced evictions.
5. Supporting extremely vulnerable victims of evictions cope with post-eviction stress (PES) through the provision of multi-purpose cash assistance, specialized counselling and legal assistance services.
6. Strengthening Eviction taskforce members, and supporting government’s effort to address evictions, including robust engagement with landlords, gatekeepers and community leaders.
7. Facilitating access to HLP and legal identity information, legal services, awareness campaigns and, communication materials.
8. Strengthen coordination as well as the functional and operational capacity of the HLP sub cluster.
9. Invest in research, studies, analyses and assessments related to HLP, legal identity and other protection specific issues identified through this response
10. Conduct evidence based advocacy and policy influence on thematic areas identified through data and evidence generated during the course of implementation.
11. Ensuring access to justice for IDPs is a priority, in line with the HLP AoR

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Use of cash in HLP is limited to victims of post eviction response where unconditional or multipurpose cash assistance is used for rental support and to overcome post eviction immediate challenges. Information gathered through HLP sub-cluster key informant interviews and NRC market assessments revealed that beneficiaries prefer and choose cash over in kind assistance for safety, security, access and reduce risk of Covid-19 due to the use of Hormud mobile money transfer.During any cash provision, NRC takes into account protection mainstreaming criteria for cash distribution, adopt locally appropriate safe distribution mechanisms, target and advocate for integrated markets, and use the accountability, monitoring and beneficiary complains system. NRC seek to integrate into project designs aspects such as the use of cash and market systems, basic environmental considerations, youth and women as unique population category with special protection needs, strategic thinking around urbanization in the context of displacement, and the use of technology to improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project will contribute to stimulation of local markets, increase functionality of markets and establish clear linkages to strength market systems. All intervention areas have local functioning and accessible local markets. Cash supported with beneficiaries will be used to enhance local production of market supply of basic items, limits flow of goods and services and reduces the risk of cash inflation. NRC protection team works with livelihood and food security department who routinely conduct market assessments to adopt locally appropriate market based approaches.
