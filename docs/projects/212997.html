Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the improved resilience of conflict-affected populations in Northwest Syria by enhancing access to livelihood opportunities and basic services for women and men in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. IYD will achieve this objective by the implementation of the following integrated activities:
1-IYD will target 30 camps located in Aleppo and Idleb governorates to rehabilitate the main roads between the targeted camps and surrounding communities so that we can prevent the flood and ensure good accessibility to the targeted camps. The graveling process will cover 30,000 M3 in the targeted camps. Based on IYD technical team, the provided thickness of graveling will be 0.5 M.
2-To further increase the benefit from graveling the camp's roads, prevent collecting the rainwater above the graveled roads, and ensure the stability of the graveling, IYD will establish 10,000 ML of closed drainage systems in the targeted camps and surrounding communities.
3-Asphalting of 5 roads (10 KM) to keep the link between the targeted camps and surrounding communities in the targeted area based on the location assessment which will be conducted by IYD. By the end of this activity, 5 roads in camps and surrounding communities will be asphalted. The planned thickness of the provided asphalt will be 7-10 cm
4-On the other hand, IYD will launch CFW activities by conducting a market assessment to determine which type of activities needed to be supported with labor. Under CFW activity, 3000 BNFs, including women and PWDs, will be supported with temporary job opportunities. The CFW activity will be divided into 10 rounds, and each round will last for 20 working days by targeting 300 BNFs based on the locations assessment that will be conducted by IYD to select the most priority works by population
5-Furthermore, with the chronic lack of livelihoods, the project activities will scale up emergency livelihood support focused on capacity building of the targeted people, restore local manufacturing, mitigate the impact of the earthquake, and enhance food security in NWS by providing vocational training to 1800 individuals in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. IYD will conduct a vocations assessment to select the most 20 preferable vocations by population. The VT activity will be divided into 6 rounds, and each round will last for one month by targeting 300 BNFs. By the end of each training, the capacity for the targeted BNFs will be improved so that the trained vocations will help the BNFs to enhance their income.
6-Following to vocational training activity, IYD will provide business support to 500 new businesses support for the best-trained BNFs to initiate their new jobs.
7-Based on market assessment, IYD will rehabilitate 15 local infrastructure projects by vendors and CFW workers, where 5 schools (around 50 classrooms), 3 health facilities, 3 water stations, 2 markets and 2 value chain related infrastructures will be rehabilitated. Therefore, the accessibility to these facilities will be facilitated and safe and equal environment will be ensured.
Here we should mention that high coordination will be made with Cash Working Group and the NGOs working on the ground to ensure CVA coordination and harmonisation. The CVA activities will foster social cohesion as they will include both IDPs and host communities who will be together while implementing these activities. In addition, community/local stakeholders at the area of implementation will be consulted on project design, especially on the daily wage rate, the number of work hours per day, and project duration. CfW daily wage rates will be considered not to distort the local labour market and draw workers away from local employment opportunities. Cash for Work Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance for NWS will be used as a base while developing the CVA activities. The market assessments that will be conducted will consider all market functions/proximty/etc

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs: The severity of the humanitarian crisis in Syria remains significant, as a result of the continued hostilities in localized areas, protracted displacement, harsh weather conditions, and recently the earthquake which hit Syria and Turkey. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview Summary published on 21 December 2023, the number of people in need increased by 1.4 million reaching 16.7 million compared to 2023, 5.5 million of them are internally displaced people. Whilst there has been a decrease of hostilities in Idleb governorate over the current year, the number of people in need had increased significantly, the impact of hostilities on civilians remains the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria. Key interventions needed Providing cash assistance to vulnerable householdsSupporting small businesses and entrepreneursCreating employment opportunitiesProviding vocational training and skills developmentImproving access to markets and financial services Implementation of Activities: Implementation of the project activities will be comprehensive, involving registration and verification of beneficiaries, contracting of service providers, conducting market assessments, and coordinating with local authorities for distribution and monitoring of activities. Regular post-distribution monitoring will ensure adherence to project and donor policies Protection Risk Analysis: Several risks have been identified, including exclusion and discrimination in service provision and potential harm to vulnerable groups during project activities. Mitigation measures include clarifying selection criteria, establishing robust feedback mechanisms, and conducting constant assessments to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy: IYD has adopted a comprehensive PSEA strategy, including adherence to the inter-agency Code of Conduct, active participation in PSEA networks, and establishment of internal reporting and investigative mechanisms. Completion of PSEA checklists and ongoing monitoring will ensure adherence to standards and prompt action against violations. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures: Various risks, including institutional, operational, social, and security risks, have been identified. Mitigation measures involve maintaining strong collaboration with stakeholders, ensuring service provider commitment, addressing social concerns, and coordinating with local leaders and security teams to mitigate security risks. Regular monitoring and updates will be conducted to ensure the safety and security of project implementation.
