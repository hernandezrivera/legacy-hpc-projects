Project Summary

Mozambique is a climate hotspot in sub-Saharan Africa, where powerful effects of climate change affect large populations of poor and vulnerable communities – over half of which are children. The Mozambican population is especially vulnerable to climate shocks due to existing gaps in access to basic essential and social services and underlying poverty and deprivations. This is evidenced in several global climate risk assessments, including the Global Climate Risk Index 2021, which assessed the impacts of extreme weather events (fatalities and economic losses) and ranked Mozambique as the fifth most affected country in the world from 2000 to 2019, and one of three countries most affected by the impacts of extreme weather events in 2019. Given the history, intensity and frequency of occurrence, the likelihood of another cycle of cyclones/ storms and flooding during the upcoming rainfall season is very high. When these occur, there are serious implications to WASH infrastructure, displacements of people and interruption of services, increasing the risk of WASH-related disease outbreaks such as AWD, cholera.
These anticipatory actions will be geographically focused on the North, Central and Southern Regions of Mozambique, targeting the districts that have been identified by the Instituto Nacional de Gestão e Redução do Risco de Desastres be most at highest risk by cyclones, floods and or droughts. The main purpose of this preparatory action is to ensure the readiness of critical WASH-related supplies, including hygiene kits, with a focus on the most vulnerable families in the targeted areas, and pre-positioning of emergency WASH supplies in critical provinces throughout the country. UNICEF has in place long-term agreements with suppliers of WASH goods and services, both in-country and offshore, that enables rapid procurement of essential WASH items for prepositioning and response. UNICEF warehouses are also strategically positioned to service the three regions efficiently, located in Pemba, Beira and Maputo. In preparation for the 2024 rainy season, UNICEF published a country-wide Expression of Interest which seeks to establish standby arrangements with active WASH sector partners for emergency response and preparedness actions. Additionally, UNICEF will support capacity building initiatives on emergency preparedness, response, and coordination for WASH Cluster partners (in particular local NGO partners) as well as government counterparts.
This project will target over 300,000 people at risk for catastrophic natural disaster.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The response will be geographically focused on the North, Central and Southern Region of Mozambique, targeting the districts that will be most affected by cyclones, floods and or droughts. The main purpose of this preparedness and response action is to provide access to critical WASH-related supplies, including hygiene kits, with a focus on the most vulnerable targeting over 300,000 men, women, children, the elderly, and people living with disabilities, with critical WASH supplies as the immediate life-saving response intervention.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The priority activities for this intervention are as follows;1. Procurement and prepositioning of critical WASH Supplies, including hygiene kits in cyclone, floods, drought prone areas2. Distribution of WASH hygiene kits and other supplies to vulnerable people, communities and institutions (schools, health care facilities) affected by natural disasters in Mozambique3. Post Distribution Monitoring of supplies4. Strengthening effective coordination and surveillance mechanisms at National and sub-national level, with links to other cluster coordination arrangements on critical inter-sectoral issues and supplies management5. Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected Populations6. Training and Capacity Building in WASH preparedness and response of partners and government stakeholders

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and the principle of ‘do no harm’ is at the core of UNICEF’s mandate. For UNICEF staff and non-staff personnel, sexual exploitation and sexual abuse constitute acts of serious misconduct and are therefore grounds for disciplinary measures, including summary dismissal. For entities working with UNICEF that fail to address sexual exploitation and abuse through appropriate prevention measures, investigation and corrective measures, such failure shall constitute grounds for cessation of the cooperative arrangement with UNICEF. To ensure adequate safeguards and appropriate action related to sexual exploitation and abuse, UNICEF’s procedure involves screening, informing, monitoring, and training of partners. Using the 2010 United Nations (UN) human rights council endorsement that access to WASH is a human right, UNICEF will work with partners to ensure that all people have equal access to safe WASH services. To achieve this, UNICEF and partners will work to ensure that communal latrines are gender separated, culturally appropriate with menstrual hygiene health and disability design components and that WASH service delivery points are well lit and accessible

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will place help desks and suggestion boxes during distribution to solicit feedback from beneficiaries. Additionally, the project will conduct post distribution monitoring on supplies to gauge to effectiveness and usage of the provided supplies

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities face some key challenges in the community setting. The may include but not limited to;• Unclear and unstructured WASH hygiene item distribution• Physically inaccessibility of distribution points• Inability to use provided aid items• Inaccessible WASH facilities• Inappropriate information and communication methods/channelsThe project will ensure that distribution points are accessible for all people including people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNICEF's concern for children extends through the totality of a child's environment, including all the factors that influence a child's survival and development. UNICEF is required to ensure that negative environmental impact is prevented and minimized in its supported programmes and strives to improve the immediate environment of children (e.g., home, school and community) wherever possible and within UNICEF's technical competence. As a member of the UN System, UNICEF has committed itself to support the implementation of the various action plans adopted at all the major UN conferences of the 1990s. These action plans, especially Agenda 21, emphasize the need for integrating social, economic and environmental factors at the policy, planning and management levels.
