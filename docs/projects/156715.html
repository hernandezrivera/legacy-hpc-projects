Project Summary

The current crisis in Syria has led to a widespread deterioration in what was a well-functioning and predominantly public healthcare system with the destruction or disruption of health facilities.
Additionally, population movement and the corrosion of public services and infrastructure have increased the risk of outbreaks of previously controlled epidemics.
Results of MoH (2018) rapid nutrition assessments of children age 6-59 months and PLWs in East Aleppo (2017) shows the incidence of GAM rate is 8.5 % in children and 20.8% maternal malnutrition.
4.70 million girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of under nutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services in 2018. 84,200 girls and boys aged 6-59 months are acutely under nourished, of which around 19,000 are in the severe category and are 11 times more likely to die than well-nourished children. 865,295 girls and boys under five years of age suffer from micronutrient deficiencies. 3.05 million girls and boys under 5 years of age require optimal feeding for adequate nutrition status while 1.55 million pregnant and lactating women require preventive nutrition services.

Needs

Based on HNO data of 2019, targeted locations (Rural Damascus, Rural Homs, Rural Hama) - 162,169 PiN for nutrition support services based on sector data including CMAM and IYCF activities and micronutrient deficiency support. Terre Des Hommes Italy plans to target 80% through screening activities in order to identify and refer cases of SAM with complication/MAM and the treatment of SAM without complication/MAM, in addition to the provision of preventive micro nutrients through blank distribution. Support PLW with folic acids and micro nutrients.

Implementation of activities

• Staff trainings/follow up on CMAM to outreach Nutrition programme. • Promoting mobile nutrition teams (composed of medical staff and volunteers) with the aim to improve the community-based nutrition surveillance systems and the MAM treatments. • The mobile teams will provide facilities and community-based counselling activities:• The volunteers of the mobile teams will do MUAC, monitoring, awareness-raising on breastfeeding and other related issues, such as Hygiene according to IYCF; • The medical staff will carry out medical screenings and Outpatient Therapeutic Programmes (OTP), will distribute pumply doses and will administer the drugs to the cases in need and the micronutrients to women and children.• During the first month the mobile team will do a survey to identify children under 5 years, lactating and pregnant women in need in new selected areas.• A referral pathway will be identified in new locations and supported in already accessed areas in order to refer five-year-old children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition to medical or specialized centres. The mobile teams will refer the cases of SAM to specialised clinics for more accurate medical checks and treatments, and will do follow up to the referral cases. • In the villages, where it will be possible, local resident volunteers, (nurse, doctor or pharmacist, etc.) will be identified to do awareness-raising and to monitor the nutrition status of the children, lactating and pregnant women.• A logistical office will be managed in areas of implementation based on approvals from related authorities to support the activities of the two mobile teams. The logistical office will be equipped to provide training sections for the doctors and the volunteers, a meeting room for the mobile teams and a warehouse. One staff member will do data entry for monitoring and follow up of the project beneficiaries and indicators.
