Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in North West Syria is dire, with protracted conflict, economic instability, and recurrent natural disasters affecting approximately 4.7 million people, including 1.8 million internally displaced. Compounding crises, such as armed conflict, insecurity, displacement, disease outbreaks, and recent earthquakes in 2023, have pushed vulnerable populations to the brink.
There are 618 health facilities in the area, but only 71% are functional, leaving significant gaps in service delivery. Nearly a quarter of hospitals and a third of primary health clinics are non-operational, severely impacting maternal and child health services. Despite nutrition services being available in 331 primary health clinics, they still fall short of meeting population needs, with limited reach of the Expanded Program on Immunization.
Children are particularly vulnerable, as highlighted by the September 2023 EPI Household Survey, which identified 3,601 'Zero Dose' children, increasing their susceptibility to vaccine-preventable diseases. Economic deterioration has led to high unemployment, soaring food prices, and heightened food insecurity, exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic.
Our program aligns with local priorities, focusing on health and well-being, humanitarian response, and resilience building. By addressing critical gaps in health and nutrition services, especially at the primary health clinic level, our program is essential for immediate and long-term recovery. It aims to improve access to quality health and nutrition services, emphasizing maternal and child health, immunization, malnutrition management, and disease treatment.
Our integrated approach to service delivery ensures the most vulnerable receive necessary care, strengthening health system resilience and fostering community ownership. SOP has a history of operational and managerial excellence, which we will leverage to support program demands effectively. Training will be integral to capacity development, focusing on project and financial management, and compliance to ensure effective program delivery. We are committed to integrating ethical controls to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, strengthening areas through specialized training and robust reporting mechanisms.
SOP has been active in the proposed area since 2016 through WGSS, CFS, and PHC, providing integrated health and protection services. WGSS and CFS collaborate with PHCs to provide a continuum of care for children and families, with referral pathways established for specialized support. Mental health professionals address psychosocial issues, and during emergencies, these facilities act as response nodes, ensuring continuous service delivery.
Our SOP Health Facility offers services such as first aid, medical consultations, CMAM department, gynecology clinic, and pharmacy (medical distribution). We have a referral system between WGSS and PHC, with the clinic providing confidential assistance for cases of GBV. Children and women with malnutrition will also be referred to the clinic for assistance.
Additionally, SOP will launch courses on social or business entrepreneurship startups, targeting 100 beneficiaries, equivalent to 100 families. We will initiate training sessions on soft skills or transferable skills for another 100 beneficiaries. Furthermore, vocational or business training such as handicrafts, languages, and computer skills will be provided. Through these measures, we prioritize integrating Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures and ensuring the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) in all stages of our program. Through assessments and tailored strategies, we aim to foster inclusivity and recognize the rights and needs of all community members.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Through this project, SOP will deliver multi-sectoral, integrated humanitarian intervention in Ariha on protection, ER, and health sectors.SOP will provide integrated services including GBV and CP, vocational training, business entrepreneurship in WGSS, and primary healthcare services to vulnerable groups. The aim is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, respond to livelihood, health, and protection needs, and increase community resilience. Risks and Mitigation Measures:Security Concerns: We'll conduct security assessments, provide staff training, and establish communication channels for emergencies.Logistical Challenges: We'll establish alternative supply routes, preposition materials, and collaborate with local partners for transportation.Access Constraints: Engaging with local leaders, negotiating safe passage, and leveraging networks will help overcome access barriers.Community Resistance: Prioritizing community engagement, conducting awareness campaigns, and collaborating with local influencers will address opposition. PSEA Strategy:SOP will ensure confidentiality, access, and follow-up for PSEA allegations. Complaints will be made anonymously via WhatsApp, email, or in-person. Trained operators handle the WhatsApp, providing information and support. Zero-tolerance policy communicated to all staff. Training on safeguarding and PSEA provided to staff and BNFs.
