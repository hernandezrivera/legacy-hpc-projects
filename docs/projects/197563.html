Project Summary

The project aims to ensure the provision of quality GBV response services to GBV survivors and
women and girls at risk throughout Syria as well as ensuring measures are put in place to
prevent and mitigate the risks of GBV.
Through the WoS approach, UNFPA works with local IPs to support the delivery of GBV services. UNFPA is committed to working with local IPs to contribute to sustainability and to its commitment to localization as part of its adherence to the Grand Bargain. UNFPA will build on its achievements, best and promising practices and lessons learned to continue GBV service provision. UNFPA also aims to enhance the COVID-19 response by adapting and contextualizing modalities for continuity of service provision for women, girls, men, and boys seeking care, based on lessons learned from the past two years. Such service adaptation will include scaling up online platforms to provide GBV prevention and response services and capacity development to service providers. Institutionalized coordination mechanisms in GBV will be leveraged to maximize the impact and quality of service delivery on the ground.
UNFPA’s interventions aim to reach the following objectives throughout Syria:
Service Delivery: Increase the availability of and access to specialized GBV services, including through the provision of CVA, especially for women, girls, and persons with disabilities. UNFPA will continue providing essential lifesaving GBV services and will provide CVA to ensure that women and girls, including persons with disabilities, can access the care they need in some of the most vulnerable areas in Syria. Integrating CVA within GBV programming has been shown to contribute to women’s feelings of safety and self-reliance, their access to health, GBV risk mitigation and response, and their urgent lifesaving needs. The integration of cash assistance within GBV case management has proven to have a positive impact both on mitigating further risk of GBV and on supporting access to a comprehensive package of GBV services aimed at improving resilience and wellbeing of survivors and individuals at risk.
Capacity Building: Improve capacity of service providers and implementing partners to provide high-quality, inclusive, and comprehensive GBV services. UNFPA Syria and Turkey XB will continue to build the capacity of UNFPA partners and, through the GBV AoR, of other GBV actors, in an effort to enhance the quality of GBV services provided and to ensure humanitarian actors are well trained, to reach a wide geographical coverage, and to raise women’s and girls’ concerns, needs, and capacities through advocacy - with special attention on Syrian grass-root organizations.
GBV Prevention and Social Norms: Challenge negative social norms that prevent the empowerment of women and girls and develop accountable models of engagement of men and boys. Through the Turkey XB hub, UNFPA will continue implementing the full scope of the GBV SC prevention program based on the awareness raising toolkit. UNFPA’s IPs will also carry out the full scope of activities under the “Adolescent Mothers Against All Odds” (AMAL) initiative, which has been designed to meet the immediate needs of adolescents girls who are pregnant or first-time mothers, while simultaneously addressing community awareness and engagement. UNFPA Syria’s four-year social norms pilot project in Aleppo will continue.
Coordination: Strengthen leadership and coordination of the GBV response through leadership of coordination mechanisms, building capacity, providing technical support, and enhancing monitoring mechanisms. UNFPA effectively leads the Whole of Syria GBV coordination structure (WoS GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR), supporting the GBV coordinators in Syria and Turkey XB hubs, as well as the NGO lead of the NES GBV sub-working group. UNFPA Syria will continue to coordinate the GBV sub-sector, while UNFPA Turkey XB will continue to lead the GBV SC.

3 - Needs

According to the UNFPA led Voices from Syria 2023, GBV continues to be a dominant feature in Syria, affecting the lives of millions of women and girls across the country. Women and girls continue to survive various forms of GBV, most commonly perpetrated male partners, male family members, and other men and boys. Women and girls faced compounded forms of violence when exposed to overlapping and mutually reinforcing forms of discrimination and social exclusion, such as for divorced and widowed women, women and girls with disabilities, older women. Virtually every space that women and girls access is marred with experiences of GBV, including online. The combination of the prolonged humanitarian crisis; the Syrian economy’s swift deterioration and the lasting effects of the COVID-19 pandemic have exacerbated women’s and girls’ risks of GBV.The impact of GBV on women and girls is tremendous, frequently aggravated by forms of victim blaming, social stigma, and isolation. Despite seeking protection and health services, family and community support, women and girls are increasingly engaging in harmful and life-threatening coping mechanisms such as self-harm and suicide. GBV service providers and Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) continue to be key allies of Syrian women and girls in their daily struggle against GBV. The impact of GBV services is also demonstrated in women’s and girls’ demands for their increased number, improved accessibility and long-term sustainability.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will increase availability of and access to specialized GBV services through static facilities, mobile teams and remote services. UNFPA will also continue to increase its support and services, including through CVA and dignity kits distributions, to female headed households, adolescent girls, widows, divorcees and women and girls with disabilities and focus on empowering them through women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), community wellbeing centers (CWC), safe spaces within health facilities, integrated RH/GBV outreach services and through remote/on-line services. UNFPA will continue to lead the GBV coordination and enhance the quality of the GBV response through building the capacity of member organizations, strengthening and updating referral pathways and SOPs. 1.1 / 1.2 Provide lifesaving and quality GBV response services. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Providing GBV services is critical to ensure survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. Beyond confidentiality, if services are not survivor centered, this will have a negative impact on help-seeking behavior with dramatic consequences on survivor's health. Communities may be resistant to engaging around topics of GBV and ultimately prevent women and girls from attending. To mitigate these risks, UNFPA carries out thorough risks analysis of its interventions and works with communities toreduce risks of GBV. All services will aim to be confidential and survivor centered by building the capacity of partners. UNFPA continuously conducts assessments on barriers to access services. Women and girls are at the centre of the decision making at every phase of the project implementation. UNFPA organizes and facilitates trainings for a variety of sectors on GBV risk mitigation to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. UNFPA has established strong M&E systems. In addition to UNFPA’s regular M&E standard reporting practices such as onsite visits, monthly and quarterly partner meetings, UNFPA conducts quarterly assessments using client feedback forms at health facilities and WGSSs, KIIs with service providers, and FGDs at WGSSs and an annual impact assessment. Third party monitors are used to verify program quality and implementation


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNFPA places great importance on PSEA along with other UN entities; as such, UNFPA and other UN agencies have been tasked to assess and score a partner’s capacity based on a set of eight core standards on PSEA organizational policies and procedures to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). UNFPA Turkey XB conducted the assessment of all its IPs and they now have full capacity to safeguard, identify, report, and investigate PSEA cases. UNFPA has established a hotline to report any SEA incidents, and all UNFPA and UNFPA-supported IP staff are required to complete training on PSEA. Feedback and complaints mechanisms set up include: complaints box; WhatsApp number; a hotline; informing IP staff directly through verbal complaint; TPM; PSEA banners provided by the PSEA network are an integral part of dignity kit (DK) distributions carried out by GBV Sub-Sector/Sub-Cluster members. Information, education, and communication (IEC) materials also provided by the PSEA network have recently been embedded in each kit with information about the interagency network hotline and messaging on the free nature of all humanitarian assistance. In 2021, all UNFPA Syria IPs were assessed for PSEA, either through a self-assessment reviewed by UNFPA jointly with the PSEA in-country network, or other UN agencies who shared the results and details with UNFPA. Capacity development and system strengthening plans are being developed concurrently while addressing some of the PSEA risks


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access, permissions and bureaucratic hurdles may slow the implementation however, UNFPA actively works with its national partners to over come these challenges. Furthermore the possible non renewal of the SCR will have a tragic impact on women and girls’ life, not being able to access life-saving services anymore. Security issues and shifting lines of control will lead to a hampered access or even material damage. This is mitigated by a detailed contingency plan for every segment of the UNFPA intervention. Assumption that communities will continue to engage in preventing GBV. While GBV remains a difficult subject in many communities, this can be overcome by actively engaging with communities. Another risk associated with the project is mainstreaming fatigue as various projects aim to mainstream GBV, gender, disability and other protection issues. The GBV AoR assumes that this fatigue will occur and has prepared to provide tools, checklists and technical support (Skype calls, email, meetings) to facilitate technical support in developing and implementing mitigating measures across sectors. The GBV AoR, as a mitigating measure, will work with colleagues addressing gender, disability and protection risks in order to synthesize and streamline processes e.g. conduct a FGD that addresses the GBV risks associated with person(s) with disabilities or pilot an indicator that addresses GBV and disability for women, men, boys and girls.
