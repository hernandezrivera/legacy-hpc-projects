Project Summary

The project aims at providing Emergency healthcare and nutrition services to respond to the needs of the conflict affected people in new and existing areas of operations to IDPs, Returnees, pastoralist and most vulnerable communities in 6 states of Sudan, namely: Khartoum, Central Darfur, Blue Nile, South Kordofan and West Darfur where World Relief (WR) has been operating in previous years. In addition, for this ongoing conflict, WR plans to resume operations in the state of Jazeera to provide lifesaving interventions for IDPs and host community. As a result of this project, morbidity and mortality attributable to high malnutrition rate and burden of common diseases in the targeted communities will be reduced significantly.
The following are the targeted localities: Azum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero, Zalingei, Bindisi, Mukjar & Umdukun (8 localities of Central Darfur State); Geneina, Kerenik, Habila, Beida and Sirba (5 localities of West Darfur); Bahri, Karari, Khartoum, Omdurman, Sharg En Neel, Um Bada and Jebel Awlia (7 localities of Khartoum); Demazine, Kurmuk, Bau and Rosaries (4 localities of Blue Nile states) and 1 locality of South Kordofan (Kadugli); Sharg al Jazeera and Medani (2 localities in Jazeera). It is estimated that 560,000 (F:294,737; M:537,480) people; among which 95,238 are children under five years old and 23,330 with disability) will benefit from this project. The beneficiaries are mostly IDPs in the conflict affected states such as Darfur states, South Kordofan and Khartoum while Jazeera, and Blue Nile states, they are most scattered IDPs and refugees who are integrated in the communities and poor families.
The project will ensure that children under five years, women and especially pregnant or lactating (PLWs) are prioritized as part of people with specific needs and vulnerable to diseases and malnutrition. Thus, the services are in line with the needs of the specific and general population and considering also the services package of the Primary Healthcare (PHC). In addition, the project will focus on the life-saving emergency activities while initiating and reinforcing those strengthening and contributing to resilience of the health systems in targeted localities.
Under Health program, the activities will range from the promotional, preventive to curative of common diseases. Immunization campaign for ,1 year old children will be organized, IEC on different health topics at PHC and community level, maternal health services, ANC, PNC, delivery and newborn care; strengthening the PHC functionality through running cost and establishment of health committees as part of governance & leadership component of the PHC; supplies of essential medicines as per WHO and FMoH guidelines, early warning system activation for disease outbreak or any relevant emergency; support with incentives for the health cadres especially in hard-to-reach areas; capacity building of health cadres, etc.
Nutrition programing will focus on lifesaving preventive activities such as, protection and promotion of breastfeeding; Maternal and Young Child Nutrition (MYCN) Counseling; micronutrient supplementation for under-twos and pregnant women; early detection using Mother/family MUAC. Will also support capacity building of community volunteers & Mother support groups (MSGs) and nutrition cadres at the health facilities; curative activities focusing on CMAM; for Outpatient management and TSFPs. Management of at risk mother and infants will also be done WR has been implementing health and nutrition programing since 2005 in Darfur and other states of Sudan using an integrated approach where the center of focus will be the
patient. The project design is to engage the community in identifying the needs and their participation in the implementation of the project as part of accountability to the beneficiaries. WR will ensure the mitigation plan and other risks.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Civilians have suffered from years of conflict, exposing them to protection threats including gender-based violence, targeted attacks, and violations of basic human rights. Freedom of movement, especially conflict-affected areas remain restricted. This greatly impacts communities’ ability to engage in livelihoods, undermining opportunities to support self-reliance and preventing durable solutions to displacement. It also restricts people’s access to basic services. More importantly, the causes of the conflict particularly in Darfur remain unresolved. Long-standing grievances between communities continue to give rise to clashes which are aggravated by the involvement of heavily armed tribal militias and the proliferation of small arms and light weapons. Sexual violence particularly affects women and girls in displaced persons’ camps and in rural communities, where they are often preyed upon by armed men, especially when they engage in livelihood activities like collecting water or firewood or working in the fields. Protection will be mainstreamed across this project in order to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse, or exploitation towards the targeted population. Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services reduces the risk of beneficiaries being targeted by armed groups. Therefore, locations of health facilities, nutrition feeding centers, and agricultural demonstration plots are managed as safe spaces that are centrally located and are accessible to large numbers of the population. The locations also minimize the distance that distributed items need to be transported to the home and mitigate the risk of theft or harassment during transportation. All project staff will undergo training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and be sensitized on the risks faced by many of the women and children accessing services. The various partners and stakeholders mentioned throughout the proposal, as well as the beneficiaries themselves, will be asked to participate throughout all stages of the process from beneficiary selection to building the work plan, to scheduling and designing trainings, to monitoring and evaluation. Additionally, WRS will create a number of reporting mechanisms including a hotline, comment boxes, trained community volunteers, andmechanisms at project sites through which community members can report incidents.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WRS believes that the organization is accountable to the donor, line ministries, HAC, local authorities where the organization operates & beneficiary communities. WRS also maintains that beneficiaries have the right to know and be involved in projects designed to provide service to them. As a result, WRS promotes community participation in all stages of the project life-cycle by working with the communities from the initial identification of needs to the program phase-out. WRS's M&E system involves all stakeholders for timely information sharing and direct participation in implementation and M&E at all levels. In addition to ensuring openness and transparency, this will also ensure best use of the resources according to the scope of the project within the budget and priority areas of the beneficiary communities. To this end, WRS has established community level committees in its operational areas and they are responsible to oversee the implementation and monitoring of project activities in their respective areas. To further ensure the communities' engagement, WR strengthens community level management staff. The community level management is led by Area Coordinators (ACs) based in deep field who work closely with community leaders. Regularly the ACs review project activities and progress of the project with community committees and share pertinent information. The outcome of community review meetings will be reported to the Nutrition coordinators, Health and Nutrition Manager, Area Manager and the Program director. The information obtained from ACs will be discussed during monthly review meetings. Besides, the Health and Nutrition Manager, Nutrition Coordinator and Officers, Field Operations Officers and M&E Coordinator regularly visit the beneficiary communities to ensure the accountability measures are functioning properly. WRS uses suggestion boxes to be placed at each feeding center to get the feedback from beneficiary communities on health facilities services. The beneficiary community will be informed to put feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WRS's Nutrition and health Coordinator will collect information from the suggestion box on monthly basis and discuss with Nutrition manager, Area Manager and Program director on improvements of the beneficiary complaints and also keep up the good side of our services. Also the findings from the beneficiaries comment will be shared with SMoH for taking collective action. The Nutrition and health coordinators compile project information and share pertinent information at the state level with close supervision from the Health and Nutrition Manager and get feedback from partners to further strengthen the project implementation and information dissemination. Being cognizant of the ‘dono-harm’ principles, the project is designed to address nutrition needs of the communities while mitigating unintended social, economic, and environmental consequences.
