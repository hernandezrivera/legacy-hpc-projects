Project Summary

Nabolok has been ensuring humanitarian assistance since June, 2018 to till date and planning to continue for the coming year of 2024 in camp-24 & 26 and host community. The planned areas and assistance for the Rohingya community are respectively- whole Block-F for full WASH activities under camp-24; whole Block-E for sanitation & hygiene activities and sub-blocks F8, F9, F10, G3, G4, G5, G6 & H10 for water supply under camp-26. Besides, the planned areas and WASH assistance for the Host community are respectively- Ward No# 8 & 9 under Nhilla Union and in Sabrang Union in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar. Considering the camp context, field experience and assessing the needs of beneficiaries regarding WASH services, we found that the maximum facilities have already been installed in our planned areas in camps and host, but as these were installed a long time ago it is needed to ensure the functionality of these facilities by providing regular repair and maintenance services. Considering the new challenges or demands, we have also planned to install some new WASH facilities in both Rohingya and host (Sabrang Union) to ensure standard sanitation and hygiene facilities for the beneficiaries. It is also need to ensure the proper use & operation of these facilities as well as the quality, regularity, sufficiency, equitability, and dignified access to WASH facilities for all women, men, girls and boys, including all age groups and persons with disabilities in the Rohingya camps and targeted host communities. Besides, some latrines need to be decommissioned and the new one need to be installed. In addition, we have also planned to establish a solid waste dumping station for proper management of the collected solid waste and a drying bed for solid sludge removal of our existing latrines. We will also continue our safe water supply through ARO plant and boreholes. We will also provide regular repair & maintenance of every single WASH facility such as: pit latrine & bathhouse repair and maintenance, drain cleaning, latrine dislodging & maintaining DEWATS Plant, open space & community places cleaning, double chamber dustbin cleaning & waste management (for organic and non-organic waste), house to house solid waste collection & management, conducting hygiene promotion group session & individual HHs visit & message delivery through megaphone for raising awareness etc. In addition, we also planned to conduct an evaluation regarding our services to collect beneficiaries’ feedback. We may conduct some repair and renovation work on existing wash facilities so that people of all age groups, genders and EVIs can use our wash facilities very comfortably. Disinfection activities & fogger machine spray and key message forwarding have also been included in our plan considering dengue and other infectious diseases. In every stage, we will collect beneficiaries’ feedback before going to implement any activity and we will design our plan as per their demand so that we can ensure inclusion of all age groups, genders and persons with special needs to use WASH facilities. All of aforementioned activities are included in our plan to ensure that 100% of targeted beneficiaries have proper water, sanitation and hygiene facilities throughout the project duration.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The J-MSNA-2023 stated that in Cox’s Bazar the 64% Rohingya and 14% HC HHs received WASH assistance in the 12 months. Access to sufficient water: It also stated in J-MSNA-2023 that, “households seemed to have enough water for all purposes, but a third of refugees reported that they would worry rarely or sometimes over the 4 weeks about not having enough water. The Refugee HHs reported collecting a mean of 12 liters of water per person, but there was great variability between camps”. According to the last WASH sector analysis report conducted in 2022, “it was acknowledged that 12% Rohingya and 26% HC reporting not having enough water to meet domestic needs”. So, ensuring safe water for 100% Rohingya and HC in Cox’s Bazar is a big challenge and there still need to fill gaps as well as ensuring access to safe water, maintaining the quality, quantity and supply of water regularly. The Teknaf area doesn't have access to safe water and water scarcity is very high as it’s a saline area and underground water is also contaminated with saline. Access to safe and sufficient water in Teknaf still remains a major challenge during the dry season. In Bangladesh-Cox’s Bazar: WASH Severity Classification-February 2022 it has stated that 68% HC and 91% Rohingya HHs have access to improved water sources. In 2023, Nabolok ensured safe and sufficient water for 2746 Rohingya HHs in camp-24, camp-25 & camp-26 and 839 HC HHs in Nhilla Union, Teknaf. According to our previous experience and comparing with J-MSNA-2023 and other report’s findings of Sector Agency, there is still a need of providing safe and sufficient water assistance for both Rohingya and HC HHs in our targeted areas. Latrine and Hygiene Access: In Bangladesh-Cox’s Bazar: WASH Severity Classification-February 2022 it has stated that, in Teknaf 80% Rohingya and 80% HC HHs have access to improved sanitation services except the Sabrang Union where 26% HC HHs rely on unimproved services. Also, in Teknaf, 92% HC and 98% refugees have access to soap for maintaining proper hygiene. As per the last WASH sector analysis, only 8% of fecal sludge treatment plants meet standards while 7% of sanitation infrastructure require major repair and maintenance and 51 Rohingyas on average share bathing facilities due to lack of space. In J-MSNA-2023 it has mentioned that, the 77% of HC HHs have fixed or mobile handwashing places where 23% have no handwashing place, and 75% Rohingya HHs reported they have fixed or mobile handwashing places where 25% have no. As per our experience it is also found that in our targeted areas maximum WASH facilities were installed a long time ago which required regular repair and maintenance as well as regular sludge management for the pit latrines. Also, in our targeted project areas it is needed to ensure equal distribution of hygiene and MHM kits (provided by IOM in camp-24) and building awareness regarding proper hygiene practice by conducting regular hygiene group sessions, awareness meetings and H-to-H visits. It also requires a wide scale of Hygienic Practice monitoring, staff capacity building and meaningful community engagement. On the other hand, HC has been less supported by hygiene items and awareness raising initiatives. Solid Waste Management: In J-MSNA-2023 it has mentioned that 53% of Rohingya HHs have 2 bins with segregated waste where 40% have no 2 bins and 7% have 2 bins but not segregating waste. Considering this and as per our experience there is a need to ensure proper SWM of Rohingya HHs, community places, drains and dustbins in our targeted project areas. There is also a need of establishing a solid waste dumping station as well as a segregation point for proper waste management. By considering all of these and comparing all findings with our previous experiences it is a need to continue WASH assistance in our targeted project areas. In this regard, we have also coordinated with the Sector Agency, RRRC and other WASH actors in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is committed to address and reduce the water crisis and ensure safe sanitation facilities to the Rohingya and 25% of HC and to ensure solid waste management and awareness building in hygiene practice. To implement the proposed project Nabolok will always consult with the WASH Sector/other working groups before engaging in any activity. To adopt an integrated approach the project will ensure camp-based inter-sectoral coordination and communication. The project will build a strong coordination with WASH Sector, SMS, CiC as well as RRRC for further planning for Rohingya HHs and with the District Administration, UNO, Local Governmental Representatives for further planning for the HC HHs. For filling up any gaps and to ensure cost efficiency regarding WASH facility install, repair and maintenance, we will always follow the guidelines of the WASH Sector. To ensure predictability and continuity of our project activities we will follow the multi-year strategies produced by the Sectors. As an active project implementer in WASH Sector, we will comply our project activities with the technical standards and policies set by the WASH Sector. Apart from this, the project will also prepare a need assessment and collect approval from CiC, and RRRC including budget portions. The project team will attend monthly coordination meeting regularly at CiC Office and WASH Sector and update the progress. The project will also prepare and send the monthly progress report to the CiC Office and WASH Sector in monthly basis.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Nabolok is always committed to ensure meaningful access of every single WASH assistance both for FDMN and HC and always take beneficiaries' opinion by ensuring their active participation and giving them decision-making opportunities. We prioritize beneficiaries’ safety, dignity and always take concern about not doing any kind of activity which can be harmful for our targeted beneficiaries. In this connection, for installing any WASH facilities (such as-Latrines, bathhouses, water tap stands etc.) in the field, we first identify and address the specific needs, vulnerability, took our beneficiaries opinion and select the place by considering meaningful access of all age groups as well as taking special concern for PWDs. Our 100% staff and volunteers also trained up and well-knowledge regarding not doing any activity that can violate humanitarian principles. Our monitoring team and staff are engaged in disseminating necessary information to our beneficiaries to enhance their knowledge so that they can raise their voice and seek solutions to their problems. To build up community engagement we regularly conduct awareness sessions, meetings with the community heads, and visit beneficiaries' houses to enhance their knowledge. A well-managed complaints and feedback mechanism has always been maintained to respond to the complaints timely by ensuring confidentiality. To integrate protection mainstreaming in our proposed project we will ensure effective communication and coordination with the Sector Coordinators and ISCG focal points for further improvement. We will also ensure sharing information to the beneficiaries to promote their active participation and empowerment so that they are able to protect themselves from any harmful effects as well as develop themselves, including aware of their rights. We will also coordinate with other actors engaged in protection assistance to increase our knowledge about protection, avoid duplications and to address risks.

3.3 - Age & Disability

All the WASH facilities will be ensured by considering all age groups and PWDs. The project will provide regular repair & maintenance of the facilities so that these became easily accessible for all age groups including the PWDs. Our full-time staff will ensure easy access and enough and meaningful access of the facilities without any discrimination, gender, disability or age-related barriers. The project will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services for WASH facilities, e.g., PWD, elderly people, children, etc. To ensure easy access to water from the water distribution point, the project will ensure the distribution point near the beneficiaries’ house. The project will give priority to the Extremely Vulnerable individual (EVI). The WASH facilities will be in a safe and secure place for all and beneficiaries will enjoy to receive the assistance. The project will conduct a household assessment for select beneficiaries, and households; select the sites to establish/install new WASH facilities and identify the age groups and families who need urgently of WASH service from Nabolok. Considering the different ages women, men, adolescents, women-headed PWD families, ethnicity, different cultural backgrounds, and various lifestyles, all the WASH facilities will be in place. The project will also take special recommendations and consent from the CiCs and SMS.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will accountable to ensure dignitary and quality support for the affected people. The project is committed to addressing the crisis and reducing the water crisis and ensuring sanitation facilities for Rohingya households and 25% of the host community and support to ensure solid waste management and hygiene practice. For ensuring the quality of the support, the project will ensure the minimum of two types of accountability mechanisms i.e., Complaint Box and direct or phonically system. Projects staffs will play a positive and supportive role to ensure quality support in time. The beneficiary can share their suggestions, feedback, and complaints with the project staff verbally, and in writing using a complaint box. Besides we will conduct regular monitoring and door-to-door visit and monitor the progress and identify the problem and collect feedback about our services. The project will welcome complaints and feedback for further improvement and will be ready to respond to the complaint, and feedback on a priority basis.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Nabolok is an active member of the PSEA Network of Cox’s Bazar. We have 2 PSEA Focal Persons (Teknaf based) to address SEA related issues. The Focal Persons are well-trained up by the organization regarding PSEA as well as they are regularly attending PSEA Network meetings. To avoid any incident of SEA, in every month the Focal Persons conduct internal PSEA training among the 100% staff and volunteers in the field level. Our trained volunteers deliver SEA awareness messages to the beneficiaries through conducting daily group sessions at the field level. We also have complaint boxes in our field level and maintain a complaint & feedback register to address SEA related complaints raised by the beneficiaries as well as our staff or volunteers. Furthermore, we always prioritize safety, dignity, well-being of the survivor while addressing SEA related complaints including our reporting and investigation. The identity of the victims and complainers is always kept confidential. For further improvement of PSEA mainstreaming in JRP-2024, we will actively engage in good communication and coordination with the PSEA Network of Cox’s Bazar and our Focal Persons will receive ToT conducted by the PSEA Network. We also have a plan to use awareness materials such as-posters, videos to deliver proper messages to our beneficiaries including our 100% staff and volunteers. We will also actively engage to ensure our targeted beneficiaries are aware of their rights, services and where/how to access them, that sexual exploitation is not tolerated, and how they can report any concerns related to SEA. There will be an effective, safe, and accessible feedback and response system to report any suspicions of SEA of our beneficiaries. We will arrange integrated PSEA information campaigns, awareness-raising, training and meetings with our targeted beneficiaries and ensure that victims of SEA have access without delay to the assistance and support that is required with a victim-centered approach.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. As Nabolok has been working in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar from 2018 and it is a natural disaster-prone area of Bangladesh, so we have in-depth experience in pre-preparedness and response in any kind of natural disasters. In Hazard Response Plan-2022 it has mentioned that, “SMSD Sectors incident reporting within the camps from 2018 to 2021 indicated that the key risks likely to impact the camps are: wind/rain storms, floods (flash-flooding and water-logging), slope failure/landslides and fires. In addition, there is a periodic risk of cyclones during the two cyclone seasons from April-May and October–November. According to the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), between 1960 to 2017, seven Severe Cyclonic Storms (max wind speed of 220 km/hr) and two Cyclonic Storms (max wind speed of 115 km/hr) directly hit Cox’s Bazar.” In this connection, we will allocate a contingency fund for addressing any environmental or climate change related incidents in our project areas. We will have an emergency response plan regarding DRR to disseminate warning messages to the beneficiaries, keep ready emergency response team, training for the team, ensure sufficient assistance after disaster as well as we will ensure effective coordination and communication with the Sector coordinators and the ISCG EPR focal point and government entities (DRRO, RRRC, CPP) to address any potential issues.
