Project Summary

South Sudan remained in serious humanitarian crisis due to the cumulative effects of years of conflict and violence against civilians, which have destroyed people’s livelihoods and forced 4.2 million people to flee their homes—nearly 1.5 million inside and 2 million outside the country. This has contributed to an extended humanitarian crisis, which has been exacerbated by unusually high levels of flooding and the COVID-19 pandemic. Although conflict has reduced due to the signing of the Revitalized-Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS), violence has continued in many parts of the country, threatening to reverse gains made at the national level since the peace deal was signed in September 2018. Conflict with non-signatory groups in the Equatoria Region has continued causing displacements, loss of livelihoods and in instances loss of life. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms is expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020.
2021 HRP will provide ARC an opportunity to continue with on-going life-saving interventions to affected populations by responding to GBV needs identified through data collection and analysis in the course of implementation of the ongoing interventions and periodic assessments conducted jointly by UNOCHA and other partners. ARC has operational presence; in Aweil West, Aweil Centre, Kajo Keji, Ulang, and Kapoeta South and Budi Counties, South Sudan. ARC runs GBV response and prevention interventions in all but one proposed locations. It is only in Aweil West County where we currently don't have active GBV services but have on-going nutrition interventions. Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) funds will be used to complement ongoing OFDA support in; Kapoeta South, Budi, Kajo Keji and integrate GBV into our nutrition activities in Aweil West. In Aweil Centre, GBV activities are funded by UNICEF and UNWOMEN and the funding will be ending on June 30th. ARC therefore views HRP 2021 funding as an opportunity to sustain the project. In addition, HRP funds will be used to broaden the scope of GBV activities to reach more beneficiaries especially in locations where ARC has on-going activities such as; Kajo Keji, Kapoeta South and Aweil Central, Ulang and Budi. Through our GBV programming in the aforementioned locations, gaps are emerging in nearby payams of the counties we are operating and these new vulnerabilities to GBV are being identified due to evolving context as a result of floods and related displacements of IDPs, returnees and host communities against a background of limited funding for GBV. This cumulative effects of these emerging gaps therefore calls for scaling up GBV service provisions to locations and populations that have not been benefiting from our current GBV programs. In Aweil West, GBV services have been non-existent as identified by our nutrition staff providing Nutrition services in Aweil West. The HRP 2021 funding will provide an opportunity to integrate our protection interventions with nutrition services already being provided in Aweil West County.

1 - Needs

Kajo Keji: ARC participated in inter-agency assessments carried out between 1st to 5th February 2021 in Kajo Keji to assess the needs of displaced persons caused by renewed fighting in Kajo-Keji County. Findings from these assessments highlight demand for life-saving humanitarian services notably; GBV response and prevention, WASH and shelter, food and nutrition and NFIs. Hot spots with protection concerns are; Jalimo, Bori, Ajio, Kerwa and Korijo with reported cases of rape and sexual violence exacerbated by both the warring parties and the cattle keepers in Kajo-Keji County. The REACH Humanitarian Situation Monitoring report in Kajo-Keji between April to September 2020 showed that the proportion of people who do not feel safe increased significantly from 6% in April to 74% likely as a result of insecurity that impedes people’s ability to access services. HRP funding will broaden the scope of GBV services to those who were displaced from Korijo camp in Logo, Kerwa and Kajo-Keji Central and complement BHA/OFDA funded GBV and WASH in Korijo where ARC is the main GBV service provider. Kapoeta South (KS) and Budi. KS and Budi are no different from the shocks of 2020 especially insecurity and violence, floods and COVID-19 pandemics. All these shocks have disproportionately affected vulnerable people including children, older persons, survivors of gender-based violence and conflict-related sexual violence, and people living with disabilities. REACH Humanitarian Situation Assessment conducted in April-September 2020 , Kapoeta South and Budi counties had the highest proportion of settlements with limited services due to remoteness. Kapoeta South and Budi are classified as severity level 3 and 2, so lack of response to underlying situation will revert the counties to 4 and 3. ARC is providing support to GBV survivors and those at risk through case management, PSS activities and other WGFS services and coordination of GBV Sub Cluster roles. Protection risks associated with firewood collection among women are common in this region highlighting the need for fuel efficient stoves to mitigate GBV risks. Cases of early child marriage are rampant among nomadic Topossa community. This calls for sustained awareness raising on GBV as a joint effort to dismantle retrogressive cultural norms and practices. Aweil Centre and Aweil West: ARC is currently implementing GBV response and prevention project funded by consortium of UNICEF and UNWOMEN in Aweil Centre. The project is ending on 30th of June 2021. ARC exiting in Aweil Centre will not only leave a gap in GBV service provision but also in coordinating GBV since ARC is the GBV Sub Cluster lead partner. HRP 2021 funds will be used to support all these gaps. Also, ARC foresees an opportunity to expand GBV services to Aweil West County where GBV services are unavailable and partners have to refer GBV survivors to Aweil centre in over 65KM to access services. The South Sudan 2021 HNO showed, Aweil West falls under GBV severity level 4 and therefore, if we do not support GBV services, it will increase protection risk factors to the affected population of Aweil West and it will degenerate the county to GBV severity 5 and this will be catastrophic level which we aim to prevent by the funding from HRP 2021. Ulang County: Ulang continues to receive returnees from Ethiopia and Malakal PoC. This adds to the needs caused by the 2020 flooding in Sobat River Corridor. From IRNA assessment October 2020, in Ulang County alone, 25,626 people have been displaced by the flooding with no facilities to support them. ARC's 5 women centres and 1 youth centre are overwhelmed todate due to increased need to receive services. ARC's GBV services funded by BHA/OFDA is however found in only 5 Payams out of 20 Payams in Ulang County, meaning majority of people do not have access to basic GBV services. The HRP 2021 funding will be used to upsacle services to other Payams

2 - Project activities

Cluster Objective 1: Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areasActivity 1.1 Setting up systems and procedures for case management in Aweil West ARC has on-going GBV response and prevention activities in all locations except in Aweil West where GBV project will be established. System set up will include; recruitment and training of Caseworkers, establishing private rooms for case management, procurement of necessary supplies printing tools needed for documenting survivor’s stories . Activity 1.2: Comprehensive case management and referrals. Survivor centered and confidential case management services to GBV survivors will be through individual and group counselling and consent based referrals. 200 survivors are targeted by this project. Referral pathways will be critical in supporting effective referrals of GBV survivors. To this end, ARC will work with referral pathway partners to ensure that a localized and updated pathways is in place. Activity 1.3 Staff and community frontline trained in case management 100 participants will be trained in case management by ARC with support from SSHF. Participants will be GBV staff and other frontline service providers in the referral pathways. ARC will use the staff already trained as Training of Trainers (ToTs) in case management to roll out this training/organize refresher trainings as needed at the County level. Activity 1.4: Establish, equip and operationalize one WGFS in Aweil West ARC will construct, equip ad operationalize one WGFS in Aweil West. WGFS activities will conform to UNICEF’s WGFS guidelines for South Sudan. Activity 1.5: Provide PSS for women and girls at the WGFS. WGFS will be an entry for women and girls to access other services. The main activities will include; case management, age appropriate information sessions, empowerment/skill building activities, and recreational activities among others. ARC will identify a fully dedicated WGFS facilitator from the beneficiaries to coordinate skill building and PSS activities so that caseworkers can fully focus on case management. This will also foster sustainability and ownership of WGFS activities by the community. 6,000 women and girls are expected to access WGFS services in all six locations (1000 per location). Cluster objective 2: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilienceActivity 2.1: Community sensitization and awareness raising on GBV. GBV prevention programming will involve awareness raising on services available and referrals. ARC will adopt different strategies in passing GBV key messages to the community such as house to house visits, use of posters and community conversations. 12,907 women, girls, boys and men will be reached with GBV messages through awareness raising. Activity 2.2: Distribute dignity kits ARC will procure, transport and distribute dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age. as a GBV risk mitigation measure. 540 dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable women and girls. The dignity kits contents will be guided by the GBV Sub Cluster. Activity 2.3: Conduct safety audit. ARC will lead joint routine safety audits with other sectors to strengthen integration and develop a holistic approach in responding to GBV. Safety audit findings will be shared with all partners with key actions for each sector. Activity 2.4: Train Community Mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning ARC targets to build the capacity of 82 community mobilizers on hot spot mapping, safety audits, disaster reduction and preparedness among other topics. Activity2.5: Training on fuel efficient stoves as a risk mitigation measure 50 women and girls will be trained and will be supported to cascade

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA: ARC staff have signed a code of conduct which includes zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse policy. ARC commits to continuously educate beneciaries on PSEA and other violations and to inform them on reporting mechanisms in place when such cases occur. WGFS are used as entry points for reporting PSEA cases for women and girls while County coordinators are another opportunities for boys nad girls to report the same. ARC believes that PSEA cases may not only affect women and girls but equally men and boys, for this case, an alternate reporting through the County Coordinators will help address single line reporting. ARC will support survivors of PSEA to access support in a manner that does not compromise their dignity and privacy and confidentiality.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Person-centred approaches and service user involvement are integral part of ARC's project implementation mechanism. ARC values special interest groups as an integral part of its beneficiaries and take proactive measure to ensure that they are involved in all stages of its project; from planning, designing, implementations, monitoring and evaluations. By so doing, we are contineously learning to serve them better and get feedback on how best they wish to be served. These continous engagements with the most vulnerable members has made it possible for them to approach ARC staff and register either complaints or vote of appreciations. We will thus build on experience we had in different locations to continually improve and make our services accessible to all categories. ARC also placed special attention to capacity building of local staff to ensure that beneficiaries are served by staff both male and females known to them, connected to them by belonging and language so that they can easily interact and register feedback in their native language. This facilitate feedback from the most vulnerable community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ARC's commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) focuses on the effective implementation of three long-held principles: 1) our customer is the IDP/host community/returnees, 2) IDPs/host communities/returnees define quality and 3) we collect feedback including complains and solve them alongside IDPs/host communities/returnees. These principles hold us accountable to improving the quality, relevance and impact of our work in the interest of beneciaries. To demonstrate ARC's commitment to AAP in practice, ARC will ensure strong accountability in its responses toward affected communities and individuals directly affected by disasters by involving them directly in program design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. ARC will use periodic focus group discussions (FGDs) and an ongoing feedback/complaint collections and response mechanism throughout this project to obtain feedback from beneciaries. Feedback from beneciaries will be analyzed from time to time and ARC will coordinate appropriate action internally and bilaterally with respective partners. Feedback/Compliant Response Mechanism: ARC places a strong emphasis on receiving feedback from the individuals and communities that we seek to serve in order to improve ownership and the quality of our programs. A feedback/complaints response mechanism that both receives inputs from beneciaries and regularly provides feedback which will be utilized during this project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback supported real -time adaptation of projects to the needs of their target groups and contexts, and accountability of programmes and service providers. ARC believed that the process of giving feedback empowers beneficiaries and make partners respond to the needs of the beneficiaries. Context continually changes and therefore the modality to deliver time specific and relevant responses are informed by use of the feedback mechanism. ARC therefore uses these beneficiaries feedback to make theri interventions relevant to the beneficiaries needs and adapt services that suit the needs of the beneficiaries. ARC believed in sympiotic relations between them and beneficiaries and for this relationship to be furthered, ARC must deliver services that respond to the real needs of the beneficiaries.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The quality, timely and inclusive protection (GBV) services is enshrined in Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Quality Assurance Tool that offers health care providers, facilities, and program planners and people who work with children and women a straightforward way to start, strengthen or expand post-GBV health, mental health, psychosocial services through the use of evidence-based standards. ARC adopts these standards while providing GBV services to ensure that it's services are; quality, timely and inclusive to the affected population. We attribute quality to include responding to real needs of the affected population. Given that we do not provide all the services that people might need, we transcend quality into other partners by training them on the quality assurance tool so that their service delivery is guided by adhering to the standards.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

GBV interventions will contribute towards reducing or decreasing proportion of population facing food insecurity by making women, girls, boys and men contribute towards agricultural activities and decision making process of how harvests at household levels are managed. Power is one of the most abused decision tool in South Sudan. This transcends to household level since the patriarchal system is so much embbeded in society management. ARC's GBV project placed importance in awareness raising to influence attitude change towards women and girls. By so doing, it will increase population participation in food production and decision on harvest management to improve house hold level of nutrient intake. This is important as it helps to decreased population facing IPC3, 4 and 5 as more people will partciapate in food production. It must be realized that, food production in South Sudan is left in the hands of women and children while men and boys are involved in wars and in some communities, cattle rastling. By involving these traditionally inactive population in agriculture will increase food production and reverse progression towards food insecurity in the Country to severity level 3,4 and 5

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

GBV is the most extreme manifestation of gender inequality and a fundamental human rights violation. Protracted crises can create and exacerbate many forms of GBV, having a devastating impact on all the sectors by reducing the capacity and productivity of survivors as a result of illness, injury, stigma and discrimination. By implementing GBV prevention and response activities, ARC integrates and collaborates with other sectors within its operational areas to ensure equitable access to; WASH, Nutrition and Shelter services while participating in local referral mechanisms to ensure that the population access holistic services within the environment. Being a long term protection agency, ARC take special interest in ensuring special interests like; Disable persons, children, elderly are given quotas in their programming to ensure that an affirmative action is in place to target them in service delivery. These categories are also linked to services to partner agencies within the referral mechanism in situation that ARC doesnot provide the services they are seeking to receive.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

When women are hurt, food insecurity and poverty prevail in many households. Eliminating gender-based violence (GBV) is crucial, not only because it violates human rights but also because it reinforces many contributing factors as an interview with FAO’s Deputy Director-General, Daniel Gustafson outlines on the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. Malnutrtion in South Sudan is linked to decision making process at house hold level. If women are empowered to make decisions at house hold level through the GBV awareness raising processes, resource allocation towards providing nutrient rich foods can help decrease acute malnutrition among children under 5. Gender-based violence (GBV) is pervasive and according to FAO, on average one in three women experience physical or sexual abuse in her lifetime. This is one of the most widespread human rights violations in the world, and it not only affects victims and survivors, but also their families and communities, as well as the peace and prosperity of nations at large. In humanitarian emergencies, food insecurity and poverty are increasing the prevalence of GBV, undermining our efforts to ensure household and national food security and nutrition. It is seriously impacting people’s physical health and emotional and mental well-being, as well as their ability to work and participate in community life. GBV affects primarily women and girls in their productive and reproductive years, compromising their capacity to be productive workers, earners and caregivers - re-enforcing the vicious cycle of poverty and jeopardizing agricultural productivity, food security and nutrition. Therefore, ARC's GBV project plays a central role in de-escalating acute malnutrition among children under 5 by empowering women and girls who have been left out in deceision ladder to contribute towards combacting global acute malnutrition in South Sudan
