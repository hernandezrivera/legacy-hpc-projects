Project Summary

Mercy Corps aims to strengthen our protection interventions using a gender sensitive lens to support the overall protection cluster objectives towards the provision of quality integrated protection services. Mercy Corps will focus the intervention on vulnerable adolescent girls and boys and young women utilizing community-based approaches taking into considerations the existing resources in the communities. Mercy Corps will expand on its commitment to integrating protection outcomes into other sectoral interventions including cash, food security and livelihoods, WASH and social cohesion. Mercy corps will implement projects through 2 different but complementary modalities : direct implementation and though local partners in north east Syria.. Mercy Corps projects aim to ensure vulnerable adolescents and young people exposed to acute and/or protracted crises are safe, protected and more resilient and have the capacity to address root causes of inequalities. This will be achieved through three pillars of integrated programming, 1) 1)Psychosocial interventions ( structured and semi structured), life skills and mentoring to improve positive coping, functioning, self-efficacy and well-being whilst being provided with opportunities in livelihood and resources including cash grants or cash assistance; 2) Individuals, couples, and community leaders are supported to unlearn harmful values and social norms and to adopt new values and attitudes and become more committed to equality and diversity 3) Adolescents and young people who are survivors and marginalized understand their rights, exercise self-efficacy and they will be linked to existing specialized services if needed in a timely manner. These interventions will complement existing sectoral programming where Mercy Corps or its partners are operating in and will not be standalone. Mercy Corps will also continue to ensure protection mainstreaming is embedded across all our activities and programming. Mercy Corps staff and partners are trained in protection and gender mainstreaming principles and approaches, protection risks analysis and Safeguarding/PSEA; Mercy Corps community accountability feedback and response mechanism are designed in consultation with communities and feedback received are use to track red flags; community awareness sessions to inform the communities of their rights and entitlements, complaints and feedback mechanism and the code of conduct of all individuals providing aid. 3 - Needs

Diverse and interlinked protection needs are widespread. Inadequate living conditions in NWS and NES and the loss of livelihoods, traditional safety nets, property, and assets compound GBV risks and child protection concerns. Protracted crisis has disrupted social & public services, strained community safety nets, and resulted in psychological distress. HNO estimates there are 15 million PIN of some form of food and agriculture assistance (7% increase from last year). Mercy Corps and HNO assessments show the conflict has depleted the population's resource and capacity for resilience and has increased harmful coping mechanisms including suicide and substance abuse. Depreciation of local currency, basic commodity inflation, and food & fuel shortages are expected to worsen, drive increased vulnerability, and lead to reliance on humanitarian assistance. Protection monitoring reports suggest child labor is a persistent concern, including recruiting children to combat/support. Girls and boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities are at higher risk of other forms of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. Psychosocial needs of girls & boys are also unmet. Voices of Syria 2021 reported that GBV remains a major protection risk and pervades the lives of women & girls, particularly domestic/family violence, early/forced marriage, and sexual violence and harassment. Protection monitoring reports indicate GBV response & prevention remains a need in Northern Syria.

4 - Implementation of activities

Interventions will target adolescents and young people and women and girls using an integrated approach. Activities will aim at enhancing their capacity and the protective environment. Programs will be designed in collaboration with girls, boys and young men and women in a participatory manner including PWDs. Monitoring will be conducted at the end of each cycle of interventions and baseline and endline (i.e. functioning and KAP) will be collected. Structured PSSl support, life skills and mentoring: Adolescents and young people who have experienced acute/chronic stress are more likely to experience symptoms of mental health and engage in negative coping strategies. Through PSS support they will be able to improve functioning, strengthen positive coping and build social connections. They will also have the opportunity to learn new skills and be supported by mentors and peers. Vulnerable adolescents and young people without opportunities are also more likely to feel disenfranchised and be recruited into fighting and exploitative labor conditions and young girls or women may be forced to marry or engage in risky behaviors. Hence, in addition to psychosocial support they will also have access to multipurpose cash and livelihood opportunities so as to promote resilience. Community based interventions addressing GBV and inequality: Through community based social behaviour change interventions Mercy Corps will work with girls, boys, women and men to unlearn harmful attitudes

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Interventions include different community actors increasing risks of SEA (high impact, medium likelihood). Mitigation: 1) on-boarding and training on PSEA and COC 2) community awareness on rights & entitlements, COC of aid workers 3) feedback & complaints mechanism designed in consultation with community 4) proper recruitment procedures 5) service mapping and safe referral are in place. Recruitment of children into armed groups (high impact, medium likelihood). Mitigation: 1) identify safe locations and timing in consultation with community 2) train staff to identify risks, refer to specialised actors 3) build capacity of adolescents & young people 4) monitor red flags 5) parents understand risks and red flags 6) regular mentor-parent check-ins. Mental health is highly stigmatized (High impact, medium likelihood) and poorly understood. Mitigation: 1) existing relationship within targeted community & community consulted 2) program uses wellbeing instead of mental health 3) program widely targets from different groups. Promotion of gender equality and rights of marginalised groups and changing gender roles casemay excerbate GBV (High impact, medium likelihood). Mitigations 1) updated gender analysis 2) consult M, W, B, G and marginalised on project design 3) ongoing monitoring of red flags through audits 4) strengthen feedback and complaints mechanism 5) staff trained on supporting survivors 6) SADD data collected and analysed.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive a mandatory orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to assess the risk their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are systematically trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Approval from local authorities (high impact, medium likelihood) Local authorities restrict activities due to lack of support for protection interventions or access issues. Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavor to support partners in their intervention methodology and engagement with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. Increased insecurity (high impact, medium likelihood) Situations in the northeast and remain volatile. Due to insecurity Mercy Corps might experience issues in accessing certain communities and lead to project delays. Mercy Corps security team and humanitarian access team to scenario plan and adjust the project and intervention to minimize impact and risks to affected communities. Covid-19 pandemic (high impact, high likelihood) Ongoing covid- 19 pandemic had resulted in delayed delivery of services and project adaption to ensure public health measures are observed. This is expected to continue in 2023.
