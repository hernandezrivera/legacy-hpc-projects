Project Summary

As per the 2023 MSNA report, 75% of shelters in NWS are classified as inadequate or damaged. The overall Shelter PiN has risen by 1%, reaching 2.1 million, over 800,000 living in tents and makeshift shelters (90% of which are more than 1 year old), illustrating the compounded effects of the challenging present circumstances and the earthquake on the region, particularly impacting the host communities, thus repair and rehabilitation efforts are crucial, alongside a multisectoral response to guarantee access and availability of basic services.
The severe winter months pose a significant humanitarian challenge in NWS. The majority of people reside in old tents in IDP sites that lack proper sewage, drainage, electricity, and water supply.
During winter, there is an increase in respiratory infection outbreaks, leading to a rise in hospitalizations, morbidity, and mortality. The absence of warm clothing, insulation, and heating further increases the risk of respiratory infections, hypothermia, and preventable deaths among children and the elderly. Access to essential items such as (NFIs) is crucial for survival. However, despite their importance, these items remain out of reach for many individuals due to affordability issues.
The project aims to strengthen shelter resilience for host communities, returnees, and internally displaced persons (IDPs) both inside and outside camps. This will be achieved by restoring essential infrastructure to mitigate flood risks, replacing aging tents, and providing alternative transitional shelters within camps. Outside camps, the focus is on rehabilitating damaged houses. Additionally, the project targets winter support to meet the needs of the population in camps and host communities, also prioritizing households most in need through NFI kits distribution based on cluster targeting criteria.
The primary objective of these interventions is to improve current shelter conditions and enhance the resilience of the targeted population against adverse weather conditions. This includes:
a. Provision of core NFIs.
b. Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs.
c. Installation of new/ replacement of existing tents.
d. Installation and/ or construction of temporary transitional shelters.
e. Implementation of site level improvements/ infrastructure.
f. Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing and capacity building of individuals to undertake this intervention.
g. Supply and installation of HH-level solar energy systems.
Actual needs evaluation will be conducted through a Community Level Assessment within the selected sub-districts, taking into account dynamic changes in the humanitarian context. Flexibility in intervention strategies will be maintained to ensure adaptation according to evolving needs. The project will prioritise the most vulnerable individuals, both within in-camp and off-camp settings.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Risks and Mitigation: 1- Risk: Overlap for some locations due to other NGOs’ intervention, loss of access. MS: Multilevel coordination will be implemented, involving coordination with stakeholders at both the cluster and field levels, particularly during the design phase. 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety conflicts. MS: Validate all relevant documents throughout the project duration and maintain clear communication with all stakeholders, including camp management committees, LCs, and KIs, particularly in the event of any conflicts arising.3- Risk: certain beneficiaries may be displaced due to prevailing conditions on the ground. MS: ATAA will monitor all updates on the ground if any of the beneficiaries are displaced, we will choose alternative beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the exact location. 4- Risk: misuse of cash by beneficiariesMS: customizing the cash assistance program to align with identified needs. Additionally, targeted sites will be chosen through gap analysis, identifying them as high-need areas. During the community engagement FGDs the team will refer to the purpose of this cash assistance and the related risks of misuse to raise the awareness of the targeted community regarding this point. ATAA employs a scientific risk management approach by developing a risk matrix during project design that considers all potential hazards and mitigation measures. As part of the management of this risk, the S&S department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects and building a trust relationship with the local authorities by clarifying for the local authorities the impact of the project on the region. Due to the unpredictable nature of conflicts, such as shelling or armed clashes, the S&S department closely monitors the project area, conducts daily risk assessments, and communicates potential scenarios and appropriate responses to the crisis cell. In addition, the department updates evacuation and contingency plans and provides field staff with training on expected scenarios and adherence to approved SOPs for a secure operational environment. ATAA is considering the PSEA in the top of priorities by active membership and participation in PSEA networks activities, and developed internal framework and control system to enhance PSEA including all the core standards, in addition to include the prevention measures in all the humanitarian interventions of ATAA.Additionally, ATAA created a solid response system starts from the active and accessible reporting channels, and investigation planning and procedures with qualified and trained staff and network of the focal points with gender balance to enhance access to survivors and provide the response interventions based on survivor based approach.Ataa is updating PSEA assessments with partners and received a UNICEF assessment result as full capacity low risk partner since mid of 2023.
