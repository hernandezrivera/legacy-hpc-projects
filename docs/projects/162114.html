Project Summary

This project will be focused to enhance disaster resilience at camp and host community level through access and site development/renovation work like construction of new infrastructural facilities e.g. road, canal and bridge and renovation of cyclone shelter in Baherchara & Palongkhali Union at Ukhia Upazilla in Cox’s Bazar district. Capacity development in term of disaster preparedness and response of the targeted Rohingya and host population is also another focused area of this project. In this regard, training will be provided with Refugee Food Management Committees (RFMC) and Block Development Committees in Camp level and Union Disaster Management Committee and community leaders at host community level. Refugee Food Management Committee (RFMC) a people focused, participatory and representative beneficiary committees at camp level, which will ensure that WFP programme activities are truly accountable to the affected population- registered refugees and Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs). RFMCs are formed at camp level to promote beneficiary ownership, participation in project cycle management and transparency of WFP activity implementation at camp level. The project is planning to arrange different types of awareness meetings, orientation, training for RFMC and Rohingya Refugees community to build awareness on early warning and disaster preparedness. As part of the cyclone preparedness measures mock drill will be organized for each ward and proposed camp. During the Mock Drill people will know about the scenario of how the camp looks like and how people react during a disaster and how people react, respond, adapt and recover from an emergency. Billboards with messages and awareness on early warning and preparedness will be installed at ward levels. CPP volunteers will provided early warning message such as first aid, fair safety etc. CPP volunteers will be provided with visual IEC materials for wider dissemination in the camps and ward catchment.
Cyclone shelters located in host communities at Palongkhali and Raja Palong union in Ukhia Upazila and not surrounded the camp area. The project will target 5 community structures reinforced and rehabilitated as cyclone shelters under 5 CfW schemes, focus will be to strengthen the early warning systems towards cyclones (rainfall/wind).
Site selection, beneficiary selection, approvals will be done in close consultation with the CiCs and SMS agencies and other SD actors to avoid overlapping and targeting areas most vulnerable. The project will also be represented in weekly camp-level coordination meetings and regular 3W/4W reporting will be maintained. CAID will remain as the SMS agency for camps 14 and 5 till December 15th and over the last 2 years the organization have developed close linkage and working relationship with the camp actors, sector leads and CiC/ACiC. Over the last 10 months CAID has also implemented DRR activities in 10 camps.
Beneficiary selection will be based on agreed vulnerability criteria such as women headed households, family with PWD, family with elderly and chronically ill people. Community orientation on selection criteria and selection process will be conducted to encourage ownership and participation. Recognizing the challenges of vulnerable groups such as PWD and elderly, lighter responsibilities will be given to them for cash for work activities. CAID is committed to ensuring that the Core Humanitarian Standards are adhered to throughout the project cycle. Communities will have access to safe and responsive complaints and feedback mechanism consisting of feedback boxes in each targeted block, help desk at distribution sites and one-to-one consultation with beneficiaries.
CAID also has in place several policies and procedures to prevent, respond and tackle exploitation, harassment and abuse. These include Diversity and Inclusion, Safeguarding, PSEA, Whistleblowing, Information Security, Data Protection, Misuse of Funds and Anti-Bribery.

Objectives

Improve the settlement areas and their immediate surroundings, through community-participatory and mid-term macro planning, linked to construction and maintenance works that enable safe, dignified and resilient living conditions ensuring access is for women, girls and children.

Needs

The people who are living in the camps-14,15 & 16 and the adjacent host community with limited infrastructural facilities and find it difficult to communicate with other blocks and accessing relief distribution points. The only way to move around in the camp is by foot. This has limited the ease of assistance, hence the vulnerability here is more serious than in the other camps. More than 50% of the residents are under 18, around 77% are women and children and 11% households have members with disabilities according to round-5 of the REACH Camp Settlement and Protection Profile (July, 2019). There is still a lot that needs to be done here, such as improved pathways and roads, and natural disaster warning system being 2 of the top five areas of need. In camp 15 and 16 there are 11,174 and 4,839 families respectively, amongst which almost 80% of individuals are women and children. For both camps, top 10 most commonly reported protection concerns were natural disaster, animal attack, children getting lost, site problems, lack of documentation, movement restriction, crime, human trafficking and harassment.

Activities or outputs

• Rehabilitation of 3000 meters of access roads and footpaths under 15 cash for work schemes in the camp level and the host community for accessible cyclone centres.• Construction of 2000 meters drainage of 10 CfW schemes.• Improve & rehabilitation of 1000 sqm of 10 brick stairs & pathways in the camp level and host community.• 10 bamboo bridges will be constructed to improve access to the distribution points. These are bridges connecting sub-blocks with each other and will in addition make it possible to shorten the distance between blocks.• 5 slope stabilisation with tree plantation, 10000 square meters is at the higher end.• Repair and Renovation 3 existing Cyclon Shelters with Access Road/ ramp/drainage/ Toilette (need based).• 20 orientation and training for Refugee Food Management Committees (RFMC).• 7 DRR training sessions for CPP, SMC and UDMC.
