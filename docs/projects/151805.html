Needs

Loss of property and security of tenure are common consequences of displacement and major factors in exposing families to risk. The lack of shelter constitutes one of the top-three reasons that refugees list for not returning to Somalia. The provision of shelter is, therefore, essential to anchoring and sustaining return and reintegration in Somalia. Returnees need adequate, dignified, safe and secure shelter with lockable doors and security of tenure. However, they are frequently constrained by their limited resources, capacities, and skills to construct or undertake desired improvements to existing dwellings. Rental arrangements are often used by returnees where no adequate immediate transitional or permanent shelter is available. Expenditures on rent prevent families from meeting other needs such as education or health. In parallel, land tenure is a key source of insecurity for returnees, and for members of receiving communities. Land tenure is also a requirement to protect returnees from forced evictions which have become a regular feature in IDP and other urban settlements, particularly in southern and central regions. According to shelter cluster data from the recent JMCNA about 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents, or under trees. Therefore out of the estimated 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia, 2.3 million can be concluded to be in need of humanitarian shelter assistance. As of August 2018, UNHCR statistic indicates that over 121,000 people have voluntarily retuned from ten countries of asylum since 2014. Approximately 738,000 persons in Somaliland, 100,000 persons in Puntland and Galmudug, and 544,310 persons in South Central Somalia are in need of core relief items and other sanitary items.

Activities

Construction of new permanent Shelters and infrastructure established, improved and maintained to support reintegration of returnees, IDPs and host communities. For returnees, UNHCR plans to construct about 1700 permanent shelter and will explore the provision of cash for shelters and encourage owner-driven approach where applicable. In line with the Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF) approach, UNHCR will aim at developing integrated settlements together with a wide range of partners, including government agencies, other UN agencies and humanitarian partners, as well as the beneficiaries. The integrated settlement will include shelter, a school, a health center, a police post and a market/ communal area. UNHCR will construct permanent shelters in return areas, while the operational partners will construct the structures to provide the other services. Together with the partner organizations, UNHCR will advocate with the government to acquire land for the returnees. Provision of rental subsidy to 500 households in Mogadishu will be piloted as a learning process. Due to limited funding and number of returnees, shelter interventions in Somaliland will remain limited to the provision of conditional cash grant for shelter to a small number of returnees, based on vulnerability. Given the long drought situation and often the protracted nature of displacement, provision of basic and domestic items remains a priority. Most of the population, especially women in the reproductive age and lactating mothers, do not have sufficient income that could enable them to get the necessary support. It is also evident that women and girls in the reproductive age are not able to buy sanitary towels. UNHCR Somalia will support approximately 35,000 households with core relief items, either in kind or cash. In addition, UNHCR will support approximately 25,000 women and girls of reproductive age with sanitary kits.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

UNHCR, government and partners are seeking to create bridges to bring displacement on to the development agenda and find permanent solutions through local integration. In line with the Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF) approach, UNHCR will develop an integrated settlement together with a wide range of partners, including government agencies, other UN agencies and humanitarian partners, as well as the beneficiaries. The integrated settlement will include shelter, a school, a health center, a police post and a market/ communal area. UNHCR will construct permanent shelters in return areas, while the operational partners will construct the structures to provide the other services. Together with the partner organizations, UNHCR will advocate with the government to acquire land for the returnees. UNHCR through its implementing partners will continue to provide permanent shelter to facilitate their reintegration. The Ministries will be responsible to take over the responsibility of the integrated shelter.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Forceful evictions remain one of the most severe and prevalent protection concerns throughout Somalia, particularly for IDPs and poor households, due to an underdeveloped regulatory framework, hampered access to justice, low awareness of and respect for HLP rights and a weak protective environment. Both government and private land owners contribute to the forceful evictions of thousands of displacement communities. Families are often displaced without substantial household items. UNHCR aims at countering evictions through different activities. Among others, UNHCR will undertake awareness raising on relevant HLP laws, to ensure that IDPs are aware of their housing, land and property (HLP) rights. UNHCR will also advocate with government for non-discriminatory application of HLP laws in coordination with partners/clusters. Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration. Awareness raising amongst host communities and IDPs will take place to promote peaceful coexistence, in coordination with peaceful coexistence activities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The selection criteria for shelter, CRI and NFI assistance will take into consideration the vulnerabilities and specific needs of the beneficiaries. Among others, Female-headed households with Specific Needs, Persons with Chronic disease and elderly persons are prioritized. The most vulnerable Returnees and IDPs will be identified through collaboration with Returnees and IDP committees and provided with permanent shelters and support for sustainable livelihoods and basic services. Women in the reproductive age and lactating mothers do not have sufficient income that could enable them to get the necessary support. It is also evident that women and girls in the reproductive age are not able to buy sanitary towels. UNHCR will support approximately 25,000 women and girls of reproductive age with sanitary kits. UNHCR will continue to advocate and coordinate with other shelter/NFI cluster members to support in filling the gaps.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

• Displaced women, men, girls and boys in Somalia remain victims of various protection incidents and other human rights violations. Violence is occurring in places of refuge and in areas which are controlled by non-state actors and which are not accessible by humanitarian actors. Continuous displacement and lack of livelihoods has also reduced the capacities of these uprooted communities to access humanitarian and medical aid. • UNHCR will continue to monitor closely all protection incidents occurred within the context of forced displacement in Somalia. By advocating and putting in place response systems, every protection incident should get a referral or direct assistance. Additionally, the information gathered through a thorough monitoring system feeds into the wider system to better analyze main protection incidents in the country and better tailor programming to needs. • Influenced by the HCT’s Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will pilot monitoring in areas which are occupied by armed non-state actors. Especially in South West State and Jubbaland State, individuals will be identified with strong connections in areas which are currently not included in humanitarian programming and assessments. Expected is that these Independent Monitors will feed protection related information to UNHCR and the wider humanitarian community on specific protection needs among these communities.
