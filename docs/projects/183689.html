Project Summary

In early 2021, Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children (ASDC) conducted a needs assessment on inclusion barriers to assess the situation in public and the private schools in Gaza regarding inclusion and the effects of COVID-19. (ASDC) will implement the project with 4 partners in 4 governorates in the Gaza Strip: Bait Al-Mostqbal, Al-Ataa Charity Association, Union Health Work Committee (UHWC), and SOS Children’s Villages Palestine. The target group will be as the following: 500 (6-17 years) children with and without disabilities (250 females, 250 males 20% with disabilities and 80% without disabilities), 200 teachers, counselors and principals, 500 caregivers; where the needs are as the following:
1- 500 children are in need for education intervention: dropout or at risk of dropout in the 5 governorates. 100 children per governorate will benefited from the intervention with coordination with the 4 partners as ASDC will be the lead and implement the activities in the Gaza Governorate.
2- 200 CWDs (50%F, 50%M) will benefited from distribution of assistive devices to facilitate their daily life. ASDC will be responsible of distributing the different types assistive devices to the children with coordination with the partners according to selection criteria such as wheel chairs, hear aids, auxiliary crutches and others devices.
3- 500 (50% F, 50% M, 20% with disabilities, 80% without disabilities) children are in need of MHPSS intervention
4- 500 caregivers (50% F, 50% M) need to know and implement the right methods to support their children
5- 200 teachers, counselors and principals need to acknowledge and identified about PSEA guidelines
The proposed intervention takes into consideration gender and age disaggregation in all its activities where 1200 (500 children with and without disabilities, 500 caregivers and 200 teachers, counselors and principals) beneficiaries will benefited from COVID-19 precautionary measures.
The training about PSEA guidelines for schools' staff will be conducted in the first quarter of the project duration in order to follow-up with the process of implementation and to enhance any needed procedures.
The project intervention will be under the Strategic Objectives of Education #1 Strengthen advocacy and reporting on education-related violations across Gaza and the West Bank and ensure that school staff and students affected by the education-related violations and COVID-19 pandemic benefit from protective environment, #2 Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and #3 Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies

1 - Needs

Gaza Strip has been under blockade since June 2007, where the on-going blockade has caused irreversible damage to the Gaza Strip’s economy and overall deterioration of social determinants of wellbeing. According to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) almost 44.5 percent of elementary-school-age children (6 to 9 years old) with disabilities have never enrolled in school. The prevalence of children with disabilities (CWDs) aged 6 years and over currently enrolled in school is 54.2 percent for elementary-school-age children and 57.8 percent for children of preparatory-school-age (10 to 15 years old). An estimated 579,173 Palestinians (56% females) across the oPt, the vast majority (96%) of whom are school-age children including around 13,398 CWDs will need of humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022, 74 per cent live in the Gaza Strip.The Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR) estimates that some 678,000 children (345,800 boys ad 332,200 girls) across the oPt are in need of child protection and MHPSS services , including 122,0007 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. More than half (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need, and 12% of all children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Additionally, 185,000 caregivers overall, including 137,000 caregivers in Gaza, are also in need of various MHPSS services. Many CWDs and resource room students, and children in preschools were excluded from online education However, major capacity gaps in school-based psychosocial support (PSS), particularly in the most exposed areas, have prevented the educational system from properly addressing the impact. This has resulted in an extra burden on teachers, who are often untrained to deliver PSS, and has increased the risk of long-term mental health disorders among affected children. 25% of the HHs with school-aged children interviewed as part of the MSNA (WB 24.3 % and Gaza 25.4 %) reported that PSS was not available at the school for children.19% of HHs interviewed in the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) with at least one CWDs reported at least one CWDs was not attending school 25% in the Gaza Strip. For the rest of the children enrolled in education, they face higher risk of dropout, due to gaps in adapted facilities and assistive devices at schools, adequate transportation means, and staff capacity to accommodate for their needs; 61% of the HHs in MSNA reported that their CWDs is facing challenges in accessing education services 71% in Gaza Strip. In early 2021, Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children (ASDC) conducted a needs assessment on inclusion barriers to assess the situation in public and the private schools in Gaza regarding inclusion and the effects of COVID-19. The results show that 64.6% of children with disabilities did not have access to quality education. 52.3% of teachers reported lack of skills and knowledge to deal with learners with disabilities. 66.4% of the parents reported that CWDs could not access the free education in public schools because the schools were not adapted to include children with different kinds of disabilities (physical, hearing, visual, or intellectual). As a result they had to enroll their children in specialized private schools for children with disabilities with very expensive fees. Apart from this, 53.3 % of the CWDs did not have the assistive devices needed for inclusive education. The assessment also showed a large gap in community awareness about the rights of CWDs to inclusive education. 71% of parents and 72,7% of teachers believed that it would be better for the CWDs to be in segregated, special schools. 83.2% of the children with disabilities reported about bullying at schools or in the streets. An assessment conducted by ASDC in March 2020 on the right of children to protection shows that 19% of children aged 6-17 years old reported that they did not receive medical care when they needed it, while this percentage increased to 36% for the CWDs. 26% of the children reported lack of school counselling support, while this percentage increased to 40% for CWDs. COVID-19 suspended educational activities in Gaza in March 2020 and shifted to online learning without a concrete strategy and the needed support infrastructure for online learning. However, ASDC’s assessment showed that 53.3% of the families do not have laptops or smart devices, 54.2% of families do not have access to quality internet connection for online learning and 73.8% lack support for their children’s online learning which increased to 91.6% during COVID-19. In addition, parents (87.9%) reported that their children need remedial classes to overcome the obstacles of online learning while the majority of children with disabilities (96.2%) prefer face-to-face learning or blended learning modalities (a combination of online learning and face to-face learning).According to statistic 2019 of Ministry of Education (MoE) about the dropout in schools in Rafah and West and East of Gaza as the target group was children (from 9-15 years) it shows: 18% were females while 82% are males. According to Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), Education Cluster "For the academic year 2021-2022, the Government has decided to open schools with full face- to -face learning, but it’s unclear if this decision will hold as the cases of COVID 19 have spiked in September 2021. In the previous academic year 2020-2021, 1.3 million children have lost at least three months of schooling and have not been able to engage in summer and extracurricular activities"The proposed intervention will be implemented with 4 NGOs and CBOs in Rafah, Khanyounis, Middle area, and North Governorates as ASDC will be the lead-applicant in the Gaza Governorate.
