Project Summary

Amidst the ongoing Anglophone Crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon, the humanitarian fallout has been particularly harsh on education. Schools have become direct targets, suffering attacks, kidnappings, and military occupations, leading to widespread closures since 2017. Despite these challenges, a positive turn has been noted in the 2022-2023 academic year, witnessing the opening of 884 new schools, totaling 3013 operational schools that can accommodate 54% of the expected 771,186 students.
However, the crisis has taken a severe toll in regions like Bui, where separatists have prohibited government schools, permitting only Community-based education schools to operate. This situation has compelled some children to join armed groups, exposing them to abuse and exploitation, necessitating urgent psychosocial support. While urban areas have seen increased school attendance, a recent assessment by ACOHOF revealed that remote communities in Bui and Donga-Matung Divisions have been neglected, leaving crisis-affected children without adequate education and psychosocial support.
In response to this dire situation, our intervention aligns with the Education Cluster strategy objectives and UNSDG4, concentrating on 80 targeted schools and community learning centers in Bui and Donga Matung Division. The primary goals include providing educational materials for 60 community schools and delivering psychosocial support services to the same number in these two divisions.
To achieve these objectives, our strategy involves:
Training PTA, Teachers/Facilitators: Conducting training sessions on school safety, child safeguarding, disaster risk reduction, and crisis mitigation for teachers and facilitators in selected schools and community learning spaces.
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support: Offering mental health and psychosocial support services to crisis-affected children and youths in the targeted community learning centers and schools, integrating referral mechanisms for specialized services in collaboration with child protection and MHPSS task teams.
Recreational and Didactic Material Support: Providing recreational materials for crisis-affected children in 60 schools and delivering didactic material support to 60 community schools in Bui and Donga Matung Division, addressing the critical shortage of learning aids.
The selection criteria for beneficiary schools prioritize the most vulnerable, those unreached by previous assistance, remote schools, and those facing acute challenges. Our multifaceted approach, encompassing teacher training, mental health support, and material aid, seeks not only to reopen schools but to revitalize education and foster resilience among the young minds affected by the crisis. Your support can make a lasting impact on the future of these children and the broader community.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The project is designed to address critical and multifaceted needs arising from the Anglophone Crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon, with a specific focus on education and protection. The assessed needs encompass the following key areas: Educational Gap: The prolonged crisis has severely impacted education in the region, with schools being direct targets of attacks, kidnappings, and military occupations. The majority of educational institutions have been closed since 2017, resulting in a significant gap in the provision of quality education for the affected children. The project aims to address this educational gap by focusing on 60 targeted schools and community learning centers in Bui and Donga Matung Division. Psychosocial Support: The crisis has forced some children into armed groups, exposing them to abuse, exploitation, and witnessing atrocities. Urgent psychosocial support is needed to address the emotional and psychological trauma experienced by these crisis-affected children and youths. The project includes the provision of mental health and psychosocial support services in the targeted community learning centers and schools, integrating referral mechanisms for specialized services in collaboration with child protection and mental health task teams. Access to Education in Remote Communities: While recent studies indicate increased school attendance in urban areas, remote communities in Bui and Donga-Matung Divisions have been overlooked. The assessment by ACOHOF revealed that crisis-affected children in these areas lack adequate education and psychosocial support. The project aims to bridge this gap by specifically targeting remote schools, ensuring that even those in the most challenging and isolated locations receive the necessary support. Material Aid Shortage: The crisis has led to a critical shortage of learning aids and materials in schools, hindering the quality of education provided. The project addresses this by providing recreational materials for crisis-affected children in 60 schools and delivering didactic material support to 60 community schools in Bui and Donga Matung Division. The assessed needs underline the urgency and complexity of the situation, necessitating a comprehensive approach that combines teacher training, mental health support, and material aid. By focusing on the identified needs, the project aims not only to reopen schools but to revitalize education and foster resilience among the young minds affected by the crisis. The selection criteria for beneficiary schools prioritize the most vulnerable, those unreached by previous assistance, remote schools, and those facing acute challenges, ensuring that the project effectively targets those in greatest need.
