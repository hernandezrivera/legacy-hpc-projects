Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to strengthen the resilience of conflict-affected women and girls, promote their socio-economic recovery, and contribute to stabilizing targeted communities in the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states of Northeast Nigeria. The project will reach approximately 10,000 beneficiaries with the majority being women and girls (70%) who will benefit from a combination of activities that will focus on promoting their access to livelihood and economic opportunities that will enhance their recovery; alleviate the risks and impacts of GBV them and enhance their participation in recovery processes including, conflict resolution and reconciliation. Approximately 3,000 men and boys will benefit directly through enhanced awareness about the benefits of promoting women’s rights, gender equality and empowerment of women, and participation in initiatives to promote peaceful co-existence and social cohesion.
The renewed large-scale brutal Boko Haram attacks in Yobe state during April and May 2021 had occurred after a relatively long period of stability in the affected communities, where many displaced people, persuaded by the perceived improvement of the security situation (among the state government authorities, including the security sector, humanitarian actors, and the general population) had returned and resettled. Thus, the repeated attacks conducted by NSAG, which triggered another mass displacement of civilians and large-scale destruction of private and public property, as well as critical infrastructure, could not have been predicted. As a result of the renewed attacks, however, many people who had returned to communities in some 11 LGAs across the northern part of Yobe State, have had to flee again to seek protection and shelter in other parts of the state. In Borno State also, the Government’s initiative to relocate IDPs from the camps to their original communities was reversed because of the unexpected increase in the attacks conducted by the NSAG in the areas of return, with the returnees fleeing to safer locations within the state and to locations the neighboring states of Bauchi and Adamawa.
Due the unanticipated large influx of IDPs, the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian organizations operating in Northeast Nigeria have struggled to provide adequate basic services and protection to the increasing needs of the affected population, particularity female headed households. Further, the influx of IDPs into unprepared and under resourced host communities continues to add pressure on resources and infrastructure in these communities thus leading to tension and rivalry between the groups. Inevitably, in a context where the government and humanitarian partners struggle to provide minimum humanitarian response standards, it is the most vulnerable, especially women and girls, who are suffering the most, due to their increased care burden and exposure to the risks of sexual and gender-based violence, and the inadequate prioritization of their gender-specific needs by the humanitarian actors. Pre-existing gender inequality exacerbates the effects of these shocks and stresses on women, particularly female headed households, single, and elderly women.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe) States of Northeast Nigeria reached a new level of escalation resulting from a significant increase in brutal attacks conducted by the armed insurgents on several LGAs that were previously experiencing relative safety both in Yobe and Borno States since April 2021. According to available statistics, about 200,000 of the current IDPs in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa were displaced by the recent attacks in 2021, with most of them (80%) being women and children, who have experienced multiple displacement . Cumulatively, the conflict has killed more than 35,000 people and displaced over 3.3 million persons, including 2.2 million internally displaced in the Northeast , thus creating one of the largest humanitarian crises currently in the whole world.The humanitarian situation has consequently deteriorated further since April 2021, relative to 2020, with the current situation being characterized by extreme levels of food insecurity, rising violence and insecurity, heightened protection risks, and the loss of livelihood opportunities, especially for women and girls. Compounding the impact of the conflict, is the ongoing COVID 19 pandemic, which has limited mobility, disrupted household livelihood, and created socio-economic stress. Findings of household-level data collected during the 2020 MSNA6 indicated that 37% mentioned cash income or livelihoods. The livelihood status of most household has depreciated and there is an urgent need for interventions. Women and children, who constitute most of the displaced population are the hardest hit. As many families have been separated, women and girls have become heads of households in a context where men and boys are conscripted or killed by Boko Haram (BH) and detained by the state security forces. In addition to their care burden, women and girls in displacement are exposed to heightened protection risks, and many of them have suffered various form of sexual and gender-based violence, including rape, abduction, and child marriage. Female headed household, and adolescent girls, are extremely vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse including survival sex, early marriage and forced prostitution. While gender-based has been a significant problem in Northeast Nigeria, with a prevalence rate of 30% as compared to 28% at the national level , the ongoing conflict has significant worsened the situation as available statistics estimate that every 6 out of 10 females have reportedly experienced one or more forms of gender-based violence in the region during period of the conflict. The crisis in Northeast is occurring within a context of deep existing structural gender inequalities that promotes negative masculinity and the subordination of women and girls by their male folks. Prior to the crisis, the situation for women and girls particularly in the Northeast, was poor. Globally, Nigeria ranks 152 out of 187 in the Human Development Index (HDI), which is well below the average for sub-Saharan Africa. In the northeast, women tend to become mothers in their teens, at 17 to 19 years of age, only 35 per cent of adolescent girls are literate and early/forced marriage is pervasive. These pre-existing gender inequalities are further exacerbated by the conflict and have increased women and girls’ vulnerability to gender-based violence; and the widespread of gender-based has been worsened by the limited access to services, disruption of livelihood as well as harmful social and cultural practices.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection needs remain at the forefront of the Nigeria humanitarian response. Women and girls are mostly targeted with rape, abduction, and sexual slavery, while men and boys are mostly targeted for recruitment by non-state armed groups and are at higher risk of being killed and arbitrarily detained. Gender based violence (GBV) including sexual violence as well as forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity, and poor living conditions in IDP camps and host communities. Breakdown of livelihoods, restriction of movement, and insecurity have resulted in an increase in negative coping mechanisms such as survival sex, and child marriage. Due to gendered roles, men and male youth who have suffered/witnessed trauma have little space to express their struggles and obtain appropriate support (e.g., men/male youth groups/ men friendly spaces). This project will ensure protection issues are well mainstreamed through the following. Engage with and provide income generating activities to women and provide male support groups with focus on promoting positive masculinities. Establish and maintain peace huts to address women’s and girls’ protection, psychosocial and livelihood needs. Provide orientation/training to project staff, police and military on protection, gender, GBV and PSEA awareness. Work closely with other sectors to design and implement intervention that addresses the barriers to access to services for girls, boys, women and men and other vulnerable population

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UN Women will work with community members including women, men, girls, and boys, particularly adolescent female-headed households, and people with disabilities to establish gender-responsive and social inclusive multi-compliant mechanisms in project target locations. The established complaint channels will provide women and girls, especially female adolescent-headed households, and people with disabilities safe channel to raise complaints about the quality of program and or staff behavior including volunteers. UN Women and partners shall work with the project team, beneficiaries, partners, Protection Early recovery Sectors and local actors like Volunteers and community support groups to develop a combination of feedback mechanism, starting with the simple ones such as simple forms for staff and volunteers to record key feedback and program complaints heard from community members during the early stage of a response. Stationed desk or designated places with focal persons will be established considering gender and diversity factors. All focal points will be trained in gender and PSEA.
