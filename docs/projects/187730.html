Project Summary

The project aims to support the primary health services in NWS, through this project,
the following health facilities will be supported:
- Orient medical complex (PHC+ physical rehabilitation center)-Idleb-Idleb-Idleb. Code:
G0700050. Target= 20,534, male=10,420, female=10,115, IDP= 7287, host= 13,247.
- Orient radiology center- Idleb-Idleb-Idleb. Code: G0700099. Target= 7,956, male=4,037, female=3,919, IDP= 2,823, host= 5,133.
- Orient mobile clinic-Idleb-Idleb-Idleb. Code: G0705057. Target= 8,395, male=4,260, female=4,135, IDP= 2.979, host= 5,416.
The project aims to Sustain and increase access to essential health services in the implementation areas. including routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal, and child health services, non-communicable diseases, infectious diseases, and advanced radiology services, prioritizing the most vulnerable groups: children under 5 years, women of reproductive age (15 – 49 years), people living with disability, persons over 50 years of age with NCDs and/or elderly over 65, internally displaced personnel IDPs and returnees.
All the project activities will be fully coordinated with the health cluster, Idleb health directorate, and other health partners in the area, to ensure the optimal coverage of the needs without any duplication or overlapping, for example, most of the service provided by Orient radiology center will be provided to patients referred from other health facilities, through the referral network.
the project will also include capacity building component, by training the health care providers on GBV first-line response including, ensuring the safe identification and referral for GBV cases to the specialized service providers in the area, such as Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), GBV in emergencies, GBV referral. in addition to training on infection protection and control.

4 - Needs

A decade into the Syrian crisis, factors driving humanitarian needs among Syria’s population have multiplied, according to 2022-23 HNO Syria, 12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022, 4.4 million of them are displaced persons, in addition to 1.33 million children under 5 years, including 503,000 live births expected in 2022, and while non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are estimated to account for 45 percent of all mortality in Syria, the number of older people that require inclusive health services is more than 500,000. Both the physical and mental wellbeing of the Syrian population continues to be affected by the availability and quality of health services across Syria, due to the chronic challenge of the fragile health system in Syria, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems, with very low levels of COVID-19 vaccination, lack of adherence to preventive public health measures, and the emerging variants, with more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths across Syria. According to the 2022-23 HRP Health Sector Chapter draft, Idleb is the second governorate with the highest number of people in need of health care services, with 2,073,823 PIN, while the population of Idleb subdistrict, where the project will be implemented, is 295,287, with a severity indicator is 3, meaning that 60% of the population or 177,172 are in need of health care services.

5 - Implementation of activities

Orient medical complex is already up and running, all the staff required to provide the health care services are hired, primary health care service will be integrated with additional services provided in the center, including physical rehabilitation services, mental health, and nutrition services throughout facility-based lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, and treatment of acutely malnourished U5 children, and PLW’s, integrated with IYCF services, and community-based integrated nutrition, health, and protection services throughout 10 RRT’s, in addition to the advanced diagnostic services provided by Orient radiology center, to increase the accessibility of beneficiaries, a mobile clinic will be also linked with the center. All Orient facilities will be provided with a triage system, to minimize the risk of infection for staff and beneficiaries, in addition to strict IPCmeasurement and distribution of PPEs to all the staff. Monitoring will be conducted at every stage of the program through the collection and analysis of routine data on a continuous basis throughout the life of the program. Data collection tools are appropriate and tailored to the program's needs. A feedback mechanism will be established to affirm that all beneficiaries have the right to complain and provide communication channels to the communities to voice their suggestions and provide their feedback about our intervention.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Orient will report any attack on health facility, and support any efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. -Gender-Based Violence GBV: Hiring of female staff to increase access to health services for women and girls, training gynecologists, midwives, and nurses on the clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBVcases, and safe referrals, and linking the health facilities with the social services to ensure safe referral pathways between both of them. -Protection from Sexual Exploitation and abuse PSEA: Orient is an active member in the WoS PSEA network, all the staff have been trained on PSEA in all health facilities, all the recruitment process includes PSEA in the criteria of selection and all the staff should sign Orient code of conduct, and PSEApolicy. -COVID19 spread among health workers as well as among beneficiaries: All Orientfacilities have been provided with triage tents and other infection prevention and control measures, in addition, all the staff has been trained on the IPC, Orient will insure to provide PPE equipment to all the staff.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Orient is an active member in the WoS PSEA network, all the staff has been trained on PSEA on all health facilities, all the recruitment process includes SEA screening the criteria of selection and all the staff should sign Orient code of conduct and PSEA policy, in addition, information on PSEA policy and procedure, including the code of conduct and reporting channel details, will be displayed in all offices and at project sites. All the contracts and partnership agreements will include a standard clause requiring contractors, suppliers, consultants, and sub-partners to commit to a zero-tolerance policy on SEA and to take measures to prevent and respond to SEA. A systematic vetting procedure will be in place for job candidates (e.g. reference checks, police records, Google searches) in accordance with local laws regarding employment, privacy, and data protection, including checking for prior involvement in SEA or other safeguarding concerns. Mandatory training for all personnel on the organization’s SEA policy and procedures will be held every 6 months, including a definition of SEA (that is aligned with the UN's definition); 2) a prohibition of SEA, and 3) actions that personnel is required to take (i.e. prompt reporting of allegations and referral of survivors). Mechanism and procedures for personnel, beneficiaries, and communities, including children, to report SEA allegations that comply with core standards for reporting.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Changing front lines, changes in the parties controlling the implementation areas, and significant population movements as they flee shifting conflict areas: Orient regularly monitors the local situation, and Context-specific Safety and Security Management Plans will be in place, and regularly reviewed and updated. - Risks due to the COVID-19pandemic, that may result in lack of access of beneficiaries to the project sites or even the suspension of project activities: relevant program adaptations have been developed and are currently in place, in addition to strict IPC measurement. - Interruption of WHOsupplies line if the UNSCR was not renewed: Orient is supporting all advocacy efforts about the importance of the UNSCR renewal and works closely with the health cluster and other partners to develop a contingency plan to mitigate this risk.
