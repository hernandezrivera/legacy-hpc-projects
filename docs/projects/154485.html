Project Summary

UN-Habitat will provide water and sanitation support to highly vulnerable returnee and resident families within Zummar, Sinjar and Sinuni Sub-districts. The project will proactively address the drilling and installation of vacuum pumps and renovation or relocation of sub-water stations in the area. UN-Habitat will also strengthen the broader water supply network across the sub-district for 14 villages, covering a population of over 4,500 families or 27,000 individuals. The villages of Tel Khezer, Al Aziziya and Ain Al Fures will be prioritized for the installation of internal piping networks for each village. Additionally, Al Rabeea Quarter and Al Shuhada Quarter of Zummar District Centre will require the installation of internal piping networks for these areas, covering a population of over 1,400 families or 8,734 individuals.
Furthermore, in Sinjar and Sinuni Sub-districts, areas of increasing rates of return and largely under-served in terms of WASH infrastructural repair, assistance will be targeted through the repair of water pumps for deep wells, cleaning of existing wells, electrical reconnection of wells and rehabilitation of internal water pipe networks. It is estimated that over 13,000 families, or almost 78,500 individuals will benefit from these activities in the immediate term.
In total, the overall project is expected to support over 19,000 families or over 114,000 highly vulnerable people needing assistance for a durable return within their area of origin, across all stipulated sub-districts.

Needs

According to recent data provided by the Directorate of Water of Zummar and assessment data from Sinjar and Sinuni Sub-districts, as well as subsequent consultations with Directorate staff, urgent needs for the repair and rehabilitation of local WASH infrastructure exist. Furthermore, the September 2018 round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix published by IOM identifies “Sinjar (Sub-district), in particular, (as) the district with the highest number of locations with very high severity of conditions over the total of this sample.” The Directorate of Water has recommended that UN-Habitat carry out cleaning and drilling works for the delivery of water to vacuum pumps in Zummar Sub-district and to identify an alternative location for a municipal vacuum pump. In situations where this is unfeasible, they have proposed the relocation of these sub-water stations to alternative optimal positions or the construction of treatment tanks. 14 villages within the coverage area have been identified as being in need of this assistance. This would amount to over 4,510 households or 27,000 individuals’ needs being addressed. The Directorate has also identified the need for the installation of urgent internal pipe networks in five priority locations within the Sub-district: Tel Khezer Village, Al Aziziya Village, and Ain Al Fures Village as well as Al Rabeea Quarter and Al Shuhada Quarter of Zummar District Centre, thereby covering the needs of 1,423 households or 8,734 individuals. Additionally, the Directorate of Water in Sinjar District indicated two priority requirements: installation of a mains water supply pipe for Al Wardya Village in Sinjar Sub-district which is a priority for 103 families, or 605 individuals; and the re-activation of 40 wells in 40 villages by re-connecting currently in operational well pumps to the local electricity grid. The overall population of these 40 villages is approximately 70,000 individuals. Finally, the Municipality of Sinuni recently communicated with UN-Habitat and highlighted the need for the rehabilitation of three deep wells situated in the Galy Karvi area of Sinuni Sub-district which serves the needs of 1,303 families or 7,818 individuals in Khanasor Village. The investment in the rehabilitation of this key infrastructure will drastically improve the quality of life of the families who have already returned to the area meanwhile also create a positive and enabling environment for additional returnee families considering this option in the future. Needs assessment data has been shared and cross-checked with the WASH Cluster Coordinator and IM Focal Point to confirm that no other service providers have intentions or existing plans to implement similar activities in the same communities and sub-districts. Similarly, all proposed project activities have been presented in detail to the WASH Cluster Coordinator for review and confirmation has been received that they fall clearly within Cluster objectives and technical parameters.

Activities

Under this project, UN-Habitat will provide technical expertise and equipment for cleaning and drilling works for the delivery of water to vacuum pumps in Zummar Sub-district and to identify an alternative location for a municipal vacuum pump. Where this is not possible, sub-water stations will be re-positioned or new treatment tanks will be constructed. The following villages will benefit directly from this assistance: Domiz Collective, Girkaver, Abo Wajna, Dor Muasker Ain Zahala, Musefna, Hamrat Al Afeen, Al Sumood Collective, Barzan, Ain AL Fures, Ain Zahala, Kerjal, Aldor Hajarya, Al Bardiya Collective and Hajar Al Abyazz. This would amount to 4,510 households or 27,000 individuals’ needs being addressed. Five locations within the same sub-district: Tel Khezer Village, Al Aziziya Village, and Ain Al Fures Village as well as Al Rabeea Quarter and Al Shuhada Quarter of Zummar District Centre require the urgent installation of internal pipe networks. These local supply grids vary in length between 2km and 4 km, using 4 inch diameter polyethylene pipe, with the exception of Al Shuhada Quarter which requires a far shorter distance of galvanized pipe of only half a kilometer. This intervention would benefit 1,423 households or 8,734 individuals. In Sinjar District, a mains water supply pipe for Al Wardya Village in Sinjar Sub-district will be installed. This will meet the needs of over 100 families, or 605 individuals. Furthermore, up to 40 wells are currently unable to operate as their connecting lines with the local power grid have been severed during the conflict. By simply re-connecting the electricity supply to these pumps, water supply from these pumps to 40 corresponding villages will be restored, making a fundamental impact on the quality of life and health status of as many as 70,000 individuals (or 11,666 families based on a family size of six members). Within Sinuni Sub-district, UN-Habitat will rehabilitate three deep wells situated in the Galy Karvi area which serves the needs of 1,303 families or 7,818 individuals in Khanasor Village. Protection Risk Analysis

The following activities were assessed as having the potential for protection risks associated with the activities of the project:a) Water quality and purity: despite all due diligence being applied and the highest professional technical standards being maintained, an outside possibility exists that initial supplies of water from restored wells and piping networks may not meet drinking purity standards;b) On-site occupational safety: while all industry-standard site safety requirements will be maintained to ensure that local community members are not exposed to heavy earth-moving machinery and similar heavy equipment, risks remain possible; c) Local prevailing security situation: Although the military operations against ISIL have ended in Nineveh Governorate, the security situation remains complex and volatile in retaken areas, including due to the threat posed by explosive hazards; d) Project site clearance, screening and selection: to ensure the safety of the teams and beneficiaries, all site works, excavations and installations will be conducted in safe locations. Protection mainstreaming will be at the center of the response. The proposed action will fill gaps in access to water for women, girls, boys and men, including people with disabilities in all the communities mentioned above.

Mitigation Measures

a) Water quality and purity: UN-Habitat engineers will work closely with local Water Directorate colleagues to ensure that all necessary water quality tests are thoroughly completed and that quality and purity standards are met. Supplies will only be authorized for human consumption and use by local Water Directorate officials;b) On-site occupational safety: UN-Habitat’s field engineers will closely supervise local engineering contractors to ensure that all work sites and on-going operations adhere closely to the highest occupational health and safety standards;c) Local prevailing security situation: UN-Habitat has comprehensive security procedures in place for addressing any security deterioration and for situations that would require evacuation. This includes the suspension of operations up to evacuation. All UN- Habitat staff will adhere to the Security Plan as defined by UN-Habitat and UN Department of Safety and Security. Furthermore, the security situation is monitored on a continuous basis and essential information shared with project staff;d) Project site clearance, screening and selection: The project will only be implemented in areas that are clear of explosive threats. If necessary, removal and disposal of debris as well as clearance from unexploded devices will be completed in collaboration with the Government to ensure that the plot is cleared.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The principle of “do no harm” is at the heart of UN-Habitat’s activities across the country. The approach employed by the project will ensure full consultation with, and participation of, local authorities, including mayors’ offices, water directorates as well as formal and informal community representatives. The needs of men, women, boys and girls remain essentially at the center of the intervention. The approach employed by the project will ensure the consultation of beneficiaries during the implementation and evaluation phases of the project. This allows for the teams to present UN-Habitat, the project and the approach employed to local leaders, authorities and beneficiaries, and to secure their support. Coordination with local authorities and humanitarian partners will be key to the success of the restoration of pumps and water networks across all three sub-districts. The project’s approach, main objectives, key activities and limits will be explained in a transparent manner. In order to be effective and in line with the principles of “do no harm”, all the project’s interventions are highly context-specific, and to the extent possible, participatory in nature. Furthermore, UN-Habitat will continue to liaise closely with other humanitarian partners and local authorities to gather current information on WASH-related activities in the target location, including mapping of actors involved in water supply network rehabilitation and water pump restoration activities. Regular feedback will be sought from water directorate authorities, local leaders and beneficiaries on the services provided to ensure that all needs are tackled and services are adjusted as needed. UN-Habitat will also use the “ActivityInfo” platform to report on achievements against project targets.
