Project Summary

Concern proposes a shelter project to benefit 8,738 of the most vulnerable people in the blast-affected areas of Beirut. The final target locations will be determined in coordination with the Shelter Sector, but Concern intends to focus on Medawar, Bachoura, Remeil and Borj Hammoud as they – particularly Medawar – were some of the poorest and most vulnerable areas before the blast.
Within those areas, the project will focus on households and individuals who were most vulnerable before the blast and who have therefore felt the greatest emotional, physical and financial impact of the explosion. Poor women, adolescent girls, older people, and people with disabilities or chronic illness will be priority targets. All nationalities will be targeted but specific attention will be given to migrants, Syrian refugees and extremely poor Lebanese.
The objectives of the project will be aligned with Priorities 1, 2 and 3 of the Shelter Sector Plan: (1) to respond to the immediate sheltering needs of the most vulnerable, (2) contribute to longer-term repair or rehabilitation of damaged buildings, and (3) support multi-sectoral approaches to rehabilitate holistically the affected areas.
The activities will include distribution of weatherproofing kits and support for installation (200 households); relocation to safe shelter (40 households); cash-for-rent (200 households); minor repair (250 households) and rehabilitation of damaged buildings (80 households); and implementation of a multi-sectoral community project in two of the identified neighbourhoods (2000 households).
Concern plans to implement the shelter project as part of a multi-sectoral response that also includes WASH and protection activities. The project leverages Concern’s expertise and experience working in these sectors in Lebanon since 2013.

1 - Needs

On August 4th, a warehouse at the Beirut port exploded causing widespread casualties and destruction. The Government of Lebanon has declared a two-week state of emergency in Beirut. The death toll has reached 220, over 7,000 people have been injured, and more than 300,000 have been left displaced. The impact of the explosions has caused ‘severe damage’ within six kilometers of the epicenter; ‘moderate damage’ up to 10 kilometers; and ‘light damage’ up to 20 kilometers. An estimated 30,660 individuals live in buildings with severe damage; 51,300 individuals live in buildings with moderate damage and about 89,313 individuals live in buildings with light damage. Damage is visible at household level and within the common area of some buildings, increasing accessibility constraints, especially for older people and people with disabilities. Some people have had the means to relocate to other, safe neighborhoods or municipalities while the majority, especially those in poorer areas, have had no choice but to stay in damaged buildings, facing severe risk to their lives and wellbeing. Structural damage has been most acute in the neighbourhoods of Quarantina, Ashrafiyeh, Central district, Gemmayzeh, Remeil, Marfa’a and Mar Mikhail. Other neighborhoods such as Bourj Hammoud, Zuqaq El Blat and Bachoura have suffered mostly moderate or light damage, but due to pre-existing vulnerability, they are likely to have a high level of need. These areas are overcrowded, home to large extended families, and host many migrant workers, refugees, poor Lebanese and informal sector workers who have less capacity to cope in times of emergencies. Migrant workers are of particular concern because many had become unemployed and homeless due to the economic crisis, as many employers cut them loose with no place to stay and often with wages unpaid. Many have faced increased racism as the economic situation has deteriorated, and many have no means of returning to their home countries. Large numbers of migrant workers lived in the highly affected neighbourhoods of Quarantina and Bourj Hammoud. The preliminary results of a Lebanese Red Cross (LRC) survey in the blast affected areas show almost four out of five respondents have no savings and 37% reported no income. In addition, about 25% of respondents are older people, and more than 50% suffer from a medical condition. The shelter damage whether severe, moderate or light, is no doubt exacerbating health and protection concerns. With many broken doors and windows, privacy and security - especially for children and women - is likely to be severely compromised. Approximatively 81,000 women of reproductive age, including adolescent girls, are displaced as a result of the explosion (UNFPA). Those living in temporary relocation and/or shared shelters with unrelated families are likely facing heightened protection risks as well as reduced access to water, sanitation and hygiene services, making it difficult to maintain basic hygiene – particularly menstrual hygiene – with dignity. The blast came at a time when Lebanon was already at breaking point, struggling with a severe economic crisis, political upheaval and ongoing COVID-19 outbreaks. Eviction was on the rise for the poorest and most vulnerable before the explosion, meaning many found themselves in very poor quality housing before the blast. There is now an urgent need to support these families and individuals with the means to protect themselves from danger by covering the cost of rent and/or relocating them to a safe shelter. In addition, due to a lack of infection prevention and control (IPC) items for COVID-19 virus, such as personal protective equipment (PPE), many affected by the blast are reluctant to access the services they need. First responders are also lacking PPE, and there is a general need for increased IPC measures across the response.

2 - Activities

Concern will work closely with local organisations, associations and volunteers who have been responding to blast-related needs of the most vulnerable, especially migrants. These organizations will help the project efficiently identify people in need of shelter support, install weatherproofing materials, and identify empty shelters and apartment owners willing to provide shelter. In response to Priority 1, Concern will support affected families through the distribution of emergency shelter materials for damaged residential/non-residential buildings and support installation of the materials. The aim is to facilitate the urgent weatherproofing needed by households, especially before the colder and wetter months arrive. Concern will support any families and individuals living in structurally damaged buildings that are unsafe or overcrowded to relocate to a safe shelter. Emergency shelter materials will be provided by UNHCR, with whom Concern has an existing partnership, and will be distributed after completion of the planned LRC assessment of the affected areas. Engineers, architects and local community members will be mobilized to guide residents and/or install the kits to seal broken doors and windows. Concern will provide cash-for-rent for 3 months per recipient. The amount provided will be based on the sector guidance (200 USD per month). Concern will liaise with cash-for-rent actors operating in Beirut to understand the best modality for cash disbursement and with partners in the shelter sector who have access to unoccupied shelters for relocation of vulnerable families. Concern will also link individuals to local initiatives where families volunteer accommodation to people affected by the blast. In response to Priority 2, Concern will conduct minor repair and rehabilitation of residential units. Prioritisation of units for rehabilitation will be based on the LRC assessment, field visits and referrals. Concern will negotiate with landlords as much as possible to agree rent decreases or rent freezes and increase awareness of residents regarding their housing, land and property rights to promote secure tenure arrangements and more harmonious cohabitation. Common areas in some sub-standard buildings will also be upgraded, especially where accessibility is problematic. To respond to Priority 3, Concern will lead a multi-sectoral intervention in one neighborhood that brings together the above shelter support with WASH and protection activities to promote a more holistic and lasting recovery of the affected community. The community support project will be designed jointly between Concern and the selected community, with participation from the most vulnerable. Preference will be given to locations with a high number of migrants. Concern is already distributing shelter kits in Rmeil and Bourj Hammoud. Concern has also been implementing a similar shelter project in North Lebanon since 2015 including minor repairs, major upgrade, relocating families and providing cash-for-rent. The Beirut shelter response will be complemented by plumbing repair under the water sector in buildings where vulnerable families need improved WASH services. Prioritization of beneficiaries will consider socio-economic vulnerability, presence of specific needs based on gender, age, disability as well as other vulnerability criteria as they arise. Migrants and Syrian refugees will be specifically targeted. Beneficiaries will be fully integrated in the project. Concern will apply a protection lens across its activities to follow a Do No Harm approach and mainstream PSEA. Beneficiaries will be referred to needed services and be capacitated on Concern’s Complaint Response Mechanism. Concern will ensure all program participants and partner associations are trained in and following COVID-19 IPC measures and are equipped with appropriate PPE during their activities.
