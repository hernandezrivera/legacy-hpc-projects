Project Summary

Eight years after the toppling of the former Gaddafi regime, Libya continues to suffer political turmoil and armed conflict, which has affected millions of people - almost half of Libya’s population - and resulted in high numbers of internally displaced persons (IDPs). According to the International Organization for Migration (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) the number of displaced people has increased steadily for the past year, hitting a record high of 343,180 Internally Displaced People (IDPs) in October 2019. The West of Libya, with Tripoli mantika, is the most affected region with the highest number of IDPs, 219,840, followed by the South region with 72,645 IDPs and the East with 50,695 recorded IDPs.
The contamination level of explosive remnants of war (ERW) is very high and constitutes significant dangers to the population. The presence of unexploded and abandoned ordnance, landmines, and ammunition and cluster munitions across Libya is a constant threat for the population and a hindrance to the reconstruction of safe cities and communities.The political landscape in Libya has continued to deteriorate since mid-2014 in contrast to the expectations of the Libyan population.
Establishing a safe environment, by conducting Humanitarian Mine Action (HMA) activities, is a major precursor to the overall security and stability of Libya, that brings about development, re-established infrastructure as well as socio-economic services. Essentially, the Explosive Hazards (EH) contamination affects the stabilization and reconstruction efforts by curbing rehabilitation and development projects and limiting humanitarian access.
In 2020 the Danish Demining Group (DDG) plans to implement a complex HMA programme, including Battle Area Clearance (BAC), Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), Risk Education (RE) and Non Technical Survey (NTS) activities in Sabha and Benghazi.
By way of its multi-donor long term strategy and in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Libya 2020, DDG plans to achieve the following Overall Objective: To recreate a safe environment where the conflict-affected communities in Libya can live without the threat of mines and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW.)
The proposed action has the following Specific Objectives or Outcomes:
SO1: To remove and destroy ERW in the South and East of Libya.
SO2: To reduce risk-taking behaviour in conflict affected and vulnerable communities at risk of EH in the areas of intervention.
SO3: To strengthen national capacities for Humanitarian Mine Action across Libya.
In order to deliver on its outcomes, DDG plans to develop the technical and support capacity of national staff from Sabha and Benghazi to lay the foundations for creating future local NGOs in the South and East following the successful development of DDG’s implementing partner, Free Fields Foundation (3F), in the West. In order to achieve this, the following outputs will be followed:
Outputs:
Op1: Increased technical capacities of national staff and EH clearance operations conducted in Sabha and Benghazi.
Op2: Increased awareness of the risks posed by mines and EH in the areas of intervention.
Op3: Increased institutional capacity of local HMA organizations.
Based on the above mentioned objectives and activities, the proposed intervention aligns with both HRP priorities for 2020, namely:
1) Population’s safe, equitable, and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral services to access resources and reduce their vulnerability, in accordance with international legal standards and
2) Enabling national and local authorities to respond, and foster community resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses stemming from existing and future crises through strengthened coordination, evidence-based needs analyses, strategic preparedness and disaster management.

1 - Needs

The South has remained neglected in terms of coverage and attention paid to the humanitarian needs due to security and high level of EH contamination. Since January 2014, the region has been the centre of recurrent and intensified tribal confrontations and armed conflicts and DDG International staff has not been able to return. However, the presence of HMA INGOs is highly valued since there is still a need to conduct clearance of areas heavily affected by EH contamination. DDG International Staff has not been able to travel to Sabha since 2014, as a result, it has adjusted its approach and strengthened its remote management modalities to ensure the continuation of HMA activities. DDG has cleared roads, buildings and farms making the areas accessible for the transit of civilians and the safe return of IDPs in Sabha in 2019 and therefore, it plans to work with both NTS and EOD teams in the areas of Tamanhint, Samno and Brak Al-Shati if these areas are still a priority suggested by LibMAC for 2020. Since DDG is very well known in the south, most of the work is done through emergency phone calls where people who find an EH report the area to DDG and the EOD team dispose the items. In 2019 in Sebha DDG deployed 2 EOD and 2 NTS teams as well as 4 RE mobilizer teams with funding from various donors. In the East, the battle for Benghazi between the Libyan National Army (LNA) and an array of Islamist militants and other fighters deteriorated when the LNA launched “Operation Dignity” in 2014. The LNA forces tried to retake control of important strongholds in the city, resulting in heavy fighting over important facilities for civilians, such as hospitals, hotels, and universities. After three years of continuous fighting, Benghazi was liberated by the LNA in July 2017, leaving many urban areas of Benghazi wrecked by heavy shelling and air strikes. This has created intense pressure to conduct EH clearance and rebuild the city. Hence, there are very high expectations on the part of the displaced population from Benghazi for such work to be done quickly, enabling them to return to their homes. In Benghazi, Ganfouda district has been identified as a priority to conduct HMA activities under this action for 2019 and will continue in 2020. This district is located in the suburbs of western Benghazi and it was heavily affected by the clashes between Shura Revolutionary Council, the Islamic State (IS) and the LNA. As the last line of defence, Shura Council of Benghazi Revolutionaries and IS occupied 12 blocks in Ganfouda for several months until the LNA launched Operation Dignity and re-gained control of the area. Ganfouda witnessed one of the heaviest clashes where all types of projectiles were used. In addition, IS took hostages from LNA, families, and planted IED's to prevent LNA from liberating more blocks. This caused severe damage in the neighbouring buildings and caused many schools and vital facilities to be closed until now, due to the explosive hazards lying around. Therefore, DDG prioritized this area to start clearance operations after the approval and pertinent task order received by LibMAC. Currently DDG is deploying 4 EOD teams, 3 BAC teams and 2 NTS teams with funding from various donors. DDG is seeking to maintain the same operational capacity in 2020 as in 2019 in both Sebha and Benghazi, but as some of the funding awarded last year will run out at the end of Q1 2020, the organisation can only deploy the same amount of teams on the ground if extra funding is secured through other funding streams.

2 - Activities

Land Release: DDG adopts various land release methodologies, with land cancelled and released through technical survey (TS), NTS survey and clearance.TS/NTS: As part of the land release process, DDG focuses on comprehensive surveys of suspected hazardous areas to collect and analyse data about the presence, type, distribution and surrounding environment of explosive hazards contamination. This enables DDG to define better where (or not) contamination is present and to support land release prioritisation and decision-making processes through the provision of evidence. BAC: is undertaken for areas that have been the site of armed confrontation and where no mines are suspected, there often remain large areas that are contaminated by unexploded ordnance that needs to be cleared. These pose physical risks to communities in proximity and heighten risks to aid workers, since injuries can occur when people who want to make use of land move or tamper with explosive items. BAC can contribute to two outcomes: the release of cleared land for future use and development, and the reduction of casualties in circumstances where people were previously coming into contact with the EH in the cleared areas. EOD: In many cases, the contamination with EH is scattered and not found within defined battle areas. Thus, it is not always efficient to carry out widespread search/clearance and HMA programmes establish EOD teams to react to reports of individual items of EH found. The EOD teams do not release large areas by clearance but their work is critical as they provide a rapid response to endangered communities.EH RE: activities that encourage safe behaviour around mines, explosive remnants of war, and small arms and light weapons. Risk education is both a tool for preventing suffering and promoting security amongst populations, a process intended to reduce casualties by modifying behaviour around EH.

5 - Mitigation measures

1. DDG strives to mainstream protection elements into all HMA activities, not only to ensure compliance with standard protection principles but also to facilitate meaningful access, safety and dignity for all the beneficiaries.Through the provision of survey and clearance beneficiaries can reclaim spaces and livelihood opportunities while through RE sessions they will be empowered and able to avoid the dangers posed by EH. 2. By recruiting and training staff from the most vulnerable communities, DDG maximizes the chances for members of local communities to actively participate in the implementation of all HMA activities that aim to address their needs, in a meaningful and effective way. As all activities will be planned beforehand with the relevant national authorities, there is potential for areas to be prioritized based based on where the needs are the greatest, thus targeting the most affected communities first.3. DDG integrates conflict sensibility in its programming by ensuring different population sections are targeted, not left behind or completely excluded, therefore reducing the chance of exacerbating conflict among already vulnerable communities. All activities undertaken are performed in close cooperation with national, regional, and local authorities and with the participation of beneficiaries - both prior to and following implementation. 4. DDG has zero tolerance for abuse and misconduct, including Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, and goes a long way to ensure the prevention of abuse and exploitation in the work place, especially regarding the asymmetrical power relationships between DDG staff and beneficiaries. DDG ensures that all beneficiaries are aware that all humanitarian activities implemented come at no cost for beneficiaries and that all people are aware of their rights.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

DDG ensures high levels of accountability through the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certification, which focuses highly on beneficiary information and involvement in all phases of project implementation. DRC/DDG will proactively seek on-going input from beneficiaries through community engagement and discussions especially during Community Liaison efforts. Moreover, DRC/DDG has established a complaints/feedback mechanism which beneficiaries can access to report issues relating to unfair treatment during service provision, violations of the DRC/DDG code of conduct by staff members, as well as pose queries or provide feedback on service provision.
