Project Summary

The proposed activities depend on the need on the ground with strong benefit from Qatar Charity’s presence in the same proposed areas, a deep understanding of the reality and need, and more than eight years of experience in WASH services and projects by implementing direct or by QC’s implementing partners. Therefore, the proposed activities will directly support the implementation of the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The permanent water, sanitation and hygiene services for the conflict-affected population in the Idlib and Aleppo regions ranked high in the severity of the water, sanitation and hygiene needs. This will be achieved through delivery of an integrated and comprehensive response to potable water, Solid Waste Management (SWM) and sanitation needs of approx. According to HNO 2024 the Acute PiN are 2,367,614 individuals in NWS of which an estimated 1,627,928 IDPs. Areas have been selected based on People in Need (PIN) and Severity of Need as presented in the draft Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2024.
After thirteen years of the Syrian conflict and in line with the strategic objectives of HRP 2024, part of the proposed activities includes sustainability solutions through the application of a hybrid energy system in some water stations or linking water stations to the electricity network in the areas that have electricity access, connecting camps with water network and water station in addition to starting the application of a cost recovery system Fair and built on community engagement. Hygiene promotion activities will continue to be offered in clustering areas, with a special focus on preventing and responding to cholera outbreak and other water borne diseases. The intervention will cover the WASH needs as below:
Water Supply, Water station rehabilitation including installing solar panel to ensure the targeted beneficiaries have increased access to safe,and with a sustainable solution, water networks fixing to ensure that water services reach the largest number of beneficiaries and to reduce the negative effects of water leakage within the network or mixing of drinking water with black water, support of operation and maintenance of waters stations to ensure the targeted beneficiaries have increased access to safe, potable water with 35-45 l/p/d.
Summer months according to WASH cluster recommendation, water trucking this intervention will meet the daily needs (consumption and personal hygiene) of IDPs which living in IDPs sites, everyone will receive 35 liters per day, water tanks distributing, to address a current shortage of water storage tanks, Women will be consulted in selecting the most appropriate location for the water tanks.
Cost Recovery mechanism, in line with the cluster recommendations and in line with each targeted area to reach sustainability in the water services
Solid Waster removal, in communities and in IDPs site to ensure the mitigation of the negative and environmental impacts of the accumulation of garbage in the open and in residential areas.
Waste management operation cost, through provide the necessary operational materials for the SWM vehicles and staffing.
Sewage and drainage system rehabilitation.
Toilets and showers constructing and fixing in IDPs sites, to reach the Sphere standards in # of latrines users.
Water treatment plant, to exchange the black and grey water to irrigation water.
Hygiene kits distribution, in line with the cluster recommendations regarding the amount of soap and cleaning materials to ensure the continuation of the hygiene practices necessary to prevent Covid-19 and cholera outbreak.
Hygiene awareness, at HHs level, to ensure that the infection is not spread during the sessions The project will target men, women, boys, and girls both host communities and IDPs from within cities in those governorates as well as the surrounding areas or other parts of the city who live in the targeted neighborhoods.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The 2024 Protection Risk Analysis for WASH highlights areas of risk for which we have already established effective mitigation measures. In line with the WASH sector, QC will ensure ‘Do No Harm’ principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities. We will ensure that SPHERE standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation, especially during emergency sanitation infrastructure implementation phase when the risk of GBV is higher. Measures will also be taken to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female headed households) to greater protection risks, and particularly to GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. We also ensure that all staff sign code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols related to PSEA, with all staff trained on PSEA and Child Protection The project will carry continuous Protection Risk Analysis as part of the implementation plan. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence and exposure to explosive hazard. The project will mitigate the risk of inequality in accessing services by selecting of the target beneficiaries based on need assessment and that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context for each activity of the project and the project will coordinate with the stakeholders to ensure equitable access to the project activities. The project will address risks related to work safety by adopting the standard safety measures during the operation and maintenance of the WASH facilities. Workers will use the Personal Protective Equipment PPE to minimize risk of injuries or exposure to diseases. The project will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, etc.) to GBV. The project team will ensure women’s voices is heard during design of WASH interventions and identify any GBV risk(s). The locations of the WASH facilities will be central, accessible and in well-lit areas with good visibility of the surrounding area. staff will be trained on GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors.
