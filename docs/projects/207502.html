Project Summary

Bhasan Char was established by the Government of Bangladesh where it is planned to relocate 100,000 members of the Rohingya community from Myanmar on a voluntary basis. Aimed at addressing the overcrowding in Cox’s Bazar and improving living conditions, the project is led by the Government with relevant Ministries and agencies actively involved, as well as national and international NGOs. With the MoU between Government of Bangladesh and UNHCR (on behalf of the UN) in place, UN operations have been developed on the island and as of 31 October 2023, 30,881 refugees has been relocated there. Humanitarian community will continue to make efforts to supplement and complement the humanitarian activities of the national and international NGOs in support of this national led effort. The project will support refugees on Bhasan Char, who are highly vulnerable and entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Women, children, and person with specific needs reported the most significant unmet needs, leaving them vulnerable to negative coping strategies. Bhasan Char is vulnerable to climate risks such as cyclones, storms, frequent lightning and flooding due to monsoon and tidal surges. The Inter-Agency efforts will provide support and assistance to refugees including protection, shelter, food and non-food items, water, sanitation and hygiene, health and nutrition, education, livelihoods and skill development. To mitigate disaster risks, emergency, preparedness, and response (EPR) will be part of the inter-agency response.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The socio-economic vulnerability of Rohingya refugees on Bhasan Char has impacted their physical and mental well-being, quality of life, and protection environment. Refugees need ongoing humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs and access essential services in health and nutrition, food security, protection, education, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), electricity, and non-food items. Community resilience to disasters and climate change impacts requires multi-sectoral inter-agency cooperation on emergency preparedness and response. Although there is progress in developing and strengthening protection and assistance services. Children and caregivers report significant barriers to access education for adolescent girls and children with disabilities. The number of skilled teachers particularly female remains low due to the unavailability of skilled teachers among the relocated populations. There are reports of risks of child labour, child marriage and physical and sexual abuse. There are barriers for people with disabilities in accessing services. The lack of a temporary safe shelter and One Stop Crisis Centre (OCC) for GBV survivors and children facing risks continues to impede the delivery of services. Refugees requiring specialized health treatment which is unavailable on the island - such as surgical, pediatrics, and orthopedic cases - are referred to services on the mainland. However, accessibility is a major concern due to the three-hour travelling time required, and the lack of dedicated transport to facilitate the referral process. Mental health remains a key concern, especially for patients with severe mental health illnesses receiving clinical mental health consultations. There are limited opportunities for Rohingya refugees about livelihoods and self-reliance. Refugees do not have access to land for agricultural practices except for ponds and limited free land within the clusters. There are also limited opportunities for skills development, as well as for earning cash to purchase essential items. A small portion of Rohingya refugees can find volunteer opportunities while women often engage in marketing handicrafts. Although there has been substantial investment in infrastructure; regular maintenance of refugee houses, facilities and public infrastructure is needed. Bhasan Char is not connected to the mainland power grid; thus, electricity is only provided through generators. There is insufficient fuel supply to meet demand. Without adequate and sustained access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), preventable disease outbreaks will continue and worsen. Acute watery diarrheic (AWD) is a high risk for Bashan Char and can aggravate deteriorating malnutrition rates of Rohingya children. Women and girls face challenges with latrines such as lack of separation of latrines and bathing facilities, privacy, and frequent blockage of latrines. Hygiene practices and the barriers to achieving them remains a critical challenge.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Bhasan Char Inter Agency Response is based on a shared understanding of needs, Sector objectives, and priorities among partners. This prevents duplication of effort, improves coverage—especially for assisting the most vulnerable—increases response continuity, and provides coordinated, coherent, collaborative, sustainable, and cost-effective responses. Some of the efforts to promote rationalisation of response include: (a) geographic gaps or overlaps in planned activities are avoided and a one-partner-per-settlement approach has been considered and optimized where appropriate, (b) partners are members of the PSEA Network and have PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points, (c) partners have, or can, obtain appropriate permissions from the Government in a timely manner, (d) maintain continuity of presence - working in the same Sector and cluster/settlement in 2024, (e) appealing partners comply with the minimum technical standards, experience, and meet the minimum service package requirements, (f) partners have realistic resource mobilization plans to support their planned activities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Inter-agency activities will target to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of joint intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks; ensure access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without any barriers; pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services; set-up appropriate mechanisms through which refugees can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints and support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to access their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. This will be ensured by supporting different sectors when conducting regular needs assessments and participatory discussions with the beneficiaries to ensure that the services provided are tailored to their changing needs; also supporting regular monitoring & evaluation of the quality of the services provided.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Inter-agency response has factored in the different capacities, needs, and exposure to protection risks of the refugee women, men, girls, and boys targeted in the programme, and have incorporated them into inter-agency assessments, planning, implementation, monitoring, reporting, and evaluation. Disability and inclusion will be mainstreamed across interventions. Activities will continue to enable access to services and assistance services to diverse persons we serve including children and adolescent girls and boys as well as persons living with disabilities and older persons.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Inter-agency actions taken in relation to AAP include protection hotline, participatory assessments, feedback boxes, public information boards, community meetings, interviews, counselling sessions, and home visits. Work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct and a Zero-Tolerance Policy for Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment. Training and awareness are cascaded to community members on identifying and safe reporting of SEA.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) is a core commitment of the UN Secretary-General and the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). To deliver on this commitment, the Bhasan Char Inter-Agency Coordination has committed to a PSEA mainstreaming across the work of all Sectors. All partners must commit to prevent, mitigate, and respond to SEA cases and inter-agency coordination will play a facilitate role. As a minimum standard of PSEA, all Sector members and partners working on Bhasan Char are required to undertake mandatory PSEA training.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Bhasan Char hazard profile is characterized by exposure to multiple hazards including tropical storms and cyclones, inland flooding from heavy rains, destructive winds, lightning, and fire. Disaster Risk Reduction measures are integrated into sectoral response. The SMS Sector is assisting the Government to work with key stakeholders to strengthen emergency preparedness measures and to facilitate a coordinated response to natural disasters and hazards through continuous monitoring, drills, and simulations, as well as prepositioning essential relief items. Cyclone preparedness (CPP) and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) volunteers are trained and mobilized in each cluster to support communication with communities during anticipatory, response and recovery phases of disasters.
