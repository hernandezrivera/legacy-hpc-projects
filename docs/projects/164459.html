Project Summary

The severity of humanitarian needs within the Syrian crisis has been well documented with an unprecedented level of declining access to essential service such as water and sanitation. Access and usage of water for hygiene, sanitation and drinking will remain a pivotal issue for 6.2 million people within Syria (UNOCHA, HNO, 2019) limiting the extent to which quality of life and the economy can be improved in the country. Regular access to water challenges persist, particularly, in rural areas where people are relying on unsafe water sources to meet their requirements. Thus, medium-term options to improve the drinking and sanitation water situation are urgently needed within rural areas.
The project aims to enhance access to sustainable, clean and equitable water supply for 14,844 male and female individuals (3,110 IDPs and 510 returnees) within the eastern countryside of Hama province in central Syria. The water strategy under this project will implement a localized system which takes into account prevailing supply infrastructure and growing local demand. By sourcing, treating, storing, and distributing water locally, the water supply system will be able to adapt to sudden shortages or outages of the target areas, thereby ensuring quality, equitable access to water and reducing the need to externally source water which is of questionable quality and often not accessible during periods of water shortage. The increase in water reserve capacity will be able to reach an appropriate total catchment of 14,844 individuals within Barri al Sharqi Sub-District.
In order to facilitate community access to equitable and improved usage of water services for communities in the target villages, the proposed action proposes maintaining active community engagement within project design and implementation through the creation of a community water management committee. This is important to promote long-term sustainable water services of rural households. Furthermore, to address the limited access to sustainable water service provided by local authorities, water service systems will be strengthened to improve the quality and usage of these services. For this purpose, the project will focus on improving the technical and managerial capacities of male and female water technicians who are mainly from the local authorities in the target Sub-District.
The main project activities include; (1) drill 1 deep groundwater borehole in Barri al Sharqi village and provide equipment necessary for the operation of this deep water well, (2) install primary water treatment and Reverse Osmosis (RO) units, (3) established a voluntary community water management committee representing the communities within the target villages and (4) Hand over the water treatment facilities and equipment to the Water Local Authority in Barri al Sharqi Sub-District.

4 - Needs

The villages of Barri al Sharqi Sub-District within Salamieh District in the eastern part of Hama governorate have been identified as project locations. The target villages have experienced the brunt of the crisis as they have been considered as “front-line” and some villages were taken over by ISIS between 2014 and 2018. Since October 2017, the security situation in the target Sub-District has improved markedly. Nonetheless, the majority of the target population remain vulnerable, with the key drivers of vulnerability being displacement, chronic illness, lack of livelihood opportunity, female headed households, and host family overcrowding. The issue of declining access to essential service such as water has been exacerbated by the influx of IDPs from areas of active conflict during the early years of the crisis. The scarce amounts of water coming from the three major sources of water in the district of Salamieh (Al Qantra pumping station, Al Shomrya wells and Bergan well) do not contribute to water requirements of the populations in Barri al Sharqi. Also, the distances between this Sub-District and the three water sources are relatively long, with poor water infrastructure (e.g. connecting pipelines). The shortage of groundwater, the sole water supply, has reached a dangerous level as a result of drilling hundreds of new home wells to meet the increase in water needs for drinking, washing and agriculture purposes, with very poor water management system employed by the government. It was estimated by AKF that 90% of the groundwater wells identified in 2013 were dry. Results of water tests conducted by the Veterinary Service Laboratory (VSL) showed that most of the water sources used by the population are contaminated and unsafe for drinking. Presently, the quantity supplied to Barri al Sharqi Sub-District is on the order of 540 m3/month. Water supply has fallen to an irregular supply of 2.2 liters/person/day, far below the WHO minimum of 50 liters/person/day.

5 - Implementation of activities

The primary source of water will originate from the secondary groundwater reserve which naturally replenishes; however, contains a high concentration of sulfuric content. AKF, in collaboration with the target communities and local authorities, will help drill 1 deep groundwater borehole (600 and 700 m depth) in Barri al Sharqi village. The project will provide all equipment necessary for the operation of the constructed deep water well such as submersible, pipes, solar-powered energy supply and wellhead accessories. Under the project, a two-step treatment process will be implemented: (1) a primary treatment unit which has the intake capacity of 850m3/day; and treat water through a cooling, sedimentation, gas elimination, and filtration process. About 750 m3/day of the water produced from the primary treatment unit will be pumped into the main water grid to be used locally for hygiene and sanitation purposes. (2) The remaining 100m3/day of water will then be piped to another treatment stations to undergo a Reverse Osmosis treatment process. The purified water estimated at 70 m3/day will be pumped into a separate water grid for to be used locally for drinking water. One community water management committee representing women and men from target villages will be established to oversee the operations and management of community water storage structures, collectively decide when resources should be utilized, and implement usage regulations to ensure equitable access to water. Committee members will receive technical, management and governance training. All project equipment, assets and materials will be handed over to the Local Water Authority through a gradual process which includes training a cadre of water system technicians comprised of women and men. Process and outcome monitoring activities under the project will include baseline and endline assessments of the target group, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries and FGDs with project stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Gender related cultural and traditional norms and roles may limit women ability to participate in project activities and services and, even expose them to various levels of exploitation. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. AKF will continue to adapt a culturally sensitive, locally appropriate and consultative gender equality agenda. The tap stands will be designed to facilitate gender parity in the provision of water. The design will be also adapted to the specific needs and capacities of different gender and age groups (e.g. providing better access/line for women and PwDs). Each stand will be equipped with two drinking water taps, one of them will be mainly used to serve females, children, elderly and PwDs. Project teams will also be established with mixed genders. It has been found that gender dynamics are often more positive within team environments and especially ones that are led by women. B: Voluntary community water management committee members might be criticized or accused of bias in relation to RO utilization. Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will ensure that management committee are inclusive and representative of local stakeholders and oversee the collective decisions over when resources need to be utilized, and implement usage regulations to ensure equitable access to water.C: Project beneficiaries may be exposed to discrimination from other villages not targeted under this project. Likelihood: Low; Impact: Low. With the help of Community Based Organizations (CBOs), AKF will disseminate information on the project information and location selection criteria. AKF is implementing other socially inclusive models such as community productive assets, social small grants and cooperatives under which other locations are targeted. AKF will also orient community representatives (CBOs) form non-targeted villages to transfer project information to rural people from their communities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Lack of adherence to deliver the required activities and services on the part of local water authorities. Mitigation: A firm contract will be signed with water authorities to guarantee the high quality of works. AKF will conduct tests at various points and monitor activities and services to ensure quality standards of each component and deliverable. Risk2: Lack of local authorities’ adherence to deliver required services and permits to establish the community water management committees. Mitigation: AKF will use competent lawyers to improve the engagement of the authorities on project-related discussions and design a road map to establish the required committees. Risk3: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the programme and communicate with project stakeholders as needed. Risk4: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in program areas is deemed a high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk5: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in successfully procuring required items for delivery to Salamieh. Mitigation: AKF has extensive experience undertaking water projects. During the crisis -in extremely difficult situations- AKF could successfully procure and install a $2.1m emergency drinking water treatment plant capable of providing 5,000m3 a day. AKF has been successfully undertaking infrastructure programming, health facility equipping, school start-ups, and community productive assets in extremely difficult places.
