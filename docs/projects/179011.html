Project Summary

To respond to the needs of the most vulnerable households across NES, this project will focus on providing temporary employment opportunities using CfW method as well as livelihoods restoration support to small and medium enterprises (SME) and Vocational Training using the Graduation Approach. All activities will target some of the most vulnerable households left with no means of income generation to meet their most basic needs as a result of the conflict and COVID-19. This includes families engaging in child labor, families with only one or few potential wage earners, persons with disabilities, and households without an existing vocation or source of reliable income. Whilst in rural areas, this project will focus on supporting agricultural production (please see Agricultural sector), in urban settings the NGO will support SMEs through cash grants and business training. Meanwhile, CfW activities in urban and semi-urban settings, will comprise public works and services which benefit the whole community such as small rehabilitation, cleaning and cosmetic improvements of public spaces. The NGO will also continue to implement CfW activities in response to COVID-19 pandemic. CfW participants will support COVID-19 mitigation measures in Menbij camps by sanitizing surfaces and cleaning WASH facilities.
Many small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) have been severely affected by the consequences of COVID-19 including the imposition of a curfew, non-essential market closures, travel restrictions and border closures and other supply chain-related disruptions. The restoration grant to SMEs is designed to support skilled workers to restore their productive assets and resume income-generating activities. This endeavour will also target women and disabled persons who have skills but lack the tools or equipment to expand into a viable income-generating activity without initial investment. The NGO will support 250 of the most vulnerable households (approx. 1,500 individuals) targeted in the same neighborhood where the NGO will provide food security and shelter support with a view to supporting revitalize the economy in these areas, increasing work and livelihood opportunities. At the same time, where possible this project will directly support households who are receiving food and/or shelter assistance under this grant. This will improve the resilience of the most at-risk households by giving them the means to generate their own income and eventually transition from depending on humanitarian assistance.
This project will also provide to individuals that have been unable to practice their trade for a long period of time, a tailored Graduation programme that will comprise technical courses where existing gaps in the worker’s skills have been identified, a business grant to restart their Income Generating Activity (IGA) with support on the creation of a business plan to drive the use of the grant, and coaching sessions to support the household through their recovery. A comprehensive micro-business management training will allow them to develop their business management skills and create a business plan, enabling them to start their own business or home-based business. The mentoring and coaching sessions will take place in each individual households as well as in groups with other participants. Individual mentoring sessions and life skills training will aim to strengthen the resilience of the households, reduce their exposure to protection risks and boost the individuals’ confidence in their own ability to succeed in the establishment of their IGA; while group sessions with other participants will allow for sharing difficulties and initiatives between participants. During the training period, the households will receive a monthly cash payment equivalent to the value of the SMEB to ensure that they are able to stabilise their living conditions and fully commit to the training and time needed to build the necessary skills.

4 - Needs

Levels of food insecurity in NES have reached a critical level with underserved and hard-to-reach communities most severely impacted. The average wage for an unskilled labourer is far below what a household needs to survive. The Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme, reports that ‘lack of technical skills’ have been identified by 55% of the respondents in NES to the recent study as barrier to households’ employment hampering their ability to diversify their income. As households struggle to access employment opportunities, more and more Syrians are dependent on humanitarian aid to survive. Lack of livelihood opportunities is evident across NES with REACH reporting a lack of employment opportunities that match people's skills as barriers to meeting basic needs in 73% of communities. Moreover, most labour is precarious and insecure. The median SMEB cost in NES went up from 104,079 SYP in Jan’2020 to 195,669 SYP in Sept’2020 driving up food prices and affecting the purchasing power of households that rely on daily work. REACH reported that the average day labourer would need to work 64 days to earn the monthly cost of the basic Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) items.The activities will focus on answering those needs. Temporary employment opportunities provide a source of income and fosters the development of technical skills of CfW participants; restoration grants to local Small and Medium Enterprises will participate to increasing the job opportunities on the labour market; vocational training, based on the Graduation model, will allow the most vulnerable households to develop their technical and entrepreneurial skills while also supporting them to develop their life skills and become more resilient.

5 - Implementation of activities

Temporary employment activities will be provided to vulnerable and poverty-stricken IDP and returnee households through a Cash for Work (CfW) scheme. Beneficiaries will receive 5 USD per day (20 days per month) for a maximum of 5 hours of work per day. Skilled supervisors will work alongside the CfW participants providing technical guidance In addition to CfW supervisors, technical experts from the local departments (Water, Environment) will also provide technical guidance and supervision as necessary. Many small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) have been severely affected by the consequences of COVID-19 including the imposition of a curfew, non-essential market closures, travel restrictions and border closures and other supply chain-related disruptions. The restoration grant to SMEs is designed to support skilled workers to restore their productive assets and resume income-generating activities. This endeavour will also target women and disabled persons who have skills but lack the tools or equipment to expand into a viable income-generating activity without initial investment. This project will provide to vulnerable households vocational trainings specifically designed to allow beneficiaries to acquire or reinforce hard skills to undertake technical vocations and to develop their business management skills, enabling them to start their own business, their home business or to find a stable employment in the labour market. This project will deliver micro-business management training which will help the trainee to create and monitor basic household/small business budgets. They will also provide coaching and mentoring sessions as well as life skills training to strengthen the resilience of the households.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Among CfW teams, women will be encouraged to work in smaller teams of 3-5. Older people will also be selected, and the range of work activities will be tailored as per their capacity. People with disability (PwD) will also be encouraged to participate according to their capacities and specific needs. The NGO's system of internal referrals allows for extremely vulnerable households identified by the NGO's Protection team to be included in the beneficiaries list even when they haven not applied to be considered CfW scheme. This project will provide proper work gear/equipment (e.g. helmet, boots, gloves, googles, facemasks, etc.) to all CfW participants. They will also receive proper health and safety training prior to start of CfW activities, as it’s necessary to ensure that participants do not come to harm at work. Workers and supervisors will also receive mine-risk awareness training as a standard. For vocational training and livelihoods restoration activities, for those with difficulty leaving the home due to childcare responsibilities (in the case of women and female-headed households) or disability, the NGO will support home-based businesses tailored to the skills and capacities which the participant may already have and which the person can utilize at home. As with all the NGO's programming, this project will adhere to “do no harm” approach to ensure that any support provided will not put the participants at risk. Livelihoods staff will be trained on protection to be able to identify any protection risks on beneficiaries. This project will also ensure that female staff work with female participants.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. • Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. • Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area • COVID-19 SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
