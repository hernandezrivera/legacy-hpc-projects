Project Summary

GBV: Bahar’s objective of this project is to sustain the GBV services that were being delivered during the last 9 months in Afrin and Ain al Arab, and will expand the intervention to Ma’batli sub-district-Afrin district, through supporting the current 3 WGSSs in each of Afrin, Ma’batli, and Ain Al Arab. Bahar through this project provides two types of GBV interventions: 1- specialized GBV services for GBV survivors embodied in case management, focused PSS, and referrals, in addition to CMR and other health services for survivors in Afrin and Ma’batli only where it has health facilities. 2- GBV prevention and mitigation activities through applying a community-based approach concretizes in awareness raising sessions utilizing the Awareness Raising toolkit developed recently by the GBV SC which will target women, adolescent girls, and men, as well as, IEC campaigns, and non-focused PSS; which all will be implemented within the WGSSs and through outreach teams using diverse manners such as individual and group sessions, door to door visits, brochures distributions, group discussions, and social events, note that men and key people will be also engaged in awareness sessions on GBV since they represent the major part of decisions makers who can play a positive role in leading entrenched advocacy for fighting GBV among society. Moreover, this project includes empowerment component by providing vocational training on sewing in each of Afrin, Ma’btali, and Ain Al Arab WGSSs, and educational courses in Ain al Arab, targeting by these VTs vulnerable women and adolescent girls.
CP: Bahar aims to provide CP services in Ar-Raqqa and Ain AL Arab sub-district, through establishing 2 Child Friendly Spaces CFSs, in addition to run outreach teams covering the whole sub-districts of Ar-Raqqa and Ain Al Arab, where taking into consideration the current situation in Aleppo governorate and the inevitable ensuing psychological and social distress affecting children and adolescents in particular, and parents and caregivers in general, and the other manifold types of Child Rights’ violations; Bahar will be targeting Ain AL Arab, and Ar-Raqqa sub-district and its camps and informal settlement in the North East areas of Syria NES by CP interventions where children there have an intermittent and rare access or don’t have access at all to the basic protection services. Therefore, the CP services will be exemplified in regular PSS sessions for Children (6-17) with particular focus on adolescents >13 years old as Child labor and early marriage were one of the main CP concerns in Aleppo governorate according to the 2019 HNO 2019 Initial Analysis, in addition to parenting program for parents and caregivers, community-based outreach activities, transportation for Children with Disabilities CwD, specialized case management services, and referrals for CP cases to other humanitarian sectors when needed.

Needs

Ain Al ArabAlthough the area is considered to be stable and is recovering, GBV of all the types is continuously reported in Bahar current WGSS in Ain Al Arab; the most reported cases in Bahar’s WGSS in Ain Al Arab during 2018 were: emotional and psychological abuse, SIPV, early marriage, physical assault, and sexual exploitation, which is compatible with GBV findings of HNO 2019 stating that “gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe.” According to Bahar’s M&E surveys, it was highly recommended by the surveyed women and girls providing vocational training and educational sessions to practically empower them and strengthen the social networks through implementing broader vocational training or empowerment activities. Additionally, Ain Al Arab sub-district was ranked on 3 protection severity scale according to HNO 2019, however, children in Ain Al Arab don’t have sufficient access to child Friendly spaces CFS, where according to the WoS Protection Sector Dashboard, only 10% of the targeted community was reached through CFS intervention, which adds further pressure on the overall psychological situation of children, considering that the only CP services available in the city of Ain Al Arab are parenting program. Thus, an establishment of a new CFS in the city will be of a quite usefulness for the purpose of enhancing children and adolescents’ well-being, as well as, Children with Disabilities CwD, who are the most group in need for such interventions, considering the significant percentage of disabilities in the area which is 3.4% according to the WoS Nutrition sector figures for Jan 2019. AfrinDuring 2018, Bahar was running a WGSS in Afrin city, providing both specialized and non-specialized GBV services, Based on Focus group discussions FGDs conducted by the case manager, women and girls stated that along with the most common types of GBV, kidnapping started to come to the surface gradually, and particularly kidnapping of adolescent girls, which is also, besides many, considered one of the women and girls’ barriers to access the safe spaces, or seek help; and that was one of the main findings of 2019 HNO As Bahar health facilities started to report more cases of GBV survivors. Some cases of GBV types which have been recently reported were not existing broadly in Afrin previously, especially the early marriage and Intimate Partner Violence IPV. As in Ain Al Arab, based on the reports from the GBV centres of Bahar, women, and girls requested more vocational training which assists them to access income-generating resources and enhance their resilience. Ar-RaqqaA year after conflict ceased in Ar-Raqqa, damage to the city remains widespread, residents continue to return in large numbers despite the complex safety and security context. However, the crisis, implying psychological distress, and fear, had and still negatively affecting children and exposing them to further violence. Reach’s latest Area-Based Assessment of Ar-Raqqa city released in October 2018, states that “Early Marriage and child marriage come as one of the most issues that were reported to be common in almost half of the city’s neighbourhoods”, which 2019 HNO CP findings confirm “Recruitment of children, particularly adolescent boys, by all parties to the conflict continues to be reported in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria” and “Child marriage is reported in 12 out of 14 governorates mainly affecting adolescent girls, though adolescent boys are also at risk”. These facts indicate an imperative need to strengthen CP prevention and response interventions aiming to expand and foster community-based preventive approach through outreach activities and CFSs.

Implementation of activities

GBV:The GBV component will be covering Afrin, Ain Al Arab, Afrin, and Ma’batli by establishing 3 WGSS in each location, and providing specialized and non-specialized services. Specifically, the activities of this project are:- Awareness raising sessions for women and girls and group and individual PSS\counselling sessions.-Awareness raising sessions for men on GBV-related topics.-Training of GBV staff on GBV basics, PSS, and Case management.-Training for non-GBV specialized actors on the identification and referral of GBV cases and PFA.-Empowerment activities for GBV survivors, at-risk, and vulnerable women and girls, i.e. vocational training for women and adolescent girls on sewing in each of Afrin, Ma’batli, and Ain Al Arab (3 VT centres), and English language courses in Ain Al Arab.-Community-based outreach activities targeting the whole district of Ain Al Arab, and sub-districts of Afrin and Ma’batli, and implementing awareness raising sessions and IEC campaigns on GBV topics.-Case management and referrals for GBV survivors. CP:The CP intervention will be concentrated in establishing 2 CFS in Ain Al Arab and Ar-Raqqa, and providing the following services:-Regular PSS sessions for children (6-17 years) considering the mainstreaming of children with disabilities CwD through inclusive activities.-Providing transportation for CwD to ease and increase their access to the CFS.-Parenting program for parents and caregivers to enhance the overall environment surrounding children; with a particular focus on certain issues concerning children in the area such as child labour and early marriage.-Outreach teams covering not only the cities, but the whole sub-district of Ain Al Arab and Ar-Raqqa, and targeting mainly schools to conduct awareness raising sessions and recreational activities after coordination with schools’ principles, in addition to public spaces, and camps…etc.-Case management services for children exposed to CP concerns, and referral to other sectors.Moreover, Bahar will coordinate with MA actors in Ain Al Arab and Ar-Raqqa to provide MRE sessions within the CFSs and will aim to get supplies of MRE printing materials from those actors and distribute them through the outreach activities. Generally, in the locations where Bahar implements both GBV and CP services, survivors of children< 13 will be registered by the CP case managers in the CFSs, while the GBV case managers will deal with children>=13, surely when applicable and referral between both case managers will take place upon obtaining Children’s informed consent. Also, beneficiaries reached by each component will be reported separately from the WGSSs and CFSs as each component has different activities from the other, also, adolescent girls who attend regular PSS sessions within the CFSs will be reported to the information management officer (only) of the WGSSs in the same location to avoid any overlapping.Bahar Organization has specific procedures to ensure the accountability to the affected population throughout the entire phases of the project. The adherence to these policies is monitored and observed by the MEAL department of Bahar using multiple tools and mechanisms including the face-to-face interviews, feedback and complaints Boxes in addition to direct communication with the beneficiaries, as well as, Bahar aim to make sure that targeted groups of community contribute to the design of the program since the first phases (need assessment, data collection, and plan adaptation). Bahar, in addition, will conduct KAP evaluations and carry out regular meetings with stakeholders including beneficiaries and service providers to collect their feedback and inputs aiming to enhance the service quality.

Protection Risk Analysis

GBVProtection risk/threat 1: Resistance from the community and community leaders on GBV messaging; backlash from family or community on women participating in activities at WGSS; women and adolescent girls with child-bearing responsibilities and people with disabilities might have difficulties accessing services in the WGSS.Mitigation measure 1: Bahar will promote community engagement in every phase of the project cycle, especially of vulnerable groups (persons with disabilities, adolescent girls); ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities; adapt GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture, equip all WGSS with child-care services, provide transportation for persons with disabilities, ensure all WGSS staff are properly trained on how to identify persons with disability in need of treatment for a timely referral to relevant services.Protection risk/threat 2: Resistance from community for a woman only space; facility targeted by airstrikes- especially when PSS services are integrated in clinics in certain areas Mitigation measure 2: Bahar will promote community engagement in every phase of the project cycle, especially of vulnerable groups (persons with disabilities, adolescent girls); safety measures for low visibility and underground centers in some areas.Protection risk/threat 3: Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance; family resistance to girls mobility; lack of quality services and referral expose girls survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, reprisal and further trauma, lack of quality services also lead to fewer girls' disclosures and lack of trust in services.Mitigation measure 3: Bahar will target adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point: engage with local authorities and promote clear communication on project objectives; provide service mapping including only those that can provide girls' centered services; promote adolescent girl strategy, SOPs and awareness toolkit roll out, for all staff of organizations involved in girls' programming. CP Protection risk/threat 1: Risk of tension between communities due to CP services delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Mitigation measure 1: Bahar will ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services; utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies.Protection risk/ threat 2: Risk of doing harm to girls, boys and parents through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained.Mitigation measure 2: Bahar will ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards, services will only be provided by trained staff; establish feedback mechanism for communities.Protection risk/threat 3: Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialized services.Mitigation measure 3: Bahar will establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place and will raise awareness for CP-related topics among parents, other caregivers and community members.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Population in the targeted locations are familiar with Bahar’s WGSSs and trust the staff which will positively reflect on the number of people reached with accessible GBV and services through this project.Risk 1: Some men might not respond efficiently to the project activities which could affect the rate of beneficiaries.Mitigation 1: Bahar will target men, youth, and key persons (teachers, Sheikh, and Mukhtar) with awareness sessions to explain the objective of the project and that it’s not against social norms and traditions but its designed to protect women and girls and emphasizing their important role in society.Assumption 2: Survivors of women and girls would disclose their exposure to violence to Bahar GBV specialist due to the good reputation of the case managers Risk 2: Population will not accept the GBV project activities and the overall GBV concept or will not know about the new WGSS.Mitigation 2: Bahar’s outreach team will conduct campaigns to inform the population, women, and girls in particular, about the new safe space and types of services provided within it.Assumption 3: Communities (children, parents and other caregivers, schools, and other key persons) are familiar with Bahar’s CFSs and are open to the provided CP activities.Risk3: Communities (children, parents and other caregivers, schools, and other key persons) will not accept the CP project activities or will not know about the CFSs.Mitigation 3: Bahar’s outreach team will conduct campaigns to inform the population, parents and children in particular, about the CFSs and types of services provided within it.Assumption 4: Security situations would remain staple after the recent conflict and Bahar will be able to implement the proposed project as planned.Risk 4: A sudden change in the security situation which could make Bahar Organization unable to implement the activities in the same locations.Mitigation 4: Bahar will aim to coordinate with the new authorities and obtain any approvals needed to sustain the activities, and will be also coordinating with the respective parties/actors existed in the target locations and where applicable, Bahar will refer all the beneficiaries to other actors, particularly the survivors after taking their informed consent.
