Project Summary

In the aftermath of Hurricane Beryl, which caused catastrophic damage across Saint Vincent and the Grenadines and Grenada, children and families are extremely vulnerable. With the destruction of homes and displacement, children face heightened risks of becoming unaccompanied or separated from their caregivers, emotional distress, as well as increased exposure to physical violence, including gender-based violence, sexual abuse, child labour, and environmental hazards. This project aims to provide critical child protection and psychosocial support services to address these urgent needs. Key activities include training frontline workers and community volunteers in Psychological First Aid (PFA), gender-based violence in emergencies (GBViE), and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Project Activities

Training of frontline workers in child protection, education and shelter management sectors and community volunteers on Psychological First Aid (PFA), gender-based violence in emergencies (GBViE) and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)., Roll out of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and Return to Happiness (RTH) programme for children, adolescents, and caregivers. This will be done in collaboration with the Education sector and PAHO and UNWomen under the Health sector., Establishment of Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) within and around shelters and communities. This will be done in collaboration with our partner Global Support and Development, who will support the procurement of RTH/CFS materials., Promotion of online self-care and protection tools including the Mychild Helpline app and online MHPSS resources (young Caribbean minds)., Support frontline workers and shelter managers with information on the identification of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and procedures for family reunification, Community outreach on child safeguarding and GBV; and protection mainstreaming in Shelter and WASH responses, Distribution of PSEA materials and awareness building and sensitization sessions on available reporting mechanisms.
