Project Summary

Gender-Based Violence (GBV) – including rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices – is a persistent and serious problem in South Sudan. GBV affects men, women, boys and girls, but it disproportionally affects women and girls. According to data collected by the Gender-based Violence – Information Management System (GBV-IMS) in South Sudan during 2017, about 95% of reported GBV incidents affected women and girls. According to protection trends- (Protection Trends SS Jan- April 2017), the GBV IMS, the reported cases of GBV was 560 cases. Other GBV cases that were in this quarter included emotional violence at 16%, denial of economic resources at 10%, forced marriage at 6% and sexual Abuse at 4%. Incidences of forced marriage increased this quarter from 21 cases last quarter to 33 cases this quarter.The humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate and is further compounded by sporadic fighting and serious hunger. According to Multi-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment report done on Internally Displaced Persons. The entire IDP community is vulnerable and defenseless. Many IDPs have no place to stay as the result they are living under trees, incomplete structures etc. abduction of women and children by some armed men is high. The above backdrop also affects Panyijiar, Yambio and Yirol East counties. Panyijiar county have an influx of IDPs who have trickled into the county fleeing conflict and violence primarily from Leer, Mayendit, Koch and Guit areas of Unity State. These population have suffered multiple violence especially women and girls. Cases of GBV have been reported among these population with perpetrators being armed groups and sometime relatives. The victims suffer with trauma due to lack services or awareness of available GBV services.
In Yambio since August 2015 there have been multiple security incidents, especially in Rimenze,Nadiangere,Gangura, Bazungua, Lirangu and parts of Yambio payams which led to multiple displacements and violence disrupting livelihoods. Heavy looting, sexual and gender based violence, killing, forced recruitment and burning of homes were widely reported.
According to IRNA conducted on 25th July 2017, women were particularly exposed to dangers of rape and other GBV risks. According to the assessment 3 women were reportedly raped and had no access to health nor psychosocial support services as basic infrastructure such as health facilities were destroyed further exposing the IDPs to danger.
In Yirol East of Lakes state,the IDPs in Pagarau payam have suffered multiple conflict situation. This is as a result of inter communal conflict and cattle raiding.According to the multi-agency assessment conducted in Pagarau Yirol East,there are dire needs for GBV interventions.

Needs

Survivors of GBV need to access lifesaving psychosocial case management services to meet their complex and multiple needs. HACO established a women and girls friendly space in Panyijiar county at Ganyliel. During its interventions HACO noted an influx of more IDPs in the area who have suffered sexual abuses from their previous locations. This has caused constrains on the available resources and staffing. Cases of domestic violence are also rampart in ganyliel which are not reported since the community perceive this violence as "normal" the available referral pathway needs to be further strengthened since the personnel conducting CMR services are men and the women do not feel comfortable addressing sexual issues with men. Community based psychosocial support and counseling is also a priority due to the high level of trauma among these communities. In Yambio county GBV Services remain limited and reporting of GBV cases is low due to limited skills of service providers, and fear of stigma(Source: HACO access to justice project evaluation report in Yambio). The community is unaware of GBV consequences and the acceptance of GBV indents is still high. Early/forced marriage is a normal practice among this community. Risk mitigation measures are often inadequate or not-existent showing the need to increaseprevention efforts including community outreach and widespread awareness rising encouraging early reporting of GBV incidences. lack of dignity kits exposes women and girls to further exploitation by men and endangers their lives.The reduced access to essential and lifesaving reproductive health services, and the lack of basic items which respond to the hygiene and reproductive health needs of women and girls, make living very difficult for displaced women and girls in Yirol East to maintain acceptable hygiene standards. Displaced women and girls in Yirol East need basic items in order to interact comfortably in public and maintain their personal hygiene,particularly menstrual hygiene. Without access to culturally appropriate clothing and hygiene items, the mobility of this women and girls is restricted and their health might be compromised. Furthermore, without the dignity kits this women are unable to seek basic social services, including humanitarian aid, which increases their vulnerability to GBV.

Activities

1.Provide comprehensive case management services to GBV survivors2.Establish women and girls friendly space 3.Provide individual and group psychosocial support services (PSS) at the women and girls friendly space4.Legal awareness, assistance and counseling for GBV survivors5.Establishing and supporting of referral mechanisms6.Provision of dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls7.GBV awareness raising & prevention messaging

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

HACO has made efforts to ensure that the target beneficiaries receive information about the assistance.The beneficiaries were involved during assessments and will be part of planning, implementation, and monitoring of the project activities. A staff will also be appointed as a focal person for community to access any information they need.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Considering the difference in literacy level among the communities in South Sudan, the messaging will be tailored according to a specific context and translated to the local dialects so as to reach a wider audience.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

HACO is committed to preventing and addressing SEA as per the PSEA objective in HCT Protection Strategy. HACO will establish a complain and feedback mechanism with other stake holders so as to address emerging issues on PSEA. All staff will be oriented on the code of conduct and sign as stipulated by HACO policies.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The target beneficiaries were consulted through focus group discussions at the community level. The beneficiaries were also consulted through Safety audits. Individual interviews were conducted in Panyijiar so as to identify the needs of the girls in the area.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

HACO will establish a complain and feedback mechanism with other stake holders which will comprise of a non HACO staff to address complains about the project and its staff. A suggestion box and a specific telephone number will be dedicated for this purpose.

Community Engagement?

Yes

The community will be engaged in the project monitoring and evaluation through sharing of the activity plans project objectives and also being involved in the final evaluation through assessments and focus group discussions

The project is alive to the fact that women and girls have a responsibility to take care of the domestic chores in this communities. To address this barrier and to prevent further harm by their male counterparts, HACO will include staff members from the target community and set its activities in consultation with the target beneficiaries.
