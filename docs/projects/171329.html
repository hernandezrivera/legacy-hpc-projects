Project Summary

The project aims at providing life-saving WASH services to most vulnerable 12,000 persons (5760 women, 3840 men, 960 boys and 1440girls) of all ages in Banadir and Dhusamarreeb District Somalia by constructing latrines in IDP camps and host communities including disabled friendly latrines, rehabilitation of community water points such as berkads and shallow wells and hygiene promotion campaigns in IDP camps and host communities.
The population in need of adequate WASH services in 2021 includes approximately 1.16 million IDPs 3.36 million host communities, 53,906 Returnees and 28,002 Refugees. Among the people in need, 2.9M are children, 0.7M are women and 0.7M are men. Among those groups, the cluster also recognizes that 15% are persons with disabilities and 4% are elderly persons who will require specific attention. Persons with disabilities face additional challenges and barriers. Barriers include environmental barriers, which inhibit access to WASH facilities, as well as social barriers, leading to discrimination within communities and further aggravating the risk of exclusion to WASH services. Physical barriers are often due to the design and distance to water and sanitation facilities, which makes it difficult for persons with disabilities to have equal access to WASH services. The lack of consultation and engagement with persons with disabilities in WASH designs, limits the opportunity for an inclusive response and contributes further to the marginalization of this vulnerable group. Therefore, in regards to persons with disabilities the wash services will be designed in a manner which will make it easier for them to use. Meetings with people living with disabilities will be conducted throughout the project phases where by they will be consulted and engaged in regards to the designs and location of the wash services.
The internally displaced are among the most vulnerable people in Somalia. A combination of poor access to safe drinking water, lack of adequate sanitation facilities especially in IDP settlements and poor hygienic practices combine to threaten the survival and development of Somali population.
Limited access to WASH facilities and services pose a huge burden on women and children. Women who lack safe water are more prone to WASH related illnesses, such as hookworm infestation and hepatitis, which, when occurring during pregnancy, is linked to low birth weight and slow child growth. Women and girls are often vulnerable to harassment or violence when they have to travel long distances to fetch water, use shared toilets, or practice open defecation. The shame and indignity of open defecation also affects women’s self esteem, as does a lack of facilities for menstrual management, lack of water for washing clothes and personal hygiene. There will be consultations with women, girls, boys and men throughout the project phases to ensure equal participation of all the selected beneficiaries
As a result, the project will aim at improving sanitation conditions by construction of 50 latrines , Establishment of 20 community water points in the IDP camps and provide water to the vulnerable population, hygiene awareness campaign for 12000 IDPs and host communities to the most vulnerable conflict and drought displaced as well as vulnerable host communities/men, women, boys and girls including the elderly and people living with disabilities of all ages.

Project Activities

The project will implement the following activities :- -Extension/rehabilitation of existing water supply systems -Activities related to WASH/Safety and Protection: latrines with solid walls, locks on doors, light if justified, gender separated if justified, reasonable walking distance to water points and toilets, involvement of population in design/location of WASH facilities -Hygiene promotion campaign -Distribution of hygiene kits Constructing 20 latrines in IDPs and host communities. To preserve the safety and dignity of users, the latrines will be build separate for males and females and marked clearly in pictorial form. For privacy, secondary enclosures will be provided around facilities and the doors will also be lockable. Establishment of 10 community water points in the IDP camps. Considerations will be in place for children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities. A Raised platform, ramps and handrails on both sides of the latrines will be constructed to ensure easy access of the services for children, pregnant women and people living with disabilities. Hygiene promotion campaigns will be conducted by hygiene promoters. SSWC will ensure the promotion services are respectful and inclusive of religious and cultural practice and there is appropriate number of female hygiene promoters. The hygiene promoters will work in the selected IDP camps and host community, they will conduct door to door visits in the camp to disseminate information to the residents of the camp. SSWC will use 20 hygiene promoters in sensitization campaigns who will target 5000 individuals drawn from the targeted camps. SSWC will train hygiene promoters on how to pass key hygiene messages and how to use the tools for gathering information (Hygiene Promotion Tracking tool/Check list). The 20 hygiene promoters will be trained for two days. In the campaign SSWC will also use IEC materials to disseminate hygiene messages and provide timely information to the public. The project will provide hygiene kits for use by the vulnerable persons in IDP camps. the items in the kits will improve the hygiene situation of both at household levels and the camps at large. The hygiene kits contain sanitary pads, water containers, cleaning detergents, mats, chlorine and others.

Mitigation Measures

The risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services may occur. SSWC will ensure that the water sources are located in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement. Lights will be installed near the communal facilities. Accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls e.g. fetching water in groups will be introduced to the beneficiaries during the project implementation period. SSWC will also identify central locations for distribution points to avoid beneficiaries from traversing long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. Distributions will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centres will be central and the distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.During the project implementation the Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents may be identified . SSWC will ensure that all selected beneficiaries are aware of all the mapped out referral services through its field monitors. The beneficiaries will also be encouraged to listen to radio programs sponsored by SSWC which highlight GBV issues and inform people on the available referral services in Somalia .The Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms may arise. SSWC will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at distribution site, phone lines , setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. SSWC will also provide clear information to distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance.
