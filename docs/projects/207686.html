Project Summary

The proposed project by NRC aims to contribute to a sustainable solution by facilitating access to economic empowerment and self-reliance opportunities through integrated programming. The project will invest in comprehensive skills development, encompassing vocational training, basic literacy, numeracy, life skills, business startup kit, and in-kind support to address the holistic need of the youth. The primary focus will be on market-driven business and entrepreneurship skills for providing youth with the opportunity for employment and self-sufficiency post-skills training.
Beyond ensuring access to learning, and skills training, it will address the psychosocial impacts of crises by offering a sense of normalcy, structure, and hope for the future. Improved access to livelihood in Cox’s Bazar enhances survival skills and coping mechanisms for adolescents and youth. This response aligns with the Livelihoods and Social Development Services (LSDS) sector, NRC Education strategy, and community needs, creating provisions for livelihoods in both refugee and host communities.
NRC will work with 5 trades for examples Sewing Machine Operation, Handicraft, Teachers' Professional Development, Solar System Installation and Maintenance, Plumbing etc. for vocational education and non-formal training for the Rohingya population. An Accelerated Adult Literacy (AAL) package will be incorporated to meet the minimum educational qualification. After successful completion from the course, a business startup kit will be provided to promote livelihood opportunities. NRC will also collaborate closely with the government and other actors to create diversified livelihood opportunities for learners across different sectors. Additionally, NRC aims to target 30% of the graduates to provide further mentoring and coaching support to enhance their livelihoods pathways. This targeting is based on the interest of the graduate learners, affordability of the skill training centres, and that the new batch of skill development training receives adequate focus from the trainers.
Beyond the camps, NRC will work with the host community on vocational and skills development and non-formal training aligning with national Training and Vocational Qualifications Framework (NTVQF). In addition, business startup kits, coaching, and mentoring support will also be provided. NRC will closely collaborate with various stakeholders such as the local government, vocational training institutions, local market authorities, and other humanitarian actors to create diversified livelihood opportunities for the participants. The stakeholders will be consulted, seeking feedback, and mutual support in various emergency situations for preparedness and preventive measures throughout the project cycle.
NRC places youth well-being at its core, focusing on enrolling marginalized adolescents and youth at risk of exclusion, persons with disabilities who are currently not in employment, education, or training. NRC ensures Safe and Inclusive Programming (SIP) by mainstreaming child protection, disability, gender, and well-being in line with SDG 4 and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) guidelines. Targeted youth receive psychosocial support through the NRC Better Learning Programme (BLP), a non-specialized, classroom-based psychosocial support program enhancing mental health and psychosocial well-being.
To promote safe learning environments, the response plan involves establishing or rehabilitating learning spaces following the Comprehensive School Safety Framework (CSSF). These facilities include gender-friendly and disaggregated WASH facilities, handwashing stations, and classroom setup furniture. Vocational instructors will undergo capacity building on inclusive, protective, and socio-emotional learning. Community mobilization and awareness campaigns aim to reduce barriers to accessing skills training, particularly for female learners for increased participation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The proposed project aims to address the skills development needs of displaced Rohingya youth and vulnerable host community youth. Rohingya individuals living in camps face restrictions on access to formal education and prohibitions against work and income generation. With limited prospects for integration within the host country or safe and dignified return to Myanmar, Rohingya refugee communities in Bangladesh rely entirely on aid provided by local and international humanitarian organizations and United Nations agencies.As per the LSDS sector 2023, the PIN is 694,445, while the targeted LSDS population is 88,312 individuals. Children constitute over half of the refugee population and are disproportionately affected by the crisis, experiencing physical, psychological, and social vulnerabilities. According to the MSNA 2023, 95% of females (age 18-24) and 93% of females (aged 25-59) are not engaged in any income-generating activities, making them susceptible to risks such as child marriage, early pregnancy, human trafficking, involvement in armed groups or criminal activity, labor exploitation, and substance abuse. The youth, especially, are underserved, with interventions focusing primarily on adults or children, often neglecting education initiatives that should provide basic education.A youth-led assessment by the Norwegian Refugee Council in 2023 reveals that adolescent girls and young women face heightened vulnerability, compounded by tighter movement restrictions and more severe cultural and social constraints. Youth groups are frequently disbanded forcibly, and overall, the participation of the refugee community in directing and delivering humanitarian programs remains limited and tokenistic. Additionally, adolescents and youth aged 15 and above have minimal educational opportunities, with over 49,500 adolescents (15-18 years old) and more than 120,000 youth (19-24 years old) lacking access to any form of learning through formal education. There are limited interventions promoting vocational skill development among this population.Emergencies and displacement disrupt the transition of young people into adulthood, affecting their education and employment opportunities, as well as their sense of belonging to their communities and the community-based social fabric. Restrictive government policies against access to education and livelihood opportunities for Rohingya refugees contribute to the exclusion of refugee youth, contradicting Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4) on inclusive quality education for all children and youth. Without access to quality education, skills training, or dignified work, Rohingya refugee youth face uncertain and limited futures, unable to meet their immediate basic needs within the camp setting and at high risk of rights violations, compromising their well-being and security.Refugee youth are prematurely burdened with adult roles, experiencing forced maturity, trauma, fear, depression, and a loss of hope for the future. Additionally, young women and girls are disproportionately affected by gendered discrimination, encountering challenges related to sexual and reproductive health, such as early pregnancy, gender-based violence, and the feminization of poverty.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

NRC collaborates closely with the LSDS sector, CiC office, site management, and camp-level focal points to ensure that services are tailored to meet the specific needs of the camp. NRC adopts a targeted approach, focusing on specific age groups to minimize gaps in community intervention coverage. Additionally, NRC integrates coordination efforts with other agencies to harness synergies and prevent duplication. This collaborative approach enhances understanding of skill needs and gaps in project locations.Emphasizing evidence-based programming, NRC prioritizes sector-related data collection, analysis, and advocacy. The initiative places a premium on engagement with other actors and stakeholders within relevant coordination mechanisms, such as the LSDS sector and sub-working groups like the Disaster Risk Management Working Group (DRMWG) and the Inter-Agency Advocacy Group when appropriate. Leveraging its expertise in youth programming, NRC incorporates youth wellbeing mainstreaming and adopts a positive youth development approach.NRC contributes to identifying and addressing youth needs and gaps in vocational skills development through specialized sector-relevant technical assessments. This approach aims to overcome barriers faced by refugee youth and vulnerable host communities. Simultaneously, NRC remains committed to fundraising and engaging diverse funding partners to ensure the ongoing success of education and skills development interventions.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

NRC remains committed to mainstreaming key protection principles throughout the project cycle, including a) safety, dignity, do no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability, and d) participation and empowerment. Special attention will be given to persons with disabilities, female learners, and gender-diverse groups, with a particular focus on encouraging the participation of girls, utilizing the SAAD criteria. Protection, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, and accountability to the most vulnerable populations are top priorities for NRC. The organization has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) to encourage beneficiary feedback on interventions and modalities. NRC is actively working on making the CFM child-friendly, ensuring that feedback from children is considered, and the referral pathway includes child protection agencies. To effectively mainstream protection, NRC will foster community engagement and build community capacity to actively participate in ensuring that adolescents and youth have access to skills development activities in safe environments. NRC volunteers undergo training on PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) as part of their induction process, further emphasizing the organization's commitment to ensuring a protective environment for all involved.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The context presents specific risks for women, girls, persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups, and gender-diverse populations, who continue to encounter heightened barriers in accessing skills training services. To address this issue, meaningful participation of project participants is crucial. The project aims to enhance the involvement of these diverse groups and NRC is committed to ensure inclusivity throughout the project . All NRC skills activities and reports will incorporate sex, age, and disability disaggregated data (SADDD). NRC recognizes the gender impacts, distinct needs, and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys, and girls. The organization acknowledges that women, in particular, face socio-cultural barriers limiting their access to quality and safe learning opportunities. Moreover, there is a lack of formal adolescent girl and youth leadership among the Rohingya population, restricting opportunities to hear their voices. To mitigate the risks of delivering inequitable assistance or reinforcing existing inequalities, NRC will implement community-led creative interventions, fostering meaningful participation of adolescent girls, youth, and caregivers in decision-making processes related to girls' livelihood opportunities. Staff and volunteers will receive training on inclusion in livelihood , Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA. Community awareness sessions will cover the prevention of child labor, child trafficking, and child marriage. Physical inclusion will be addressed by identifying and implementing needed changes in learning facilities, following the Washington Group Questionaries (WGQ) approach. Information, Communication, and Education (ICE) materials and content will be developed and shared to reinforce key messages. The project will respect gender diversity, with targeted outreach and community sensitization focusing on the participation and engagement of girls and LGBTQI persons.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In alignment with the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) principles, the project's targeted objectives will be realized by providing services that prioritize information sharing, communication, feedback mechanisms, and transparency. This commitment aims to ensure participation, engagement, and inclusion through diverse gender perspectives across all phases of the humanitarian project cycle.The learning and feedback obtained will be systematically reflected through appropriate processes and pathways, taking into consideration the specific contexts and needs. This reflective approach is essential to demonstrate accountability to the beneficiaries/project participants and to continuously improve the project's responsiveness to their evolving requirements.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC prioritizes the safety and wellbeing of children and youth by ensuring that its staff and volunteers undergo training on child safeguarding. They strictly adhere to organizational policies designed to protect children and youth from sexual abuse and uphold the principles of the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The organization has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) with the goal of encouraging beneficiary feedback on interventions and methodologies employed.The CFM not only addresses general feedback but also handles reported cases of sexual abuse and PSEA. To support these efforts, a protection referral pathway is in place, ensuring that reported cases receive appropriate and timely case management. NRC is actively working to enhance the child-friendliness of the CFM, ensuring that feedback from children is considered and that the referral pathway is inclusive of child protection agencies.In order to effectively mainstream protection, NRC is committed to fostering community engagement. Communities will be empowered and equipped to actively participate in ensuring that adolescents and youth have access to education and skills training in safe environments. This collaborative approach underscores NRC's dedication to creating an environment that is not only protective but actively involves the community in the safeguarding process

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

NRC is an integral part of the Disaster Risk Management Working Group (DRMWG) and remains committed to active participation in interagency disaster preparedness efforts for responding to disasters and emergencies. The Emergency response team at NRC will play a key role in providing capacity-building training to both staff and volunteers, ensuring they are well-prepared for effective responses in times of emergencies.Furthermore, NRC recognizes the importance of environmental sustainability. In line with this commitment, the organization will incorporate principles of reduce, reuse, and recycle, making environmentally conscious decisions to minimize the impact of its activities.To harness the potential of youth and engage them in community-level initiatives, NRC will provide opportunities for youth groups to lead initiatives that address challenges at the local level. The aim is to empower these youth-led initiatives to contribute actively to community-led solutions for reducing disaster risks. This approach aligns with NRC's broader commitment to fostering resilience and proactive disaster risk reduction strategies within the communities it serves.
