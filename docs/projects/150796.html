Project Summary

Poverty, the consequences of past hostilities that continue to be felt, displacement, political instability, weak protection systems, sub-standard housing and overcrowding continue to increase vulnerabilities, especially for women, children and persons with disabilities. In addition, since March 2018, Gaza is facing an ongoing humanitarian crisis during where more than 21,000 have been injured - including 4,250 children (OCHA figures, 4 October 2018), posing serious concerns in terms of civilian protection and access to emergency health.
Through its programmes and services, UNRWA will mitigate the impact of the erosion of coping mechanisms and increasing poverty, which heightens protection risks for the most vulnerable. Thematically, specific Agency efforts will focus on Gender Based Violence (GBV), child labour and the aftermaths of the
ongoing Great March' mass demonstrations.
Operationally, UNRWA will seek to strengthen inter-programme coordination and cooperation, referral systems, protection mainstreaming, and internal capacity. The training of front-line staff will continue, focusing on basic protection principles, referral systems and GBV (response, mitigation and prevention) in emergency situations. UNRWA will also raise awareness on the protracted and current humanitarian crisis in Gaza and its protection implications for Palestine refugees through inter-agency coordination and direct contact with external interlocutors. In particular, the Agency will take advantage of visits by foreign delegations to Gaza, providing up-to-date information on the humanitarian and protection environment and advocating for refugee rights.

Needs

In Gaza, more than 21,000 people have been affected by violence from the Great March of Return, UNRWAs funding crisis has led to job cuts and triggered further instability and uncertainty. These latest developments come on top of rising unemployment (44%) alarming levels of poverty (more than half the population depend on emergency food rations) an eleven year blockade, repeated hostilities, unresolved political instability, and issues of over-crowding and sub-standard housing. This situation has eroded the resilience and coping mechanisms and left the majority of the population dependent on assistance from UNRWA. Some of the most vulnerable are women, children, and those with disabilities. In such a context, the Protection Unit responds to urgent protection needs across the massive scale of UNRWA’s operations, which provide essential services in food, education, health/psycho social support, basic sanitation and shelter to more than 1.3 million refugees (49% women and 51% men). Palestine refugee children (of all ages and gender) are particularly vulnerable to a number of protection threats, including physical and emotional violence, early marriage, detention and child labour. Child labour in particular is a worrying trend, as the number of working children (ages 10 17), both boys and girls, reportedly doubled to 9,700 over the past five years (2,900 are below the legal employment age of 15) (Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics). In 2018 the Child Protection Rapid Assessment (by the Child Protection Working Group), showed that percent of key informants knew children doing or performing harsh work. The CPWG has prioritised child labour and called for increased effort to prevent and respond to children working. Child labour can have a detrimental effect on the development of children and adolescents, compromising their physical, mental or educational development. While child labour can impact both boys and girls, OCHA reported in April 2016 that out of 364 cases of child drop out from school, the majority of the cases were boys who entered the labour market. The types of labour children engage in are gendered: boys are more likely to work in more physically demanding types of work and in traditionally dangerous locations (garbage dumps, heavy lifting, fishing, etc), whereas girls are more likely to be working inside the home, Child labour perpetuates intergenerational poverty, and can put children and adolescents at increased risk of exploitation. In the prevailing context, many refugee women of all ages in Gaza are also at high risk of harm from violence and abuse. According to UNFPA, 51% of women in Gaza are survivors of gender based violence (GBV). UNRWA data, reflecting these trends, demonstrates that the primary perpetrators of GBV are close relatives of the woman, including husbands and fathers. Specialized services for survivors are limited, and access is further compounded by existing barriers to services due to social stigma and fear that intervention will increase harm to the survivor, children in the household, and those providing services. Such precarious humanitarian conditions reinforce the Agency’s commitment to respond to material and protection needs of the refugees, including continuously advocating for the rights of Palestine refugees and especially for those who are most vulnerable, in light of a continued lack of accountability and persisting violations.

Activities

Boys and girls are removed from situations of child labour and receive adequate support - Identify boys and girls under 18 years old for intervention. Obtain consent for age appropriate interventions from families and design interventions based on a participatory approach, in order to ensure that the safety and dignity of beneficiaries are respected.- Coordinate with UNRWA services such as health, education to identify the actions needed to protect these children - Follow-up with UNRWA services on implementation of appropriate services and progress of children and their families - Coordinate with Ministry of Education on those who need to attend literacy classes - Medical examination for the prospective trainees Particularly vulnerable families no longer rely on children’s income - Social workers identify families reliant on working child's income in harsh economic situation without other options- Provide cash injection to households to relieve hard economical situations- Social worker support family, and check on progress and provide ongoing guidance on managing expenditure and building economic resilience- Organize guidance sessions with parents/caregivers in order to increase understanding of the value of education The family, as the key protective environment, is strengthened and well-informed about their children’s needs - Conduct awareness raising sessions with staff and communities to promote responsive behavior and active participation in monitoring the situation of children involved in child labour. - Liaise with other organizations (especially the Child Protection Working Group) to share information and to make referrals for additional assistance. Publish advocacy materials in collaboration with other humanitarian cluster members. - Document the risk factors of child labour and present learning at relevant forums such as CPWG, to contribute towards building knowledge and strengthening responses Acute protection situations of women, in particular GBV survivors, are responded to or mitigated. UNRWA has a standard operating procedure (SOP) for identifying, managing and referring protection cases. Identification happens in UNRWA installations including health centres, schools and relief and social service offices. Once a GBV case is identified it is managed by a social worker who designs an intervention plan. Referral support is internal to UNRWA services including legal counselling, health care, food assistance. High risk cases are referred to the Ministry of Social Development, where additional support can be provided such as police protection. In some cases cash based assistance is provided when it makes a significant difference. Interventions aim to restore dignity and integrate principles of do no harm. Cases are followed to ensure objectives are achieved and that protection threats are mitigated. Ongoing capacity building is part of the work and includes training frontline staff on the SOPs, how to detect and refer cases and managing and handling cases. Provision of relevant, timely and accurate information to raise awareness on the direct and indirect impact of the GMR on Gaza’s protection environment, support advocacy interventions and inform programmatic responses - Systematic monitoring, documenting and analysis of the impact of the GMR on the safety and wellbeing of Palestine refugees and on UNRWA service provision.- Production of weekly protection bulletins containing context analysis and updated statistics on Palestine refugees impacted by GMR- Collection and analysis of disaggregated data to identify vulnerable groups facing specific protection risks- Active engagement in and proactive provision of information to humanitarian coordination and protection mechanisms and relevant international human rights bodies- Collaboration with, and strengthening the capacity and effectiveness of Area Protection Committees through identifying and addressing information gaps and needs.
