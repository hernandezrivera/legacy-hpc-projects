Project Summary

The proposed project will address eye care needs of Rohingya population and service gaps exist in the Rohingya camps located at Ukhia and Teknaf sub-strict of Cox’s Bazar district. Orbis Bangladesh proposes to provide basic eye care services to those who are living in the Rohingya camps of these two sub-districts addressing the immediate eye health needs and Orbis will establish vision points and a mobile unit will work camp areas to conduct primary screening of children and adults. The project is for one year and the specific key outputs are as follows:
• 32,000 people screened, 55% will be female
• 3,000 children and adults received medicine and spectacles, 55% will be female
• 1,000 Surgeries planned to be performed * 250 frontline health workers and ophthalmic personnel will also be trained.
The project will integrate primary eye care into primary health system at the camp and improve the eye health service delivery at the camp to ensure prevention and treatment of the blindness. The project will be implemented through a coordinated approach and implementing partner will be Cox’s Bazar Baitush Hospital (CBBSH)

1 - Identified Needs

Preventing blindness and visual impairment can be critical in refugee populations. There is no epidemiological data on avoidable blindness and visual impairment. Orbis International carried a comprehensive situation analysis and found that there is huge need of eye care services for cataract, refractive error and human resources. This report outlines key recommendations for ensuring a Minimum Initial Service Package for eye care in refugee populations to address the needs. Key need includes: a) Addressing preventable blindness. b) Integrating primary eye care (PEC) into the refugee primary health care (PHC) system. c) Establishing guidelines for eye referrals. d) Providing basic eye services (cataract surgery and provision of eyeglasses) without discrimination, as resources allow. According to the findings of Rapid Assessment for Avoidable Blindness (RAAB) 2019 conducted jointly by IAPB Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and Orbis International, prevalence of blindness is high among Rohingya populations. Major Causes of blindness are Cataract, Corneal opacity, Phthisis and posterior segment. Uncorrected refractive error is high and spectacle coverage is very low even for presbyopia. The Bangladeshi National Eye Operational Plans call for a cataract surgery rate (CSR) target of 3,000 surgeries per million persons per year. Applying this rate to the refugee and host populations in Cox’s Bazar indicates that over 9,800 cataract surgeries would be needed each year in this district. Making provisional conservative estimates on refractive error and presbyopic correction needs indicates that well over 150,000 spectacles would need to be dispensed in this district each year. Additionally, there are likely to be about 980 blind children in the combined population, with more than 300 of these being cataract blindness.

2 - Activities or outputs

Project orientation including orientation on COVID guideline, Organize training and orientation for frontline health workers and doctors on PEC, Establish vision point at community, Perform Eye Screening, Perform cataract Surgery, follow-up, Organize coordination meeting for eye health, Develop communication materials for awareness raising, Establish referral system from community to VC to CBBSH and the tertiary hospital, Introduce digital platform for Community Health Workers to deliver primary eye care and referral services, an technological innovation to avoid mass gathering for eye screening.
