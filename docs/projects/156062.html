Project Summary

This project is aiming to support vulnerable internally displaced population and host community members to contribute towards increased resilience of vulnerable households through sustainable and restorative socio-economic interventions, with income generating activities, and Vocational Training, in addition to Implementation of 2 community supporting projects, that should benefit 2000 households of the targeted community population,

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection principles are incorporated into all phases of Bahar’s humanitarian response, and Bahar will undertake activities to safeguard both IDP beneficiaries and host community from violence, coercion, and discrimination resulting from this intervention and will aim at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individuals. Furthermore, Bahar will avoid causing harm and guarantee equitable access for all gender groups, support beneficiary participation and empowerment, and promote Bahar’s own accountability to beneficiaries. In order to avoid tension between host communities and IDPs or added risk for the beneficiaries of the project, Bahar will ensure strong information sharing on selection criteria, implementation schedule and overall targets of the project with IDPs, host community representatives and local authority. Hence, all stakeholders are fully aware the goals of the project and that their input and suggestions are taken into account.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Beneficiaries sell their In-Kind grants in order to gain immediate cash assistance instead of starting their own business. Mitigation 1: Explaining the purpose of the In-Kind grants and emphasizing on the desired long-term impact through the peer-to-peer consultancy sessions with beneficiaries of IKGs. Risk 2: Designed In-Kind grants cause conflict among beneficiaries as a result of competition in local market. Mitigation 2: Distribution of different types of IKGs with focus on livelihood aspects that contribute to self-reliance more than negative market competition. Assumption: Beneficiaries of CfW scheme are skilled to execute needed work as part of community support project appropriately and in high-quality results. Risk 1: The community support project require more/ less manpower than planned. Mitigation 1.1: Relocation of CfW beneficiaries between the two funded community support projects as needed in consultation with IOM. Mitigation 1.2: Referral of possible beneficiaries for selection by IOM as the caseload stated for each component and at the selected location. Risk 2: Gender imbalanced implementation of CfW scheme. Mitigation: assigning administrative duties and non-physical jobs for female beneficiaries of CfW with respect to social norms and traditions Protection principles are incorporated into all phases of Bahar’s humanitarian response, and Bahar will undertake activities to safeguard both IDP beneficiaries and host community from violence, coercion, and discrimination resulting from this intervention and will aim at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individuals.
