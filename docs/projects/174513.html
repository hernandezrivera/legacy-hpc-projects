Project Summary

The ongoing conflict between armed opposition groups continues to have widespread impact on communities in the Far North, particularly on their livelihoods and food security, limiting communities’ ability to meet their basic needs and exposing them to various protection risks. The two-year proposed intervention is built on the lessons learned and achievements of activities implemented by IRC through OFDA support over the past three years. It aims to provide direct support to beneficiaries to meet their basic needs across multiple services areas. These include provision of drinking water, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, the restoration of livelihoods (cash support for agricultural inputs; cash support + entrepreneurship training for non-agricultural livelihoods), and actions to promote community-based protection and prevent gender-based violence (GBV). Over the two years of planned implementation, the IRC will continue to position itself to respond swiftly to spontaneous movements with mobile services that enable access to hard-to-reach areas in persistent and precarious environments. To support sustainability beyond the project period, the IRC will also continue to reinforce local capacities to ensure a smooth exit strategy.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Lake Chad Basin, including Cameroon, has been facing incursions and attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) since 2014. Despite the presence of several state and regional armed forces, the security situation remains precarious and continues to severely impact the population. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, one million people are in need urgent assistance in the Far North region of Cameroon due to the crisis. Approximately 461,664 persons are displaced in the Far North due to the conflict, including 297,380 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 47,305 out-of-camp refugees and 116,979 returnees. Those displaced in the Far North face significant protection risks, and 64% are under 18 years old. Displacement trends do not appear to be abating; there was a 7% increase in the displaced population (33,375 people) between August 2019 and December 2019, and the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) does not predict a decrease. The departments of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga are among the most affected, with 91% of the displaced populations. Host communities receiving large influxes of displaced people have experienced added pressure on already limited basic services. In Logone-et-Chari, displaced populations represent 41% of the total population, 38% in Mayo-Sava, and 13% in Mayo Tsanaga. The ongoing crisis has also negatively impacted livelihoods and local economies in a region already characterized by endemic poverty (74.3% of the region’s population is living below the poverty line, compared to 37.5% nationally), and lack of natural and financial resources and market opportunities. Agriculture, which is the main source of income for more than 80% of the population in the Far North, has been particularly affected by the ongoing insecurity, as has cross-border and independent trade, resulting in the collapse of household income. In order to respond to the most urgent needs of the displaced and vulnerable host communities, and given the continued context of insecurity in the Far North, the IRC in Cameroon has prioritized lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance through a multi-sectoral, coordinated response to address needs in WASH, protection for vulnerable individuals, emergency basic needs and economic recovery. The most vulnerable groups impacted by the crisis will be supported to access critical services and avoid negative coping strategies through both static and mobile services as the IRC remains agile in the volatile context to address spontaneous displacements or newly arising needs, all in coordination with other actors and local authorities and community structures. The multi annual programming will help to build and strengthen local capacities and community resilience to face future hazard and shocks. Sectors included in the proposed intervention are: Agriculture and Food Security, Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS), Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), Protection, Health, and WASH.
