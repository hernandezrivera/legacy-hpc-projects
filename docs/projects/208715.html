Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to provide life-saving project activities and programmes to the affected population in Yobe state. As a result of the ongoing AOG attacks within the state. Yobe state is one of the most affected states, facing excessive attached and displacement of several communities. BCF will target (F, M ) beneficiaries in Bade LGAs, who are the most affected and where BCF has a strong presence already. BCFs response will follow an area-bases-approach in the services delivery to ensure that the affected communities will have access to basic supports which will help address GBV, Disability Inclusion, BCF will rely on its strong strategy and the humanitarian principles towards implementing these projects which will address the breaking the barrier and disability inclusion programs, effective response and prevention of Gender based Violence and Early marriage, unwanted pregnancy , street hawking and other forms of abuse which in turn will build resilience and provide promote response on protection activities, help build and strengthen innovations and skills to empower survivors, IDPs, returnees in camps and camp-like settings, host communities will be assisted through improved access to appropriate solutions including the counselling, trainings, skills, awareness and sensitization.
Prevention of Gender-Based Violence: Raise awareness and implement preventive measures to reduce the occurrence of GBV within the community.
Empowerment of Individuals: Equip individuals, especially women and girls, with the knowledge, skills, and resources needed to protect themselves from violence and exploitation.
Promotion of Gender Equality: Advocate for and work towards achieving gender equality, challenging harmful gender norms, and promoting respect and fairness.
Support for Survivors: Establish mechanisms to identify and support survivors of GBV, providing them with essential services, counseling, and resources for recovery.
Community Engagement and Mobilization: Engage community members in discussions, workshops, and activities that promote a collective understanding of the impact of GBV and the importance of combating it.
Creation of Safe Spaces: Establish safe spaces within the community where individuals, especially survivors, can seek support, share experiences, and access necessary services.
Advocacy for Policy Changes: Advocate for policy changes and legal reforms that strengthen protections against GBV and ensure justice for survivors.
Capacity Building: Build the capacity of local organizations, community leaders, and volunteers to effectively respond to and prevent GBV.
Economic Empowerment: Provide economic opportunities and livelihood skills to women and girls, reducing vulnerabilities that may lead to GBV.
Cultural Sensitivity and Community Ownership: Approach interventions with cultural sensitivity, respecting local traditions while challenging harmful practices. Foster community ownership of the initiatives.
Partnerships and Collaboration: Collaborate with local authorities, NGOs, healthcare providers, and other stakeholders to create a comprehensive and coordinated response to GBV.
Education and Awareness: Implement educational programs to increase awareness about GBV, its root causes, and the importance of fostering a culture of respect and equality.
Monitoring and Evaluation: Establish monitoring and evaluation mechanisms to assess the impact of interventions, identify areas for improvement, and ensure accountability.
Preventing Recurrence: Develop strategies to prevent the recurrence of GBV, including addressing underlying issues such as poverty, inequality, and lack of education.
By addressing these purposes, Belgra Care Foundation aims to contribute to the overall well-being of individuals and communities by fostering environments free from gender-based violence, promoting equality, and supporting survivors on their paths to recovery and empowerment.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection mainstreaming and integration involve incorporating protection and measures into various sectors (such as WASH, Food security, Education etc.). In programming and policies to ensure the dignity, safety and rights of vulnerable individuals, especially in humanitarian and development contexts. The approach is aims to make protection as an integral part of all activities, rather than a separate or standalone component.These component will be implementing various activities :Training and Capacity Building: We will Conduct training sessions and workshops for staff, volunteers, and stakeholders on the need for protection principles, human rights, gender equality, child protection, conflict sensitivity, and other relevant topics. Develop training materials and guidelines for integrating protection considerations into different sectors such as health, education, livelihoods, and humanitarian response.Needs Assessment and Analysis: BCF will Conduct comprehensive assessments and analyses to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities, and needs of different vulnerable groups in diverse contexts. We are going to use participatory approaches to involve affected communities in identifying protection concerns and priorities.Integration Policy: BCF will always review and revise existing policies, strategies, and guidelines to include specific protection components and align them with international human rights standards. Advocate for the inclusion of protection measures in national and local policies and plans.Community Engagement and Empowerment: B.C.F will engage with the community to organize dialogues, awareness campaigns, and information-sharing sessions to engage and empower communities in understanding their rights, identifying risks, and accessing available protection services.Establish community-based mechanisms for reporting and responding to protection concerns.Risk Mitigation Strategies: Develop and implement strategies to mitigate protection risks such as gender-based violence, child labor, trafficking, discrimination, and exploitation.Establish safe spaces, referral systems, and support mechanisms for survivors of violence or abuse.Coordination and Collaboration: Facilitate coordination among various stakeholders, including government agencies, NGOs, UN agencies, local authorities, and community leaders, to ensure a coherent and comprehensive response to protection needs.Foster partnerships to share resources, expertise, and best practices in protection mainstreaming.Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning: We will set up monitoring systems to track progress in integrating protection measures across programs and sectors. Conduct regular evaluations to assess the effectiveness and impact of protection mainstreaming activities, and use findings for continuous learning and improvement.Advocacy and Awareness: BCF will advocate for policies and practices that promote protection and rights-based approaches at local, national, and international levels. Raise awareness among decision-makers, the public, and stakeholders about the importance of protection mainstreaming and the needs of vulnerable populations.Adaptive Programming and Innovation: Continuously adapt programs based on feedback, assessments, and changing contexts to address emerging protection concerns effectively.Foster innovation by exploring new technologies, methodologies, and approaches to enhance protection efforts.Inclusivity and Non-Discrimination: Ensure that interventions are inclusive and non-discriminatory, addressing the specific needs of diverse groups without bias based on gender, age, ethnicity, disability, or other factors.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Establishment of variety of accessible context appropriate complaints and feedback channels. This will be achieved with the support of B.C.F Feedback and Complain mechanism (FCM) Unit by conducting regular community engagement meetings with the beneficiaries and stakeholders to receive complaints and feedbacks related to project activities through solicited channels (eg. FGDs, KIIs, help desk and in person interviews). During project implementation, B.C.F will ensure that complaints and feedbacks provided by beneficiaries and stakeholders related to program quality are used to improve the quality of project implementation and addressing them appropriately in line with the B.C.F Nigeria FCM SOP.Ensuring that the target beneficiaries understand the project deliverables. This includes ensuring that communities are engaged in community engagement meetings with State/L.G.A representative, tradition / religious leaders, youth representatives, women representatives and camp leaders to share relevant project information’s like the eligibility criteria, beneficiary selection criteria, rights and entitlements, duration of project, activities in the project and communities of implementation. This information will be shared with the above category of people with the task of extending to the entire community members and then the B.C.F Feedback and Complain Mechanism unit with support from program team will conduct a follow-up sensitization and information sharing sessions in the communities.These would be achieved through adherence to the principle of Feedback and Complain mechanism (FCM) that B.C.F committed to respect throughout it projects implementation as outlined in its B.C.F FCM standard operating procedure (SoP).
