Project Summary

The proposed project aims at restoring rural and urban livelihoods for conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria. To achieve this goal, ACTED will provide temporary cash for work opportunities to vulnerable households. These will include, on the one hand, vulnerable farmers and beneficiaries of kitchen garden kits who need an immediate source of cash to address their basic needs before the harvest; and on the other hand, vulnerable host community’s members and IDPs who do not have access to land but are nonetheless in need of cash. They will participate in cash for work scheme aimed at performing community works such as school painting, canal cleaning, road repairs. In addition, ACTED will support existing businesses and start-up with cash grants and training, enabling them to either start or restore and expand their business.

4 - Needs

In NES, livelihood opportunities are particularly limited in-camp. Off-camp, the cost of the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) has drastically increased in the past 9 months. The compounded effects of the conflict and the recent crises have borne a drastic negative impact on employment opportunities available in Northeast Syria. In addition, for small and medium businesses, difficulties in accessing the materials required for the businesses coupled with the negative effects of Covid-19 on the market, have hampered their capacity to operate. NWS witnessed a rapid decrease in available livelihoods opportunities, particularly following the Covid-19 crisis. Small and medium enterprises are particularly affected by the Covid-19 restrictions, economic downturn, displacement waves, and insecurity, which severely limits their access to stable markets for both procurement and sale. The strain on commercial activities and businesses disrupted supply chains, further reducing the job opportunities available in the area. In January 2021, in Aleppo and Idleb governorates respectively, 96% of IDPs and 91% of residents reported daily wage labor as a common source of income. The Covid-19 crisis had a significant negative impact on the informal market, severely reducing such livelihoods opportunities of people relying on daily waged labor, resulting in the adoption of negative coping mechanisms.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention aims at covering immediate households’ food needs as well as strengthening the early recovery and enhance resilience of conflict-affected populations in Northeast and Northwest Syria through a restoration of rural and urban livelihoods. This will be achieved through the following:  Restoration of agricultural livelihood opportunities through individual and community-level interventions  Support to agricultural-based businesses (to strengthen the existing value chains more broadly) Restoration of urban livelihood opportunities  Provision of cash grants to existing small-scale businesses and start-up, to enable them to restore and expand their business  Provision of cash grants to home-based businesses In addition, ACTED will provide temporary livelihood opportunities to vulnerable households through cash for work schemes. These schemes will benefit different individuals including vulnerable households in the same locations where agricultural activities take place. These households do not have access to land, however they are still vulnerable. They will takepart in cash for work schemes of a duration up to 4 months and will participate in community-based works including canal rehabilitation, school cleaning and painting, installation of trees.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the road rehabilitation. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.

ACTED immediately stops activities if observed• The light profiling methodology allows for remote KII in case physical access is prohibited Resource availability:• Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned• Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement and ACTED’s analysis of all offers led by Regional office. Evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and Regional Capital. Preparation of bid analysis, and full documentation is then done by Capital.• Recruitment related risks: process entails 2-week advertisement. Longlist prepared by Turkey sub-coordination office and shortlist by Area Coordinator. Selection process is completed after interview records are received and reference check completed by Turkey sub-coordination office HR.Community and cohesion dynamics:CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and complaints
