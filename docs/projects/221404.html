Project Summary

IMC UK is one of the organizations working with WFP since July 2016 for nutrition assistance projects for refugees and host populations in Cameroon, specifically in the North, East and Adamawa Regions. Several hundred thousand refugees of various nationalities, but mainly Central African and Nigerian refugees, have found refuge in Cameroon for several decades. UNHCR figures from the March 2023 factsheet of CAR refugees in the Adamawa region reveal that there are 77,470 refugees from CAR, of which 17,746 live in the camp of Ngam and Borgop with 2,615 children under 5 years of age. The large presence of refugees in these communities has aggravated the prevalent food insecurity situation among host communities where opportunities for income-generating activities, such as subsistence farming, livestock and small businesses are limited.
Despite the efforts made so far, anemia among women of reproductive age remains high, at 41.4%, low birth weight remains a challenge, with 12% of infants born underweight while the country has made strides in reducing stunting and wasting, 29% of children under 5 are still stunted, and 4.3% suffer from wasting5. The prevalence of stunting varies across regions, with peaks of 36.4% in Adamawa region. Preliminary results of the SMART/SENS surveys (2022) have shown that the Adamawa region has a Global Acute Malnutrition rate (GAM) of 3.8%). High rates of malnutrition are correlated with suboptimal IYCF practices. Particularly in the Adamawa region, infant and young child feeding practices remain low with an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 53.8%. Early breastfeeding initiation is practiced by only 17.5% of women. 45.2% of children aged 6-23 months in the Adamawa consume the minimum dietary diversity. The dietary profile of children aged 6 to 23 months shows that in Adamawa region, 52.7% of these children do not consume meat, fish and eggs while, 27.6% of children aged 6-23 months do not consume vegetables and fruits. The 2021 Fill the Nutrient Gap (FNG) analysis found a strong correlation between the prevalence of stunting and the level of affordability of a nutritious diet6. This study found that 61% of the population in the Adamawa region cannot afford a diet that meets their nutritional requirements, indicating that not having economic access to a nutritious diet is an important underlying factor for malnutrition in Cameroon. The project will focus on the development of mechanisms to increase access to safe, adequate and nutritious food to crisis-affected populations that include refugees and the host population in Adamawa and North regions with an interest in prevention of the risks of gender and GBV and on improving access to livelihood activities aimed at strengthening their self-reliance and support early recovery. This will include improving capacity of households to engage in agricultural and livestock activities, and setting up small businesses, which will enable them to meet their basic needs to and contribute to the empowerment of these households achieve food self-sufficiency and in turn reduce malnutrition at the community level.
The implementation of this project will require the full participation of the refugee communities and host populations and other stakeholders including the state sectors such as health, agriculture and rural development, fisheries and animal Industries and the municipality.
Overall objective: Contribute to the improvement of food and nutritional security of individuals and communities affected by crises and shocks in the Adamawa & North regions through the implementation of livelihood strengthening and nutrition-sensitive activities.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Adamawa and North regions host refugees which is one of the poorest regions of Cameroon where the local population itself is highly vulnerable. Poverty in these regions disproportionally affects women and girls, who must spend additional time performing income generating activities to support their subsistence. Forty-four percent of the surveyed households2 were headed by females with an average household size of six people, with four children under 18 years of age. These female-headed households experience a dual burden of caring for the family while engaging in income generating activities. Cameroon is exposed to climatic hazards that result in drought, floods, and landslides. Bush fires, commonly used to clear farmlands during the dry season pose a major risk of environmental destruction, especially in the savannah areas. These have resulted in an additionally high demand for preexisting and scarcely available resources like lands for livelihood activities in host communities. Furthermore, there is a growing socio-economic decline leading to increased violence, ill-health due to poor living conditions, and deprivation of cultural values, that disintegrates the social fabric of the communities. Refugees and host communities in the Adamawa and North regions are thus left with substantial humanitarian needs, among which are food, protection and access to basic services such as nutrition, health, education, and potable water that require a combined humanitarian and development response to bring about immediate and medium-term solutions. For the last 5 years, IMC UK has been implementing blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP) activities in the Adamawa and North regions with WFP funding and, as part of supporting communities to diversify diets. IMC UK with the financial support of WFP, has also been implementing livelihood activities since October 2022. The provision of a supplementary ration of Super Cereal Plus (SCP) in addition to the normal meals consumed has contributed to preventing children aged 6-23 months from becoming acutely malnourished. Through the BSFP platform, IMC UK provided other services such as education and awareness building sessions, cooking demonstration, and screening for malnutrition. The distribution sites also served as platforms for the health district for vaccination, deworming and other primary healthcare activities. Over the past years, the absence of structural improvement by the government makes the vulnerable population dependent on humanitarian assistance. Hence, this project is designed to sustain the livelihoods of the vulnerable populations for their early recovery while contributing to the improvement of the nutritional status of children 6-23moths in the targeted communities. The proposed nutrition program will, therefore, continue to target children aged 6-23 months to reduce the effects of food insecurity in this age group. This program focuses on the prevention of malnutrition in children aged 6-23 months, by providing specialized nutritious foods, and to prevent further deterioration of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) into severe acute malnutrition (SAM) for children aged 6-23 months with MAM, as there is no existing Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program for the treatment of MAM as described in the Cameroon 2017 Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition Protocol. This response follows WFP’s country strategy for 2022-2026 to prevent malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), reducing the impact of shocks on the most vulnerable. IMC-UK plans to continue to support government institutions to implement a BSFP as well as activities to strengthen and improve good nutritional practices, especially infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices within the communities to prevent malnutrition. IMC-UK will encourage and support linkages to livelihood interventions that increase food accessibility and resilience.
