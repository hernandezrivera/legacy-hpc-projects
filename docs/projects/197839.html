Project Summary

The nutrition situation in the North-West and South-West regions remains unclear due to the absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1 per cent and 7.1 per cent in the North-West and South-West regions respectively. Analysis from Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners from July to December 2021, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 1.5 and SAM prevalence of 0.4 per cent in the North-West and the South-West regions
As of 4 January 2022, 32,901 people were internally displaced according to the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM), among whom 10,000 children under 5 years and 5,500 women of child-bearing age. First screenings performed in the Domayo-Djarma IDP location has identified 10 children with wasting.
The situation related to the main underlying causes of malnutrition remains fragile with limited access to drinking water, especially for refugees, IDPs and their host communities. The availability of drinking water is slightly below the minimum emergency water standard: the average amount of drinking water available per person, per day, in the region is 13.5 liters.
Goal:
Community food security intervention through provision of Nutritional services to displaced and vulnerable host populations, especially to children 6-59 months, caregivers and pregnant women in crisis affected communities in six health districts in the North West Region of Cameroon.
Objectives:
• Empower parents and caregivers (7000 women and 2000 men) on nutrition interventions strategies and monitoring of children nutritional status
• Implement IYCF (Infant Youth Child Feeding) practices, MUAC screening of children for malnutrition and referral & treatment of SAM cases, general health sensitization.
• Improve nutrition access coverage for communities through insufficient human/material resources.
• Prevention of stunting, wasting, micronutrient deficiencies and overweight in children aged under five years
Activities
• To Support Community engagement for Behaviour and social change in 6 health districts in NW/SW for 12 months
• To provide Integrated management of severe acute malnutrition in children 6-59months in 6 health districts in NW/SW for 12 months
• To provide micronutrient supplementation programme for 2100 adolescent and Pregnant Women in 6 health districts in NW/SW for 12 months
• To provide BSFP supplementary ration to 7000 children 6-23 months and 2100 PLW givers in 6 health districts in NW/SW for 12 months
• To provide micronutrient supplementation programmes for Children under five years (14280 M, 13720M) support in 6 health districts in NW/SW
• To promote infant and young child feeding in emergencies for Complementary Feeding (including breastfeeding) in 15,000 care givers in 6 health districts in NW/SW for 12 months
• To ensure that 2000 women are registered into baby corner to promote infant and young child feeding in emergencies for Exclusive breastfeeding support in 6 health districts in NW/SW for 12 months.
• Monitoring and information systems for nutrition, including nutrition assessments, provide timely and quality data and evidence to guide policies, strategies, programmes and advocacy
• Children aged under five benefit from diets, practices and services that prevent stunting, wasting, micronutrient deficiencies and overweight
• Children aged under five years benefit from services for the early detection and treatment of severe wasting and other forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition in early childhood
• To ensure sustainability of the project and monitoring of children’s growth and nutritional status, Mothers and cares givers (12000) of children less than 2yrs will be trained on the family screening of the children using the mid upper arm circumference approach (Family MUAC approach) .

1 - Besoins / Needs

It is estimated that in 2023, more than 230,700 people including children under five and pregnant and lactating women will need nutrition services in the North-West, South-West, West and Littoral regions. Up to 48,800 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop acute malnutrition in these regions including 11,600 with severe acute malnutrition. Populations living in remote areas, IDPs in urban/peri-urban areas, and households/communities hosting IDPs are vulnerable to malnutrition since they are exposed to aggravating factors (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions, sanitation and hygiene services, and morbidity due to overstretching of basic social services). The nutrition situation in the North-West and South-West regions remains unclear due to the absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey us ing SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1 per cent and 7.1 per cent in the North-West and South-West regions respectively. Analysis from Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners from July to December 2021, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 1.5 and SAM prevalence of 0.4 per cent in the North-West and the South-West regions. However, these findings are not conclusive of the nutrition status in the two regions as there could be pockets of high nutrition vulnerability that are not yet identified. Prior to the crisis, the North-West and South-West regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36 per cent and 28 per cent in the North-West and the South-West respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5 per cent for the North-West and 58.3 per cent for South-West, of exclusive breast-feeding rates. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) was reported to be low at 31.8 per cent in the North-West and 20.7 per cent in the South-West. Approximately 40 per cent (44.3 per cent in the North-West and 41.9 per cent in the South-West) of children aged 6 to 59 months were reported to have anemia and with the ongoing humanitarian crisis, it is very likely that the poor situation of MDD has worsened. Poor host households and displaced populations will continue to face acute food insecurity as a result of the crisis. According to the results of the Cadre Harmonisé analysis of March 2022, 612,453 people in NW and 365,272 in SW (25.9 per cent and 25.7 per cent of the population of the North-West and the South-West) are in acute food insecurity (phases 3 to 5). Access to health care remains a challenge due to insecurity and persistent attacks on health facilities. 71 per cent of the facilities are functional with the majority (58 per cent) of IDPs with limited access to health care due to distance (45 per cent) and financial constraints (17 per cent). Furthermore, the weak disease surveillance system has put the population at risk of epidemics with late detection and limited response capacity resulting in repeated pockets of cholera outbreaks reported in the South-West. Acute malnutrition of children aged 6 to 59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations. Despite low programme coverage in 2022, over 1,300 children 6-59 months were admitted for SAM treatment in the North-West and South-West regions.With the major drivers of malnutrition (food insecurity, morbidity, poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacement) expected to follow the same trend in 2023, the nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate.
