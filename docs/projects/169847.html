Project Summary

In line with the Lebanon Flash Appeal food security sector objective to enhance food security through immediate life-saving food and nutrition security assistance, this project will provide in-kind food assistance for 500 households directly affected by the Beirut explosion in Nabaa area of Bourj Hammoud, Mount Lebanon. In-kind food assistance will distributed on a monthly basis for 3 months. The amount provided will cover 50% of the full daily requirement of 2,100 kcal/person/day. ). This cluster-recommended ration size in emergency situations has been adjusted from 73% to 50% to as assessed households were found to still have access to enough resources to supplement the food parcel with fresh fruits, vegetables, bread and dairy products – all of which are generally considered part of the households daily diet – which are not possible to include in the in-kind parcel. In order to ensure that households will be able to prepare the food assistance provided by distributing kitchen non-food items based on household needs assessments. In order to account for differences in household size two different food packages will be available, one for families up to 3 members (30%) and one for households with four or more members (70%). The project will target vulnerable households from all nationalities impacted by the explosion including Syrian refugee, Lebanese and other refugee and migrant worker households. Selection criteria will prioritize female-led households, elderly and and those with special needs (chronically ill, persons with disability, elderly headed households) with delivery mechanisms to be adapted as appropriate to ensure accessibility. This project will scale up an existing 12-month food assistance project for 1,000 households in blast affected neighbourhoods including Nabaa. The majority of personnel and general operating costs for this project will be covered by the funding already secured for that project.

1 - Needs

On August 4, 2020 an explosion at the Port of Beirut caused widespread destruction across Beirut with re 180 reported deaths and more than 6,000 injuries and many still reported missing . Approximately 300,000 people are without adequate housing as a result of the explosion across a number of sectors of Beirut that are home to vulnerable Lebanese households, refugees from Syria and other migrant or refugee communities. The explosion comes at a time when Lebanon was already suffering from growing food insecurity. A recent assessment reported that more than 50% of the population was living below the poverty line to the economic crisis and covid-19, the value of the LBP to the USD had decreased by 80% since October 2019. While the availability of food is a concern, with WFP and others supporting the import and storage of food supplies in response to the explosion, no food shortages are anticipated. In the days following the explosion, no increase in food prices was reported by the World Food Programme’s Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping (WFP’s VAM) unit but it remains a concern. Lack of food security is due to previous increases in prices and growing lack of employment that has been exacerbated by the explosion. According to a WFP assessment on the impact of the economic crisis and Covid-19 nearly 70% of Lebanese had either lost employment or had their income reduced . Most Syrian refugees lack proper documentation and 95% rely on daily labour in the informal sector . The WFP impact assessment, conducted before the explosion, also found that 50% of Lebanese, 63% of Palestinian, and 75% of Syrian households were worried about their food consumption in the coming month. While there were many one-off food distributions immediately following the explosion, those stocks are beginning to deplete, and PARD’s household assessments have identified that food security is again a priority concern. In order to deal with increasing food insecurity many households were found to be reducing their food consumption following the explosion including 50% in Nabaa, 70% in Bourj Hammoud and 80% in Karantina. The rapid needs assessment conducted by ACTED also identified households with people with special needs such as female-led households, disabled and elderly, as likely to be more negatively affected . Before the explosion, 19% of Lebanese, 23% of Palestinians and 32% of Syrians reported going a whole day and night without eating or skipping meals. With all nationalities including migrant workers and non-Syrian or Palestinian refugee populations being affected by the explosion, the humanitarian response is being targeted to all affected regardless of nationality.

2 - Activities

Beneficiary selection: The project will target all nationalities and participants will be selected based on household assessments by Popular Aid for Relief and Development (PARD) staff and eligible referrals from other agencies based on approved targeting criteria and ongoing assessments. When a household meets the established vulnerability criteria, the family is registered with PARD and their data is entered into a computer database. The database also helps to ensure that there is no duplication of assistance and items provided as PARD will be cross-checking beneficiary lists with WFP and other food security actors operating in the same location. Procurement: PARD will conduct a tendering process according to their procurement policy every three months for the packaging, transportation and delivery of 500 in-kind food parcels per month for twelve months. The initial tender will include the kitchen items needed for a one-time distribution. Bids will be evaluated based on price, quality, delivery time, financial capacity and accountability of the company, and previous working experience with NGOs or other agencies on similar projects. Packaging and Transportation: Packaging considerations and expected packaging sizes have been taken into account. Packaging and transportation costs will be included in the tender for the food parcels and will be handled by the selected supplier. Food distribution: In-kind assistance to cover 50% of dietary needs will be distributed on a monthly basis for three months. In order to distribute in a manner which takes into account the dignity of participant households as well as ongoing COVID-19 concerns, each household will be given a food basket token indicating the items allocated to each household for that month’s distribution and provide a date, time and location in which to come collect their food parcel. Participants will be informed of any changes in distribution and, if applicable, ration plan and reasons for such changes. All distribution procedures will be in line with WFP guidance developed for COVID-19. Should household mobility be restricted including due to self-isolation as a result of COVID-19 exposure or other specific need of the household, PARD will ensure assistance can be delivered by alternate means to the beneficiary household's home. Kitchen items distribution: Initial assessments also indicated that there is a need for cooking equipment for those whose kitchens were impacted by the explosion. An estimated 40% of participant households will receive a one-time distribution of kitchen NFI items. PARD's kitchen kit includes a standard list of items used in previous projects however this will be confirmed through the household assessment and registration process. Monitoring, Evaluation and Reporting - Ongoing monitoring and evaluation will include household surveys and focus groups conducted by PARD staff. Beneficiary input will be sought following each in-kind food distribution. Project beneficiaries will be told that any problems faced should be reported immediately to a member of the volunteer team, who will then report the problem Field Assistants. Any concerns will be followed-up immediately by the Project Manager and Project Coordinator. All reporting requirements will be fulfilled with support from MCC's external grants coordinator.
