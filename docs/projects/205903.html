Project Summary

This project is grounded in the reality of Rubkona County, where systemic change in the education sector will be needed to drive results for all children in the state, with a focus on girls and children with disabilities, while also supporting recovery and the return of refugees and IDPs. Additionally, given the impact of displacement, returnees, conflicts and crises, and extreme levels of poverty, learners and teachers will require well rounded and holistic support to ensure that they can learn and teach and that children can progress in education.
Our theory of Change will focus on conflict-sensitive interventions, that move communities from emergency to recovery. RDAA will ensure that school and out of school children in the target county have flexible, responsive routes into learning opportunities, that support their transition into further levels of education and that support their holistic wellbeing and development. A large portion of the out of school children, especially IDPs, refugees and returnees face multiple intersecting exclusionary factors, for example, young mothers, returnees, children recruited into armed conflict, survivors of conflict related sexual violence and children living with disabilities require targeted responses to ensure their access. A holist package of support that enables access, quality, inclusive practices, well-being, safety and a wider range of support will be implemented for steps towards all children’s equal participation.

1. Needs

According to Education Cluster’s 2021 Education Needs Assessment (ENA) South Sudan report, in 2021, 57% pre-primary school age, 63% of primary school age and just 13% of secondary school age children were accessing education (MoGEI, 2022 p.48). An estimated 2.8 million children were out of school in South Sudan (Education Cluster, 2021).The report continues to highlight multiple crisis drivers continue to have a negative impact on education services, disrupting access to education and learning. The development and reach of South Sudan’s national education system has been constrained by chronic crisis conditions over the last decade. According to OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2023, there are 337,629 people with humanitarian needs in Rubkona County (a sharp increase from 200,000 in 2021). This is one of the highest figures in South Sudan and the highest in Unity State. This represents approximately 98% of the total projected population of Rubkona County reported in the HNO, with all of Rubkona’s 195,678 IDPs deemed to be in need. The county is said to have acute levels of protection needs, including GBV and children protection needs, with over 50% of the population in need of these services according to the 2020 HNO. Rubkona County is home to fifty-eight (58) primary schools and eight (8) secondary schools, which are concentrated in Rubkona and Bentiu Town payams and according to UNHCR report July 2023, there are 110,385 refugees,122,573 returnees and 429,361 IDPs in Unity state alone.The project will adhere to safe programming and implement specific protection interventions and gender issues. Boys, girls, men, women have experienced the consequences of conflict and displacements differently. We will Increase girls and 'boy's participation in the project activities to identify and report persisting issues to child help desks and other forums for PSS will be strengthened. The project will strive to increase the enrolment of girls in supported schools. One strategy is mobilizing trained female teachers to serve as mentors for girl and foster safer environments for girls in classrooms. Protection shall be mainstreamed to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. We shall carry out consultation with stakeholders to determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to all beneficiaries.RDAA in consultation and in coordination with the County Education Department, state Ministry of Education will identify the location of the schools, mobilize teachers and PTA/SMCs to be trained and with local authorities identify out of school youth and adolescents for livelihood trainings. The main target of this action are 1,964 beneficiaries. Out this we shall have 1,778 learners (889 boys, 889 girls from 6 primary schools) 54 teachers (40 male,14 female), 78 PTAs members (50 male and 28 female),54 SMC members (44 male, 8 female). The beneficiaries are composed of host community, IDPs and returnee populations. We shall also have 40 (20girls and 20 boys) out of school for livelihood trainings.Beneficiary messaging will take into consideration those with less access to information due to social barriers and discrimination, we shall use a combination of existing networks of male and female groups/network leaders, Religious Leaders Networks, schools, community-based disability groups, women leaders, community complaint box in safe places, Hotlines and other humanitarian networks. During community based participatory planning, marginalised members of the community shall be given a special audience to air out their views and these views to be captured in the project implementation. RDAA in partnership with Education Cluster and HRP will increase access to education in emergency/crisis-affected children and youths in Rubkona County where both formal non-formal education activities will be carried out.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Conduct 8 community mobilisation meetings for out of school children and youth and inclusion of girls in education. Activity 2: Conduct 4 mobilisation radio talk shows for out of school children and youth and inclusion of girls in education.Activity 3: Conduct 5 teachers’ trainings on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills for 54 teachers (40 male,14 female).Activity 4: Develop and implement child safeguarding policies, staff and teacher Codes of Conduct, reporting mechanisms 1 per school for 6 primary schools.Activity 5: Strengthen capacity for child safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation in 6 primary schools.Activity 6: Conduct 10 training workshops for 54 teachers (40 male,14 female), 78 PTAs members (50 male and 28 female),54 SMC members (44 male, 8 female) and learners on referral mechanism in 6 primary schools.Activity 7: Conduct 5 teacher training workshops for 54 teachers (40 male,14 female), on psychosocial support (PSS) with disaggregated by gender, age and disability.Activity 8: Conduct 5 teacher training workshops for 54 teachers (40 male,14 female), on identification of children at risk and on referral mechanisms.Activity 9: Provide PSS, guidance and counselling to 1,778 learners (889 boys, 889 girls from 6 primary schools) at school level.Activity 10: Provide peace building and social cohesion programmes to 1,778 learners (889 boys, 889 girls from 6 primary schools) at school level.Activity 11: Mobilize 40 (20 girls and 20 boys) out of school adolescents and youth in IDP locations, returnee and host communities and provided with life-skills through various activities and relevant livelihood training such tailoring, salon, carpentry etc.Activity 12. Establishment of 6 child-friendly, accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms in each of the 6 targeted primary schools.Activity 13: Conduct monthly and joint stakeholders monitoring and evaluation.
