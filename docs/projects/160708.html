Project Summary

CRS works across Jonglei State to improve food security and livelihoods, supporting farmers' and producers' groups with seed, tools, technology transfer, and extension services, supporting producers to expand acreage of cultivated areas and diversify their sources of income along the value chain, and uses the the community managed disaster risk reduction (CMDRR) approach to support communities to build productive assets that reduce their vulnerability to natural disasters and economic shocks, among many other activities.

1 - Needs

There are many livelihoods needs in Jonglei. Though many people own livestock, there are no strong, well-equipped veterinary service providers and few resources to keep livestock healthy in a harsh environment. Though a lot of fish is harvested, little is sold due to poor knowledge and technology to handle, process, and market the fish. Climate change is taking a toll across the state, with cycles of drought and flood and outbreaks of crop and livestock pests and diseases, so there is a need for climate-smart technologies to improve crop production. Low levels of employment for youth drives violence and the risk of military mobilization in some areas.

2 - Activities

CRS's FSL Project supports the activities in the bullet points below:• Support livestock producer groups with extension and veterinary services.• Train and equip fishery-based producer groups on improved technologies and sustainable fisheries.• Support farmer producer groups with basic inputs, training and extension services.• Support farmers to improve their management of natural resources and improve crop production.• Support transfer of sustainable technologies for improved production.• Provide returnees, IDPs and disaster affected populations with farming inputs and support government staff to provide extension services to improve crop production.• Engage youth groups and women on commercial honey production, processing and marketing • Supporting farmers to produce at a larger commercial level to increase both farmer productivity and income and food security for the wider population• Build household productive assets through credit-based livestock restocking • Support HH savings, asset rebuilding and access to markets.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.4: CRS conducts awareness-raising on GBV and protection issues during FSL activities, and has strong feedback and response mechanisms and a code of conduct socialized to all staff. Anyone found to be affected by a protection concern is referred to relevant thematic service providers. The risk here is that sometimes service providers available/accessible to beneficiaries are limited in some way - they may lack capacity to fully treat the needs of the affected beneficiary. 8.12: Chiefs and community leaders sometimes try to exert their influence to distort beneficiaries’ selection criteria which can lead to unfair distribution of resources. CRS mitigates the likelihood and impact of this through careful targeting involving multiple members of the community, and beneficiary selection based on specific needs and identified household food insecurity level. The project also limits the number of participants per each household. Targeting and limits are transparently communicated to all members of the community so that they know the criteria and what they can expect. 8.16: There is a risk that women may be excluded due to protection and safety risks and the addition to their already-stretched time burden. CRS prevents and mitigates this risk by ensuring that there are guidelines on the selection of safe and secure access to project areas, close to beneficiaries' residences. CRS also limits the time beneficiaries spend on FFA activities to an absolute maximum of 4 hours a day understanding that they, especially women, have many other responsibilities. CRS also limits working hours to ensure that beneficiaries have plenty of time to return to their homes in daylight hours.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Before the set-up of the feedback and response mechanism, CRS held broad consultations with community members of diverse backgrounds, positions, and genders (for example, leaders, displaced persons, women, men, boys, girls, and others) were all consulted on the best way they would prefer to give feedback to the project. As a result, CRS has put in place several different vehicles for beneficiary feedback to ensure that different members of the community are able to access at least one of them. For example, for those community members who have low literacy, there are verbal options like help desks and interviews. For those community members with limited mobility (the elderly, those with disabilities, and those who may be busy at the home with other obligations such as mothers) the project sends interviewers to each area to speak with a broad cross-section of the community. The project employs both male and female staff to reach both male and female beneficiaries according to what is appropriate and comfortable in each community. CRS ensures that responses are shared with beneficiaries.
