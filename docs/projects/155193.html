Needs

The humanitarian situation for Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh remains dire, with a total of 901,185 Rohingya refugees who have fled systematic violence in Myanmar settled in makeshift and spontaneous settlements in Cox’s Bazar. Children under 18 represent 55 per cent of the refugee population. Congested and difficult terrain conditions coupled with restriction of movement, lack of livelihood and educational opportunities has contributed to increase the vulnerability of children to violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse. According to several reports and anecdotal evidence from Child Protection (CP) actors, forced and early marriage, child labour, child trafficking and violence against boys and girls are key child protection issues prevalent in both refugee settlements and host communities. A Joint Rapid Needs Assessment for Child Protection and Education confirmed that child marriage has been used as a means of self-protection for Rohingya households to address potential stigmatization, access to resources and ensure access to social networks. According to a Rapid Assessment on risk indicators for Trafficking In Persons (TIP) , boys and girls as young as 7 years old, have been confirmed working outside the settlement locations. Some mothers mentioned sending away their 7 to 11-year-old girls to work in the city as domestic workers due to their poor economic condition. When it comes to child labour, children are kept from participating in temporary learning centers and child friendly spaces to be able to assist their families with household chores, including firewood collection . Children reportedly bear increasingly heavy loads of firewood on longer journeys, causing physical, deleterious harms to their physical and emotional health. In addition, the needs of “invisible” children, including children with disabilities as well as adolescent girls due to cultural norms are not being addressed as their mobility to access services is restricted and few humanitarian organizations target their specific needs. Lack of interventions targeting adolescent boys is also contributing to their engagement in hazardous work and forced labour.In addition, the crisis has significantly affected hundreds of Bangladeshi hosting refugees and at peripheries of camps and settlements, many of whom were already facing a range of economic and social poverty as well as protection risks such as trafficking, GBV, exploitation and abuse. According to an inter-agency assessment conducted in Teknaf , lack of clarity around land tenure rights and ownership has exacerbated tensions between refugee and host communities and further undermined by aid disparity in service provision to host communities which has resulted in increased incidents of violence, intimidation and harassment at an inter-communal level. This calls for the need to bolster support and engagement with community level protection mechanisms and enable strategies to ensure positive coping that also includes a strong integration of resilience activities and Increase the number of reliable and operational friendly spaces for women, children and vulnerable individuals. Furthermore, environmental hazards including cyclical cyclone and monsoon conditions threatens to undermine gains and creates a profound destabilizing factor, putting children at further risk.

Activities or outputs

1. Children have access to PSS, recreational activities & awareness sessions: by integrating Child Friendly Corners in IOM WGSS and through mobile CFS, which will provide safe access to age-appropriate, disability and gender-sensitive recreational and cultural activities. PSS activities will allow to identify extremely vulnerable girls and boys in need of specialized services. The mobile approach is particularly relevant in this context as girls are often restricted to their homes, making mobile approaches paramount to reaching out invisible groups. Children will be trained on DRR to increase their resilience and their families in the event of natural disasters. Participatory monitoring will be used to measure the adequacy of interventions and identify areas to improve direct service delivery to beneficiaries. 2. Girls and boys at risk are identified and provided with quality Case Management (CM) & alternative care: children identified with protection concerns, mainly through mobile CFS and child friendly corners, will receive CM services, including UASC and other children at risk. IOM will continue working with implementing partners in the provision of adequate care and protection to vulnerable children, including CM and provision of family like alternative care. Considering the complexity of context and volume of case load, the contextually adapted CM Supervision and Coaching tools will be used in CP CM with IOM. These tools will be used to reinforce national staff capacity in supervision and coaching skills.3. Adolescents’ clubs are established & vocational training is provided: to empower adolescents and ensure meaningful participation on matters that affect them. Weekly sessions will be provided to build adolescent’s knowledge and skills and adolescents will be engaged in risk mapping and in action planning to address risks. In host-community areas, an assessment will also be conducted to understand how adolescent clubs can contribute to conflict resolution between host community and Rohingya adolescents. Vocational training will provide a unique opportunity to improve adolescents’ skills at the same time as they socialize with their peers. The provision of vocational training to adolescents will be used as a preventative mechanism that aims to provide adolescents with a brighter view of the future, in times of uncertainty.4. CBCP mechanisms roll-out: by assessing and strengthening the capacity of families and communities to care for and protect children and provide children a space where they can have a say in decisions affecting their own lives. To ensure community ownership and sustainability of the project, CBCP members will be involved in awareness-raising sessions targeting communities on key child protection issues, including child trafficking or violence against children.CP Mainstreaming: IOM CP will continue providing training to non-protection staff on child protection and safe programming and continue monitoring IOM activities, including monitoring of shelter and NFI distributions to ensure that children are protected from abuse and exploitation and actions are taken to support vulnerable children and families. IOM’s PSEA focal point is responsible for conducting orientation to all staff and a messaging plan to affected populations will be developed, together with a feedback mechanism for accountability. IOM CP will continue strengthening existing local authority mechanisms, including the provision of trainings to local authorities on child protection risks, prevention and response to ensure sustainability of the project.+A45 IOM is a key actor within child protection coordination in Cox’s Bazar and a key contributor to the CP Sub-Sector, CP Case Management Task Force, GBV Sub-Sector and the GBV Case Management Task Force and has contributed to the development of several key CP technical documents, message campaigns, adaptation of materials for inter-agency child protection trainings.
