Project Summary

A. Needs
The ongoing conflict in Syria has caused massive destruction and unimaginable losses to the Syrian people. The healthcare sector was severely affected by the war and earthquake in February 2023, which resulted in healthcare facilities being completely or partially destroyed. A large number of doctors, nurses, midwives and technicians have emigrated abroad, in addition to the difficulty of providing medicines and modern medical equipment due to their high prices and economic sanctions.
Sanjar and its countryside face a very difficult health situation. There is no healthcare center providing primary or secondary medical care services in the area, with the nearest center located at least 60 kilometers away, leading to increased rates of disease prevalence and incidence. The region suffers from a severe shortage of medical personnel and Transportation difficulties. Emergencies become more serious, leading to poor health outcomes, sometimes death. Pregnant women face inadequate care during pregnancy and the postpartum period, leading to high rates of complications and mortality. The February 2023 earthquake caused various injuries, requiring urgent specialized medical care. Sinjar and its countryside are witnessing high rates of gender-based violence, with very low levels of protection for women and children in particular.
B. Intervention, Implementation of Activities
Sanjar was chosen due to its location in the center of the surrounding areas. The project aims primarily to provide health care to all segments of society to enhance the health status and early recovery of the healthcare. The hospital provides basic primary and secondary healthcare services, through a 24-hour emergency department, internal clinic, and laboratory, with special attention to women and children through its obstetrics and pediatrics department. It also aims to provide free medicines. The project provides services to war victims, earthquake survivors and people with disabilities through physical rehabilitation. In addition to raising the protection levels for ailing individuals vulnerable to violence, especially women and children, through individual and group sessions, psychosocial support interventions, and treating survivors and victims.
C. Protection
- Exploitation or assault or sexual harassment by employees/high impact/mitigation involves a dedicated protection policy within the association to all employees and imposing penalties that may lead to dismissal for offenders.
- Distinguishing between beneficiaries when providing moderately impactful services/moderate impact/mitigation involves complaint mechanisms.
D. Strategy for protection from exploitation and sexual abuse.
Al-Birr and Social Services Association in Idleb adopts a code of conduct and a protection policy that all employees and volunteers in the association commit to. It prohibits any act or behavior indicating exploitation, harassment, or sexual abuse and does not tolerate any behavior that may compromise the dignity or privacy of beneficiaries.
The association has assistance and protection offices that receive any complaint according to confidential procedures that ensure the safety and security of complainants. Training is provided on protection policies and procedures for individuals and beneficiaries as needed.
E. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigation Measures.
• High-impact lack of electricity supply: Mitigation involves generators and a solar power system.
• High-impact depletion of drug supplies: Mitigation involves monthly delivery of drug shipments and reports about warehouse.
• Medium-impact hospital fires: Mitigation involves trainings to staff and fire extinguishers.
• High-impact theft: Mitigation involves a guard and surveillance cameras.
• High-impact natural disasters: Mitigation involves appointing emergency exits.
• High-impact equipment vandalism or misuse: Mitigation involves educating employees on proper usage.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

PSS: Emergencies, conflicts, and disasters affect protection levels in general, especially for children, women, and persons with disabilities. Therefore, the project aims to enhance protection levels, primarily through psychosocial support interventions. Regular awareness sessions will be conducted to discuss protection levels and enhance them. Interactive activities will be done for dealing with fear, anger, loss, and grief, and identify appropriate measures to reduce the harm to mental and physical health.GBV: Special importance will be given to addressing GBV by providing theoretical lectures followed by practical training using interactive mechanisms to promote individual and collective participation, enhance equality concepts and respect for human rights regardless of gender, religion, or color.AAP: In its protection policy, the association commits PAA, through the assistance office within the project. The office provides beneficiaries with access to safe and confidential complaint mechanisms when they experience any behavior that compromises their dignity, abuse, or exploitation in all its forms. The office holds regular sessions for beneficiaries to explain their right to hold staff and volunteers accountable and to file complaints and their procedures. The association does not tolerate any behavior indicating misconduct or harassment and takes disciplinary action when necessary.PSEA: Regarding sexual exploitation and abuse, the association establishes independent mechanisms to prevent these acts in all circumstances, including wars and disasters, and ensures that displaced individuals are aware of their rights, services, and how to access them. PSEA is included in media campaigns and awareness-raising activities along with training and meetings for all staff. The association ensures that victims/survivors of sexual exploitation and abuse receive the assistance and support they need without delay.M&E: Throughout the project life cycle, the association adopts a M&E strategy to study the efficiency of project implementation. This policy is implemented by the M&E office. Monitoring involves daily activity monitoring, information collection based on direct and clear indicators, and assessing working conditions. Evaluation is done by systematically reviewing outputs based on the project's main objectives. The office holds periodic meetings, conducts regular field visits and provides recommendations to all staff to improve their performance and address any issues arising.PRA: The association carries out field surveys, focused discussion sessions, periodic interviews, and review of previous projects to plan the type of intervention that suits the needs of the targeted area. We also holds workshops involving government officials and local residents to integrate various efforts for the common good. We always aims to promote collective action, empower beneficiaries to analyze their problems and capabilities and implementing them.
