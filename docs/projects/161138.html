Project Summary

The project is intended to provide shelter and non-food items to 26,970 conflict affected newly internally displaced persons, returnees, protracted IDPs and affected non-displaced host communities in need of this support in areas of Jonglei; Duk, Pibor, Uror, Bor South, Twic East; Awerial in Lakes and Juba in Central Equatoria.
HDC is static in these locations.
To promote the current move for integration through humanitarian hubs, HDC will strategize on its static position in Uror as a way to do integrated response in Uror and surroundings.
18,000 IDPs in need of emergency support will be provided with in-kind life-saving shelter and non-food items.
A small scale CBI project will be implemented in one of the areas that have functional markets especially Bor South, Twic East, Duk or possibly Pibor based on the analysis and market reports as part of resilience program towards recovery.
The CBI will target 2,000 which is only 10 percent of the total project
To ensure quality programming of the project, HDC will capitalize on or carry out the following activities;
To mainstream protection and gender, HDC will use protection experienced staff (CCE/Protection Officer) and static protection staff in the project implementation to ensure that protection risks especially for the most vulnerable are mitigated and addressed in appropriate ways e.g. use of GBV pocket guide, referrals of SGBV cases, ensuring the PSEA is signed and adhered by all project staff, vulnerable persons have meaningful access to assistance provided especially the elderly, disabled, women at risk accessing the distribution site.
To promote Accountability to Affected Population, HDC will continue to use community focal points (CFPs) in all the project activities; will be trained on project implementation process, cluster standards, handle complaints, do dissemination of information.
The team will meet with local authorities and the community for their introduction, project and cluster standards especially on casual labour rates. Informing the local authorities and harnessing their support where necessary legitimizes the response and beneficiaries are also recognized legally. This ensures safety of the project staff and minimize any complaints, challenges during implementation as the community understands the project.
There will be continuous training of field staff, CFPs and enumerators on implementation of activities for every cycle. The training will also focus on cluster strategies and standards to build on community’s resilience and coping mechanisms. A strategy to mitigate risks associated with protection that often result from inaccurate project implementation by field staff and promote cooperation with local authorities and communities.
HDC will work with its legal department to assist returnees claim their HLP (housing, land and property) rights and also sensitize the community on HLP issues. Orientation will be given to staff on HLP Due Diligence principles and standards before they go to field. Sometimes they will have to just review them each time there is a mission.
HDC will mainly work through joint missions that will also include other sectors of protection and WASH coordinated by SFPs. This is because in all previous S/NFI responses, the monitoring results have always shown that the community needed other support especially livelihood, health and education. This also aligns with the cluster and HRP strategy to promote sectoral integration.

1 - Needs

-In Jonglei S/NFI Cluster plans to target an estimated 149,740 people who are in need of shelter and essential non-food items and 34,331 in Juba (2020 HNO – S/NFI Cluster). Out of the 11 counties in Jonglei, HDC will target 5 of those (Duk, Bor South, Pibor, Uror and Twic),Awerial in Lakes plus Juba in Central Equatoria with an estimated 135,296 people affected by conflict who are newly displaced IDPs, protracted IDPs, returnees and non-displaced host community affected by conflict. Out of this number, HDC will target 8,671 people to be provided with needed shelter and non-food items. -The IPC findings and projections from August 2019 – April 2020 show that there will be acute food insecurity for Jonglei and Lakes. Duk and Pibor will be the worst affected. This report was released just as the flash floods were starting that has now affected severely Pibor. Duk, Twic East are also affected. There is great connection between food insecurity and need for S/NFIs as communities affected tend to always first sell their few belongings in order to survive.-General needs of the affected groups; Newly displaced persons often run and leave behind all their belongings and carry a few essentials especially food. This group are often in need of life-saving emergency shelter (mainly plastic sheet) and non-food items. Though there is strong expectation that the ceasefire and peace agreement will hold, there are anticipated pockets of armed conflicts that may take place which will lead to new displacements. IDPs in protracted situations in POCs, informal and formal collective sites need replenishment of their shelters as these materials are most often out of their reach because of (perceived) insecurity and broken community linkages that they might get attacked while in search these items in the surrounding host community environment. Also the materials are in some cases located far from them. IDP and Refugee returnees who return to collective sites or areas of origin are habitually in need of basic NFIs to re-establish their lives provided as a once off return package that integrates needs of other sectors as well. Those that are currently trickling in in Uror, Duk and Twic East mainly need sustainable shelter and basic non-food items; kitchen sets, mosquito nets, sleeping mats and blankets. The host community who by themselves are vulnerable due to the compound negative effects of the long armed conflict and economic crisis, are always the first to assist the displaced. This impacts further on their situation. 5% of the most vulnerable from the host community will also be target for provision of essential non-food items that they are need of. -Needs of particular categories of people; During S/NFI Analysis of needs, over 50% households in need of shelter and non-food items are headed by women and will often be caretakers of orphaned or abandoned children from their close family members. They face risks of sexual violence when fetching firewood or moving at night. Putting up a shelter for the family becomes a big challenge and risk to them; the intense labour required, moving into the outskirts of the area to collect materials, having to take care of big family that has small children under five years of age and unruly adolescents. They also feel that their complaints are not heard by their community leaders because of their vulnerability and gender. Young adolescent girls and boys face risk of SGBV and also being recruited into wrong peers/groups whenever they seek shelter from neighbours or close family members who can support them. Their parents who is often a woman that is divorced or widowed or separated will have no proper control over them which can be attributed to her vulnerability and lack of shelter or essential non-food items. Children also risk contracting malaria and other diseases associated with the cold due to lack of mosquito nets and blankets.

2 - Activities

1. Alert Field monitoring by field assistants and monitors of any needs and movements in the location and alert the cluster through the SFPs 2. Analysis planning meeting: This meeting will be conducted by HDC, in case of a joint mission it will be led by another partner or HDC once a response is approved by the SFP. The team agrees on the terms of references; desk review, program activities, tools to be used and team composition, finances and logistics. There will be particular focus on reviewing indicators to ensure activities meet the targets for M&E Purposes. -Desk review: HDC staff with a guide from the analysis report template does the exercise by contacting relevant partners and staff/partners on ground to identify protection risks, HLP issues, trends in displacements, movements and conflict within the area, etc. The information gathered helps in mission planning. -Meeting with local authorities and separately with the community for introduction of the team, activities and cluster standards especially on casual labour rates that is always a risk to the project and the staff. -Recruitment of 4 gender-represented community focal points (CFPs) and enumerators. -Training of CFPs, enumerators and field staff on methodologies of response and cluster standards and project activities and implementation process (how to conduct assessment, use tools especially phones for data collection), cluster strategies, protection, gender and HLP mainstreaming, conflict sensitivity, monitoring of activities, engagement of the community in activities (AAP) and handling of complaints. -Establishment of complaint and feedback desk and suggestion box: -Assessments including on needs through Safety Audits, Focused Group Discussions (FGDs), Household interviews, Key Informant Interviews (KIIs). -Market assessment can be conducted along with needs assessment if the area was found to have not been assessed during desk review and there is a functional market, -Reporting and analysis: The team will then produce report of the assessment exercise by filling the cluster standard analysis report template. 3. Response a. Gift in Kind Training of static staff (Field Assistants), enumerators and CFPs by Project Officer on procedures of activities implementation including targeting criteria, verification registration and issuing of tokens process. -Setting up of CFM desk and suggestion box like in the analysis stage. -Verification against target criteria, registration of beneficiaries meeting the criteria and issuing them with tokens. -Distribution of S/NFIs to selected beneficiaries. -Rapid monitoring by CCE Officer before, during and after distribution. -Team regularly reviews CFM cases and draft an action plan and lessons learnt to be immediately/ later incorporated into the project implementation b. CBI Training of staff, enumerators and CFPs on the CBI implementation process. -Set up a CFM desk and suggestion box. -Targeting and registration of beneficiaries. -Calculation of transfer value against the MEB. -Distribution of commodity vouchers for registered households. -Monitoring of the market and process of implementation and acting on complaints as the beneficiaries redeem their vouchers through selected FSPs (financial service providers) -Reconciliation of the vouchers with SPFs and their subsequent payment.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

On arrival to the field, the first point of contact are local authorities for project introduction. The team also meets with the affected community and 4 CFPs are selected from them to be involved in the project implementation.During needs assessment, the community is consulted on how best the assistance can be delivered.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is based on cluster objectives that are linked to national objectives.The project will ensure that assistance is delivered in a safe and accessible manner without causing of harmThe project targets mainly the internally displaced persons who are vulnerable

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

To do this, a desk review of the environment on protection risks and other concerns is done prior to the team landing in the place. Also the areas where HDC is to respond we have static presence.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

A group of HDC staff received a number of trainings organized by the cluster on handling HLP related issues. HDC also as a protection partner to UNHCR implements HLP projects in Juba, Bor and Malakal.S/NFI team will always consult with our HLP department especially during desk reviews. Desk reviews are vital in finding out any protection risks especially those related to HLP before the team departs to the field.During assessments on ground, the questionnaires are updated with HLP sections to help identify related issues early during the analysis stage before any response. The identified issues are forwarded to legal department for advice on way forward. The distribution exercise is inclusive with local authorities, chiefs are made aware to provide protection to the staff and beneficiaries and also the items. The team also hires CFPs, crowd controllers, guards to ensure that there is as much safety as possible. Before the team departs to the field, a detailed and clear targeting criteria are set. These criteria are communicated in advance to the stakeholders.The CFPs and enumerators are also trained on how to identify the persons that the project is targeting.The use of CFPs ensures that the project goes beyond the gate keepers and also any interferences are communicated early in time for a prompt action.The staff are also trained in keeping the ethics and principles of humanitarian work. The CCE/Protection Officer is dedicated to ensure that correct procedures are followed by the staff. During analysis stage, safety audits are conducted before the assessment of needs to identify potentially safe areas also unsafe ones plus other risks to guards against.The CFPs are also a reliable source of information in finding safe locations for the beneficiaries that are also accessible by those with protection needs especially those living with disabilities.These locations are often as possible close to the beneficiaries; public places, airstrips, etc
