Project Summary

Currently, about seven million South Sudanese are in need of some forms of humanitarian assistance being food, health, nutrition, shelter, water, among others. However, malnutrition had been a major public health concern in the country immediately after the war broke out in December 2013. Multiple assessment reports indicated that, the leading causes of death being directly or indirectly among children under five are Acute malnutrition related. The number of 6-59 months children who are acutely malnourished and are in need of treatment is about 860,000, the number of SAM and MAM cases are 260,000 and 600,000 respectively while the number of Pregnant or lactating women who are acutely malnourished and are in need of treatment is about 597, 000 (IPC Phase 5, 2019).
Jonglei State has the highest proportion of people estimated to be food insecure between May and July 2019, with 73% of its population facing Crisis acute food insecurity or worse. Though the entire country is affected by food insecurity but Jonglei State has the highest proportion of people estimated to be food insecure between May and July 2019, with 73% of its population facing Crisis acute food insecurity or worse (IPC Phase 3, 2019).
Nyirol County has suffered several shocks that has aggravated the state of human suffering in the county. The FSNMS round 23 report has indicated that the GAM rates in Nyirol county is 19%. This report also indicated that 15% of households in Nyirol are in severe emergency due to hunger. The current IPC classification has put Nyirol county in IPC phase 4 for both food security and acute malnutrition. Currently floods has displaced over 90% of the population who have lost their assets and livelihoods as such it is anticipated that many children and women will suffer from acute malnutrition in the nearest future. Coupled with the fact that most of the households in the county are headed by women, the likelihood of adherence and coping with the shock would be difficult and this would tantamount to severe suffering.
Chuil Payam is located in the remotest part of Nyirol County cut off from the main service centers like Lankien, louder distress calls of high food insecurity have been heard. There has been reported influx of IDPs from Nasir, Baliet, and Malakal. Chuil itself has received little humanitarian assistance ever since the start of the crisis.
In light of this context, DARO seeks to support treatment and preventive services of acute malnutrition in these five payams of Nyirol county in 2020. Under this proposed project DARO will be implementing the following activities:
• Management of children 6-59 months with SAM through outreach sites in each of the targeted 3 payams.
• Management of Moderate Acute Malnutrition PLW and under 5 years through mobile outreach sites in each of the targeted 3 payams.
• Provision of counselling to mothers and caregivers of children 0-23 months
• Provision of Vitamin A supplementation and Deworming to children 6 – 59 months.
• Conducting CMAM training for Nutrition workers
• Conducting MIYCN training for nutrition workers.
• Conduct trainings for mother to mother support groups on hygiene education to help prevent diseases and acute malnutrition amongst children 0 to 23 months.
• Conduct training of CNVs on MUAC screening, defaulter tracing and follow-up.
• Supporting establishment of kitchen gardens
• Training of PLWs on skills of kitchen gardening
• Conducting cooking demonstration to PLWs in order to prevent malnutrition
The total budget required to implement the project will be $100,000.

1 - Needs

As we are approaching the end of 2019, South Sudan is still in a serious humanitarian crisis due to the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and now coupled by natural disasters such as flooding. The livelihoods of many civilians have been destroyed and many of them have been forced to flee their homes. Currently, about seven million people are in need of humanitarian assistance being food, health, nutrition, shelter, water, among others. However, malnutrition is and had been a major public health concern in South Sudan immediately after the war broke out in December 2013. Multiple assessment reports indicated that, the leading causes of death being directly or indirectly among children under five are acute malnutrition related. The number of 6-59 months children who are acutely malnourished and are in need of treatment is about 860,000, the number of SAM and MAM cases are 260,000 and 600,000 respectively while the number of Pregnant or lactating women who are acutely malnourished and are in need of treatment is about 597, 000 (IPC Phase 5, 2019). Though the entire country is affected by food insecurity but Jonglei State has the highest proportion of people estimated to be food insecure between May and July 2019, with 73% of its population facing Crisis acute food insecurity or worse (IPC Phase 3, 2019). Nyirol County which is targeted location in this project has suffered several shocks that has aggravated the state of human suffering in the county. The FSNMS round 23 report has indicated that the GAM rates in Nyirol county is 19%. This report also indicated that 15% of households in Nyirol are in severe emergency due to hunger. The current IPC classification has put Nyirol county in IPC phase 4 for both food security and acute malnutrition. Currently Nyirol county is suffering from floods which has displaced over 90% of the population who have lost their assets and livelihoods as such it is anticipated that many children and women will suffer from acute malnutrition in the nearest future. Coupled with the fact that most of the households in the county are headed by women, the likelihood of adherence and coping with the shock would be difficult and this would tantamount to severe suffering. Chuil Payam is located in the remotest part of Nyirol County cut off from the main service centers like Lankien, louder distress calls of high food insecurity have been heard. There has been reported influx of IDPs from Nasir, Baliet, and Malakal. Chuil itself has received little humanitarian assistance ever since the start of the crisis. For these reasons DARO is proposing to provide nutrition services in hard-to-reach resource constraint populations in Chuil (Thol), Pading and Waat Payams. DARO will target a total of 14,804 beneficiaries who are in difficult to access locations through provision of mobile nutrition services. DARO will work with the existing partner for TSFP and OTP services in order to be able to access supplies and all reports for the activities will be submitted to the nearest OTP/TSFP site where the partner will be able to share the reports to the Nutrition Cluster.

2 - Activities

For DARO to realize its strategic operation the continued scale up of treatment of acute malnutrition targeting the current and future caseload is a high priority. Further expansion of services to previously insecure areas such as Chuil (Thol Payam) for both treatment of severe acute malnutrition to reach such less accessible areas. Attention will be given to addressing other factors identified as major contributing factors to acute malnutrition to prevent acute malnutrition in the Payams like Waat, Pading. DARO will work closely with other partner on ground such as Oxfam in pultruk and Nyabor Payams. The prevention efforts shall focus on child care practices including improving quality of food consumed by children, treatment and prevention of childhood illness. Supporting access to fresh food to vulnerable households, including children and pregnant and lactating mothers.Under this proposed project DARO will be implementing the following activities:• Management of children 6-59 months with SAM through outreach sites in each of the targeted 3 payams. • Management of Moderate Acute Malnutrition PLW and under 5 years through mobile outreach sites in each of the targeted 3 payams. • Provision of counselling to mothers and caregivers of children 0-23 months• Provision of Vitamin A supplementation and Deworming to children 6 – 59 months.• Conducting CMAM training for Nutrition workers• Conducting MIYCN training for nutrition workers. • Conduct training for mother to mother support groups on hygiene education to help prevent diseases and acute malnutrition amongst children 0 to 23 months. • Conduct training of CNVs on MUAC screening, defaulter tracing and follow-up. • Supporting establishment of kitchen gardens• Training of PLWs on skills of kitchen gardening• Conducting cooking demonstration to PLWs in order to prevent malnutritionWith the above activities DARO will reach 2,000 Children under 5 years of age with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM U5), 1,623 Children under five years of age with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM U5), 1,980 Pregnant and Lactating Women with Acute Malnutrition (MAM PLW), 1,399 Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), and 7,802 Vitamin A Supplementation and deworming (VASD).
