Project Summary

The overall purpose of this project is to strengthen protection of the right to health for all Palestinians in the occupied Palestinian territory. The project comprises four objectives:
1. Enhance monitoring of attacks on health care;
2. Enhance monitoring of barriers to health access;
3. Enhance advocacy for the right to health, including the prevention of health attacks and improved health access;
4. Enhance the integration of a human rights-based approach to health and empowerment for patients and health workers.
Expected beneficiaries include 52,000 patients (12,000 in Gaza and 40,000 in the West Bank) requiring secondary care outside Ministry of Health facilities and 3,200 frontline health workers (1,000 in Gaza and 2,200 in the West Bank) susceptible to attacks on health care in the occupied Palestinian territory

Implementing Partners

World Health Organization will continue to work closely with partners in the Ministry of Health and Palestinian Coordination and Liaison Administration for the purposes of:
- Strengthening data collection on barriers to health access and attacks on health care (the latter within the global Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare or SSA)
- Capacity building for the right to health, including monitoring and reporting obligations for human rights treaties and conventions relevant to the right to health and collaborative work to define an indicator set to strengthen right to health monitoring.
Other key implementing partners for work on monitoring of barriers to health access and attacks on health care include:
- Palestinian Red Crescent Society (PRCS)
- Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS)
- UNRWA
- Union of Health Workers Committees (UHWC)
- East Jerusalem Hospital Network
- Terminal authorities for exit monitoring from Gaza
WHO intends to build the capacity of local partners for advocacy on the right to health through work within the Health Cluster to implement the oPt Advocacy Strategy, adapting messaging and ways forward in line with health needs and priorities.
WHO will also continue to work closely with UN and human rights organizations to facilitate availability of quality and timely data to support advocacy, legal processes and accountability mechanisms.

Needs

The right to the highest attainable standard of health is protected in International Humanitarian and Human Rights Law. This right encompasses the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of health care. It also incorporates aspects of life such as access to adequate food, shelter and safe drinking water that are considered underlying determinants of health. Crosscutting components of the right to health include participation in public life and the right to health-related information, as well as fundamental principles such as gender equality and non-discrimination. In 2018, there was a substantial increase in attacks on health care with the Great March of Return in Gaza. From 30th March to 6th October, 3 health workers were killed and 462 injured in 270 recorded incidents against health staff, transport and facilities. 92% of those affected by attacks were men and all affected were adults of working age. 8% of injuries were with live ammunition, 4% were from shrapnel and 11% were from direct hits with gas canisters. In the same period, there was damage to 75 ambulances, 5 other vehicles used to transport health goods and services, and 2 health facilities. WHO globally aims to improve monitoring and advocacy through its Attacks on Health Care programme. Obstacles to access in the occupied Palestinian territory persist for patients, patient companions, health staff, ambulances, medical supplies and equipment, posing a major barrier to the functioning of the health system as a whole. In 2017, patients from Gaza faced the lowest approval rate for permits to exit via Erez since WHO began its monitoring in 2008, with almost a half of applications (46%) delayed or denied. Three-fifths (60%) of patients that year missed at least one appointment because of delay or denial of a permit application. A third of patient permit applications from Gaza are for people with cancer. One in eight (16%) of those applying are under 5; a third are children under the age of 18 years; with a fifth over 60. Almost a half (48%) of patient applications are for girls and women. Patient companions face even lower approval rates for exit via Erez, with less than half (44%) of applications approved in 2017. Since 2012, there has been a shift to an increasing reliance on older, female relatives to accompany patients. Men and women aged 18 to 40 years, and men aged 41 to 60 years, have significantly lower rates of approval (20-30% approval rate) than women aged 41 to 60 or men and women over the age of 60 (approval rates over 50% for these groups). Health staff exiting Gaza face greater barriers (44% approval) than those attempting to enter (78% approval). In the West Bank, 90% of ambulances entering East Jerusalem from the rest of the West Bank are made to undergo back-to-back transfer of patients. Health access is particularly difficult for vulnerable communities in Area C, the Seam Zone and H2 of Hebron. Long-term shortages and depletion of essential medicines and medical disposables affect access to lifesaving treatments for patients, especially in Gaza. This includes depletion of drugs for chemotherapy, biological therapy and hormonal therapy for cancer patients. In the first three quarters of 2018, on average, 47% of essential medicines and 28% of essential medical disposables had less than a month’s supply remaining. In September 2018, 40% of essential medicines were completely depleted. Vital equipment for effective diagnosis, such as nuclear medicine imaging for cancer, and treatment, such as radiotherapy, are lacking. Timely access to effective treatment for cancer patients also depends on early detection and appropriate medical treatment before any surgical intervention. Outcomes for cancer patients in the Gaza Strip compare poorly to international standards. 5-year survival for breast cancer, for instance, is 60% compared to over 80% in Israel. Initiatives to strengthen patient empowerment improve outcomes for cancer patients.

Activities

1. Enhance monitoring of attacks on health care In 2018, WHO implemented its Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA). The SSA is an online global system for strengthening data collection on health attacks, aiming to improve reliable and impartial data; advocacy to prevent attacks and protect health workers; and build best practices. WHO oPt conducted workshops in 2018 to expand the number of SSA partners registered to input, incorporating those who are most susceptible to attacks - including the Ministry of Health, PRCS, PMRS, UNRWA and UHWC. WHO was able to build on its existing system for data collection with key partners, including the Ministry of Health, to implement this system. Of the five countries in the initial implementation phase, the oPt recorded the highest number of incidents and injuries to health workers, in the context of Gaza’s Great March of Return. 2019 planned activities:- Provide training and equipment for key partners to improve timely and quality data collection for attacks on healthcare- Provide disaggregated data on health attacks to health, protection and human rights partners for effective advocacy and reporting, and strengthened accountability 2. Enhance monitoring of barriers to health accessSince 2008, WHO has worked with multiple partners, including MoH, the Coordination and Liaison Administration, terminal authorities and local authorities, to improve the collection and disaggregation of data on health access, establishing a system for regular monitoring and reporting. Analysis is carried out monthly, with production of WHO monthly and annual reporting on barriers to health access. Gaza patient access data is of high quality, but requires improvement in the West Bank. Broader barriers to health access, and implications for the health system, require strengthening. 2019 planned activities:- Provide training and equipment for key partners to enhance timely and quality data collection for barriers to health access- Provide disaggregated data on health access for health, protection and human rights partners for effective advocacy and reporting, and strengthened accountability 3. Enhance advocacy for the right to health, including the prevention of health attacks and improved health access:- As Health Cluster lead agency, develop a health sector-specific advocacy strategy with health partners, on the basis of the advocacy strategy of the Advocacy Working Group- Conduct capacity building activities through workshops with Health Cluster partners, including local NGOs and the Ministry of Health, to strengthen health advocacy activities and response - Produce advocacy materials on the right to health, incorporating messages on health attacks and barriers to health access in Gaza 4. Enhance the integration of a human rights-based approach to health and empowerment for patients and health workers:- Develop an indicator set for monitoring the right to health with the Ministry of Health and key health and human rights partners- Enhance referral pathways for the major patient groups requiring referral out of Gaza through in-depth analysis of structural gaps, processes and patient outcomes to inform service strengthening and advocacy messaging- Carry out workshops and focus groups with patients and health workers to enhance the capacities of patient groups and community advocates to improve participatory models of care reduce barriers to health access
