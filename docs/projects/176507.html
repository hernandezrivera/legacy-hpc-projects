Project Summary

According to HNO and IPC 2021, More than half of South Sudan population, 7.24 M people are in need of humanitarian assistance. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic, 1.6m people remained internally displaced and 2.2M are refugees. The IDPs, refugees and returnees are putting pressure on the limited resources by host communities.
According to IPC reports, the number people with acute food insecurity has been worsening over the past 2 years 2019 and 2020. IDPs 28% and returnees 36% live in places where there is no active food market, they depend on host communities and humanitarian assistance for food. Lack of food markets and crop destruction has been identified as a major barrier to food security.
This project will focus on objective CL4/CO2 “Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)” and CL4/CO3 Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. This project will target 50,000 HH (300,000 Individuals) to improved food security and livelihood across 15 counties. Segregated by gender
Men = 40,000
Women = 60,000
Gils = 100,000
Boys = 100,000
The following activities will be implemented
1. Community mobilization and sensitization of households
2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to targeted households.
3. Formation and training of lead farmer groups in every county on identification and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods.
4. Training of Agricultural extension workers, per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication.
5. Provision of farm level support of farmers in every county on improved production/modern farming methods.
6. Providing farmers with emergency livelihoods support such as crop kits, fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds
7. Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these counties.
8. Mobilization and sensitization of communities and identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs)
9. Collection and distribution of Livestock Kits to the CAHWs and regular replenishment.
10. Conducting monitoring, surveillance and reporting of diseases in the target areas.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has remained in protracted crisis in 2020 due to instability and lack of sufficient investment in essential service provision. Covid-19 pandemic, floods, and intercommunal conflicts arising from cattle raiding, housing land and property have worsened the humanitarian situation. According to HNO and IPC 2021, More than half of South Sudan population, 7.24 M people are in need of humanitarian assistance. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic, 1.6m people remained internally displaced and 2.2M are refugees. The IDPs, refugees and returnees are putting pressure on the limited resources by host communities.According to IPC reports, the number people with acute food insecurity has been worsening over the past 2 years 2019 and 2020. Aweil South, Pibor and Tonj North have populations in IPC 5 bordering catastrophic while the majority of Upper Nile state and Jonglei state has populations in IPC4. According to global support unit, Tonj East and Tonj South too are in IPC5 (catastrophic). IDPs 28% and returnees 36% live in places where there is no active food market, they depend on host communities and humanitarian assistance for food. Lack of food markets and crop destruction has been identified as a major barrier to food security. About 1.4 million south Sudanese children under the age of 5 are acutely malnourished. This can be attributed to food insecurity, flooding and covid-19 that interfered with market functionality and household food production. Due to floods, households’ crop was submerged and animals swept away. There is an urgent need for food assistance for households in IPC 4 and IPC 5. This assistance needs to be complemented by emergency livestock support crop and vegetable farming in order to transition households to recovery. This project will focus on building adaptive capacity among community members, to provide them with crop kits, vegetable kits, farming and fishing kits. The project will also aim at building capacity of households and farmers in climate resilience agriculture, proper farming harvesting and food storage. Households will also be trained in proper fishing techniques, fish preservation. Coalition for Humanity aims to address the identified needs in the following counties. Akobo, Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Tonj East, Tonj North Mayom, Pariang, Panyijiar, Koch, Rubkona, Rumbek North, Maiwut, Ulang, Longechuk, This project will achieve the following activities as falling under CL4/CO2 “Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)” and CL4/CO3 Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks” This project will target 50,000 HH (300,000 Individuals) food security and livelihood across 15 counties. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for humanity has a PSEA code of conduct signed by all project staff and enumerators, all humanitarian staff, community leaders are taken through 1 day training on PSEA and sensitize the community members are also sensitized on their right to access the humanitarian assistance without being exploited

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project has designed a house-to-house monitoring form to be used by community leaders and and village weather and agriculture information officers, the form will be used to follow up on households that are not able to use suggestion box, they will provide means in which they can be contacted by phone, or will give verbal feedback to extension officers to document. The Post distribution survey will be administered verbally and entries made by literate enumerators who will collect feedback. households will be provided with telephone contacts to call.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Coalition for Humanity has engaged with various communities affected baseline survey, focus group discussions, community consultations before and during the development of this Project. Moving forward, Coalition for Humanity will continue engaging the communities through risks assessment, GBV related risks discussions, community engagement meetings/Consultations (CEC) and PDMs involving the affected community before and during the project implementation. The community needs will be verified through focus group discussions and Key Informant Interviews with leaders. A project inception meeting will be held with local authorities involving women, men, boys and girls to ensure participation and feedback. A feedback desk and complaint box will be set up during distribution with beneficiaries able to register complaints. Coalition for Humanity will adapt participatory monitoring and evaluation. A post distribution survey will be conducted with the affected community. The survey will seek feedback on the conduct of activities and satisfaction by the affected persons. Affected community will make recommendations for future

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from community will inform the Food insecurity situation, GBV risks around distribution points, most needy Payams, Bomas, most affected population, and this will inform prioritization at community level. Feedback on needs beyond this project will be referred to other partners in the county who have capacity to handle cases beyond this project. Further feedback will inform change in strategy, and future proposals

8.11. Mitigation Measures

There is a risk of external stakeholder interference, Coalition for Humanity will conduct a stakeholder engagement meeting at the start of the project to define objectives of the project, their role and responsibility. The meeting will spell out mechanisms on how beneficiaries are identified. Coalition for Humanity will hire non partisan enumerators to support registration and distribution process. There is a risk of minority, people with disability, women, girls and boys not participating effectively in the project. Coalition for Humanity will conduct focus group discussions targeting people with disability, boys, girls, women separately to make sure their needs and concerns are captured. Coalition for Humanity will ensure latrines constructed are segregated by gender and are disability friendly. Coalition for Humanity has a PSEA code of conduct, all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, Community leaders are also trained on PSEA to raise awareness in the community so that beneficiaries can know they have a right to humanitarian aid without their dignity being compromised. Beneficiaries will be provided with suggestion box, telephone contacts for feedback. They will also have an opportunity to participate in post distribution survey on beneficiary satisfaction. Beneficiaries will also be given an opportunity to channel complains through local authority.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The provision of food assistance, and livelihood crop, vegetable and fishing kits will improve food production. The food production will improve food supply to the markets and food stores at household levels. This will intern increase food consumption, reduce malnutrition hence improving the IPC status of population under this project.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Women children elderly and people with disability are the most affected during floods and conflict, they live in IDP camps with limited access to source of livelihoods. This project prioritizes support to this groups, to given priority as beneficiaries for food assistance, crop, vegetable and fishing kits hence improving their livelihoods and IPC status

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Malnutrition is directly linked to lack of access to stuffiest balanced dietary intake. The support of households with food assistance, crop, vegetable and fishing kits will improve access to food at household level and supply of food to the markets. This in turn will improve dietary intake hence reduction in acute malnutrition among children.
