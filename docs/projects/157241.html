Project Summary

This project is planned to contribute in protection of children, men and women from being maimed, killed and injured as result of landmines/ERW related incidents in most affected areas in Sudan. The project will focus on displaced population, returnees and communities currently living in or close to experienced landmines/casualties in hard to reach and conflict-affected areas, including Baw, Giussan, Altadamon and Alkurmuk localities in Blue Nile and Kadugli, Dalami, Habillaa and Alrief Alshargi localities in South Kordofan State. The overall objective of this project is enhance access to Mines/risk knowledge among 143,200 at risk individuals and will enhance and sustain local MRE local capacities in Blue Nile and South Kordofan states through dissemination core of MRE messages using various techniques, tools and methodologies
The action will target most affected segments of the communities (students, farmers and animal herders, IDPs and returnees ) with specific and tailored MRE messages through direct presentation , public performance, training and capacity building, community based mine risk education approach will be adopted though capacitating and strengthening the community based change agents and formation of MRE community based structures. GAH will also assist the at-risk populations in hard to reach areas in the two states to build knowledge about dangers of Mines& ERW in communities as well as enable them to identify and adopt measures to reduce the risk posed by Mines& ERW through developing, publishing and broadcasting of MRE messages in attractive forms of song and drama through radio in local languages which is proved to be very effective mean for transmitting of messages in inaccessible areas
GAH will enhance the capacity of the community to understand their mines/ERW problem and reintegrate MRE within the overall community development plans. GAH will identify and build the capacity of 500 community resource persons including ERW survivors on landmines/ERW technical knowledge and communication skills and equip them MRE material to disseminate the messages to their respective communities. Moreover, GAH will provide the mine action information management system with the relevant new Data, and support the de mining teams and other mine action partner by community liaison and information sharing, in addition to advocacy for the CRPD for the ERW survivor.

Needs

South Kordofan and Blue Nile are the 1st and 2nd most landmines/ERWs-contaminated states in Sudan respectively. In Sudan there are 204 open hazardous areas based on IMSMA January 2019 report. In Blue Nile, there are 20, In South Kordofan, 137.The same states have also borne the most casualties of landmines/ERWs-related accidents in the country. The ongoing conflict in two states hinders physical access, particularly for international NGOs to the areas where mine action is most needed, severely impeding the operations of demining and MRE teams. Landmines and ERW are considered as significant threat to the safety and security of the local population in these areas. Landmines/ERW contamination causes social and development disruption, preventing the local population from their normal human activities, little emergency assistance can be provided, and economic development is hindered to take place in these areas.The presence of landmines/ERW posts a significant and one of the primary physical risks to death and injury of children both boys and girls and adult as well in humanitarian contexts. In addition, their presence exacerbates psychosocial distress among children, their care givers, families and communities and their impacts on family livelihoods can leave children at greater risk of violence, abuse and exploitation. Women and girls on the other hand are also vulnerable either as direct victims or as part of families who become even further economically, socially and psychologically marginalized by the loss of key income earners and burden of care for accident survivors. Incidences are occurred due to lack of knowledge about the risk of landmines/ERW or for economic and domestic reasons. Women are collecting water, children gathering firewood or playing, men working on the land or tending cattle. Anyone who goes about their normal day in a mined area is at risk. Based on the role women play in society and their daily routine jobs are more likely to expose to landmines/ERW danger. The action is urgently needed to support save resettlement of IDPs and returnees, especially for in the affected parts in the two states, because their homes were in disputed areas. Initially, the numbers of causalities by mines/ERW are very high. The majority of victims were children herding cattle and domestic animals. However, there were also some reports of farmers who were injured or died as a result of farming on un-cleared mine fields. This planned project will reduce the number of incidents in village’s residential sites close to minefields and dangerous hazards areas. These landmines/ERW represent a new danger to IDPs and returnees as most of the strike locations are close to villages and/or pasture and agriculture lands. The threat thus significantly facing post-conflict communities in many parts two states. It is therefore landmines/ERW Risk Education is the most practical way of decreasing accidents, death, casualties and injuries

Activities

Formation, Training, equipment, and deploying of 5 MRE mobile Teams consist of 20 personal (10 Male and 10 Female), 5 team leader and 15 trainers, to target 5,600 people at Risk in prioritized areas in the two States for 10 months. GAH MRE teams disseminate MRE messages through participatory approach using different methodologies including (Interpersonal Communications, Direct MRE Sessions, street theatre, songs and puppet show. The team is planned to organize 2,640 sessions (440 sessions per team with average of 50 per session. In total 110,000 will be reached with direct MRE messages in the target areas (39,300 boys, 45,300 girls, 10,500 men and 15,000 women)Printing and distribution of MRE Information Education and Communication (IEC) material (leaflets, posters and booklets) for 8,400 beneficiaries (2,700 boys, 2,600 girls, 1,100 men and 2,000 women) Conduct MRE 20 Public campaigns targeting 10,300 beneficiaries (2,900 boys, 3,600 girls, 1500 men and 2,300 women)To select and train 500 community volunteers (50% Female, 20 trainings, 5 days each, 25 person per training) including Mines, ERW survivors to act as MRE Recourse Persons in the targeted areas) to pass the important messages among their respective communities Develop, record and broadcast MRE Radio Messages though the local radio stations in the two states targeting 14,000 at risk communities in the two states (2,200 boys, 2,700 girls, 4,500 men and 4,600 women) Conduct 2 KAPB survey per and post to the project activities to measure the behavior and attitude change among the targeted communitiesReport any mew DA, SHA and mine/ERW accident to IMSMA. The team is trained on using IMSMA formats.
