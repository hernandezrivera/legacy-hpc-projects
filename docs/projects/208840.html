Project Summary

Through an integrated mine action and gender-transformative protection response, the project will enhance the protective environment of vulnerable communities, especially vulnerable women and girls, affected by mine-related incidents. Based on the assumption that strengthening the female protective environment passes both by enhancing their inclusion in key community initiatives and addressing their specific protection risks, DRC and its implementing partner will pull together their specific expertise to 1) strengthen female leadership in key prevention initiatives related to mine incidents and Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and 2) provide a community-based protection response that directly prevents and mitigates mine action and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risks, to which women and girls are particularly exposed.
The innovative approach that consists of integrating mine action and gender-specific concerns into the protection response will particularly materialize through the implementation of HMA and GBV-sensitive protection monitoring tools, specific capacity-building for female community members on mine action and GBV prevention, and the strong interaction between Psychosocial Support (PSS) and prevention activities on mine incidents and GBV

1 - Needs

While the UNMAS recently raised the need for explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) to be delivered to the affected people in rural communities, the DRC’s Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted in October 2023 shows that 48% of the respondents do not have knowledge on Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) in Adamawa, including 41% in Madagali, Michika, and Mubi North LGAs. The PSNE assessment in Adamawa LGAs hosting Cameroonian refugees (2023) shows that farming is considered as being the riskiest activity in terms of mine incident exposure (38% of the respondents), followed by herding domestic animals (14%), children playing (10%), collecting scrap metal (10%) and collecting firewood (10%). In Borno, the DRC MSNA highlighted that around 34.4% of respondents in Monguno and Ngala have limited to no knowledge about ERWs. This observation is substantiated by key informant interviews, revealing occasional ERW awareness campaigns in Monguno, which are insufficient for comprehensive community education. Conversely, Ngala Local Government Area requires urgent capacity enhancement for involved organizations due to significant growth, necessitating additional resources to effectively address ERW-related issues and more worrying even in Bama and Monguno more than 49% of people that never attended a risk education session while it has been seen through several FGD discussions since October 2023 that those were areas where landmines could be found while going to the farm. Women and girls are particularly exposed to the risks of ERWs due to their heavy involvement in farmlands to avoid adopting negative coping mechanisms such as transactional sex. In addition, women and girls led to move away from their house for farming, attending school, collecting firewood or fetching water are highly exposed to GBV (such as rape). Women and girls particularly raised a need for support for safe spaces as coping mechanisms to help them deal with stress and express their trauma, including a commonplace to play/socialize. DRC’s MSNA shows that specific community awareness on GBV is also needed with high prevalence rates of domestic violence in Madagali (49%) and Michika (48%), and cases of teenage pregnancies often resulting from transactional sex and exploitation. In Borno, In Borno state, particularly in Askira Uba, tensions between men and women have escalated due to programs related to GBV prevention and female participation. This perceived rejection has led to a surge in domestic violence. According to FGD participants in Ngala, Bama, Pulka, Gwoza, Banki, MMC, and Jere, women and girls are the primary victims of GBV in all locations. Across all age groups (men, women, boys, and girls engaged in FGD), 70-98% of perpetrators include men, Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), security forces, and intimate partners and many of them have had to use negative coping mechanisms including survival sex but were also survivors of different forms of sexual exploitation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC is committed to facilitating inclusive participation of girls, women, boys and men across all relevant age groups elderly and vulnerabilities (especially PwDs) throughout all phases of the action. While DRC’s AGDM policy ensure this commitment is met, DRC will build on its own expertise to mainstream the beneficiaries’ inclusion, consultation, participation, and information in all the programming and will follow action-specific guidance on accountability to ensure that all practices are harmonized and allow for a greater inclusion of the beneficiaries by consulting them through FGD, KIIs, feedbacks surveys. The implementation team will share regular feedback to communities through disaggregated FGDs to inform and confirm findings from assessments.While the proposed action is built on the multisectoral needs identified by and with the communities, DRC always engages the beneficiaries from the beginning by engaging in extensive consultation, coordinating with and informing governing bodies, local community leadership and key community gate keepers on the proposed response (including NEMA/SEMA, LGA chairpersons, ward counsellors, local government leaders recognized by the government and forming a hierarchy starting from Bulama to the Emir as the highest level of local leader). DRC will also engage with security personnel to explain why the partners are operating in the areas, helping to minimize the risk of security forces interfering or imposing additional restrictions. Beneficiaries will be included during meetings held with community leaders and members to introduce the objectives and activities of the action. Community volunteers will be selected from the targeted communities, trained, and supported to promote local acceptance/ownership of the program and improving local capacity. Inclusion will materialize through the participation of people of concern in determining the types of activities conducted and the information they would like to receive.
