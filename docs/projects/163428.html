Project Summary

The project focuses on the urgent needs of women and girls affected by the humanitarian crises in the country: Boko Haram crisis in Far North region.Overall goal of the initiative is to ensure that women and girls lead, participate in, and are empowered by and benefit from humanitarian response and recovery efforts by meeting specific needs of women and girls as well as mainstreaming gender in humanitarian actions. It also contributes to the prevention and mitigation of violent extremism, addressing some of the key drivers of radicalization such as limited economic opportunities. It builds on the success of UN Women’s humanitarian work since 2014 in response to the refugee crisis from Central African Republic(CAR) in the East and the Boko Haram crisis in the Far North, much of which was supported by the Government of Japan (GoJ). These consecutive initiatives have improved the lives of thousands of women and girls by providingan integrated service package at women cohesion spaces created in the refugee camps.

This project consists of four outcome areas:
• Outcome 1: Women and girls in affected areas have increased protection against gender-based violence (GBV)
• Outcome 2: Women and girls have new sources of income and economic opportunities
• Outcome 3: Women’s leadership and engagement is increased in community
mobilization, social cohesion and decision making
• Outcome 4: Gender analysis and assessments are reflected in multi-sectoral humanitarian crisis response planning and programming

Besoins / Needs

1. Sexual violence perpetrated by armed groupThe safety audit reports combined with the risk and vulnerability mapping clearly show that highly militarized areas present numerically important high-risk locations. These reports also indicate that most perpetrators of rape are especially men in armed group who are not very often worried by justice. In the same vein, there is not enough capacity building for armed forces on international protection and particularly in the area of sexual and gender-based violence to influence behavior. 2. The influence of the environment in prevention activitiesThe socio-cultural environment has a significant impact on prevention activities. There is a need to adapt and model behaviors through behavior change communication that is part of a benefit-based approach that explains what is gained by protecting vulnerable women and men on human rights that has an injunction value and inscribes fear into the mentality. 3. Need of official documentationThe vulnerability of women and girls in the context of humanitarian crisis is also reflected in the lack of official documentation that gives them legal personality. It is the national identity card, the birth certificate and marriages certificate that lead to their protection in private and public spheres. the detention of these documents by women and girls will reduce the incidences of sexual and gender-based violence committed either by armed groups. 4. Increased needs for protection against gender-based violence (GBV) of various forms, including early marriage and sexual exploitationA humanitarian crisis magnifies the existing power inequality, leading to various forms of violence particularly against women and girls. Absence of law and order coupled with unmet basic needs put them at heightened risk of violence. Also, traditional harmful practices such as early marriage increase in humanitarian settings. And yet, due to socio-cultural norms and stigma attached to victims among other factors, many cases go unreported, which perpetuates the cycle of vulnerability, violence and impunity of aggressors. This invisible nature leaves GBV considerably under-addressed and underfundedcompared to the risk and estimated level of incidences. The comprehensive approach has resulted in identifying and providing services to more than 800 GBV survivors as well as 225 Boko Haram escapees. Nevertheless, given the dire situation of women and girls in refugee camp, IDP settlement and host communities in the context of on-going insecurity, enormous needs remain for GBV services and systematic coordination. 5. Livelihood opportunities as critical life-saving needs for womenIn conflict situation where people flee from homes, lose property and livelihood, women and girls bear the brunt of the consequences, as the existing gender inequality increases their vulnerability.Recognizing the linkage between women’s economic dependence and GBV risk. The field evidence suggests that given the required inputs such as skills, materials and supportive organizational framework, displaced or vulnerable women and girls can be empowered to increase their income to meet their basic needs adressed.The comprehensive approach has resulted in identifying and providing services to more than 800 GBV women and girls survivors as well as 225 Boko Haram escapees in refugee camp, IDP settlement and host communities.This project consists of four outcome areas: • Outcome 1: Women and girls in affected areas have increased protection against gender-based violence (GBV)• Outcome 2: Women and girls have new sources of income and economic opportunities• Outcome 3: Women’s leadership and engagement is increased in community mobilization, social cohesion and decision making • Outcome 4: Gender analysis and assessments are reflected in multi-sectoral humanitarian crisis response planning and programming
