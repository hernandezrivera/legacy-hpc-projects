Project Summary

Concern Worldwide plans for a multi-sectoral refugee response in 2024 focusing mainly in West and South Kordofans where the number of South Sudanese refugees are significant.
This intervention will link to the HRP Refugee Response Strategy for 2024 by strengthening life-saving and the protection environment for refugees, to enable access to timely assistance and equitable access to basic services in their home communities.
Our 2024 refugee response plan targets 35000 individuals in both South/West Kordofan states through a multi-sectoral programming that will integrate health, nutrition, WASH and FSL activities. Under the Emergency Shelter/NFI sector intervention, the programme will distribute 500 NFI kits, and rehabilitation of 2 water yards, Construction of 2 communal latrine in addition to 6 water supply by water trucking. Under the FSL sector intervention, we will focus on distributing emergency food rations for 15000 Individuals, provide agriculture support for 1200HH and livelihood support for 1200HH, mainly for refugees who have got safe dwelling but limited or no livelihoods and food security resources as a result of the conflict in Sudan. Under the health sector, the project will deliver Inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition with complictions for children 6-59 months(SC) (targeting 120 individuals), Outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition without complictions for children 6-59 months (targeting 4500 individuals), Increase access to health services by supporting mobile medical units and referral pathways (Targeting25000 individuals), will do antenatal care sessions to provide essential primary and secondary health care services (Targeting 35000 individuals)for vulnerable refugees, and their host community population. Integration of nutrition and institution WASH services in the targeted health facilities and their catchment settlements will be the priority focus, through treatment of moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM) treatment for refugee children under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactative women through CMAM approach. The proposed plan is integrating WASH services in the same catchment locations by renovating water facilities that would provide water for the targeted health facilities that would increase the general hygiene promotion in the region. Targeting will follow the RRF approach and will prioritize localities where needs are critical, and response were limited in the last period. Overall, the response will focus on the cluster recommended localities and states, however there are some areas e.g. Meriam in West Kordofan State with existing needs and gaps, therefor response will be utilized in these locations, as well, subject to funding.
Concern Worldwide will mainstream cross cutting issues such as S/GBV risks mitigation, conflict-sensitive targeting to minimize exploitation that are usually associated with the fact that refugees have to share the same resources like host community population, now triggered with high IDP caseload in most of our Kordofan regions. In order to reduce these risks, flexible programming e.g. using cash-based support instead of in-kind assistance, where possible, joint PDM for service deliver monitoring etc. will be applied. Based on Concern's current present in West and South Kordofan, response will mobilize local networks, available social structures especially in places where refugee settled with local/host community population.
Concern Worldwide refugee response plan follows the RRF recommendations, as well as the cluster recommendations (e.g. for NFI/shelter costing), although local prices and availability of resources will be also considered, and may play an impact during the roll out of the action.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities, including refugees, we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups, including refugees within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create if it is not properly designed. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, refugee mothers and their children from the host communities who walk many hours to reach the centres, as well as for distributions, will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behavior change activities at community level. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. In the community where refugees settled, there will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly one female, and one male) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in high focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Concern's CRM system that was updated in 2022 will play an important role: the programme will work with CRM officers across all operation areas, and we will activate the CRM reporting system including sensitive and no-sensitive reporting. Each health facility that we are targeted will serve as a CRM hub where one male and one female person will be identified and oriented on how to receive report and manage individual cases. Post-distribution monitoring will be organized after each in-kind, cash etc. deliveries (max. two weeks) that will be a support and opportunity for participants to feedback about the services received. PDM feedbacks will inform the programme team about the next step for the activity (including future programming), and also will provide support in case finding (both sensitive and non-sensitive) and address the complaints and feedbacks. Our intervention has a large value in-kind distribution (cash and non-cash based), therefore PDM will be a ‘must’ exercise to roll out for the programme. Feedback will also help the programme adapt the quality of services delivery e.g. health treatment, also the quality of the food distributed (like/dislike) choices will support programme team to improve the services.
