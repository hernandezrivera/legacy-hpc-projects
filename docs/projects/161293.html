Project Summary

Food insecurity and malnutrition needs are strongly linked to poor WASH standards especially amongst PLW and Under 5s. IPC projections for the first quarter of 2020 indicate a decline in food security in the locations targeted, with populations estimated to be in phase 3 and above as follows; Twic East – 70% (81,905), Bor South – 60% (196,549,) Terekeka 45% (110,916) Yei – 20% (54,239), Morobo – 25% (26,026) and Kajo Keji – 20% (44,380). An increasing number of people face severe food shortages in the locations targeted; The increased number of people facing acute food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in the projection period are also expected to contribute to increased prevalence of acute malnutrition that require urgent and significant scale-up and intensification of humanitarian services.
Access to safe water in the locations is less than 30%, with less than 20% of the populations practicing safe excreta disposal. Provision of WASH services will reduce occurrences of malnutrition amongst U5s and PLW as well as reduction of gender based violence in the quest for water.
CIDO targets 20,000 persons with much needed WASH services inline with the cluster strategy of Integrating WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package;
Integrating WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level;
Provision of timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees and mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence .
The latest economic update indicates a significant decrease in the number of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor thereby unable to purchase essential WASH NFIs such as soap.
Protection issues related to access to WASH further hinder access to WASH services with the project targeting mainly women and the elderly.
Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is a national NGO with adequate footprint in the counties of Yei, Morobo, Kajo Keji, Terekeka, Bor South and Twic East and is currently implementing interventions in the thematic areas of Nutrition, Health, NFIs, WASH, Protection and livelihoods. We work with mainly the most vulnerable targeting mostly female-headed HHs and have reached over 20,000 people with Equatoria and Jonglei with humanitarian support.
CIDO believes an integrated response will ensure visible gains in the areas targeted and will work with cluster leads UNICEF and NRC to ensure adherence to strategy, standards and practices.
The project targets 20,000 people (65% female) as follows: Yei – 5,000; Morobo – 3,000; Kajo Keji – 2,000; Terekeka – 2,000; Bor South – 4,000; Twic East – 4,000. 58% of these are IDPs, 5% PWDs.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in parts of Kajo Keji, Morobo, Yei, Terekeka, Bor South and Twic East in former Central Equatorial and Jonglei States continues to be affected by effects of conflict and displacement. Dire needs are manifested especially amongst children, PLW, PWDs, women and the elderly, which cut across the thematic areas of nutrition, WASH, health, protection, food security and livelihoods. IPC projections for the first quarter of 2020 indicate a decline in food security in the locations targeted, with populations estimated to be in phase 3 and above as follows; Twic East – 70% (81,905), Bor South – 60% (196,549,) Terekeka 45% (110,916) Yei – 20% (54,239), Morobo – 25% (26,026) and Kajo Keji – 20% (44,380). Challenges in physical access due to insecurity, flooding and long distances continually hamper community efforts to gain stability. Majority of the populations in these locations are agro – pastoralists, and have been diversely affected by issues ranging from flooding, loss of livelihoods, livestock diseases, poor harvests and insecurity. An increasing number of people face WASH challenges in the locations targeted; The increased number of people facing acute food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in the projection period are also expected to contribute to increased prevalence of acute malnutrition that require urgent and significant scale-up and intensification of humanitarian services especially in the thematic area of WASH. Over 68% of the persons in need are women, girls and children. The most recent Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMS) indicates that multiply displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecure households than host communities with female-headed households also having higher prevalence of food insecurity a possible indication of gender-based vulnerabilities. The same observation is seen in WASH data. More than 70% of the populations targeted practice open defecation; with girls often subject to harassment in their search for dignity. Hand pump breakage is often due to over usage and lack of proper maintenance mechanism; resulting in non-functionality and exerting pressure on the available bore holes. Communities rather resort to unsafe sources that are deemed more accessible hence getting exposed to water related diseases. AWDs directly contribute to malnutrition especially amongst children, Women and girls are the primary direct users of WASH services and GBV risks have significant implications for the success of WASH interventions (SS WASH Cluster, 2019). REACH bulletins further indicate more women and girls feel unsafe accessing a water point or latrine in due to long distances and fear of sexual violence and abuse (SEA), hence majority of these resorting to alternative avenues to access services that are below acceptable standards. 35% of assessed settlements reported that most people did not feel safe in their settlement. CIDO will provide WASH services at health centers, nutrition centers and schools) and to IDP communities.

2 - Activities

• Carry out and participate in needs assessments (agency, inter cluster and inter agency) in the locations of Kajo Keji, Morobo, Yei, Terekeka, Bor South and Twic East in former Central Equatorial and Jonglei States; • Rehabilitation/repairs of the non-functional water supply facilities such as boreholes, hand dug wells.• Provision of appropriate and safe sanitation services while considering people with special needs including people with disabilities, women and girls through construction/rehabilitation of communal latrines at displacement sites, supporting and promoting household latrine constructions, construction of institutional latrines for schools, nutrition (OTP/SC) centres and health facilities• Distribution of WASH NFIs including buckets, soap to enhance WASH conditions of communities. These will include MHM/Hygiene kits for women and adolescent girls. • Distribution of household water treatment tablets including PuR and Aquatabs on a case-by-case basis to HHs and communities. • Training of community WASH members comprising of hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water management committees with at least 55% female representation. • Carry out hygiene and sanitation awareness promotion in the locations of Kajo Keji, Morobo, Yei, Terekeka, Bor South and Twic East in former Central Equatorial and Jonglei States; • Facilitate coordination in the locations of Kajo Keji, Morobo, Yei, Terekeka, Bor South and Twic East in former Central Equatorial and Jonglei States;
