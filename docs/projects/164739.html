Project Summary

This project will provide the primary and secondary healthcare services to cover the urgent needs of the northern rural of Idleb, the project aims to support Alsham Central Hospital which operates by AHF in Sarmada in Idleb.
The hospital will serve over than 4,000 patients per month in the different specialized services we offer for free, the two main surgical services we currently provide are orthopedic & general surgeries & consultations for these surgeries, we also have one neurology specialist and one Internist doctor, who also specializes in Cardiology, provide consultations in the Emergency Department & the follow-up with our ICU inpatients, we have 76 staff members including 8 specialized doctors and 6 resident surgeons working in the 8 sections of the hospital:
1- Operation theatre with 4 rooms, 2-ICU with 4 beds and 4 ventilators, 3-Inpatient Departments has 14 beds (10 for men + 4 for women), 4-Surgical clinics (2 clinics: general + orthopedic), 5-Laboratory Department, 6-Radiology Department, 7-Emergency Department (5 beds).
Alsham Central Hospital was under Hama Health Directorate before, & according to the Directorate, the hospital was covered 75% of Hama's healthcare needs in its areas of specialty (primary care, consultations, orthopedic and general surgeries), the hospital will improve the surgical services and will participate in saving lives of PIN from IDPs and LCs in the area.
Hospital beneficiaries in many cases are multiple trauma patients & multiple trauma is a challenging task, & every minute can make the difference between life & death.
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. The project is designed according to the need assessment of the local community. As well as We will conduct surveys to obtain the feedback. Priority has been given to the principle of non-harm in the project by working. We will verify of the activities do not pose a risk to the patients especially women, children, and the people with disabled are not exploited or harassed.

4 - Needs

1- After the last military escalation, the situation and the needs in NWS has been changed, & a lot of new gaps shown up, CCCM Cluster tracked 201,492 individuals/38,755 HHs moved to different communities in Aleppo and Idleb governorates between 1 - 16 May 2019. This brings the total number of IDPs displaced from Northern Hama and Southern Idleb to 239,647 individuals/45,653 HHs between 1 April 2019 and 16 May 2019, and the largest IDP recipient subdistrict is Dana with 105,288 individuals received where we will response with ambulance services, 90,410 are in camps and reception centers and need the ambulance services badly to have the access to the PHC centers. According to WHO SURVEILLANCE SYSTEM FOR ATTACKS ON HEALTH CARE (SSA) a total of 49 health facilities are partially or totally suspended activities which provided on a monthly average over 171,000 medical outpatient consultations, over 2,760 major surgeries and supported 1,424 newborn deliveries, and these numbers confirmed with HHD's report named ''Report on the humanitarian and health situation in Hama'' published on 15th May 2019 and shows that 4 hospitals were directly targeted by warplanes, 6 PHC centers were targeted directly and 2 ambulances were destroyed (1 of them belong to our ambulance system), so 16 health facilities have been suspended out of 19 exists in Hama, also 2 hospitals in the close rural of Idleb were targeted in the last 2 weeks (Kafr Nobol hospital and Has hospital) and the 2 health facilities are out of service.As can be seen from the mentioned reports, the health care system is suffering badly and in a very poor condition, it is similar to the collapse of the health system, with about 800000 population in Dana, and a high pressure on the health services in rural of Idleb because of the huge number of IDPs. Alsham Central Hospital one of the first hospitals in NWS, it has provided tens of thousands of major surgeries and hundreds of thousands of services through the last 6 years.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: average, effect: high mitigation: Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for patients –Allocating the guard to regulate the flow of patients –Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism and activating the role of the M&E team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with these problems. -Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts 2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly and the disabled): possibility: average, effect: high mitigating measures: Employing female staff to ensure that services are being provided women–Ensuring that the wheelchairs are available for elderly and disabled people - Activating the role of the monitoring and evaluation team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with the project problems. 3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: average, effect: high mitigating measures: A comprehensive services mapping of the area and selecting accessible and safe locations of HFs. Monitoring the risks: Post-monitoring/ feedback forms are used. Resources Required: Monitoring staff. Acceptable Level of Risks: Yes.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the health cluster and the TWG, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention. the Equipment are available the place is available, the staff are available the required supplies and medication available the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators Risk 1: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas) Risk 2: Conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors and shifting alliances. . Mitigation: If any unseen circumstances happened, we will take the following actions: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations..
