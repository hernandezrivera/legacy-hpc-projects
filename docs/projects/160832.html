Project Summary

HI’s intervention aims to improve food-security, access to essential goods and services, and support sustainable livelihoods for acutely vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities in Yei, Morobo and Torit. The first component will adopt an integrated and phased approach consisting of unconditional cash-transfers, livelihood development, savings and asset transfer to promote household resilience among acutely vulnerable populations. The project will target Yei, Morobo and Torit, all IPC3 classified locations, and where HI already has existing presence. For Yei the fluctuating security situation and sporadic influx of IDPs provides a suitable entry-point from which to conduct core project activities, and in Morobo these inputs are vital to support existing populations and returns whilst preventing additional pull-factors into Yei. In Torit increasing number of returns and the fragility of markets and food availability also provide a timely and suitable environment for the intervention.
The projects second component will provide cross-cutting support to mainstream disability inclusion among FSL partner responses across South Sudan, addressing exclusion and ensuring the safe and equitable access to food and livelihoods support. Specifically it will focus on promoting the collection of disability disaggregated data, barrier analysis and staff capacity building with the aim of identifying and removing all barriers to food items and livelihoods interventions. The projects first component aligns closely with the 2020 FSL cluster objective CO3 - Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks, as activities are aimed at promoting self-reliance and resilience of acutely vulnerable populations. The second cross-cutting project component directly contributes to CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations, as it will build their capacity to identify and support acutely vulnerable groups and persons with disabilities in IPC 4-5 locations in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, the protracted nature of the crisis, including soaring inflation, disrupted livelihoods and food scarcity have increased the need of all community members for basic services and livelihoods, especially that of persons with disabilities. Despite relative improvements in the security situation throughout the country, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are still classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). The nexus between extreme vulnerability of populations and food insecurity are evident in South Sudan. For many groups including persons with disabilities, informational, physical and attitudinal barriers prevent access to essential food items, cash-based assistance, livelihoods support and essential services. In addition, such groups often lack the social capital and means to turn to traditional coping mechanisms in event of food scarcity, creating greater risk for their health and survival. An integrated quality of life survey conducted by HI in Yei and Juba (May 2019) found out of all respondents only 3% of persons with disabilities had access to socio-economic opportunities, with the majority adopting coping strategies such as rationing or missing meals due to shortfalls in food. In households consisting of one of more persons with disabilities, female-headed or elderly people, there is an inability to cope with shortages and address needs – specifically as caring for relatives and other non-paid duties are incredibly time consuming. In Yei, availability of food, fluctuating costs and access to markets were all cited as key contributors to food insecurity. For vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities, the lack of appropriate skills or tailored approaches to engage in food security and livelihood activities, lack of access to training opportunities or sources of credit remain major challenges for accessing market opportunities. In terms of the provision of food security and livelihoods programming in South Sudan, gaps in the collection and analysis of information for vulnerable groups, identification and solutions mapping for overcoming barriers and meaningful participation continue to prevent access to food-items, cash-based assistance and livelihoods support. Whilst the needs of the wider population are significant, those for such groups remain far more acute and unmet – which requires a considered and careful approach to inclusion and adaptation sector-wide.

2 - Activities

The following activities are proposed to meet the stated gaps, and are closely aligned to the Food Security and Livelihood cluster priorities, objectives and activities for 2020. CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations: To support improved targeting of vulnerable HHs & populations: woman, girls, men & boys, elderly, disabled. HI will provide a tailored package of support to FSL cluster partners, including barrier and gap assessments, disability inclusion training and sensitization, integration of Washington Group Question Sets in data collection tools, and promoting accessibility adaptations, identification and targeting criteria to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities. CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks: HI will include unconditional cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives, a multi sectoral model combining livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security with gender and disability inclusion mainstreaming activities and trainings / asset transfer activities designed to diversify and restore livelihoods in each location through market-based approaches. In key locations within each County, HI will also pilot inclusive village savings and loan schemes to provide crucial access to credit and a means to promote equitable socio-economic outcomes across different cadres of society.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneficiaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text and phone-line as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews and post-intervention monitoring surveys. Detailed sensitization of communities on when, how and why to report is critical for the functioning of the system, as is HIs internal review, response and feedback protocols – designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can access feedback and complaints mechanisms, HI will include considerations such as the use of local languages, reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities (i.e. accessible venues, sign-language interpretation, sighted guides, accessible information materials), as well as gender-specific mobilization and messaging to minimize any barriers these key groups encounter.
