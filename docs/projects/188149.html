Project Summary

REACH, a joint initiative of IMPACT, ACTED and the United Nations Operational Satellite Applications Programme (UNOSAT) aims to facilitate evidence-based planning and programming for humanitarian actors in Syria by providing information management and coordination support to at least 225 humanitarian organizations. This intervention has been designed on the basis of needs identified in the field, as well as successful experiences in implementing similar activities in Syria over the past eight years. The activities aim to address the challenges humanitarian organizations and the humanitarian coordination system face in accessing and coordinating information and data to effectively identify gaps, as well as plan and implement a humanitarian response. REACH plans to do this by increasing the number of humanitarian actors utilizing information management services, increasing the number of humanitarian actors directly contributing to information products, and providing support for coordination, data collection, and technical guidance to clusters and humanitarian actors.

4 - Needs

Up to date, timely, and accurate information is essential given the scale, complexity, and severity of Syria's humanitarian needs. While IM and response partners have produced a wealth of data on the situation in Syria, important gaps in the information structure remain. First, accessibility and security issues make systematic data collection with a single research methodology difficult. Second, partner data collection is often solely aimed to inform specific programming. Third, despite the availability of data, partners often lack the analytical capacity to combine and triangulate findings from information sources. IM actors can help to address these needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

When possible, REACH collects information directly. Where access is limited, REACH collects information remotely or through humanitarian partners, such as in areas where permissions from local authorities cannot be obtained or in areas experiencing active fighting. In the current context of COVID-19, REACH employs a combination of remote and in-person data collection in NWS and NES. Remote data collection is done in the form of Key Informant (KI) methodology, which involves selection of KIs who report on the situation in their community. KI networks are established through REACH's expansive network of enumerators in close coordination with local councils and governance structures. In-person data collection is employed when necessary for random sampling for representative household-level assessments as well as intermittently to uphold relationships with key informants. After data is collected, it is cleaned and analyzed by REACH data teams based in Amman. Assessment teams interpret findings and present them in factsheets, reports, and presentations, which are published and disseminated widely. REACH also provides technical support to actors when requested, through ad hoc assessments and analysis, Information Management Officer secondments, and dedicated support to the MSNA. REACH supports OCHA on the MSNA as a technical partner, along with HNAP. While HNAP collects all MSNA data, REACH supports with survey design, sampling, data cleaning, and data analysis.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: growing hostility of target communities towards INGOs if the latter's neutrality is compromised. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: ACTED/REACH has developed strong community acceptance through previous work in NES and NWS and works closely with local authorities to obtain all necessary authorizations needed to conduct activities. Should there be tensions that occur during the project period, REACH will increase engagement with local communities and reaffirm the organizational commitment to neutrality. Risk: Identities of partners participating in the data collection initiative in south-central Syria are exposed. Likelihood: Medium. Mitigation: REACH will build upon its global expertise to put in place stringent SOPs around communication between partners, anonymization of data and outputs, and proper disposal of data following assessments. This includes communication via encrypted platforms, anonymization of forms, and collection of data that is related to existing partner activities. Risk: Escalation of security situation or shift in control over target areas. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: If security situation in some locations seriously deteriorates, REACH will lose contact with key informants, in which case a snowballing approach will be used to establish new contacts if it would prove low to no-risk to those contacted. REACH may also shift away from informant data to GIS and remote sensing analysis.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Women and girls subjected to Sexual Abuse and Exploitation or Gender-Based Violence within the framework of this project, for example, by male enumerators interviewing female-headed households. Likelihood: Medium. Mitigation: Through REACH's operational partner, ACTED, PSEA and Child protection policies have been developed and are signed by all ACTED/REACH staff members as part of the Code of Conduct. All ACTED/REACH national and international staff receive a two-hour induction training session on PSEA. Furthermore, ACTED has a MENA Regional Protection Advisor who advises and supports all of ACTED/REACH's field missions, including Syria, through the provision of training, fact-sheets, and guidance to uphold protection principles, including gender mainstreaming and PSEA. ACTED recently nominated two PSEA focal points to join the inter-agency PSEA-CBCM network and contribute to the improvement of all humanitarian partners' PSEA policies and measures in Whole of Syria, particularly ACTED/REACH.Under this project, GBV and sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment complaints would be channeled to ACTED which would lead on the investigations, according to the protocols defined in the ACTED PSEA policy.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Restriction of movements and limitations of access to data collection locations imposed by the local authorities due to the Covid-19 global pandemic. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: In accordance with the IMPACT global April 2020 Standard Operating Procedure for data collection during the Covid-19 pandemic, REACH can switch to a remote management set-up when necessary to ensure the safety of all staff in cases where movement restrictions are imposed. Remote data collection reduces the ability of enumerators to verify the accuracy of survey respondents, but this can be mitigated by more diligent data cleaning. Risk: Change of government policy/attitude in Turkey leading to a closure of the borders and/or negative impact on humanitarian access to Syria. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: REACH enumerators have been trained not to engage with any armed groups and to inform their supervisors immediately if such groups should in any way seek to put pressure on them or control any data collected. Should this occur, REACH will immediately pause data collection and monitor the situation to ensure the safety of enumerators and integrity of data. Any changes in the political landscape are likelyto lead to changes in the movement of IDPs and/or access to areas. Both will require REACH to look into alternative means of data collection, such as remote data collection methodologies.
