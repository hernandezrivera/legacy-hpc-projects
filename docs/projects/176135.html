Project Summary

This project aims at providing support to conflict-affected and most vulnerable girls, boys, youths, women, and men in Jur River County of Western Bahr El Ghazal State, Mvolo, and Ibba Counties of Western Equatoria State in South Sudan where we have a static presence to reach 11940 beneficiaries (1350 boys, 2100 girls, 4530 men, and 3960 women).
Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) propose this project that will contribute to achieving South Sudan’s HRP objectives of GBV cluster (PO 1) in ensuring the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and (PO 2) Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
We shall achieve the objectives by responding to the needs of gender-based survivors through various activities; among them being the provision of a timely, comprehensive case management process to 300 beneficiaries (50 boys, 50 girls, 150 women, and 50 men) that will address their needs from the point they were identified until when their cases shall be closed. The provision of case management will be strengthened by the training that we shall deliver to frontline service providers.
We will provide individualized and group psychosocial support and recreational services that will target 300 boys, 300 girls, 300 men, and 600 women as per their individual needs. We shall also have recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be reinvigorated. Basic livelihood skills like basketry, breadmaking, and embroidery will avail an opportunity where participants will generate income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships.
By carrying out protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging that will target 7800 people (900 boys, 900 girls, 3000 men, and 3000 women) we shall pass key messages that will focus on awareness of gender-based violence, prevention/ harm reduction. We shall pass messages that are short, memorable, and communicated in a local language that shall be understood by the beneficiaries in Jur River, Mvolo, Ibba Counties.
We shall carry out training and mentorship to service providers with a focus on changing their knowledge attitudes and beliefs on gender, GBV, and comprehensive case management targeting 360 service providers (180 women, 180 men) in Jur River, Mvolo, Ibba Counties.
We shall also aim to strengthen and equip women and girl-friendly spaces in the targeted project locations. Our focus will be to support the establishment/ strengthening of 6 WGFS (2 in Jur River, 2 in Ibba, and 2 in Mvolo) where they will be equipped to offer context-based skills training and recreational activities on effective techniques for break making, mat weaving, etc. to 750 women and 750 girls.

1 - Needs

An IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) field assessment in Kuajiena Payam of Jur River County on 25 February and 18 March 2020 to obtain more information about clashes between pastoralists and farmers indicated that people who fled the Akou area needed food, shelter, and non-food items. Therefore, protection interventions like the ones proposed in this project which facilitate safe access for such vulnerable people to services are important. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021 indicated that Protection was the sixth commonly cited form of priority assistance needed for men, women, and children. From our programmatic experience in Jur River County, there are gaps in the provision of timely referral services to survivors of GBV who are residing in hard-to-reach areas like Akou, Mapel, and Kayango that require services like CMR, especially when there is communication breakdown/ insecurity. Such survivors require immediate attention to ensure that they receive inter-agency referrals from their locations of residence to service points. Our interventions in this project will target such locations and seek to use our presence and coverage of such locations to link persons in need with inter-agency services as soon as we identify them.With reference to a REACH situational overview in Western Equatoria that was conducted from April- September 2020, humanitarian needs in Western Equatoria State (WES) increased due to a deteriorating security situation, displacement, unusually heavy rainfall and flooding, and COVID-19 related movement restrictions. However, due to insecurity, bad road conditions, and flooding, the food security situation has raised concerns in Greater Mundri where this project will be implemented. Such emergencies create an environment where SGBV would take place, and thus the need for safeguarding women, men, boys, and girls against protection risks through activities like the ones proposed in this project.In Mvolo County, 27% of assessed settlements in May reported civilian casualties (compared to 6% in March) and 18% looting, likely partially caused by raiding incidents Through our project, we shall facilitate access to services for survivors by sensitizing them where services are available, the requirements of accessing those services, and focal persons. This will increase the uptake of inter-sectoral services to survivors of violence who require various services. Community awareness-raising will be one of the strategies used by this project to pass such information. The training that we shall offer to frontline service providers will empower them with skills that will enhance their understanding of GBV and thus be better prepared to offer services to survivors who will come to seek services from their institutions/ service points.Domestic violence remained a concern in Mvolo County, with 72% reporting it as the main concern in September, similar to 71% in March, notably higher than the 14% average in the western counties. For girls, early marriage was the most commonly reported protection concern (61% in September). An increase in teenage pregnancies was reported, likely as an outcome of school closures at the end of March, which could result in girls not being able to continue their education and married off early without targeted assistance. Such incidents of SGBV expose women, men, girls, and boys to protection risks and violations of their rights due to widespread conflict that causes multiple shocks and stresses. The PSS activities proposed in this project will address the stress and trauma that the survivors have undergone to improve their wellbeing, to relieve emotional suffering so that they can rely on their resources and cope more successfully with the hardships they face on the road to recovery.

2 - Project activities

To enable SEM to achieve the set objectives by the GBV cluster (PO 1) in ensuring the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and (PO 2) Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience; we propose to undertake the following activities: 1- Provision of timely, and comprehensive case management process to 780 beneficiaries (150 boys, 150 girls, 300 women, and 180 men) that will address their needs from the point they were identified until when their cases shall be closed. 2- Provide individualized and group psychosocial support and recreational services: we will target 300 boys, 300 girls, 300 men, and 600 women as per their individual needs. We shall also have recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be reinvigorated. Basic livelihood skills like basketry, breadmaking, and embroidery will avail an opportunity where participants will generate income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships. 3- Conducting protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging: we aim at targeting 7800 people (900 boys, 900 girls, 3000 men, and 3000 women). We shall pass key messages that will focus on awareness of gender-based violence, prevention/ harm reduction. We shall pass messages that are short, memorable, and communicated in a local language that shall be understood by the beneficiaries in Jur River, Mvolo, Ibba Counties. 4- Conduct training to frontline service providers: We shall carry out training and mentorship to service providers with a focus on changing their knowledge attitudes and belief on gender, GBV, and comprehensive case management targeting 360 service providers (180 women, 180 men) in Jur River, Mvolo, Ibba Counties. 5- Strengthening and equipping women and girl-friendly spaces: we shall support the establishment/ strengthening of 6 WGFS (2 in Jur River, 2 in Ibba, and 2 in Mvolo) where they will be equipped to offer context-based skills training and recreational activities on effective techniques for break making, mat weaving, etc. to 750 women and 750 girls.

3 - Project indicators

For us to determine the success of this project, we shall refer to the following indicators: (1)Number of women, men, boys, and girls with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services: we shall increase the number of beneficiaries who receive timely, comprehensive case management process which will address their needs from the point they were identified, assessed, referred and up to the point of case closure. (2)Number of women, men, girls, and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS, and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills): we shall seek to increase the number of survivors who receive individualized and group psychosocial support, recreational services, and basic livelihood skills. (3)Number of women, men, girls, and boys reached through awareness-raising activities: we shall increase the number of boys, girls, men, and women who receive key messages that will focus on awareness of gender-based violence, prevention/ harm reduction, and service points that are available to them in the community. (4)Number of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV minimum Standards ( Case management, PSS, etc.): we shall seek to increase the number of frontline service providers who have been trained with an emphasis on changing their knowledge attitudes and beliefs on gender, GBV, and comprehensive case management. (5)Number of women and girls who accessed services in WGFS: through the establishment/ strengthening of WGFS, we will increase the number of women and girls receiving context-based skill-building and recreational activities like bread making and weaving mats.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

While consulting beneficiaries on implementation, and during community awareness-raising, we shall inform the beneficiaries and community members of our stand on zero tolerance of PSEA, and inform them of channels that they can use to report their concerns for the integrity of this project.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We will encourage our social workers to use local languages familiar to the beneficiaries, and provide sign language interpretation to persons who are deaf directly or via caregivers familiar to them, verbal interpretation and explanations on feedback to persons who are blind, those who are not able to read and write, as well as verbal communication to persons who have intellectual disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

We will verbally inform our beneficiaries as we interact with them in the community and through written means in posters placed in public spaces on beneficiary complaints/ feedback mechanisms. Community awareness raising will also be another platform for information dissemination of our feedback/ complaints mechanism channels that they can use.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

We will establish a beneficiary feedback mechanism for our beneficiaries to give information on our modalities of service provision in order to adapt our service provision style to adequately meet the needs of our targeted beneficiaries and stakeholders. The feedback will help to increase the participation of beneficiaries in the project and for the maintenance of relevance to our beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for children and survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection) will be mitigated through maintaining a current list of local resources, intersectoral services that are available in Ibba, Mvolo, and Jur River Counties and make it a standard part of the information available to survivors of protection incidents and their family/ caregivers. We shall further disseminate information through leaflets, posters, and information cards to the community to reach as many people as possible. To access services among people with disabilities, we shall adequately consult them while ensuring reasonable accommodation to overcome barriers that may arise during project designing, and implementation stages. This will include using interpreters for persons who are deaf, personal assistants for those with physical and intellectual disabilities. Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established via suggestion boxes that will be strategically placed in public spaces and display of phone numbers for complaint and feedback focal points as well as dissemination of the information to all project stakeholders to provide an avenue where they will freely share complaints and feedback on this project. Unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries- By making use of our code of conduct, all our staff shall be expected to abide by the clauses that refer to the safety of data and responsible use of collected data. This will prevent the distribution, publishing, or sharing of any collected data without the consent of the beneficiaries and SEM to safeguard the confidentiality of our beneficiaries.
