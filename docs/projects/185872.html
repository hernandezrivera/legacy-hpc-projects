Project Summary

The Anglophone crisis started in 2016 as a result of marginalization of the English speaking North West and South West Regions has intensify for close to four years now and has witness increasing killings by both the separatist arm groups and government forces on men and youths. This has lead to the flee of affected and non affected families and communities to neighboring villages, communities and division out of North West Region to the French speaking Regions in Cameroon. The increase population especially women and children is largely driven by poverty.
Women make up about 52% of the Cameroon population and the gross inequalities and inhuman conditions they endure are both as a result of the crisis, the situation is particularly worst and presents real concerns for their fair treatment, access to food, livelihoods and income generating activities.
The situation of displaced persons especially women and girls from North West Region to Kouptamo, Koutaba and Foumbot subdivisions in Noun Division of the West Region is deplorable as they have indulged into prostitution and other forms of criminal behavior like stealing of food crops from host farms, stealing from shops and markets etc.
A critical element of hardship emanates from the women’s increasing roles as providers of basic needs or amenities to the members of their households
The Anglophone crises have created gross poverty and unemployment levels among the Internally Displaced persons (IDP) especially women and girls both in urban and rural communities in Kouptamo, Koutaba and Foumbot Subdivisions the West Regions. These challenges amongst other issues have precipitated a vicious cycle of poverty and hopelessness from which many women and families find impossible to overcome.
The project will focus on capacity building for agricultural production, livestock rearing and start up material support for 3000 IDPs in Kouptamo, Koutaba and Foumbot subdivisions in Noun Division especially women and girls to enhance job creation, livelihood and sustainable income generation. Women and girls are sexually abused because of their social and economical incapacities to meet up their needs because they are internally displaced persons in Kouptamo, Koutaba and Foumbot subdivisions in Noun Division West Region
SO3 : Reduce vulnerabilities and strengthen resilience of 3000 people affected by crisis SPO3.2: In 2022, 3000 vulnerable people are supported with inputs, services, and skills for livelihood activities, to reduce vulnerabilities and contribute to sustainable solutions. The outcomes of this project are;
1) A total of 3000 IDPs in host communities including Women and youths IDPs in host communities are economically empowered through improved competencies and capacities for sustainable income generation, self employment through Market gardening, mushroom production and processing, small livestock/poultry production and marketing which will lead to new jobs created
2) By the end of 2022, the use of negative coping strategies such as stealing, prostitution and other juvenile activities is mitigated for 3000 vulnerable people affected by crisis through micro capital for inputs and skills.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In 2020, 6.2 million people in Cameroon need humanitarian assistance. The COVID-19 pandemic, which broke out in Cameroon in March 2020, affects the whole country, increasing the number of people in need of assistance and exacerbating the needs of people already affected by humanitarian crisis. In November 2017, tensions in the North West and South West regions turned into violence with dramatic humanitarian consequences. Almost 680,000 Cameroonians are now internally displaced. An additional 58,000 people have sought refuge in neighboring Nigeria.3 80% of health and education services in the two regions were non-functioning even before the COVID-19 outbreak. IDPs and host communities, particularly in rural areas, need immediate protection, food, shelter/NFI, water and sanitation assistance (OCHA 2020)The critical problems of the population affected by the crisis in the North West and South West regions are Limited access to employment and loss of livelihoods due to insecurity and displacementThe effects of displacement are often different for women, men, girls, and boys – each face unique protection risks within the humanitarian context. There are continued needs to consider alternative and flexible income- generating schemes to address livelihoods problems. The protracted Anglophone crises in the North West Region has highlighted unique challenges and opportunities facing humanitarian efforts suggesting a need for greater innovation to respond effectively to the evolving economic and protection challenges faced by displaced persons. Economic insecurity has increased the protection risk for the displaced as they seek income-generating opportunities, creating a need for a more integrated approach to livelihoods initiatives. In a community where 80% of the population depends on agriculture for livelihoods and food securities, the absence or shortage of short term innovative agricultural practices is a serious threat to livelihoods and food security for these vulnerable IDPs especially women and girls and their host communities.
