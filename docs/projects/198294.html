Project Summary

Millions of people are living in poor situations due to violent conflict in Northeast Nigeria for more than Ten (10) years of war, which has also destroyed the social fabric that kept peace in local communities. Immediate intervention is required to foster peaceful cohabitation in communities ripped apart by violence and widespread displacement. The complexities of the conflict surrounding the reintegration of the hundreds of women and children taken captive by Boko Haram demand special consideration. Therefore, Larif Peace Empowerment Foundation proposes a 1-year program with an overall goal of strengthening the resilience and reintegration of conflict-affected people, building on previous actors’ efforts to promote peaceful reintegration of women and girls survivors of Boko Haram captivity and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV).
This project will be target 7000 vulnerable women, girls and their children.

1 - Needs

Years of violence by the Boko Haram insurgency in the BAY States have destroyed the lives of millions, forcing them to flee their homes and lose access to their livelihoods and protection resources. A disproportionate response by security forces against civilians has aggravated protection concerns, while community militia groups established to combat Boko Haram have also turned against civilians and are adopting ruthless and violent tactics against them. Fear and mistrust have caused neighbors and family members to turn on each other; traditional conflict resolution mechanisms have failed, and the social fabric has been torn apart. Displaced communities are almost entirely reliant on humanitarian aid and government support, the scarcity of which exacerbates existing communities, underlying religious and ethnic tensions, as well as creating rivalries with host communities. Within this context, the return of the thousands of men, women, girls, and children who voluntarily or involuntarily joined Boko Haram to their communities is a highly contentious process. The devastation wreaked by the insurgent group has generated intense fear among community members of anyone associated with the group. Returning women and girls face significant stigma, as many were forcefully married to Boko Haram members and raped while in captivity. Sexual violence continues to carry a heavy cultural stigma for the victims, who are seen as having had their purity tarnished. For those that have given birth to children because of this SGBV, the stigma upon their return is even more acute, as their community members believe that the children carry the “bad blood” of their Boko Haram fathers. As a result, when they escape or are rescued from Boko Haram, women and girls face a painful return process to their communities, whether in IDP camps, host communities, or their areas of origin. They suffer discrimination, rejection, verbal abuse, and sometimes even physical violence, even from their families, friends, and neighbors. There is a dearth of services specifically tailored to address the trauma they have experienced, compounding their psychological distress. In this context many women and girls isolate themselves from their communities, while some have even returned to Boko Haram, feeling that they are treated with greater dignity by the insurgents. Such continued social fragmentation and distrust, coupled with the ongoing broader violence, is a transparent barrier to peace. It prevents communities from working together to address the root causes of conflict arising from stigma among themselves and risks generating new tensions and violence around the reintegration of the millions of people displaced by the conflict.In this context, Larif intends to develop a GBV reintegration programme that aimed to create a supportive and protective environment for the reintegration of women, girls, and boys returnees, while also supporting these women, girls, and boys to have re-enforced self-esteem, and knowledge to develop self-reliant skills to improve their standard of living.

2 - Activities

The project’s overall goal seeks to address the rate of rejection of women, girls, and boys affected by Boko Haram violence through increased sensitization, community engagement, and capacity building on GBV prevention and promotion of sustainable acceptance to ensure reintegration.Objectives:1. Community and religious leaders are equipped to understand and address the challenges of GBV in relation to reintegration 2. Stigma against women and children associated with Boko Haram is reduced 3. Community members work collaboratively to identify and resolve GBV-related conflict4. Improve living standards and creation of self-relianceProject ActivitiesThe project is designed to build the resilience of the said communities by reinforcing the protection and livelihood activities of vulnerable women and girls and mainstreaming peacebuilding to ensure the sustained promotion of stability.
