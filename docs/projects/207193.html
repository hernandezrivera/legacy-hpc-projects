Project Summary

The armed repression of the 2016’s protests in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon against the linguistical and cultural marginalisation of the Anglophone population exacerbated in an armed conflict between government military forces and organised armed groups that continues to this day. The ongoing socio-political crisis impacts the civilian population of the two regions caught between the two parties armed clashes. Among the impacts for the civilian population, we register the destruction of medical health centres and consequent issues of access to medical care as well as a number of protection issues affecting the most vulnerable segments of the population (SGVB, stigmatisation and violence against people with disabilities) without any adequate systems of prevention and mitigation and any responsive psychosocial services to support the survivors.
Treat Me Right is a multisectoral (health/protection) one year emergency project targeting 3 health areas in the health district of Wabane within the Lebialem division of the South West region of Cameroon where due to the ongoing conflict the most vulnerable segments of the population (women, children under 5 years old and people with disabilities (PwD)) are lacking appropriate medical care and protection systems catered to both their specific and circumstantial (conflict induced) psychosocial needs.
The overall objective of the project is improving the physical, mental and social health conditions of host and displaced populations in the Lebialem department in the South West region. The specific objective of the intervention is Improving the quality of and access to health care and protection services for displaced and host populations and vulnerable groups in the 3 health areas of Wabane district (Bamumbu, Bechati and Fotang). This objective is pursued with the following targets:
- 16 141 patients (8629F, 7512M, 12 636 <18 y/o, 131 PwD) access medical care in the 3 targeted health areas.
- 10 000 people (5.100 F e 4.900; 1.900 <18 y/o; 650 PwD) are supported by protection monitoring activities.
The expected result from the health sector’s activities is that of improving the access to primary health and psychosocial support services thanks to an increase in the safety, accessibility, and supply of integrated health centres in Bambumbu, Bechati, Fotang and Nkong. Specifically, the targets are:
- 4 integrated health centres (Bamumbu, Bechati, Fotang and Nkong) are equipped with basic hygienic services in line with protection and sustainability standards.
- 23 medical professionals (10 F; 13M) are adequately trained on medical and mental health care.
- 5584 consultations in general medical care and 8694 consultations in paediatrics are provided.
- 720 ANC1 (ante-natal care1) are provided, and 386 assisted deliveries are performed.
- 1200 people (650 F, 550 M, 500 minors, 100 PwD) access psychological support.
The expected result from the protection sector’s activities is that of guaranteeing access to protection and support services at the personal, family and community level for the most vulnerable groups such as women at risk or survivors of SGBV, children under 5 years of age and PwD. Specifically, the targets are:
- 100 people (90 F, 10 M; 19 - 24 families and 140 PwD (79 F, 61 M, 68 <18 y/o) access to the Peer Counseling service.
- 10 000 (5.100 F, 4.900; 1.900 <18 y/o; 650 PwD) are reached by the raising awareness and outreach activities.
- At least 30 social workers in the health sector (15 F, 15 M) and members of Community Based Organizations (CBO) are trained on inclusivity of PwD.
Beneficiaries will be selected on the basis of medical criteria, vulnerability and humanitarian needs, without any discrimination on the basis of gender, place of residence, ethnicity, religion and political affiliation.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Within the health sector the three main needs emerged by a cross-analysis between OCHA’s 2023 HRP, 2023 MSNA, 2023 HNO as well as a rapid needs assessment (NA) conducted on the field in the month of October 2023 are:1. Need to rehabilitate basic hygiene systems in health centres: In Wabane district, out of 8 public health facilities present, only 5 health centers (of which 4 are covered by this proposal) are operational, albeit partially, but require major rehabilitation.2. Need to strengthen the knowledge and skillset of health staff in the health centres and mobile clinics (also in relation to mental health):With reference to international standards (WHO, 1 doctor per 10,000 inhabitants, 1 nurse per 5,000), the gap in the Wabane district represents 70% of the health workforce need.3. Need to increase the availability of health services as well as strengthening their efficacy and facilitating access: In Lebialem, in 59.8% of the villages, it takes people more than 1 hour to reach the nearest health center (OCHA, MSNA, 2023). There are further access barriers affecting women and people with disabilities: roads that are impassable and/or unsafe, lack of female staff and services for PwD specific needs.Activities: 1.1 Construction, rehabilitation, and maintenance of basic hygiene facilities (1 water points, 2 taps, 1 block of gender sensitive latrines) in each of the four integrated health centres of Bamumbu, Bechati, Fotang and Nkong1.2 Setting up a mobile clinic, supplying equipment and strengthening epidemiological surveillance systems at the health centres.1.3 Support and/or enhancement of reproductive health and paediatric services in the four identified health centres and the mobile clinic 1.4 Capacity building of health centres staff on management of obstetric and gynaecological emergencies and Neonatal and paediatric health1.5 Training in Psychological First Aid (PFA) of health centres staff and Community Health Agents 1.6 Provision of psychological support at health centres, the mobile clinic and communities through the provision of safe spaces, a specialized psychological support by a mental health expert and listening groups for the health centres staff;1.7 Community Awareness Raising and Basic Education on Health and HygieneConcerning the protection sector three main needs have been identified: 1. Need for trained staff and appropriate integrated and outreach services for survivors of SGBV and PwD:SGBV in NW-SW mainly affects women: about 90% of survivors are female, 19% are children and 6.5% PwD (OCHA, HNO, 2023). The main safety concerns cited by women include: the risk of being attacked when moving out of one's community (45%) and within the community (33%); sexual violence and abuse (32%); lack of access to services and resources (28%); lack of safe spaces (24%), forced marriage (19%) and insufficient privacy in the home (18%) (OCHA, MSNA, 2023).2. Need for increased awareness of SGBV and disability issues at the level of community networks and local civil society: PwD do not have adequate access to information on available protection and humanitarian assistance services, due to the use of information channels not available to PwD (37%); providers of goods and services who do not consider PwDs’ need for information (32%); physical barriers in accessing and understanding information (32%). (OCHA, MSNA, 2023).Activities:2.1 Training of trainers and supervision of a team of Peer Counsellors and social workers 2.2. Training of trainers on SGBV 2.3 Creation of community support networks for the prevention, mitigation, response and referral of SGBV cases2.4 Awareness and sensitisation for the inclusion of PwD in health centres, CBOs and other services2.5 Activation of an individual and family Peer Counsellor service for identification, referral and support to PwD, CwD and their families 2.6 Distribution and adaptation of specialised devices and access to specific services for CwD and PwD
