Project Summary

The project will support 8,000 vulnerable and underserved children and 350 teachers through the provision of emergency education supplies and school-based psychosocial support. It will also provide remedial education to vulnerable school-aged children including training of teachers on the home-based learning methodology. Furthermore, it will support conflict-affected students with disability with assistive devices to ensure their equal access to inclusive quality learning opportunities.

Needs

The protracted and recurrent conflict, the Israeli blockade, the use of school facilities as shelters and for military activities as well as the attacks on and demolitions of schools in the Gaza strip have been severely impeding access to quality education in a safe and child-friendly manner for both children and teachers. These threats also negatively affect students` general well-being and their school performance and completion rates. Save the Children`s (SC`s) research conducted in February 2018 with 485 children 8-14 years old, (242 boys, 243 girls) found out that some 63% of children in Gaza experience nightmares and 68% have sleeping difficulties. Some 95% of children have reported depression, aggression, hyperactivity and a preference for being alone. Aircraft sound is cited as the single biggest source of fear by 78% of children. Nearly 60% of all 150 (78 male, 72 femalecaregivers) caregivers interviewed (90 out of 150) state that the blockade has a significant impact on their children`s daily lives with the conflict and constant insecurity being major sources of stress. The chronic and widespread electricity shortage compounding the above threats also prevents children from learning and concentrating effectively both at school and in their homes. In SC`s research, children have often expressed feeling “angry”, “anxious” and “alone” when the lights have gone off. Caregivers have stated feeling concerned about the economic impacts of the power shortage and the impact on their children`s learning. The funding cuts to UNRWA resulting in the downsizing of its activities and staff has put both the UNRWA schools and provision of education at risk as well as many Palestinian caregivers employed by the Agency. The double and triple shift system applied by UNRWA schools (70%) and Ministry of Education schools (63%) further limits the time available for studying to four hours a day and impedes remedial education, co- and extracurricular activities. According to the Gaza Education cluster, approximately 50% of all students are failing to meet their educational potential and facing reading and writing difficulties. These also erode children`s resilience and leave them at the risk of school drop-out and a number of protection risks. UNICEF`s recent report on Out-of-School Children in Palestine (July 2018) has found that about 8.8% of all boys and 11.9% of all girls at the age of five do not attend school. Out of school rates decrease at the ages of 6 to 13, while sharply increase for boys from the age of 14 (with 11.6% of boys and 2.7 of girls) not attending school. The increase continues for boys, with 22% and 29% of boys aged 15 and 16, respectively being out of school. For girls, the same figures are lower at 5.6% and 13.3%. Adolescent boys, aged 14 and 15, make up nearly half of all children, up to the compulsory school age of 15, who are out of school. More boys in this age group are missing out on education in the West Bank (18.3 per cent), compared to the Gaza Strip (14.7 per cent). This shows that there is a gender-specific pattern of missing education affecting adolescent boys in particular. The main reasons for drop-out include low-quality and irrelevant education, physical and emotional violence in schools by peers and teachers and armed conflict. More than two-thirds of children in Grades 1 to 10 report being exposed to emotional and physical violence in their schools, and – due to the conflict - more than 29,000 children in 2017 had their schooling interrupted in 170 attacks and threats of attacks on schools, students or teachers that further affects school attendance. Islamic Relief`s Community Vulnerability Mapping Study for 2017-2018 has found that there are 116 communities who are most marginalized in the Gaza strip. Out of these, 85.7% works in double shifts and 84.5% of all children need remedial education support.

Activities

The project will centre around two main components in direct support of the strategic and cluster objectives of the 2019 HRP. Responsive activities will include 1) provision of emergency education supplies to vulnerable and underserved students and teachers and 2) provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by education-related violations. Remedial activities will include 1) provision of remedial education to vulnerable school aged children and 2) supporting conflict-affected school children with disability with assistive devices. The project will target a total of 8,000 children (4,000 girls and 4,000 boys) aged 6-14 years old whose learning outcomes are affected by high level of distress and low level of motivation resulting from the violence, and poor infrastructure and overcrowding in schools. Following a rapid needs assessment, the 8,000 children aged 6-14 years old will be provided with home-based learning opportunities and recreational activities. This will include training for 150 home-based learning teachers (75 female and 75 male) who will benefit from a cash for work support. These teachers will deliver lessons in the communities as alternative form of education when schools are used as shelters or are not functioning. 2,000 children (1,000 girls and 1,000 boys) aged 6-14 years old living in very hard conditions will receive student kits in the form of e-vouchers. 200 students (100 girls, 100 boys) aged 6-14 years old with sensorial (visual and hearing impairments) will receive assistive devices to attend schools. 10 schools (5 boy, 5 girl schools) located along the ARAs and pre-identified and used as shelters and/or UNRWA schools that will be closed down as a result of downsizing will be targeted. 200 teachers (100 female and 100 male) in these target schools will receive training on Stress Management and Psychological First Aid provision for children to support the learning of children facing mental health challenges. Referral pathways will ensure that protection needs identified for specific children are addressed by relevant service providers. This will ensure a protection-sensitive approach to project and service delivery. Girls and boys will have equal access to project activities and gender-specific needs and risks will be paid special attention to. The project activities will help enhance access to quality education interventions for children who are negatively affected by the double and triple-shift school system and /or come from most vulnerable and poorest households and/or live with disabilities. SC will place a specific focus on children requiring psycho-social and remedial education support as a result of education violations, children at the risk of school drop-out and children with disabilities. Target locations will include the Gaza strip including ARAs with most vulnerable schools prioritized. These will include locations where students have to cross checkpoints and/or militarized and firing zones, where children have to cover long distances to reach school, where schools are at risk of being used as shelters during an emergency and where there is shortage of school infrastructure and staff. Save Youth Future Society as project partner and long-term strategic partner for SC will directly implement the home-based learning project component and recreational activities for the children. The partnership will both support SC`s overall localisation and capacity-building strategy, further local ownership and sustainability as well as facilitate access to local and most vulnerable communities Throughout the project, SC will work in close cooperation and consultation with the in-country Education cluster, the EiE Working Group, UNICEF and the UNRWA and other relevant education actors. SC has been delivering Education programming in the target locations since 2012 and has secured access for the implementation of the project.

SC`s dedicated MEAL department will work closely with implementing partners to train them in M&E.
