Project Summary

Executive Summary
The International Rescue Committee (IRC) proposes the Resilient WASH Response Initiative to address critical water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) needs in Rubkona, Unity State, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG), South Sudan. This initiative is a direct response to the heightened challenges of the 2024 cholera outbreak, severe flooding, and widespread poor hygiene practices, which have excessively affected vulnerable populations, particularly women, children, and persons with disabilities. The areas, which are regularly flooded, face significant challenges in Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene service provision. Various issues apart from flooding worsen the situation and these include increased population of displaced people, deteriorating the living conditions, occasional outbreaks of waterborne diseases and increased food insecurity and malnutrition. Sanitation and hygiene practices are especially a concern along with poor governance of community infrastructure and low WASH coverage. This, along with the high risk of disease transmission due to increased population movements, IDPs, persistent flooding and low access to WASH services means the WASH conditions are perpetually inadequate.
Protection issues are prevalent with latrines and bathing facilities, sometimes lacking gender disaggregation and internal locks, being unsafe for women and girls, especially at night.Women and girls especially have difficulties in accessing resources with some being forced to use pieces of old clothing due to a shortage of menstrual hygiene material. With the shortage of clean water and soap, these cloths are washed in flood water, which can lead to infections and other health issues.
Critical WASH infrastructure, therefore requires repairs to handle the affected populace. Without a response, the waterborne diseases will likely reappear, with children under 5 years and women potentially seriously affected. The program aims to improve health outcomes via the provision of WASH infrastructure, construction of gender-sensitive and inclusive public sanitation facilities, hygiene promotion, distribution of WASH kits, and rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure at health facilities. The project will focus on climate-resilient reconstruction and integrating protection measures to ensure safety, inclusivity, and long-term sustainability of the interventions. This project will implement community-centered WASH interventions to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with waterborne diseases, enhance hygiene practices, and rebuild flood-damaged infrastructure to withstand future climatic shocks. The Project proposal will aim to target 90,227 in Aweil East, Aweil West and Rubkona Counties including- 43,799 Male (23,643 children and 20,156Adult) and 46,428 Female (24,435 children and 21,993 adults) respectively.

1. Needs

Humanitarian NeedsRubkona and NBeG are encountering WASH challenges as a result of disease outbreaks, climate issues, hygiene gaps and protection concerns.1. Cholera and Disease Outbreaks: The 2024 cholera outbreak in South Sudan has shown significant deficiencies in the provision of water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, especially in Rubkona and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. These regions, characterized by deficient infrastructure, have experienced a rise in waterborne infections attributable to substandard water quality and insufficient sanitation services. The prevalence of open defecation among a substantial segment of the population exacerbates cholera transmission rates, especially in densely populated displacement areas. Displaced populations, already susceptible owing to restricted access to healthcare and compromised immune systems, are especially impacted. It is essential to address these challenges to avert additional loss of life and enhance public health resilience.2. Flooding and Climate Vulnerability: Among the most frequent issues South Sudan deals with are ones related to climate change. Particularly impacted are Rubkona and Northern Bahr el Ghazal since floods have destroyed WASH infrastructure and polluted water supplies. Rising water levels have caused entire populations to be displaced. The flooding affects the populace, which depends on livelihoods susceptible to climate issues, namely farming and cattle raising. Families forced by this displacement typically find refuge in underfunded and crowded locations where access to sanitary facilities and potable water is low. Apart from damaging physical infrastructure, floodwaters raise the frequency of stationary water, which serves as habitat for disease-carrying mosquitoes. Flooding also contaminates water supplies and leaves towns depending on dangerous drinking water reliant.3. Hygiene and Behavioral Gaps: Rubkona and Northern Bahr el Ghazal have disturbingly inadequate hygiene standards, with some of South Sudan's worst rates of open defecation. Inadequate hygiene habits, such as restricted handwashing and unsafe menstrual hygiene management (MHM), have exacerbated health concerns. Due to a lack of secure and private sanitation facilities, many people defecate in open fields or waterways, contaminating water supplies and increasing disease transmission. Cultural norms and gender-based barriers are especially problematic, as taboos surrounding menstruation and sanitation frequently prohibit women and girls from accessing or lobbying for safe facilities. Menstrual hygiene management is especially neglected, with many women turning to unsanitary methods due to a lack of resources. Handwashing techniques are similarly limited, owing to a lack of soap and clean water. Efforts to encourage safe hygiene behaviors have been inconsistent, having limited impact on rural and displaced communities. 4. Protection Concerns: Women and girls in South Sudan have considerable protection risks, particularly in terms of WASH access. In Rubkona and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, potentially hazardous and inadequate sanitary facilities have increased the risk of gender-based violence (GBV). Many latrines lack proper privacy, lighting, or locks, exposing users, particularly women and girls, to harassment and attack, especially at night. The burden of water collection, which frequently falls on women and girls, leads them to travel long distances to reach water sources, exposing them to additional risks of abuse. This predicament is exacerbated in displacement camps, where overcrowding and poor security measures expose women and girls to greater danger. Furthermore, the lack of gender-sensitive WASH programs, such as menstrual hygiene supplies, promotes their marginalization and undermines their dignity.

2. Project activities

1. WASH Infrastructure Rehabilitation and Construction: The project will rehabilitate and construct water and sanitation infrastructure to ensure access to clean water and adequate sanitation. Flood damaged boreholes, will be rehabilitated with water yards restored to enhance their functionality. These boreholes will feature climate-resilient designs, such as elevated platforms to avert submersion during flooding and enhanced sanitary seals to safeguard water quality. Accessibility will be emphasized by incorporating ramps for individuals with disabilities. Gender-segregated latrine facilities will be established at health clinics and community sites to guarantee privacy and safety. Latrine blocks will be constructed, each with 10 cubicles, 3 male and 6 female along with an MHM cubicle are planned for in the response. 2. Hygiene Promotion and Behavioral Change: The IRC will execute hygiene education initiatives to address cultural and gender-related barriers to good hygiene practices. These will integrate messages to promote regular handwashing, safe water storage, and proper practices. Successful hygiene promotion requires community engagement along with tailored messaging to address local challenges. Community hygiene clubs will therefore be established to foster peer-driven behavioral change. According to the HNO 2024, persistent open defecation and low hygiene awareness are key drivers of cholera outbreaks. This project’s campaigns will target the root causes of poor hygiene practices, giving communities the knowledge to protect their health.3. Safeguarding and Integration: Women and girls encounter risks while utilizing inadequately designed sanitation facilities, especially in displacement camps. Latrine designs will prioritize safety by integrating internal locks, lighting, and gender-segregated facilities to mitigate the hazards of gender-based violence (GBV). Women, girls, and individuals with disabilities shall be consulted in the design of WASH activities to ensure their needs are addressed. Training workshops for community leaders and WASH committees will cover safeguarding and gender-based violence prevention. Furthermore, incorporating MHM considerations in design will guarantee that women and girls have access to safe, hygienic services for managing their menstrual health with dignity.4. Capacity Development and Sustainability: The project will develop and train community-led WASH committees at all restored boreholes and water yards to oversee infrastructure, collect user fees, and perform routine maintenance. These committees will undergo training in governance, financial management, and technical maintenance to guarantee the sustained viability of WASH systems beyond the project's lifespan. Collaborations with local organizations, particularly NGOs, will enhance community ownership and resilience while tailoring sustainable practices to the local context. 5. Climate Resilience and AdaptationThe implementation of climate-smart designs will be a primary focus of the project in order to improve resistance to future environmental shocks. Solar-powered water pumps will be installed at boreholes in water yards for efficient and dependable power. Elevated water storage tanks will guarantee a steady supply and storage of water. The planting of vegetative buffers located around WASH facilities will stabilize soil and enhance water absorption. 6. Distribution of WASH Materials: This project also seeks to deliver essential aid by utilizing pipeline components, including and standardized sanitation supplies from the core pipeline. Centrally managed resources will facilitate swift responses while directly addressing the pressing community demands. Hygiene kits will be distributed to support these efforts. Each will comprise soap, water purification tablets, water containers, and menstrual hygiene products, guaranteeing that beneficiaries had the necessary resources to maintain enhanced hygiene practices.
