Project Summary

Zimbabwe is facing multiple complex crises, including protracted climatic related and economic crises, compounded by the impact of COVID-19. An estimated 7 million people in urban and rural areas is urgent need of humanitarian assistance across the country (Humanitarian Response Plan of March 2020). The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report (February-June 2020) indicates that over one million people were categorised as IPC 4 (Emergency) and about 3.3 million in IPC 3 (Crisis) in the rural areas.
This intervention aligns to Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) 2020 recommendations and the Specific Objectives 1 and 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan, Zimbabwe by addressing multiple risks and shocks faced by vulnerable people in selected districts and building resilience. The programme will deliver multi-purpose unconditional cash transfers via Cash in Transit (CIT) for the most vulnerable households to meet their basic needs for food and non-food items. The Cash in Transit has been deemed the preferred modality for the cash component due to rapidly changing and rising inflation rates.
Implementation will be through local partner organisations (Caritas Bulawayo and Caritas Masvingo). Caritas partners have ongoing interventions in humanitarian, food security and livelihoods in the target districts and have already adapted working modalities to the realities of COVID 19. Trócaire is currently active in the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster, and incorporates learning and guidance from the cluster and peer organisations.
For Food Security Objective 1, 60,000 individuals (20,800 females and 19,200 males) will each be provided $12 unconditional monthly cash-based transfer for 5 months. A total of 26,147 in Bikita District; 26,853 in Chiredzi District of Masvingo Province and 7,000 in Mangwe District in Matabeleland South Province) will be targeted. These include vulnerable women, men, girls and boys as well as marginalised and socially excluded groups, such as elderly, disabled, chronically ill, orphaned or single headed households.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Caritas’s current presence in the communities enabled ongoing exploration of needs and challenges faced by the communities including the problems and vulnerabilities have emerged in the rapidly evolving COVID-19 context. Meetings with communities progressed into needs analysis, level of assistance available and the programme participants’ rights; complaints mechanisms and strategies to ensure that programme participants’ satisfaction is measuredThe changing context and new restrictions of COVID-19 means that accountability mechanisms will be adapted. Information sharing together with feedback and complaints mechanisms will be adjusted and reviewed to ensure they are accessible to different vulnerable groups and there are mechanisms in place to receive feedback. Local capacity for accountability via complaint mechanisms will be strengthened.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

While the intervention seeks to promote crop production as part of resilience building against future shocks, it is quite conversant of how agriculture and biodiversity are intimately intertwined. Agricultural activities such as tillage, drainage, use of synthetic fertilisers and extensive monocropping have significant negative implications for wild species of flora and fauna. In the process of crop production the environment is usually impacted severely. The intervention will promote agroecological practices for crop production. An important feature of agroecology is its lack of dependency on synthetic chemicals and fertilisers in the cultivation of plants. Agroecology also promotes more efficient and less depleting uses of water and land than industrial agriculture. As a result, there is minimum environmental damage and soil ecosystems are better preserved. Agroecology will provide sustainable solutions to help restore soil fertility and increase harvests while preserving biodiversity. Trocaire has jointly developed an Environmental Stewardship Tool used in project design and implementation to ensure that environmental consideration are factored in programe design and mitigation measutes factored in implementation.
