Project Summary

Displaced women and girls are at risk of multiple forms of gender-based violence including sexual violence, abduction, intimate partner violence, early marriage, physical and emotional violence. Women and girls are also living with trauma, having witnessed the horrific murdering of some of their relatives, friends and acquaintances in their communities of origin and hence require support services for therapy to overcome the trauma.
IDP sites and host community locations lack access to information and to basic safety and assistance, leaving women and girls, many of whom have experienced conflict related GBV, exposed to ongoing risks of violence. In some instances, perpetrators of GBV commit the vice not knowing that they are infringing on the rights of their victims. On the other hand, they also suffer due to lack of basic services in their communities as well as insecurity due to inadequate shelter and lack of lighting at night.
Based on the current trends in movements of IDPs due to conflict in Cabo Delgado, which has now spread into parts of Niassa and Nampula provinces, as well as displaced persons due to natural disasters as presented in the DTM Baseline round 16 , this project aims to enhance the protection of 46,391 people in the three northern provinces of Mozambique, that is Cabo Delgado (Mocimboa da Praia, Macomia, Mecufi, Metuge, Mueda, Chiure, Montepuez), Nampula (Erati, Memba, Murrupula, Nacala-a-Velha, Nacaroa) and Niassa (Nipepe, Mandimba and Mecanhelas.
The project focus will be on providing Mental Health and Psychosocial support in the context of displacement which will also include Mental Health and Psychosocial Support and GBV, Child Protection and PSN service delivery through mobile brigades on Protection and Health to IDPs and host communities. The intervention will also include training of government staff, women and communities on GBV prevention, risk mitigation, case management, referral and response; community engagement on GBV Prevention, Community Messaging as well as Establishing safe spaces for women and girls. The intervention will identify and train general protection focal points, who will act as vigilantes in the communities we will be working, providing intelligence on the goings on in the communities, including monitoring the security situation, movements of displaced people as well as conducting needs assessments.
The intervention seeks to provide quality life-saving services to address inclusive protection immediate risks and needs (including CP & GBV) of most vulnerable populations in priority areas, prevent protection risks (including CP & GBV) of most affected and vulnerable populations in priority areas through enhanced preparedness and resilience capacity as well as Strengthen the protection environment and enhance the protection of affected populations – including related to GBV and child protection, and through collective and intersectoral action.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project seeks to expand provision of protection services in Northern Mozambique, focussing on the Provinces of Cabo Delgado (Mocimboa da Praia, Macomia, Mecufi, Metuge, Mueda, Chiure and Montepuez), Nampula (Erati, Memba, Murrupula, Nacala-a-Velha and Nacaroa) and Niassa (Nipepe, Mecanhelas and Mandimba). The main target groups for the intervention are internally displaced people fleeing due to the insurgency attacks in Cabo Delgado, which have now spilled into Nampula province. The project will also seek to assist returnees, that is internally displaced people who have finally decided to return to their districts of origin such as Mocimboa da Praia.Special interest will be given to women and girls as they suffer more during displacements and are more exposed to all forms of gender-based violence. With the cultural and/or traditional gender norms embedded in the communities in northern mozambique, women also bear the burden of unpaid care work, as well as fending for families when they resettle in new areas.The intervention seeks to provide quality life-saving services to address inclusive protection immediate risks and needs (including CP & GBV) of most vulnerable populations in priority areas, prevent protection risks (including CP & GBV) of most affected and vulnerable populations in priority areas through enhanced preparedness and resilience capacity as well as Strengthen the protection environment and enhance the protection of affected populations – including related to GBV and child protection, and through collective and intersectoral action. Key target groups for the intervention are women and girls, as well as the youth, old aged and people living with disability, who are more exposed to protection risks, and their exposure is further deepened by the insurgency attacks and displacements. The project will set up Safe Spaces for Women and Girls who are made up of women and girls activities and will be leading interventions at community level identifying cases of GBV, giving public lectures in the communities, doing case management, among others. The members of safe spaces will also be trained on the basics of mental health and psychosocial support and will be providing first-aid MHPSS in the communities they stay. We will also set up child friendly space to facilitate easier integration of children in host communities - as children begin engaging and playing together, it will also pull together the parents and hence in the medium to long term bridge the gap between internally displaced people and host communities. Mobile Brigades on Protection and Health will provide principally Mental Health and Psychosocial support in IDP communities as well as host communities.ActionAid will assist protection focal points in leading the community mobilisation sessions towards identification and referral of people of concern with specific needs to specialised agencies and government authorities to attain services being provided. The participatory vulnerability analysis will inform the process of specific services prioritisation in coordination with government authorities and service providers locally available and in permanent consultation with local authorities and the INGD. The MHPSS services will continue to be provided to general IDPs and host communities by the protection focal points trained on non-specialised services, while those with MHPSS specific needs will be referred to specialised service providers locally available

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Provision of Psychosocial Support and a basic healthcare package to IDPs and host communities via mobile brigades of protection and health – ActionAid recognizes that in emergencies, women and girls are affected in different ways and require a range of interventions. Our project will organize psychosocial support for IDPs and host communities through the development of a layered system of complementary support that meet the needs of different women and girls at all stages of response. Our Women-Led Community Based Protection (WLCBP) approach which we will implement is informed by and focuses on the first three layers of the Inter Agency Standing (IASC) Mental Health Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) pyramid, i.e., basic services and security, community and family support, and focused, non-specialized support. Under the first layer (basic services), women leaders will advocate to ensure services are provided in ways that empower women. Under the second layer (community and family support), the project will provide support for communal spaces or meetings for women and girls to discuss their issues i.e., through the safe spaces established by the project. It will also support the re-establishment of normal cultural and religious events and activities that promote non-violent handling of conflict e.g., discussions, drama and songs. Under the third layer (Focused, non-specialized supports) the project will support the provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA), setting up self-help groups, para- and group counselling for women and girls etc. ActionAid has developed a WLCBP manual which will be used to roll-out these activities.2. build capacity of government staff, women, and communities on GBV prevention, risk mitigation and responsei. Sensitisation meetings with local community-based organisations (CBOs), government, law enforcement officials and schools on existing protection concerns, their duties to rights holders and vulnerable persons and effective ways of preventing and responding to protection issuesii. Quarterly interface meetings between government officials, local authorities, service providers and communities. These will be conducted quarterly to establish ways of working together with affected populations to combat GBV and other threats to protection. Tools such as international legal frameworks and models of protection mechanisms for CBOs and schools will be provided to these stakeholders as a performance benchmark.iii. Revitalisation of the Multisectoral Groups (composed of all Government authorities that are relevant for GBV prevention and response) to build linkages and strengthen GBV referral pathway and coordination of case management and GBV actors. iv. Conduct an inclusive and participatory protection monitoring in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa Provinces - using age, gender, and diversity approach, recognizing GBV sensitivities and gender norms within the community. Discussions for safety audits will be done separately with women, men, girls, and boys, to a. assess and monitor the overall safety and GBV prevention, mitigation, and response programming in target communities, b. identify promptly GBV risks and gaps in our program, c. enhance utilisation of evidence to inform our programming, advocacy, coordination, and partnership, including informing GBV response, risk reduction and prevention specialized programs, and etc. v. Dissemination of safety audit findings and follow-ups- evidence from the safety audit will be disseminated with recommendations for various GBV actors and affected communities will be provided. GBV actors will have an opportunity to respond to the findings, and allocation of roles and responsibilities to action recommendations3. Setting up of Safe Spaces for Women and Girls in the implementation districts as well as Child Friendly Spaces in schools and/or resettlement centers to facilitate integration of IDPs into host communities

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

With the experience that has been gained over the years implementing protection projects and working with internally displaced people and host communities, ActionAid has developed mechanisms to assist in potential risk identification so that prevention and mitigation measures are activated. ActionAid works with general protection focal points as well as thematic protection focal points. The general protection focal points are influential people in communities where we work, who play the vigilance role, analysing the operating environment, monitoring movements (especially strange movements) as well as the security situation. They are trained to effectively play the vigilance role, as well as on thematic areas of gender-based violence, child protection, psychosocial support, among others. through their work in the field we are able to identify potential risks and deal with the risks before they occur and hence minimising impact. The ActionAid code of conduct which ActionAid staff sign on joining the organisation,, including refresher courses and trainings on the code of conduct is also another preventative measure that ActionAid uses to prevent potential risks, including reputational, operational, protection and ethical risk. To deter protection risk, ActionAid organises orientation sessions for staff on organisational values and practices, organises periodic sessions on ActionAid code of conduct and humanitarian principles, as well as conducts regular monitoring of staff behaviour in the field by the protection officer. Proper coordination and liaison with government authorities and other stakeholders, clarifying communication protocols and information-sharing principles with those seeking personal information of beneficiaries are some measures used to curtail the ethical risk. To deter financial risk, various risk factors have already been identified and are regularly updated and to curtail the risk ActionAid invests in proper planning and maintainance of insurance policy, having developed an oganizational policy on Fraud and Money Laundering as well as a financial policy. ActionAid regularly monitors its procurement and financial management systems and practices and ensures they are aligned to international best practices and conducts regular compliance checks to ensure efficient use of resources.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

ActionAid puts women at the forefront of all its humanitarian action and resilience building work. Our approach is women-led and aims to promote, enable and support the role, agency and leadership of women in humanitarian action- from preparedness and response to longer term resilience building. ActionAid will focus on women’s rights programming, including protection programming, so that women have the space, and agency, to lead change processes. This project will ensure that a thorough gender analysis is included in any needs assessment and situation analysis and that it informs the basis of programming, so that the different gender groups such as men, women, girls and boys are consulted in order to get diverse perspective on needs, concerns and priorities, recognizing that all individuals' lives, and therefore experiences, needs, issues and priorities. The project will prioritize awareness-raising and communication activities that have gender-sensitive content and are representative of and accessible to the entire target audience. Moreover, ensure that the appropriate language is used and is gender-neutral, and portrayals of different gender groups are varied, avoiding gender stereotypes as much as possible. All data on beneficiaries will be disaggregated by sex and age, wherever possible, and analyzed and reported accordingly. The project will inform the affected people about different Feedback and Complaints mechanisms and the channels to be used will be determined by the target population. In every intervention, within and outside the camps, help desks will be set up in visible and easy to access areas with identification signs in Portuguese and local language so that all vulnerable groups are able to report and get timely feedback. AAMoz will also put in place a weekly meeting with community leaders in and outside the camps for feedback collection and consultations as well as labelled complaints boxes. To ensure equitably participation of both man and women in trainings and awareness raising sessions, AAMoz will identify, together with IDPs and host communities, safe and easily accessible areas for establishment of safe spaces (distance should be no more than 10 km, Sphere Standards); ensure that the trainings, awareness raisings sessions and mobile clinics interventions (time, place, schedule, size and weight, etc.) do not discriminate against vulnerable or marginalized groups; adopt positive measures to redress the discrimination in distribution of dignity kits to women and girls (e.g. ensure that pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable groups are given priority).

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Through working with general protection focal points who are ably trained to do mapping as well as identification of persons with special needs, the project will identify people living with disability and provide them with support for them to take up roles in the safe spaces and child friendly spaces so that they may self-represent in different meetings and fora.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All activities affect the environment and hence in project implementation, ActionAid will mainstream environmental protection and proper use by beneficiaries. Environmental assesments will be conducted to verify that the project areas are not high-risk areas in terms of climate change and other environmental hazards such as cyclones and flooding. If such risks are identified, ActionAid will engage responsible authorities such as the national institute for disaster management to capacitate the beneficiaries - women and girls in safe spaces, children and youth in child friendly spaces, as well as the aged and people living with disability on how to prepare for such calamities, as well as through carrying out simulations. An indepth environmental assesment and analysis will be conducted prior to activity implementation, and all relevant stakeholders will play their role in the analysis and dissemination of findings, which will be implemented through a developed action plan.ActionAid will train women and girls in safe spaces to be responsible for making sure all environmental aspects are being observed among beneficiaries and local communities where project will be implemented. ActionAid will promote the use of baskets produced based on alternative material instead of plastic bags in order to stimulate craft industry and craft initiatives that has been supplying the national market, hence promoting a brand I am Bio-pleasant in the communities and among ActionAid employees. ActionAid will implement environmental monitoring in the field (sampling and analysis) and social control to ensure the effectiveness of mitigation measures, impact assessment and demonstrate compliance with legal guidelines and other requirements. The Project will carry out similar monitoring and verify the activities carried out by beneficiaries and local communities on quarterly basis
