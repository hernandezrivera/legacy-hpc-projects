Project Summary

Nigeria’s North-East continues to experience intricate and multi-faceted humanitarian crisis in the water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) sector, as a result of the ongoing protracted conflict, unpredictable and continuous large-scale population displacements, recurrent large-scale cholera epidemics and seasonal flooding and fierce storms and winds. 3.4 million people need support to access safe water and basic sanitation facilities across the three most affected states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states). The main drivers of vulnerabilities related to WASH are closely linked to the conflict, including recurrent, unpredictable and continuous population displacements. They are also a result of pre-existing low levels of WASH services.
With the protracted displacement crisis WASH solutions are needed, particularly in 43 LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states. With congested populations in displacement sites, especially in 15 LGAs and wards in Borno State, water-borne diseases spread rapidly. Spiraling cholera outbreaks have taken hold each year. In 2018, the cholera outbreak has affected 18 LGAs with a total of 4,250 cases . In addition, 1.5 million people in the affected LGAs , including IDPs and host communities, are considered at risk and require cholera prevention interventions.
Flooding, fierce winds and sand storms have resulted into substantial damages of WASH infrastructures. In 2018, at least 20 per cent and 28 per cent of IDPs living in camps in Borno and Adamawa states respectively have been seriously affected as a sizeable number of latrines, showers, handwashing points, and solar panels got partially or totally destroyed, affecting access to WASH facilities and services. These conditions contribute to a more insecure environment for the population, further comprising their safety and dignity, and exacerbating risks of exposure and vulnerabilities to GBV. New arrivals from hard-to-reach areas, especially women and children, are particularly at risk and need urgent WASH support. They include at least 100,000 children under five and 30,000 pregnant and lactating women across nine LGAs in Borno State and two LGAs in Yobe State.
3.4 million people urgently need sustained, equitable and safe access of appropriate WASH services. This includes 1.1 million IDPs, 664,860 individuals in host communities, 867,233 returnees and 818,170 people in hard-to-reach areas. New arrivals from hard-to-reach areas as well as host communities and displacement sites receiving those people need to be prioritized, especially in already congested sites. With the rainy season (during the months of April-September) exacerbating issues regarding road access to certain LGAs, especially in Borno State, the delivery of humanitarian supplies and material is rendered impossible, leaving people in those locations without access to safe water and sanitation facilities.
In response to the needs identified and in contribution to strategic Sector objective 1, 2 and 3 of the WASH sector, IOM will scale-up its provision of WASH services targeting internally displaced people, returnees and host community in camp and camp-like settings.
To this end, IOM will (A) Ensure safe access to safe water facilities and services through the drilling, installation, maintenance and rehabilitation of boreholes; (B) Ensure safe access to sanitation facilities (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) through the operation and maintenance of existing facilities and the construction of additional gender segregated facilities; (C) Promote improved sanitation outcomes and practices in targeted settings through increased capacity of established community based committees. (D) Ensure environmental sanitation (solid waste management) in operation area through establishment of WASH Committee including- male and female community members, procurement and distribution of tools and equipment; (E) Distribution of WASH non-food items (NFI); (F) Capacity building and partnership with WASH partners and stakeholders
The IOM WASH project will be implemented in three States of North East Nigeria; Borno (20 LGAs), Adamawa (6 LGAs) and Yobe State (4 LGAs), targeting 210,000 individuals.
IOM ensures that all WASH activities take into consideration the gender-specific needs of project beneficiaries. Through this project, IOM plans to guarantee the participation of female, girls, boys and males as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and age group, and specifically address women and girls’ specific needs, such as Menstrual hygiene management (MHM).. All activities related to this project will equally benefit women and men, girls and boys and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. In regards to assessment, monitoring and evaluation of gender specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible. IOM will ensure that Do No Harm principles are adhered to throughout WASH interventions, particularly when it comes to WASH activities pertaining to latrine and water point locations and construction. The IOM WASH Team consist of both male and female staff members, which is an important component to ensure that both males and females are consulted and reached through IOM’s interventions. It also enables better monitoring of the true impact of interventions as it will ensure that women’s and girl’s voices are taking into consideration/promoted.

Needs

In 2018, more than 240,000 people arrived from hard-to-reach areas in camps and informal settlements in 43 LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states, further overcrowding existing WASH facilities in these sites. In 73 IDP camps across 11 LGAs in Borno State, the average allocation of water is 14 liters per person per day, below minimum standard of 15 liters, while IPDs are sharing a drop-hole latrine with an average of 72 people, below minimum standard of 50 people per drop-hole latrine . The 2018 cholera outbreak across the BAY states has been particularly deadly. At least 18 LGAs have been affected with 4,250 cases reported, including 112 associated deaths, with women and children disproportionally affected. This includes 33 per cent of cases with 24 per cent associated deaths in Borno State; 48 per cent with 28 per cent associated deaths in Adamawa State and the remaining in Yobe State. The outbreaks, especially in Adamawa and Yobe states, reveal the lack of structural WASH investments: early recovery and development partners need to scale up their programming to address those structural needs and prevent the spiral of cholera outbreaks. Systematic blanket chlorination in all areas at risks, including urban and rural locations, displacement sites, host communities, and return areas, is required along with the promotion of good hygiene practices, and the replenishment of emergency supplies (water containers, aqua tabs for household, water treatments, and soap). The emergency nutrition situation faced by newly-arrived children from hard-to-reach areas is of critical concern for the WASH sector, with an average GAM prevalence of 15 per cent and above in two LGAs in Yobe State, and 11 per cent and above in nine LGAs in Borno State . This includes at least 28,723 children screened in nine LGAs in Borno State, between October 2017 and August 2018, with GAM rates ranging from 11.4 to 50.1 per cent. Those proxy indicators reveal a poor nutrition status and an urgent need to improve WASH conditions which are key to cutting malnutrition rates, as exemplified in malnutrition-diarrheal diseases cycle. Lack of access to safe water and other WASH facilities poses specific protection risks including sexual violence and harassment. Only 38 per cent of latrines are segregated by sex (39 per cent in Borno State; 44 per cent in Yobe State) and 31 per cent of latrines do not have locks inside, which make latrines unsafe or potentially inaccessible for women and girls . For example, in the Farm Centre in Borno State, sexual abuse of girls was reported in latrines, which were not sex-disaggregated, did not have locks and were not well-lit. In 2019, IOM aims to ensure safe access to safe water, improved sanitation and scale up the promotion of hygiene to enhance emergency response capacity in WASH service delivery to ensure better service to affected population in North East Nigeria. The WASH projects will be implemented involving the community leaders for ownership and protection of the WASH facilities for sustainability as well as prepositioning WASH items and materials in preparedness and response measures in case of any disease outbreak.
