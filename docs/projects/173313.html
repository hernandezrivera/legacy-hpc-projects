Project Summary

In response to the needs outlined, the IRC proposes to continue providing life-saving health services that target Libyans and non- Libyan migrant, refugees and asylum seekers in Tripoli, west of Tripoli, Central and Southern Regions . As more refugees and asylum seekers are residing in urban areas, the IRC will collaborate with the MoH to continue to provide equal access to free of charge primary health care services to these targeted populations through existing public structures, such as PHCCs, located in urban areas. The IRC will deploy mobile medical teams to response to rescue at sea operations and at detention centers. In addition to deployment of a medical team at the Community Day Center (CDC) in Gurj- Tripoli and provide medical screening at Seraj PoC shelter during cash or NFI distributions. Furthermore, the IRC will provide 24/7 hotline for medical emergencies, ambulance transportation and referral including C-19 suspected or confirmed cases. In addition, The proposed project aims to promote wellbeing and resilience of the Libyan and non- Libyan population in the face of COVID-19 outbreak and reduce excess morbidity and mortality related to the COVID-19 outbreak by strengthening the preparedness and response capacity of the Libyan national healthcare system through; Provision of supplies for infection prevention and control (IPC) including personal protective equipment (PPE) to support the MoH’s COVID-19 preparedness and response efforts at the municipality level, which seems to be layer working most effectively at the moment. The IPC supplies will support MoH’s surveillance work in the community for early detection and treatment to break the cycle of disease transmission. IPC supplies will also be delivered to PHCs and isolation facilities managing COVID-19 patients to protect both healthcare workers and patients from cross-infections; Conduct training for healthcare staff on infection prevention and PPE use and maintenance.; Support the efforts of the MoH to scale up health information and communication through reproduction and distribution of IEC materials. The IRC will also train community health workers and other health staff on key COVID-19 message dissemination to increase community awareness; Support the Emergency Department within the MoH to increase capacity for patient isolation through provision of four isolation units. The IRC will follow the guidance of the Emergency Department in installing isolation units in strategic health facilities providing in-patient services.
The IRC will provide primary and reproductive healthcare services by deploying mobile medical teams (MMTs) to existing MoH Primary Healthcare Centers and will maintain referral pathways between different levels of care. MMTs will provide standardized packages of primary and reproductive health services, including antenatal and post-natal care, which are aligned with the EPHS for Libya using WHO protocols and guidelines. Clinical packages also include healthcare for children and the management of childhood illnesses as well as mental health services. SGBV case workers will be trained alongside the health staff on clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS) and mental health gap action plan (mhGAP) to promote comprehensive care.
MMT activities will be complemented by robust community outreach activities. The IRC will procure and supply medicine, medical equipment and supplies, laboratory equipment, nutritional supplies, necessary furniture. Work to strengthen its existing referral systems with local and international actors to maximize the number of people who receive appropriate next levels of healthcare; Complete minor rehabilitations of healthcare facilities
The IRC will provide technical training and assistance to effectively respond to emerging health needs and improve general health seeking behaviors. Technical training will cover topics such as specific disease management and adherence to treatment protocols.

1 - Needs

Libya remains one of the most vulnerable countries in the region due to its volatility caused by the large presence of national and foreign armed groups, smuggling of migrants, the illicit trade in drugs and oil, uncontrolled borders, and widespread corruption. The dire humanitarian situation has been exacerbated by COVID-19. Since the first confirmed case was reported in Libya in March 2020, the number of cases has risen steadily and there are now 50,906 confirmed cases and 746 deaths with a significant increase, particularly in Benghazi, Misrata and Tripoli with highest number of confirmed cases and yet the very low testing capacity (from March 25 to September 17 there have been 24,936 confirmed cases of COVID-19 with 394 deaths) . Given the acute shortages of laboratory capacity, the real number of cases is likely to be much higher than reported . Complementing 2020 Libya HNO and HRP, the latest Humanitarian Response Plan Prioritization prioritizes the migrants and refugees, many of whom are already vulnerable due to growing levels of insecurity, political fragmentation and weak governance that have led to a deterioration of basic service delivery, particularly in the health and protection system. In parallel, the flow of migrants into Libya from Africa and the Middle East continues, while the number of migrants departing Libya for Italy has reduced by 75% due to increased scrutiny and policing by the Libyan Coastguard. The pandemic has deepened the vulnerabilities of migrants, especially in urban areas. Due to COVID-19 related measures, about half of the Primary Healthcare Centres (PHCCs) and Hospitals have closed. This creates additional pressure to an already decrepit health system where migrants are systematically stigmatized and discriminated against when accessing services, particularly as they are seen as vectors of the virus. Urban migrants fearing imprisonment are discouraged from seeking critical public services like free healthcare and safe protection spaces. Restriction of movements and ongoing curfews have severely affected the little access to informal work and livelihood opportunities migrants once had. The unemployment rate among migrants has gone up to 27% in August (from 17% in February 2020), and migrants interviewed in 92% of targeted locations report COVID-19 has negatively impacted their livelihood opportunities. The lack of proper documentation and a national legal framework also increases their risk of being kidnapped, smuggled, exploited, or abused. Particularly women and children are facing elevated risks of sexual violence, sexual exploitation and control by smugglers, and are unable to move safely and freely in cities to access public areas. Sexual and reproductive and GBV health services are facing alarming accessibility, availability and quality issues. Birth spacing component of the family planning services remain basically non-existent in the public facilities but is mainly offered by private sector. Access to public GBV services by female refugees is highly challenged. Reproductive health services are available only in 15% communities. More than 80% of functioning PHC facilities in the west and in the east do not provide the required antenatal care services. In 2020, thus far, no health workers have been trained on cilinical management of rape (CMR). Mental health services are basically non-existent. The predominant number of mental health consultations is reported for Tripoli area while absent in 80% of other districts in the country. COVID-19 led to the closure of the main psychiatric hospital in Tripoli and suspension of outpatient services. Non-communicable disease treatment becomes less available as almost 100% of communities report hypertension and diabetes as two the most common reported diseases and causes of death. Disability support is only focused on Benghazi, Misrata, Sabha, and Tripoli.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Protection Risk 4.1 & 4.6: IRC will conduct client exit interviews with beneficiaries, solicit feedback on services and medical teams’ behaviors, and create an open forum for additional feedback. IRC will also continue to use the feedback/complaint boxes, and feedback forms. Feedback will be analyzed and course-corrective measures taken. All new staff involved will receive training on confidentiality and information protection. Facility safety audit assessments will be conducted, and interviews and focus group discussions held with women and girls to identify and mitigate access barriers.Protection Risk 4.2, 4.3 & 4.5:The IRC services will target both Libyan and non-Libyan regardless of their age, gender or status. the IRC will continue to collect beneficiary feedback to identify gaps in services for vulnerable groups such as women, girls, children, elderly and person with disabilities. Protection Risk 4.11: The IRC will partner with Handicap International to provide assistive devices, psychosocial support and awareness raising sessions on disability issues.Protection Risks 4.13, 4.14 & 4.15: The IRC MMT will offer integrated Clinical Care for Sexual Assault Survivor (CCSAS) services through GBV case workers, who will be embedded in the MMT. Survivors will receive clinical care and PSS support through trained health and protection staff. In addition, both health and protection staff will be trained on CCSAS multi-media training tools. Health facilities will be supplied with necessary supplies and equipment, including post-rape kits, as per IRC global GBV facility checklist to ensure services provided with dignity, confidentiality and privacy. The IRC will use the service mapping produced by the protection sector to refer and receive GBV cases to/from other actors.Protection Risk 4.12: The IRC will recruit a full-time psychologist and psychiatrist as part of the mobile medical teams to provide mental health and psychosocial support services. General Practitioners will receive mhGAP training to provide a wide range of MH treatments together with the psychologist. In addition, the IRC will procure and distribute psychotropic medications. In addition to awareness raising sessions among the targeted communities. Protection Risks 4.7, 4.16, 4.17, 4.18 & 4.19, 4.21, 4.22, 4.23: Through the established internal and external referral pathways and services mapping, the IRC will refer and follow up on beneficiaries attending the health facilities and in need of other protection assistances. In addition, the integrated composition of the mobile medical teams where both health and protection services are provided at the same health facilities will allow and strengthen referral process between health and protection actors to address the legal assistants.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

Direct beneficiaries of the Action will include vulnerable individuals such as women, girls, children, elderly, chronically-ill patients, persons with disabilities, and pregnant and lactating women. The IRC maintains a conflict-sensitive approach and provides services across statuses, including refugees, migrants, displaced Libyans, and returnees. In 2021, the IRC will focus its intervention on remote, hard-to-reach populations. The IRC’s 2021 strategy also prioritizes a needs-based, client-informed humanitarian response. This approach includes soliciting feedback from clients, and adapting projects to meet client-identified and evidence-based needs. At the onset of the Action, the IRC will conduct a series of KIIs and FGD to gather primary data about needs and access constraints for the general population and for acutely vulnerable groups such as women, people with disabilities, migrants, and ethnic minorities. IRC will conduct client exit interviews with beneficiaries, solicit feedback on services and medical teams’ behaviors, and create an open forum for additional feedback. IRC will also continue to use the feedback/complaint boxes, and feedback forms. Feedback will be analyzed and course-corrective measures taken. All new staff involved will receive training on confidentiality and information protection. Facility safety audit assessments will be conducted, and interviews and focus group discussions held with women and girls to identify and mitigate access barriers.
