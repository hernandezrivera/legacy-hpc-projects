Project Summary

The project “Strengthening resilience and community revitalization by providing sustainable livelihood options through restoration, protection, and provision approach and strengthening market systems in Yobe and Borno States“ will be implemented in 6 LGAs of Yobe and Kaga LGA of Born to contribute to improving resilience of households and communities through targeted interventions; access to income through income generation activities and the village saving and loan schemes (livelihoods stabilized and income recovered), and cash transfers (cash for work) for productive assets and social protection at community level (increased access to infrastructure and basic social services), and promoting community reconciliation and social cohesion. The action will employ sustainable and innovative approaches to engage the stakeholders, the communities, and the beneficiaries to make sustainable livelihood recovery models. This will allow to create employment opportunities and to create an environment to learn and employ vocational and technical skills. Local markets will also be strengthened by creating demand and improving purchasing power of the local populations.
The project will be implemented by COOPI and will focus on three objectives; “to support livelihood stabilization and income recovery, to restore local government and community governance and enhance social cohesion and reconciliation among IDPs, returnees and host communities and to increase access to basic social services through rehabilitation of community infrastructures”. These objectives will be achieved through community-based approaches; the Cash for work activities to cover the survival deficit while helping protect productive assets, the vocational skills training and IGA start-up cash grants help to support business development especially for the youths (40%), women (40%) and men (20%), the capacity building opportunity for the local government, CSOs staff and community based structures (like PMCs, women associations) aims to enhance governance and service delivery, and lastly the rehabilitation and or construction of community infrastructures to build durable solutions.

1 - Needs

The Project has been designed based on recent data and needs assessment from the Early Recovery & Livelihood (ERL) sector that COOPI led, as well as a rapid need assessment conducted by COOPI in October 2021. According to the 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS), an estimated 8.9 million people (HRP 2021) in the Northeast remain in need of life-saving interventions, including access to food and livelihoods support. The conflicts in North-East Nigeria have paralyzed the primary food production having devastating effects on farmers' assets and the market system. Before the conflict, up to 80% of the population relied on crops and livestock as their primary source of livelihood. Currently, these people rely on food assistance programs for sustenance. The high level of food insecurity is leading to acute malnutrition. The limited access to farmland is driving farmers to adapt their farming activities to smaller plots with a consequential shrink of the harvest and profits. In addition, the current COVID-19 pandemic worsens the situation. The incapacity to supply the output to satisfy the market demand made the consumer price index (CPI) increase from 16.47% in January 2021 compared to 15.75% in December 2020. The food index also rose to 20.57% in January 2021 as against 19.56% in December 2020. The recent Cadre Harmonise report from the Food Security Sector, estimated 963,683 people in phase three “crisis” (03) to five “Catastrophe/Famine (05), from June to August 2021, where 13 areas (Zones & LGA’s) are projected as food and nutrition insecure. The situation does not present a good state of food and nutrition security situation in Yobe state. Further, insurgents’ attacks on Geidam and Yunusari in April 2021 have deteriorated the situation. According to the latest COOPI, Early Recovery and Livelihood Sector Assessment 83% of the respondents were displaced after the attack by NSAGs in Geidam and Kanamma. Moreover, 76% of the respondents moved with the whole household whereas 13% migrated alone and the remaining 11% with part of the family. 69% of the respondents reported mild to severe damages to their assets while 31% had no harm to their properties. Furthermore, 34% of the respondents declared that their livestock have perished because of the attacks, while 26% experienced losses on their production cycles. The respondents declared that an influx of finance will facilitate their re-start petty trade businesses. To conclude, the situation shows that the lack of sufficient food, insufficient start-up capital, appropriate tools, and poor business skills or training are the primary and the most important challenges in Yobe State. A lack of financing, appropriate tools, and business skills can also prevent a small business owner from expanding his/her operations unless he/she resorts to other methods which may end up crippling the business. The communities that don't have functional markets or infrastructures nearby have to travel to adjacent wards or nearby communities to access key supplies, incurring a financial and opportunity cost in the process. COOPI’s rapid needs assessment indicates a gap in the sustainability and resilience of conflict-affected populations in Yobe state. The assessments also showcase the low capacity of government and communities to recover from the effects of economic and livelihoods assets deterioration. Income generation activities and other economic reintegration programming like VSLAs and livestock restocking, cash for work should be prioritized to improve self-reliance, enhance communities' resilience, and decrease vulnerability the priority of the communities on what support for the communities they would like immediately restored.

2 - Activities

COOPI’s proposed intervention aims to build community resilience and community revitalization through achieving the following objectives and activities. Objective 1: To support livelihood stabilization and income recovery. Activity 1.0: Cash for work activities to cover the survival deficit of 572 households (4,004 individuals) while helping protect productive assets by light community infrastructure rehabilitations will directly contribute to recovery; Sub-activity 1.1: Conducting CBPP verification. Sub-activity 1.2: Community mobilization/ sensitization and registration of 572 households. Sub-activity 1.3: Conditional cash distribution to beneficiaries through third party FSP fully registered with the EFSS. Sub-activity 1.4: Post Distribution Monitoring. Activity 2.0: Training and supporting IGA activity to empower 7,120 youth and women and create a sustainable platform for them to start up small scale businesses; COOPI will provide vocational skills training and start up cash grants for business development, and support through income generation activities to improve the livelihood situation of IDPS, returnees and host communities in Yobe and Born States. Sub-activity 2.1: Identification and selection of 7,120 IGA beneficiaries through registration committee. Sub-activity 2.2: Provision of Business management training to 7,120 IGA beneficiaries as per the National Directorate of Employment (NDE) guideline. Sub-activity 2.3. Provision of start up cash grants to the IGA beneficiaries through a third party FSP. Sub-activity 2.4: Post distribution Monitoring and Technical Support through follow up visits/ monitoring. Activity 3.0: Capacity building training of Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs) to increase access to credit. Sub-activity 3.1: Identification and registration of 742 IGA beneficiaries. Sub-activity 3.2: Conduct capacity building training of 742 IGA beneficiaries. Sub-activity 3.3: Procurement and distribution of in-kind VSLA equipment to the trained groups. Objective 2: To restore local government and community governance and enhance social cohesion and reconciliation among IDPs, returnees and host communities. Activity 2.1: Identifying participatory community driven social activities and conducting preventive and or reactive community cohesion and reconciliation workshops and meetings. Sub-activity 2.1.1: Identifying participatory community driven social activities. Sub-activity 2.1.2: Conducting sessions of preventive and or reactive community cohesion and reconciliation workshops and meetings. Sub-activity 2.1.3: Establishment of Community Resilience Groups (CRG), with diverse representation, to develop community resilience plans and conflict prevention forums. Activity 2.2: Providing training opportunities for capacity building of local government and provision of office equipment to enhance governance and service delivery. Sub-activity 2.2.1: Consultation meetings and sensitization sessions with line ministry staff. Sub-activity 2.2.2: Capacity building trainings for LGA departments staff and communities, including provision of basic service materials (ex. office supplies and utilities). Objective 3: To increase access to basic social services through rehabilitation of 200 community infrastructures. Activity 3.0: Rehabilitation and or construction of community infrastructures such as critical community infrastructures, agrifarms, livestock facilities, water access points, market sheds. Sub-activity 3.1: Conducting CBPP to map and prioritize on the infrastructure rehabilitation needs. Sub-activity 3.2: Rehabilitation and or construction of community infrastructures; water access points, market sheds, livestock facilities etc. Sub-activity 3.3: Capacity-building trainings on infrastructures management to communities’ members and structures, like the PMCs, Water User Committees (WUC), and social workers.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

In alignment with the HDP Nexus, COOPI is proposing a project with a cross-cutting and intersectoral perspective which synergically combines life-saving humanitarian assistance with long-term development activities and peace-building dialogue sessions at community level. This is based on the premise that to achieve long-lasting peaceful coexistence between IDPs, returnees and host communities, it is paramount to address immediate basic needs through a development lens which does not only ensure early recovery but aims to strengthen beneficiaries' resilience to conflict-driven and climate stressors in the long-run. As a proof, besides promoting the recovery of the main livelihoods and the rehabilitation of basic public infrastructures, COOPI simultaneously aims to provide skills-training for youth and capacity-building sessions for LGAs departments as well as to carry out peace-mitigation and conflict-management dialogue sessions at community level. By doing so, COOPI's goal complimentarily encompasses a strong contribution to social cohesion, economic development and self-reliant livelihoods through a localization approach and state system's strengthening: both ensuring the sustainability of the action and thus an enduring peace consolidation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The action will adopt a participatory approach where affected communities & community leadership will be the centre of the action. Special attention will be given to the involvement of vulnerable groups and people that may be discriminated and stigmatized based on gender, ethnicity, disability, experienced violence, and abuse, ensuring they are not left behind. The staff involved will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. Beneficiaries and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistle blower policy via email or helpline phone number. COOPI Protection Officers, PSEA Focal Person and PM coordinate each other, so that at the programming level, protection is mainstreamed. The COOPI staff on site will be recognizable by wearing visibility and reflective jackets. COOPI will mainstream the protection strategy in all components of the design and the implementation of the project. COOPI conducted a protection, gender, and AAP assessment in July 2021 to assess the current protection trends and protection concerns of the vulnerable population in the assessed locations where assistance is provided by COOPI. The key finding will be implemented to inform current and future interventions with regards to identification of measures to mitigate protection risks and negative impacts. Whilst gender will be considered during the selection of the beneficiaries. The PMCs formed by COOPI will have a strong woman representation (at least 50%) and will be involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project. Gender and protection will be considered in the entire project implementation by ensuring that social behavioral change communication is mainstreamed in the proposed project. The strong presence of women in the project and their continuous protection will indulge COOPI to make sure the project respects the safety, the dignity and the integrity of the women, men, girls, and boys receiving the assistance. COOPI monitoring team will ensure that the rights of all beneficiaries are fully respected and prevent gender oppressive stereotypes. The CRM teams will also make sure that beneficiaries are aware of how to report any gender related issues (through the complaint desk, the complaint boxes). COOPI commits to the International Human Rights Law which stipules that every person has the right to work, to freely choose their employment and to be protected from unemployment. In fact, People with Disabilities are at higher risk of unemployment rate due to social bias, lack of access to the workplace and poor access to information and finance (IASC Guidelines on Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action). To integrate People with Disabilities in the proposed project, COOPI will have a global approach integrating families and local NGO partners in the identification of barriers and to ensure representation in the activities. The water points and markets that will be built as part of the project will be accessible to people with disabilities. COOPI will prioritize PSN (people with special - disabilities) during the selection and identification of beneficiaries’ processes. COOPI will ensure that the stakeholders such as PMC’s and traders/ vendors that are involved in the project, ensure People with Disabilities accessibility.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI follows the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC)’s Accountability to Affected Populations Framework and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership’s (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management to set standards/benchmarks in its accountability approach to emergencies. COOPI already has in place a Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) with Community Feedback Assistants and Project Management Committees (PMC) in alignment with guidance provided by ERL Sector and donors. COOPI ensures regular and representative consultation of affected populations before, during and after the implementation of activities to capture their preferences, their respective priority needs and document barriers they face in accessing safe and meaningful assistance and information. Dedicated protection officers who speak local languages and who are gender-balanced to facilitate communication with communities are already working with COOPI in the nine communities of implementation. To re-enforce the already existing people-centered approach, established at the community level. The CFM focal person and PMCs involved will be trained on receiving feedback and setting up of multiple feedback channels including receiving feedbacks on the phone lines, logbooks, complaint boxes as well as CRM helpdesks. To ensure the sustainability of the interventions, the Project Management Committee formed by the COOPI will be empowered to take responsibility to further review and take appropriate action on any negative feedback or complaints.
