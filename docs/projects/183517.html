Project Summary

The proposal was designed in accordance with the multi-sectoral needs highlighted in the 2021 HNO and further described in the section below, and conceived in close coordination between ACTED and WFP. Starting from the School Feeding modality already in place in several schools of Blue Nile, this Action is meant to provide more sustainable solutions to address food and nutritional gaps through the creation of school- and kitchen- gardens and through a strong soft-component of guidance and training sessions on nutrition, agricultural and improved hygiene practices in schools and at HH level. Improved hygiene practices are crucial while handling food and preparing meals, but also to decrease the incidence of water-borne diseases such as cholera and diarrhoea (see the Need paragraph for more details on figures about Sudanese children). The hygiene intervention will also include the construction of handwashing facilities in schools and the provision of hygiene kits to the pupils. Furthermore, hand washing facilities will be set up during the conduction of trainings and distributions. With regard to Education, it is estimated that over three million Sudanese aged 5-13 years are not in classroom. At 43%, Blue Nile is the worst performing state for children who are out of primary school. To tackle the issue, school gardens under this proposal will incentivize school attendance for primary and secondary school-age children, who will be supported through the ready-to-eat rations during the preparation of gardens, in case their school is not already targeted by WFP's School Feeding Program. On food purchase, ACTED will follow a competitive procurement either in Khartoum or Damazine; the ready-to-eat rations will be stored in ACTED's warehouse in Damazine and brought to the final distribution point as per project's workplan; a FS specialist will be recruited to manage the WH and maintain the optimal conditions in terms of ventilation, size, temperature and humidity, for safe storage of food material; quantities of food will be stored as per demand and expiring dates. Furthermore, pupils’ mother will receive cash for the set-up of school-gardens while being trained and guided by ACTED staff allowing them to replicate the garden in their houses. In this way, kitchen garden beneficiaries will get help for sowing and harvesting, and will also have a short-term income available to further invest in their kitchen gardens. Therefore, in terms of population target, the project will operate on multiple levels, engaging primary and secondary school-age children, mothers, women and men at HH level. The selection criteria for FSL activities under this proposal will include but not limited to the following: a) HHs living in areas classified in IPC Phase 3 or above b) Female-headed HHs; c) HHs with chronically ill or disabled family members; d) HHs with acutely malnourished children; d) HHs who do not own productive assets or livestock; e) Daily wage-earning families (i.e., HHs who rely on casual daily labor for income); f) HHs living nearby functioning schools with space and resources for gardening, and whose children are pupils in the school.
Throughout the Action, ACTED will implement COVID-19 prevention measures. ACTED teams will receive trainings on COVID-19 preventive measures and a full set of PPE stock to be used during project activities. During activities, ACTED will ensure the beneficiaries and ACTED staff follow social distancing measures.

1 - Needs

As 2021 Sudan HNO, Blue Nile State has 330.000 people in need, of which 72,7% facing a severe status. Being one of the epicenters of conflict over the past 17 years, Blue Nile hosts over 47K IDPs, of which 96% have to deal with critical living standards. Furthermore, conflict over the Sudan-Ethiopia border is likely to continue with increased military presence, and Blue Nile is expected to continue receiving refugees from Ethiopia during 2022. To date, Blue Nile is one of the 14 flood-affected states in Sudan, with almost 2K individuals who got their assets damaged or found their access to safe shelters and WASH services compromised. Hence, floods have been a key driver of food insecurity, along with SDG devaluation and inflation (387,56% in August 2021) which drastically reduced families’ purchase power. Lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 contributed to compromise livelihood and daily labour opportunities, and to furtherly reduce access to food for most vulnerable. Women and their families, who are often excluded from economic opportunities, have faced multiple challenges in meeting their basic food needs. In food insecure households, women and girls are twice as likely to suffer from malnutrition as men and boys due to a combination of biological, social and cultural factors. Malnutrition in mothers, especially PLWs, can set up a cycle of distress increasing likelihood of low birth weight or child mortality, serious disease, poor classroom performance and low work productivity. In addition to COVID-19, poor hygiene practices and access to WASH services have had a significant impact on food security and nutrition gaps detected among the population. Only 13% of communities and 10% of state schools have access to handwashing, due to lack of soap and running water. Overall, high prevalence of poor hygiene, including open defecation (OD) and inadequate treatment/storage of drinking water, contributes to high incidence of water-borne diseases such as cholera, diarrhoea, typhoid. Diarrhoea kills one in ten Sudanese children and makes them more vulnerable to infectious diseases, the risk of dying for is higher for stunted children, who represent one third of the total in Sudan. 50% of acute malnutrition cases, which affects 19% of total population only in Blue Nile, are associated with diarrhoea/worm infections related in turn to OD and poor WASH. Even acute respiratory infections – another major cause of children’s mortality- are closely linked to WASH gaps. Sanitation and handwashing represent crucial barriers to faecal-oral contamination, and OD elimination can reduce diarrhoeal morbidity by 36%. This proposal tackles the hygiene gaps through active engagement of school-age children in spreading good practices, starting from their classrooms and through provision of hygiene kits and handwashing facilities in the schools.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project will be rooted in ACTED’s global flagship initiative - THRIVE, which ACTED has developed to contribute to long-term rural community resilience. THRIVE or ‘Towards Holistic Resilience in Vulnerable Environments’ aims to integrate ecosystems, livelihoods, markets, social cohesion and grassroots governance in vulnerable environments. THRIVE is a sustainable approach to support regenerating ecosystems, through a sequenced, layered and integrated initiative that is community based and tailored to each specific context and location. THRIVE has 3 pillars: REVIVE, EMERGE and INTEGRATE. REVIVE is an agro-ecological approach to food security and agricultural livelihoods that works with natural processes for food production to restore soil health, biodiversity, and ecosystem function; EMERGE aims to support all post-harvest handling from household level to producer group and co-operative level as well as local government structures, and includes market infrastructure ranging from roads to storage facilities as well as access to market information systems; and INTEGRATE connects both REVIVE and EMERGE to promote social cohesion and joint natural resource management, using exchange visits and dialogue as its vehicle. ACTED’s THRIVE approach will underlie and link all results and activities proposed through this project. A key driver behind livelihood diversification is to ensure that communities are not reliant on one sole means of production, leaving them very vulnerable to the effects of climate change. Allowing communities, the opportunities to diversify livelihoods provides multiple means of income generation, building more resilient communities. THRIVE also ensures that community engagement and learning is a key component of activities, so that communities hold the knowledge and skills to be able to pass them along to others within the community, allowing a knock-on effect, that allows larger numbers of people to benefit from the action, building its impact and sustainability. ACTED will make sure that the proposed intervention will not cause harm to the beneficiaries’ assets and environment. ACTED prioritizes the prevention of crop attack and integrates an approach on biological control in both market gardening and rainfed crops. ACTED will train the beneficiaries on how to prevent the arrival of pests/diseases. ACTED will rely on the knowledge, learning capacity of the beneficiaries and the locally available means to prevent crop attacks. In terms of sensitization, key messages on natural and traditional methods of seed protection will be conveyed to beneficiaries during pre-distribution sensitization.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Program envisages the specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups direct and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase:- Before each intervention, ACTED conducts an assessment of the security context and protection risks, followed by a regular monitoring of the situation throughout the project’s lifespan.- The whole community will be sensitised on the scope and the activities of the project and fully informed on the selection criteria, which have been designed to address the protection risks and cover the sectoral needs of the most vulnerable (single women, PLWS, children, elders, PwDs, HHs with no source of income, HHs with PwDs, HHs with more children under 5 years old). Program Implementation Phase:- ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during its community sensitization and trainings. - First aid kits and protective wear are also provided to ACTED’s staff, beneficiaries and daily workers, in line with the type of activities. Accountability:- ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM at Area office and Field office levels, where beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff permanently deployed in the field, ACTED will also conduct regular spot checks. ACTED will evaluate if the complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the ongoing Action and inform the future projects’ design. A more detailed description of the CRM is provided under the AAP section of this proposalHuman Resources - ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct for The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and the UNHCR Code of Conduct. This CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to the coordination through a standardized communication pattern and actions will be taken by the coordination.- Finally, ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment and inclusion mechanisms.
