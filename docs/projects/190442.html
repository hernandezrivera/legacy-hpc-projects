Project Summary

Although Tigray education Bureau has decided to resume formal schooling after the two and more years’ interruption due to the COVID 19 and the crisis in Tigray, the majority of the schools have different difficulties to resume the schooling. According to the school damage assessment reports, many schools at southern east Tigray have the following problems. The majority of the schools has lost their facilities/ supplies like; black board, Desks, Books, Chalk, Science kits, teaching aids, etc. They also run out of WASH facilities and they have poor access to clean water and even some of them have lost their classrooms either fully or partially which needs renovation. In addition to this, the teachers and the students are highly traumatized where they are in a high need of PSS and SEL trainings. As the teachers were away from reading, they need to be capacitated with different trainings like; pedagogical trainings, Conflict sensitive education, teaching methodologies, and so on. In this regard, the unreleased school damage assessment report also shows that 83% of schools are either damaged or looted (Education Bureau, 2022). Besides, more than 1,000 schools were damaged or looted in Tigray affecting an estimated 160,000 students. 79 Schools in Tigray, are still occupied by IDPs limiting access to education for 40,518 children, including 20,120 girls. (OCHA 2022). According to the Southeast Zone, 96,687 children (48,134 girls) were planned to attend at the six Woredas that WV has intervening on grades 0-6. Whereas the number of children who are now attending in school are 17,013 (8,662 girls) which is only 17.6%. This shows that, 79,674 (39,472), i.e. 82.4% of the children are still left out of school. The reason behand the children’s low attendances is lack of scholastic materials and lack of food which can be mitigated by scholastic material support and cash injection as motivation for attendance of school feeding if possible. In this regard, WV has best experience to accomplish this for the reason that, World Vision aspires “educated for life” for all girls and boys with strategies; Enhanced Early Readiness of Children, Enhanced Literacy Environment and Conducive School Atmosphere. To realize those expected results, WVE has been employed various appropriate working models to each result. The models include Learning Root (LR), which goes with Early Childhood Development (ECD); Unlock Literacy (UL) it goes with reading, & Basic Education Programme - Safe school and Life skills (BEP) which is for conducive schools. Those models are selected, for each model is directly associated to each immediate outcomes of the Technical Program. In turn, each model has its own well-structured pillars, which indicate how to reach to their results in a synergic manner. P
Protection interventions are also highly needed in Tigray Amhara and Afar. The severity of these conditions was confirmed by the Multi-sector need assessment (MSNA) conducted by WV in the three regions in December 2021. Across the three regions, the top three reported risks affecting boys and girls were, in order, being killed or maimed, mental health/psychosocial problems, and sexual violence. Nearly 95% of respondents in all three regions stated that there is either partial or no access to the following services: safe spaces (56%), case management (47%), and medical care for SGBV survivors (43%), and MPHSS (54%). When asked why, nearly all HHs responded that they have either no awareness/knowledge about the service or that current service providers do not prioritize these services because they are providing for more immediate needs. These facts and field realities require a critical need for protection, SGBV services, mental and psycho-social support for the affected people in general and for children and women who suffered traumatic experiences in particular. In addition, there is critical need to other basic humanitarian assistance.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

World Vision Ethiopia (WVE) is practicing Humanitarian Program Accountability Frame (PAF) with its four major pillars of Providing Information, consulting with Communities, Promoting Participation and Collecting, and Acting on Feedback and Complaints. WVE recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities, and disposition of those with whom we work and to answer for our actions and decisions. Hence, the organization is keen and committed to mainstream this accountability frame throughout the project cycle management following the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership Standard. Accordingly, relevant program information, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiaries’ entitlement from the project will be made available to communities in a timely, accessible, and accurate manner, communities will be informed and understand and agree with key decisions relating to our intervention. WVE will devote itself to purposely empowering communities and building their capacity to participate in all components of the program cycle.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

WV will maintain its existing AAP mechanisms, described below. However, due to COVID-19 and associated restrictions, WV will make modifications as needed, especially to in-person methods, to ensure that beneficiaries can safely provide feedback throughout the project period. WV will respond to any critical or sensitive protection issues that arise with confidentially and utilizing survival – centered approach. WV will ensure the information acquired will be shared in the need to know basis and informed consent is acquired from the survivor and /or caregivers of survivors to report and refer the case to relevant services. WV will ensure access to medical, psychosocial, legal, and other services relevant to the particular need of the survival through direct support and referral services. WV will establish a feedback system called the Community Help Desk (CHD) for both beneficiaries participating in the program and the broader community. The beneficiaries will be provided with information on goods and services they will receive from the project, as well as how to provide comments and complaints about the project or World Vision. Any complaint from beneficiaries will come through the Community Help Desk and proper corrective measures will be taken. To support this, a Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) committee will be set up. CRM training will be provided for CRM committee members around complaint/feedback handling, information sharing, and accountability standards. WV understands that different types of complaints will require different approaches, and WV will ensure that sensitive complaints are met with a confidential investigation, whereas less sensitive complaints will more likely be resolved quickly once necessary changes made. This will give World Vision the opportunity to involve beneficiaries in project management by incorporating their inputs. By involving beneficiaries, World Vision will be able to resolve complaints and issues. As part of WV’s complaint and feedback handling mechanisms, the Appeal Committees will be established in the response area and will be oriented on their roles and responsibilities. This will be communicated to all beneficiaries in all operational areas. The Appeal Committees will play an indispensable role during the beneficiary selection process to solve complaints related with beneficiary selection and will continue to be crucial throughout the project period in receiving and solving any complaints from beneficiaries on any issues related with the project. This aims to improve the quality of WV program implementation, strengthen WV’s relationship and acceptance by the communities, reduce the risk of fraud or funds being used inefficiently and reduce the risk of harm to children and other beneficiaries. These information sharing points will be decided upon implementation in partnership with the community, to ensure they are accessible and relevant. Based on the accountability assessment, WV will secure feedback via face-to-face methods, an information board with a suggestion box, phone and logbook. Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) training will be provided for CRM committee members around complaint/Feedback handling, information sharing and accountability standards. WV understands that different types of complaints require different approaches in handling them, ensuring that sensitive complaints are met with a confidential investigation whereas less sensitive complaints will more likely be resolved more immediately with necessary changes made. All responses will be given both verbally and in writing, and will be recorded by the implementers. The CRM Committee serves as an effective entry point both to share information to the wider beneficiary group, as well as receive complaints or feedback from beneficiaries.
