Project Summary

The proposed response aims at providing direct comprehensive services at HH, hospitals and HF levels including specialized MHPSS, Physical and Functional Rehab and SRHR through a twin-track approach.
- Multi-sectorial mobile units composed of Social Workers, Nurses, Physiotherapists and Psychologists identify and provide tailored and individual assistance to the most vulnerable persons, especially the persons with disabilities and their caregivers who might be excluded from the community safe spaces due to mobility issues, distance or social norm barriers. Through a unique home-based intervention and integrated outreach teams, composed of a Rehab and a PSS Officer, persons with injuries and/or disabilities receive home-based Rehab and PSS services. SOPs are implemented to frame this intervention, to define the eligibility criteria (only for persons who cannot access HF). An internal and external multi-sectorial referral system is facilitated to ensure timely emergency assistance for the most vulnerable individuals.
- Support to Gov hospitals and health facilities (HF) both in the host and the refugee communities. Physiotherapists provide direct comprehensive Rehab services in the HF and build the HW capacities through trainings and mentorship. HI has 11 fix points equipped with Rehab equipment and ran by 11 teams that includes Physiotherapist and PSS Officers providing Rehab and PSS services to those with enough mobility to access the HF. Finally, the Rehab team provides assistive and mobility devices and other specific items (crutches, bedsore mattresses…) when necessary, to people with physical and functional limitations in order to improve their mobility, enhance recovery for person with injury and prevent further complications. Training and advices are provided to persons them and their caregivers on basic home-based rehab and proper use, maintenance and repair of mobility devices.
Individualized PSS aims at reducing the symptoms faced by individuals with chronic and long-term disabling conditions. PSS services focus on individual assessment to create personalized care plans to increase their functionality and enable individuals that face psychiatric or distress symptoms to take part in group activities to support their social skills whilst learning coping mechanisms. For those in need of specialized psychiatric support, HI PSS staff provides basic counseling combined with referral and follow up of medication continuation at home/center level. PSS Officers work with caregivers to alleviate their distress and ensure that persons with disabilities who require daily rehabilitation care understand the necessity of modern care services compared to traditional healers.
HI Nurses focus their outreach support on the following health issues: wound care, bedsores care, ANC and PNC. HI Nurses provide maternal care for women who cannot access HF, including the prevention and detection of pregnancy-related risks and complications. The Nurses then refer to the nearest HF and follow up to ensure they received the necessary services. HI raises awareness on the risks to deliver at home encouraging pregnant women to attend a HF for the delivery, ensuring an integrated and complementary response. Pregnant women with mobility issues are regularly followed by HI Nurses, who control their vital signs and ensure a healthy pregnancy by providing basic advices to the pregnant women and young mothers who cannot attend the HF or the fix points.
HI has been present in Cox’s Bazar for more than 12 years and is part of the Health sector, including MHPSS WG and the ADWG the aim of which is to the aim of which is to mainstream Age and Disability across all sectors through trainings on data collection (WGSQ), the types of impairments, barriers and facilitators.
Along with a strong standardized CRFM which uses different channels including individual and group feedback sessions and a hotline, HI ensures the respect and promotion of its PSEA policy.

Needs

In 2017, 721,641 Rohingya refugees fleeing violence and persecutions in Rakhine State, Myanmar have sought protection in Cox’s Bazar district, triggering the largest and fastest refugee influx into Bangladesh. Two years later, the Rohingya refugee community has stabilised around one million individuals living in extremely precarious conditions in 34 camps spread between Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar district. Psychosocial distress resulting from the persecutions, violence and forced displacements is extremely common in the refugee population. It is exacerbated by their dire living conditions and triggered by secondary stressors stemming from their inability to meet their own basic needs. Inadequate food, lack of employment, poor shelter conditions, poor water and sanitation facilities, lack of clothes and hygiene items are identified by both men and women to be among the top three causes of distress, in June 2019. The rapid growth in refugees’ population put a strain on the public health system creating significant gaps in accessing primary health and MHPSS services for both host and refugee populations. Access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal and newborn health services remain a major concern, with only 38% of the deliveries occurring in health facilities in Host Communities (43% in camps). In 2016, GBV affected 87 percent of ever-married Bangladeshi women and the rate of adolescent pregnancy (113 births per 1,000 women aged 15-19 years) was the highest in South Asia. In addition, 53% of the decision-makers within refugees communities households regarding the location of delivery are men. Only 13% of the decision-makers regarding the location of delivery are pregnant women. Moreover, 82% of the deliveries occur at home. (JMSNA, Ocotber 2019). Finally, the prevalence rate of persons with disabilities in the camps has been estimated at 9% by the UNHCR in June 2019. All women, men, boys and girls with physical impairments need to receive Rehabilitation services. Some of the challenges faced by the persons (including women, men, boys and girls) with disabilities or temporary impairments, injuries include lack of specialized services to address their specific needs coupled with physical barriers (lack of assistive devices to improve their mobility, lack of accessible facilities) and social and attitudinal barriers including discrimination, bullying, violence and abuse.

Activities or outputs

Specific Objective 1: Activity 1: Provision of physical and functional rehabilitation sessions including provision of assistive devices and specific items at health facilities/hospital to persons (including women, men boys and girls) with injuries and/or with disabilities.Activity 2: Provision of technical support to the relevant health facilities (5 government hospitals and partners' clinics) through training for health workers on basic early physical rehabilitation care to improve referrals, understanding of rehabilitation care within the health facilities and ensure comprehensive and optimum care for the patients/beneficiaries.Activity 3: Provision of physical and functional rehabilitation sessions including assistive devices through centre and home-based care to persons (including women, men, boys and girls) with injuries and/or with disabilities.Activity 4: Capacity building of the caregivers of persons with disabilities and/or injuries on basic home based-care to ensure the understanding of the importance of rehabilitation and optimum care.Activity 5: Provision of individual level psychosocial support to overcome stress related symptoms that effect individuals with long-term chronic disabling conditions. Activity 6: Provision of SRHR (ANC, PNC, awareness) through individual, family and group sessions to persons (including women, men, boys and girls) facing mobility issues in the camps. Specific Objective 3:Activity 1: Provision of community-based PSS group sessions with focus on to self identification of psychological distress and symtopms and to support the healing process of individuals obtaining specialized PSS services (psychological/psychiatric support)Activity 2: Internal and external referrals to basic and specific specialized services
