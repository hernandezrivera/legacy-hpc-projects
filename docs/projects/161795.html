Project Summary

Children separated from their parents and families because of the conflict are among the most vulnerable. Separated from those closest to them, these children have lost the care and protection of their families in the turmoil, just when they most need them. They face abuse and exploitation, and even their very survival may be threatened. They may assume adult responsibilities, such as protecting and caring for younger sisters and brothers. Children and adolescents who have lost all that is familiar – home, family, friends, stability – are potent symbols of the dramatic impact of humanitarian crises on individual lives. The breakdown of social structures and services accompanying major crises means that communities and States themselves may not be in a position to provide the necessary protection and care for children without families. It is therefore imperative that humanitarian organizations ensure that the most vulnerable children are protected.
There are the Inter-agency Guiding Principles on Unaccompanied and Separated Children, developed by this UACS working group for the purpose of guiding future action, based on international human rights, humanitarian and refugee law. They seek to ensure that all actions and decisions taken concerning separated children are anchored in a protection framework and respect the principles of family unity and the best interests of the child. All stages of an emergency are addressed: from preventing separations, to family tracing and reunification, through to interim care and long-term solutions. Advocacy must be continuous and is more effective tailored programmed for the UASC that will first ensure their protection, provision of sustenance and adequately represented

1 - Needs

The special needs of gender inclusion must be taken into consideration in implementation protection program. Establishing child-friendly spaces has become a standard activity for child protection in emergencies, especially in situations of displacement. The creation of single-sex discussion groups within the child friendly space (CFS )can be used to ensure free interaction and equal opportunities. From the total target population for the program 50% of both sex will be taken as part of the beneficiaries. Activities on SGBV will be conducted as well as assessment of the special need. Action to help Unaccompanied and separated children requires long-term commitment from the organizations involved. These should begin considering at the early stages of program implementation of their involvement when and how they will end that involvement and hand over the activities to LGAs, State and national as well as other entities for sustainability. The project will ensure in identifying all the stakeholder and relevant agencies and develop sustainability plan at early stage and ensure fully respected.

2 - Activities

* Community mapping/risk assessments.* Identify, register and provide case management services to UASC and other children with protection concerns.* Identify, train, and provide material and other support to foster families to provide alternative care to unaccompanied minors and other children in abusive or highly vulnerable households.* Place unaccompanied minors and other children in abusive or highly vulnerable households with trained foster families, and monitor their situation.* Hold weekly FTR meetings with relevant actors including SMWASD and ICRC.* Register parents/family members with missing children and use this information to cross-check with the list of unaccompanied and separated children.* Set-up child friendly spaces and adolescent centers in the targeted programme locations.* Mobilize and create awareness about the intervention among parents and children.* Provide facilitation to trained animators and youth mentors.* Periodic maintenance of the child friendly space (CFS) and adolescent centers including fence and amenities.* Provide supplies including play, recreational, educational and interactive materials and equipment.*Conduct age and gender appropriate recreation, social, learning and creative activities including storytelling, games, reading, arts and drama.* Map and regularly update an inventory of referral partners in the different programme locations for use by field staff.* Carry out FTR, including photo tracing, pre-reunification family preparations and post-reunification follow-up.* Set up children’s clubs/reactivate house structures in at least 2 primary schools per LGA covered.* Establish sports and cultural leagues, and facilitate inter-groupcompetitions.* Set up adolescent discussion groups.* Hold monthly meetings with animators and youth mentors.* Identify and refer children requiring other child protection and support services.* Carry out mapping of community groups/associations and select at least 2 groups with whom to work.* Carry out a capacity gap assessment of selected groups and based on findings, develop respective group capacity development plans.* Organize and hold community conversations and mixed community group dialogues on relevant psycho-social and child protection issues.* Development and implementation of local action to address identified risks.* Development and dissemination of service charters and referral pathways.
