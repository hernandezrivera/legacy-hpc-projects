Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at strengthening the protection environment for all conflict affected populations ( IDPs, returnees and host communities) Ninewa, Salah al Din, Erbil and Kirkuk Governorates. This initiative will provide comprehensive and holistic protection assistance packages to targeted individuals in order to mitigate protection risks as well as to foster self-resilience of affected individuals, with tailored assistance for highly vulnerable cases. Protection assistance includes protection monitoring aimed at identifying and referring vulnerable persons in need of case management or other services and collecting data on protection issues/incidents and trends to inform evidence based advocacy; legal assistance (awareness, counseling, administrative and court representation) services including civil documentation, HLP (only counseling and awareness) and family law for GBV survivors and children at risk; full case management services for Persons with Specific Needs, CP and GBV survivors. For vulnerable persons under case management, a package of internal specialized services such as psychological individual counseling, legal assistance, emergency cash assistance for high risk cases, sexual and reproductive health and structured PSS will be provided as needed; structured PSS targeting children and adults (particularly women and PWSN); awareness sessions on various protection topics and risks faced by the community including legal topics, sexual and reproductive health, general protection topics, availability of services; sensitization of the community for prevention of child labor and child marriage.
Intervention will target girls, boys, men and women among displaced and returnees communities in urban and camps targeted locations of Ninewa, Erbil, Salah al Din and Kirkuk Governorates with a mix of mobile and static interventions.

Needs

2019 is announced as a year characterized by the presence of different categories of people in need: IDPs in-camps, IDPs out of camps, returnees and non-displaced people. All mentioned categories continue to suffer from reduction of actors and services provided in certain areas and the impossibility of the Government to respond in a timely and satisfactory manner to their basic needs. Expectations about large scale returns in 2018 were not met, with 2 million IDPs who remain displaced. 65% of those said stated they have no intention to return to their AoO in the next 12 months. IDPs, returnees and host communities continue to require protection assistance including adapted services to ensure equitable access. Persons with perceived aﬃliation to extremists are at heightened risk of violence, exploitation and a range of rights violations, including: movement restrictions, seizure or denial of access to civil documents, denial of access to public services including healthcare and education, arbitrary arrest and detention, and barred returns to their AoO. The mental health and psychosocial support needs of affected populations have become more pronounced. The protracted nature of displacement, in addition to PTSD as a result of war has exacerbated existing vulnerabilities including episodes of suicide. Lack of civil documentation remains a major need; undocumented IDPs are unable to return to Areas of Origin (AoO); HLP legal issues is cited as one of the main reasons why IDPs cannot return to their AoO; IDPs and returnees lacking documentation suffer restriction of movement and access to basic services; Forced, premature and obstructed returns continue to be reported in Salah al-Din, Kirkuk and Ninewa governorates, including through coercive practices, often resulting in secondary displacement. Moreover, many families lack access to information necessary to make informed decisions about return. IDPs residing outside of camps, are not in possession of formal lease agreements, and are at heightened risk of forced evictions, as well as exploitation and abuse by owners as they seek to restore possession of their properties, and authorities seek to forcibly return IDPs to their AoO. 32% of IDPs surveyed reported that they do not intend to return home due to HLP damage/destruction, secondary occupation, or lack of proof of ownership. Vulnerable women and girls among IDPs, returnees and host communities continue to experience domestic violence, sexual violence, exploitation and abuse, forced marriage and denial of resources. GBV violations are still under-reported but approximately 63% of all reported GBV incidents are perpetrated by partners, brothers, and fathers and the majority has been reported in IDPs camps and informal settlements. Women and girls with perceived aﬃliation to extremists – in particular - are too often subject to sexual violence, exploitation and abuse by security actors in camp settings in addition to denial of civil documentation. As the duration of displacement has increased, limited resources and economic hardship have led displaced women and girls to resort to negative coping mechanisms, including survival sex. Children comprise approximately half of the people in need. In 2018, a high prevalence of poverty-induced/negative coping mechanism such as child labour and child marriage were observed both among IDP and returnee children, compared to past years when grave violations such as indiscriminately targeting of children during active conflict, and family separation, were more prevalent. 30-50% of children (across 15 districts of Iraq) show signs of psycho-social distress. Boys and girls recruited and used by pro-government armed forces or armed groups are subject to detention, separation from their families, they experience acute psychosocial distress and face challenges with reintegration into their communities. Moreover, they face barriers in access to civil documentation, education and other services. Girls and boys living in and out of camps are increasingly out of school (26%), mainly due to the lack of financial means, while children with disabilities experience physical and social barriers in accessing essential services, and are more vulnerable to violence, exploitation and abuse. A significant number of children face challenges in accessing birth certificates and other documentation, especially those born out of rape. Sources: 2019 HNO, REACH Multi Cluster Needs Assessment, internal reports.

Activities

Target activities for IDPs, returnees and host communities in Iraq and KR-I include: 1. Legal services (IDPs, returnees and host communities): including legal awareness, counselling, assistance on civil documentation, family law, HLP issues, payments of legal fees; moreover, INTERSOS will work closely with local authorities to facilitate process and find alternative solutions for most complicated cases, especially those related to individuals (such as Female Head of Households) perceived as allegedly aﬃliated to extremist groups, support Directorates of Civil ID, contributing at advocacy with relevant stakeholders in order to eliminate barriers in access legal services; 2. Protection monitoring (IDPs, returnees and host communities): at household and community level, in targeted locations (camps and non-camps) through Households Assessments (ASSIST included), identification and referral of vulnerable persons, identification of protection incidents and trends to be shared with relevant actors for advocacy purpose, Focus Group Discussions, Community Service Mappings. Thematic reporting and analysis will be developed based on the protection information gathered to highlight important protection trends and inform better programming. 3. Comprehensive case management services ( IDPs, returnees and host communities) for PWSN, CP and GBV cases and proper referrals to specialized services (internally or externally); 4. Specialized psychological and psychosocial support services IDPs, returnees and host communities) ensuring safety and confidentiality; provision of individual psychological counselling for cases under case management and community-based/structured PSS activities. Existing PSS programs for children will be enhanced through the adoption of the cluster endorsed PSS curriculum.5. Emergency cash for protection ( IDPs, returnees and host communities) for most vulnerable cases (in order to mitigate protection risks) in the framework of a defined case management plan and following specific criteria defined through Standard Operating Procedures; 6. Awareness sessions and spreading of key messages ( IDPs, returnees and host communities) at individual and community level on: Legal topics, sexual and reproductive health, general protection topics, availability of services and related criteria to access and ad hoc services for specific groups (conducted by a technical staff). Prevention of child labor and child marriage 7. Community-Based Protection.Provision of community-based social and recreational activities, BLN and English courses, targeting children, youths, women and men. Mobilization and active involvement of community volunteers, from both the IDP population and the host communities, in the planning, organization and implementation of the social and recreational activities, information dissemination, awareness sessions on protection issues as well as of the community outreach component; Supporting existing Community Committees, involving them in decision-making processes as well as in the designing, planning, implementing and monitoring of activities and identification of protection risks or concerns within their community. Engaging the IDP and host community in the areas of displacement in planning and Implementation of small-scale quick impact projects (QIPs) imbidded in on-going protection activities, with the objective of increasing self-reliance and fostering peaceful coexistence. 8. Sexual and Reproductive Health awareness and counseling ( IDPs, returnees and host communities) for women and girls at risk of GBV/GBV survivors.

Protection Risk Analysis

Target population is not supportive with the prevention component of the program Changes within the bureaucratic Iraqi legal system, volatility of legal procedures and civil directorates capacity to cover caseload. Reluctance from government oﬃcials to provide services to certain beneficiaries due to their background or perceived aﬃliation. Request from different actors to share confidential information about beneficiaries served, due to their background, vulnerability (e.g. GBV survivors) d or perceived aﬃliation. Risks faced by INTERSOS staff, including threats, arrest and detention for supporting families with alleged/perceived affiliation to access services, particularly legal assistance for obtaining civil documentation. Changes in displacement flows due to deterioration of situation Reduced or no access to areas of intervention due to instability of the security situation and/or inability to implement activities due to constraints or blockages from the governmental/security actors.

Mitigation Measures

Involvement of the community in the design of the prevention activities including in the elaboration of the key messages. Constantly monitor the situation of movements of the population and keep regular flow of communication with relevant Clusters. Maintain a certain level of flexibility in the deployment of protection mobile unit Introducing different stakeholders to INTERSOS program and humanitarian principles in the concerned area. Maintaining relations with local authorities (mayors, civil directorate oﬃcials, mukhtar, security forces/actors, judges..) and increasing negotiations with those if needed on specific issues (civil documentation, threats or arrest/detention of staff, humanitarian access…) Inform cluster of developments and advocate at cluster level for overarching challenges.

How is your project accountable to affected population

In 2019 INTERSOS will continue enhancing ownership of the community by involving them to a greater extent in decision-making processes as well as the designing, planning, implementing and monitoring of activities. Where possible, community committees will continue to be supported and equipped with the necessary knowledge and skills to analyze and evaluate their own protection needs through a range of participatory activities such as community risk mapping, risk analysis from an age, gender and diversity perspective and development of community action plans. The community committees will also be supported in setting up mechanisms at community level to quickly report any protection incidents. The participation of children in the identification of protection concerns as well as in the evaluation of the activities will be fostered through the established child-friendly feedback mechanism. This ensures that their voices are heard, increasing their self-confidence, awareness of their rights and promoting trust in the organization and structures in placeFurthermore a dedicated internal complaint mechanism through which beneficiaries can report complaints and feedback via different entry points (complaint hotline, complaint boxes, trained community volunteers, in-person walk in) will be reinforced. Complaints and feedback will be then processed according to the internal accountability/ complaint policies and SOPs with the support of an accountability assistant and Meal Manager;INTERSOS has an internal PSEA&CP policy, with identified Focal Points and a separate system to address SEA complaints and a defined internal system of investigation. All INTERSOS staff is trained and has fully acknowledged the content of the policy.Beneficiary satisfaction surveys will be conducted on a regular basis by dedicated accountability staff;Case audits and client satisfaction form will be conducted regularly to monitor quality of services delivered and compliance with standards and regulations, according to INTERSOS Case Management SOPs.
