Project Summary

Through this project Humanitarian Aid and Development (HAD) will respond to prioritized, assessed, unmet health needs or gaps in primary health service provision, to reduce morbidity and mortality of vulnerable girls, boys, women and men in conflict affected areas and other vulnerable states. This project aims to improve access to quality preventative and curative primary health care services, including reproductive health. Due to the underlying vulnerabilities of the population, high maternal and under5 mortality rates, this project will focus on improving access of quality life­saving services to pregnant and lactating women and children under5 years. This project will maintain provision of essential primary health care services to IDPs and the host community where the health system has been dysfunctional as a result of the conflict.
The project will also maintain HAD’s health mobile response capacity to act as “First Responders” in an emergency location following a needs assessment, as well as to scale up this first response in the absence of other partners.Humanitarian Aid and Development organization (HAD) deploys health personnel and life­saving assistance at short notice, to assess or respond to needs triggered by acute health emergencies in the State. HAD's mobile teams propose to respond to the needs of communities affected by outbreak disease or other public health emergencies and to communities displaced or impacted by the ongoing conflict.
This project also aims to support the Ministry of Health (SMOH) and other relevant authorities in emergency response capacity, training local male and female health workers to respond to health emergencies and providing training in disease surveillance, outbreak response, case management, reporting systems, and awareness of various health gender needs based on current disease trends.
This project will. In line with the first health sector objective, (HAD) will improve access to essential primary health care where there is a critical service gap to a vulnerable population. In locations where (HAD) supports Primary Health Care, (HAD) will continue to provide patient consultation and provision of antenatal care while working towards implementation of (MISP) and establishing effective referral systems for emergency obstetric care in project locations. (HAD) will provide Primary Health Care services for IDPs in Dalami, habila, and abukarshola Localities and the community ­based health mobile response team will respond to new displacements or vulnerable groups to increase access to essential preventative and curative health care, targeting the most at risk groups. In locations, such as Bau locality, where (HAD) is supporting provision of essential basic and preventive health care for a longer period, the project is designed to impact community behavior change through a participatory cascade model using mothers groups.

Needs

In South Kordofan, the ongoing conflict over the past years and not likely to end in the foreseeable future, has resulted in massive displacement of more than 200,000 individuals, difficult living conditions in the displaced areas, lack of access to basic services (Health, Water, education, shelter and Sanitation). Lack of health services has resulted in high rates of illnesses such as diarrhea, malaria and malnutrition, and lack of access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities has further complicated the situation as the conflict continues and the numbers of IDPs increased. O the other hand 41% of the population residing in internally displaced person (IDP) camps and hard to reach areas. The security situation in some areas is leading to a lack of access to essential services for vulnerable populations.The WHO Health Resources Availability Mapping System shows gaps in access to, and utilization of, health services. On average, 69% of health facilities are functioning: hospitals (100%), primary health care centres (92%) and basic health units (52%). Rural hospital coverage is 50%. Addressing major problems undermining health status of the target population in these areas the goal the biggest problem is poorly maintained and functioning health facilities (Hospital, Primary Health Care Center (OHCC), Health units) Although there is health intervention through different agencies in South Kordofan the gaps are still huge, efforts from different partners including ministry of health, UN agencies, NGOs and target communities needs to be intensified and well coordinated, rather than fragmented, fragile and isolated health interventions.Conflicts in Abukarshola,resulted in a considerable numbers of IDPs relocate in Habilaand Dalami localities, and as the number of these IDPs increases the existing poor health services could never tolerate such kind of pressure and that could possibly result in widespread of infectious diseases, malaria and diarrheal diseases. The project team will carry out gender analysis to identify the distinct needs of women, men, girls and boys. The project will ensure that women, pregnant and lactating women, and girls have equal access to the health services. The project team will also consider construction of gender sensitive latrines as well as separate female waiting area in the health facilitie In addition, the project intends to consider gender at the activities and outputs. The project team will target a total number of (10 midwifes per locality) with midwifery training and breastfeeding awareness rising targeting 5000 mother. The training will equip the traditional midwifes with the necessary knowledge to help reduce the rates of morbidity and mortality among pregnant women. It should be noted that all capacity building activities will target 50 % females among the total participants.
