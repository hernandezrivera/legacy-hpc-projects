Project Summary

WFP is committed to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and is guided by a strategic,
programmatic and governance instrument to operationalize its plan and achieve its project objectives to:
• End Hunger by protecting access to food and by improving dietary diversity;
• Support the implementation of the SDGs by enhancing the capacities of national institutions to implement food security strategies and operations;
This project directly contributes to strategic objectives two and three of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2018-2020, by meeting the basic food needs of severely vulnerable Palestinians and by improving their dietary diversity.
WFP’s interventions focus on high food insecurity levels, including the malnutrition levels recorded by national surveys. By assisting the poor and severely food insecure Palestinian households in Gaza and Bedouin and herding communities in West Bank’s Area C, and by disseminating information on food and nutrition practices, WFP addresses the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians and tackles one of the root causes of some of the food insecurity levels recorded by the government institutions.
These interventions aim at improving the overall food security levels including nutrition and health status of the vulnerable Palestinians and therefore at increasing their coping abilities to sudden shocks in the market for access to food.
The project also contributes to the Food Security Sector’s three objectives as well as to the National Policy Agenda 2017-2022 (policies on escaping poverty and strengthening social protection as well as meeting the basic needs of the communities) to ensure that the vulnerable Palestinians households basic food needs are met, by assisting households in enhancing their food security levels and by strengthening national institutions in implementing food security strategies and social security safety net programs to best identify and target food insecure and vulnerable Palestinians.

1 - Needs

At present, it is estimated that 1.9 million Palestinian are food insecure, with an increase of around 300,000 people compared to last year. This estimate is based on the results of SEFSec 2018, PCBS population growth rate, and inferences to quantify the effect of COVID-19 pandemic impact over the food security status of the Palestinian population. The new estimation found that 38 percent of the Palestinian population is severely or moderately food insecure, respectively 17 percent in the West Bank and 74 percent in the Gaza Strip. In addition to the SEFSec, a specific survey carried out by WFP in cooperation with UNRWA in 2016/2017, reveals that more than 60 percent of the Bedouin and herding communities living in Area C of the West Bank are food insecure, marking a deterioration in their food security status compared to the findings in a 2012 survey at 50 percent. The survey found evidence of continued use of negative strategies to cope with the deterioration of their livelihoods and socio-economic conditions. Food insecurity is driven by limited economic access to food, arising from restrictions on movement, trade and investment, high unemployment rates, high poverty rate, erosion of agricultural infrastructure, land confiscation, limited access to land and water, an overstretched government safety net and with the COVID-19 outbreak, the situation is expected to worsen in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, as many sectors have been affected by the lockdown restrictions. Gender inequalities play a significant role in the food security and nutrition status of individuals; women are especially affected by food insecurity and poverty. The prevalence of food insecurity among households headed by women is 6.6 percentage points higher than that among households headed by men – 39.1 versus 32.5 percent. Women’s economic empowerment is likely to be impeded by lower wages and domestic work. The participation rate among male in Palestine is about four times compared to female rate in 2019 (69.9% for male and 18.1% for female). At 30.6 percent, the poverty rate for individuals in households headed by women is higher than the 29.2 percent for those in households headed by men. Even prior to the Covid-19 pandemic, around a quarter of Palestinians lived below the poverty line: 53 percent in Gaza, and 14 percent in the West Bank. According to World Bank preliminary estimates, it is expected that the share of poor households will increase to 30 percent in the West Bank and to 64 percent in Gaza. Also, the unemployment rate in the Palestinian territories is expected to have further increased during this year as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak in both West Bank and Gaza Strip. In addition to limited economic opportunities and access to food, malnutrition is also an issue. Based on the Palestinian micronutrient survey conducted by the Ministry of Health in 2013 and 2014, Palestinians are facing a double burden of malnutrition: a high level of micronutrient deficiencies alongside growing overweight and obesity. Approximately 50 percent of people assessed had very low levels of essential minerals and vitamins. Depleted levels of iron were found in 20 percent of lactating women in the West Bank and in 28 percent in the Gaza Strip. Prevalence of mild anaemia in children aged 6-59 months averaged 20-24 percent, with moderate anaemia at 8 percent; similar results were found for pregnant and lactating women. According to World Health Organization (WHO) standards, anaemia is a moderate public health problem. Overweight and obesity are moderate public health problems for pregnant and lactating women, with 31 percent of pregnant women being overweight and 26 percent obese, based on body weight and height measured before the 17th week of gestation. In lactating women, prevalence rates are 37 percent overweight and 23 percent obese. Wasting, stunting and underweight are not severe public health problems. On average, 7.4 percent of children under 5 suffer from moderate or severe stunting - 7.7 percent in the West Bank versus 7.1 percent in the Gaza Strip. The rate of stunting among boys is higher, at 8.1 percent, than among girls, at 6.6 percent. Among children under 5, 1.4 percent are moderately or severely underweight: 1.5 percent in the West Bank versus 1.3 percent in the Gaza Strip; and 1.6 percent among boys versus 1.1 percent among girls. Wasting prevalence - moderate and severe - is 1.2 percent for children under 5: 1.7 percent in the West Bank versus 0.7 percent in the Gaza Strip. The rate of overweight is 8.2 percent in the State of Palestine: 9.8 percent in the West Bank and 6.5 percent in Gaza.The 2019 assessment conducted by UNICEF, World Food Programme (WFP) and Save the Children (SC) outlined a deterioration in the nutritional status of the most vulnerable children under 5, pregnant and lactating women in Gaza. There were 18% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers in access-restricted areas (ARA) who were undernourished. Above 6% of infants were never breastfed and 86% of children under 5 living in ARA did not consume a minimum acceptable diet. 210,000 children in Gaza under 5 are particularly vulnerable due to micronutrient deficiencies, that is 7 out of 10 children.In West Bank, the findings from the OCHA H2 multi-sectoral household assessment revealed high food insecurity. Only 6% of households indicated that the family had enough food. This could be considered as anecdotal evidence that malnutrition, anaemia and food insecurity became evident given the economic degradation and restricted access to goods, services and employment. An additional assessment by UNICEF and Juzoor earlier this year in H2 communities confirmed the anaemia prevalence among children under 5 years as (34%) and pregnant and lactating women as (19%) were diagnosed with anaemia. All anemic cases were referred for further follow up and supplementation plan in Ministry of Health clinics of the H2 area.

2 - Activities

WFP will carry out the following activities: Provision of unconditional food assistance - through cash based transfers (CBT), multipurpose cash assistance, in-kind modalities and nutrition awareness sessions to poor and food insecure households. WFP will provide unconditional food assistance to targeted poor and severely food insecure households in Gaza Strip, in Area C and areas with poverty and high food insecurity levels in West Bank. The estimated number of beneficiary is based on the needs identified in SEFSec survey 2018, PCBS population growth and inferences to quantify the effect of COVID-19 (C-19) pandemic impact over the food security status of Palestinian population. The target beneficiaries include 426,000 non-refugee poor and severely food insecure people including 38,000 bedouin and herding communities in Area C of West Bank that will be assisted as part of the joint emergency food assistance programme in partnership with UNRWA. Throughout the implementation of the project, WFP aims at introducing immediate cash transfer. To ensure the integrity of transfers of intended beneficiaries, WFP will contract shops that are able to offer e-voucher beneficiary’s quality and diverse food at market prices, while providing an extra layer of control. It is worth mentioning that WFP encourage all beneficiaries to redeem local/proceed food only. WFP policy is to increase local purchase where is possible. WFP will also , to the greatest extent possible, ensure the inclusion of shops owned by women, to promote women’s economic empowerment. Disability is also a key vulnerability factor taken into consideration in targeting poor and food insecure households and included in the data collection tool that is used in targeting. Disability of food insecure people is an eligibility factor to receive assistance under the National Social Safety Net programme. Operationally WFP considers disability a factor in the selection of distribution points and shops to ensure accessibility. Complementing the provision of unconditional food assistance, tailored nutrition sensitive awareness activities will also be implemented to engage diverse groups of men, women, girls and boys. In response to recommendation stemming from the strategic review, WFP will utilize available nutrition data to design and implement interventions per the nutritional -and not only caloric- needs of the beneficiary population. This will include developing and delivering basic awareness raising on healthy eating habits and nutrition. Throughout the activity, WFP will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and emphasize protection and accountability towards affected populations. All women and men beneficiaries will continue to have access to beneficiary feedback mechanisms through secure boxes placed in retail shops for CBTs and through WFP hotline. According to the Ministry of Social Development (MSD), C-19 has increased the vulnerability of the Palestinian people. The current number of detected C-19 cases has reached to 57,441 detected cases as of 16 October 2020, however, the Palestinian health system is severely impaired by longstanding challenges and critical shortages to cope with an expected increase in C-19 cases. As a preventive and protective measure for the most vulnerable, in accordance with the response plan of the MSD. Based on the Study of the Social Impacts of C-19 Pandemic in Palestine and its Implications for Governmental and Non-Governmental Policies and Interventions that has done by MSD, the results indicate that fear of food insecurity is growing steadily, with 58% of Palestinians considering “instability in the ability to feed the family” as one of the main sources of “fear” following the pandemic.PCBS was conducted a study to measure the impact of C-19 pandemic on Palestinian households’ socio-economic conditions, the main results were:-14% of the households’ main income earner in the West Bank stopped working during the lockdown period.-52% of main income earners did not receive any wages/salaries at all during the lockdown period. -42% of Palestinian household's income declined to half and more during the lockdown period compared to February 2020 (46% in the West Bank and 38% in Gaza Strip).-61% of the households are worried about not having enough food, while 57% of households have a less diversified diet and 47% cannot eat healthy food. -The monthly expenditures on food supplies declined for 41% of the households during the lockdown compared with February 2020 (42% in the West Bank and 40% in Gaza Strip). WFP has been designing and implementing its food assistance activities in such a way that they do not expose affected populations to protection risks, ensuring that their safety, dignity or integrity is preserved, and vulnerabilities not exacerbated. Safety and protection-related risks and challenges were identified through direct interactions with beneficiaries, on-site observations, monitoring, feedback mechanisms and cooperating partners’ assessments. Efforts to reducing protection risks were critical to ensure that most vulnerable food-insecure populations could access WFP’s assistance freely, safely, equitably and without discrimination. To that effect, WFP organizes its in-kind food distributions in secure and easily-accessible locations to all segments of populations. Multiple food collection facilities were established to minimize travel time; crowd control mechanisms put in place and supervised with a view to minimize waiting times and avoid disputes; and distribution workers trained on identifying, showing respect and giving priority to most vulnerable groups: pregnant & breastfeeding women, elderly and disabled people. CBT shops were carefully selected to ensure that assisted people will not be required to travel large distances, pay substantial transportation costs to redeem their vouchers and is easily accessible to elderly and disabled beneficiaries.

WFP works closely with Food Security Sector stakeholders to continually improve the analysis and identification of the food insecure population in Palestine and to target food assistance support accordingly. The targeting of WFP’s food assistance is based on the updated Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) developed by the Ministry of Social Development and World Bank. The PMTF takes into account changing socioeconomic factors which influence household expenditures and the determination of households that cannot meet their basic needs. Food assistance will be provided in a manner that reduces the exposure to risk for beneficiaries (especially that of women) and at the same time reduces the burden associated with carrying food items, particularly for female headed households. WFP uses a robust monitoring system to ensure that all beneficiaries are verified and monitored after receiving assistance to ensure that the beneficiaries meet the criteria of falling below the PA’s threshold deep poverty line. The deep poverty line is calculated by taking into consideration household consumption levels of three categories of goods and services: food, clothing and housing. Prior to any activity implementation, WFP will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities
