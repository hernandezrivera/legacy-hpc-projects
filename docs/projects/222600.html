Project Summary

Mozambique has approximately 1.5million people displaced in conflict-affected areas, 72% of these are staying with host communities in regions classified to have IPC3. Some IDPs returning are not able to go to their places of origin due to ongoing displacements within the district. Hence the community is composed of IDPs, returnees and host community and are prone to various protection risks, lack of food, water, sanitation, shelter and health services. About 3.1 million people in Mozambique have been estimated to be in IPC 3 and 220,000 in IPC 4 with 2.5million at risk of natural disasters. Meluco-a district in Cabo Delgado being affected by extremism & violence by both direct and indirect attacks. Situation has been stable in 2023, but the uncertainty has left displaced families unwilling to return to their areas of origin within the district. The district has no operational humanitarian partners despite having several needs that include food, WASH, protection, shelter, education and health. ADRA and SEPPA will support 30,000 most vulnerable, IDPs, Host communities and Returnees affected by both natural disaster and forced displacement by armed groups to provide an integrated humanitarian assistance in Meluco in a dignified and protective manner ensuring vulnerable people live in a cohesive and peaceful environment. This will include food aid with short-term livelihoods support to protect and start rebuilding livelihood assets through distribution of seeds and toolkits to ensure generating food security. Nutrition trainings to contribute to improving malnutrition risks with good water supply and proper management of waste and provision of hygiene kits, sensitization on good hygiene practices will minimise overall health risk and enhance coping capacity. Project will also support communities to be prepared to use early warning systems through trainings to reduce loss of life.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Project will follow " Do not harm" approach throughout the intervention to ensure to promote social cohesion between the IDPs and the host communities. To reduce any possible tension among the communities, project will engage communities, community leaders for the beneficiary’s selection. The selection criteria will be well communicated to the communities to ensure they understand it clearly and accepts the procedures to be followed. The complaints and feedback mechanism will be established in the targeted communities to ensure any issues/complaints and feedbacks are well raised, documented and addressed by the project within specific timeline. Targeted communities will be oriented on the complaints and feedback mechanism to ensure maximum utilization of the facilities by the affected population. Project will ensure to follow local cultural sensitivity that is well oriented to the project staffs followed by training on safe guarding policies and procedures. Project assistance will ensure to minimise the community risk and increase coping capacity and resilience. Response strategy will be well coordinated with the communities, community leader, other stakeholders, clusters and local authorities to ensure harmonize approach that would further help in reducing any possibility of conflict and tension.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

There is a need for attention to women's economic resilience and addressing the food insecurity, supporting emergency livelihoods restoration to bridge income generating activities, especially for vulnerable women (young mothers, single/widow women), that are finding themselves in high vulnerability situations. While forming or conducting community level meetings, special focus and attention needed to be taken care by the project staff to ensure women participations are adequately ensured, opportunities provided for them to raise their issues and concerns in the best possible way they feel comfortable and safe. Gender balance in recruiting project staff will play a crucial role in addressing some of these challenges especially while having Hygiene promotion and protection related activities where more engagement of women participation is needed. Project staffs will be provided orientation and training on integration of gender and inclusion. This will also be considered while establishing complaints and feedback mechanism in the project to ensure women participants are well oriented and provided information that enables them to raise their issues and specific redressal mechanism are adopted.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

This group lack access to healthcare and other difficulties in meeting their basic needs in a normal setting and in the setting of conflict they lack access to more services including local support for they may no longer be staying with close relatives that catered for them in the case of displacement. Many are unable to work due to injury or the need to care for others. They require access to humanitarian services and information that are tailored to their specific needs. Host families may face tensions with displaced communities linked to access to services. They also have reduced living space and face socio-economic hardships especially when resources are already scarce. All activities will be inclusive of people with disabilities for instance waterpoints that would be rehabilitated will be inclusive. Consultations will be done individuals people living with disabilities and old-aged people in setting up the site for distribution, besides making sure that the sites are accessible to all people including the old-aged and disabled people.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

ADRA plans not to degrade the environment through deforestation, where there will be need for setting up the distribution site. ADRA also planned to preserve the land and the Floresta by not over populate areas that do not have the capacity of accommodating many people for any distribution or open new spaces for distribution. ADRA will avoid this by using areas that already exist in the communities where many people could be accommodated as regular community meeting areas in each community.
