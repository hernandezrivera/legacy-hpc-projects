Project Summary

Sesame Workshop is a nonprofit organization on a mission that keeps us focused, passionate, and moving forward to help kids grow smarter, stronger, and kinder, and do everything possible to meet their needs in more than 150 countries. In the last 50 years, across 150 countries and generations of children.
With generous funding from the LEGO Foundation, Play to Learn is a five-year, large-scale ECD program led by SW in coalition with BRAC, the IRC, and research partner New York University Global Ties for Children. Play to Learn is an innovative humanitarian program delivering play-based early childhood development (ECD) services to children and caregivers affected by the Rohingya crisis. The program combines educational media with direct services to meet the holistic needs of children ages 3-18, including health, nutrition, protection, playful learning, and responsive caregiving by 19–59-year-old project personnel and caregivers.
The initiative is to be implemented through three intersectional components: direct services, educational content research, and advocacy. Under the direct service component, Play to Learn reaches children and caregivers with play-based early learning opportunities in child-friendly spaces, community-based learning facilities, homes, schools, and health centers in the camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. Another 8 national and International NGOs are using the contents of Play to Learn and have reached a large number of additional children and caregivers, and this will continue. The new project for 2024 is designed to target 66,175 beneficiaries assuming 33,103 are Male and 33,072 are female covering a 50% ratio to maintain gender equity and equality.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

To address the barriers limiting children of 3-6 years' access to quality ECD and education services in Rohingya camps as well as host community, SW continues and expand the operation of ECD interventions, and brings expertise and leverage programmatic experience and presence. Thus, Sesame Workshop targeted 3-6 years of children and provide play-based early childhood development opportunities, including well designed ECD courses, play-based tools, and instructional content ants and 5-18 years children to provide teaching learning materials (story books) to create teaching learning, playfull and joyful environment as well as language development. SW will leverage its global expertise in developing and using multimedia platforms and Edu-Ttech in delivering a play based ECD services and contents in Burmese and English language and Bangla where required. Rohingya Muppets first ever developed by SW and other characters will be used in the project with its series of AVs in Burmese language and will bring matches for the project. The PtL project will include the integration of technology to reach learners during prolonged school closures. The implementing partner BRAC and IRC are closely working with the Sector and Technology Task Team to develop a guideline for remote education delivery using technology and other alternative ways. The Play to Learn project has been using telecommunication and IVR to reach children and caregivers to deliver lessons since last year. SW engaged parents and caregivers of learners through sessions to equip them with the knowledge and skills to understand the importance of ECD education and to better support their children with their learning and wellbeing. SW and its Play to Learn partners will explore ways to better understand how the different community stakeholders perceive education, supporting more tailored and culturally appropriate education services and awareness raising, such as, gender-disaggregated committees or alternative approaches if meaningful female participation is lacking. The consortium together with its sub-partners will create a platform for children to engage with local authorities (camp-in-charges) and humanitarian organizations on access to their rights. Due to the huge influx of refugees in Cox’s Bazar, the quality of education provision for learners in the host communities has been adversely affected. In consultation with the Upazila Education Officer of Ukhia and Teknaf. Play to Learn project of SW and its partners working with lower performing nationalized primary schools. This project will promote and support social cohesion between host and refugee communities and importantly look to build the capacity of local partners. Implementation together with local organization will also sustain the viability of future operations and strengthen local capacities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SW as an INGO working in different conflict and crisis content around the world is committed to AAP principles, thus AAP is a central consideration in every sphere of our actions in humanitarian context wherever we work. When we select partners, we check their AAP policy and practices. When we design program and contents, the affected population is part of our program design and decision making, implementation, monitoring and assessment. We also ensure whatever data we collect and findings and analysis we do, we consult and validate those with them first. We systematically feedback to the population. We also ensure representation of all groups including age, sex, social power, and ability and other diverse groups. Through the process we aim to ensure the dignity of the affected population as well as the intervention is relevant and effective for them.
