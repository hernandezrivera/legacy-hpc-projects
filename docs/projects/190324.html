Project Summary

Currently there is a serious humanitarian crisis in Afar region. Peoples are forced to flee their homes and are sheltering in deteriorated and unpredictable conditions. The main cause for the emergency displacement is the conflict that begun in Tigray on 4 November 2020 and recently expanded into Afar and Amhara regional states. The violent conflict reached Afar on 16th July 2021 causing crisis for Afar pastoralist households who fled their homes and are living without basic WaSH, food and medical access, as well as emergency shelter and NFI items.
The IDPs have no basic household equipment such as water containers, utensils, bed sheets, mats and items for menstrual hygiene. Despite it was not enough and did not address the need, there are interventions with ESNFIs and WaSH kits to the IDPs in some parts (Yallo, Gulina, Awra, Chifra, Abala, Erebti and Semera) with the support from UN agencies and INGOs like IOM, UNHCR, IRC, WHH and SCI. As the conflict expanded to the districts of Hadelela, Semurobi, Telalak and Dawe in Zone 5, thousands of people had to move to relatively safe areas within and outside of the districts in need of protection. According to the Afar Region humanitarian situation report (January 26/2022), on January 23/2022 TPLF open fighting by heavy artillery on ten fronts and attacked including IDP sites and spillover into Barahle and Erebti woreda that resulted in a second-time displacement of IDPs from five IDP sites into Afdera and Barahle districts. The IDPs are currently in a dire situation in Afdera, Koneba, Megale, Erebti, Dalol and Barahle woredas and the IDPs specially women, children and PLW are in open space without shelter, Water, and food .
Due to the conflict erupted in Tigray region humanitarian crisis is on the rise. The conflict has now extended to Amhara region and new humanitarian crisis is affecting thousands of people living both in rural and towns in Amhara region mainly in Debrebrhan town.
Since the last month, due to the heavy fighting that’s has been going on in North and extended to South Wollo zones, specifically Tewheledere, Dessie Kombolcha, Batti, harbu and kemisse towns the conditions in these areas is highly dangerous and people have fled their homes in need of protection. Most of the people from these towns and the whole zone of South Wollo have walked to the relatively safe district/town called Debrebrhan town, they have now returned to south wollo but currently with high needs for immediate humanitarian assistance. The majority of the Internally Displaced People (IDPs) are now dispersed and living homeless in various kebeles and only few of them ( 8,955 -of which 70% are women and children. The whole IDP population is suffering from lack of WaSH and Shelter NFIs, and lack of money to buy/purchase food for their daily consumption. These resulted in starvation of pregnant women, elderly and children IDPs. Similarly, there is no protection services for IDPs mainly children, women, girls, the elderly and persons with disabilities and their life is at risk. In addition, because people have left their homes almost barehanded, they faced with significant lack of shelter, with people suffering from harsh cold weather as it is very cold and windy, lack of privacy, increased protection and security concerns. The IDPs are not having access to ESNFIs and their priority ESNFIs are blankets, bed sheets and bed mats and NFI dignity kits for women and Girls like Laundry and body soap, Reusable sanitary pad, Female underwear and Head Scarf.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

WHH and its partner will establish a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) together with the affected communities. The CRM includes various channels depending on the preferences of the affected communities, at least one of which is anonymous. Possibilities are for example 24/7 hotline, community help desks and suggestion boxes.As a pre-requisite for a functioning CRM WHH and its partners ensure that the affected communities will receive timely and clear information about the overall project and its activities, targeting and process as well as the behavior that they can expect from the project staff (e.g. Code of conduct). Moreover, WHH and its partners repeatedly and clearly explains the purpose of the CRM and how to access or use the system to the affected community, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike. The various channels of the CRM remain available throughout the project. When a complaint is received, the CRM focal person processes the information and refers it according to set standards. Especial care for confidentiality and professional handling of complaints is given in case of sensitive cases, here WHH has a professional system in place at head office level. Depending on the matter of the complaint response is given in a set timeframe and feedback is given to the complainant or the community in total. In general, complaints or feedback are welcome by the WHH and its partners and seen as an opportunity to learn and improve the project and communication with the community, this culture is supported by the leadership and trough capacity development for the project team
