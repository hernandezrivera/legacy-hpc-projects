Project Summary


This project aims to promote optimal nutrition practices, prevent and treat acute malnutrition in the most vulnerable population groups like children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers. These aims will be fulfilled through implementation of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) interventions in Rumbek Centre, Wulu, Cueibet counties of Lakes state. The project implementation period will be from January 1, 2024 - December 31, 2024. PI will ensure that most marginalized groups like those with disability, minority ethnic groups and other groups due to economic, political, social and cultural factors are reached and participated throughout the project cycle. Furthermore, preventive activities like MIYCN counselling, health, nutrition and WASH education /promotion, setting up demonstration gardens, complementary food cooking demonstration and distribution of WASH and other preventive NFIs (like soap, aqua tab, mosquito net etc.) will be done at nutrition sites and at community level. As part of treatment protocol, all children with MAM and SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol / or referred to nearby health facilities. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerrycans, Mosquito nets, and soaps) shall be provided to mothers /caregivers of children who are enrolled for treatment in OTP or TSFP or SC sites as these children are at higher risk of being infected and to promote proper personal hygiene and sanitation practice. All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled to TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery and those deteriorated while in OTP will be referred to SC. Besides, if a child in TSFP is nutritionally deteriorated, he/she will be enrolled/referred to OTP/SC program. Those who recovered shall be given protection ration for two weeks and referred to other (FSL, BSFP) services after providing counselling. When there is deworming and Vitamin A supplementation campaign, PI will support the project counties to ensure children under five years old in the counties get the services in collaboration with SMOH/CHD, UNICEF, WFP and the health partner in the county. Furthermore, PI will participate in FSNMS, SMART and other joint assessments that may happen in the counties of operation. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programme together in Cueibet (14) county and shall implement OTP in Rumbek Centre (21 nutrition sites) and Wulu (14) counties. Besides, Plan International will provide service in 2 stabilization centres; one in Rumbek Centre and one Cueibet county. In addition to the aforementioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP in Cueibet county of Lake State. In total, this project will benefit 146,386 populations (boys, girls, men, women, people with disability) in different services. Of the total beneficiaries, 126,19 are children under five and the rest 20,191 are adults.
To ensure the efficient project implementation, enhance community and local government support and ensure sustainability of the project, Plan will prioritise in local capacity building activities throughout the project cycle. The community volunteers (MSGs, CNVs and community representatives) and local government staffs in CHD and health facilities will be capacitated in different ways including training, involving them in support supervisions, feedback and complaint meetings and review meetings so as to ensure effectiveness, sustainability and ownership of this project. Besides, majority of project staff and all volunteers will be recruited locally. Existing nutrition sites were selected through participation of CHD and the community, and future expansion, be it static or outreach sites, will be established in the same way. Furthermore, the CHD and communities will be notified that it is a temporary measure that PI is doing the nutrition program and they should prepare to take over the program step by step.

1. Needs

Driven by cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflict, subnational violence, food insecurity, climate crisis, and public health challenges, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan is worsening each year. In 2023, three quarters (76%) of the population of South Sudan, which is an estimated 9.4 million people — including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children, and 337,000 refugees, were projected to need humanitarian assistance and protection services, making South Sudan one of the world’s worst food security crises (South Sudan HRP 2023). The situation is further aggravated by the current crisis in Sudan. Returnees and refugees continue to arrive in areas of origin or relocation sites in South Sudan where many of whom have experienced secondary displacement, inter-communal conflict, hazards (particularly a risk of flood impact given the rainy season), exposure to diseases, inability to meet basic family needs due to high prices of essential goods, food insecurity, and a loss of livelihoods (https://dtm.iom.int/node/25851). The recent projection by FEWS NET, South Sudan - Food Security Outlook Update, September depicted that Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes persist across South Sudan despite the start of the green harvest and some stabilization in cereal prices in unimodal areas, albeit at above-average levels. This persistence is driven by ongoing sporadic conflict, underlying poor macro-economic conditions, limited income-generating opportunities, slow livelihood recovery prospects, and localized poor harvests. In the northern counties bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. In this projection, all counties of Lakes state are in crisis stage (IPC Phase 3) until the end of December 2024. According to SMART nutrition and mortality survey by IRC, April 2023 in Yirol West County, the prevalence of GAM among the sampled children was estimated at 15.1% (11.7-19.4% C.I.) and prevalence of SAM was 4.1% (2.5-6.55% C.I.) i.e., ‘’Very High”2 based on UNICEF/WHO classification thresholds, based on weight-for-height/WFH and the presence of bilateral oedema. Besides, the survey shows that more than three in ten (31.4%) children were sick in the two weeks prior to the survey date, with leading symptoms of fever (62%), cough (31%) and diarrhoea (26%), which shows the children facing high burden of common childhood morbidity and mortality diseases. The survey result shows vitamin A supplementations rate of 29.8%, which is very far from the acceptable sphere standards. Furthermore, IYCF practice was found to be sub optimal with only 16.2% (n = 6 from 37) of infants started solid, semi-solid or soft food in timely manner. Moreover, only 15.9% and 15.3% of children had acceptable MDD and MMF, respectively. Bottle feeding was practiced by 15% of children before 24hrs of the survey. The food security situation was also found to be worse that only 12% (n=45) of respondents had acceptable or borderline food consumption, while the rest counterpart, 88% (n=388) of the households were poor consumption score in the past seven days prior to the survey. The situation in other counties of Lakes could not be far from the above facts. Given the high rate of acute food insecurity coupled with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macroeconomic crisis, conflict, insecurity, outbreak of diseases and low agricultural production, it will be mandatory to continue providing preventive and curative nutrition services in the three counties. Furthermore, providing these services will significantly contribute in the reduction of morbidity and mortality of children, PLWs and other high-risk population groups and improve the health, development and productivity of the general population.

2. Project activities

The key activities in this project are: - Nutrition, health and WASH education in side nutrition sites and at outreach sites- Providing Maternal, Infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to mothers/caregivers- Screening for acute malnutrition- Admission and treatment of children 6-59 with sever and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM) and Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with acute malnutrition • - Regular collection (from WFP and UNICEF) and delivery of nutrition supplies to nutrition sites- Doing Exhaustive MUAC assessment - Participating in FSNMS and other assessments like IRNA - Establish/promotion of chicken rearing, establishment of kitchen gardens and promotion of seed multiplication for vegetable gardens and support fish preservation in the community in coordination with FSL cluster and government counterparts - Distribution of preventive items like soap, aqua tab, mosquito net- Training of staffs, volunteers and government staffs on Community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM), MIYCN, child protection, Gender, GBV, WASH- Supporting Vitamin supplementation and deworming campaigns- Train the mother support groups on Maternal infant and young child nutrition, nutrition screening, identification of malnourished children and PLW and referral •- Start-up and close up workshop - Printing and distribution of visibility materials- Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes, suggestion books and regular meetings with beneficiaries in the SC and OTP/TSFP sites, - Training of CNWs and CNVs and other nutrition cadres on identification of children under five with Acute watery diarrhoea (AWD), treat using ORS and Zinc as a conjugate regiment- Construction/rehabilitation of latrines, water points, nutrition facilities (screening areas, waiting area, storage facilities) hand washing facilities and garbage pits- Parenting under pressure to parents attending nutrition sites (a parenting program specifically designed for families who are facing multiple hardships including mental health problems, family conflict and severe financial stress etc. The program is adopted for use in South Sudan)- Distribution of WASH NFI to beneficiaries with malnourished children- Doing GBV safety audit in nutrition sites and mainstream child protection and GBV measures in nutrition centers- Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices including celebrating Breastfeeding week campaign and others- Establish and ensure presence of referral linkage with other services/clusters like protection, FSL, Health and others.- Collaborate with health cluster to ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhoea at the nutrition service delivery.Integrate child stimulation and ECD in nutrition sites- Train CNVs on agronomic practices including fish preservation techniques
