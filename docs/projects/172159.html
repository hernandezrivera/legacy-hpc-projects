Project Summary

Shelter and NFIs needs remain high across several crisis affected districts in Manicaland, and Masvingo, and Matabeleland North provinces after subsequent damages caused by flooding and droughts, with a total number of 106,533 individuals still in need of humanitarian assistance.
The limited recovery capacity of the IDP population exacerbated by the country instability and economic constrains sums up the population into a state of emergency, reaching severity levels of high concern. There is a critical need to address the shelter gaps in order to mitigate the risk of secondary displacement that might be caused by new floods and storms to people still living in prone areas and to restore the dignity of the most vulnerable while durable solutions are found.
COVID-19 pandemic in Zimbabwe, further exacerbated the needs of the most vulnerable segments of the population increasing poverty and inequalities. The socio-economic implications of the COVID-19 has shattered food systems and damaged livelihoods strategies and social coping mechanisms, leading to further displacement or outward and inward migration.
The 2020/21 rainfall season is predicted to be a La Nina likely characterized by heavy rainfall, flooding, storms and landslides. Regions that are at flood risk in Zimbabwe may experience damage to their shelter and more displacements, including secondary displacements for those who are yet to recover from previous disaster displacements. Shelter response is therefore lifesaving since those households which only received emergency shelter (tarpaulins) during multiple crisis have deteriorated and are now inhabitable, while others are staying in makeshift structures, not suitable for human habitation. The remaining IDPs in the 4 camps in Chimanimani are in a critical situation, considering the worn-out situation of the tents in camps, and the Government has not yet operationalized the relocation plan hence worsening their already vulnerable condition.

The response seeks to provide life-saving emergency shelter and NFIs kits to 10,000 individuals (2,000 HHs) displaced by disasters in Chimanimani, Chipinge, Buhera and Masvingo districts which are perennially at risk of displacement due to natural disasters. 29,290 individuals (5,850 HHs) affected by Cyclone Idai will receive transitional shelter support in Chimanimani, Chipinge, Buhera, while a further 25,055 individuals (5,011 HHs) in protracted displacement situations in the districts of Makoni, Mutasa, Bikita, Chiredzi, Gutu, Masvingo, Zaka and Binga will be provided with shelter repair kits.
The intervention will provide construction tool kits and capacity building of community builders to construct emergency and transitional shelters and repair damaged shelters.
The response aims to strengthen coordination among partners who provide shelter assistance for the interventions to be effective, share best practices and avoid duplication.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The Displacement Tracking Monitoring (DTM) which IOM regularly conducts in crisis affected communities gathers information on intentions of affected communities and what the affected communities consider priority needs for them to recover from the effects of the disaster and shelter is always among the top needs. Beneficiaries will be provided with adequate project information in terms of materials they will receive, their quantities, information on PSEA, among others. This will empower beneficiaries to provide effective feedback. A complains desk will be established during project activities such as distribution of materials to receive any complains and issues related to the project and its activities. Periodic community meetings with beneficiaries and traditional leadership will be a platform to get feedback concerning project implementation. A hotline will established as a mechanism for accountability of beneficiaries.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The transitional shelter units will be constructed using timber which will be provided by the project. Hence there is not going to be cutting of trees and moulding of bricks. Camps and camp like settings will be established after thorough consideration of environmental issues and guidance from environmental agency.
