Project Summary

According to HNO 2022, about 3.6 million people, 97 per cent of whom are children, are estimated to need education services. Following COVID 19, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. In addition, about 139,000 refugee children also need education support. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. In May 2021, after 14 months of closure due to COVID-19, schools in South Sudan re-opened. The current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million. Additionally, gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7 per cent between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021 (2,523,204). Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. In Jonglei, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Lakes, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria, Warrap, Unity and Western Equatoria, active conflict and recurring communal violence throughout 2021 led to school closures. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities, as well ass destruction or occupation was in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021 also mentioned as barriers to access education facilities. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as impediment for learner's access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile have been the worst impacted by floods.
In August 2022 heavy fighting between armed factions in Tonga resulted in displacement of the population. An estimated 18,000- 27,000 people reported displaced from Tonga, Panykang County and its surroundings (August, OCHA flash report). According to the latest Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) cluster update, about 18,345 Internally displaced Persons (IDPs) from Panyikang and surrounding areas have arrived to Malakal Protection of Civilian (PoC) and Malakal Town as of September 19th 2022. Prior consultation on education needs and gaps in Malakal Town and PoC highlighted that the schools and learning spaces in both locations have been over stretched and facing funding constraints to expand education access to the needy children. With an increasing arrival of IDPs to Malakal Town and PoC, the current learning spaces/schools do not have the capacity to absorb additional children. As a result of compounded impact of violence, conflict and flooding in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panykang, Pigi Canal and Bor South, the functionality of several schools have been affected including displacement of children and damages on school infrastructure. This project envisages to provide integrated education and child protection in emergency services to 8,000 children affected by conflict and flood induced displacement in Malakal, Fashoda, Panyikang, Melut, Bor South and Pigi Canal Counties. The project will strengthen the capacity of teachers on teaching methodology and focuses on enhancing the capacity of Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) in education in emergency and school management in line with government education sector priorities and humanitarian response plan (HRP).

2 - Project activities

The proposed project envisages to ensure access to emergency education for children. In addition to creating access to education, the project also aims to promote life saving protection and psychosocial support and healing messaging to protect children from physical dangers, abuse, exploitation, recruitment into armed groups and psychological distress. Accordingly, War Child Holland will implement the below activities to achieve learning outcome for the conflict and flood affected children in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panyikang, Bor South and Pigi Canal Counties.- 1) 8,000 learners (2206 boys, 1451 girls) enrolled at Early Childhood Development (ECD), Primary and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) 2) 8,169 kits of teaching (169 teacher’s kit) and learning materials (8,000 student kits) of the different types distributed to 8,000 learners and 169 teachers.3) 16 Temporary Learning Space (TLS) with segregated WASH facilities in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panyikang, Bor South and Pigi/Canal re-established/supported to create education access for 8,000 children.4) 169 Volunteer Teachers (104 Male, 65 Female) trained on Education in Emergency(EIE), basic pedagogy and learner - centered methodologies inclusive of Psychosocial Support (PSS).5) 169 (104 Male, 65 Female) Volunteer Teachers provided with teaching incentives over 12-months period at the cluster rate.6) 88 (48 male and 40 Female) Members of Parent Teachers Association Trained on Education in Emergency and school Management.7) 39 Community mobilizations/back to school sessions conducted involving Parent Teacher Association(PTA) and School Management Committee(SMC) and community representatives to enroll boys and girls to school and strengthen the capacity to manage education in emergencies.8) 8 schools received quarterly school supervision visit and technical support

3 - Project indicators

War Child Holland will employ its education program monitoring framework to measure the progress of key activities against the set target. Key indicators to measure progress included:1) # Boys, girls and adolescents provided with access to quality education services.2) # Number of teachers’ trained on Education in Emergency(EIE) basic pedagogy and learner - centered methodologies3) # of schools reporting teacher's and learner's kits availability 4) # of PTAs and CMCs demonstrating commitment to own and sustain learning activities 5) % of learners successfully completing education cycle as required6) % of learners who report that school is a safe environment 7) # of back to learning campaign conducted in target project sites

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WCH will strengthen the existing feedback and complaint mechanism to ensure the participation of vulnerable groups including individuals with disability, illiterate community members, women and girls. Assessment will be conducted to identify the most appropriate feedback channels to ensure access to vulnerable groups. Based on the assessment findings, each group will be given an opportunity to provide feedback and complaints about the project using their preferred option. WCH will conduct weekly and monthly follow up to ensure that the preferred feedback options are functional and reliable.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Targeted and identified communities members will be engaged and informed on the criteria to be used for selecting children and caregivers in highest need of Support/protection concerns. Identifying and responding to at-risk children available services that they can expect and also on how they can launch complaints or provide feedback to WCH and its partners in a confidential manner.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All teachers, staff, PTA, volunteers will be trained on PSEA, code of conduct, as well as be oriented and sign to WCH Child Safeguarding Policy which includes information on how to confidentially report any suspected or identified cases of PSEA and have update and active referral pathways

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Analysis of the beneficiary feedback results will be undertaken on a regular bases and included in topics discussed during project reflection meetings, and where feasible be used to improve project delivery outcomes including project ownership by the communities.
