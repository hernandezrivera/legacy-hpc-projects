Project Summary

Providing sector approved permanent transitional shelters for IDPs and Returnees using cost effective and environmentally friendly materials/techniques. Output 1: Shelter surveys and needs assessments will be conducted to provide better analysis of the needs of individuals and households Output 2: Trainings will be conducted targeting beneficiaries as well as coordination with local authorities, community leaders and communities to formulate proper actions Output 3: Transitional Shelters will be provided to the most vulnerable communities.
IOM will provide 2000 transitional shelters (1 per household [5 persons] benefiting approximately 10,000 individuals) in locations specified in the project across two sectors: 70% of the shelters will go to the RRR sector and the remaining 30% will go to the emergency shelter and NFI sector. The IOM DTM team has registered returnees in Central, North, South and West Darfur and using this data IOM will select sites with high concentrations of returnees for the project locations. In Central Darfur IOM has registered 44,072 returnee households in four localities, in North Darfur 5,638 in four localities, in West Darfur 1,861 in four localities, and South Darfur 7,521 in three localities, totaling 59092 returnee households registered in 15 localities in Darfur. It is estimated 60% of these are in need of shelter. IOM will conduct site assessments to target the most vulnerable households in each region. IOM will follow this by conducting shelter surveys. These surveys will be collect information from the community and be used to inform shelter design to ensure that the shelters will be acceptable and appropriate for each locality.
IOM will expand the provision of shelter assistance and skills building training packages including training on local construction techniques and provide durable shelters for Vulnerable IDPs and Returnee's. The skills training for construction of shelters will focus on durable construction techniques and environmental awareness to reduce disaster risks. The provision of environmentally friendly locally produced shelters will both provide beneficiaries with protection from the elements while enhancing the skills of the community and enabling the beneficiaries trained in construction techniques to assist others in constructing shelters and possibly generate a household income.
IOM will coordinate with the emergency shelter and NFI sector and with both national and international NGOs working on the ground by having meetings and sharing information about activities occurring in each location to avoid duplication.

Needs

In April 2015, a reconciliation agreement was reached in Central Darfur State between the Salamat and Massirya tribes who were previously engaged in inter-tribal conflict (2013–2014). This conflict resulted in mass forced displacement, with some people displaced within Central Darfur State while others decided to seek refuge by crossing the border into Chad. Since the reconciliation agreement was signed in 2015, the general security situation in the state has improved creating the stable environment necessary to allow for the gradual return of displaced people to their place of origin and surrounding areas (satellite villages). These return movements included IDPs’ (returning from areas within Central Darfur state, such as Sharq Al Matar camp / site) and refugee returnees (returning from different rural areas and refugee camps in Chad). An estimated 80,387 households were reported to have returned to Um Dhukun. In December 2016 IOM deployed a Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) team who registered an estimated 80,405 returnees, consisting of 13,935 Women, 13,585 Men, 25,957 girls and 26,910 boys. The DTM team also registered returnees in Moradaf, Garaiya, and Biltabi areas within Umm Dukhun locality. In recent months the number of returnees arriving increased is expected to continue to rise. In response, the RRR sector undertook an inter-agency assessment mission (January 15 to 28, 2017) to assess the needs and service gaps across 11 out of 19 villages in the Um Dhukun locality. Inadequate shelter is cited as one of the prevalent findings under Output 2 of the mission findings. The mission reports that returnees were provided with improved emergency shelters and ES/NFIs but have yet to receive more permanent shelter solutions to allow for sustainable reintegration to take place. In March 2017, a total of 71,984 individuals were registered in Golo, of which 54,362 individuals are returnees and 17,586 are IDPs. 63 % of the registered caseload are under 18 years old, of which 30 % are under 5 years. All the registered returnees were IDP returnees. In April 2017, a total of 21,114 IDPs were registered in Nertiti. In response to the wave of displacement caused by the tribal conflict in May 2017 between Salamat and Misserya tribes in Um Dukhun locality, Central Darfur State; the DTM team was deployed to register a reported figure of 830 households. Out of the initial reported caseload, 731 households (3,032 individuals) were identified and registered. The majority (99%) were displaced in May while the rest were displaced in June 2017. Some IDPs who initially registered left Um Dukhun town and went to different locations in search of farming and grazing land for their animals. 55% of the registered IDPs live in a new gathering site, north of Um Dukhun town (El Njoom area), and 45% are either hosted by relatives, live in rented houses or are given temporary accommodation by the host communities.
