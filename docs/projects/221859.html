Project Summary

Islamic Relief Worldwide- South Sudan is a Humanitarian International Organization that has been responding the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan since 2003 providing assistance to crisis affected population through key thematic areas of WASH, Food Security and Livelihoods, Health, Nutrition, Gender Protection and Inclusion. Major humanitarian crisis Islamic Relief South Sudan responds to include manmade and natural disasters (mainly flood & drought) and conflict-affected people. Islamic Relief World wide’s food security and livelihoods intervention aims to improve food security and livelihoods for crisis-affected populations in Tonj North and Gogrial East Counties of Warrap State and Kapoeta East County of Eastern Equatoria State. This project will address the humanitarian crisis through cash interventions, food pack distributions, farmers capacity building on resilience crop production, climate change adaptation and mitigation, and seed and tool distributions to the crisis affected populations in the targeted areas.
Islamic Relief's food security and livelihoods interventions are aligned with the South Sudan Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster Strategic Operational Framework 2025, contributing to achieving Goal 2 of the Sustainable Development Goals (Target 1-3). Through this Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), Islamic Relief South Sudan aims to address food insecurity and improve livelihoods for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), Returnees, and Host Communities in Kapoeta East, Tonj North and Gogrial East Counties of Warrap state, classified under priority areas 2 and 3. Addressing the immediate food needs and underlying livelihoods challenges, Islamic Relief South Sudan aims to contribute to the long-term food security and resilience of affected communities in Warrap and Eastern Equatoria States, in line with the South Sudan Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster goals and priorities for 2025:
This project, Enhancing Food Security and Resilient Livelihoods for Crisis-Affected Populations in Tonj North, Gogrial East, and Kapoeta East Counties and of Warrap and Eastern Equatoria South Sudan,' seeks to achieve the following objectives:
1. Improve access to nutritious food and reduce hunger among vulnerable populations (aligned with Cluster Priority 1: Emergency Food Assistance).
2. Enhance livelihoods through sustainable agricultural practices, income generation, and market access (aligned with Cluster Priority 2: Livelihoods Support).
3. Promote equitable and inclusive food security and livelihoods opportunities, integrating interventions with Nutrition, Gender, Protection, and Health sectors.
4. Strengthen community resilience and coping mechanisms through cash transfers, vocational training, and agricultural support (aligned with Cluster Priority 4: Resilience and Capacity Building).

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analyzed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4 and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience Phase 5 (Catastrophe). In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. In the current analysis period (July to September 2024), 53 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 or above (Serious or worse). During the post-harvest period of October 2024 to March 2025, the acute malnutrition situation is expected to deteriorate in 13 counties, while improvement in acute malnutrition is expected in only four counties. The AMN classification in 76 counties is projected to remain the same in the post-harvest projection period. During the lean season (April to June 2025), the severity of acute malnutrition is expected to deteriorate in 62 counties, remain similar in 16 counties and improve in two counties. The situation in Balliet County of Upper Nile State is projected to deteriorate from Phase 4 to IPC AMN Phase 5 (Extremely Critical) because of elevated morbidity.The Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) modality is ideal for this project. It provides beneficiaries with flexibility and choice, allowing them to prioritize their needs. CVA also supports local markets, promotes dignity and empowerment, and is efficient and cost-effective. Additionally, it aligns with Islamic values of justice, compassion, and self-reliance.

2. Project activities

Agricultural Support1. Distribution of seeds and tools, in order to recover the agro inputs lost during the displacements caused by the floods and the conflicts, the project will Provide high-quality seeds and essential tools to 15,000 farmers with 50% women being considered as they comprise the highest percentage of the productive population. More emphasis will be focused on climate-resilient crops.2. Farmer training, in order build the capacity of the farming folks to maximize agricultural production, 12,000 farmers with 60% women composition will be trained on climate-resilient agricultural practices, soil conservation, and irrigation management in order to boost agricultural production in the face of climate change.3. Climate resilience training,5,000 farmers again 50% women will undergo adaptive agriculture training, practices such as hydroponics, conservation agriculture, agroforestry etc. will be trained to the farmers as strategies for climate change mitigation and adaptation.4. Agricultural extension services, to enhance efficiency of information and knowledge sharing within the farmers, the project will recruit 24 extension workers who will be deployed in the communities to provide technical assistance to 10,000 farmers.Livestock Support1. Livestock distribution: Distribute livestock (goats, sheep, cattle) to 7,500 households (50% women-headed).2. Veterinary services, given the facts that all the displacements due to disasters come with lots of zoonotic diseases, the project will provide veterinary care and vaccination services to 10,000 households free of charge to enhance health livestock production3. Livestock management training, to promote sustainability of the project, 5,000 livestock owners (50% women) will be trained on improved livestock management practices.Income Generation1. Cash and voucher assistance, the project will provide cash and voucher assistance to 3,000 entrepreneurs (60% women) inform of small grants to support business start-up or expansion.2. Business skills training, to enhance sustainability,3,000 entrepreneurs (60% women) on will be trained on business management, marketing, and financial literacy to enable them efficiently manage their financial resources.3. Market access support, the project will Facilitate linkages market access for 2,000 entrepreneurs (60% women) through market research and linkages as well as provision of market shades. Food Assistance1. Food pack distribution, at scenarios where the markets are not fully functional, the project will procure and distribute food packs to 10,000 vulnerable households in the disaster hit areas of Gogrial East, Tonj North and Kapoeta East.
