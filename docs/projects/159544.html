Project Summary

Project aims to increase access to sustainable safe water, adequate sanitation facilities and hygiene education for drought affected population and areas that are high risk of AWD/Cholera due to extremely low water and or sanitation coverage in Somalia. Long-term displaced populations in prioritized locations will be provided with durable solutions to WASH services where land is availed by the government. Access to safe water will be increased through construction and or rehabilitation of water sources including 8 boreholes (3 new), 17 shallow wells, 1 river water intake system with water treatment, 1 desalination system using existing sources and extension of piped water network. New borehole construction will be preceded by hydrogeological survey and safe pumping regime will be designed. River water intakes will be preceded hydrological study. In all cases, an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) will be undertaken for new water sources and mitigation measures put in place for negative impacts. All water systems will be equipped with distribution infrastructure and hybrid energization systems. Pipeline extensions will also be made. Taps stand will be located within a radius of at least 500m to reduce distance and enhance security of women and girls. Project will also construct 1 major improved water dam in locations that lack other options. All well heads will be raised at a minimum 0.5m to reduce risk flooding and contamination. Water committees comprising men and women from all community groups including the minority and the marginalized will be trained on management, operation and maintenance. Local technicians will be trained and provided with O&M tools to undertake repairs whereas consumers will pay for the technician’s services. For major water supply in urban areas, Private Public Parternship (PPP) management system will be adopted. Adequate sanitation in 4 rural villages will be provided through construction of sanitation facilities, using Community Led Total Sanitation approach when feasible. Project will construct 400 latrines for families reintegrated through the durable solutions. NRC undertakes sanitary surveys periodically to identify contamination risks for timely mitigation. All latrine will be installed with handwashing equipment and community sensitized to use soap for handwashing. Design of infrastructure will take into consideration the needs of special groups in the community including the disables, old, children to ensure they have equitable access to the services. Life-saving WASH interventions will be implemented in case of sudden disaster to reduce the related mortality and morbidity especially among children. This will be through temporary provision of safe water through direct delivery or restricted cash depending on context and market functionality, provision of hygiene kits through cash or in-kind, household water treatment and/or point of use water treatment to 3000 hhs, construction of 450 simple design emergency latrines, latrine emptying (450 latrines) and safe fecal sludge disposal and hygiene promotion. Schools and health facilities will be provided with private space for menstrual hygiene (60 institutional latrines). All latrines will be raised above ground level to reduce the risk of flooding during rains. Women and girls will be involved in identification of sites for facilities to ensure their safety and security is taken into account. Latrines will be gender separated, provided with lockable doors and handwashing equipment. Solar street lights (35 posts) will be provided near water points and latrines to improve the security and safety of women and girls especially in crowded IDP settlements. Throughout the implementation period the beneficiaries will be provided with adequate information on NRC’s complaints, response and feedback (CRFM) mechanisms to enable them channel complaints or share feedback to be incorporate as necessary.

Mitigation Measures:• Hold an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures and make clear the entitlements for all. • Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all • Emphasize on inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings among household clusters. • Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be, outside the settlements.• Condition implementation to an equal and direct access with all communities and make this very clear to gatekeepers. Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and servicesLikelihood: High Impact: Very high Mitigation: • Involve women and girls in siting locations for communal WASH facilities considering their security and safety and ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. • Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement and install lights near communal WASH facilities • Where possible avoid central distribution on hygiene kits to avoid long ques and exposure of items to evil doers but rather use mobile money cash transfer for beneficiary to purchase items at own convenient time • Construct gender separated latrines, lockable from inside, and add lighting • Increase number of water points to reduce women and girls overcrowding, waiting too long at the ques and getting caught in darkness • Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities Communities are forced to leave their place of residence in order to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor) Likelihood: High Impact: medium Mitigation: • Providing the host community with access to WASH where they are experiencing shortages of basic WASH services and facilities. • For extremely hard to reach areas that are inaccessible to internationals, implement project through local partners with supervision by NRC’s national staff hailing from the target locations• Where possible and appropriate, deliver assistance through cash or voucher to minimize exposure. Please provide further explanation

Through various donors including DFID, UNHCR and NMFA, NRC has been at the forefront of the Durable solutions agenda and has supported thousands of displaced people acquire land and issued them with land title documents. The support by the government offers a protection environment that if well utilized will see many IDPs reintegrated. In the target locations, the IDP are protracted and have therefore intermingled with the host community over time and established positive cohesion. Project contributes significantly to the Resilience Recover Framework and the National Development plan which envisions "Adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation for all". Project contributes to achievement of the NDP 4 of the 6 NDP WASH targets whereby it will: • Increase equitable access to safe and affordable drinking water for 85,000 individuals • Increase access to gender sensitive WASH facilities with menstrual management facilities for 5 schools • Increase the percentage of people living in open defecation free environment • Increase the use of hand-washing practice with water and soap. Water infrastructure will be designed taking into account environmental sustainability thus safe abstraction of ground and surface water. New water sources will be installed with equipment to monitor abstraction at the sources. Water systems serving large population will preferably be managed through private public partnerships to enhance functional sustainability thus long term solution. Mini water supply system at village level will be managed by water management committees to be selected by the community and trained by NRC’s competent WASH staff. All water supply systems will be designed, implemented and periodically monitored jointly with the beneficiary community and the government authorities. Once completed, the facilities will be handed over formally to the community and the government and a certificate of hand-over signed by all parties. Construction of latrines through the community driven approach, CLTS, leads to immediate ownership and sustainability of the facilities. It is envisaged that promotion for use of hygiene items through provision of restricted cash will instill a culture of purchase of this vital items among the beneficiaries and also support the market chain that can run even after the project ends.
