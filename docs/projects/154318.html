Project Summary

In line with the 2019 strategy, REACH will continue providing support to the CCS Cluster to facilitate and enhance strategic decision making and coordination modalities in the humanitarian community of Iraq, at all levels. It will be facilitated through joint, coordinated and streamlined assessments, the provision of common data and information repositories, and the provision of data which strengthens the situational awareness of operational partners working across Iraq.
In collaboration with coordination structures such as OCHA and the Assessment Working Group, REACH proposes to bolster an information gathering approach aimed at providing situational understanding of the multi-sectoral and specific needs of vulnerable populations both at country-level and at area-level. Additionally, REACH will promote information sharing through common dashboards and multi-platform information materials, to support coordination efforts and mitigate assessment fatigue amongst affected populations.
Under these activities, the following outputs will be developed:
- Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment Report of accessible areas of Iraq;
- Thematic profiles on needs, displacement patterns, structural damage and drivers of displacement in inaccessible areas;
- Thematic/ cluster specific reports and datasets as well as publicly available datasets;
- Mapping of displacement patterns and exogenous factors on conflict affected persons;
- Area Based Assessment cleaned dataset and thematic mapping;
- Area profile including sectoral factsheets.
Due to the nature of REACH activities which are focused on informing the humanitarian community and coordination structures, no direct target beneficiaries were specified under the “Clusters” section as the exact catchment areas and populations will be specified based on the coordination with CCS cluster and/or stakeholders. It is however expected that the intervention will directly benefit the humanitarian community at large, thanks to the production of outputs which will be available to all clusters and partners. Internally displaced persons living in both informal and formal sites / camps will indirectly benefit from improved service delivery and humanitarian assistance by partners and coordination structures, based on the findings of the Multi Cluster Needs Assessment, thematic/cluster specific reports as well as mapping of displacement patterns both at country-level and at area-level. IDPs, host communities, returnees, and those never displaced all living within the catchment areas of the CRCs will indirectly benefit from programming planned against the area-based assessments.

Needs

2019 will see the humanitarian context shifting from previous years, as active conflict against ISIL has largely ended, however, an estimated 6.5 million are still in need of assistance, according to the HNO 2019. Returns to areas of origin have slowed down through 2018, with the barriers to return not easily fixable, including damage to housing or property, and security related concerns, indicating that there will be a high case load of IDPs in protracted displacement, in both in camp and out of camp locations. Furthermore, those who were never displaced or those hosting IDPs continue to face vulnerable conditions.Although intentions’ surveys highlight regional variances, several themes can be highlighted across IDP populations in and out of camp as barriers to return persist and these include (in different weighing depending on areas of origin and in/out of camp displacement): 1) security related concerns (lack of security forces, fear of discrimination), 2) damaged or destroyed homes, 3) lack of livelihood opportunities and in particular in the Mosul Response camps 4) lack of finance. Other factors include not sufficient basic services in areas of origin, the presence of explosive hazards and challenges related to the social cohesion. Given these significant barriers to return, formal and informal sites are likely to remain throughout 2019. Lack of basic services, high unemployment rates in addition to damages to infrastructure and personal residences will continue to pose the biggest challenges for the conflict affected populations in 2019, including these returning to their areas of origin, in protracted displacement, highly vulnerable residents in host communities, and non-displaced families in newly accessible areas. As such, analysis country wide and over time is required to better understand the needs of different crisis-affected population groups to support a principled, effective and inclusive strategic coordination and evidence-based humanitarian action at the national, regional, governorate, and local levels. In light of the complexity of this scenario, there is a need to ensure that the needs of IDPs, returnees, host communities and non-displaced families are equally met, also to avoid social tension and foster peaceful coexistence and the stabilization process.

Activities

To support the CCS Cluster in one of its key objectives for 2019 – strengthening of all-level strategic and operational coordination through common needs assessments and reliable data collection, processing, and analysis – REACH proposes to conduct following activities under its CCS 2019 strategy: 1: Seventh round of Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA VII)REACH will conduct the MCNA across all accessible areas of Iraq and across multiple conflict-affected population groups. The MCNA is designed to provide macro-level information on different crisis-affected population groups to be used in strategic and governmental planning and targeting of specific assistance. The MCNA will be timed to inform the HNO-HRP process, thanks to harmonized and multi-cluster indicators and comprehensive dissemination plans. Assessment design, data collection, data analysis, and reporting will be facilitated by REACH – however, these steps will be implemented under the framework of the Assessment Working Group and in full collaboration with the ICCG. Data collection will cover all accessible areas of Iraq and will apply sampled household-level assessments, representative at the district level. REACH will coordinate joint data collection to enable humanitarian partners to participate in the MCNA exercise and ensure a wide geographic coverage. Findings will be disaggregated by geographic area, age, gender, and population group. Under this activity, following outputs will be developed:a) Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment Report of accessible areas of Iraq;b) Thematic profiles on needs, displacement patterns, structural damage and drivers of displacement in inaccessible areas;c) Thematic/ cluster specific reports;d) Mapping of displacement patterns;e) Mapping of exogenous factors on conflict affected persons;f) Publicly available datasets.2: Multiply Area Based Assessments (ABA)REACH will conduct multiple ABAs to provide baseline data for CRC programming, prioritization, strategy, and advocacy purposes. The number of ABAs conducted will be based on how many CRCs are opened in the given period, and will build off of the ABA framework validated by the CRC Steering Committee in 2018. The methodology combines secondary and primary data collection to produce a multi-layered understanding of areas of return: household needs and vulnerabilities, the state of public services and infrastructure, and security and access concerns. The specific objectives of this exercise can be described as follows: a) Identify priority multi-sectorial needs of mixed population groups residing in these areas; b) Identify vulnerable subsets of affected population groups and specific needs of these groups; c) gauge the perceptions and expectations of residents regarding service delivery and reconstruction efforts.The ABA will employ mixed methods data collection: a) KI interviews: Community leaders and local experts; b) Community group discussions: Community perspectives on service provision and access; c) Community participatory mapping: Spatial identification of service areas; d) Household survey: Capturing prevalence of needs and vulnerabilitiesSix to twelve months after the initial ABA, REACH will conduct a follow-up assessment to evaluate how response gaps and needs have been addressed since the baseline, utilizing a similar data collection approach as outlined above. The follow-up will support a better understanding of the shifting needs of the affected populations.REACH also proposes to fund a dedicated staff member, who can assist the CRC Steering Committee and CCCM Cluster in developing processes and best practice, in developing the connection between the data collected through the ABAs, the 5ws and how to create a localised, granular area-based approach in the CRCs.Under this activity following outputs will be developed:a) ABA cleaned dataset;b) Area profile including sectoral factsheets;c) Thematic mapping.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measure 1: It is assumed that REACH will be able to access each location. Authorization from local authorities and local leaders will be sought for each location prior to data collection. In case the access challenges occur in certain parts of the country, for example Anbar, the data collection will include combination of direct, in-person interviews and remote, phone-based interviews.Mitigation Measure 2: Should security deteriorate, project areas would be switched to those still accessible and/or trainees would be encouraged and subsidized to travel to accessible areas, if feasible. ACTED security protocol shall be applied.Mitigation Measure 3: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation. Close collaboration with relevant stakeholders to ensure access to targeted sites.Mitigation Measure 4: ACTED and REACH enforces the use of track 24 with speed limits and daily incident logs, combined with driver training to mitigate this risk. Pre-planning of missions, including security clearance for movements of the program teams, take into consideration the types and quality of the roads.Mitigation Measure 5: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation. Security / evacuation plans established.Mitigation Measure 6: For locations where the risk of security incidents is high, ACTED establishes movements in convoy with multiple communication mechanisms, tracking capabilities based on GPS and Thuraya Satellite phone systems as redundant communications, as well as hourly communication checks in order to allow an on-time monitoring of the teams. ACTED has developed the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) which are the individual safety and security rules to be followed when a specific incident occurs.Mitigation Measure 7: All staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe and which are not.Mitigation Measure 8: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation.

How is your project accountable to affected population

All activities proposed under this project are beneficiary-facing exercises which ensure that the needs of beneficiaries and affected IDP populations in formal, informal sites and areas of return are involved in the different stages of partner programming. Their core purpose is to create site-specific, multi-sectoral understandings of the situation as reported by the affected population in order to better inform the humanitarian community and enable effective prioritization of assistance. All assessment activities are designed with consideration for protection concerns and sensitivity towards the population of interest. Indicators and questions will be designed with these factors in mind, including ensuring minimal collection of personal identifiers and the removal of any such data prior to dissemination of datasets. Further, prior to commencing the interview, REACH enumerators will obtain informed consent from the respondent and explain that he or she has the right to withdraw consent at any point during the interview. With respect to qualitative data collection, focus group discussions will be sex segregated in accordance with cultural norms and to ensure female participants are able to vocalize their opinions comfortably.REACH has designed and will implement the current proposal using an approach which contributes significantly to gender equality in partner programming. The research activity specifically allows the disaggregation of the data by gender and vulnerable sub-sets where possible in order to inform programming that addresses the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons. Based on the data provided by REACH, partners will be able to tailor gender specific activities to the specific needs of these groups by providing such services as: gender-segregated information sessions, gender-segregated distribution lines, and gender-segregated toilet and shower facilities.
