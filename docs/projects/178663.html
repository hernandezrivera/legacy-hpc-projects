Project Summary

In 2020 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including ERL, reaching 35,081 beneficiaries in total. In 2020 ZOA secured $4,439,160 in funding, of which $1,331,000 was SHF funding. In 2021 ZOA has to date secured $3,888,000. The cost per beneficiary for the proposed ERL project will be around $2150.
ZOA proposes to increase livelihood opportunities for communities most in need in Aleppo, Rural Damascus & Quneitra through the provision of small business (SB) start-up & business recovery grants. 400 vulnerable beneficiaries falling under set criteria will be targeted in the proposed target locations. A needs based approach will be followed. The proposed average size of each grant is $2500 based on Sector recommendations. Beneficiaries will receive basic trainings on how to start up and run successful business with ongoing support from ZOA during implementation. In summary, all selected 400 beneficiaries of SB will benefit from sector activities indicators 2.1, 3.1 & 4.1. The project targets IDPs, returnees and people from host communities according to needs.
An additional apprenticeship activity with another 400 beneficiaries is proposed for this project, which will be linked to each business grant disbursed. The aim of this activity is to encourage on-job trainings for skilled beneficiaries. Since the crisis has mostly affected the IDPs (HNO March 2021 p. 65) 60% of the beneficiaries of this activity will be IDPs, to mitigate social rupture. Each apprenticeship will last four months and each beneficiary will get conditional MPC for 250,000 SYP. The condition to receive the MPC is to work with the assigned small business grant beneficiary at least 120 hours per month. The 200,000 SYP is based on the Cash Working Group February 2021 recommendations and the additional 50,000 SYP will cover transportation costs. Each beneficiary accepted for the small business grant will only receive the funding under the condition of employing one beneficiary from the apprenticeship activity for 4 months without being responsible for the MPC allocation mentioned above.
ZOA will prioritize communities with high severity of needs, and beneficiaries will be selected following a set criteria. ZOA’s vulnerability criteria also incorporates protection concerns. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project and implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach and impactful support to households. Beneficiaries are selected after presenting all the needed documentation and a project proposal. Project committees will be set up in all locations to have their input in selecting the beneficiaries.
For all activities, vulnerable households will be taken into consideration according to a set criteria. Families with members with disabilities will be targeted, and training as well as job opportunities will be explored for people living with disabilities to gain an income independently. Also, women will be encouraged and given opportunities to equally benefit from the project.
Projects will be monitored and adjusted accordingly by ZOA staff/volunteers and local communities. Combining small business grants with apprenticeship on jobs deemed relevant in the local communities will give ZOA important insight into the effectiveness of each modality in a Syrian context towards the objective of improved livelihoods for vulnerable families.
The proposed intervention is in response to the HNO 2021, which states that “This widespread loss of livelihoods and income has led and will most likely lead people to resort to harmful and negative coping mechanisms.” The project is aimed at generating employment and sustainable livelihoods for the most vulnerable households in target locations, providing a catalyzing effect on the local market.
The selected locations are accessible by ZOA as well as its implementing partners. Targeted sub-districts fall under the severity score 3-4 based on the HNO 2021 severity master sheet.

4 - Needs

The 2021 HNO issued in March states that the number of people in need of early recovery and livelihoods interventions is 11.6m. There has been an increase in the number of people in need, largely due to households depleting their savings and assets and struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their basic needs as the crisis remains protracted. Massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment and poverty coupled with high food prices, sanctions and inflation have all contributed to increased humanitarian needs across Syria. “Due to COVID-19, there has been a decrease of 19.8 per cent in average employees in the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) sector. In particular, there was a decrease in employment of 18.2 per cent for males and 19.7 per cent for females. Most enterprises lost significant revenue ranging from 40 per cent to 100 per cent, and about 13 per cent had to close their operations” (HNO 2021 p.65). Furthermore, the effect of COVID-19 on the local economy was obvious: “As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, before the pandemic, 41.9 per cent of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7 per cent can do so. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. About two-thirds of households lost one or more income sources, and about half lost between 75 per cent to 100 per cent of their previous income” (HNO 2021 p. 65).

5 - Implementation of activities

ZOA will work closely with relevant local authorities, the sector and local communities to ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable. The steps undertaken to achieve results are: General Activities: 1. Market assessment. 2. Establishing Community Committees and provision of required trainings. 3. Training & capacity building of project staff and volunteers, which will include topics on accountability to beneficiaries, protection and gender mainstreaming, safety and security and COVID-19 awareness. 4. Consultations and coordination with affected communities. 5. PDM - Follow up with small grants and apprenticeship beneficiaries. 6. Reporting and M&E are the responsibilities of the country based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework- indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and for demonstrating impact. Small Business (SB) Activities: 1. Receive proposals from potential beneficiaries. 2. Selection of 400 applicants based on feasibility studies as well as eligibility according to set criteria. 3. Agreement to provide on site training to 1 apprenticeship beneficiary. 4. Basic training to successfully set up and run a business. 5. Allocation of funds - each selected beneficiary on average will receive $2500. Apprenticeship Activities:1. Getting applications. 2. Selection of 400 beneficiaries, based on set criteria. 3. Connecting the successful apprenticeship applicants with SB beneficiaries. 4. On site training for 4 months. 5. Allocate 250,000 SYP per month to each beneficiary over 4 months.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Tension in community - for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the ERL sector, such as UNDP beneficiary targeting tool; 2. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonised as possible throughout protection service providers; 3. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to ensure that selected beneficiaries to which ZOA/partners just provides four months of assistance can be covered through other actors for the remaining months; 4 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/ changes. Risk 2: Selection criteria, influenced leading to project not accessing most vulnerable. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 1. Clear complaint and feedback mechanisms; 2. Ongoing engagement with community leaders and clear community communication; 3. Humanitarian standards, accountability and PSEA training for staff. Risk 3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1. Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2. Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3. Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4. Regular staff training; 5. Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Government entities require agencies to adhere to new regulations or additional approval levels that are required in order to be able to implement activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: 1. Coordinate well within the sectorial coordination spaces, to be aware of how other actors deal with the challenges; 2. Continue to engage closely with authorities; 3. Continue to build capacities of local actors and continue to implement through them. R2: Market and exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2. Transfer funds into Syrian bank according to the immediate needs. 3. Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency. R3: Movement restrictions due to COVID-19. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Carry out activities as soon as possible through existing infrastructure before more restrictions and lockdowns are enforced; 2. Consider working with local partners and faith based organizations who might have easier access to beneficiaries in target locations in case of lockdowns; 3. Explore remote alternatives for field monitoring. R4: Fraud and corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements, using multiple channels; 2. ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its Code of Conduct; 3 Procurement, Logistics, Fraud and Corruption Policies in place for the mitigation of fraud and corruption risks; 4. All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption and abuse; 5.All financial transactions are documented and filed; 6. Regular finance spot-checks and unannounced audits are carried out.
