Project Summary

The proposed intervention will support community-based protection activities in the camps adjacent to the host community and support joint initiatives towards social cohesion between the two communities. In addition, the analysis from protection monitoring will be used to tailor community-based protection responses to the needs of specific groups, such as supporting women and girls in accessing services or peer support groups for elderly and persons with disabilities. Formation of community groups will also take into account identified gaps in community participation to foster social inclusion for less empowered groups, such as adolescent boys and girls, youth, and women. Beneficiaries under this project will be targeted using an AGD approach and target estimation was calculated based on this year experience and available data/assessments. HC intervention will seek to engage with the relevant ministries and government service providers (particularly DSS/MoWCA) to support exchanges on good practices, joint consultations and capacity-building at the field-level (particularly on referrals and improving access to services).
Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (currently rolled out in camps by DRC SMS) will be also available in host communities through community centres; and consultation and feedback mechanisms are incorporated into the methodology of all DRC protection activities. DNH principle is at the core of all DRC Protection approaches, is reflected in activity SOPs and tools (incl. selection/targeting approach, risk analysis, etc.). Social cohesion work will be based on careful analysis of communities’ dynamics and community-led process to identify opportunities for joint initiatives which would contribute to improved relationships. All DRC staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct with specific PSEA provisions, and CoC trainings are provided to all joining staff and volunteers; DRC has a robust CoC response mechanism, with Protection staff as PSEA focal point, and links to CFRM and protection referral.
DRC is an active member of PWG and PWG TF (as well as CPSS, GBVSS and CXB PSEA Network) and will coordinate within these fora to ensure that protection activities are complementary and holistic in the humanitarian response.

Needs

During 2018, DRC is has been implementing a general protection program in the Rohingya refugee camps and in displacement-affected host communities. The Rohingya population is extremely vulnerable and there are significant protection concerns in the sites. There high levels psychosocial distress among refugee men and women (as well as children) as a result of traumatic experiences in Myanmar. The spontaneous nature of the sites means most lack site planning and coordination, therefore a lack of protection, cultural and gender consideration in the layout and infrastructure is particularly having an adverse effect on women and girls. Insufficient lighting and the distance from latrines leaves women and girls exposed to GBV and theft, and a lack of culturally and religiously appropriate clothing is contributing to their movement restrictions which have been steadily increasing, limiting women’s and girls access to humanitarian assistance and services. According to Multisector Needs Assessment (July 2018) between 34-40% of women and girls reported latrines and bathing areas unsafe, and only 5% of adolescent girls (12-17) and 10% of women (18+) reported attending activities at an NGO-run space where only women and girls are allowed; these numbers are below the average in camps 10, 11, 12, 13 (for girls), 14 (for women). Men and boys in their turn are facing higher risks of physical violence and forced/exploitative labour when they leave the camps in search of casual work. Elderly persons and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable to obstructions to access to humanitarian assistance and services, with very few adequately designed assistance modalities to meet their specific needs. Households with specific needs represent around 31% of the overall population. DRC PM data (qualitative), shows that camps 11, 12, 13, 14 experience widespread issues around access to services (esp. WASH, food, health) and access to information on available services, which are exacerbated for certain groups (women, adolescent girls, elderly, PwD) and result in negative coping mechanisms and conflicts/violence, indicating the need to expand CBP to strengthen self-protection mechanisms and to build community capacities to reduce and mitigate protection risks.Similarly, camps 13 and 14 have more regular reports of violence or harassment during firewood collection and tensions with the host community indicating the need to focuse in these areas on joint initiatives between HC and refugees to decrease social tensions. Livelihoods and living conditions in the host community surrounding the refugee camp have been negatively impacted by the influx of Rohingya refugees, and further weakened the protective environment in Ukhia upazila, an area already lacking social and economic development. The provision of support only to refugees in camps has created serious adverse consequences for both refugees and host community, through an increase in social tensions and violence between the groups, as well as a reduction in access to services for host communities. Existing actors have been stretched to provide sufficient protection support across the refugee and in host communities. There is a need for a more thorough understanding of the protection risks, vulnerabilities and capacities of communities and significant gaps exist in service provision. Given the scale of the influx and the administrative status of Rohingya in Bangladesh, the ability of individuals to access basic services and to seek redress and remedy through referral mechanisms and administrative follow-up with the various providers of services and humanitarian aid is also weak. Protection monitoring will further identify protection risks and vulnerabilities, and social dynamics with a direct bearing on the ability of the persons of concern to access basic services and benefit from protective systems.

Activities or outputs

COMMUNITY-BASED PROTECTION (host community and camp):1. Creation of community-based groups (with male and female representation, incl. different age groups) in order to strengthen understanding and referrals of protection concerns at each community level, including through the identification and capacity-building of community outreach volunteers and supporting groups with specific needs - such as women and girls, persons with disabilities, the elderly - in strengthening their access to services2. Implementation of community-driven safety and protection plans to foster prevention and social cohesion and facilitate community groups to identify protection risks and threats, and support implementation of community-designed priority risks and threats, through provision of technical, material, or financial support. This activity will be supported through technical guidance from DRC’s environmental advisor to assist communities in identifying and responding to environmental risks, as well as looking for opportunities for linkages between host community and refugees through DRR projects and ensuring that projects in either community do not have negative environmental impacts for the other. 3. Running of community centres in 4 host community locations, which will serve as a multi-functional site for community-based awareness-raising, outreach, and protection groups, as well as a protection desk providing individual counselling and case managementDRC will coordinate this activity with actors on the ground in the camps and host communities to avoid any duplication. In the targeted camps, CPB will complement other DRC protection activities implemented through other grants. PROTECTION MONITORING AND RESPONSE (host community):4. Protection monitoring and information management to identify trends in vulnerability in host community locations and inform programming, response, and advocacy. Approximately 200 monitoring visits are planned for the year (approximately 2400 male/2400 female participants of different age groups from HC). Note: protection monitoring in camps is fully covered under UNHCR funding and not included in this JRP submission by DRC. 5. Case management for the most vulnerable persons in host community locations, through counselling, referrals or individual assistance, as well as provision of psycho-social support (tracked with age/gender disaggregation). Case Management target was estimated at 450 based on this year’s work in the targeted host communities (recently started) and case management in camps. Protection referral pathways will be rolled jointly with the Protection Sector, the protection camp focal points, inclusively of all the protection actors on the ground to ensure DNH principles and safeguards of protection cases is in place.CAPACITY-BUILDING AND AWARENESS-RAISING (host community and camp):6. Awareness-raising and outreach to the community (women and men of different age groups) to improve understanding of protection, rights, and vulnerabilities, and support community-driven protection responses. This activity will be implemented through CBP groups, IEC/outreach, and activities in community centres and is estimated reach roughly 30% of HC and 10% of camp population. 7. Capacity-building of government counterparts and humanitarian actors to support protection mainstreaming across the responseADVOCACY8. Development and implementation of an internal advocacy strategy at local, national, regional and global level relating to the refugee situation and response in Bangladesh
