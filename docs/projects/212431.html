Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, of them 2.83 million ( 17%) are with disability. The provision of trauma, referral, disability and rehabilitation services declined due to the heavily disrupted health system in Syria because of the protracted crises exacerbated by the devastating 2023 earthquake. Identified needs are:
-Poor access to health services / lack of functioning facilities,
-Injuries as a direct result of bombing, fighting and mines/UX.
-Lack of proper and secured referral system.
-Disruption in medicines and medical equipment supply chains for trauma.
-Need to ensure safe and sufficient blood transfusion services, especially in NES.
-Needs for life-saving medicines, supplies, burn kits, fully equipped ambulances, and assistive devices.
To address these needs in 2024, WHO SYR will
- Procure and distribute medicines, trauma and surgical kits, blood safety kits and equipment to main emergency-providing health facilities.
-Train health workers on emergency, burn, Physical rehablitation and trauma management including WHO-developed MCM training.
- Rehabilitate the infrastructure in health facilities/emergency and burn units.
- Strengthen the provision of physical rehabilitation services through distribution of prosthetic and orthotic supplies.
- MOU with NGOs to provide surgical interventions and physical rehabilitation services,
- Establish a national register for PWDs.
- Contract a private hospital in NES to provide emergency and trauma services to IDPs.
- Procure ambulances to enhance the referral system.
After 13 years of destruction, Syria’s health system was further affected by the devastating earthquake, that struck in Feb 2023, leading to 4,540 deaths and 10,485 injuries in NWS, increasing the demand on an already fragmented and under-resourced healthcare system.
Due to the heavily disrupted health system in NWS the provision of trauma services, as well as the provision of disability and rehabilitation services declined. Additionally, the referral system which has been critical for the resilience and adaptability of the health system in NWS, was also severely disrupted referral services, and left the treatment of emergency cases depending on donors' funding.
In 2024, WHO aims to enhance the trauma care package, encompassing prehospital care (first aid & life-saving referrals), emergency room care, trauma surgery capacity, intensive care, and rehabilitation. This comprehensive approach will improve the referral system for emergency and non-emergency care and ensure the continuity of care for Syrian patients and constitutes an integral part of the adaptability and resilience plan of the healthcare system. In NWS, WHO will
-Continue leading Trauma & Disability Technical Working Group, aiming to improve the quality and distribution of service and enhance access to the rehabilitation services
-Continues leading the coordination of the referral system and strengthening its functionality
-Conduct an ambulance and referral system mapping under the coordination of the Health Cluster
-Provide technical support and capacity-building for health cluster partners
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
