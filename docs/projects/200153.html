Project Summary

Within the framework of the ERP 2022-2023 under the funding of LHF, Tdh in collaboration with its local partners PWHO, Sanabel Janoub ,and TSS propose the project “Girls, boys, at risk and survivors of violence exploitation and abuse have access to holistic and tailored CP and GBV services in South Lebanon and Nabatieh.”. The project grants access to holistic, relevant, and tailored CP and GBV services to children at CP and GBV risk and their families in the most disadvantaged localities in South of Lebanon and will increase their capacities to promote practices that protect them. The project will contribute to inclusive and sustainable child protection services in Lebanon. The child protection component of the project aims to ensure the protection, wellbeing and resilience of the children and youth, while the social behavioral change aspect will support them to become active agents of change against violence, reverting in healthier and gender-balanced relationships. Community members at large will benefit from an enhanced equal social dynamic, where violence against women and girls becomes an issue of community support, reducing progressively harmful practices (child marriages, intrafamily and domestic violence) and discriminatory social norms. Capacity building of the local partners is at the core of the project to foster localization and sustainability. Tdh and the local partners have strong experience in the provision of CP and GBV services in the South and Nabatieh. Through long presence in the areas, all partners have trustful relationships with local communities and local authorities, which facilitates the access to most rural areas. Based on existing project data of ongoing projects, alongside with most recent vulnerability assessments, Tdh and local partners identified the most vulnerable areas and actively advocates for integrated and tailored CP and GBV services in the South and Nabatieh.
In total, the project will target 3,535 direct beneficiaries and 10,605 indirect beneficiaries tailored and integrated GBV and CP prevention and response activities, including CP and GBV case management, FPSS for children and caregivers, and community-based prevention activities. Under the GBV component, 910 girls and young women are having access to mobile and static female safe spaces. Within the Safe space component, 60 cases will receive GBV case management following the survivor centered approach and integrated within the case management 75% of the cases (45) will receive emergency cash assistance. All beneficiaries will receive legal services based on their needs (info session, counseling, representation).
Under CP, 300 girls and boys will be assisted through specialized services within child protection case management. Child protection cases at risk and/or separated or unaccompanied will benefit from the alternative care services. Based on their needs, around 75% of the cases will receive Emergency Cash Assistance. Referrals to other services, such as education, basic services and health will be ensured. Following a holistic approach, 365 girls and boys and 365 parents/caregivers will receive from Focused Psychosocial Support.
665 girls and boys are supported through community-based psychosocial support, while 665 parents and caregivers will receive positive parenting sessions. 12 Social Behavioral Change - Qudwa initiatives are conducted to prevent child marriage, child labor and violence against children and women in the targeted communities. Furthermore, all 3,535 children and caregivers will be capacitated to access a safe and accessible channel to report sexual exploitation and abuse.
The mentioned project is linked to ERP’s priorities for protection and GBV, with MoSA’s and UNICEF’s Social Behavior Change Communication Plan - Qudwa Plan, with MoSA’s action plan for the national project to combat begging and child labor for children in street situation and with Child Protection Sector’s Plan for Child Labor.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under the GBV component, this project aims at providing 1. Survivors of GBV and those at-risk access timely and quality case management, ensuring a multi-sectoral and comprehensive access to services, including medical, psychosocial, legal and financial support and 2. Reducing GBV risks through targeted prevention activities through: CLPRO1/CA1: 910 girls and young women are having access to mobile and static female safe spaces; CA2: 60 GBV survivors and those at risk of GBV supported receive GBV case management, including legal assistance and ECA based on needs (75%); CA3: 20 staff of partner organizations are capacitated to provide multi-sectoral prevention and response to GBV. Under the CP component, this project 1. provides access to safe and dignified package of quality response services for girls and boys at risk and survivors of violence, exploitation and abuse along their impacted caregivers by the multiple crises in Lebanon in South and Nabatieh. and 2. reduces CP risks through community based protection activities. CA4: 300 children will receive CP case management. CA5 75% of the 300 children under CA4 will receive ECA. CA6: 365 children and 365 caregivers will benefit from FPSS. CA7: 665 children will receive CBPSS and 665 parents positive parenting. CA8: 12 Child protection initiatives will be conducted.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Children at CP risk, survivors of GBV and their caregivers are reluctant to trust child protection actors and are difficult to track, since they are on a constant move. M: Tdh will maximize its support and time to build relationships of trust with street connected children and their parents/caregivers. Outreach/mobile teams will be present and accessible during day and night shifts, and children and caregivers have 24h/7 access to a hotline. R: Children are recruited and manipulated by an organized network that prevents them to participate in the activities. M: Identification of the group leader and planning a strategy to approach and mediate with him in order to give the permission for the children to attend the activities. Consultation with the children about the best mediation way. Coordination with local authorities in case of children’s exploitation or other risks. R: Local community and neighbors are negative towards activities. M: Development of a community engagement plan

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The 4 pillars of accountability are ensured: 1)Beneficiaries’ participation and empowerment in activities design, ensuring inclusion and representation of the different groups with G&D markers. 2) The Feedback Complaint Response Mechanism is managed by the accountability team, informing every stakeholder of its existence and functioning modalities. 3) Prevention and response to issues linked with Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), G&D, Child Safeguarding, fraud/corruption allegations and program quality through dedicated PSEA focal points. 4) Suggestion, Feedback and Complaints Boxes installed inside offices. 5) Hotlines and dedicated accountability email addresses for reporting any kind of breach. 6)Focus group discussions and satisfaction surveys are planned with the participants to the activities to collect feedback around the quality of service received and any faced challenges; that shall feed as well in the process of adapting the activities’ implementation and decision making.
