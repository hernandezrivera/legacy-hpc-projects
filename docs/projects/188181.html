Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to increased access to meet food and basic needs for the IDPs and most vulnerable households in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates. To attain this, IhsanRD proposes to distribute E-food voucher. The value of E-voucher transfers will be in an average of 67$/HH/round, for 8 rounds to cover the food needs to the value of 70% based on the coverage of the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) as determined by the CWG and based on REACH price monitoring in October 2021. Using the SMEB as the basis for a transfer value and conducting market assessments to ensure these items are available in the targeted markets means that households are likely to spend the funds on the intended basic food needs items, something which is monitored by PDMs.

4 - Needs

According to the OCHA’s situation report number 31 as of 31 September 2021, in north-west Syria, 97 per cent of people live in extreme poverty (they are part of a household where each individual lives below $1.90 per person per day). Coupled with the low purchasing power of the Syrian Pound (SYP), poor families are struggling to meet their most basic needs, furthermore, monitoring study by the cluster for Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) shows 91 per cent of the residents live in sites without adequate lighting on main roads. Lack of lighting is a protection concern, especially for women and girls. The study shows that most displacement sites are overcrowded with poor living conditions, increasing the risk of COVID-19. Syria is projected to be one of the countries most impacted by the climate emergency, with prolonged droughts and flash floods. A third of displaced people in the north-west – 500,000 people – live in sites that flooded last year. Regarding COVID-19 the data suggests that the pandemic is peaking in the north-west, with a 61 per cent positivity rate. Since mid-August, COVID-19 cases rose sharply. By 4 October, there were more than 76,632 confirmed cases (45,997 in Idleb Governorate and 30,635 in Aleppo Governorate).

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA is at the core of IRD responses, and all IRD staff must comply with it. Effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to SEA acts by integrating PSEA in IRD policies and making sure that all personnel read and sign the code of conduct and that all contracts with local council/hawala agents include clauses on PSEA. IRD also provides awareness training to all staff in the organization on PSEA principles.The proposed program is built taking fully into account: Do No Harm, Age, Gender, Diversity mainstreaming (AGDM), and commitment to providing dignity and equal access to assistance. The project has been designed based on the needs of all groups affected by the ongoing crisis in the target location through different channels: direct field observations of IRD field team, local councils, community feedback. Before starting the project, IRD will carefully analyze and map all the stakeholders present in the targeted areas, their roles, influences, and potential impact on the intervention. This will allow the project team to set the necessary participatory mechanisms and decide which stakeholders need to be involved in the different implementation stages.
