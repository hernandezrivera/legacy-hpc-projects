1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Food insecurity is rising as conflict and repeated displacement, compounded by climatic shocks, have disrupted communities’ agricultural activities and livelihoods in Cabo Delgado. According to FEWS NET: Mozambique Food Security Outlook October 2021 to May 2022: Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected to persist in Cabo Delgado through May 2022. An estimated 642,000 IDPs in Cabo Delgado are living with host families or resettlement areas with little access to their basic livelihood activities. Montepuez is currently hosting more than 58,930 IDPs who are relying on emergency humanitarian services to meet their basic needs. High food prices and pipeline breaks have exacerbated their vulnerabilities.The project aims to provide 1,000 IDPs in the resettlement sites with emergency lifesaving food assistance and support 700 IDPs including vulnerable host populations with seeds, tools, training for agricultural and livelihood activities. The activities will be implemented in Massasse, Upajo, Compone, Ujama, Mapupulo Piloto, Nanhupo and Nicuapa in Montepuez district. The direct target beneficiaries will include 1,700 (424 girls, 636 women, 407 boys and 233 men) aged children under 18 years, adults 19-59 years and elderly above 60 years. The project intends to enhance the food security and livelihoods of IDPs and host communities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1.0. 1,000 IDPs affected by conflict in Massasse, Upajo, Compone and Ujama Resettlement Sites provided with emergency life-saving assistance to ensure their nutritional needs are met● Train staff and government officials.● Identify IDPs in need of life-saving food assistance.● Plan and set beneficiary figures for food distribution in consultation with appropriate stakeholders.● Provide recipients with distribution plans with information on distribution points, quality and quantity to be provided.● Distribute life-saving food assistance to targeted IDPs.● Monitor and report distribution of life-saving food assistance. 2.0 700 IDPs and host communities in Mapupulo Piloto, Upajo, Nanhupo and Nicuapa provided with agricultural inputs and training for livelihood and primary production.● Train 700 IDPs and host populations in modern agricultural methods including post-harvest management.● Provide 700 persons with seeds and tools to increase agricultural production.● Establish 16 demonstration plots to teach various agricultural techniques.● Monitor and Evaluate activities.● Co-ordinate activities with SDAE, local leaders, other NGOs and FSL cluster.● Provide regular reports to FSL Cluster and other stakeholders.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

JAM is keen to protect all beneficiaries with particular attention to girls, boys, and women in its different interventions. As this project will involve girls, women, boys, and men, JAM will ensure: ● Prioritize safety for all beneficiaries and avoid causing further harm to the IDPs and host communities.● Encourage meaningful access to interventions to all IDPs in proportion to their need and without discrimination. JAM will provide special attention to more vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities who usually have difficulty accessing services.● JAM will enhance accountability by setting up an appropriate mechanism for the IDPs and host communities to measure the adequacy of intervention and address the concerns and complaints.● JAM will promote the IDPs and host communities' participation and empowerment by developing self-protection mechanisms and self-awareness within the beneficiaries to claim their rights. The gender analysis survey will provide the potential risks for each target group, and JAM will respond accordingly as per the above-proposed approaches. These approaches will enhance safety and dignity and promote IDPs and host communities' human rights without perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

JAM has conducted several participatory needs assessments to inform its intervention, with a recent assessment conducted in July-August 2021. Through its participatory assessment, JAM incorporated systemic engagement to identify diverse perspectives, risks, and capacities of different groups, including girls, boys, women, men and persons with disabilities, minority groups, and analyze their needs distinctly to respond to their specific needs. The enormous needs from different beneficiaries whom JAM works with have therefore informed this project. JAM will organize a project launch to share project information, to sensitize on the existence of complaints mechanisms, and the right to provide feedback and voice their concerns without victimization. JAM will provide suggestion boxes, phone services, and face-to-face meetings with households and community leaders for this exercise. The complaints received would be followed up promptly and handled transparently and fairly. All feedback will be collected by JAM staff and collated by the data analysts for action. JAM will ensure beneficiaries access information and recognize appropriate feedback channels on distribution, entitlements, acceptability, quality of products, and lodge complaints. It will help JAM identify feedback loops and close them so that beneficiaries who participate see changes as the result of their efforts.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

JAM will be guided by the IASC Guidelines on the inclusion of Person with Disability in Humanitarian Action (2019) which encourages organizations to promote meaningful participation, remove barriers, empower persons with disabilities, and disaggregate data for monitoring inclusion. Specifically, JAM will:i. Review organizational policies, guidelines, and tools to ensure they affirm the rights of persons with disabilities to access and inclusionii Ensure feedback and complaints mechanisms are designed to ensure accessibility for persons with disabilities.iii. Train staff on the inclusion of persons with disabilities, familiarize staff on IASC guidelines, encourage staff to identify persons with disabilities and involve them in implementation and propose measures to overcome barriers iv. Seek advice, guidance, and support from organizations, and coordination platforms specialized in working with persons with disabilities and mainstreaming inclusion.v. Ensure that all data collected is disaggregated at a minimum by gender, age, and disability, and analyze data accordingly.vi. Ensure that persons with a range of disabilities participate in key informant interviews, FGD, and in needs assessment teams. Guarantee representation of persons with disabilities by including girls, boys, women, and men.vii. JAM will understand the needs, capacities, and difficulties of persons with disabilities and their caregivers.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project will have some medium environmental impact due to waste generated from life-saving food assistance and the effect of livelihood interventions on the environment. To minimize the environmental impact of the project, JAM will ensure: i. Conduct environmental screening assessment of the projectii. Prepare an environmental plan to mitigate the finding of the screening exercise.iii. Carry out mitigation measures, including: ● Environmental awareness sessions with the target beneficiaries, other stakeholders, and other implementing partners● Plan the solid waste management scheme in collaboration with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, agricultural land, diseases, and odors.● Encourage climate-smart agriculture practices that promote Water resource management, soil preparation, and agroforestry.● Consult the beneficiaries to get their views and meaningful, involve them in new environmental coping strategies.● Promote tree planting projects in the target locations.● Identify the environmental impact on human livelihoods and promote the best practices that have less impact on the environment.
