Project Summary

The idea of the project is to provide surgical services to patients on the waiting lists at the MoH, this idea meets the 1st HC SO & the 2nd HRP SO, adding to HNO 2020. Statistics of MoH 2019 indicated that 9,000 patients are registered on the waiting list including 2,500 patients in need of ENT surgeries. Knowing that the HC is planning to target 4500 patients in 2020.
The project aims to conduct 1500 moderate & major electives surgeries targeting women, men, girls, boys, elderly & PWD from all over the Gaza Strip (GS), representing 33% from the targeted beneficiaries in 2020. The services will include medical examination, lab. & radiological services, surgical operation, hospitalization & medication. Psychological support services will be provided to patients of all categories, especially children pre & post-op.
To facilitate the access of beneficiaries from different locations in the GS, the services will be provided in 2 UHWC’s health facilities: Al-Awda Hospital–Northern GS & Al Awda Health Center–Southern GS.
This project is a continuation of a previous project funded by HF which targeted 827 patients from the MoH waiting lists in 2018-2019. By the implementation of this project the No. of beneficiaries from surgical op. services at UHWC’s facilities will be increased from 5494 in 2018 to 6994 in 2020.
Upon the importance of involving the local community in determining the needs & priorities, the UHWC held 5 FGs in 2019 targeting different categories of gender & age groups. The result of these FGs indicated that there is a need for the patients who are registered on the waiting lists at MoH to undergo surgical operations in order to reduce their suffering & prevent complications.
Another 5 FGs will be held in the mid of the project to evaluate the first phase in order to adjust the project activities if any deviations are found in achieving the objectives. Moreover, 5 FGs will be held at the end of the project targeting beneficiaries form the project with different gender & age groups including PWD to assess the project activities & measure immediate impact on them. While 100 questionnaires will be distributed to the beneficiaries to measure their satisfaction. This feedback will be used as direction to design future interventions.
There is also complaint boxes in each health facility to receive the beneficiaries’ complaints & to take the necessary action to address them.
To achieve the principle of protection, the project will target different age groups, including boys, girls, men & women, as well as elderly & PWD, without discrimination. To respond to gender needs, medical staff of both sexes (M & F) will be available. Moreover, pediatric surgeons will be available to respond to the age needs. At the level of PWD, the environment of Al Awda Hospital is prepared to receive them.
To meet the principle of (DO No Harm), the cases will be targeted upon the list that will be sent by the MoH, where the project will ensure that the beneficiaries are not harmed during the provision of the services, by providing quality health services under the supervision of highly qualified specialists. Adding to providing stable psychological situation for the beneficiaries by the provision of psychological support pre & post-op. A special guide pre & post-op will be distributed to all cases. To achieve the highest level of Do No Harm, a training course will be held for 12 persons from the medical staff in order to train them on the Sphere Standards related to health services & the principle of Do No Harm.
To reach accurate & easily accessible information, HIS system will be used, as UHWC is currently working on designing it & by 2020 it will be operational. In terms of documenting project activities, photography & audio-visual report will be prepared taking into consideration the dignity & privacy of patients.
It is worth mentioning that the activities of this humanitarian intervention has been designed according to Sphere Standards.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2020 indicated that there are 9,000 patients in 2019 in need of surgical operations scheduled on the waiting lists at the MoH while 50% of them are expected to suffer from deteriorating in their health status. Knowing that the HC is planning to target 4500 patients in 2020.Upon direct contact with the Head of Coordination Unit with NGOs at MoH, he stressed the importance of the project as it meets a basic and important need for the community and the MoH in term of reducing the suffering of patients and the burden on the MoH. He also stressed that more than 2500 cases are scheduled on waiting lists in need of ENT surgical operations and they have to wait for their role for more than 16 months, adding to the urgent need for surgery from other specialties in particular urology and pediatric surgery. Furthermore, the idea of the project was discussed with the Health Cluster Coordinator, who stressed the importance of this intervention as it meets a vital need in the health sector. In line with the abovementioned, the UHWC has held 5 Focus Groups (FGs) in 5 governorates of the Gaza Strip targeting different gender and age groups in order to identify the most important priorities of the community in the health issues. The participants of these groups stressed that surgical operations of patients scheduled at the MoH waiting list is one of their priorities in order to alleviate the suffering of patients and reduce the complications of the disease. Although the UHWC has implemented a project funded by HF, which ended in 4/2019, during which 827 surgeries were carried out targeting patients from the waiting lists in the Northern Gaza Strip, and at the same time other institutions carried out similar projects, there are still 9000 patients registered on the waiting lists at the MoH, as the MoH suffers from a series of crises related to the severe shortage of health resources such as medications where the zero stock exceeded 44% and medical consumables zero items reached 28% (MoH report 9/2019). This is in addition to other crises suffered by the MoH, such as the electricity crisis and fuel needed to run generators in addition to the disruption of medical devices and the difficulty of repair, adding to the crisis of human resources resulting from the early financial retirement imposed by the Palestinian Authority on public sector’s employees in the Gaza Strip. Hence, this crisis will be further exacerbated by the year 2020, especially in connection with the continuation of the Great Return Marches and the preoccupation of the Ministry of Health facilities in providing emergency health services to the trauma cases. The increase in waiting time for patients in need of surgical interventions increases their suffering and pain, beside the likelihood of having complications due to delay in conducting the needed surgical operations, especially on the category of children with ENT health problems, as the delay in conducting such surgical operations will contributes to educational difficulties for school children due to their reduced hearing level. The project will take into consideration the dignity of the beneficiaries during receiving the services on the one hand and it adopts the principle of equality and non-discrimination during the selection of the beneficiaries on the other hand, as the project will be implemented in coordination with the MoH and according to the priority of each case and the period of waiting and its seriousness.

2 - Activities

The project activities are in line with the UHWC's philosophy based on its slogan (Health Service is a right to whoever needs it). It is also in line with the 1st HC SO & the 2nd HRP SO. The project will expansion the No. of UHWC surgical beneficiaries from 5494 in 2018 to 6994 in 2020, an increase of 1500 elective surgeries will be provided completely free of charge for patients who are on the waiting lists & transferred from the MoH to Al-Awda hospital in the North, and Al-Awda health center in the South. Project activities will be broken down as follows: 1st: coordination and complementarity (one of the core humanitarian standards – Sphere standards): One of the core humanitarian standards within the Sphere Standers is the coordination and complementarity during the delivery of humanitarian assistance, therefore the project includes the following measures: - Coordination with MoH and HC Coordinator at the needs assessment stage has been done. - Coordination also will take place with the MoH to send us the lists on a regular monthly basis. 2nd: Building local capacities for more effective humanitarian response (one of the core humanitarian standards- Sphere Standards): To ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed while receiving services on the basis of dignity & protection, 12 medical staff from both sexes will be trained in Sphere standards and Do No Harm through 30 training hours (5 days*6 hours/day), to create a positive deviation in the behavior of workers while providing health services to beneficiaries in a positive manner and away from any discrimination. This training impact will be sustained to benefit other beneficiaries beyond the project duration. 3rd: Providing elective surgeries in effective and timely manner (one of the core humanitarian standards- Sphere Standards): 1500 moderate and major surgical services will be provided to the beneficiaries free of charge including boys, girls, men, women, the elderly and PWD, according to the lists received from the MoH on a monthly basis. The surgical operations will be conducted in 2 of UHWC’s facilities: - Al Awda Hospital – Jabalia - Al Awda Health Center – Rafah. The surgical operations services include: • Medical examination by specialists. • Pre and post-op investigations • Surgical operations. • Hospitalization. • Dispensing of necessary medications to patients after discharge. • Follow up. • Providing psychological support to patients pre & post-op, especially for children. 4th: Community participation on the bases of communication, participation, feedback and complaints (one of the core humanitarian standards- Sphere Standards): This will be achieved through the following: - 5 FGs contributed to the identification of needs - 10 FGs (10 people in each group 50% of whom are females in addition to PWD) will contribute to the project’s follow-up & evaluation. - UHWC will be accountable to the beneficiaries by informing them properly about the project and by receiving their feedback on staff performance and quality of services by filling out 100 satisfaction questionnaires. - Upon the importance of dealing with the complaints of beneficiaries and benefiting from their observations, the UHWC distributed complaints boxes in each center & deal with it on daily basis where response is given to each complaint. 5th: Documenting Project Activities taking into account the dignity and privacy of patients (one of the core humanitarian standards- Sphere Standards): Documentation of project’s activities will be done through: - Photography & audio-visual report by ensuring respect of the beneficiaries’ privacy and dignity. - The number & names of the project’s beneficiaries will be documented in HIS and the information will be disaggregated by age and gender & will be shared with the MoH and the HC. - Mid-term & final financial & narrative reports will be submitted to the donor.

3 - Indicators

The project Indicators: # of patients benefitted from provision of elective surgery. # of patients benefitted from provision of psychological support pre and post the surgical operations. % of patients satisfaction about the provided services at Al Awda Hospital # of trained staff in Sphere standards and Do No Harm. Monitoring and Evaluation mechanism: UHWC will follow up and monitor the project activities through methodologies and tools to ensure the progress of activities according to the executive and financial plan and through the use of the standards of quality, performance and efficiency. Once the project approved, the project coordinator will conduct a meeting with the project staff where he/she will present the project activities, goals, indicators and results to the project staff to prepare action plan and to distribute responsibilities and duties. The project team will also document the project activities in coordination with the Public relations department through photos and audio-visual report that will be published at the UHWC website, face book and YouTube channel at the end of the project. The project coordinator will follow up and monitor activities on the ground on a daily basis through direct contact with the project team. UHWC project coordinator will conduct regular field visits to the project location to insure continuous flow of information on the progress of activities, and to see work on the ground. Monthly reports and statistics will be sent from the field to the project coordinator via HIS report where all statistics will be desegregated according to age and gender. UHWC project department will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. The Project coordinator will compare the financial plan with the real expenses as well as action plan with the achieved activities in order to know that we are on the right track. UHWC gives special attention to the participation of beneficiaries in the identifying, designing, implementing and evaluation of their services in order to gain their satisfaction through the provision of high quality services, in addition to achieving the accountability and transparency. Therefore, the project staff will conduct 10 focus groups to measure the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and to have their feedback about the provided services. Where UHWC will share the information of the project with beneficiaries through its website and facebook page. Also, UHWC is following complaints system in its facilities, there are complain boxes inside UHWC facilities where the management investigate any complaint and meet with the beneficiaries to solve any problem, in addition UHWC will use questionnaires to evaluate the project services and to know the patients satisfaction, suggestions and feedback.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Upon the importance of the principle of Do No Harm while providing services to our beneficiaries, the UHWC strives to train its health staff in the principles of humanitarian action, which is consistent with the principles of international human rights law, including the right to health. The UHWC has signed on the NDC Code of Conduct for NGOs, moreover it has its own bylaw which upon the UHWC is committed to the following principles:1. Respect and commitment to the laws.2. Development and sustainability priorities.3. Participation.4. Transparency.5. Accountability.6. Integrity.7. Prevent conflicts of interest.8. Justice, equality and inclusiveness.9. Good governance.10. Efficiency and effectiveness. The project will ensure that the beneficiaries are not harmed during the provision of the services, by providing quality health services under the supervision of highly qualified specialists. Within this intervention UHWC is planning to conduct a training course for the employees in the Sphere Standards mainly the standards related to health services in addition to Do No Harm principle in order to emphasize the positive behaviors of the staff during the performance of their humanitarian duty to provide health services to patients. This in addition to the recruitment of 2 psychologists to ensure the provision of psychological support to patients’ pre and post the surgical operations, in order avoid any psychological harm to them. UHWC is having control dept. to ensure that the employees’ positions are not exploited to achieve special gains at the expense of beneficiaries, as they have to deal impartially with all beneficiaries during the provision of the services. Ongoing guidance is given to staff to report improper behaviors on the part of their colleagues towards beneficiaries in order to modify the behavior and hold accountable any employee who deals with the beneficiaries in a manner that touch their dignity and privacy. The UHWC has a strict confidentiality policy regarding the patient’s information and no information can be disclosed from patient files.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The UHWC adopts the principle of equality and impartially in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups that need humanitarian interventions to improve their health and humanitarian status. It provides its services without any discrimination on the basis of age, sex, religion, ethic, race or political affiliation, in order to achieve the principle of equality. Knowing that through this project the UHWC will receive the lists of beneficiaries from the Ministry of Health. And to achieve the principle of impartiality in the provision of services UHWC will adapt those lists and will provide the surgical services to all those mentioned in the lists on the basis that each patient has its individual characteristics and special needs, especially individuals with disabilities, children and women as the most vulnerable in the society and who need special attention and protection depend on Human dignity, confidentiality and non-discrimination.
