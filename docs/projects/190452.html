Project Summary

As September 2021, the Ethiopia National Displacement Report 10, highlighted an increase to 4,239,636 displaced individuals nationwide due to several factors, such as conflict (85%), drought (7%) and seasonal displacement (3%). The MYR reported 14.8m of people in need in Ethiopia, without including Tigray. In 2021, the triggers of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia were armed conflict, inter-communal tensions, climatic shocks, desertlocusts and disease outbreaks. In 2022, the humanitarian situation for Ethiopia is still characterized by severe shocks, including the conflict in north of the country, drought conditions in southern and southeastern pastoral areas, and persisting inflation (34.5%in January 2022). According to the strategy paper of CCCM cluster, the conflict in the Tigray region, and the spill over into Amhara and Afar regions has resulted in more than half of the increase of displacements figures, and it could be leading to a long-term, protracted crisis, which requires a strengthened protection in affected areas. COOPI is operational since 1995 in Ethiopia. Currently is present in Oromia, Somali, Benishangul Gumuz, Ahmara, Afar, Tigray and Addis Abeba, implementing programmes of development and emergency in WASH, Shelter&NFI, FSL and Energy. This project proposal will provide support in CCCM in the areas where COOPI is operational. The proposal is built around the strategy of the cluster composed by the following pillars:
1. Camp/Area Coordination, including Information Management; 2. Camp Maintenance & Improvement, Decommissioning including Rehabilitation; 3. Community Participation, Mobilization/Self‐Governance; 3.
Under those pillars, the project will:
• Camp/Area Coordination and Information Management, including meetings organized at displacement locations, producing and sharing information products, in order to request and coordinate for assistance delivery.
• Camp Maintenance & Improvement, Decommissioning including Rehabilitation, will include site assessments, partitioning of communal living spaces; installation of communal facilities, partitioning of rooms to improve privacy in communal shelter, support the implementation of the safety, security measures and ensure dignity of targeted IDPs;
• Community Participation, Mobilization/Self-Governance, including through support of community self-governance structures, running of a Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM), mainstreaming protection, and awareness raising.
• Capacity Building of key stakeholders (for staff, authorities, partners and other stakeholders) on key CCCM concepts and protection mainstreaming.
The possible areas of intervention proposed by COOPI are: Ahmara, Afar, Oromia, Somali. COOPI has pre-identified Zones and Woredas in those regions; however, it can shift rapidly the activities in other areas in the same regions or in Benishangul Gumuz, currently reporting no targets but likely to need this kind of support in the short run. The actual implementation of the activities will be based on the agreements with the Cluster. Continuous training of COOPI staff will be assured through the deep engagement with the cluster. The project will be integrated with possible intervention in Shelter & NFI as well as WASH considering COOPI presence and expertise in those areas.
Finally, considering the nature of the activities, COOPI will be stricly coordinate with Authorities at National, Regional, Zonal, Woreda level by: ensuring the preparation of MoU stating roles and responsibilities and terms of the collaboration; organizing regularly coordination meetings and ad-hoc meetings; ensuring their meaningful participation in the capacity building activities planned under this project.

1. - Projects activities

The following activities will be carried out for the supporting access to appropriate service in accountable manner:1. Support coordination with relevant stakeholders (woredas, local authorities, partners) through the organization of regular meetings at local level. The project will involve community self-governance structures (Site/Kebele Management/Coordination Committees, Women’s Committees, Youth Committees, etc.) through training, community mobilization events, consultations, and liaison between beneficiaries and local authorities.2. Set up a functional Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) as an Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) channel for beneficiaries of COOPI and other stakeholders involved in the response. The complaint and feedback will be recorded, analyzed, and followed up. They will be also used to inform advocacy at sub-national and national levels. 3. Monitoring and information management activities, including mapping partners presence, monitoring of service quality provision in sites compared to humanitarian standards (e.g. SPHERE); preparation of monthly site profiles providing information on level of response and gaps.Under site planning/maintenance and Upgrades the following activies are foreseen:1.Site feasibility assessments (together with multi-sectoral teams); partitioning of communal living spaces; installation of communal facilities such as kitchens and distribution points; fire safety measures; drainage decongestion of living spaces, decommissioning/restoration of site after IDPs vacate the site, installation of hand washing facilities for COVID-19 response, among others. COOPI will focus on addressing protection risks, reducing the risk of gender-based violence (GBV), such as through the installation of solar street lighting, or supporting a proper disaggregation of sanitation facilities in the site. Under the Capacity Building of key stakeholders, the project will promote the knowledge and understanding of the CCCM concepts and best practices in camp-like settings, including protection mainstreaming. This activity will be coordinated with the CCCM Cluster. The topics covered will be: •Roles and responsibilities of Site Management and Coordination in sites•Protection mainstreaming and its application in in Site Management and Coordination;Tools to improve Infection Prevention and Control in the context of COVID-19 and other communicable disease outbreaks.Under capacity building, COOPI staff will continuosly be trained with the support of CCCM Cluster.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

COOPI adhere to the humanitarian accountability principles and all COOPI Ethiopia staff is trained of the 5 commitments of the Accountability of the Affected Population. COOPI will make sure that benefi ciaries know they have a right to equitable and safeassistance, where and how to obtain it.Following COOPI Standard Operating Procedures, to ensure informed decisions COOPI staff will involve partners, communities, local authorities, beneficiaries and especially vulnerable groups in the planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating of this project. This includes engaging beneficiaries at the design stage by conducting focus group discussions and individual interviews with both men and women in various age groups, including elderly and people with disabilities. During the action, beneficiaries at all levels will take a leading role in the formation/consolidation of the committees described in the proposal (committees), who jointly with local authorities and project staff, will co-lead in the selection of the recipients and in the monitoring of the action. COOPI will facilitate opportunities for women, people with disability and elderly to hold positions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committees. An accessible, well understood and confidential mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions and complaints, with a clear follow-up system on all reports.COOPI’s feedback and complaint mechanism includes information sharing on: Project objectives, outcomes and outputs; implementing agency and donor; selection criteria and mechanisms to report issues; entitlements under this project. COOPI Project management team will strengthen local capacities, foster local knowledge and community coping strategies, as well as enhance protection mechanisms providing where possible psycho-social services through referral mechanism. Due to the volatility of security conditions and their impact in terms of protection and well-being of beneficiaries, COOPI has included in its staff: Head of Operations, in charge of monitoring safety and security; Protection focal points to timely report alerts and issues that may affect the target population as well as advise on measures to be taken. Particular attention will be provided on PSEA. COOPI will provide training on its internal policies to the project staff and main stakeholders. The policy includes the concepts related with PSEA, preventive measures, referral mechanisms as well as feedback and complaints mechanisms. Besides that, COOPI will brief the staff also using the Ethiopia PSEA Network Training Package. COOPI willset up a feedback and complain mechanism to allow beneficiaries’ participation,improving monitoring and accountability reporting and promptly adjust the implementation of the project. Mechanisms to report issues will be done through:•Provision to local stakeholders of main contacts for COOPI staff (phone, email and office address),especially for the SMT program staff • Provision of help-desk contact(help@coopi.org) • Establishment of complaints and feedback box and notice panel at the field (depending on level of literacy of beneficiaries)• Participatory selection of focal points at community level, for oral complaints/feedback.COOPI will record all possible complaints and feedbacks. According to the severity of the issue COOPI SMT will respond, in at least 4 days, involving different staff at different level:• Minor complaints/ request of information: social promoters/wash advisor/emergency lead.•Major complaints: Programme Manager/Emergency ResponseLead/CountryAdministrator/Head of Mission/HQ
