Project Summary

This project design will be specifically targeting the communities and IDPs sites that are at high risk of cholera outbreaks that was because, In Syria, nearly two-thirds of water treatment plants, half of all pumping stations, and one-third of water towers have been damaged as a result of the conflict. Water networks are unable to provide full water quantities to the population due to the lack of a stable power supply, the high cost of diesel to operate the power generating systems and fixed budgets, and the prolonged drought. Faced with acute shortages in safe, public drinking water, vulnerable households must increasingly rely on costly water supplied by private vendors who truck potentially unsafe water to neighborhoods and communities. Consequently, nearly half of people rely on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs (vis-à-vis 34% in mid-2021). In addition, at least 70 percent of the discharged sewage is untreated, and at least half of the sewage system is not functional. When access to water is limited, hygiene practices are poor, and adequate sanitation is not available, the risk of disease outbreaks increases. And this project was designed to overcome the current gaps through the following suggested activities:
1. IYD will assign a specialist team across NWS; this team consists of 10 female and ten male staff to Support the training of key actors in the communities, IDP sites, health centers, and CTCs on WASH AWD/cholera response and prevention.
2. Targeting 60 HCs, CTCs, and CTUs across NWS In collaboration with Health and RCCE, support the deployment of integrated multisector rapid response teams to enable a timely response to the report of AWD/cholera.
3. five communities are located in (Dana, Atareb, Azaz, Jarablus, and Idleb sub-districts) in the prioritized locations based on the 2022 HRP.
4. 35 camps are located in four main sub-district (Idleb, Marrat masrin, and Dana ); IYD has proposed Dana and Jarablus sub-districts to thirty-five camps in significant need of of water and sanitation networks to Ensure safe excreta disposal in at-risk IDP camps/sites and other high-risk areas, in addition to a renewable system to operate the water station inside the camp.
5. Connecting ten camps from the 35 camps above to the existing water network and sewerage network to provide a sustainable solution
6. Support systematic and relevant water quality indicator monitoring and reporting in high-risk areas and healthcare facilities.
7. Providing O&M support for eight water systems to Ensure access and the use of safe drinking water to at-risk populations at water collection points, households, and healthcare facilities.
8. Provide a basic WASH emergency package to schools affected by AWD/cholera and in high-risk areas.
9. Provide and distribute essential WASH supplies to affected and at-risk populations (for example, cholera kits to affected households and neighbors, soap in at-risk IDP camps/sites)
10. Implement emergency solid waste management action in high-risk areas.
11. Conduct capacity-building activities with WASH focal points and staff working at treatment centers and healthcare facilities on WASH interventions during cholera outbreaks.

3 - Needs

the need assessment conducted by several organization including IYD observed the following findings related to water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion sector: according to the KII responses in the assessed locations, the primary water source reported was the distribution of water by NGOs (64%), followed by purchasing water (21%), and finally through piped water to dwellings (14%). In the assessed locations, 18% of respondents reported challenges faced by women in accessing water while the remaining 82% of respondents stated that there are no challenges in accessing water. Similar results were reported for men with only 16% of respondents reporting challenges in accessing water. When asked to explain the barriers in accessing water, 45% noted the distance to an available source of water was too far, 33% reported high prices, 15% highlighted an absence of any available water sources while 7% mentioned a lack of household water storage capacity. Respondents also discussed the availability of latrines for families in the assessed areas. Regarding WASH services provided in the assessed locations during the last three months, 38% reported that water has been provided, 23% stated solid waste management services were in place, 20% highlighted that sanitation services have been provided, 10% stated that no WASH services were available while 8% report that hygiene kits distributions and hygiene promotion interventions were conducted.

Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly.Mitigation:• Identifying the target community as an accessible area where the needs of the affected population are the highest.• IYD will use existing mapping and coordinate at the local level with other early recovery actors to ensure balanced coverage responding to needs; avoid overlapping or concentration of activities.Risk 2: Tension in the community for perceived inequality, discrimination, and favoritism, including risk for project staff.Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly.Mitigation:• Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish these criteria. • Follow Guidelines adopted by the CFW actors and ensure that they are properly applied in the project location. •Avoid the exclusion from the assistance of persons with no identity documents by adopting alternative mechanisms for identification.Risk 3: The backlash from familyor community on women participating in project activities and preventing them from traveling to other areas and accessing services.Likelihood: low. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IYD has adopted the inter-agency Code of Conduct, Which condemns the SEA (Endorsed by the Humanitarian Liaison Group - 2020) for the Syria cross-border response, which is intended for all staff and representatives of humanitarian organizations as well as their associated contractors, and volunteers•IYD is an active member of the PSEA network, and it is already familiar with the network’s monthly reporting tool where IYD reports its PSEA monthly activities such as (the number of beneficiaries reached by the distribution of awareness-raising materials, the number of beneficiaries reached by providing PSEA awareness-raising sessions, the number of field staff with direct interaction with beneficiaries who trained on PSEA)•IYD has established an internal SEA reporting and investigative mechanism, which will not tolerate an atmosphere of impunity toward SEA. Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct•Complete PSEA Checklists: The organizational checklist was completed by the management and the HR unit in communication with the CEO. Also, the Field Observation Checklist for PSEA Mainstreaming will be completed by IYD's program managers, who will utilize these tools in all our interventions during the implementation and monitoring stages to identify gaps and risks related to PSEA


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the project leading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. However, IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge • Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up with the service providers day by day to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them. • Social risks: From a social standpoint, this project presents no risk to the IYD and local communities. Although women are still underrepresented in decision-making bodies, their concerns have, over time, been taken into account, and this project will seek to ensure their better presenceand representation. •Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas and deterioration of the security situation. Has a strong relationship with neutral communities’ leaders and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities orIYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up on the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety •
