Project Summary

The objective of the project is to support at risk and vulnerable communities with Protection and Child Protection services, through addressing root causes of protection risks and threats and supporting development of positive coping mechanisms. Through provision of structured and sustained group psychosocial support activities, aimed at rebuilding and/or enhancing resilience, the psychosocial wellbeing of children and women in particular will be improved. This includes support groups to rebuild or enhance support networks specifically for women (at risk) and older persons without support networks. Through awareness raising, based on community-identified priorities and needs, DCA works to enhance knowledge and understanding of rights, and aims to reduce prevalence of protection risks by addressing behavioural, social, and cultural practices. Targeted support to respond to individual needs and exposure to risks/threats will be provided through case management, including referral to enhance access to services as well as specialized support (GBV, MHPSS, HLP, and cross-sectoral). DCA will maintain its static facilities and employ mobile approaches to enhance coverage to informal settlements, and communities out of reach of DCA’s Community Safe Spaces in Menbij, Kobane, and Ar-Raqqa. DCA will continue to include persons with disabilities (PWD), including children, in its activities provided in said spaces. DCA used spaces have floor level access to facilitate attendance by persons using mobility devices. Extensive training of staff conducted throughout DCA’s operational presence, on a variety of mental health disorders allows dedicated support to individuals with cognitive disabilities. Where possible, individuals with visual or hearing impairments will be included with adjusted communication methodologies used, and DCA will attempt to support in providing required assistive devices where possible through coordination with other actors or as part of individual support interventions.
All interventions implemented by DCA are monitored by an independent MEAL team, using a variety of tools. Group activities make use of attendance record-keeping, as well as pre-post surveys (such as wellbeing for PSS, or knowledge-assessment questionnaires for information dissemination sessions). Further, every DCA activity participant is provided the voluntary opportunity to partake in a so-called Satisfaction Survey conducted by MEAL team staff. These surveys aim to further understand value-add of activities, perceived sustainability of gained information/services as relevant to the specific intervention methodology, as well as staff conduct, service accessibility, exclusion, and safety. DCA adheres to data protection protocols, as stipulated in its dedicated SOP. Individual assistance modalities, such as case management, are guided by senior Protection staff with various levels of approvals required. The system also makes use of systematic record keeping, ad hoc visits by mid-level (supervisory) staff where case specifications allow and based on consent provided participation in specific surveys conducted by MEAL team staff.

3 - Needs

According to HNO-23 draft, continuous exposure to various forms of violence, deepening poverty, and limited or lack of access to services results in heightened likelihood- and impact of adapting negative coping mechanisms (Draft HNO-23, 31 Oct. 22, p. 58). DCA assessments (Raqqa rural areas, Oct. and Dec. 22) observations and coordination, notes school drop-out prevalence due to lack of schooling available, or HH income gaps requiring additional sources of income. 48% of families with school-aged children do not let their children attend school to support the latter (HNO-23, p. 30). DCA notes child marriage prevalence throughout NES operational presence, reported in 78% of communities (idem, p. 31). Heightened stress of the living environment impacts mental health and may increase domestic violence, other instances of GBV, and child abuse. Women and girls are already highly affected by violence, human rights violations, and discriminatory cultural practices (idem, p. 15&32). DCA assessments see this heightens perceptions of unsafety and hopelessness. Children are especially susceptible. Lack of recreational activities for adolescents (boys and girls alike) is consistently noted to DCA. Some behaviours such as harassment are prevalent and socially accepted (idem, p. 57). Limited positive coping strategies appear known or implemented by adults to deal with their own stress. Coping strategies include corporal punishment, as by caregivers’ own admission to DCA.

4 - Implementation of activities

Provision of protection and child protection services, with a rights- and needs-based approach. Through proactive community engagement and coordination with governmental and non-governmental institutions, DCA will determine priority topics for awareness sessions. These focus on protection and child protection issues in underserved areas and locations. A curriculum of 10 structured and sustained psychosocial support sessions, spanning 2-3 months, will be implemented for children aged 6-12. Through recreational activities, such as art, music, and knitting, adolescents, women, and elderly in particular will enhance their support networks. A core component of these sessions is linked to awareness raising in static structures and provides a safe environment to discuss shared challenges and solutions. Individual case management will focus on children (CPCM) and adults, ensuring identification of root causes of experienced risks and threats. Case plans will prioritise sustainable solutions, with CVA and in-kind support to facilitate this. Identification of individuals in need of specialized support will be conducted throughout all activities, enhancing access to services through referrals. Through independent monitoring and feedback, led by DCA MEAL teams – overall quality of services will be assessed throughout the implementation phase. This, in combination with community feedback session as well as noting of ad hoc feedback will further feed into project adjustment and design.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of intra- and inter (neighbouring) community tensions resulting from service delivery reaching a limited number of individuals/communities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: Ensure community outreach explaining services and criteria as relevant prior to activity start, ensure active engagement with communities for early warning purposes, ensure engagement of individuals in decisions relating to modalities. Protection Risk: Lack of acceptance of topics addressed during service delivery, resulting in backlash against participants.Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: Ensuring two-way communication with affected populations, with clear requests for and noting of perceptions and potential reactions and adjusting messaging in a consultative manner. Protection Risk: Extremely vulnerable or marginalized groups are unable to access services, either physical access or through lack of knowledge of services.Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation Measures: Ensure coordination to ensure access to community-level mappings, with other agencies and community representatives to have up to date information on present population groups with AGD disaggregated figures. Ensure programme design includes support for access to services outside of individual assistance intervention modalities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA Staff members Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation Measures: All DCA Project staff will be regularly trained on DCA Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding, and PSEA, including reporting mechanisms. All staff will conduct introductory sessions with clear messaging on PSEA and CFM mechanisms. These messages will include specific notes on the non-reciprocal nature of assistance. All group activities are conducted with a minimum of two facilitating staff members, where culturally appropriate one of each sex. DCA is a member of the PSEA Network, and through the network will be continually updated on new/revised messaging, best practices, and reporting mechanisms. DCA PSEA FP will ensure dissemination of messaging to all project staff and management. Ensuring presence of a complaint box in static structures and appropriate systems in mobile units.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in locations impact project implementation causing delays and changes in operational areas and threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: DCA has robust safety protocols and SOPs, including designated staff in each area of operation who actively monitors the security situation. The management team remains flexible, and changes project locations and modalities as and when required from a safety and overall needs perspective. DCA’s Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are regularly reviewed, and last comprehensive update was conducted in September 2022. If the security and access situation deteriorate, staff and beneficiary will prevail. Any pull out from programme areas will be determined on the basis of a simultaneous protection risk analysis, including assessment of risks in conducting the best practice of informing populations of intervention pause/end. Risk: Inability to conduct group activities/closure of CSSs due to COVID-19 or other air- or vector-borne disease infection prevention control measures. Impact would be delays and decreased access. Mitigation measures: DCA has a previously developed online module for children and caregivers. This will be reviewed to ensure continued relevance but is ready to be implemented as is. Access for beneficiaries would be reliant on internet connectivity, and availability to platforms such as Zoom and WhatsApp.
