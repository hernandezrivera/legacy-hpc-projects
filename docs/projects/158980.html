Project Summary

The planned action is in line with the strategic and cluster objectives SO2, SO3 and cluster objective 1 CO1 and CO2 respectively. Recent needs assessments and Focus Group Discussions with all local municipalities in the Access Restricted Area (ARA) informed PUI about the urgent needs for shelter interventions due to recurrent natural hazards notably flooding, the severe deterioration in the socioeconomic situation, and the Gaza protracted crisis in general. Therefore, PUI will prioritize its intervention to protect households who are at risks of floods through urgent winterization assistance and urgent climate protection support taking into consideration the specific needs of the affected population (i.e. women, girls, boys, people with disabilities etc.). At emerging needs level, PUI will focus the intervention to target households who were evicted from their homes or at risk of eviction through temporary shelter assistance with focus on most vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, widows, divorced etc. PUI will also target households with limited economic means who live in inadequate shelter who do not have the minimum required shelter standards through the rehabilitation of their houses. Protection threats combined with the absence of adequate shelters are particular hard on the most vulnerable groups such as women, elderly, children and PwDs, which the project will take into consideration by including a diverse representation of communities in all stages of the project. Especially due to an often sharp gendered division in housework, women’s participation in all phases of the programming will be essential as well as the focus on special needs of particular groups (e.g. the household’s different needs of space for privacy and socializing to ensure children’s possibilities of finding space for studying). PUI will also provide targeted beneficiaries with Housing, Land & Property (HLP) awareness sessions in collaboration with PCHR. The proposed action is part of a multi-year program. Over two years, the program will be implemented with the same activities, but including new beneficiaries and possibly new implementation areas. PUI coordinated the planned action with the Shelter Cluster and key actors working in the area of intervention.

1 - Needs

Based on the needs assessment conducted by PUI in 14 municipalities located in the Access Restricted Areas (ARAs) in September 2018, flooding of houses covered by asbestos and metal sheet was one of main challenges faced by the population living in these communities during the winter season. In September 2018, PUI conducted FGDs with households who were evicted or at high risk of eviction from rented houses due to the deteriorated socioeconomic situation in Gaza Strip. 49 evicted households from 5 localities (Abbassan Al Kabira, Khuza’a, Al Fukhari, Wadi as Salqa, and Al Shouka) participated in the discussions. The main common challenges were reported as follows: unsuitable living conditions in the temporary shelter (i.e. no privacy, overcrowding, low level of hygiene); households felt as an extra burden for the host families; and increased level of domestic violence and tensions inside the families, leading to divorce in some cases. In addition, PUI carried out meetings with the MoSD and the MoPWH to explore the most relevant response to support this emerging vulnerable group and to obtain official statistics. A list of 160 households evicted or at risk of eviction was shared by MoPWH and showed that more than 2,000 households were at high risk of eviction according to the Ministry’s estimations. Moreover, PUI carried out a needs assessment of 650 substandard housing units in Wadi as Salqa, Abbassan Al Kabira, Khuza’a, Al Fukhari, Al Shouka, Bani Suhaila, Auraiba, and Gizan Abu Rashwan during April and September 2018. The main findings emphasized the need for urgent winterization repair interventions where 93% of the assessed shelters were affected by floods during the last winter storms. In addition, 98% of the housing units are very vulnerable to floods and other extreme winter condition as the worn-out roofs, windows, and/or doors allow the leakage of rain water into houses according to the technical assessment. Moreover, 58% of the households indicated that their houses are hot in summer and very cold in winter. 61% of the houses do not have solid lockable doors into toilet/shower. 69% of the houses do not have at least one room/bedroom with solid lockable door. 57% of the housing units are overcrowded (i.e. the covered space per person is below the minimum standard (5.5 m2) determined by the shelter cluster). 80% of the houses do not have toilet with flushing system, nor marks of leakage, with openable window or a mechanical ventilation system. The first priority intervention for most of the households is to be protected against exposure to extreme winter conditions in both winter and summer. Main problems faced by women are the lack of privacy and the small size of kitchens. For children, problems of overcrowding and light affect their preparations for studies. Finally, all visited households lacked essential NFIs and are in need of immediate winterization repairs and upgrading of their houses. During 2018, PUI upgraded 115 substandard housing units in the Southern Governorates through different funding sources. PUI collaborated with NRC and PCHR on providing targeted beneficiaries with awareness sessions on HLP. The pre- and post-tests showed a concrete need for better understanding of land tenure issues and legislative and customary rights.While PUI will receive 2019 HPF funding to assist around 300 households through winterization repairs and 300 households through temporary assistance among the 677 assessed housing units categorized as substandard and houses, recent primary findings from the data analysis of the MoPWH of its ongoing assessment of 23,766 substandard housing units showed that 9,984 need the rehabilitation of roof, 10,193 need the rehabilitation of windows and 3,864 need shelter adaptation for PwDs. The needs are therefore still high compared to available funding opportunities.

2 - Activities

PUI will give priority to urgent needs such as plastic sheets, NFIs, winter clothes and winterization repairs for households affected by harsh weather and those under protection threats, particularly for evicted individuals or at risk of eviction. PUI will carry out the following activities to preserve the dignity and safety of the targeted beneficiaries and contribute to the protection of affected population:Activity 1: Provision of temporary shelter assistance to 50 evicted or at risk of eviction due to deteriorated socioeconomic situation households (285 individuals).Activity 2: Provision of urgent winterization assistance to 150 households (855 individuals) (i.e. plastic sheets, mattresses, mats, etc.).Activity 3: Provision of urgent climate protection support (repair works for roofs, windows and external doors) for 150 vulnerable households (855 individuals), who are the same households targeted through the activity 2 at risk of being affected by floods and extreme winter conditions.Activity 4: Rehabilitation of substandard housing units to 50 acutely vulnerable households (285 individuals). Households will be selected among those who will benefit from the climate protection interventions during 2019. This activity will be implemented through the owner-driven (self-help) approach. Special care will be given to groups with specific needs in regards to their use of the shelters both for privacy, socializing and work. Activity 5: Awareness session on HLP. PUI will target all its beneficiaries (250 individuals) through awareness sessions. In case of needs for legal assistance, PUI will refer the cases to other partners. PUI's implementing main partner will be responsible for the selection and follow up of civil engineers, participation in the beneficiary selection process, report on progress of activities, follow up feedback mechanism, update PUI’s on the challenges faced and lessons learnt during the implementation of activities and effectively contribute to M&E processes. Beneficiary selection and prioritization criteria that will be followed for identifying the target group for each type of assistance are as follow:A- Cash assistance to evicted people: an updated needs assessment will take place at the beginning of project, selection of beneficiaries will be based on a scoring system with socioeconomic criteria (accumulated debts, number of family members, number of special cases, sources of income, etc.).B- Winterization repairs and distribution of plastic sheets/NFIs: an updated technical assessment will take place at the beginning of the project, beneficiaries will be selected based on identified needs whether for winterization repairs, plastic sheets and or NFIs. There will be flexibility in the type of assistance, with possibility for mixed repair/winterization using sealing-off kits as well as protection mainstreaming activities such as selection of the NFIs according to the needs of the family with specific age/gender aspects. C- Upgrade of substandard housing units: selection of beneficiaries will be based on the scoring system of the highest scores for technical and socio-economic situation. Priority will be given to housing units that represent security and safety issues for the family members. For the different types of assistance mentioned above, priority will be given to women-, children-, elderly- and disabled- headed-households, households with PwDs, households who have people with chronic disease, households with pregnant/lactating women, households sharing the housing units with others (host families/IDPs), households that hosted IDPs in the previous conflicts, houses affected by previous conflicts or flooded in the winter storms and hardship cases identified by MoSD. PUI will take into account women, girls, boys, and men’s specific needs in the rehabilitation process, take into consideration the specific needs of PwDs and consult with all targeted beneficiaries about their preferences.

3 - Indicators

PUI M&E team will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels between the organizations (PUI and local partners). PUI will ensure regular updates on FTS and 4Ws systems. PUI will establish selection criteria based on the different vulnerabilities. Selection criteria will also include the ownership of the houses with official documents. In case PUI faces specific cases with ownership disputes, it will seek collaboration with other key legal actors such as NRC for legal aid and the Protection Cluster for protection threats. Introductory meetings with affected population and community representatives will be arranged to provide accessible and timely information on procedures, types of project activities, wages, beneficiary selection process and criteria and any other relevant information. Monthly monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. PUI will consult with targeted beneficiaries during the implementation of the action to adapt the upgrade works to the needs of the beneficiaries. Written report summarizing the main findings will be issued after each monitoring visit. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be set up, in collaboration with the local partner and CBOs, to make sure that the opinions of affected population are heard and taken into consideration. Complaints regarding the beneficiary selection or suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to. Individual written response letters are sent to all of those who submit feedback and complaint forms. Moreover, the contact details will handed out through complaint and feedback cards and the responsible officer will be available to all affected communities and their representatives so that they can communicate any complaints, objections, and feedback. Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results. Indicators: - Number of individuals (disaggregated between age, gender and PwD) who received temporary shelter support (Target value: 285). - Number of households provided with climate protection assistance (emergency shelter or household NFIs in-kind or in cash) (Target value: 150). - Number of households assisted to rehabilitate Substandard housing unit (Target value: 50).- 85 % of beneficiary households with improved knowledge on housing, land, and property.- 80% of beneficiaries reporting that assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible and participatory manner.- Number of assisted families reported improvement in living conditions and reduction in the seasonal movement and displacement (target: 250).

c - Complaint box

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries are involved in all aspects of the Action through the community-based protection approach that involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of vulnerabilities and needs. As soon as the action starts, PUI will meet with community representatives and all selected beneficiaries to inform them about the accepted project and explain how beneficiaries are expected to participate and contribute for the success of the action. This informative meeting will allow participants to inform PUI about their needs, aspiration and involve them in a participatory approach. All participants to this meeting will be informed about PUI Feedback & Complaint Mechanism (FCM). During those meetings PUI staff members will ensure to hand out information cards on the mechanism and explain about the FCM. PUI will emphasize that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI M&E department independent from project team and be dealt with in respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future.PUI will work with the communities and partners to ensure full transparency and to ensure a continuous information on the mechanism in the field. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards), in addition to all other available media of the organization. Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / partners interventions as well.The feedback is well studied by M&E department team and written answers are provided to clearly explain PUI’s position/decision on certain choices. When feedback and /or complaints draw the attention of management team about any potential improvement, a meeting is held between operation teams and management team to make the intervention more efficient and effective.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PUI/partners is fully committed to principles of do no harm, and promoting safety and dignity. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action with a special attention to provide legal information session on HLP rights. Through assessments, continuous evaluation and consultations with community representatives and involved participants as well as through the FCM. The targeted communities and beneficiaries will be actively involved in all phases of the Action and all feedback will be taken into account in best possible manners. The community-based protection approach involving CBOs and DPOs and participants of the Action is designed to ensure privacy, safety, dignity and accessibility for men and women, with or without disability and to contribute to the enhancement of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights. Lastly all involved staff is trained in PSEA policies and the action continuously engage in GBV mainstreaming by as an example having staff trained in identifying and referring GBV survivors as non-specialized actors. PUI ensures participatory approach at design phase, during implementation and the evaluation of its action. This contributes to ensuring that the action preserves the safety and dignity of the target beneficiaries. The action will be inclusive, as much as possible, in terms of age, gender, and diversity, and activities will take into consideration the specific needs of particular groups, whenever possible. The action is designed to assist the most acutely vulnerable households in the area of intervention through providing them urgent shelter assistance, winterization assistance, winterization repairs and with adequate shelters that meet shelter standards defined by the shelter cluster and ensuring access to a dignified life. This will improve the coping strategies with the psychological, social, and economic hardship and meet beneficiaries’ protection concerns such as mitigating harmful coping mechanism, harsh weather conditions, privacy and overcrowding. PUI’s feedback and complaint system will also contribute to the protection of beneficiaries, providing them a venue to freely express their concerns about the project.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is specifically designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI/partners will analyse possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with community representatives, CBOs, PDOs and beneficiaries with a specific focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneficiaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities by adapting the implementation to the specific needs of households. Specific vulnerabilities such as e.g female headed households’ participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved households and committees in order to tackle challenges and possible solutions.
