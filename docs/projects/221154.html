Project Summary

This intervention adopts an integrated, rights-based approach to address the protection and HLP needs of conflict- and displacement-affected communities in South Sudan. It aims to empower individuals and communities to identify and address their needs, reduce risks and vulnerabilities, and challenge systemic and socially reinforced exclusion, particularly of women, girls, and persons with disabilities. By promoting sustainable solutions and building resilience, the project prioritizes displaced and crisis-affected populations, including individuals with specific needs such as newly returned individuals, IDPs, asylum seekers, refugees, and host communities. Target groups include PWD, women, girls, the elderly, and other individuals disproportionately affected by crises. Grounded in conflict-sensitive analysis, the intervention integrates tailored protection and legal analyses to guide HDP nexus and area-based intervention that responds to the immediate needs of the persons at risk and prepare them for resilience. It will connect individuals to shelter, NFIs, and cash-based assistance through referrals to service providers in target locations. Core activities include protection and HLP monitoring, risk identification and screening for service provision, responsive information services, and targeted assistance delivery based on assessed needs. These efforts will be bolstered by area-based programming, ensuring effective referrals and coordinated service delivery in collaboration with humanitarian actors. IRC will strengthen its leadership in protection risk analysis through the Global Protection Cluster PAF and the piloting of its Legal and Justice Systems Analysis toolkit, currently ongoing in South Sudan. Complementary funding will support both local partners and IRC staff in implementing these approaches. To enhance community engagement, the intervention will employ diverse communication strategies, such as protection desks, FGDs, and risk communication campaigns. These efforts will leverage collective service mapping, participatory message design, and integrated messaging to ensure responsiveness to community needs. Services will include psychosocial support services, tailored individual assistance, high-quality referrals, and two-way accessible information services/ sharing and protection advocacy. The intervention will also leverage IRC’s ongoing Responsive Information Services in Emergencies (RISE) toolkit pilot, in targeted locations, to help individuals secure shelter, access medical care and water, reconnect with family, and rebuild their lives. It will also build on IRC’s contextualized PAF and LAF toolkit for protection and legal risk analysis. This project complements IRC’s ongoing programs in South Sudan, including initiatives in access to justice, child protection, WPE, health, economic recovery and development (ERD), and nutrition. IRC’s PROL team will collaborate with partners through the cluster system, including the Protection Working Group and Rule of Law Technical Working Group, to ensure coordinated responses, advocacy, and minimized service delivery overlaps. In Renk, Maban, and Panyijiar, IRC leads the Protection Working Group in partnership with UNHCR, facilitating coordination among protection actors. An age-, gender-, and diversity-sensitive approach will be maintained through community engagement at national, state, and camp levels. Activities will include FGDs, house-to-house visits, and interactions at protection and legal desks to address diverse protection needs. Partnerships with local organizations will amplify community voices and strengthen protection mechanisms. Joint assessments and coordination with UNHCR, the humanitarian community, and relevant working groups will ensure a strong protection focus. Regular safety audits, protection analyses, and responsive assessments will ensure intervention adapts to emerging community needs while promoting and sustainability. CCCM is implemented in Maban

1. Needs

1) Psychosocial Support Needs: Vulnerable groups—including refugees, returnees, host communities, and IDPs—experience acute psychological distress caused by conflict, displacement, recurrent floods, and disrupted livelihoods. Protracted crises and natural disasters have left many living in fear of future catastrophes, exacerbating feelings of helplessness, social fragmentation, and mental health challenges. 2) Critical Lifesaving Services and Assistance: Immediate access to safety, shelter, food, and responsive information on available services is essential for at-risk populations. Both cash-based and in-kind assistance are crucial to addressing urgent needs and reducing protection risks. PWDs face unique vulnerabilities, including limited mobility and inadequate service access, further isolating them and heightening stress. 3) Community Safety: People need to feel secure in their communities. Fragile and unpredictable conditions hinder freedom of movement and expose individuals to violence, exploitation, and overcrowding in transit centers, which often lack basic services. Tensions between returnees, refugees, and host communities over access to resources and cultural differences is a reality. Transit sites require urgent interventions to reduce prolonged risks and facilitate safe onward movement. Many returnees, particularly South Sudanese nationals (83%), express a desire to return to their areas of origin but face barriers such as limited funds, poor road conditions, and reluctance to relocate. 4) Responsive Information Services: Access to accurate, timely information is vital for refugees, returnees, and vulnerable groups to make informed decisions about available services, movements, HLP, and securing documentation for public services. 5) Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) Needs: Vulnerable populations—including returnees, refugees, and IDPs—require secure access to housing and land, support for reintegration, and stability for inform community resilience and recovery. Women’s land rights in ownership and allocation further complicate the HLP landscape and necessitate targeted interventions. 6) Civil Documentation: Securing civil documentation is critical to enabling access to public services, asserting legal rights, and supporting long-term stability for displaced populations returnees. 7) Protection and Justice Services: Enhanced access to protection and justice services is essential, including tailored emergency assistance, safe and effective referral systems, and responsive information mechanisms. Strengthened efforts to address human rights violations and provide legal support are paramount for mitigating risks and building resilience.

2. Project activities

Comprehensive and context-specific interventions will address protection risks and enhance the well-being of returnees, refugees, IDPs, and other vulnerable groups in targeted locations. 1) Protection and Legal Risk Analysis will be prioritized to assess evolving threats and inform programming through protection cluster platforms. These analyses will guide tailored responses in target areas and strengthen decision-making processes. IRC has been piloting and contextualizing GPC PAF and LAF toolkits and approaches in its programming, working with local partners since 2022. IRC will also actively participate in interagency assessments. 2) Protection and Legal Monitoring activities will expand IRC's community-based approaches, including protection and HLP monitoring, including detention monitoring where there is need, in collaboration with humanitarian partners, government authorities, and community networks. Existing protection and legal desks will be bolstered to identify vulnerable individuals and provide prompt referrals to appropriate services. Tailored referrals by protection staff will ensure that diverse needs are met, with findings used to produce actionable protection information products. 3) Community-based Psychological Support (PSS) will focus on strengthening community centers to offer group therapy, storytelling, cultural activities, and referrals to service providers. Frontline staff and volunteers will be trained in Psychological First Aid (PFA), with structured and unstructured PSS activities targeting adults and youth. PSS services will be linked to GBV and CP management, while campaigns will address stigma and promote mental health care. 4) Targeted Individual Assistance will provide tailored support for persons with special needs, including cash and non-cash assistance such as NFIs, shelter, and food referrals. In Maban, integrated support from IRC’s Protection, CCCM, and WPE sectors will ensure comprehensive care for individuals with heightened risks. 6) Legal Assistance will complement existing protection activities by providing legal counseling, HLP support, and targeted assistance. Through community-based networks and paralegals, IRC will enhance access to justice and documentation while advocating for vulnerable populations through established mechanisms. 7) Responsive Information Services in Emergencies will empower affected populations by disseminating tailored messages on protection, legal rights, and psychosocial support through two-way communication channels. Platforms like protection desks, WGSS, community radio, and outreach activities will facilitate the flow of accurate and actionable information. 8) Protection Case Management: The response will prioritize protection case management by utilizing established guidelines and context-specific toolkits to complement GBV and child protection (CP) case management in locations across South Sudan. Protection case management interventions will focus on clients who fall outside the GBV and CP management caseload. These interventions will include the provision of quality referrals, capacity building for caseworkers, and enhancing linkages to service delivery to ensure comprehensive support for clients. 9) Protection Advocacy. IRC will actively participate in Protection Cluster activities, including PROMOS, to advocate for the integration of protection principles across humanitarian responses. Efforts will include producing protection information services at area-level, promoting protection and gender mainstreaming, and applying the Humanitarian-Development-Peace (H-D-P) lens in programming and awareness initiatives. These activities will aim to ensure that protection remains a core priority in all aspects of assistance and coordination.
