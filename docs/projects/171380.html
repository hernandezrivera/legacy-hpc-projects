Project Summary

The project will increase food security for 15 localities communities in the eastern and northern areas of the Gaza strip through provision of e-vouchers for food commodities. The project will target 15 localities in the eastern and northern areas of Gaza five governorates through provision of e-vouchers for food commodities. The intervention will be integrated with SCI-nutrition and child protection current interventions and will provide a database for the potential beneficiaries. It will also contribute to addressing malnutrition, particularly with children and will increase the impact of our health/nutrition interventions. Applying a cash-based voucher modality will empower food insecure households to prioritize and meet their own needs while enhancing their access to sufficient food intake. The intervention will target 4,000 vulnerable HHs (approx. 22,000 people) with e-vouchers. SCI will target exceptionally vulnerable HHs such as HHs with members with disability, children under 5, pregnant or lactating women, infants, child/female-headed HHs, and HHs of extended size only. The E-voucher value is expected to cover 100% of HHs` food needs factoring in coping strategy practices that they may use to complete their needs such relying on less preferred and less expensive foods, borrowings and assistance from relatives or host families and use of available savings. The project is in line with Country Strategic Objective 3 (SO3) and FSS objective 2 “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs” and will respond to the root cause in malnutrition resulted from increase in poverty and high food insecurity impacted by the reduction in per capita income and the rise in unemployment.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade, the deteriorating economic situation and the recurrent conflict events and public employee crisis have resulted in high rates of food insecurity in Gaza. The World Bank predicted a full-year decline of at least 7.6 percent on economic growth for 2020 due to the Covid-19 pandemic. The reduction in per capita income, the rise in unemployment and poverty will continue in the coming period . period. In Gaza, 42.7% percent of those in the labour force were unemployed in the fourth quarter of 2019, and this percentage is predicted to increase with the Covid-19 outbreak (according to World Bank Report, June 2020). According to the last survey implemented by the Ministry of Labor in Gaza in April 2020, around 130,000 persons (representing 93,000 HHs registered) have been affected due to measures taken to mitigate the risks of COVID-19. 11,604 registered cases are trade (27%), Health &Education (21%), Construction (16%), transportation& communication (12.5%), (9%) are from the agriculture sector, Hotels and Restaurants (8.5%), and others (6%). In the eastern and northern areas of Gaza strip where HHs depend on agriculture to secure their income, the farmers face many security, financial and environmental challenges that led to income reduction and greater food insecurity. SCI with partners updated a socio-economic assessment in North Gaza and eastern Khan Younis, with a sample of 280 poor households (HHs), by phone, whose main economic activity is farming to measure the change on their income and the effect on their food security and coping mechanisms. The assessment has shown that the average household income has fallen to NIS 580 per month. Food insecure families spend approximately half of their income on food. Other essentials are deprioritized, and female-headed households experience even worse food insecurity and livelihood situation. The public sector salary crisis and restriction of movement due COVID19 crisis have affected a great volume of households further weakening household income stability and purchasing power which in turn has affected the local market, farmers and small businesses. According the results of FGD conducted in the last months in the eastern kahunas and Rafah, the farmers stated that Covid-19 pandemic also affected negatively the value chain of the agriculture products; the farmers were subject to heavy losses due movement restrictions. In addition, the chronic electricity crisis negatively affects farmers and their ability to secure regular irrigation for their farms, which led to low production and income. The prevailing poverty, poor access to livelihood opportunities and basic services is increasing health risks and level of malnutrition. According to HNO 2021 nearly, 72 per cent of the food insecure (1.6 million people), live in the Gaza Strip, where the pandemic and the PA’s financial crisis have compounded the socio-economic impact of the longstanding Israeli blockade, the internal Palestinian divide and the frequent escalations of hostilities. The unemployment rate in the second quarter of 2020 reached 49.1%, a historical high up from 45.5% in the previous quarter. The results of multisector assessment conducted by SCI- WFP-UNICEF in 2018 showed that the percentage of households with poor Food Consumption score has increased from 7% in 2016 (SEFSec 2016 ) to 23%. The results also showed that 71% of surveyed HHs are reducing the number and quality of meals to cope with the crisis.

2 - Activities

The project is in line with FSS objective 2 “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”. The project will target 15 localities in the eastern and northern areas of Gaza five governorates. The intervention will be integrated with SCI-nutrition and child protection current interventions and will provide a database for the potential beneficiaries. Beneficiary selection will prioritize food insecure vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, children under 5 and dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, malnourished children, injured or disabled, and children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services. SCI will use the data from current nutrition and protection interventions to identify the target communities’ protection concerns and malnutrition and children suffering from multiple child protection concerns. SCI will coordinate with the existing Community Protection Committees and CBOs to ensure community active participation and acceptance of the intervention modality. The intervention will target 4,000 vulnerable HHs (approx. 22,000 people) with e-vouchers. To ensure complementarity and coordination among stakeholders, SCI will target exceptionally vulnerable HHs such as HHs with members with disability, children under 5, pregnant or lactating women, infants, child/female-headed HHs, and HHs of extended size only. This methodology has been designed to ensure HHs` food security needs are met to the extent that the risks of malnutrition and insufficient level of food intake are as much mitigated as possible. The e-voucher amount will be based on the MEB developed by the Cash Working Group (CWG). The transfer value is set at US$150 per month, which covers the food requirements of an average family of 6, calculated based on the FS sector standard of 1,800 kcal/person/day for 30 days. Food items allowed list will be based on the list of food items in the FSS basket with the addition of fresh fruits and vegetables, fresh meat (chicken and beef) and eggs. This assistance will be topped-up for the duration of the project, 4 top ups per each HHs. The E-voucher value is expected to cover 100% of HHs` food needs factoring in coping strategy practices that they may use to complete their needs such relying on less preferred and less expensive foods, borrowings and assistance from relatives or host families and use of available savings. Specific project activities will include: 1 -– SCI and partner will conduct Rrapid assessment of needs, gaps, and market functionality as part of the feasibility risk 2 -– SCI will Kkick off and preparation of partners 3 - Communication with stakeholders 4 – SCI will work on Bbeneficiaries’ nomination, selection, registration, and baseline 5 -– SCI will work on the Ddistribution preparation (contracting with merchants and Palpay financial service provider) 6 -– Project partner will conduct Oorientation sessions to beneficiaries on the use of e-vouchers and sensitization session 7 -– SCI will work on Ddistribution of E Vouchers and redemption 8 -– The project partner will Monitoring of e-voucher use (Post-Distribution Monitoring). 9 – End-line to measure outcomes Toto increase the impact of the project. , SCI with implementing partners will conduct 200 sensitization sessions for target HHs and key community members to explain the planned intervention and discuss the pre-selected food and non-food items. The project steering committee will include local partners and key community members, and it will conduct regular meetings to manage potential challenges and social tensions during implementation.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The Project is designed through a child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse from staff/representatives (including volunteers). We ensure that activities engaging with children and their careers have informed consent, are non-exploitative and do not increase risk of harm or vulnerability. From the child-safeguarding angle, we ensure that all concerns, allegations, and incidents are reported, recorded and investigated according to our local country procedures and hierarchy of reporting within 24 hours. Moreover, SCI embed with CSG the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), our complaints handling mechanisms are robust enough to elicit and deal with PSEA related reports and we do regular awareness training with our staff, partners and community on abuse and the duty to report. SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits and cluster consultation. SCI will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed. The project will address adaption guidelines for implementation, to reprogram/adjust programming at country level due to COVID-19 to minimize the harm.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to both women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only groups to facilitate their participation. The selection criteria will focus on female headed HHS. SCI, together with project partners, will identify the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse. SCI Gender Advisor, supported by the SCI Health Advisor, will ensure gender mainstreaming in the assessments and interventions. SCI will ensure that the selection criteria prioritizes the needs of pregnant/lactating women and children with malnutrition and/or CWDs from larger and women headed HHs. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI’s policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities to assist in their access to food security services.

Project staff are required to attend PSEA training.The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse of children is dealt with under our Child Safeguarding policy. This policy is specifically concerned with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) of adults. This includes direct or indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in the wider communities in which we work. For anyone under 18, refer to the Child Safeguarding Policy. For issues in relation to staff refer to the Global Anti - Harassment policy which covers sexual harassment, SEA and bullying of SCI staff. The PSEA policy contains essential principles and rules that reflect the behaviours and standards expected from employees, trustees, volunteers, interns, trainees, consultants, partners, and other representatives working for, or contracted on behalf of SCI in any capacity and covers our international development and humanitarian work.SCI is committed to reducing the opportunities for abuse and exploitation to happen through effective prevention, ensuring that survivors are not exposed to further harm through good reporting and responses, and ensuring that survivors receive appropriate care. Effective prevention and providing safe responses requires all staff, volunteers, interns, partners and other representatives to be clear about their individual accountabilities and responsibilities. It also requires all senior staff and managers to ensure that their working environments minimise the opportunities for sexual exploitation and abuse, and to ensure that reporting and response do not expose survivors or reporters to any further harm. Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is a gross breach of both standards of aid, and of the rights of those in need. The PSEA policy applies 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and 365 days a year. Any breach of the policy will be taken extremely seriously and responded to. Breaches of the policy may be grounds for termination of engagement with SCI. SCI takes the position that early reporting of any concern supports both prevention of further abuse and safe response through the hotline available 24/7 (1800-724-726). There must be increased awareness about reporting, including the importance of reporting of any and all concerns, however apparently minor. SCI’s commitment to Zero Tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse means all programme design and planning must include a gender power analysis, which also looks at the roles and structure of SCI staff, volunteers, interns and partners and a safeguarding risk assessment SC has a PSEA Focal Point at the country level and the field sites, who is responsible for driving awareness of the policy and on how to report, inviting, receiving and collating all causes for concern. When these collected pieces of information generate a picture of a site, or a programme, or a particular person, the PSEA Focal Point must escalate the matter to trigger further inquiry and/ or investigation.
