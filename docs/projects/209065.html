Project Summary

This Project intends to support the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), Returnees, and Vulnerable Host Communities in the localities of El Fasher, Sara Omra and Tawila in North Darfur State of the Republic of Sudan through provision of lifesaving and life sustaining Food Security and Livelihood interventions.
According to the Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan 2024, 42% of Sudan’s population (20.3 million people) are now experiencing high levels of food insecurity of IPC Phase 3+ (crisis and above), driven by conflict and displacement across several states particularly in Khartoum, Darfur and the Kordofan regions. In North Darfur State, the impact of the conflict on the population has been both direct and indirect. Insecurity along trade routes for example is reducing trade flows, input supplies and causing prices of goods to rise above seasonal levels, thereby negatively affecting household access to staple foods and related commodities at a time when reliance on market purchases is typically high. Most people who used to produce their own food have been driven to camps and are unable to access their farms which has reduced levels of production. The conflict has also triggered a large-scale displacement of 5.1 million people across the country with estimated 1.1million in North Darfur State alone (DTM Sudan - Internally Displaced Persons 2023 Estimates 31st December 2023). It is projected that if the conflict continues unabated, it is likely to exacerbate Sudan’s food insecurity in the immediate as well as mid-to long-term as the already severe food security situation in Sudan is worsening amid a recent expansion of hostilities, large-scale population displacement, atrocities against civilians, the destruction of goods and infrastructure, pervasive looting, and poor humanitarian access. “Nearly one-third of the population of Sudan is in need of emergency food assistance, making it one of the world’s worst food crises.” according to FEWSNET.
This project will focus on lifesaving and life sustaining food security and livelihoods assistance to people in need, targeting the most severe food insecure population groups (IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable Host Communities) through unconditional in-kind food and cash based transfers as well as essential agricultural livelihood support. In targeting direct beneficiaries, particular attention will be given to women-headed households, households with children under the age of five, orphans, People with special needs (PWSNs), disabilities, elderly people and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will be targeted as well through a harmonized integrated gender sensitiv approach to prioritization and targeting criteria(geographical & household level) in line with SO of the FSL sector.
The main objective of the project is to improve the food security situation through gender-responsive lifesaving and life-sustaining food and CBTs to IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities in the localities of El Fasher, Sara Omra and Tawila in North Darfur State by also improving communities’ capacity to sustain household livelihoods. The expected result of the projects is reduced mortality and morbidity through improved livelihood, increased food and nutrition security. The main activities of the project will include: 1) Providing life-saving food assistance to IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable Host Communities through in-kind and cash transfers using the most feasible cash-based intervention modality. 2) Providing life-saving agriculture and livelihoods support to IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable host communities and 3) Providing life-sustaining food, agriculture and livelihoods assistance to beneficiaries through supporting the establishment of vegetable gardens, supporting rain-fed agricultural practices, proving tools for farming and distributing seeds like watermelon, okra, tomato, cucumber, carrot, rocket among others to vulnerable households, providing trainings and establishing agriculture committees.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

HDPO will maximize the positive impacts of the FSL programs by ensuring that beneficiaries are covered by the key pillars of protection mainstreaming of safety in access to services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of FSL assistance.HDPO will ensure safety and dignity of beneficiaries without causing harm by: Ensuring that all all the frontline staffs are trained in PSEA policy and this is mandatory in HDPO HR policy for every newly recruited staffs and regular refresher trainings to avoid abusing beneficiaries. HDPO will further apply its data protection policy to ensure that the beneficiaries list and personal data is kept confidential and used only for intended purpose. HDPO will also ensure that all her FSL activities are safe and accessible to all beneficiaries with special focus to women and girls. Usually HDPO staffs engages each category to best tease and filter their special protection and safety needs. HDPO will ensure the following during distribution and of food and NFIs like seeds: HDPO will Identify a central location that is safe so that the beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through unsafe conflict zones to access distribution points. All distributions will take place during day thus giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall and possibly avoiding placing distribution points at or near the military installations. HDPO will ensure that in the course of distribution there are female staffs to cater for the female gender, work with women led organisations and will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place significant measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from food distributions also solve conflicts arising from these distribution sites. HDPO usually consults both IDPs and host communities on food security and livelihoods needs assess during assessments on whether assistance could breed tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. HDPO in its livelihood programming always consults and abides by the available and applicable labour laws, by laws and this is done through agreements with State and local authorities. HDPO will rank the different beneficiary categories and identify some of the unsafe livelihood practices like child labor, negative coping mechanisms like survival sex and usually prioritize these groups for intervention. HDPO has an on ground policy of associating with beneficiaries at family level and ensures that its livelihood programs promote family unity, household and child caring practices. In order to Promote equitable and impartial access to all HDPO will collect and enforce data disaggregated by age, gender and different categories of beneficiaries. During data collection process the consent of the beneficiaries will first be seeked and privacy/confidentiality maintained.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

HDPO will use community feedback to adjust implementation strategies to best suit beneficiaries' direct and primary needs. Feedbacks will be got through religious leaders, community leaders, institutions of learning, and beneficiaries themselves, staffs of INGO, NNGOs and UN. HDPO will provide suggestion boxes at every project site for feedback and response. Involvement of the community leaders during project inception for ownership will be key for understanding the scope of all the FSL projects then the monitoring and supervision will also be key. This comes after the inception meetings of the community leaders and during the orientation on the roles of the different stakeholders to inform them about the project's goals, scope, timeline, and benefits, and will invite them to share their feedback in a respectful and constructive way. Real-time feedback data that identifies evolving issues and opportunities enable HDPO to be more responsive and fluid. A focal point in HDPO will be appointed on this. HDPO will use on-site surveys, emails and letters, and phone calls, and make time to meet face-to-face. It will give our staffs insights into their work from the people they work most closely with resulting into everyone being able to reach better decisions, improve performance, and generally succeed in their role aimed at achieving the goals of informing, consulting, involving, collaborating, and empowering. The community engagement will build deeper, stronger and more trusting relationships between HDPO and communities. HDPO will undertake community feedback surveys through the 7 key steps: Setting the objectives, writing the survey questions, testing internally and then on a pilot group, deploying the survey to members, Collecting responses, analyzing the responses and acting on the results, Generally the community feedback will help prevent performance from going off track or becoming misaligned, wasting valuable time, energy or resources. Appreciative and positive constructive feedback and recognition will help build confidence in HDPO as a sense of being valued by the community. It helps to reinforce positive habits and encourages more of the performance that HDPO will want to see in all the FSL projects.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Through community participatory feedback sessions and gender, disability, and social factor analysis conducted by our project staff ( nutrition, Gender, and protection) in collaboration with other key sectors ( WASH, FSL, Health, Social Protection/Protections, Education) on monthly, quarterly, bi-annually and annual basis to assess the perception, acceptability, knowledge and practice changes towards positive mindset for improved nutrition. This will be done in our activities through face to face with individuals (at the household level) during home visits by extension workers and FSL cordinators, face to face with individuals (in other venues/locations) at community centres, schools, face to face with group consultations, face to face with community leaders, Billboards, Flyers, Complaints/suggestions box, Help desks/kiosk. The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policy and course is mandatory for all HDPO staffs and forms part of HR policy hence not negotiable to all staffs. HDPO will ensure all the staffs adhere to the PSEA key principles of knowing that mistaken belief regarding the age of a child is not a defense, exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior is prohibited. AAP is an active commitment HDPO staffs to use power responsibly to take account of, give account to, and be held to account by the people we serve and all HDPO staffs adhere to the five commitments of leadership, transparency, feedback and complaints, participation, and design, monitoring and evaluationHDPO will follow its Safeguarding policy and strictly instruct its staff to communicate PSEA related issues with the Safeguarding focal person through the communication means that outlines in the strategy.
