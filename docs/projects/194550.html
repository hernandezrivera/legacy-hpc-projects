Project Summary

The Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes providing emergency medical assistance to internally displaced people and host communities affected by conflict and flooding in Twic (Warrap), Ezo, and Nagero Western Equatoria, South Sudan in 2023. This project will provide life-saving, basic health care services through stationary (PHCC and PHCU) and mobile health services, procurement of essential medicines and training of health workers, surveillance of epidemics, vaccination against vaccine-preventable diseases, and provision of health services in the community. According to the February July 2022 IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, issued April 9, 2022, Twic County was classified as Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4) associated with shocks of ongoing endemic conflict resulting in loss of life and property, displacement, disruption, and destruction of livelihoods.
IHO conducted a cross-sectoral rapid assessment in Twic on 17-20 July 2022 showed In mid-February and early March 2022, armed civilians from Twic and Abyei clashed in Agok (disputed area). The clashes in Agok resulted in the displacement of 33,869 IDPs who were staying at 14 IDP sites in Twic County. These health facilities need support as they serve an estimated number of more than 40,000 people. Gaps identified in these healthcare facilities include human resources, medical equipment, medicines and poor infrastructure. In addition, the latrines of the health centers are in a desolate condition and need to be renovated.
According to the February July 2022 IPC Analysis of Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition dated April 9, 2022, Ezo County was classified as a crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Nagero County as an emergency (IPC Phase 4) with acute food insecurity due to insecurity resulting from intercommunal Violence classified as trade disruptions, high food prices, and low crop production. IHO conducted a multi-sector rapid assessment in Nagero County, July 13-16, 2022, Ezo County, July 14-17, 2022, Ezo Payam in Ezo County continues to host the majority of the Tambura Crisis' internally displaced people. 9361 HHs, 56166 people. Out of 30 health facilities in Ezo County (1 hospital, 4 PHHC, and 25 PHCUs), 21 are supported and 9 have a gap. Nagero is currently hosting 5,080 IDPs with no record of new arrivals. Nagero County has 13 health facilities, 2 PHCCs, and 11 PHCCs, of which 5 PHCU are not yet operational. The two counties face high malaria cases. Respiratory infections and diarrhea, limited BmONC, EPI services, MPHSS, essential medications and staff (IHO assessment report).

1 - Needs

After the conflict erupted in December 2013, the Republic of South Sudan continues to face significant humanitarian problems, which have increased significantly. Affected counties include Twic, Ezo, and Nagero counties. In addition, a serious crisis erupted in July 2016 with ensuing inter-communal fighting that further aggravated the humanitarian situation, leading to secondary or third-party displacements with populations under stress, lacking access to basic services, and limited humanitarian access. The conflict situation and flooding in the target districts have a great impact on social services, and also the health system faces insufficient human resources and insecurity, attacks on health care, and workers have restricted movement and access, which limits the documentation of the cluster response. limited access to clean water, poor waste disposal and infection prevention and control measures, poor infrastructure, and potential risks for disease outbreaks, e.g. B. Cholera, Ebola, etc. According to the February July 2022 IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, issued April 9, 2022, Nagero and Twic Counties were classified as Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4) and Ezo County was classified as a crisis (IPC phase 3) associated with shocks of ongoing endemic conflict resulting in loss of life and property, displacement , disruption, and destruction of livelihoods. IHO conducted a cross-sectoral rapid assessment in Twic on 17-20 July 2022 which reported that in mid-February and early March 2022, armed civilians from Twic and Abyei clashed in Agok (disputed area). The clashes in Agok resulted in the displacement of 33,869 IDPs who were staying at 14 IDP sites in Twic County. The survey further found that among the 14 existing camps for IDPs, MSF Swize supports 6 large ones including Majook Non, Aweng, Majak Aher, Abyei Adau, Gomgoi and Nyindeng Ayuel areas, while 8 camps remain without support. These health facilities need support as they serve an estimated number of more than 40,000 people. Gaps identified in these healthcare facilities include human resources, medical equipment, medicines and poor infrastructure. In addition, the latrines of the health centers are in a desolate condition and need to be renovated.IHO conducted a multi-sector rapid assessment in Nagero County and Ezo County continues to host the majority of the Tambura Crisis' internally displaced people. 9361 HHs, 56166 people. Out of 30 health facilities in Ezo County (1 hospital, 4 PHHC, and 25 PHCUs), 21 are supported and 9 have a gap. Nagero is currently hosting 5,080 IDPs with no record of new arrivals. Nagero County has 13 health facilities, 2 PHCCs, and 11 PHCCs, of which 5 PHCU are not yet operational. The two counties face high malaria cases. Respiratory infections and diarrhea, limited BmONC, EPI services, MPHSS, essential medications, and staff. There is a need to provide emergency care services through a stationary primary health care unit, along with a medical mobile unit and community interventions (BHI) to support other health partners in treating common diseases to reduce excess mortality and morbidity. In this regard, IHO plans to reach 40,000 people (male children 10,147, female children 9,366, adult male 9,762 and female adults 10,725), 2,000 people with disabilities (male children 507, female children 468, adult male 488 and female adult 536) with health service to reduce excessive morbidity and mortality from epidemic-prone diseases, improve access and expand the response to vulnerable populations through the mobile unit 4 and 2 PHCC 4 PHCU through close cooperation with the cluster partners Nutrition, FLS, WASH and Protection In the same respect, the project will monitor outbreaks of diseases at the places of use in order to Inform and take immediate action Response.

2 - Project activities

In order to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine-preventable illnesses, such as measles, among male children under 5, female children under 5, male children aged 5 - 17 years, female children aged 5 - 17 years, male adults aged 18 - 60, female adults aged 18 - 60, male adults aged over 60 and female adults aged over 60; IHO will; 1. Promote epidemic disease surveillance, prevention, and control including outbreak detection, investigation, and response through IDRS and Submission of EWAR reports.2. Undertake Immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases including Measles,3. Undertake IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using scorecards, provision of supplies and protocols, and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities4. Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM To provide access to life-saving essential healthcare, including mental health to women, men, girls and boys, including ability challenged persons and ensuring vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action. In addition, Reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls and boys at increased risk of mortality and morbidity and protection incidents/threats in priority areas through protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness-raising and risk mitigation, IHO will provide life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (PHCU and mobile healthcare services), procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers.To ensure that population groups targeted to receive assistance are consulted throughout the entire cycle of the response, and their needs are taken into account in decision-making by humanitarians. IHO will promote community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM. To ensure vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions to displacement with respect to their rights, IHO will conduct community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance. To strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs at national and sub-national levels, IHO will provide technical and operational assistance to health facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and promote the humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IHO will ensure the project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) by creating different channels that allows them to share concerns anonymously. Examples of feedback and reporting channels may include telephone hotlines, satisfaction focus group discussions, community feedbackcommittees, voice recorders and feedback boxesWhile conducting discussions with general topics and IHO move towards more sensitive topics as the participants become more comfortable. Guide group discussions towards general, rather than personal conversation, so people do not feel pressure to disclose their own experiences of violence.IHO will ensure that staff member are trained to speak privately with participants who require additional psychosocial/emotional support and/or referrals to other services and will have experience working with survivors of GBV. IHO will be flexible when and where consultations take place.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IHO will facilitate the provision of feedback from affected people on the services provided through feedback meeting which will be held Monthly basis and upon completion of the project. IHO will administer a perception questionnaire to beneficiaries. Team will ask beneficiaries to give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary. Finally, IHO will also conduct evaluation exercise involving men, women, girls and boys. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors. All IHO staff members are trained on PSEA Awareness-Raising. The organization staff or contractors or suppliers are prohibited from exploiting beneficiaries for exchange of services for sex or any form of abuse. All IHO staff members have a duty to report in good faith any SEA allegations, concerns or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA and SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result indisciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate. IHO will ensure all service provision points are accessed in safe and secure place.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IHO will use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance to promote accountability, IHO will promote transparency during the project implementation by directly involving the community in every stage of the project to ensure clear understanding of objectives of the project, expectations and stakeholders. IHO has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions.The locations of the Primary health care unit and Mobile team services targeted for this project have been identified in consultation of the CHD and state ministry of Health. In addition, the Mobile Teams placement shall be based on the community prioritization and community leader consultations. IHO has as conducted initial needs analysis to identify the needs of the community targeted by the project. During service provision, IHO shall ensures facilitation feedback mechanism from affected people on the services by holding monthly feedback meeting and on Monthly basis IHO will administer a perception questionnaire to 10 beneficiaries. Team will ask beneficiaries to give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary. All project activities will engage local authorities to oversee their implementation. The implementation teams are recruited with attention to a balance of women and men, cultural diversity and age. Communities will be educated on the services provided to enable them make informed decision on service utilization and as well as IHO will promote community dialogue to continuously incorporate the needs of the community. Finally, IHO will also conduct evaluation exercises involving men, women, girls and boys. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible. this will allows beneficiaries to hold IHO and its staff to account and reduces the possibility of mistakes, abuse and corruption. IHO will also be able to assess and improve the quality of work, which benefits both the IHO and the people it shall reaches. IHO will also use beneficiaries’ feedback to respond to their issues in real time and act upon it before the project ends and lastly IHO will use use beneficiaries’ feedback Empower communities and promote project sustainability.
