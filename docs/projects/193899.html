Project Summary

CARE is planning to implement this project in partnership with Abdel Sha Community Health Association (ACHA) and Palestinian Medical relief Society (PMRS).
The project aims: Firstly, to provide Sexual and Reproductive and prevention services to women including: detection and primary care of gynecological problems; ultrasound examination; advice and treatment for menopausal women>18 years including with disabilities; testing during pregnancy.
The project will also provide family planning services to women>18 years and adolescents 14-17 years including females with disabilities. These services include information and counselling regarding all possible contraceptive methods: natural methods, condom, injections, pill and IUDs, in order to facilitate an optimal and responsible free choice. Meetings and workshops on reproduction and family planning will be organized for various target groups with special focus on adolescents 14-17 years besides provide ante and postnatal care to women> 18 years: pre-natal counselling services including ultrasound examinations, will be offered to women>18 years. Pre-delivery courses will be offered to women> 18 years in the last quarter of pregnancy consisting of suitable physical exercises, relaxation and breathing techniques and health education sessions regarding pregnancy, delivery, breast-feeding, baby-care and family planning. Post-delivery counselling and services will be offered for women who have recently given birth and who will receive information on breast-feeding, family planning, baby care and conduct physical exercises.
Secondly: capacity building for frontline staff 24-55 years from health specialized actors on self- care and stress management, health counselling and contingency planning, GBV identification, follow up and referral mechanisms, PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance, , feedback and complaint mechanism and providing strategic directions to health and nutrition programmes and advocacy
Third: Procurement of medical laboratory tests, hygiene and sterilization materials and other needs of medical supplies and disposable.
CARE/partners. will use their PHC health centers to provide services and will coordinate with health/women CBOs to facilitate targeting beneficiaries.
The project activities go in line with the HNC priorities to improve Access to essential
Health Services including SRH. CAR/ partners will coordinate with all relevant actors to ensure the complementarity and avoid duplication. At the same time implementing a clear feedback and Accountability mechanism, MEAL, security, and full access to project services based on humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence & humanity).
All project's activities will be delivered FREE of charge.

1 - Needs

CARE and its partners (ACHA and PMRS) conducted a rapid needs assessment including gender analysis in the period between 16- 19/09/2022 using literature review of MSNA 2022, HNO 2023, and MOH reports as well as interviews and focus groups discussions with target groups including women and others mostly affected by the humanitarian crisis, while having sex, age and disability disaggregated data. MSNA (2023) presents that 18.7% of households reported having a member of their household that was pregnant or lactating at the time of data collection. 86.1% of households reporting that women of reproductive age (15 - 49 years) had no access barriers to specialized reproductive health services. Of the 8.2% of female-headed households in Gaza, 27.3% were assessed to include a member of their household having a disability, compared to 20.4% of the 91.8% of male-headed households. HNO (2023) states that according to the MoH 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women and 225,000 children including 30,000 neonates in need of the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, GBV, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services.Pregnant and lactating women and children < 5 are considered most vulnerable and they may not be able to receive essential healthcare in response to the crisis because of the re-prioritization of health services. Gendered inequalities in the oPt further limit freedom of movement, decision-making power, and access to health, education, and basic services and resources differently for men, women, boys, and girls. Also, socially and economically disadvantaged women, male/female youth, and marginalized populations (i.e. PWDs) are more disproportionately affected. When overlapped with existing gendered disparities, the response to both the protracted crisis and C-19 becomes increasingly complex. Global trends indicate that women and girls are bearing specific burdens of the C-19 crisis such as: increased household responsibilities and family care, restricted mobility, and rising levels of GBV.CARE’s assessment (April 2020) noted that significantly fewer female respondents (58%) than males (86%) have safe access to health facilities inside and outside of their community, which they attributed to a lack of cash and inability to travel. The prevalence of male medical and security staff also creates barriers for women accessing health care, mental health support, basic hygiene, as well as the right to privacy and comfort, given the taboos and norms related to gender mixing. The assessment findings conducted by (ACHA) in September 2023 with the ACHA staff and volunteers reveal critical needs that came in in alignment with HRP 2023 Priorities and Eligible Actions. It showed the frontline health staff are in need of self-care capacity building training, SRH, and also noted the need for PSEA, complaints/response mechanisms, and remote health counselling. A raid needs assessment conducted with Women in reproductive age and pregnant/ lactating women noted the lack of quality maternal services in governmental health facilities, primarily access to SRH, family planning, prevention and referral of GBV, screening, diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation, medical lab tests; individual and group PSS, couple therapy, specialized mental health treatment, social/legal counselling; home visits, dignity/hygiene kits and community awareness raising. Participants with cervical and breast cancer indicated the need for screening, diagnosis and rehabilitation. Women and girls with physical disabilities noted the need for assistive mobility devices (mobile chairs, walkers), while those with hearing disabilities lack assistive audio devices. SGBV cases lack integrated case management services, and prevention/ referral services. Finally, a broad advocacy is needed to raise the voices of women and girls about their critical health gaps/needs. HNO (2023) indicated that redirection of resources from provision of SRH, obstetric care and emergency nutrition services affected an additional 80,000 vulnerable women and 210,000 children, including 25,000 neonates. Currently,1800 patients visit the Gaza center monthly to receive medical and healthcare services, out of whom 150 are pregnant and lactating women. Rafah Medical center receives 800 patients, of whom 100 are pregnant and lactating. The majority of women (18-45yrs) visiting PMRS for SRH services, are the most vulnerable; 10-15% are women headed Households, lacking source of income, health awareness and healthy behaviors

2 - Activities

The project aims: to improve and enhancing the sexual and reproductive healthcare of pregnant and lactating women including with disabilities, breast cancer survivors, GBV survivors, IDP, refugees, and FHHs.The planned activities were guided by the HRP2023 Priorities and Eligible Actions, HNC Objectives and activities mainly Objective: 1 Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt, and Objective: 2 Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises. It also goes in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected women within the targeted communities. The activities are closely coordinated with HNC, health providers, local CBOs, while being aware of what is offered in the targeted locations. The activities will be implemented in partnership with Abdel Shafi for Community Health Association (ACHA) and Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the field level (provision of health services). CARE will be responsible of the overall quality management, MEAL, reporting, FAM mechanism, capacity building of the team in PSEAH and will act as the main contact for donors and stakeholders. The project activities will be as follows: Activities for O#1: Provision of comprehensive Women health care services: Provide health care and prevention services to women including: detection and primary care of gynecological problems; ultrasound examination; advice and treatment for menopausal women; testing during pregnancy; provide family planning services to women and adolescents including awareness and counselling regarding all possible contraceptive methods in order to facilitate an optimal and responsible free choice; awareness sessions on reproduction and family planning will be organized for various target groups with special focus on adolescents, provide ante and postnatal care to women: Pre-natal counselling services including ultrasound examinations, will be offered to women. Pre-delivery courses will be offered to women in the last quarter of pregnancy consisting of suitable physical exercises, relaxation and breathing techniques and health education sessions regarding pregnancy, delivery, breast-feeding, baby-care and family planning. Post-delivery counselling and services will be offered for women who have recently given birth and will receive information on breast-feeding, family planning, baby care and conduct physical exercises.Activities for O#2: Capacity Building for medical team: Conducting capacity building for frontlines staff on self- care and safety, PSEA measures, stress management, contingency planning health counselling, GBV identification, follow and referral, orientation on the complaint and response mechanism (FAM). The project will provide medical supplies to the medical teams.CARE partners will be responsible to provide all health services, while CARE will be responsible to procure and deliver all medical supplies and equipment and will provide PSEAH training to the partners teams, and managing the FAM mechanism, and supervise the overall work and the implementation including ME and the reporting to the donor

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CARE is committed to assuring accountability to stakeholders in all aspects and phases of the project. At the inception stage, community members helped inform the design of project activities, composition of the teams, frequency and type of the services, urgent health issues, etc. CARE/partner also took into consideration the different health needs of the different groups residing in the targeted communities (sex and age). Monitoring has been carried out through the on sight monitoring of distributions and activities, and it always includes segregation of data by sex and age (SADD). CARE’s Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) builds on our previous experience with humanitarian accountability, and it is in-formed by standards and guidance to ensure quality and accountability to our partners and stakeholders, and to support existing organizational and technical standards, with which to bring about sustainable change to communities in need. It was designed and piloted in other projects within CARE allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE for any feedback or complaint about the project activities, criteria, services, behavior by any of the project’s staff CARE will continue to raise community awareness regarding FAM, regularly reporting on feedback and complaints as part of CARE’s continuous learning agenda. CARE has started the distribution of the FAM cards in the targeted communities and started to receivefeedback/complaints. Feedback/complaints are documented in a log sheet by the Focal Point / Accountability Officer responsible for monitoring the progress of each input until the feedback loop is closed. The data will be reported and used for management decisions and analyzing trends. CARE also is planning to conduct a community-based survey that will further assess accessibility of themechanism, as well as preferred channels of communication; a patient satisfaction survey will be conducted to see the feedback to enable CARE to respond accordingly

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

To create an effective and easily accessible mechanism for all stakeholders, CARE Palestine WBG considered the existing challenges and constraints that participants may face in the local context, including the continuous restrictions on movement, security risks and sudden changes to security, etc., as well as the social mores and gender norms. Before fully launching the mechanism, CAREpiloted the FAM to gain deeper insight into beneficiary / stakeholders’ satisfaction levels about the quality of and access to services, as well as about the extent to which the most vulnerable community members were being reached and their needs were being met, to promote community empowerment, participation, and ownership of the feedback mechanism, and to help improvemonitoring and evaluation activities by incorporating beneficiaries’ perspectives into reporting practices through real time analysis of CARE’s work on the ground. CARE continues to evolve FAM by soliciting stakeholders’ opinions about preferences with respect to access, methods / channels through which to provide feedback and complaints. With respect to the clinics where the mobileteams work, Arabic instructions (cards) for lodging complaints are posted in clear view of visiting patients, and contact cards are distributed to participants via home visits, through local CBOs, local councils, focal points in the communities, etc. Where the mobile teams work without a physical clinic, CARE will distribute the FAM cards in the communities including the beneficiaries so they are informed about the mechanism.
