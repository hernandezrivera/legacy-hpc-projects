Project Summary

Since 2014, the crisis unfolding in Lac Province has been characterized by numerous challenging factors, such as insecurity, flooding, large-scale and continuous population movements (Internally Displaced Persons, returnees) as well as community clashes and socioeconomic-related tensions. While Chad habitually faces seasonal flooding due to severe rains, the flooding in 2022 has been the worst flooding since 1961, with the Chari River rising to 8.11m to date, causing massive displacement within the city of N’Djamena. During the initial months of July – August 2022, flooding as a result of excessive rainfall led to over 440,000 individuals being affected across the country, while in N’Djamena itself, 5,678 houses were completely or partially destroyed. While the displacement had subsided temporarily, a secondary flooding due to an rising water levels of the Chari and Logone rivers since October, has led to 118,700 people displaced in N’Djamena. These individuals are currently residing in 8 official displacement sites, with an unconfirmed 34,824 more displaced in 5 other sites based on recent estimations of UN OCHA. To provide lifesaving assistance, IOM suggests the implementation of a three-fold response:
1) Strengthening of displacement data management to support decision-making, planification and direct assistance, through the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM)’s neighborhood flooding assessment and rapid kobo registration in displacement sites.
2) Shelters and Non-Food Items (NFI) procurement and distribution for most vulnerable displaced people.
3) Supporting affected population with psychosocial support in the displaced sites in N’Djamena.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

From the DTM data collection carried out in October in 7 sites in N’Djamena, multiple lifesaving needs were reported by the affected population: food and shelter were identified as the most urgent needs. In addition, it was highlighted by all actors that the capacities at field level to respond to priority needs in N’Djamena are insufficient. Hence, there is an urgent demand to have concentrated shelter, NFI and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS)-based interventions in N’djamena for adults and children. To track the ever-changing development of the flooding situation in N’Djamena as the rivers continue to rise and ensure an informed response, there is an urgent need for updated information on the impact on affected individuals and infrastructure as well as to quickly register beneficiaries as they continue to be displaced. In this context, the provision of lifesaving shelter and non-food assistance is key to supporting populations displaced by flooding and conflict. Furthermore, the availability of regular updates on displacement trends, numbers of displaced people, profiles, living conditions, needs and access to services is of utmost importance to keep informing efficient humanitarian responses based on reliable and updated information. Over the years, IOM has supported the Government of Chad in the provision of assistance to displaced populations but the worsening displacement situation in the country calls for renewed efforts to strengthen humanitarian assistance in the country, especially in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic – which has affected movement of goods and personnel – in addition to creating a public health emergency. To provide lifesaving assistance, IOM suggests the implementation of a three-fold response: 1) Strengthening of displacement data management to support decision-making, planification and direct assistance, through the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM)’s neighborhood flooding assessment and rapid kobo registration in displacement sites. 2) Shelters and Non-Food Items (NFI) procurement and distribution for most vulnerable displaced people.3) Supporting affected population with psychosocial support in the displaced sites in N’Djamena. IOM’s planned response is driven by comprehensive community-based assessments that engage community members and relevant local authorities at every stage of the planning process, to utilize existing resources available within the community. Protection mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations (AAP) will be integral parts of all planned activities.
