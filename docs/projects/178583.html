Project Summary

UOSSM is proposing to provide women and girls safe space project in the community, culturally-appropriate and tailored to the context aiming to reduce risks and prevent further harm during acute emergency by providing an area where women and girls can: • Socialize and re-build their social networks • Receive social support • Acquire contextually relevant skills • Access safe and non-stigmatizing GBV response services • have a safe entry point for services and a place to access information. In order to provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including case management, psychosocial support and reinforce referral pathway UOSSM will continue supporting WGSS in Idleb and two mobile teams (one of male and one of female) in order to reach most vulnerable categories in the remote areas. one female and one male will implement outreach activities where the female team will conduct home visits to offer access women and girls whose movement may be restricted in some way. Meanwhile, the male team will conduct outreach activities aiming to engagement with community structures, religious and community leaders to prevent GBV by dissemination adapted GBV messages included in the GBV awareness tool kits developed by the GBV sub cluster and to inform community members about activities and services. UOSSM will provide the following: The center will be women and girl-led and offer an inclusive and empowering environment for them. Women and girls will be included in project planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation of the space to ensure relevance and ownership. While the centre will be a space meant for and run by women and girls, its sustainability will require the input and support of many stakeholders. Husbands, parents, and community leaders have a lot of influence over the ability of women and girls to participate in programmes. It is, therefore, essential to understand the perspectives of these individuals while setting up a safe space, and to mobilize community support for the WGSS so that women and girls are able to safely participate in all activities by utilizing outreach activities to engage men and adolescent boys in GBV awareness raising and community mobilization. the project will maintain balance between structured activities, services, and times to socialize. Activities and approaches will be culturally and age appropriate as the needs and interests are bound to be different. First UOSSM will utilize the referral pathway, developed by the GBV Sub-Cluster, which contains organizations that provide specialized services such as MHPSS and health services and CVA by referring GBV survivors to service provider. Second, UOSSM will abide by the GBV SC service mapping in the targeted area, creating a resource detailing all services provided at the community-level and by the other humanitarian actors, and updating it on a regular basis. Finally, UOSSM will utilize their internal referral pathway to refer cases to health and CMR services, provided by UOSSM in the surrounding area. by training health team on GBV Core Concepts, (PFA) and other topics of relevance for addressing the protection needs of local communities and individuals in NWS. The project will take into consideration, the special needs of women and girls living with disabilities by rehabilitating the Center to be eligible for PWDs (ramp and wheelchair). Recreational activities will be implemented in the center including Sewing, make-up, hair dressing, computer literacy, language, crochet, painting, drawing led by women and girls with resources procured by the UOSSM. Life skills sessions for women and adolescent girls customized and age-appropriate will be held in the center.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 In Syria; especially child/forced marriage, sexual harassment and sexual violence (including rape), denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse continue to pervade the lives of women and girls, resulting in very few spaces where they feel safe. Sexual harassment, including on the way to school, in the market, and at distribution sites, as well as sexual violence are concerns raised by consulted women and girls that contribute to psycho social distress and limit their movements. Reported incidents of rape are on the increase, which does not necessarily mean an increase in prevalence but signiﬁes an improvement in the quality of services. Girls as young as 11-12 years old are being married by families believing it will protect them and ease the ﬁnancial burden on the family, whereas working women are often reportedly denied the opportunity to marry to continue supporting their family ﬁnancially. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families. Where gender-based violence services exist, women and girls have conﬁrmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with Women and Girls Safe Spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psycho social and legal support) and other needed services(e.g.vocational and skill training). During 2020 UOSSM established WGSS centers in Atmah & Hassan, provided GBV CM services in 6 PHCs. According to the monthly reports confirmed that domestic violence, including verbal and physical violence, is common in the communities. Child marriage to be increasing in the area, with very young girls getting married, having babies and already being divorced.

5 - Implementation of activities

Provide CM, focused psychosocial and referral services to GBV survivors through two (2) mobile protection teams and one (1) WGSS, in the community of Dana (Atma). the team will be supervised by team leader and technically supported by protection officer. Any case ﬁles, documentation of services, and client data kept at the center should be properly secured. All the services will be survivors cantered and in line with the case management SOPs developed by the GBV sub cluster. the case workers will utilize the localized service mapping to provide safe referral pathways for survivors. UOSSM will conduct basic GBV, Protection and PSS trainings for all staff on: GBV Core Concepts, Communication Skills, GFS, Stress Management Strategies, (PFA), GBV CM.Group Facilitation and Skills for Recreational Activities then team will undertake the GBV SC online training on disability inclusion.Objectives: •M&E team will be trained on M&E toolkits developed by GBV SC. •Monitor changes in NO of BNFs of project per month •Assess quality of services provided. •Various programs efforts, to the extent possible, link such program efforts to outcomes, thus assess the effects and impacts of program interventions •Provide standardized tools and indicators for the monitoring and evaluation of all activities in that area •Establish clear data ﬂow channels between the different stakeholders,1 Establish consultation and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place 2 Monitor the adaptation and development of messages 3 Availability of GAR 4 Include indicators by age groups to capture adolescent programming 5. Include in long frame and monitor engagement sessions with local authorities to discuss project 5. Pre and post tests for trainings.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Providing GBV specialized services to including case management, PSS support and legal services Protection risks: 1- Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma 2- Privacy concerns along with conﬁdentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services Likelihood: high Mitigation measures: 1. Service mapping including those that can provide survivor-centered services and with coordination with GBV sub cluster and other GBV actors. 2. ensure conﬁdentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Activity: Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities protection risks: 1. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging 2. Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment 3. Women and adolescent girls with child-bearing responsibilities might have diﬃculty accessing services in the WGSS Likelihood: medium Mitigation measures: 1. Community engagement from the onset of the project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders 2. Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities 5. Equip all WGSS with child-care services 6. Provide transportation support for persons with disability.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Prohibition of Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse (PSEA)UOSSM continues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, UOSSM has PSEA code of conduct it’s included on to set forth the expectations of conduct and mutual respect in regard to sexual exploitation and abuse and the process of complaint if these expectations are not met or violated.Risk of doing SEA by not PSEA trained staff utilizing the authority (Provision of the services):Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigation measures: 1. UOSSM has developed PSEA code of conduct in line with the PSEA network. All the recruited staff should read and sign the PSEA code of conduct.2. Recruited staff should attend PSEA training provides by UOSSM’s PSEA Focal Point.3. Establish a PSEA reporting mechanism for beneficiaries to report any misconduct by staff. give feedback and complaints even to the local councils or our field staff. In addition, we will include (phone numbers, e-mails, verbal reporting and complaints’ box) in any awareness materials.4. distribute awareness images inside the centers in addition to use the child satisfaction forms (expressive images and tacit questions) to make sure that no abuse has been done. Parents will also be encouraged to register any complaints expressed by children.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The threat of poor security situation and in the areas. Assumption: The team will receive training in PFA, staff care. UOSSM will mitigate the risk by daily communication with the security and safety team, following security advisories, recommendations, updating contingency plans, provide PFA to the new IDPs and activate the mobile teams. Conduct consultations with women and adolescent girls on the appropriate timing for the activities and inform them with any change in the implementation approach resulted from the security situation. Exit strategy will be put in place with communication with community members and other service providers with revision regularly based on the changes happening in the ﬁeld - The risk of disruption of activities due to change of dominant forces on the ground The activities of the project will be locally closed at the level of the communities beneﬁting from the services of the project and inform (local councils, .) about the time of closing the project. Activation of safe evacuation and destruction of documents and posters. Activation the Exit strategy when needed with the line of the GBV SC ethical closure of GBV activities as mitigating measure for GBV programming and check list will be prepared to ensure updating of the GBV exit plan as needed, close consultation with the beneﬁciaries. the lessons learned and the challenges that have been faced with the implementation of the project will be shared with the concerned managements for future use in designing of similar projects.
