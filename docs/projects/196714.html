Project Summary

The project contributes to increasing the resilience of the local community (46%), returnees (40%) and IDPs (14%) in the four neighbourhoods: Karm Jazmati, Seken Shebebi, Karm al Tarrab, and Dahret Awwad. The project will be held by Caritas Germany (CG), planned to be back-funded by the German Federal Foreign Ministry (GFFO) and implemented from 01.04.2023 -31.03.2025 by Caritas Syria (CS). The preparation of the proposal is still in process, therefore all of the numbers are only estimates.
The Outcome 1 aims in 2023 at meeting the basic needs of 10.000 individuals of 4 conflict-affected neighbourhoods, and they are empowered to better protect themselves. 1.1: Hybrid Food Package Distribution (Oil and Sugar; blanket approach); percentage of most vulnerable will receive additional items depending on family size; 1.2.: Hybrid Hygiene Basket Distributions (Shampoo and washing powder; blanket approach); percentage of most vulnerable will receive additional items depending on family size; 1.3: Distribution of winterization items (carpets, blankets, heaters, clothes). Output 2 aims in 2023 at the reduction of protection risks of 2.000 vulnerable families as well as 100 CS staff. The activities are implemented at the community center in Karm al Jazmati. 2.1.: Awareness Sessions for women, men, children and adolescent girls and boys. 2.2.: Training on and implementation of community initiatives for youth, men and women. 2.3.: Psychosocial Support Sessions for elderly, children, women and men. 2.4.: Literacy and life skill courses for women and adolescent girls; 2.5.: Tutoring sessions for schoolchildren and offering a summer club during school holidays. 2.6.: Capacity building and Protection Activities for the Project Staff; 2.7 Case management and referrals for protection cases
The NFI and Food Kits distribution will be coordinated with the respective sectors and alligned with their strategies. The exact modalities that make sense in this context are still being assessed. The protection activities are alligned to the protection sector strategy. The regular coordination with the Aleppo-based sub-sectors Protection, CP, GBV, and FSL, CS ensures avoiding duplication of efforts. The community center in Jazmati has been well received by the residents since its opening in 2019 and continues to be the central point for the provision of integrated community-based protection services, to women, adolescent girls and boys, children, men and elderly.
The Community center follows an open-door policy, ensuring the active engagement of the community, allowing real-time evaluations and timely adjustments of activities. Following a participatory approach, the needs will be regularly assessed, i.e., via PDMs applying a proportional piling method, and FDGs. Furthermore, a robust FCM will contribute to ensure the engagement and thus self-identification of the needs in the community. Hence, throughout the project lifetime, the engagement of all community members will be promoted, establishing a two-way communication with the community. This will ensure a timely incorporation of community feedback.
In response to the feedback collected from beneficiaries, i.e. via PDMs, satisfaction surveys, and FGDs, and following the strategy of the protection sector, the activities are regularly adjusted to the actual needs, or new activities are included.
The target groups are selected interconnectedly, aiming for greater impact within the families and, in turns, the entire community. With a newly established safe referral policy, CS aims to enhance their assistance to cases that need other or more specialized support.

3 - Needs

According to KI with UN agencies in Aleppo and the HNO 2022, the city suffers from the same challenges that is affecting the whole country. The economic hardship as well as lacking basic infrastructure in terms of medical, social, educational and income-generating opportunities leads the most affected families to apply more and more negative coping strategies to survive such as child labour and child marriage. In 2022 winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have become more inaccessible in comparison to the previous years, further deterioration is expected. Especially during the harsh winter seasons, such items are crucial to ensure the well-being. With the UN expecting major funding cuts in the next years, the needs of vulnerable groups for basic assistance (Food, NFIs) are expected to increase. At the same time, protection risks for the most vulnerable are expected to become even more concerning.Despite the ongoing response from various humanitarian actors on the ground, the needs remain significantly high compared to the existing capacities, especially in the suburban areas. The four chosen Neighbourhoods of the project (Karm Al Jazmaty, Karm Al Tarrab, Sakan Al Shababi, and Dahret Awad) are considered the suburban areas of Aleppo that were highly affected by the conflict and lack even the most basic infrastructure such as electricty. A more detailed Needs Assessment is still in the process for the four neighbourhoods.

4 - Implementation of activities

In line with the sector strategy, an integrated approach, targeting all family members, is applied. Trained case workers and volunteers in the CC (except for elderly activity - outreach) conduct activities (3months cycles; each week one awareness session for 8 weeks). Elderly and CP activities run the entire year for the same participants. Sessions’ topics are identified via participatory methods. Protection Activities (2.000 HH + 100 staff):- Provide protection-related awareness sessions to groups of women, youth, and men- Provide non-specialized PSS for women, children and elderly.- Conduct community initiatives for 3 community- Conduct protection related trainings (i.e. youth initiatives), and staff care activities- Literacy and skills courses are provided by trained CS staff enhancing the empowerment of women - boys and girls benefit from interactive learning and PSS sessions and recreational activities during school holidays- Provide Case management and referrals for cases that need more specialised assistanceBasic Assistance (2.000 HH):Hybrid Food Package Distribution: Oil and Sugar (blanket approach); percentage of most vulnerable will receive additional food items depending on family sizeHybrid Hygiene Basket Distributions: Shampoo and washing powder (blanket approach); percentage of most vulnerable will receive additional hygiene items depending on family sizeDistribution of winterization items (carpets, blankets, heaters, clothes)

Distribution areas are spacious and away from crowded areas, accessible for women, older people/ Pwd; 3. Beneficiaries maintain distance of 1.5 meters during distributions; 4. All staff regularly use hand sanitation and follow general hygiene practices


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection risk: Exploitation or abuse of beneﬁciaries, especially children, women, and elderly, by staff members/external service providers when accessing assistance and services Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Ensure knowing and understanding of all Caritas Syria project staff of Caritas Internationalis (CI) Code of Conduct. Caritas follows a zero-tolerance strategy of sexual abuse and exploitation. Supported by an external consultant, CS has reviewed and updated its Safeguarding policies in the first quarter of 2021. 2. Provide trainings and ensure regular recaps of CI Code of Conduct and safeguarding policy among all CS staff members and volunteers 3. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended. In each CS regional office there is a Safeguarding Focal Point appointed to ensure that the policies are followed by each staff member and FCM for beneficiaries and staff are understood and followed-up on. 5. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation, ensuring conﬁdentiality of the reporting. Verbally as well as with sensation material visible in the center or during distributions, beneficiaries are informed about their rights and about safe feedback channels. (https://www.caritas.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Caritas-Internationalis-Children-and-Vulnerable-Adults-Safeguarding-Policy.pdf)
