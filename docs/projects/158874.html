Project Summary

Project Objective: Civilians living in high risk conflict-affected communities in the Gaza Strip are better prepared to protect themselves in case of armed conflict and are empowered with the knowledge they need to mitigate risks associated with explosive weapons.
The proposed interventions will be in line with the NPA Palestine Country Strategy 2020-2023 in Gaza and are aligned with Protection Cluster Objective 1: “Prevent and mitigate the effects of the Occupation and conflict related violence.” Project activities are also specifically aligned with Protection Cluster Objective 2 of the HRP, “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence,” and will contribute to this objective through the provision of Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) and Explosive Remnant of War (ERW) risk education for civilian residents (adults and children) of the Gaza Strip.
The proposed interventions are planned to take place in 2020, representing the third year of NPA's CPP implementation for 2018-2020. Over three-year programme period, the programme will be implemented using the same methodologies; training adults and children, but for new beneficiaries and new communities which will be prioritized based on Ministry of Education planning and in consultation with the Protection Cluster and the Mine Action Working Group.

1 - Needs

Since the 2014 war on the Gaza Strip, civilians have witnessed dramatic changes in all aspects of their lives, including their safety and security. Gaza remains in a state of protracted low intensity conflict in which Israeli military incursions and airstrikes are sporadic yet common place. People living in Gaza are exposed to perpetual uncertainty and political instability that could surface and become volatile with very short notice, or completely unexpectedly. Surrounded by the possibility of military escalation in the Gaza Strip, its residents and communities must be prepared for this eventuality and preemptively implement safety measures to mitigate the risks associated with armed conflict. By far the greatest conflict related risk threatening civilian life in Gaza is the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Explosive weapons such as aerial bombs, missiles, projectiles, etc., cause three simultaneous effects: blast, heat, and fragmentation. Experiences from previous wars in Gaza clearly tell a story that local populations unable to seek refuge during use of explosive weapons have a better chance for survival through better preparedness. Safety and preparedness measures exist which can help civilians mitigate the risks associated with the three effects of an explosion and help to improve civilian survivability during and/or after an attack occurs. Knowledge of safety measures, risk mitigation techniques, proper evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, and basic First Aid can help civilians prevent loss of life either during or in the aftermath of an attack. Public ERW awareness also plays a critical role in protecting civilians against ERW which can litter populated areas during a conflict and pose a threat long after an attack occurs. So far in 2019, for instance, there have been a number of accidents involving ERW in Gaza resulting in 10 casualties. Since 2014, a total of 163 people in Gaza have fallen victim to ERW (18 dead, 163 injured) and the prospect of renewed high-intensity conflict underscores the need to educate civilians about these threats. Despite the clear and present need to raise awareness about emergency preparedness and protection in Gaza, concerted efforts focused on improving individual and household risk mitigation measures have been limited. A field survey conducted in 2015 by NPA with 1,689 head-of-households (465 women, 1224 men) in vulnerable areas of Gaza showed that the vast majority of civilians (99.98%) in these areas had never received emergency preparedness and risk education training, many civilians had little to no first aid (84%) or fire safety (91%) experience, and the presence of ERW was still a perceived threat (93%). Civilians surveyed generally held many misconceptions about what constituted safe behaviour during a conflict and lacked basic information that could help improve the safety of themselves and their loved ones in the event of an attack. The implementation of Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) emergency preparedness and risk education training sessions in schools and communities in 2016-19 has proven effective at improving public awareness about conflict-related risks and informing people about the safety measures they can implement to improve their safety during an emergency. NPA’s pre-/post-training testing of both child and adult CPP beneficiaries has demonstrated that beneficiaries retain the information taught and are far more knowledgeable than the baseline (ie. untrained) regarding what actions and positions are safest to take during an attack, how to protect one’s head and body, how to breathe, how to safely evacuate a building, and what they should do if they encounter ERW. By emphasizing that there are indeed actions which civilians can take to better protect themselves during conflict, emergency preparedness training is helping to empower civilians, motivate them to seek adequate protection, and combat feelings of indifference or helplessness.

2 - Activities

As a leading global advocate against the use of explosive weapons in populated areas(EWIPA), Norwegian People’s Aid aims to remedy the existing gap regarding emergency preparedness risk education in Gaza and other conflict affected countries throughout the world. NPA’s CPP) initiative empowers civilians to mitigate risks associated with armed conflict and explosive weapons. This project will utilize NPA’s existing CPP framework, methodologies, and educational materials which have been effectively employed in Gaza since 2015 to expand the provision of emergency preparedness risk education training to adult heads of household and school aged children. To date, NPA has provided emergency preparedness risk education training to over 67,648 beneficiaries across the Gaza Strip in cooperation with the MoEHL and other local partners. Participants of NPA's CPP risk education trainings receive instruction on the following topics: home preparation and blast protection safety measures, conflict risk mitigation measures, safe evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness, and improvised First Aid solutions. NPA’s CPP framework utilizes separate curriculums for adults and children, designed to convey safety messaging in a manner which is appropriate and effective for each age group. All training sessions incorporate high quality training aids, educational materials, and media to enhance the learning experience. This project aims to achieve its objective via the following activities; conducted in partnership with the MoEHL, the UN Mine Action Service (UNMAS), and the Ma’an Development Center: Activity 1: Provision of CPP and ERW risk education training for children (girls and boys) in vulnerable conflict affected communities in the Gaza Strip; 1.1. Train 50 school teachers to deliver emergency preparedness and risk education training in the classroom and equip each with high quality training aids and informational print materials; 1.2. Conduct CPP emergency preparedness and risk education training for children attending government elementary and secondary schools through interactive in classroom training sessions; 1.3. Conduct educational CPP summer activities (theatre performances, games, film screenings, etc.) for children, promoting awareness of emergency preparedness and risk education principles through interactive and child-friendly approaches; Activity 2: Provision of CPP and ERW risk education and support for adult heads of household (women and men) 2.1. Conduct CPP risk education training for adult heads of household across Gaza’s five districts through community-based training sessions; 2.2. Provide emergency equipment and supplies (grab bags, First Aid kits, fire extinguishers, etc.) to households in Gaza’s most vulnerable conflict affected communities using voucher mechanism; Target Locations: Project locations will be selected in coordination with the MoEHL based on assessed need and coverage under past programming. All five governorates of the Gaza Strip will be targeted, with priority placed on high-risk communities in north, east, and south Gaza adjacent to the border with Israel. Gender/Age: Selection of schools targeted under this project will be done in such a way as to ensure a proper balance of female and male student beneficiaries. CPP training activities in elementary schools will be targeted at children in grades 2-9, while peer-to-peer trainings at secondary schools will target grades 10-11. NPA will also coordinate with the MoEHL to ensure that an equal number of female and male teachers are selected to implement CPP training in their respective schools. For adult focused emergency preparedness and risk education activities, NPA will work with the MoEHL and with local partners to ensure a high female participation rate. All educational materials and media used in this project have been designed to be gender inclusive and to promote women’s empowerment.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Preventing harm to civilians is the core of NPA’s CPP programme and, as such, ensuring the safety and dignity of targeted communities is critical to our project plan. NPA is committed to the humanitarian principle of ‘do no harm’ and has designed this project’s activities to empower beneficiaries and mitigate threats against them without jeopardizing their wellbeing. Special considerations regarding age, gender, disability, and displacement, among others, will be considered at all stages of the project.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NPA’s CPP framework has been designed to meet the needs of those most vulnerable to the direct and indirect effects of armed conflict. NPA will prioritize targeting accessible at-risk areas and communities most severely affected by past conflict, as well as vulnerable populations such as women, children, people with disabilities (PwD) and caregivers, and refugees where possible. In 2018, NPA has worked with its partner, the Deir El Baleh Rehabilitation Society (DBRS), to adapt its CPP its messaging and methodologies to better meet the needs of PwD and these changes will continue to be implemented under our 2020 programme activities.
