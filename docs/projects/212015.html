Project Summary

The project addresses the longstanding formal education crisis in Syria, where 30-35% of school-aged children are not in school. Many families can no longer sustain education due to economic hardship and safety concerns. This initiative aims to boost school enrollment and reduce illiteracy, child labor, and crime among adolescents in northern Syria. Key outcomes include financial incentives for teachers, provision of school supplies, hygiene tools, school equipment, rehabilitation of damaged schools, and teacher training. Direct implementation by IR Syria, in coordination with the Education cluster, ensures effective identification of schools and beneficiaries. targeting 23712 students.
Needs:
According to HNO 2024, The crisis in Syria compounded by earthquakes, severely impacts education. 7.246 million children and educators, including 6.9 million children aged 5 to 17 and 230,000 education personnel, require urgent support. Over 2.4549 million Out of School Children (OoSC) face dropout risks, especially in conflict-affected areas like Idleb. Economic hardships hinder access to education, exacerbated by inadequate infrastructure and a shortage of qualified teachers. Attacks on schools further disrupt learning and endanger children's safety.
Intervention and Implementation of Activities:
The project focuses on increasing school enrollment in northern Syria through financial incentives for teachers, school supplies, heating materials, hygiene tools, equipment provision, school rehabilitation, and teacher training. Direct implementation by IR Syria, in coordination with the Education cluster, ensures targeted identification of schools and beneficiaries.
Protection Risk Analysis:
Exposure to Sexual Exploitation and Harassment: Medium probability. Mitigation: Awareness sessions, communication means, and a Code of Conduct.
Traditional Barriers for Boys and Girls: Medium probability. Mitigation: Local community awareness sessions.
Inaccessibility for People with Disabilities: Medium probability. Mitigation: Infrastructure improvements, psychological support, awareness, and referrals.
Lack of Privacy in Schools: Medium probability. Mitigation: Gender-specific facilities, segregation, awareness, and case management.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy:
Mitigating PSEA risks involves staff training, Code of Conduct adherence, awareness for beneficiaries, and emphasizing PSEA in MoUs with the Directorate of Education. A focal point in each school manages PSEA and GBV cases, ensuring effective reporting channels.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:
No Access to Project Location: Mitigation involves changing the project location in coordination with stakeholders.
Closure of Turkish Border: Alternative material sources or coordination with Turkish authorities will be sought.
Interference of Armed Groups: Engagement with stakeholders ensures the non-involvement of armed groups.
Risk of Children Dropping Out: Specific criteria and parental meetings encourage school attendance.
Spread of Cholera: Awareness sessions, WHO guidelines dissemination, and coordination with health centers are preventive measures.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In response to the enduring education crisis in Syria, where a significant percentage of school-aged children (30-35%) are not enrolled due to economic hardships and safety concerns, this project seeks to revitalize education in northern Syria. Over twelve years into the crisis, families can no longer sustain education for their children, viewing schools as neither safe nor a beacon of hope for a better future. The initiative targets school-age children (5-17 years) and aims to enhance school enrollment rates, reduce illiteracy, child labor, and adolescent crime in northern Syria. The project's multifaceted approach includes financial incentives for teachers, provision of school supplies, hygiene tools, school equipment, rehabilitation of damaged schools, and teacher training. These activities address the core issues hindering education, ensuring a conducive learning environment. IR Syria's direct implementation, coupled with coordination with the Education cluster and education directorates, guarantees effective identification of schools and beneficiaries. By providing financial incentives to teachers, the project not only ensures the continuity of the education process but also raises material income levels for teachers' families. The provision of school supplies, hygiene tools, and equipment creates a conducive and safe learning environment. The Protection Risk Analysis identifies potential risks associated with the project's implementation. Exposure to sexual exploitation and harassment, traditional barriers for boys and girls, inaccessibility for people with disabilities, and the lack of privacy in schools are acknowledged. Mitigating measures include awareness sessions, communication means, a Code of Conduct, local community awareness, infrastructure improvements, psychological support, gender-specific facilities, and effective case management. The PSEA strategy emphasizes comprehensive mitigation measures, including staff training, Code of Conduct adherence, awareness sessions for beneficiaries, and incorporation of PSEA in MoUs with the Directorate of Education. A focal point in each school is designated to manage PSEA and GBV cases, ensuring efficient reporting channels and case management. The risks, assumptions, and mitigating measures encompass potential challenges such as no access to the project location, closure of the Turkish border, interference of armed groups, risk of children dropping out, and the spread of cholera. Mitigation strategies involve changing project locations, seeking alternative material sources, engaging stakeholders to prevent armed group involvement, implementing specific criteria for child acceptance, and conducting preventive measures against cholera. Through these comprehensive strategies, the project aims not only to increase school enrollment but also to create a safe and inclusive learning environment for children in northern Syria, mitigating risks and fostering long-term educational resilience.
