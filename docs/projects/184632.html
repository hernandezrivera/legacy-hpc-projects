Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to contribute towards the improvement of living conditions of Drought, Covid-19, and locust-affected communities in target areas of Sool and Sanaag regions in Somaliland through the supply of clean and safe water, appropriate sanitation, and knowledge of protection mechanisms against water-borne diseases. The WASH intervention of HAVOYOCO will contribute to the life-saving of most vulnerable communities and it will improve the condition and good practice of hygiene through the provision of clean water and in extensive hygiene awareness campaigns. In response to the current crisis. the project will provide much-needed humanitarian assistance to vulnerable people in targeted areas of Lasanod and Erigavo districts. About 13,220 numbers of beneficiaries will benefit from the project intervention. The proposed actions will include, water trucking/water voucher rehabilitation of Berkads, construction of new Berkads, rehabilitation and equipping of boreholes, construction of gender-sensitive Latrines, and hygiene and sanitation promotion activities. Moreover, a total of 9 water and sanitation committees will be identified and trained on safe water handling, hygiene promotion, and water source management. The proposed project will ensure community involvement and participation at all stages. This will promote ownership and in turn ensure the sustainability of the projects. Training and capacity building of water committees selected by the community and the use of Hygiene volunteers from the target villages will ensure continuity of the program beyond the project lifetime.
According to the HAVOYOCO rapid assessment 17-21 September 2021, about 70 percent of the population in the assessed area are in need of urgent water trucking due to acute water shortage, similarly,40 % of existing water points are broken and non-functional. There is huge shortage of water in all the assessed areas and people are susceptible to acute watery diarrhea. Women, girls, the elderly, lactating, and pregnant mothers are most affected. Increased distance to water sources further affects women and girls who are traditionally tasked with water collection. There are no permanent functional water sources except two Boreholes and one semi-borehole in Shululux, Waxari-kaciday and Yeyle which are in need of rehabilitation, maintenance and equipment while the assessed areas in Sanaag are mostly experiencing challenges related to access of safe and sufficient water for both human and livestock consumption. The majority of the community never treat their drinking water, open defecation is widespread with very few washing their hands with soap. These coupled with poor hygiene knowledge & practices and inadequate access to healthcare services exposes women & children to be more susceptible to increased risk of diseases and high mortality rate. Poor and erratic rains cause severe depletion of pasture and water triggering food insecurity. When competition for water and pasture sets in, inter-community hostilities and conflicts, including population displacements occur. In response to these crises, HAVOYOCO proposes in response to the expected crises to provide humanitarian assistance and response to 13,220 vulnerable targeted people in Erigavo and Lasanod, districts. The proposed Intervention include access to water voucher, rehabilitation of Berkads and Shallow wells, hygiene, and sanitation promotion activities including hygiene campaigns, distribution of sanitary kits and construction of gender-sensitive latrines and capacity building of the WASH committee

1. - Projects activities

The interventions are relevant to WASH cluster objectives, the project will carry out water and sanitation intervention to increase clean water access through water voucher, rehabilitation of water sources, improving hygiene promotion, construction of gender-sensetive latrines according to WASH cluster standards, as well as building the capacity of community Wash committees. The project will target 9 villages under Sool and sanaag regions that are facing acute water shortage according to the latest assessments including HAVOYOCO's rapid assessment in September 2021, and OCHA's latest report.
