Project Summary

Since October 2016, the political conflict and security crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon has led to the displacement of more than 705,000 people internally and over 61,000 people to Nigeria. Conflict related displacement and violence have led to the deterioration of basic services in the North West Region coupled with rising protection, health and nutrition needs among the affected population. In response to this crisis, International Medical Corps proposes to support the Ministry of Health (MoH) in providing integrated health (including mental health) and nutrition services for IDPs as well as host communities affected by conflict in the Mbengwi and Njikwa Health districts.
Targeted beneficiaries will receive free primary health care, nutrition and mental health services through 4 supported health facilities and at the community level with the support of Ministry of Health (MoH) staff as well as IMC staff to be recruited under the project. Under the health sector, IMC will support the delivery of curative consultations; reproductive health services; the provision of pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, and commodities; training and supervision of health staff and community health workers; community outreach services which include community-based disease surveillance and the integrated community case management of common diseases such as malaria, respiratory tract infections and diarrhea; the strengthening of the referral system between primary and secondary care; and mental health and psychosocial support services. A referral and counter referral system will be set up which will involve the payment of bills for beneficiaries referred for secondary and tertiary care. Under the nutrition sector, IMC will support both the treatment and prevention and malnutrition through support of outpatient treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) in the 4 health facilities; screening in communities and in health facilities; training of health staff and CHWs; and actions focused on improving IYCF practices through social behavior change, including mother support groups and cooking demonstrations.
The intervention will not create a parallel system but will rather support the existing system which has been weakened as a result of the protracted crisis. The implementation strategy is designed based on findings of a needs assessment conducted by International Medical Corps in seven health districts in the North West and South West regions. The selection of the four health districts in the North West has been motivated by the number of displaced persons, the absence of other partners, as well as other health and nutrition indicators and logistical and security considerations.
Program Goal:
To contribute to the improvement of the health and nutrition status of displaced persons and vulnerable communities affected by conflict in the North West region of Cameroon.
Purpose(s):
To contribute to the improvement of the health and nutrition status of displaced persons and vulnerable communities affected by conflict in the North West region of Cameroon through the provision of quality health care and nutrition services including mental health and community-based surveillance of priority diseases.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since 2016, the security crisis in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon has led to the displacement of more than 705,000 people internally and over 61,000 people to Nigeria according to the OCHA multi-sector needs assessment conducted in August 2020, and the UNCHR registration of Cameroonian refugees as of October 2020, respectively. As a result of this displacement, in addition to the loss of basic infrastructure and livelihoods, the crisis is estimated to have affected 3 million people in the region, of which 2.3 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. This includes approximately 1.1 million children and adolescents in need of child protection assistance, 1.4 million women in need of gender-based violence assistance, 2.4 million people in need of emergency health assistance, and 360,000 children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in need of nutrition assistance. Among these groups, IDPs, returnees, and host community members are expected to be the most affected and with acute needs. From September 7 to October 30, 2020, International Medical Corps conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in the South West and North West Regions with the aim of assessing the needs of IDPs and vulnerable host communities with respect to health care and nutrition. Quantitative and qualitative data was collected through a desk review of secondary sources in addition to an assessment of 24 health facilities in seven health districts using a health facility checklist, a community based assessment, focus group discussions with community members (4 groups per health district), and key informant interviews with key health district authorities (six district medical officers) and 10 community leaders (chiefs, community leaders and administrative authorities). The major needs identified by sector are provided below: Concerning healthcare, there is a lack of adequate medical staff and infrastructure with more than 35% (255 out of 723) of the Government run health facilities not functional in the two regions as of 2019 as per the IOM MNSA conducted in August 2019. The remaining health facilities are/were only partially functioning. Attacks targeting health personnel have further reduced the deployment and availability of human resources and access to health facilities by the population. Furthermore, only 49.4% of the population have access to health services due to the distance from the health facilities (43.6%); closure of health facilities (16.5%) and high cost of healthcare (17.2%). International Medical Corps’ comprehensive needs assessment in 7 health districts in both regions found that only 74% of these health facilities were functional, consistent with the findings of the IOM MNSA conducted in 2019 when considering IMC’s assessment only covered 23 accessible health facilities. According to IMC’s comprehensive needs assessment, mental disorders are the 6th most common cause of health seeking consultation in both regions representing a heavy burden, for both health workers and the community. The number of those with mental health disorders is likely higher than the number of consultations, as mental health cases are poorly reported in the health facilities due to the lack of training of health staff on mental health conditions. Based on MUAC screening conducted by nutrition cluster partners from January to August 2020, the severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence among the children screened was 1% (1.1% in NW and 0.4% in SW) and the moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) prevalence among children screened was 2% (3.7% in NW and 1.1% in SW). It should be noted that not all areas of the North West are conducting regular screenings, and children that are acutely malnourished as per low weight-for-height are not being identified in screenings, so these figures are likely to be an underestimation of the actual situation. According to IMC’s needs assessment, food is the first need expressed by the IDPs.
