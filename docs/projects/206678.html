Project Summary

The outbreak of fighting in Sudan on April 15, 2023, has triggered a devastating humanitarian crisis in neighboring South Sudan, particularly in the country's northern states. Since the onset of the conflict, a massive influx of people fleeing Sudan has overwhelmed the border points between the two countries, primarily in Upper Nile, Northern, and Western Bahr el Ghazal States. As of November 22, 2023, over 394,006 individuals have crossed the border, with the majority entering through the Wunthow border point in Upper Nile, seeking refuge from the ongoing violence.
Since the start of the Sudan crisis, IOM has facilitated over 118,000 returnees with Onward Transportation Assistance. In 2024, IOM seeks to assist an additional 150,000 returnees with OTA assistance so that they can reach their final destinations. Onward transportation assistance is a critical component of the Emergency Response Plan (ERP) and will be facilitated by IOM via river, air, and road to ensure that returnees reach their final destinations safely and securely. IOM will also provide protection and basic needs assistance to South Sudanese returnees at transit and reception locations, offering multi-modal transportation options to their final destinations. Additionally, IOM will support local authorities providing transportation to returnees and collaborate closely with them to facilitate the onward movement from border crossing points to transit locations and onwards to final destinations. Cash assistance will be provided to returnees to help cover last-mile transportation costs from state capitals to their final destinations.
IOM will continue to conduct rapid vulnerability screenings in order to identify and register the most vulnerable people at points of entry (PoE) and ascertain their voluntariness to return to their areas of origin or intended destination (if different from area of origin) and thereafter facilitate OTA. Prior to provision of movement assistance, protection safeguards through provision of specialized protection interventions will be undertaken for individuals with heightened protection needs, risks and vulnerabilities including unaccompanied children and victims of violence, exploitation, and abuse.

1. Needs

S outh Sudan is facing an increasing influx of returnees from Sudan due to the ongoing conflict in that country. Many of these returnees are arriving in South Sudan with limited resources and no means of transportation to their final destinations. As a result, there is a critical need for OTA to support these returnees in reaching their homes and communitiesAs of 19 November 22, 2023, more than 394006 individuals have arrived in South Sudan, mostly though Renk. 83% of the arrivals are South Sudanese, 15% are Sudanese, and 2% are from other nationalities. These figures represent only those identified by humanitarian actors at borders, thus actual figures might be higher than reported. Among the arrivals, 49% are female, more than 46% are below 18, while 4% are older than 60 years. The people reaching the border are exhausted and without resources to proceed to their destination. South Sudan has a limited transportation infrastructure, making it difficult for returnees to travel to their homes and communities. This is especially true for returnees who are located in remote areas. As of November 19, 2023, IOM has supported more than 118,000 returnees with OTA, but more assistance is needed. On average 1500-2000 people enter South Sudan from Sudan with over 80% of them are returnees. Estimates from the HCT and data based on the arrivals; suggest that 40% of the projected new arrivals will need OTA meaning an additional 150,000 individuals could be stranded in Renk and other PoE location. Protection monitoring conducted at PoEs indicates a high number of vulnerable arrivals, including unaccompanied children, PwDs, and those with urgent medical needs. According to the last head count conducted by IOM DTM , an estimated 26,892 returnees are in Transit and collective centers and 26,785 returnees have been integrated into host communities. Furthermore, on average there are 2000-4000 returnees in Malakal waiting for their onward flights and boats every week. According Joint Intention Survey conducted in October 2023, in Renk transit over 88% of returnees indicated that they wish to go to their areas of origin or relocation.

2. Project activities

Registration and vulnerability screening: IOM will continue to conduct vulnerability screenings to identify, register and prioritize the most vulnerable individuals at PoE and ascertain their voluntariness to return to their areas of origin or relocation. Registration and vulnerability screening also allows the provision of documentation, humanitarian services, and referrals to other partners for the returnees. This activity will be implemented at the point of entries (PoE), transit centers, docking station and airport.Chartered flights: To facilitate the return of displaced people to their areas of origin or relocation, IOM will arrange charter flights from various locations (Malakal) to the nearest accessible points (Wau, Bentiu, Kuajok and Juba). IOM has already contracted several air transport service providers for this purpose. The charter flights will be offered to those returnees who face challenges in travelling by road or boats due to lack of infrastructure and security risks. The cost of air transportation is high, as the available aircraft are small and can only land at limited airports. Voucher for boat movement and fuel supply: Transportation Token/Tickets: IOM provides transportation tokens/tickets to returnees who want to travel from Renk to Malakal and then to other places by boat, such as Unity, Fangak and so on. To facilitate the movement of returnees from Renk, Malakal and other places IOM will collaborate with the Government of South Sudan and other private entities/NGOs that run boats/vehicles by supplying them with fuel.Cash assistance for last-mile movement: IOM will provide one-time cash assistance equivalent to USD 20 for last-mile movement to the final destination. The cash amount is not determined by the household size, but by the individual needs of men, women, boys and girls.Improve conditions of Transit site: The transit site in Malakal on average can host 3000-5000 returnees, which needs continous repair and maintaintaince. As the transit site is part of OTA, so IOM will continue to maintain and operate the tranist siteHealth screening: IOM will continue to support returnees with health screening while on transit
