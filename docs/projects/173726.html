Project Summary

UNICEF is both a service provider under the partner presence rationalization in the sector and the provider of last resort (POLR). The largest response is in NE Nigeria (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states) where protracted armed conflict has continued to force displacement of population into camps and camp-like settlements for more than a decade. In other regions, the need for response arise from sporadic conflicts, natural disasters like floods or risks of outbreaks/spread of infectious diseases such as cholera, which is endemic in Nigeria, and the recent covid-19 or other hazards. Gaps also arise as a results of new population displacements or diminished capacity/departure of other humanitarian WASH partners. As a service provider, UNICEF is optimizing the water production and distribution, increasing attainment of the required minimum standards. UNICEF is rolling out the approach of urban type network water distribution in deep field LGAs with high yielding boreholes and high-density population of displaced persons or returnees and host communities. This approach will support decongestion in camps and expansion of WASH services to schools and health/nutrition facilities. UNICEF will also expand cash in wash programming for hygiene kits and other WASH NFIs.
Through this project, UNICEF will have specific target populations in needs in IDP camps in Borno State, specifically in Maiduguri Metropolitan City and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kara Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala), whilst in Adamawa and Yobe, and possibly other states, the target population will be determined by locations where needs will arise. UNICEF will adhere to the SPHERE standards and scale up the sustenance of the services as needed.
To meet the demands of the POLR responsibility, UNICEF may need to mobilize additional resources. Through this project UNICEF will provide the initial lifesaving response to fill the gaps, prioritizing areas receiving newly displaced people or under an outbreak of cholera, hepatitis or other WASH-related diseases including covid-19. UNICEF will strive to sustain the basic initial WASH services until further response becomes available from other sector partners. The project is also aimed at sustaining critical humanitarian water, sanitation and hygiene services to displaced population affected by protracted armed conflict in NE Nigeria (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States: target 5,540,800 people) and emergency affected population in four states including other locations. 500,000 of the target population are at risk of covid-19 infection.
The project objectives and targets are:
1) Affected people have safe and equitable access to safe water for domestic needs, as per SPHERE standards. This objective aims at scaling up sustainable water supply coverage in IDP camps to reach 15 litres per person per day. Target: 624,000 people.
2) Affected people have sustained access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene. Target 1,261,670 people.
3) Affected people have safe and dignified access to gender and disability-sensitive facilities at a rate of 20 users/pit for latrines. This objective aims at scaling up sustainable sanitation coverage in
IDP camps to reach latrine coverage standard of 20 users per latrine. Target: 229,320 people.
4) Affected people sustainably access clean, disinfected and desludged latrines. Target 875,520 people.
5) Affected people reached with key hygiene messages. This objective aims at reaching 1,985,000 people in IDP camps and host communities and other affected locations with risk informed behaviour change communication messages in 2021.
6) Affected people living in camps and camp-like settlement reached with critical WASH supplies and/or cash for NFIs. This objective will also cover WASH supplies to newly affected people in all locations with initial response hygiene kits. Target: 500,000 people.

1 - Needs

According to the MSNA data, 2.8 million people, comprising 583,000 children (57%) and 200,000 women (20%) lack sufficient access to safe water, while 1.3 million children and 487,000 women have no access to sanitation and 1.6 million children and 576,000 women have no access to a hand washing facilities with running water and soap. Area-specific data from camps and settlements highlight the specific needs relative to the sector standards. These include:a) Water Supply to ensure 15ltrs/person/day: humanitarian support is required for the management of operations, maintenance and repairs on handpumps, motorized systems (hybrid and solar) and efficient restocking of consumables for water treatment and safe collection, transportation and storage to meet needs for sustained access to chlorinated water in sufficient quantities. Community ownership, recently introduced, require strengthening to forestall vandalism and theft of submersible pumps in locations with high water stress issues.b) Access to Sanitation: more than 20 people share a latrine, and some opt to defecate in the open because of poor hygiene behaviours, inadequate space or landowners declining to give access to their lands to allow construction of more latrines. c) Lifespan of latrine designs: The impact of adverse weather and high dislodging needs accelerate the wear and tear of emergency design of facilities. A prolonged delay of policy shift and investment to improve designs have left thousands of sanitation facilities not functional. More permanent solutions are required given that the residence of displacement persons in camps is protracted, now exceeding a decade since the beginning of the armed conflict. d) The interim solution of regular desludging of latrines is not cost effective in the long term. Major investment is needed for an integral faecal sludge management from containment, collection, transportation and disposal of faecal sludge from camps for displaced persons and host communities. The faecal sludge management is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desludging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for yearly outbreaks of cholera. Additionally, flooding is a major concern as most of the faecal sludge worsens with households facing exposure of untreated waste. e) Hygiene promotion and environmental awareness: Due to low awareness of personal hygiene, the need to train more hygiene promoters especially due to COVID 19 outbreak is critical. The WASH sector has also included environmental hygiene clean ups through mass mobilization campaigns. Experience over the years highlights a need of providing cleaning materials to support environmental campaigns, mainly in schools and other social places (like markets) which are hotspots for cholera and AWD outbreaks .f) Supply and replenishment of hygiene kits and other WASH NFI is not keeping up with the needs. Supplies are depleted and replenishments are provided long beyond useful life of the commodities.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement seven key activities (outlined below) through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts, Small Scale Funding Agreements (SSFA) or Project Cooperation Agreements (PCA) with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with the state governments in Borno, Adamawa, Yobe, Sokoto and Kano. This project will consolidate UNICEF capacity as a sector partner implementing in allocated locations/camps in Maiduguri Municipality and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kala Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala), where UNICEF will reach SPHERE standards. Additional resources will be needed for UNICEF to play the second role of provider of last resort (POLR) where there is no other capacity from the sector. The key activities to be implemented under this project are: 1) Water trucking for limited periods to provide temporary access to safe water to newly displaced people or people affected by water, sanitation and hygiene related disease outbreaks. UNICEF will deploy LTA contractors on water trucking to the areas in need. The same population will be provided more permanent water sources. Target: 60,000 people (also part of the target below). 2) Optimizing water production, storage and distribution to provide 15 litres per person per day, increase storage and expand distribution to reduce water collection time to within 30 minutes round trip including queuing time. This is based on assessed needs and tested optimal water production of existing water resources. Dysfunctional facilities will be repaired or rehabilitated according to needs. Higher capacity pumps matching the tested safe water pumping will be installed. Storage tank capacities will be increased to cover two days' water demand in case of breakdowns. Priority are large population camps with high capacity boreholes. This activity will optimize benefit for the affected population and enable possible development of water supply for host communities and other population. Construction of new facilities will be a last resort when there are no pre-existing facilities. Target: 624,000 people. 3) Daily chlorination and management of the operation and maintenance of water supply services to sustain access to safe water in IDP camps and urban areas in Maiduguri, Yola and Damaturu. Implementing partners will support the management of existing water systems in camps allocated to UNICEF: daily chlorination of water using automatic chlorine dossers; monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels and faecal contamination of the water at production point and at the shelters using the water; and organizing orderly queuing at the water collection points and support repairs in case of breakdown. The target is to restore service within 48 hours. Distribution of water treatment chemicals to Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States for municipal water treatment and distribution in cholera affected LGAs. Target: 1,261,670 people. 4) Increasing access to safe sanitation. UNICEF will construct additional latrines to reach the SPHERE standard. Target 229,320 people. 5) Daily cleaning and disinfection of latrines including desludging latrines in allocated camps and per demand from CCCM for other camps. Target: 875,520 people. 6) Distribution of hygiene kits including cash for kits in Maiduguri. Target: 500,000 (the same people are part of the below target)7) Risk informed hygiene promotion in camps and host communities, and cholera hotspots, using a range of communication channels. Target: 1,985,000 people.

7.1.d - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

The project will strengthen the safety of water supply chain used by unregulated water vendors, explore the extent of expanding water supply distribution networks to host communities and other population in the LGAs.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The project will improve living conditions and build resilience to cope by contributing to healthy living conditions of safe hygiene behaviours, adequate access to safe water and improved sanitation. The project interventions will strengthen the capacity of the beneficiaries to prevent occurrence or breaking out of water and sanitation related infectious diseases. The beneficiaries will also improve their knowledge and hygiene practices. Taken together, these interventions will lead to prevention of diarrhoea and so improve nutrition.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Safety and dignity of the beneficiaries are high priorities. The beneficiaries are advised of the programme interventions and their representatives are always consulted throughout the project process. Service personnel are trained and made aware about preventions of sexual exploitation and abuse and gender-based violence at the work sites. Reporting mechanisms are in place, with mandatory obligation on staff and service personnel to report suspected or actual incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. Beneficiaries are trained on how to properly use facilities provided through the humanitarian response. This is done to avoid injuries or accidents. Contractors are obliged to provide personal protection equipment to their personnel and this is strictly enforced by the monitors. Finally, all monitors and the contractors are oriented on GBV and commit to observing the highest standards of preventing it and reporting it if observed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF over the years has worked closely with community leaders and community-based volunteers and network groups in the design, implementation and monitoring of services including taking feedback from these groups for improvement in service delivery. The community are also reached through the "U-Report", a social platform created by UNICEF, available via SMS, Facebook and Twitter where members of the community express their opinion and play the role of positive agent of change in their communities. During this funding window, similar consultations and experiences from all were considered for the designing of this project The project will build a strong community ownership with implementation and monitoring being done jointly with thecommunity hygiene volunteers and the Camp Coordination and Camp Management on one hand and the traditional leaders recognized by the beneficiaries on the other hand who will serve as community liaison on the project providing community feedback through their traditional community meetings.
