Project Summary

DCA has developed a curriculum framework for female Rohingya youth and adolescents to support their acquisition of foundational literacy, numeracy, and life skills and to enable them to more easily navigate the daily challenges of living in the camps. The project will continue to reach female youth and adolescents through the delivery of the first level of the curriculum framework and reach the previous graduates of DCA’s programs through the development and implementation of a second level of the curriculum. DCA recently conducted a study with female Rohingya youth and findings show learners desired longer learning cycles and an opportunity to advance to another level where they could acquire additional skills. DCA will continue to deliver learning sessions in female only, cross-sectoral spaces and through a home based approach that accounts for the mobility restrictions women and girls face. Additionally, a recent study on the gendered impact of COVID-19 highlighted how the pandemic will likely exacerbate existing challenges for women and girls to access education so DCA will work with its Alternative Learning Committees to raise awareness on school reentry. DCA will continue to reach learners through a low tech, self-regulated learning process. Based on DCA’s successful pilot of remote training using low-tech resources, this project will use a blended approach to remote teacher training for both host community and Rohingya female teachers using a combination of video conferencing and WhatsApp for host community teachers and interactive audio instruction using MP3 players for Rohingya teachers. This project will support the continuous professional development of Rohingya and host community female teachers, provide critical learning opportunities for women and girls, support the development of structured curricular material for adolescents, and develop a guidance and strategy for self-guided learning that can support other Education Sector partners who are also implementing continuous education sessions.

1 - Identified Needs

The education response to the Rohingya crisis in Cox’s Bazar is well into its third year. And while significant progress has been made providing safe and systemic access to education opportunities, particularly to children aged 6-14, the Education Sector has identified over 500,000 Rohingya children, adolescents, and youth still in need of education services. Even more alarmingly, according to the 2019 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, 83% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old do not have access to any educational activities. Access to sustained formal and non-formal education services for girls and women is further compounded by sociocultural barriers, domestic support roles, movement restrictions, and significant safety and security risks within the camps. DCA recently conducted a study with Rohingya adolescent and youth women and girls which showed that of the respondents, only 8.7% were participating in any sort of educational activity. Only 2.7% reported attending secondary school and 100% were identified as preliterate or nonliterate in English and Myanmar language. Semi-structured teaching and learning materials for this population are needed to support quality education. DCA will continue to print and implement its own Literacy and Numeracy curriculum and develop a second level of the framework for those who have completed the previous iteration. The 2020 MSNA notes that the most frequently reported challenge for remote learning was lack of learning materials. The development of contextualized and age-appropriate materials to support learning is critical to reverse the learning losses due to COVID-19. Additionally, according to the Education Sector Strategy, of the 6,000 learning facilities, only 1% are considered cross-sector shared learning facilities. Because education activities can be implemented in existing structures and integrated with other partners’ activities such as GBV, PSS, DRR, the Strategy has advocated for the use of cross-sector shared spaces as the best way to reach marginalized populations. DCA already operates its activities in these co-shared spaces as well as through a home-based approach and will continue to do so, bearing in mind considerations for COVID-19 safety measures and aligned with the Back to School Guidance. The home-based approach also allows DCA to reach learners with physical disabilities who are unable to move to the spaces. DCA will use the Washington Group questions to screen for disability and integrate inclusive education into all training modules. Findings from the Rapid Gender Analysis show that the current COVID-19 crisis increases the risks for women and girls as forced and voluntary isolation in many contexts is proven to lead to an increase in intimate partner violence, women engaging in risky coping strategies, lack of access to SRH, and increased stress for women. The project will be integrated with DCA’s GBV interventions to ensure inter-sectoral linkages and provide holistic programming to women and girls. Teacher professional development for both host community teachers and Rohingya teachers remains a major need. DCA has developed a Remote Teachers in Crisis Contexts Training Package to support remote teacher training. Furthermore, continued PSS and PSEA training for both Rohingya and host community teachers is necessary to promote positive psychosocial well-being and ensure there is a safe and supportive space where participants can learn, develop relevant competences to face current challenges, and cultivate practical coping strategies. DCA’s recent study also highlighted the need for community awareness raising. Rohingya women and girls emphasized that in order for them to be allowed to participate in education sessions, community leaders needed to sensitize household heads first to promote education. Therefore, DCA will expand its existing community networks and learning committee structures.

2 - Activities or outputs

1) Deliver foundational literacy, numeracy, and life skills sessions for women and girls in female only spaces, through a home-based approach, and through self-guided learning 2) Design and develop the second level of DCA's Curriculum Framework for Rohingya Adolescents and Youth aligned with LCFA 3) Provide ongoing teacher professional development including training sessions on distance education and how to support self-regulated learning 4) Train female Rohingya teachers in pedagogical themes, how to support self-learning, DRR, PSS, and inclusive education 5) Establish a Teaching Training Package including Facilitation Guide, tools, and assessments for female Rohingya Teachers 6) Develop a self-guided learning strategy to support autonomous learning for non and low-tech settings 7) Deliver ECD programs and Parenting Education in tandem with the education sessions for participants with children 8) Establish Alternative Learning Committees and conduct regular awareness raising sessions on how to support education for women and girls
