Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face multiple intersecting crises which is having a profound and adverse impact on food security in the country. Some of the difficulties the nation is experiencing include insecurity, subnational violence, influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan, developing regional and national socioeconomic challenges exacerbated by the global economic slump, and the effects of climate change.
In late July through August, multiple small-scale incidences of armed clashes, petty crime, abductions, banditry, and cattle-raiding occurred in several areas in South Sudan, including in parts of Uror, Nyirol, Twic East, Duk, and Akobo of Jonglei; state. Although the incidents have remained localized in nature and are not expected to lead to large-scale coordinated mobilization, they remain of concern given the disruption caused to household livelihoods, assistance delivery, and trade flows, particularly at a time when food needs are high, and resources are scarce. (FEWS NET - Food Security Outlook Update August 2023)
RCO seeks to provide Agricultural support to populations affected by crisis in Jonglei (Duk, Twic East, Uror, Nyirol, Akobo and Canal/Pigi counties. The support will be delivered through provision of main and seasonal crop kits and agricultural tools to food insecure and displaced households and with an additional input of either vegetable or ﬁshing kits depending on the suitability of the additional inputs to speciﬁc locations. This will be coupled by capacity building on modern agronomy practices as well as capacity building of fish handling and preservation whilst formation of farmers’ producer groups and formation of fisher folk groups.
The project will target 10,000 HHs (15,000Men, 15,000Women, 15,000Girls & 15,000Boys) vulnerable populations by building their resilience and restoring their assets that were depleted by drought, floods, or conflicts. This will be done through supporting the identified beneficiaries with conditional cash-for assets activities, including cash for restocking. Target group will also be supported with agricultural inputs including Crop and Vegetable kits and tools taking into consideration the seasonal calendar activities whilst riverine beneficiaries will be supported with fishing kits.
RCO will also promote the use of improved agriculture technologies through the distribution of early maturing nutritious dense vegetable kits, high yielding, drought-tolerant & disease tolerant crop varieties to gain short term results coupled by capacity training on improved agronomic practices and ﬁsh preservation/handling techniques and training of farmers on land –harvest, storage and market linkages that will develop communities’ knowledge and skills that will improve food access, increase and build their resilience and future self-reliance by creating and expanding delivery models that support community empowerment and accountability.
Being a key FSL actor in the country, RCO is cognizant of the various gender challenges in the context and will duly target specific groups particularly identified as being vulnerable such as IDP households headed by women and children. In addition, an inclusive community-driven approach will be applied to ensure that women and men are involved in the planning, selecting, implementation, and approaches of programmes, especially on decisions of the selection of cash programming schemes, taking into consideration the different obligations, challenges, time constraints and priorities of women and men across the seasonal calendar.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple intersecting crises. Insecurity, subnational violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges compounded by the global economic downturn, the effects of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict, all adversely impact the food security of South Sudanese families. Jonglei state in general and Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, Uror, Duk and Twic East counties in particular, the severity of food insecurity continues to grow due to compounding shocks amplified by intensified sub-national violence, three consecutive years of widespread flooding, localized dry spells and an ongoing protracted macro-economic crisis with heightened poverty and vulnerability (more than 80% of the population is reportedly below the poverty line). As a result, the prices of food items continue to be high and access to basic goods and services has become a challenge due to partially dysfunctional markets and reduced purchasing powers due to disrupted livelihoods. (REACH South Sudan Factsheet, August 2023). Notably, the levels of acute food insecurity in these counties will remain high during the main harvesting period from October 2023 through January 2024, with the persistence of Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes in many counties. In the Jonglei where forecasts anticipate a deepening of seasonal rainfall deficits and where crop production is already typically limited, households are likely to harvest at most a few months of stocks. Conditions are expected to exacerbate further by persistently high staple food prices, high needs linked to large-scale arrivals of South Sudanese returnees and refugees from Sudan, and associated disruptions in trade flows from Sudan and the region. Additionally, the seasonal reduction in assistance despite rising needs in the northern areas and the anticipated large funding shortfalls that are likely to interfere with a necessary scale-up in assistance in the north is expected to contribute to persistent high needs in the harvest period. Overall, Emergency outcomes are expected in 18 counties mainly in Upper Nile, Jonglei, among others while Crisis outcomes will remain widespread, with 54 counties assessed to likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Meanwhile, Canal/Pigi of Jonglei; is one of the counties that will remain of greatest concern due to the protracted negative impacts of conflict, the expectation for below to significantly below average harvest, and the large-scale influx of South Sudanese returnees. (FEW NET August 2023)Given the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in the target counties, Food insecurity is expected to be more severe during 2024 as conﬂict persists and macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate. It is in light to this that Relief Corps Organization (RCO) proposes for a project that will immediately scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the targeted counties, particularly those with a high share of populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Further, the project seeks to build resilience support and enhance a disaster risk reduction (DRR) strategy to mitigate the effects of climate change. All this will be in line with the FSL HRP cluster objectives 2024.

CLFSC/CO1 1. Beneﬁciary identiﬁcation, registration and provision of unconditional food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers 6000 households. (FSLCO1) 2.. Post Distribution Monitoring and Evaluation of the Project. (FSLCO2).1. Beneﬁciary identiﬁcation, registration and distributing of staple crop kits and nutritious-dense early-maturing, high-yielding and drought-tolerant vegetable kits and tools to 12,000HH in target Counties. 2. Identification, registration of riverine beneficiaries coupled by distribution of fishing kits to 9,000 Households in target counties. 3. Establishment and maintenance of 20 demonstration plots and kitchen gardens at nutrition facilities in the target counties. 4. With the aid of the trained CAHWs, distribute 10 solar powered cold chain facilities in the targeted locations and vaccinate 30,000 livestock in the target locations. CLFSC/CO31. Identification and training of 200 extension farmers on improved agronomic practices post-harvest management, pest control, preservation/storage, sales and market linkages. This training is aimed at improving their agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption, mitigate irreversible coping mechanisms and build their resilience to withstand future shocks. 2. Training of 100 extension farmers on pest control including scouting and Fall Army Worm and desert locust surveillance, detection, prevention and mitigation. 3. Formation and training of 5 farmers’ producer groups of 20 members each on to oversee post-harvest, storage and sales and marketing in the target Counties. 4. Capacity development and training of 100 CAHWs on livestock handling, treatment and vaccinations and safeguarding basic animal health. 5. Training on 100 fishermen in the target counties on fish handling procedures processing and preservation methods.
