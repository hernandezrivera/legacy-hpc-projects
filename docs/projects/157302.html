Project Summary

Six years since the start of the conflict in south Sudan, Sudan continuous to receive a regular flow of South Sudanese Refugee arrivals every month. According to RRP 2019 Sudan hosts the largest population of South Sudanese refugees in the region, with more than 850,000 individuals reported across the country as of December 2018 & 50,000 people are expected to arrive in 2019. CARE project targets 129,437 South Sudanese refuges- living in camps & out of camps settlement -as well as vulnerable host communities in South Kordofan, East Darfur and Khartoum States. The majority of refugees are women & children (82%), who arrive in poor health after traveling many days to reach Sudan, often by foot, & who are in urgent need of protection, WASH, nutrition, shelter & health support. Many new arrivals are also coming from parts of South Sudan experiencing high rates of food insecurity & malnutrition. Approximately 79% of the current South Sudanese refugee population are living outside of official camps, alongside host communities, mostly in remote & underdeveloped areas, where resources, infrastructure & basic services are extremely limited. In some out-of-camp areas insecurity, lack of access during the rainy season & geographic isolation can pose challenges to regular access by response partners.
There has been a dramatic deterioration in the economic situation in Sudan in the past year, with a rate of inflation at 68%. The on-going fuel crisis and bread shortage have compounded the humanitarian needs of the already vulnerable populations. Households are unable to cover their basic needs due to increased food prices as well as other basic commodities. The deteriorating macroeconomic conditions, continue to erode household purchasing power in Sudan. Sorghum and millet prices are about 200 per cent above the five-year average, according to FEWSNET. According to the IPC analysis from July 2018, food insecurity levels across Sudan are high with the number of people in IPC phases 3 and 4 projected at 6 million, noting an increase from 5.6 million in April (FEWSNET). Furthermore in 2018, the unemployment rate in Sudan stood at 12.71%. Displaced populations and conflict affected communities in the Darfur and Kordofan are unable to re/establish livelihoods.

Needs

South Sudanese Refugees continued to arrive into the targeted areas, by Dec 2018 100,142 refugees in East Darfur, 38,296 in South Kordofan & 288,445 in Khartoum including 22500 people in Nivasha open area & 50,000 are expected to arrive in 2019. The influx is expected to continue reducing because of the signed peace agreement in South Sudan. Despite this however, there is a need to continue sustaining all the services in the targeted areas & expand it to out-of-camp settings where 68% of the refugees are in East Darfur & 100% in South Kordofan integrated with the host communities sharing their limited resources. The expansion of services will help to enhance peaceful co-existence in addition to meeting the lifesaving needs of both groups. There has been a dramatic deterioration in the economic situation in Sudan in the past year which has compounded the humanitarian needs of the already vulnerable population. Rate of inflation is very high making the price of basic household necessitates such as bread and medicines unaffordable for a significant proportion of the population forcing them into further vulnerability. This has been compounded by a fuel crisis across the country which has further increased food prices, hindered agricultural activities for communities as well as humanitarian operations across the country. The impacts of the economic situation on refugee communities is compounded by limited access to livelihoods and high poverty. Despite the ongoing response by different partners, many areas are still below emergency standards, significant gaps identified, remains un met for example: a majority of children remain out of school; water availability& latrine coverage are below standards, with high rates of open defecation; access to health services is inconsistent across the response, with supply gaps in basic medicines; high food insecurity, with most refugees unable to afford the local food basket or purchase firewood; and critical malnutrition rates in many camps and settlements. These issues are compounded by high limited access to livelihoods & household income, with knock-on effects including increased tensions with host communities, prevalence of child labour, early marriage and other child protection issues, & SGBV risks. According to RRP document -2019, in 2018, average daily water quantities for the refugees stood at 10.2 L per person per day across the response, with just 5 l/p/d in some areas of South Kordofan, as well as low latrines coverage associated with bad usage practices, open defecation, poor personnel hygiene especially for women and girls at reproductive age. Poor WASH situation is driven factor for water born diseases & spread of diarrheal among households. The health and nutrition status of newly arrived refugees and those in protracted situations remains a concern, especially among South Sudanese refugees arriving from areas of South Sudan facing emergency levels of acute malnutrition and food insecurity.

Activities

CARE plans to address multi-sectoral needs of the refugee communities in collaboration with national NGOs, sectors and UN agencies. In relation to WASH needs, Water Supply, water trucking, boreholes drilling, construction and rehabilitation of other water systems; formation & training of water user committees; CLT triggering & latrine construction; Households & school latrines construction; rehabilitation and decommission of latrines; Shower construction/rehabilitation/ maintenance; support operation & maintenance of water supply facilities; distribute water voucher/cash; convert generators/diesel power to solar power hybrid system pay monthly incentives for pump operators and water point attendants; Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns, Garbage collection & safe disposal facilities construction; Chlorination & water quality testing; Distribution of hygiene items & supplies (soap, child potties, Jerry cans, etc.), distribution of basic female hygiene kits for women & girls; recruitment & training of hygiene promoters; Health &Nutrition: Construction/rehabilitation/equipping of health facilities; Provide general clinical services including outpatient consultation services for diagnosis & treatment for common illnesses, basic laboratory investigations, short stay admission & emergency referral to hospital for selected cases; provide maternal & new borne health care services including RH package, antenatal, postnatal care, EmONC, referral services & pregnant women vaccination, & skilled care during child birth; Provision of essential drugs & consumable supplies through the PHC facility; Provide EPI services; pay monthly incentive for seconded staff from SMOH; vaccination for children U1; conduct health awareness campaigns; training of health care staff, community health works on primary health care services provision; train midwives & birth attendants on safe motherhood infection control & EmOC; admission to TFP (OTP + SC) feeding program, conduct SENS survey; PLW

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project has made important analysis in designing the interventions in consultation with humanitarian actors such UNHCR and local government ministries. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment are included in the analysis and reduce potential impact of key interventions. Regular water and sanitation facilities supervision, emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners are important preventive actions. Environmental cleaning campaigns and engaging the youth to take leadership in such efforts is showing improvement in the sector. Youth, women, men and others will be organized in to natural resource management groups where appropriate and will be trained for sustainable use of resources. Conflict sensitive planning and implementation is at the heart of the interventions planned. The surrounding host communities who will share resources will also be part of the project and participate in all stages of the project implementation.
