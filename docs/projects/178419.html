Project Summary

The project aims to provide wide-ranging support for WASH activities and lifesaving intervention seeking to increase access of at least 242,674 crisis-affected people to inclusive, accessible, conflict-sensitive, and gender-responsive WASH services. The project will contribute to the following objectives of the WASH cluster: Sector Objective 1: Support to water, sanitation/sewage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria, Sector Objective 2: Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services, and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people, Sector Object 3: WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced and Sector Objective 4: Improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria.
Under ﬁrst objective: Islamic Relief plan a number of activities include: repair and rehabilitation of water systems, Support to water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems, Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems. Under the second objective, IR plan to Distribution of essential WASH non-food items (NFI), hygiene promotion, and messaging on COVID-19. Under Objective three, IR plan to implement Strengthen prevention and mitigation of WASH-related protection risks, while under Objective four, Islamic Relief will provide Support to WASH facilities and services in schools and child-friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors
The action will target the groups identified in Islamic Relief’s needs assessment as most in need, including IDPs (displaced, in camps and in communities), host communities, and particularly women and girls and persons with disabilities). Islamic Relief will work with gender-balanced local Community volunteers which will include representatives from each community group, community leaders, and local councils as entry points for community consultation, mobilization, and as key implementation actors. The proposed response aims to meet critical, unmet needs as identified in the previous sections and the Gender-based analysis around the lack of safe and accessible WASH services, compromising health and dignity. The proposed actions developed on the basis of a primary need assessment conducted by the IR team. The actions aim at reduced suffering, increased and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for conflict-affected IDP and Host communities, women, men, girls, and boys in NW Syria (Aleppo and Idlib Governorates), Syria.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance is12.2 million of WASH services. Based on WASH assessments, 59% of the Syria population requires WASH assistance, nevertheless, to adequately understand the scale and diversity of WASH needs, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance - 12.2 million, needs to be completed with figures presenting specific WASH needs related to water quality – 3.3 million and quantity – 1.4 million people, sanitation – 3.2 million people, solid waste management – 4.1 million people, and heavy financial burden linked with purchasing water – 7.5 million or hygiene supplies – 6.4 million people. Further, 5.1 million people face barriers to effective handwashing. Over 7 million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in IDPs last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive. In addition, the entire population of Syria relies on drinking water treatment chemicals provided by the humanitarian community.WASH infrastructure requires significant support and investments With 36 percent of the general population relying on alternative to piped water and often unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs at least 70 percent of sewage is discharged untreated, and at least half of the sewerage systems not functional and about 29 percent of garbage inappropriately disposed of, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant repair and operational support (incl. capacity building for operation and maintenance of infrastructure and on-grid electricity supply).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection and gender mainstreaming IRW has an extensive understanding of the diverse vulnerabilities regarding gender, age, and other vulnerable groups, whose needs are taken into account at all stages of program design, implementation, and monitoring. Specific and disaggregated data and information regarding gender, age, and vulnerable groups will be collected during the needs assessment to support ongoing adaptation of the intervention. When conducting FGDs, IR will ensure that all gender, age, and vulnerable groups are consulted and represented and have the best understanding of the situation. Within implementation, the following measures will be taken: the facilities developed and/or rehabilitated throughout the project, will be set in locations that allow reducing the threats related to their access. Community consultations will be held to ensure appropriate choices for these locations and. Water and Sanitation facilities will be equipped with Lighting devices (solar-based solutions) in IDP camps, the design of emergency latrines should consider the diversity of the population (age, gender, disability). 7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. IR’s CFM uses multiple channels to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback including a complaint box, hotlines, during-distribution and post-distribution monitoring, community committees, casual enumerators and program staff. In addition, during action activities, random interviews, spot checks and dialogue will allow beneficiaries to report concerns. The received complaints and feedbacks will be classified and handled as per IR policy for CFM and high-risk complaints, such as those relating to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, will be prioritized and promptly managed. Confidentiality and data protection are ensured as per Islamic Relief’s data protection policy. IR’s commitment towards Safeguarding against misconduct will be upheld, and options for safe access to IR’s feedback mechanism, especially to the most marginalized groups such as women, children, persons with disability, and those who cannot read and write, will be sought through WhatsApp, telegram, Skype and SMS.
