Project Summary

Through its integrated Mine Action/protection component, the organization will seek to alleviate and/or mitigate the accidents related to the presence of explosive ordnances (EOs) in contaminated areas and increase the protection of at-risk persons through direct and indirect risk education activities, contamination impact surveys, and through the identification, marking and disposal of EO spots.
The organization will address the protection-related risks associated to the high level of land contamination by EOs in Syria. The INGO will provide Risk Education for most at-risk populations living and working in highly contaminated areas in Syria. Based on context, access and needs analysis, Risk Education will be conducted either through direct RE sessions at households’ level or through Trainer of Trainers (ToT) to improve community participation and sustainability. The organization will identify key and influential community focal points (teachers, imam, community leaders, head of farmers, survivors) as to enable them to dispense life-saving messages and become advocates for the protection of their peers. This activity aims to strengthen the skills of key local actors in order to ensure the appropriation of the messages disseminated and thus the sustainability of the activity. In addition, safety briefing will be proposed to humanitarian workers to spread RE knowledge within the humanitarian community and thus to promote safe behaviors and minimize the risk of accidents among aid workers as they provide services to affected population. Risk Education Teams will conduct RE activities, Contamination Impact Survey (CIS), Accident and Victim surveys in affected communities, mosques, community centers, educational facilities, IDP camps and other public places in an effort to reduce immediate threats caused by Explosive Ordinance (EOs). Priority will be given to at-risks persons living in contaminated areas, to ensure they have the necessary information to be able to adopt safe behaviors and reduce the threats posed by the presence of explosive hazards. Safety briefings will be provided to other humanitarian actors (local and international NGOs) to raise their awareness about risks and the consequences of explosive hazards they could face when in the field.
Finally, the Organization will produce and externally disseminate evidence-based advocacy factsheets so as to mobilize and persuade local, national, regional and International stakeholders and raise key humanitarian and protection issues for populations and humanitarian actors in Syria.
In clearance, Non-Technical Survey (NTS) teams and clearance teams will identify, mark and dispose EOs. Surveys will be conducted to obtain more accurate information on presence, type, distribution & level of contamination, and will be prioritized dependent on the presence of IDPs in communities, the identification of visible EOs in areas and/or accidents that have occurred in that community over the past 6 months and where people are living and/or returning.
The organization will also implement clearance activities in Raqqa Governorate, while maintaining the possibility of deploying Clearance teams to Deir Ezzor if the context allows. In this perspective, NTS activities will only be carried out in Deir Ezzor if demining operations in the area is confirmed.
During the provision of Risk Education, people who need multi-disciplinary rehabilitation services will be internally referred to supported rehabilitation centers in Hasakeh, Raqqa and Deir el Zour governorates, where they will have access to a variety of services depending on the outcomes of their needs assessment process. The organisation’s advocacy team will continue to lobby for the ban of the use of Explosive Weapons in Populated Areas (EWIPA) as well as the shelling of hospitals/health facilities to prevent death and injuries to civilians. These activities will be funded by different donors and will complement the proposed HMA project

4 - Needs

One in two Syrians lives at risk of explosive hazards as an effects of the ongoing hostilities. As reported in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), 10.2M Syrians are at risk of being injured or killed by explosive hazards as a direct result of the conflict. Of these injured, 45% are expected to sustain permanent injuries (e.g. amputation, spinal cord injury, brain injury). Presence of explosive hazards not only affects personal safety but can have severe socio-economic impacts depriving the affected population of livelihoods and sources of income as well as preventing or limiting humanitarian access.The organisation’s Mine Action (MA) surveillance system (dashboard) reports a total of 1,088 Accident and 1,344 Victim reports, from November 2018 to October 2019 out of which 57% resulted in fatalities (deaths) and children constituted 40% of the total casualties, the numbers of accidents reported were: 359 in Aleppo, 268 in Deir Ezzor, 192 in Raqqa, 182 in Idleb, 42 in Hassakeh, 17 in Homs, 9 in Rural Damascus, and 2 in Daraa governorates. 56% of the accidents resulted in permanent impairments with the main causes being attributed to landmines (38%), ERW (36%) and IEDs (12%). From 837 Contamination Impact Survey conducted by RE teams, 72% of surveyed villages are reported to be contaminated. This has been exacerbated by the latest spates of hostilities in northeast has resulted into EO recontamination and increased displacement. Findings from a KAP survey carried out by the organisation in Idleb and Aleppo governorates in 2019 revealed that 1 out of 3 of the 386 respondents in 28 communities did not have knowledge of how to protect themselves from the risks posed by explosive hazards demonstrating the need for continued Risk Education in the targeted communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

S.O.1: 3. The organization will conduct thorough analysis on its internal data and on secondary data, share relevant data with other advocacy actors (in a confidential and secure way), and produce and disseminate two evidence-based advocacy factsheets highlighting protection concerns regarding the use of explosive weapons in populated areas to the national, regional and international community. S.O.4:4.1 Risk Education (RE) sessions: A composite intervention methodology will be implemented, allowing the activity to remain flexible to changes in context and needs. Thus, based on context, access and needs analysis, the RE will be conducted either through direct RE sessions at households’ level or through ToTs. 4.2. Contamination Impact Survey (CIS) constitute the first step of RE intervention through collecting information about evidence of contamination. Field teams are deployed at community level in order to gather relevant information through questionnaires, about EOs related accidents which has occurred and the victims of these accidents, as well as the presence of contamination using accident and victim reports. The simultaneous implementation of CIS and RE sessions will permit to gather the necessary and relevant information on accidents and victims. The field teams will then be able to identify record and refer victims of EOs and IEDs to appropriate services. As a third step of survey NTS will be conducted to gather and analyze data on suspected and confirmed hazardous areas and EOs to support prioritization for clearance by providing evidence of contamination. The information is fed back to the Multi Task Teams (MTT) who will take over for the clearance. 4.3 RE sessions will permit to gather the necessary and relevant information on accidents and victims. Following this, the field teams will be able to identify record and refer victims of landmines, CW and IEDs to specialized services, either provided internally (MHPSS and rehabilitation) or external

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Protection of Field staff and partners involved in aid provision.Mitigation measures (MM): To ensure the safety of parties involved in RE activities: The INGO will ensure low visibility to protect the identities of its staff. The INGO does not conduct MA activities in Syrian government controlled areas, to avoid unnecessary risk to partners, staff and beneficiaries. RE is conducted under different forms and the INGO chooses in each context the most appropriate form to reduce the risk of exposure-Housing, land and property (HLP) disputes that may arise in relation to clearance. (MM): The INGO will adopt a conflict sensitivity approach in carrying out clearance. The organization will conduct prioritization assessment of land/property or areas to be cleared based on: local priority, land/property ownership, evidence of contamination & density, future land/property use, etc. Areas with public ownership, benefiting more people and with less potentials of generating disputes will be considered.-PSEA, (MM): The INGO enforces a PSEA policy for the staff, partner staff and suppliers forbidding SEA, ensuring proper investigation and follow up of reported cases as well as setting clear accountability measures. The INGO also focuses on raising the awareness of the community on the INGO’s policy on PSEA and developed accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms.-Safe closure of facilities and protecting data. (MM): Field staff are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect and/or destroy data/information.-Logistic and fund transfer issues. (MM): Fund transfer issues will be mitigated by assessing several transfer partners as well as by enhancing coordination with INGO in the area.-Information confidentiality & protection of identities. (MM): The INGO collects data of beneficiaries solely for humanitarian purposes. The staff who are in charge of collecting, analyzing, storage and sharing of data are bound by the INGO info sharing policy.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Loss of access to intervention areas:Access to intervention areas is not maintained due to security deterioration or to a shift of control.(MM): The INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted to the context. The INGO also continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the conflict analysis and how it affects its operations. The strategy includes the possibility of suspension/relocation of activities to safer areas, and adapting the team’s work modalities to a remote management of activities in the field.- The INGO advocates to engage the UN and all parties to the conflict on access to health and protection topics and respecting the international humanitarian law. The INGO will also implement part of its RE through partners originating from areas of interventions. This will reduce the risk emanating from accessibility & acceptability issues. - Acceptance of local community and authorities is maintained. In order to successfully carry out activities in the community, the INGO needs to maintain the approval, acceptance and support of the community and the local authorities. - (MM): teams and partners are well-perceived on the ground and always make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. - Reduction/ lack of funding: An abrupt reduction or cut in funding from donors due to deteriorating security in our areas of operations may result into risks of losing acceptability and trust from the communities in areas where the affected population see the INGO as the only alternative source of such services. (MM): Interventions shall be planned based on available funds. Expectations shall be managed by informing communities, local authorities and partners about the extent of the INGO’s commitment. This allows better decisions to be made about how to employ local resources, so to avoid differing expectations.
