Project Summary

GGSI's proposed intervention will aim to strengthen coordination, inclusive governance structures, improve coordination among humanitarian partners and relevant stakeholders through facilitating local coordination meeting and strengthen community governance structures. It will also strengthen information management to foster coordination and engage the community through community-led initiatives to achieve an empowering approach that allows displacement affected communities to take the lead in activity design, facilitate community-led site improvements and maintenance and ensure the protection of IDPs affected by fire outbreaks, flooding, and forced evictions, through the provision of ESKs and NFIs. Finally, in order to address the prevalent protection risks in informal settlement in MMC, and JERE the Action aims to bridge existing protection gaps through CCCM trainings, monitoring and awareness sessions in community centers especially designed for this purpose.

1 - Needs

In NE Nigeria, it is estimated that 60% of the IDPs live in the host community, with some residing in spontaneous makeshift structures in hazardous environments, rented apartments within the urban or peri-urban areas, particularly in informal settlements where state capacity to deliver services and infrastructure is weak, while others live with host families. It is acknowledged that there has been limited capacity and engagement from the humanitarian community to adequately address the need of the affected populations. Apart from suffering the effects of the insurgency, the displaced population and the host community are both exposed to serious risks including natural disaster, communicable diseases, food insecurity and marginalization. The large presence of displaced populations in urban or peri-urban areas has had significant impacts, placing additional pressure on local markets, as well as on the social, economic, cultural and administrative structures that are already weakened by effects of the insurgency. Their vulnerability is expounded as the host community has equally been affected by displacement. The disparity might cause tension and incite violence and scapegoating and especially during tension and changing phase of an emergency or during communicable disease outbreaks. As a result of displacement, they lost assets, housing, have limited social networks, separated from family members, lost documentation and poor access to available services, and are often undergoing trauma. IDPs living in urban areas are often exposed to exploitation, extortion, organised crime and antagonism from host communities. Frequently, IDPs become victims of forced evictions and expulsions. Ensuring security and protection of IDPs in urban areas therefore remains one of the most significant challenges facing the humanitarian community.

2 - Activities

The proposed Action has been designed according to the needs identified in Nigeria by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), multi-sector needs assessment, and through communication with OCHA, sector leads and donors. This section of the proposal will be divided into the objectives outlined above: objective One: Enhance displacement management in camps, areas of return and out of camp sites to ensure equitable and dignified access to services, assistance, and protection for IDPs through CCCM responses. objective Two: Enhance resilience and improved ability of communities and local partners to cope with displacement and ensure local ownership and self-governance through inclusive participation, gender mainstreaming and engagement of displaced persons. The GGSI will extend urgent need for support to the vulnerable groups by providing the IDPs and Returnees sufficient services to cushion their hardship among other activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. GGSI will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the well being, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to Protection and CCCM assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All GGSI staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to GGSI’s Code of Conduct, including GGSI’s Policy on PSEA. Moreover, as an agency and Partner in CCCM Protection and HLP in all proposed target sites, GGSI will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer protection cases identified through the CRM. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: 1) Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: GGSI will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. GGSI’s team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. Gender-segregated queues with mandatory COVID-19 social distancing measures and available disinfectant materials will be mandated to all camp-level communal and distribution sites. Under the CCCM and General Protection component, GGSI will integrate gender and protection mainstreaming, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups in decision-making processes. Strong referral mechanisms will be enacted, enabling direct referral of any vulnerable IDPs to relevant service providers, including victims of assault or GBV. 2) Ensure meaningful and equal access: the Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. GGSI will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy. 3) Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers, hired to support the construction of the drainage network, will be recruited from the IDP community to provide employment opportunities, with hiring process reliant on a strict equal opportunities basis and considerate of protection and gender aspects. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints. Moreover, PDM exercises will assess beneficiary satisfaction and inform further programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GGSI’s will establish Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM) and Mobile Protection Helpdesk (MPHD) mainstreamed throughout all interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. GGSI’s CRM and MPHD enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, while informing GGSI’s partner coordination efforts at all the sites and thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. Installed CRM mediums, including complaints boxes, complaints desks, a telephone hotline and email, allow for contactless communication methods that mitigated COVID-19 transmission risks, while granting anonymity for confidential reporting of protection concerns and feedback to GGSI as CCCM and Protection agency. GGSI’s independent AME Unit will maintain responsibility for collecting and responding to all reports, addressing complaints through protection-centered case-management support and systematically investigating, referring and following up on beneficiary complaints with protection partners and other service agencies in a timely and transparent manner. GGSI’s trailed and tested CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as GGSI continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. All GGSI staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct, and any breach of the code of conduct uncovered through the CRM will be responded to with serious measure.
