Project Summary

Through this proposed intervention, GAH will contribute directly to the protection and education cluster objectives which aim at enhancing the capacity of the communities affected by conflict and displacement to address, prevent and respond to protection risks among refugees and Host Communities.
GAH is intervening to increase coverage of and access to primary education and protection services for vulnerable communities within South Darfur (Dimso, UmDafoug, Assalam and AlRadoum), East Darfur (Abu Jabra, Assalaya, Al firdous and Bahr Al arab-Kario), Khartoum state (Jabal Awlia, SSR Bantio camp), South Kordufan (AlLiri) and West Kordufan (AlMeriam, Al Nehoud, Keilak).
GAH already has a presence in Dimso from 2017-2021, as well as in Abu Jabra and Assalaya and AlNehoud since 2016 to 2020. Hence, GAH could assess the gaps of the mentioned localities in the protection and social welfare services. This situation was worsened by the continued instability particularly within South Darfur Dimso, which further exacerbated the existing numbers of displaced persons.
In addressing these challenges, humanitarian efforts have been made targeting mainly refugees in the camps and out of camps settings and vulnerable host communities. However, major gaps remain in the provision of basic services in protection. In this context, GAH proposed the use of an integrated intervention to maximize the impact, targeting vulnerable communities with essential service provision within the sectors of education, and protection, as well as tackling food security through livelihood measures (vocational training).
Under the education sector, GAH will improve the provision of and access to safe and protective learning environments for children, thereby increasing access to and enrollment of children to schools. This in turn will ensure a smooth and continuous flow, while keeping children away from the dangers caused by idleness and exposure to harm.
Under the protection interventions, through strengthening and capacity development of existing structures, GAH will increase awareness among communities, while enhancing monitoring and linkages and integration to service provision at the individual, household, and community levels.
Through these proposed sector interventions, it is therefore hoped that by using an integrated approach to tackling challenges faced by communities within target localities, GAH will not only provide much-needed life-saving services; but also will set a firm foundation for their sustainability through community awareness, engagement and ultimately adoption of key lessons for better self-reliance.

1 - Needs

This continued influx of refugees and returnees has brought about increased tension between them and the host communities, putting more pressure on the already existing limited resources. According to the multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA) March 2012, East and South Darfur had the highest proportions of households with extreme multi-sectoral needs, at 41% and 42% of all non-displaced households. Further, the percentage of households with unmet needs in more than one sector was higher, for a number of sectors, compared to other states in Darfur; households were thus more likely to have unmet needs in three or more sectors including Education, protection. Given the lack of sufficient education services within the targeted localities, the majority of the children are missing out on essential basic education, and facing serious protection risks such as child labour, recruitment into armed groups, and physical and sexual violence. GAH has had a presence in the mentioned areas was in protection, education and WASH activities in collaboration with UNHCR, SHF and UNFPA for the year 2016-2020. Through these projects, GAH Identified major community issues and gaps, including the people’s income level, and the need to secure them with income-generating activities. There is a high level of children out of school due to the lack of educational facilities in the area, also the need for establishing protection structures in the area and further strengthening community protection mechanisms, and due to the location environment and lack of labour opportunities for men and boys, there is a need for socioeconomic empowerment and vocational training. Many of these children have endured protracted conflict and continue to be exposed to ongoing clashes. in some specific states, mostly in Darfur and West Kordofan, girls’ primary school enrolment is significantly lower than boys 20% lower in those two states respectively. The gender gap becomes even wider at the secondary school level, and particularly so for rural areas where only 20 per cent of secondary school-aged girls attend school. This project aims to support the affected children among South Sudanese Refugees (SSRs) in East Darfur, West Kordofan, South Kordofan and Khartoum states, and Central African Refugees (CAR) in South Darfur. Particularly those who have recently arrived in targeted states and those in out of camp areas, together with their host communities. In the Education component, the project is targeting 12 localities ( East Darfur: Abu Jabra SEV 4, Assalaya SEV 4, Bahr Al Arab SEV 3, Al Firdous SEV 3 - Khartoum Jebel Awlia SEV 3, SD Damso SEV 4, Al Radoum SEV 3, Assalam SEV 3, Um Dafug SEV 3, South Kordofan AlLeri SEV 2, West Kordofan An Nuhud SEV2, Al Meiram SEV 3 and Keilak SEV3), the total PiN target of Education subsector in the target locations is 127,416 of which this intervention is directly targeting 18,388 amounting to 14.43%. In the Child protection component, the project is targeting the same localities except for Khartoum, ( East Darfur: Abu Jabra SEV 3, Assalaya SEV 5, Bahr Al Arab SEV 5, Al Firdous SEV 5 - SD Damso SEV 5, Al Radoum SEV 5, Assalam SEV 4, Um Dafug SEV 5, South Kordofan AlLeri SEV 5, West Kordofan An Nuhud SEV 4, Al Meiram SEV 4 and Keilak SEV 5), the total PiN of CP subsector in the target locations is 70,690, and the total of CP target for these localities is 39,335, of which this intervention is targeting 19,481, amounting to 49.5%. To address the protection needs in the targeted areas beyond Child protection, the General Protection component of this intervention also targets 34,794 individuals in the target localities with awareness-raising, community-based protection and MPCA, out of the 277,631 total GP PiN, which amounts to 12.5%.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As a protection-based organization that specializes in children's and women's rights. This ensures that protection mainstreaming is not only visible in all of the project activities but also implemented. Consideration will be given to ensure that all services are offered with dignity. Given that there will be a clear strategy of Communication With the Community's vulnerable targeted beneficiaries will easily be reached the service centers. The project then develops referral pathways for support to the identified Protection of Civilians to enable them to receive other services from other sub-sectors this ensures an integrated approach. Within the organization will take into account preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of its intervention which could increase people's vulnerability and diminish the purpose of the intervention. GAH considers ways to minimize tension and conflict between beneficiaries, by ensuring that accurate information reaches all relevant community groups, including children, adolescents, older persons, and persons with disabilities, using different means of communication and dissemination of AGD appropriate information. GAH will hold general and refresher training on protection mainstreaming, GBV, and PSEA prevention for its staff at HQ and field level, as well as the relevant community workers. Specifically tailored gender mainstreaming processes will be performed for each intervention and reports will be shared with the Protection sector and GBV subsector, and included in the reporting of the project. AGD disaggregated data will be collected for reporting purposes in all stages of the project. All interventions especially interventions for GBV survivors and women at risk will be done confidentially to ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries and avoid exposing them to further risks. Since GBV is known as a very sensitive issue in the targeted areas, GAH will avoid any approaches interpreted as inappropriate in the community context or insulting to local sensitivities. Project staff or key representatives of the project will be carefully selected to have strong knowledge of the community and a thorough understanding of the local political context. GAH will ensure that special arrangements (including but not exclusive to community outreach and volunteers) are made to provide meaningful access to services for those who may not be able to reach such as older persons and persons with disabilities. GAH interventions will not discriminate against any group and will consider whether men and women have different capacities to access the IGAs interventions compared with in-kind assistance. GAH is aiming to promote participation among the target communities and empower the community by making sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, and where and how to obtain it.
