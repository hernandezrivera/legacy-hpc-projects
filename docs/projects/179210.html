Project Summary

POINT aim to address community needs in camps in Azaz, Aleppo, and Al-Dana camps in Idlib. The needs emerging for integrated protection interventions, build protection capacity among the targeted camps, women protection, & empowerment interventions.
This project is designed on a community-based approach by involving beneficiaries in the design phase, involving communities in awareness campaigns for IDPs in camps, empower women to start up their small businesses to serve and address the needs of their communities. The total direct beneficiaries of the project are 1400 women and girls.
Coordination is already established with the camps managements during the previous intervention of POINT in the same locations and the MoU signed between POINT and Turkish and Syrian local authorities. Further coordination will be with NGOs, CBOs working in the camp; and with service providers in the targeted area to facilitate the referral activities. Coordination is already established with the ministry of development and humanitarian affairs for Al-Dana.
The project is drawn according to our 6 years expertise working in camps, empowering women and youth, developing protection service providers and humanitarian workers quality and impact, and enhance women skills to manage women centers and lead activities in Sarmada camps. POINT has established 40 women committees in the targeted 40 camps in Dana based on community participation and volunteering approach. The committees were led by women.
The project aims are:
1. Providing protection services of PSS, GBV and referral pathways for women and women with disabilities. And the main purpose of these activities is to support them restore their balance psychologically and mentally and help GBV survivors overcome negative impacts through awareness-raising, information sharing on GBV services available, safe identification, and referrals.
2. Raising community awareness of women, women with disabilities, and young girls on several topics such as parenting skills principles, early marriages, women's rights, and many more to increase their resilience.
3. Ensure safe spaces and community centers for women and girls led by POINT trained and GBV, PSS specialized staff.
4. making protection monitoring for each camp.
5. Equipping women and women with a disability with vocational/life skills, and starting up small business training and mini-grants to implement those businesses, in order to restore and enhance their dignity by positioning their selves positively in the community as an active member, and decreasing their dependency on their husbands or relatives.
6. Participating in declining child labor by decreasing women's reliance on their children as major or minor income sources.
This will be achieved by:
a. Hire GBV PSS referral pathways specialists. and refer to health and nutrition cases to related sector service providers.
b. Conduct awareness sessions led by women, women with disabilities, and young girls on PSS and GBV.
c. Increasing family cohesion by conducting parenting skills sessions.
d. Building protection capacity by conducting protection awareness-raising sessions provided by specialists. led by specialists on GBV, PSS, and CP. As well as building the protection capacity for CMCs.
e. Provide vocational/skills training for women, women with disabilities, and young girls in the two centers
f. Provide women, women with disabilities, and young girls based with support and information about starting their small business based on a curriculum approved by International Labor Organization ILO
g. Award 100 women with mini-grants and follow up, coaching sessions to implement and sustain their established small businesses.
MEAL department will work on monitoring and evaluating project through:Monthly progress reports,Cases and referrals reports,vocational/life skills training report, Campaigns and reached population report,Mini-grant success stories. The tools will be consistent with GBV SC M&E Toolkit.

4 - Needs

Preliminary Assessment Findings for HNO 2021 demonstrates at the start of 2021, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection in Syria has increased by 21 percent compared to one year ago, from 11.1 million to 13.4 million.Protection risks have increased with the displacement, lack of shelter options and sub‐standard living conditions in areas of displacement, specifically related to gender‐based violence (GBV), child protection (CP), and exposure to explosive hazards. Overcrowding in IDP sites and makeshift shelters and a lack of WASH facilities places women and girls at particular risk of GBV.People in last resort sites face severe deprivation that cut across all sectors and are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic survival and protection needs. The prevalence of self-settled, informal sites that lack camp management systems means that the delivery of dignified protection and multi-sectoral assistance cannot be guaranteed by humanitarian actors in both north-west and North-east Syria.Critical protection needs persist and have been aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic, including gender-based violence (GBV), with women and girls across the country reporting that it has become a feature of everyday life, which highlights the needs for more awareness among IDPs to minimize the infection probabilities.Desperate to generate additional income or reduce household expenses, families also engage in coping mechanisms which increase protection risks, particularly for children and adolescents. 22 percent of assessed communities report child labor as a means to support household income as frequently occurring, with a slight increase for children 12 years of age and older.

5 - Implementation of activities

Providing GBV specialized services including case management and PSS support: POINT through women empowerment center will provide PSS, GBV, and referral pathways services to 500 women in Azaz and Al-Dana’s camps according to the GBV SC awareness toolkit and SOPs with taking advantage of the inter-agency capacity development initiatives like PSEA network initiatives and GBV training tools.Provide activities to empower women and girls on GBV prevention activities: by conducting10 campaigns on GBV awareness campaigns to be led by women to join and participate in centers’ activities and reach target1000 women. To increase family cohesion and reduce risks on children, POINT will conduct 10 parenting skills sessions for women.Protection Monitoring: monthly PM surveys to monitor protection gaps. This process takes place under supervision and in coordination with PMTF. All PM surveys are directly filled on the Kobo application and forwarded directly to PMTF. Protection monitoring must be independent, neutral, conducted with respect to do-no-harm concept.Provide livelihood opportunities to empower women and girls and strengthen their resilience: POINT will participate in providing inclusive strategies to empower women by creating skilled women capable to design their own businesses. target is 300 women. This will be done through vocational, life skills and SME trainings.POINT will depend also on its expertise in empowering women economically to find livelihood opportunities and restore dignity. POINT will provide 100 mini-grants to 100 women with a value of 1000$ each will be delivered on a tranches base according to POINT developed SME SOP.

Sharing the selection criteria with the local community before starting with the implementation.Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance grants among beneficiaries. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Point will share the developed selection criteria for participant and the grants mechanism including the proposed projects’ assessment criteria.Risk: Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; the training activities and the mini-grants may lead to increased risk. Mitigation: announcing and community sensitization for the project in the name of Point in collaboration with camps management and community committees will increase the level of trust on the project and will allow women to participate safely.Risk: Privacy concerns along with conﬁdentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services Likelihood: High. risk: High. Mitigation: Explain self-care strategies to be followed by all organization staff along with GP principles, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place online as soft-copy and as hard-copy. Having female staff members and build trust bridges with beneficiaries.
