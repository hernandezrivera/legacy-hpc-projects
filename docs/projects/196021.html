Project Summary

The project targets 26 schools and learning centers in NWS:
-All targeted sub-districts are considered as high severity.
-The project targets 21,141 beneficiaries about 2 % of them are CwD.
This project aims to address the following education needs which are considered as gaps in 6 sub-district of the targeted areas:
-Provision of formal education for 26 schools and non-formal education for children.
- Provision of TVET to 200 children in Aleppo to OOSC.
-Rehabilitate classrooms in formal schools and establish 2 TLCs (caravans) in camps.
-Provision of stationery and learning materials for teachers, schools, and students in
formal and non-formal settings.
-Provision of operational and running costs including fuel for heating.
-Training of teachers in emergencies
-Teaching essential life-saving skills and supporting the provision of information on GBV and
other forms of violence, anti-trafficking, unexploded ordnance, diseases, nutrition, health,
hygiene and psychosocial health.
-Provision of schools’ furniture and students’ desks.
-online education will be used as a plan B when needed.
This project contributes to increasing access to education for girls and boys by addressing the needs identified in HNO 2023. The project will provide safe and attractive education and learning environment in targeted areas and schools. It’s worth to be mentioned that GAN will rehabilitate schools and TLCs to be easily accessed by children with disabilities by providing rumps, special toilets, and WASH facilities.
If the funding secured is lower than what is requested, GAN will conduct the same proposed activities targeting the same kind of beneficiaries but will decrease the targeted communities and/or schools depending on the most schools in need of rehabilitation and/or schools' furniture.
A placement test (developed by GAN and WCH) will be conducted to help us better plan and focus on the needed type of educational activities based on the results for non-formal education, also to capture the impact of the intervention, children will be provided with learning materials (bag, stationery, notebooks…etc.) the quantities and colors will be differentiated according to the level, gender, and age.
Following formal education will be followed by the project manager in cooperation with a consultant to provide periodic training TOT to supervisors in schools to train teachers in schools then evaluate the performance and provide additional training as required.
Teachers and education personnel will be provided with teaching and learning material and incentives for 9 months, and schools also will receive the needed tools, on other hand,
The curriculum used is SIG and a high level of coordination with ED and other working organization will be ensured to avoid duplications.
GAN will identify 200 OOS children aged (15-18 years) in Atereb who left their schools and work to support their households and need specialized vocational theoretical and practical training to master their crafts in the areas of mobile and laptop maintenance, electric and electronic devices, and programming to get better job opportunities.
GAN MEAL system is such one that stresses regular and systematic consultation with beneficiaries about the project's different activities to ensure their relevance and appropriateness to the needs, so that beneficiary feedback contributes to the project quality monitoring and consequent improvement.
GAN will use individual interviews and FGDs to evaluate activities in addition to collecting observations from the field. GAN will also analyze student data and the results of the quarterly tests, and all beneficiaries will be able to submit complaints and suggestions through the complaints boxes and hotline (WhatsApp), and through the M&E field teams.

3 - Needs

There has been a 4% increase in the sector PIN compared to 2022, an estimated 12% of children do not attend school. International assistance is on a declining trend, while at the same time local resources, where available, have significantly reduced. Attendance rates are similar for boys and girls up to 11 years, after which there is gender disparity with more boys not attending.The transition to secondary school remains a challenge, with less than 10% of children aged 6-17 years attending secondary school. This is further exacerbated by a lack of secondary or vocational schools.The main reason for children not wanting to go to school is that they do not perceive it as useful or insufficient learning is taking place (66%). Dropout due to marriage is more of a barrier for IDPs in camps (5%) than other population groups.Only 2% of children attend non-formal education.Schools have limited absorption and retention capacity, and lack flexibility as well as the flexibility to accommodate learners of different abilities across learning levels. Dilapidated learning environments are unconducive to learning due to overcrowding, lack of school furniture/school supplies, and inadequate heating, lighting, and operational WASH facilities.must ensure teacher training, basic PSS provision, PFA, and MHPSS for children and teachers.S:HNO2023

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1:Risk of explosives, like landmines, exploitations, harassment that students encounter on their ways to and from schools/learning facilities.MM1: -Reduce time/distance traveled by decentralizing learning facilities.-Consider sunrise and sunset times when setting learning hours.-Provide students, their caregivers, and school personnel information on safer routes/times to travel and on what to do and what not to do if they face different types of threats.- R2:Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff/related entities.MM2: Teachers and other education personnel will be regularly trained on positive discipline and child safeguarding. The application of knowledge and skills that are taught will be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. R: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students, Students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers.MM: Learning facilities will have an enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying, corporal punishment, and physical, verbal.Teachers and school personnel will be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, violence, physical punishment and bullying, and the use of positive discipline The application of these skills by all school personnel will be monitored.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. mitigation measures: GAN is a member of the PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN has developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, staff members will sign the policy and received systematized and refreshed training on PSEA. GAN Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies are in place, practiced and monitored. Polices cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. The Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level.GAN will provide awareness sessions about the organization’s policies and procedures.GSN ’s staff and targeted communities will have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report (complaint) anonymously.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1-Access to the target schools’ locations. 2- Availability of the needed materials & equipment. 3-The security situation is somehow stable during the last year. 4-Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring country (Turkey). 5- Schools are opened and physical learning is applied. Risk 1- the risk of shelling by Government of Syrian (GoS) forces and its allies in all or some targeted locations and/or infighting between different armed groups continue causing loss of access to the targeted locations: MM: GAN will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again, in case of GoS advance in the south of Idleb, GAN will assess new schools in the accessed areas. Risk 2- Lack or unavailability of needed materials in Syria. Mitigation: GAN is able to secure all required materials from Turkey. GAN has all required protocols to ship materials through Turkish – Syrian Borders and has good relations with Turkish service providers. Risk 3: Schools are still closed, and quarantine enforced: Mitigation: In this case, GAN will apply distance learning methods. All GAN-supported schools are equipped with distance learning materials. Risk 4- heavy shelling by the GoS forces and its allies for a long time or the target locations becomes under its control MM: Shifting program locations to more accessible areas in coordination with the Education cluster and other.5-Apply online Edu when COVID-19 spread.
