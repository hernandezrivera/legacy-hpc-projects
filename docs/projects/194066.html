Project Summary

ADRA has been working in Sudan for over four decades, working in providing both direct relief and development projects. Currently, ADRA Sudan is present in 5 out of the 18 states in Sudan; Khartoum, White Nile, Blue Nile, West Darfur, and Central Darfur. ADRA In WDS, had implemented about 10 WASH projects through DANIDA fund Targeting 60,000 beneficiaries in Geneina , Kreneik , and Jabal Localities, in WDS. ADRA had rehabilitated 12 Hafirs and drilled and rehabilitated more than 120 HPs, benefiting and serving about 150,000 IDP and host communities. In BNS ADRA was one of the first responders when the civil conflict broke out in 2010. Through its work with communities, ADRA was successful in reaching remote and war-torn villages in BNS and providing lifesaving assistance.
For the submitted project PWS, ADRA Sudan targets 310,600 affected populations whether from conflicts or from natural disasters such as floods. The IDPs, returnees and host communities in the four targeted states are West Darfur State, Central Darfur State, White Nile State, and Blue Nile Region. The project will target to fulfill the needs and gaps in WASH services for the IDPs and Returnees. The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) situation has deteriorated due to a worsening economic crisis, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, and decreased/insufficient revenue collections. For Water supply needs, the project will carry on the rehabilitation of 60 non-working Hand pumps.12 water yards are going to be rehabilitated, operating them through solar systems instead of using diesel which is not eco-friendly, besides its high operation cost which could be affordable to the communities which are vulnerable. Therefore, the solar systems are going to ensure the sustainability of fetching water by the targeted affected people. 6 wells will be rehabilitated in three locations (WD, BN and CD).
For Sanitation, PWS will enhance the sanitation condition for the targeted groups by coordinating with the local partners and the Government line ministries and targeted groups themselves in conducting the interventions. PWS will construct 15 institutional latrines in all targeted states to cover schools, health centers and public markets. 300 will be constructed and another 300 will be rehabilitated in the targeted states.
For Hygiene, the project will target the youth groups male and female to train a team of hygiene promoters, to enhance good hygiene practices through hygiene promotion for IDPs and returnees’ people. Hygiene kits are going to be distributed. Menstrual Hygiene Materials will be provided in addition to Soaps, jerry cans , Buckets and Ibrigs and all these activities will cover the four states(WN,CD,WD and BN).
Aguatabs and Purs are going to be distributed to ensure that people there would not be exposed to water contamination dieases and PWR will be focus on capacity building through training of the different groups of target communities which will cover the all WASH topics.

1 - Needs

ADRA through PWS project is targeting the affected populations in West Darfur, Central Darfur and White Nile states, besides Blue Nile region. The prolonged war and communal conflicts that existed in WD & BN have considerable influence reflected in increasing the numbers of affected and vulnerability ratio among the population in those regions. On the hand, natural disasters, in particular, floods colossal impact which on Central Darfur and White Nile with (41,747) and (41,747) affected people respectively (OCHA Sep.2022). As a result, there are many displaced and affected people who have lost access to basic needs including lacking of access to improved water services, proper sanitation and poor hygiene practices. Most of the existing Water supply systems are damaged due to the war and inter-tribal conflicts in spite of the fact there would be a need if they were fully operated. In General, results indicate that 77 percent of the population have access to improved water services from improved water sources without factoring the collection time. About 28 per cent of water sources do not have sufficient water, and 25 percent of water sources are not functioning (MSNA2022,MSNA2021,2018S3M). Putting the microscope into Blue Nile, ADRA has made several assessments during the last two years. results showed that 50% take more than one hour (60 min) to fetch water.After the signed peace agreement revolutionary front and the transitional government, It is highly expected to have dramatically increment in the number of returnees and IDPs back to their areas in BN and WD, furthermore, it could not be overlooked that the last conflicts in WD and BN have displaced affected people who need urgent WASH services from safe drinking water, from a huge need for sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion services.Unsafe water, inadequate sanitation and poor personal and environmental health and hygiene have considerably increased the threat of contracting a growing number of WASH-related diseases such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD), cholera, dysentery, hepatitis E, typhoid, and vector-borne diseases including malaria, dengue fever, Rift Valley fever, chikungunya fever and acute respiratory infections (ARI). COVID-19 is also linked to poor handwashing practices with soap and water. Floods and dry spells (resulting in depletion and extinction of water sources) are also the key hazards affecting people (HNO2023). Hence, there is a gap in Sanitation needs services to be covered. For Hygiene, there is a huge need in the proposed locations where there is more than 93,458 effected by floods (OCHA Oct.2022) added to the affected people from conflicts since the governor of Blue Nile region stated that there is 200,000 IDPs due to the ethnical conflicts. Hence, the need for hygiene kits and related NFIs is existed.Displaced people, returnees, vulnerable residents and refugees are all affected by WASH constraints. Approximately 36 per cent of the displaced population report they are unable to afford water. Lack of gender-segregated latrines, undignified, unlit, and insecure sanitation facilities pose a risk of GBV for girls and women. Menstrual hygiene management is often not prioritised, negatively impacting girls' attendance and enrolment in schools. WASH needs for vulnerable populations are expected to increase due to natural emergencies including dry spells and floods, which are attributed to climate change (HNO2023).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project aims at addressing the emergency needs through the following activity breakdown:1- The rehabilitation of water supply systems, and this will be tailored towards the needs making sure that the approaches are coordinated and efficient. In areas such as West Darfur, White Nile & Blue Nile this will include Emergency water trucking to 40,000 households in site settings, while also providing out of camp populations with rehabilitation of 160 hand pumps and (ensuring the addition of solar panels to ensure cost efficiency). In Wad Madani and Khartoum, this will include the provision of solar panels to support the grid provided water systems already in place and reduce the disruption of water provision in the targeted areas. All water systems will be supported by water quality supplies. 2- Sanitation of facilities will include both sanitation of hh and communal/institutional latrines. This will include both the construction of those latrines (40 communal and 15 inst), especially in areas like West Darfur where gathering sites had been burnt as well as rehabilitation (70 latrines especially in collective center sites such as Wad Madani). In areas such as West Darfur and White Nile ADRA will also undertake incamp lat rehabilitation, as well as construction of 300 hh pit latrines for. Latrine operation will also be maintained through the set up and training of community committees and Water and Sanitation User Committees as well as the provision of cleaning tools and solid waste management support. Rehabilitation of handwashing stations will also be included when and where necessary as indicated by the needs. 3- For hygiene ADRA aims at following two main modalities to meet the needs; first would be direct cash support based on the market analysis and where markets and access allows, this will be included with the MPCT kits, and areas like Madani and some locations in Khartoum have been indicated as possible locations based on ADRA’s assessments. In areas such as Darfur ADRA plans on prepositioning supplies at the CHAD border in order to allow immediate access in the event of an open corridor. The kits will be prepositioned with assistance from the ADRA Chad office at the Warehouse in Adre and/or Abeche according to the ADRA Chad warehouses. The project aims at providing a total of 30,000 kits in both modalities. Furthermore, Menstrual Hygiene Materials (re-usable, 6 pads/ pkd) to cover 3 months for 30,000 women and girl (a Min of 4500 for each state). 30,000 individuals will be reached by hygiene messages in the 4 target states.30 Hygiene campaigns in communal set ups (villages, schools, health facilities, markets and with media) will be delivered to at least cover 30,000 in the four location WN,BN,WD and Madani. For Public health, vector control campaigns (spraying, draining standing water) will be conducted in all locations (2 for each state) to protect people from insects, pests and from diseases related to them. To ensure high quality of water aquatabs and pur is going to be distributed to 5000 HHs among the four states. Capacity building is one of the main activities for WASH sector, 200 individuals will be trained in various subject starting from best water management, maintenance of hand pumps, proper using of latrines and how to manage them, hygiene promotion activities…etc.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ADRA Sudan will follow environment preservation approaches under the umbrella of Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) by contextualizing projects to the environmental realities of implemented areas. Key actions will be assessing projects for potential adverse environmental impact, mitigating negative impact by modifying the project design or compensating for adverse consequences and enhancing environmental benefits by introducing environmental technologies and training on them— for example, using and maintaining solar pumps and solar lights. ADRA Sudan will encourage using hand pumps and solar instead of using diesel in extracting groundwater from water wells, using solar will cut down the weather contamination by the emanating smoke out of diesel engines or generators which will have negative impacts on the Ozone. by using Hand pumps and solar in supplying safe drinking water to the IDP and returnees will enhance their health by avoiding smokes of diesel engine. The project will also encourage the water user committees and communities to make use of surplus water from the Hand pumps, water Yards and Hafirs to cultivate trees around these water supply devices to increase the plantation cover that has a positive impact on the enhancement of the environment. Moreover, will also reduce the accumulation of stagnant water around water yards and hand pumps which results in encouraging mosquito breeding which has a negative impact on the health of the water user.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

While providing WASH services and facilities, ADRA Sudan will consider girls' and women's security and safety. Their engagement will be critical while identifying water points and new water sources to save women. The conflict WD, CD and BN. has had an adverse impact on the majority of communities and tribes however, the effects have been different for women, men, boys, and girls. Women and children make up 60% of the displaced population in Sudan; women and girls represent 25% and 30% of the population, respectively. In addition, Sudan ranks 139 of 155 in the UN Gender Inequality Index, and the culture in BNS WDS, CDS is more severely patriarchal than the rest of the country. Women and girls are targeted for GBV and other forms of abuse— while fetching water or collecting firewood for cooking. Displaced women and children are the most vulnerable and at the greatest risk of being exposed to violence and GBV during their movements to and from markets, for water and wood collection, and for farming activities. Water collection is done by girls, women, and young boys, from ages ranging as young as six up to 60 years. Due to restrictions on access to services and to movement, many household members are opting to move at night when they can. Though older women have a heightened ability to engage in community leadership, women face the following cultural or socioeconomic barriers to community participation and leadership: • Women engage in farming and herding, but the income goes to men. • In public gatherings, women and men sit in separate areas, with women sitting with their backs toward the men. • Women are usually not allowed to complain or air their concerns in public. • Additionally, domestic violence and rape are unfortunately common, but cannot be openly discussed and are rarely reported, causing increased psychological trauma. ADRA Sudan will promote female participation at all stages of the project cycle. Both male and female community members will be consulted during the design of the project through focus groups as well as needs assessments. ADRA Sudan uses its knowledge of the context in the project area, as well as consultations with local staff, in the design of gender-sensitive and tailored approaches. Equal female and male representation in Water User Committees (WUC). Females will be encouraged to openly voice their needs and concerns through regular segregated meetings facilitated by the female Water User Committee members. Since females—especially young girls—are the main water collectors, they will be consulted prior to the rehabilitation of water taps. During distributions, separate queues for women will be established, and females will be consulted on the most appropriate time and place for distributions. Protection -Tailored awareness methods will aim to be inclusive the different segments of the community. Every WASH training is going to have advocacy sessions on protection topics.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ADRA Sudan will ensure coordination with the WASH Sector at both the federal and state levels. ADRA WASH Technical Specialist represents ADRA Sudan in sector meetings at the national meetings, while the Project Manager/WASH Coordinator attends the WASH Sector meetings at the field level. All project activities and targeting were developed based on the gaps identified by the sector through sector reports and meetings. Prior to the project conception, ADRA conducted a joint meeting with OCHA, HAC (Humanitarian Aid Commission of Sudan), SPLA-N, WES (Rural Water Authority & Drinking Water Authority), and UNICEF at the field level, whereby the activities and villages were identified. During implementation, ADRA will ensure regular reporting to the WASH Sector. ADRA will coordinate with different national and international partners in the area, such as World Vision International (WVI), International Organization for Migration (IOM), and ADD (Addition for Disasters Assistance and Development). As WASH is closely tied to Health & Nutrition, ADRA will collaborate with International Medical Corps (IMC) and RI Blue Nile to engage community nutrition/health volunteers and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) groups in assisting in both the design and the dissemination of hygiene information. Health stickers and posters will also be presented to PLW when they visit the health facility, as they are one of the high-risk groups. In this time of the second COVID-19 wave, ADRA will take into consideration all Covid 19 measurements getting feedback from the affected people IDP and returnees in the project's targeted areas. also while collecting the SEA and abuse complaints which will be preserved as top secret. because it harms the dignity of the beneficiary if unfolded by ADRA staff or by health workers.
