Project Summary

The organizations’ current existing and new home-based learning centers (HBLCs) will be used as the spaces from which inclusive, safe, and accessible learning will be provided. Learning facilities will be equipped with inclusive and equitable design (infrastructural access for CWD, gender segregated WASH facilities, water filters and hand washing stations installed and WASH supplies provided, following assessments, which will be rehabilitated and refurbished ahead of the annual monsoon season, in order to withstand damages from disasters. There will be a focus on the back to learning campaign for both out-of-school/drop out children through peer-based education by “champion” students (for 6-18 years aged girls and boys in a home-based delivery mode), as well as remedial lessons from among the learning centers cohort of children who are slow/lagging learners and with specific learning needs (for 1200 children), along with competency testing to assess and measure their learning progress and outcomes, to ensure the right support and placement of students in their grade levels. Educo and partners will address access barriers for girls and CWD through alternative learning approaches, which includes a combination of home-based learning through HBLCs, face-to-face delivery, and peer support through “champion” students who will reach out to out-of-school learners, especially among girls. Training will be provided as per curriculum by drawing from the existing Teacher Professional Development modules with a focus on inclusive education pedagogy, alternative learning modalities, gender responsive learning, and universal design for learning, along with better recruitment, upskilling of educators from host communities, and uptake of more female teachers to enable the participation and retention of more girl children at schools. Training and sensitization activities will be facilitated through community engagement activities, for parents and caregivers, and community members, to enable the return to school for children, to stress the important of education for children while also focusing on attaining social and behavioral change on gender norms among duty bearers and community and religious leaders. The learning supplies and materials for HBLCs to enable CF learning environments. Mentors will be appointed at HBLCs to ensure children’s wellbeing. Establishment of 3 Multi-purpose Center (MPC) for adolescent and women and support with facilitators, materials and maintenance in Camp-8E, 14, 17. Each MPC will cover 120 learner in each camp.360 Adolescent girls and boys, women will be encouraged to participate in the life skills session facilitated by the trained staff in the CFS. Learner will use this space for their meeting space, exchanging views, organizing events. The project will support the CFS with renovation, replenishment of learning and recreation materials. It will be a multipurpose hub for the learner. The project will support the adolescent with skills including materials and Learning aid for learners to promotes adolescents’ resilience and positive coping to the demands and challenges of everyday life. This will be operated following contextualized life skills curriculum that meets the respective needs of girls and boys within agreed framework by the CPSS. CPMS and CPSS approved guideline will be followed in operation of the CFS.

1 - Identified Needs

"After more than 4 years on, the Rohingya refugee crisis remains a protection and solutions crisis at its core (JRP 2021)”. The Rohingya who arrived in 2017 and living in extremely congested camps conditions, do not have refugee status in Bangladesh and Rohingya children do not have access to adequate educational and protection services. The protection environment leads Rohingya refugees to resort to harmful coping mechanisms, including undertaking dangerous onward movements, which expose them to trafficking risks, as well as child protection risks and gender-based violence. In the latest J-MSNA on both communities (May 2021), it was identified that the loss of access to education services because of the COVID-19 outbreak generally constituted a major concern to households in both Rohingya communities. On the one hand, most households reported having faced challenges in supporting their children studying at home, limiting the effectiveness of such remote study, citing the lack of learning materials, guidance from teachers and household members to support children. Even under normal circumstances, access to education for Rohingya children was constrained and since the pandemic, children have faced many additional challenges regarding distance learning and home-schooling. On the other hand, disrupted daily routines and limited access to temporary learning centers also led to concerns about children’s well-being, while households reported an increase in child protection issues in their communities, most notably in child labour, early marriage and children going missing, reported to have increased in both communities. Impacts of exacerbated protection risks may have been further compounded by the limited presence of protection actors in camps following the enactment of COVID-19 containment measures. Currently 884,000 Rohingya refugees living in 34 camps in Cox´s Bazar, of them 51.09% (451,662 Children ( <18 yrs ), 220,111 girls, 231,551 boys) are children, are highly exposed to neglect, abuse, trafficking, distress, and other forms of rights violation. Their forced displacement also hampered their chance for a better life, as 43.9% of these children and 83% of these adolescents have no access to education (JRP2021). Build on the on the experience and learning from previous projects, this 12-month project targets 1560 refugee children and adolescents in Cox´s Bazar in year 2022 who are deprived from access to basic education, and their adult care givers, families, educators and community members. The project will implement a holistic approach integrating Education to provide these groups with immediate access to quality and equitable learning opportunities in a safe a protective environment.The project will ensure active participation of children and adolescents and community members including girls and women, through capacity building actions and by involving them in implementation as frontline educators, trainers, and facilitators. To address the huge gaps in the delivery of learning opportunities and education, Rohingya children and adolescents need support through the provision of not only materials for studying, but well equipped and trained teachers and supporting committee structures, alternative learning methods in the form of home-based approaches to ensure that children continue their learning despite the closure of the TLCs or to prevent the possibility that they may not return when the centers open. As Rohingya adolescents, aged 6-18 years, are concerned about the lack of learning opportunities which prevent them from accessing future opportunities or livelihoods, vocational training is needed to fill their skills gaps.
