1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Since 2017, Cabo Delgado province has been experiencing attacks by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG). Some of the districts affected by violence were also hit by Cyclone Kenneth in 2019. As of January 2021, it is estimated that more than 565,000 people have been internally displaced from Cabo Delgado, with some also fleeing beyond Cabo Delgado to seek refuge (numbers multiplied fourfold since March 2020). Recent attacks have triggered population movements from northern districts into Niassa and Nampula provinces, further increasing the risk of spreading COVID-19. More than 900,000 people in Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula are now facing Crisis or Emergency levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and 4) (HRP 2021). According to Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWSNET), in Cabo Delgado, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes persist as most IDPs have lost access to their basic livelihoods due to the conflict. Neighbouring areas that were previously classified as Stressed (IPC Phase 2) now face Crisis (IPC 3) outcomes due to an increase in the number of IDPs. At national level, according to the available evidence, Cabo Delgado is considered one of the most food and nutrition insecure regions in Mozambique , meaning that their diet is more vulnerable to seasonal fluctuations, climate and economic shocks. Since January 2020, due to poor hygiene practices, lack of access to clean water and sanitation facilities, many cases of diarrhoea have been reported in Cabo Delgado, increasing the risk of malnutrition in a region that already has the highest acute malnutrition prevalence (4 - 11percent), according to the latest IPC/SETSAN (June 2019) findings. In order to effectively respond to the emergency situation and to support the nutritional needs internally displaced persons in the northern region of Mozambique in the context of COVID-19, WFP will implement three interventions: i) Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme, ii) Provision of life-saving nutrition services through the national Nutrition Rehabilitation Programme (PRN) at the health center and community level , and iii) Outreach of HIV prevention and treatment services to the community level targeting vulnerable groups such as hard to reach communities, truck drivers, female sex workers including adolescent girls and young women. The key target groups of such interventions aimed preventing an increase in acute malnutrition, and associated mortality, for the most vulnerable, are specifically children under five years and pregnant and lactating women, including those with HIV. Support to the National Nutrition Rehabilitation Programme (PRN) will focus on treatment of moderate acute malnutrition in children 6 – 59 months and acute malnutrition in PLW, including those with HIV. Blanket supplementary feeding will focus on prevention of acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in children 6 – 59 months through provision of a specialized nutritious food through the General Food Assistance supported through the Food Security Cluster. In addition, social behavior change communication for nutrition will be coupled with cash-based transfers delivered through Food Security Cluster. Social Behavior Change Communication will be implemented to raise awareness about the causes and consequences of acute malnutrition and where to seek treatment and optimal infant and young child feeding practices. In addition SBCC will focus on raising awareness and reducing stigma related to HIV and providing key information on where to access.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project has three strategic objectives: SO1: Lifesaving support for treatment of acute malnutrition in children under-five and pregnant and lactating women to reduce mortality and related morbidity including those with HIV and other vulnerable groups (elderly and disabled)PRIORITY ACTIVITIES RELATED TO SO1: - Restore and expand capacity for life-saving nutrition interventions through health facilities and outreach activities in communities with high influx of IDPs (including host communities) and accommodation centers/ resettlement site; - Treatment of Acute Malnutrition of children under five and PLWs, both from IDPs and host communities living in the affected districts;- Procurement and delivery of specialized nutritious foods, nutrition supplies for therapeutic feeding and other supplements as well as nutrition equipment, for treatment of acute malnutrition in children 6-59 months and PLWs, including those with HIV;- Support the supply chain logistics for nutrition;- Capacity strengthening of health staff on PRN protocol, outpatient and inpatient treatment, M&E and stock management;- Undertake nutrition assessments and SMART surveys for assessing the nutritional status of the populations at risk. SO2: Prevent the increasing of Acute Malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in children under-five and pregnant and lactating women including those with HIV and other vulnerable groups (elderly and disabled).PRIORITY ACTIVITIES RELATED TO SO2:- Implement active case finding and referral of malnourished children and monitor the nutrition situation through MUAC screenings;- Implementation of blanket supplementary feeding programme for children 6 – 59 months integrated with the general food assistance- Social behavior change communication for nutrition sensitive cash-based transfers that promotes healthy diets in support of the food security cluster activities SO3: Promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices, and overall Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices messages for caregivers of children under two including those with HIV.PRIORITY ACTIVITIES RELATED TO SO3: - Promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices, and overall Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices messages for caregivers of children under two either though awareness sessions and cooking demonstration in the communities with high influx of IDPs (including host families);- Training of Community Health Workers (APEs and ACS) on topical areas related to IYCF and HIV to provide one-on-one sessions at household level. - Community radio to provide radio spots, live debates, novellas, and call in programmes related to IYCF and HIV. This package of interventions will be implemented in the Provinces of Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula, specifically in the following districts: Cabo Delgado: Ancuabe, Balama, Chiure, Ibo, Macomia, Mecufi, Meluco, Montepuez, Metuge,Mocimboa da Praia, Mueda, Muidumbe, Namuno, Nangade, Pemba; Quissanga, Palma. Niassa: Lichinga, Lago, and Cuamba. Nampula: Erati, Meconta, Memba, Monapo, Nacala Velha, Nacroa, Nacala Porto, Rapale, Mossuril, Ilha de Mozambique, and Nampula

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Working closely with the Protection Cluster and PSEA taskforce, WFP will coordinate in dissemination of awareness raising on gender-based violence and child protection. Furthermore, training sessions on protection, accountability to affected populations and protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) aim to strengthen awareness of field-based teams to ensure the centrality of protection throughout the assistance. Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM). During all stages of the project life cycle, to ensure beneficiaries can refer their feedback, complaints, suggestions or seek clarifications on any aspect of the assistance, WFP ensures awareness of the affected population on three key CFMs: a) suggestion boxes made available on distribution days; b) helpdesks/ clearly indicated focal points to directly address issues on distribution days c) tollfree inter-agency hotline, Linha Verde da Resposta a Emergencia (1458). 5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Definition of the nutrition response program are based on the sectoral contingency plans and the Nutrition Cluser Plan that are define through a multi partner and multisector exercise under the lead of the Ministry of Health. The contingency plans are adapted to the emergency scenario to generate a specific response plan. Overall nutrition situation will be evaluated through the periodic monitoring through MUAC screenings and the vulnerability assessments to be made by SETSAN, the Technical Secretariat for Food Security and Nutrition. The services offered are at the health facility level in the affected communities to facilitate access to lifesaving services for malnutrition. Likewise, information on the services available will be disseminated through community radio so that affected populations are aware of malnutrition, its causes and consequences, and where to access services and how to prevent malnutrition as well as optimal IYCF practices for PLW with HIV, HIV prevention, testing and treatment. Additionally, community health activists (CHA) will receive training on PRN and support the screening and referral of cases from community to health facility level. CHA will serve as a link between health facilities and affected populations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

There is overlap between beneficiaries that are disabled and those that are acutely malnourished and the same quality of services will be provided regardless of disability status. Additionally, the BSFP is blanket for all children 6-59 months, including those children with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

There are not strong environmental considerations related to this project nor any environmental assessments planned.
