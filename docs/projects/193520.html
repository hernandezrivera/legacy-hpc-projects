Project Summary

The overall project's objective is to contribute to enhancing the coping capacity and meeting the basic needs of vulnerable HHs with protection concerns due to their substandard housing conditions through shelter rehabilitation/adaptation support.
Specifically, the project aims at supporting substandard shelters of the most vulnerable HHs exposed to harsh weather and with protection concerns to meet basic shelter needs and enhance their coping capacities (winterization, summarization, and shelter upgrade, rehabilitation, and repair).
The targeted groups include women, men, girls, and boys with protection concerns such as persons with disabilities (PWDs) including people with vision impairment, people with down's syndrome and female-headed HHs (FHHs), and those in fragile and critically under-served communities affected by the ongoing crisis in the Gaza Strip.
The objectives aligned with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and Oxfam's recent assessment conducted in September 2022. This is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country 2023 HRP strategy specifically objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”. It’s also aligned with objective #2 of the shelter and NFI cluster which is about supporting vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities.
In partnership with the Youth Empowerment Center (YEC), Oxfam plans to target 230 HHs with substandard shelters (including 70 HHs for PWDs). In addition, 130 (a subset of the targeted 230 HHs) vulnerable HHs at risk of exposure to or affected by natural or manmade hazards (e.g. summer/winter storms, flooding) will receive essential protective shelter NFIs.
A community engagement approach will be adopted during the design and implementation of the intervention for a balanced representation, including the specific needs, views, and feedback of women and PWDs.
Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established/activated in coordination with the MoSD to ensure the committees’ direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries. This includes nominating and selecting beneficiaries and collecting their feedback and level of satisfaction during and after implementation, etc.

1 - Needs

The action is informed by Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) HNO 2023, Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA 2022) conducted in July 2022 by OCHA, Shelter Cluster objectives, needs, and priorities for HRP 2023, the preliminary findings of the MoSD and Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH) assessment (August 2022), and the minimum standards of shelter repair and upgrade (July 2018). MoPWH substandard shelter assessment illustrated that 21,500 inhabited housing units across Gaza Strip are classified as substandard. Those units do not meet the minimum requirements for adequate living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, and require different degrees of rehabilitation. Vulnerable groups, namely female-headed households (FHHs), and PWDs including people with vision impairment and people with Down syndrome, were identified as the main priority by the shelter cluster. The MSNA 2022 assessment indicated that over 46,948 FHHs in the Gaza Strip suffer from damages and defects at their shelters, and live in severe and extreme shelter conditions that do not meet minimum standards for protection (in terms of privacy, covered living space, and resistance to extreme weather conditions), or suffer from unrepaired damage or inadequate WASH facilities. Similarly, 57,889 PWDs in the Gaza Strip live in shelters with some kind of damage or defect, of whom 15,373 PWDs live in severe and extreme shelter conditions. The MSNA 2022 assessment also illustrated that 59.8% of the assessed HHs in the Gaza Strip reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues at their shelters, such as cracks in some walls (42.7%), openings or cracks in the roof (30.3%), broken or cracked windows (17.3%), Lack of or bad condition of the kitchen (12.7%), lack of or bad condition of the bathroom (9.2%), and lack of privacy (4.9%). The MoSD confirmed that out of the 21,500 substandard shelters, more than 348 hardship HHs and 94 FHHs urgently need shelter support in Gaza (East of Gaza), Middle Area (Deir Al Balah), Khan Younis (East of Khan Younis). Furthermore, according to the National Society for Rehabilitation (NSR) and the Right to Live Society (RLS): more than 200 PWDs, including people with vision impairment and people with Down syndrome in the same areas, need humanitarian assistance to access adequate shelter indispensable to providing people with a minimum level of privacy, protection, and dignity. Thus, the aforementioned governorates were prioritized to be targeted under this project in coordination with the Shelter Cluster and the MoSD, MOPWH. Targeted HHs selection will consider the nominated beneficiary lists of 348 HHs shared by NSR for PWDs, RLS for Down syndrome people, and AISHA association for FHHs. The nominated lists will be validated and verified by the MoSD and MoPWH. Based on the aforementioned needs and those highlighted in the updated HNO 2023, Oxfam and YEC carried out a rapid need assessment (RNA) in coordination with the RLS, AISHA association, and NSR during August 2022. The assessment which aimed to identify the specific needs of targeted beneficiaries included 8 FGDs (4 women and 4 men) carried out in the targeted communities with 132 participants (61 women and 71 men) and 3 KIIs conducted with local community committees' representatives. The FGDs were conducted with FHHs, PWDs including people with vision impairment and people with Down syndrome. The RNA illustrated that 80% of the attendees' houses are substandard, and do not protect their residents from rain and wind due to cracks in the roof and walls and unfixed windows. Also, the structure of the houses with asbestos or metal ceilings and unrendered walls makes it inadequate to resist cold or hot weather, 63% of the participants don't have privacy in their houses due to limitation of the internal space against the family size with the absence of doors and separation between sleeping, and living spaces, 63% of the participant don't have safe and private access to clean bathing and toilet facilities to exercise good personal hygiene at all times due to unlockable door, no tiling for floor and walls, bad ventilation and light, leakage in water sources, unsuitable discharging system, and un-furnished with supporting tools for persons with disabilities. Overall, 90% of the attendees, including (HHs with PWDs and FHHs) are suffering from major gaps in core shelter items (doors, walls, roofs, windows, shower/toilet, kitchen, electrical connection, and access to WASH facilities) needed to provide the minimum level of physical and weather protection, safety, privacy, dignity, health, and wellbeing of male and female family members of all ages, especially family members with physical disabilities. Furthermore, the need to promote women's participation throughout the project implementation was highlighted to ensure that men are more receptive to women's representation and participation. Noting that participant women preferred implementing the project intervention through an external contractor under Oxfam supervision. This is due to their concerns that received money will be spent by the husbands for other needs and will not be allocated for shelter repair.

2 - Activities

The project will respond to the needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted areas, including children, the elderly, and PwDs. Oxfam, in close coordination with the MoSD, National Society for Rehabilitation (NSR), Right to Live Society for Down syndrome people, and AISHA Association will provide shelter adaptation and rehabilitation to the most vulnerable HHs with PWDs, including long-term or permanent disabilities and people with vision impairment. Works will be carried out according to SPHERE and (IASC) minimum shelter standards. This will provide safe and dignified shelter to targeted HHs and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women. The beneficiaries will be selected considering a combination of protection, economic, and shelter technical vulnerability criteria that was developed in consultation with key stakeholders and partners. Protection vulnerability includes age, disability, number of dependents within HHs, FHHs, and families suffering from harsh living conditions, including protection-related problems. On the other hand, economic vulnerability factors will include main income, source of income, debts, and the status of household assets. Factors reflecting shelter vulnerability include gaps in core shelter items within the 12 set categories based on the Shelter Cluster agreed on Minimum Standards for housing in Gaza. All beneficiaries will be cross-checked with the Shelter cluster and approved by the MoSD to avoid duplication of service and inclusion/exclusion errors. Beneficiaries will be from the identified areas in the eastern parts of the Gaza Strip, namely: East of Gaza city, Deir Al Balah, and East of Khan Younis. Oxfam and YEC will monitor the work of the contracted company. The adaptations include expanding areas for toilets, installing railings and ramps, expanding doors to be suitable for wheelchair access, installing entrance ramps to housing units or WASH facilities, tiling, and other specific relevant works, etc. Oxfam will implement the action in partnership with YEC and collaborate with the relevant stakeholders to ensure access to systems, procedures, and standards to achieve effective, high-quality delivery. Oxfam will ensure the proper delivery of the action as per the project action plan and budget in compliance with the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial, and procurement guidelines. Oxfam will provide advice and support on gender and protection-sensitive project activities and gender mainstreaming. Furthermore, Oxfam will ensure that information on reporting fraud and PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is available to the project and the partner. They are in line with PSEA network recommendations and according to IASC principles and guidance. YEC will lead the community mobilization actions, the beneficiary's selection process, the technical engineering assessment, and the day-to-day follow-up of the rehabilitation work in the field. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The action, in alignment with Oxfam programming parameters, will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly important in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks, and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, and refreshed if the context changes.Oxfam is committed to following an inclusive approach in the design, implementation, monitoring, and funding of assistance to respond to vulnerability in all its forms and reach all marginalized people (including children, youth, older people, people with disabilities, ethnic groups, and others who are marginalized due to their social status). This entails observing and working towards:Participation: We will systematically engage with all affected people and encourage active and meaningful participation of the most marginalized, including people with disabilities, to ensure that their views and capacities are reflected and built into all aspects of the response including assessment, design, delivery, and monitoring and evaluation.Data: We will collect, fully disaggregate, and utilize data for different population groups in order to provide robust evidence to design, revise and learn from programming that is reflective of and appropriate for the people and needs identified.Responding to women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life; community mobilisation sessions on gender, water, and sanitation will be implemented, encouraging the participation of men and women community leaders and mentors. These sessions will also sensitise HHs and the community to accept women's participation. This will contribute to mitigating any harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys, are addressed in line with Oxfam's gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action's response is grounded on the fact that all women's and men's lives and dignity, including people with disabilities, youth, children, and the elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at the community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured by conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided and safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence and coping with stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women's self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved well-being of the family, especially children as care receivers.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam's humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieving equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the vulnerability and the need for the intervention. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows clear selection criteria and presents an equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration, meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities, and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age, or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, using virtual platforms for information sharing, and actual work in the field will also work to address any obstacles that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people's concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles to access, whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention, and would inform the response to ensure equality and impartiality.
