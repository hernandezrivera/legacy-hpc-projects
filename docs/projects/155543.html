Project Summary

The goal of the protection activities is that women and adolescent girls receive information, resources and support to mitigate the risk of GBV and seek GBV services based on their individual needs in a timely and appropriate manner. The IRC will do this, in part, by training community volunteers in GBV prevention activities as well as disseminating information on the existing medical and psychological support services for survivors and vulnerable women and girls. The IRC will carry out awareness-raising activities with the community volunteers in the affected locations, either through mobile teams or at the static women’s centers (WSS and CWC). The mobile case management teams will be deployed and will provide safe, confidential case management services and individual psychosocial support to survivors. The IRC will also conduct these prevention activities in collaboration with existing community-level women’s groups to ensure greater continuity of messaging and to draw upon the existing ties and knowledge of these groups to the targeted communities. The IRC women’s protection and empowerment team will undertake secondary prevention activities including conducting regular safety audits to identify risks faced by women and adolescent girls, conducting focus group discussions, and providing information on services. As part of risk reduction, the IRC will also identify 1500 vulnerable women and girls who will benefit from a dignity kit distribution. Other goal of the protection activities is that GBV case management and psychosocial support service provision be made available in the established centers and through mobile assistance. The IRC will establish case management services within two locations in Fako and Meme divisions. For ensuring access to survivors who are in hard to reach areas, a mobile protection team will deliver psychosocial support once a week per target village/site. While the proposed protection programming will focus on women and girls, the IRC will also ensure men and boys in the areas of intervention have access to information and resources that help reduce their exposure to protection threats, given their vulnerability due to their high risk of being caught, recruited or killed by armed groups.

Needs

Since 2016, Cameroon’s North West and South West regions have experienced social instability and unrest following protests against the treatment of the Anglophone population and the government’s harsh response to these protests. The socio-political NW/SW crisis gradually turned into insecurity and armed violence as early as October 2017. In 2018, escalated tensions and multiple conflict outbreaks between the area’s Separatist Militias and the country’s Security Forces have affected civilians – more than 260 security incidents have taken place through September 2018, with more than 400 ordinary civilians killed in the fighting. Overall, 437,500 people have been internally displaced as a result of the NW/SW conflict. In the South West Region, the nexus of crisis, approximately 246,000 people have been displaced within the region as of November 2018. Through a multisector needs assessment conducted by the IRC and local partners in Meme and Fako in September 2018, Women and girls make up the majority of the displaced population (68%) and face a number of protection risks, particularly related to gender-based violence (GBV), with services to prevent and respond to GBV few and far between. Rape, sexual violence, survival sex, unwanted pregnancies and giving birth in the bush are top health and protection concerns for women and girls in this crisis.
