Project Summary

Malnutrition is one of the top nutrition-related causes of death in children under five in South Sudan. It is estimated that a child with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) or moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) is twelve or three times more likely to die than a well-nourished child, respectively. Likewise, the evidence is strong on the contribution of vitamin A deficiency (VAD) on child mortality and the potential of vitamin A supplementation (VAS) in under-five to prevent 24% of all causes of child deaths. Acute malnutrition has been a protracted problem and one that requires sustained investment.
The needs are substantial and widespread, and it is apparent that there is an absence of even the most basic services in many parts of the country. The protracted conflict further exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition due to the reduced ability of children under five years of age. Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), and chronically ill persons are unable to meet their nutritional needs because of displacement and increased morbidity caused by disease outbreaks, lack of access to clean water, sanitation facilities and basic health services.
Through the 2020 HRP, World Vision will prioritise life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. The response will expand on nutrition specific interventions, as well as reinforce integration and linkages with other clusters to address the key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional child care and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. It will prioritise areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and internal displacement coexist.
Throughout the year 2020, World Vision intends to achieve the following nutrition cluster objectives: 1) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in the former Upper Nile, Warrap, Western Equatoria and Central Equatoria states; 2)To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in the target states; 3) To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in the target state; and 4) To strengthen nutrition information system for evidence-based nutrition response in the target locations by the end of the year 2020.
To materialize the nutrition cluster objectives, World Vision will provide lifesaving nutrition emergency interventions through community based management of acute malnutrition and maternal infant and young child nutrition practice by targeting under 5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the former Warrap, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria states. The proposed emergency nutrition interventions which will be implemented in the communities, at the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Targeted Supplementary Therapeutic Programme (TSFP), and Stabilization Centers (SCs) and will target: 42,300 under 5 children with SAM ( of which 6,300 complicated SAM), 108,000 under 5 with MAM, 53, 800 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with MAM, 142,800 beneficiaries with Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) activities, 675,800 beneficiaries with Vitamin A Supplementation, 29,710 children under 5 with BSFP and 7,800 PLW with BSFP.

1 - Needs

In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity persist in South Sudan. The counties with nutrition intervention are classified between IPC 1 and 4.* Acute malnutrition has been a protracted problem in South Sudan and one that requires sustained investment. The needs are substantial and widespread, and it is apparent that there is an absence of even the most basic services in many parts of the country. The protracted conflict further exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition due to the reduced ability of children under five years of age, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), and chronically ill persons to meet their nutritional needs because of displacement and increased morbidity caused by disease outbreaks, lack of access to clean water, sanitation facilities and basic health services. * According to the FSNMS round 24 results, with a prevalence of GAM of 16.2%, the country has crossed the emergency threshold of 15%. The areas with the highest prevalence observed include Upper Nile and Warrap states. Nine out of the 10 states show a prevalence of 15% or more. *The nutrition cluster estimates that currently, among people in need of nutrition services 77% are from the non-displaced host community, 16% from IDS, 6% from IDP returnees, and 1% from spontaneous returnees. While anyone can experience malnutrition, people who are particularly vulnerable include young children, adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women, older people, people who are ill or immuno-compromised, indigenous people and people in poverty. Groups migrating or displaced due to conflicts, droughts, floods and other natural disasters, famines or land tenure issues are also at acute risk and vulnerable to malnutrition. Other groups at risk of malnutrition and with limited access to services include persons with disabilities or limited mobility, unaccompanied children, minority ethnic groups or those marginalized. Additionally, households and communities that host IDP returnees are more exposed to malnutrition aggravating factors e.g. food insecurity, WASH and morbidity. *Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices continue to be sub-optimal particularly complementary feeding. Incidence of malnutrition rises sharply among children 6-23 months as exclusively breastfed infant transition to complementary foods. According to the FSNMS Round 24 (2019), the proportion of children who meet their minimum acceptable diet remains extremely low at a meagre 7%. Morbidity further compounds the situation with more than a 1/3 of children reported to have been sick with nearly 1 in 2 of the sick children reported to have had a fever/malaria and a quarter of the children having had diarrhea, both of which have a direct negative impact on the nutrition status of young children. * Those not displaced by fighting have faced difficulties in cultivating crops due to interruptions in trade and supply corridors for seeds and supplies. Deteriorating food security is compounded by lack of access to clean water, sanitation and basic healthcare, increased prevalence of disease, and negative impact on infant feeding practices. With the hope progress on the horizon and the possibility of having returnees in the country, the available resources will be overstretched. The country's immunization coverage is still one of the lowest in East Africa making children vulnerable to preventable diseases, thus reinforcing the vicious circle of infectious diseases and malnutrition.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the project will recognize legally the rights of beneficiaries. Being a child-focused organization, World Vision will mainstream child protection in all components of the project. World Vision supports the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child; children have the right to be protected "from all forms of physical or mental violence, injury or abuse, neglect or negligent treatment, maltreatment or exploitation, including sexual abuse". World Vision works to uphold this right through working with local communities and national and international governments to address both the causes and manifestations of violence against children and will incorporate child protection into this program through ensuring that all beneficiaries are protected and their rights are upheld during the project implementation. Project staff members will receive training in protection, child protection, and humanitarian standards to uphold accountability. The special concerns of physically and mentally disabled people will be addressed through careful targeting and active project participation. To promote peace building, project stakeholders will be involved in a Local Capacities for Peace/Do No Harm analysis at the initial stages of this project. World Vision also recognizes that all organizations that come into contact with children have a fundamental duty of care towards them. As such, World Vision acknowledges the level of responsibility to keep children safe in both relief and development interventions, and is working with a number of other agencies to develop and promote good practice in child protection among international organizations that come into contact with children.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

World Vision will ensure that all the target beneficiaries, in the four states, have access to nutrition specific and sensitive interventions through both fixed and outreaches modalities. World Vision will use its Fragile Context Programming Approach ( FCPA) which allows flexibility in the modalities of the lifesaving nutrition interventions. With such approach, World Vision will move from provision of nutrition intervention of OTP, TSFP, SCs to rapid response mechanisms in case where Rapid Response can be the only option which can permit accessibility to beneficiaries. Depending on the humanitarian context and the needs of the target populations, World Vision will either used one approach , example of provision of emergency nutrition at the nutrition sites plus integrated outreaches; or World Vision can use the rapid response mechanism modality or use a combined approach: fix - outreaches - RRM.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

World Vision will implement this project in collaboration with the MoH at national level; the state ministry of health at state level and the county health department at the county level. In ensuring a strong integration of the project, World Vision will work in collaboration with the Ministry of Irrigation and Infrastructure, the Ministry of Agriculture, etc.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The nutrition and health project will be implemented in line with the South Sudan health policy, basic package of health and nutrition services, CMAM and MIYCN guidelines.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/ stable. Safety measures will be taken in the disposal of waste in the nutrition facilities by use of an incinerator and nutrition staff will be sensitized on proper and appropriate disposal measures. Environment and natural resource being a critical, target beneficiaries will be encouraged to conserve the environment and avoid practices that contaminate or affect the nature during the project activities. Hygiene promotion will help to promote messages about the importance of water conservation in addition to basic hygiene and sanitation practices. It is predicted that these sanitation messages will also have a positive impact on the surrounding environment as communities are encouraged to avoid water contamination through keeping their area clean and clear of possible diseases or infections. As all the interventions planned to protect and further promote environmental sensitivity, this project will positively impact on the environment in the project location.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

World Vision will strengthen community support structures to improve community resilience and create an enabling locally based prevention and response mechanism. World Vision will build the capacity of health workers and the health department to prevent, identify and treat cases of malnutrition so that health staffs are able to carry out their work after the project ends. With the involvement of the Community Nutrition Volunteers and the County Health Departments, there will be sustainability of the project when World Vision exits the target locations. World Vision will handover the project to the SMOH when it is time to exit.
