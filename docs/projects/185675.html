Project Summary

The project is to concentrate on building coherence between host and refugee communities in camps 24, 25 and 27 and ward # 7, 8 and 9 of Nhila union of Teknaf upazila of Cox’s Bazar district. Social cohesion will be strengthened through the introduction of dialogue platforms for conflict resolution, through improved access to protection services and increased knowledge about protection problems, as well as through increased safety in the immediate surroundings of the communities.
The project is designed to serve 4453 direct beneficiaries (3155 RRs and 1298 HCs) consisting of: (Community Protection Facilitators (CPFs) and supervisors (63), Community Protection Group members (3360), Dialogue Forum members (600), Adolescence club members (150) and Risk and Vulnerability (280). Apart from this, 20000 indirect beneficiaries will be benefitted as a spillover effect. Among the project participants 50% will be women and in Disability and Inclusion sector standard will be followed.
Sustainable outcomes will be achieved through the participatory nature of the project, especially facilitated through the Community-based Monitoring Committees. Community members and local authorities are involved in the design of the new structures (dialog fora, Community Protection Groups, RVMV), which will increase their ownership to maintain these after the project end and they will at the same time be capacitated to do so. Covid-19 aspects and protection concerns are considered because volunteers will be specifically trained to identify such risks together with their communities and to develop plans in a participatory manner to overcome the identified risks and vulnerabilities.
All core activities (around the Community Protection Groups, Dialog fora, and Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteers) will be implemented in a participatory manner. In addition, two Community-based Monitoring Committees with respective authorities will be formed to monitor not in a technical sense, but to assess the project process and to propose adaptations where needed. Representatives from all beneficiary groups within the project will be chosen in a participatory manner to sit in the Monitoring Committees.
14 Risk and Vulnerability Volunteers Teams will be formed with 14x20=280 community people. These teams will be trained to identify their communities’ Risk and Vulnerabilities regarding to natural disaster and accordingly prepare action plan to reduce their vulnerabilities. These teams will be ready to respond to disasters with the assistance of internal and external actors support if and when emergency situation arises
Project will form Community Protection Groups (CPGs) with 50% women and girls’ members and other forum and platforms will also ensure women participation. At all level project will rightly inform through sessions about women and girls rights, pessimistic effect of early marriage, GBV and SEA. It is expected that CPGs with direct support of CPFs and other project team members will develop stronghold on protecting BBV and SEA. Moreover, they will rightly educate to use CRM system either of this project or any other organization.
The project will build communities capacities and strengthen community engagement to ensure accessibility to life-saving information and knowledge on rights and available resources to communities. The project will develop need-based actionable two way culturally appropriate communication resources and will strengthen dissemination and access to information. The project further strengthens participation of affected population through effective community engagement and functional feedback and complaint response mechanism.
The project has been quite comprehensively engaged with the Sector. On monthly basis the project will keep continue submitting 5W report, Sitrep and effectively attending in meetings. The guidance and suggestions from the Sector will be incorporated in project plan and will ensure its implementation.

1 - Identified Needs

According to the J-MSNA 2021, 56% of household informed that they needed Protection services and 34% of households reported that needs of children in their community were not adequately met. 14% of households mentioned that members in their community wanted to access protection services but were not able to. Furthermore, according to the ISCG Social Cohesion Bulletin (June 2019), the attitude of Host Community members towards Rohingya is characterized by 59% of respondents reporting no interaction with them at all and 45% stating they were unhappy or very unhappy with their presence. Only 15% of Host Community members were stating they were happy with the Rohingya presence. The bulletin furthermore indicates that only 25% of the Rohingya Refugees and 20% of the Host community feel that Rohingya are welcome by the Host Community. Only 18% of Rohingya and 15% of the Host Community population feel there is harmony between Bangladeshi and Rohingya communities living in that area, whereas 73% of Rohingya and 34% Host Community members would like the opportunity to meet each other. According to J-MSNA 2020, 16% of households reported having witnessed tensions between Rohingya and host communities. As per J-MSNA-2020, 68% conflicts occurred over waste in the common places and 48% due to increasing crimes in those communities. The tensions caused by security concern accounts for 44%, awhile environmental degradation is a cause for 39% conflicts. Prices hike of regular commodities and declining moral standard have been reported to be causes of tension for respectively 16% and 14% of cases. Furthermore, other reasons are contributing to conflicts between the two communities, like access to government services, higher transport costs and access to grazing land.Increased social cohesion and strengthened conflict resolution are essential to assure the safety and well-being of both Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities. In order to achieve this, both communities have to enter into dialogue with each other. However, conflict and tensions within the two groups must also be resolved before a dialogue with the other group becomes possible. Furthermore, the RRs and HCs need to have equal access to protection services and need support in reducing the (environmental) risks surrounding their homes. Next to providing live-saving assistance, the Joint Response Plan 2020 also calls for promoting peaceful co-existence between the Host and Rohingya Communities, facilitating equal access to quality services for the two communities, as well as to strengthen public service infrastructure and delivery through system and capacity strengthening, which is where this project will be rooted. Furthermore, it is problematic that RRs and HCs don’t have a common platform where conflicts between the groups can be addressed. HC can turn towards the Union Parishads and RRs can address the CICs, but qualitative structured dialogue between the two groups is not given any space. Moreover, local government representatives find it difficult to resolve those conflicts because of lack of resources and lack of awareness among the communities. Protection is also an issue among Host Community Households and according to J-MSNA 2021, 52% of households having reported community members needing or accessing protection services reported barriers. Furthermore, according to innovations for Poverty Action, 46% of Host Community Members are aware of gender-based violence in their community.

2 - Activities or outputs

Mobilize RRs and HCs for conflict resolution through dialog fora on different levels (block, camp, ward, union level): By this, dialogues will be structured within and between the communities and integrated into existing structure of the Union of Parishads. The ability of community members to resolve conflicts will be strengthened, enabling them to know to whom/to which forum they can turn in case of conflicts. This leads to the structuring and adequate addressing and resolution of conflicts within and between RR and HCs, which minimizes the number of new conflicts. Moreover, these fora will be capacitated to take initiatives to raise awareness on GBV across the communities and will take effective measures for PSEA.Organising platforms for inter-community events among RRs and HCs and theatre performances: This will give space to discuss about protection issues and to foster exchange between communities in general. The platforms will be organised by the CPGs together with establishes adolescent clubs, which will also prepare theater performances for the platforms. In total, 10 platform events will be organized per year, during which dialogues for interested people, sports games and theater performances will be held. The platforms will be educated about GBV, SEA and will be strengthened for PSEA.Formation of Community Protection Facilitators (CPFs, 63) and -Groups (CPGs, 3360) and capacity development to deepen their knowledge on WASH, Nutrition (Maternal and Child Health and Nutrition), Psychosocial Support, Safety and Empowerment Workshops, so that they can support community members to access protection services and to address protection problems. Moreover, Protection Materials including Solar Street Lights will be distributed. 50% of CPGs and CPFs will be women at least. Initially, there will be separate CPGs for RR women, men, boys and girls and respectively also for the HC, to make sure that each group is able to address their specific needs and to create a safe space in which problems can be shared and discussed. To create awareness for the other groups' needs, the CPGs will be mixed later on. CPGs will be educated about women and girls rights, negative effect of early marriage, GBV and SEA. It is expected that CPGs with direct support of CPFs and other project team members will develop stronghold on protecting BBV and SEA. Moreover, they will be educated to use a existing CRM system.Provision of equipment for the CPGs: To carry out the tasks described above; CPGs require protective equipment to ensure their safety. Therefore, each group will receive protection equipment (224 groups), consisting of torch, umbrella, raincoat, mask, first aid kits, hand-wash, hand sanitizer kits, flute, stick gears (wheelchair) for disabled persons, with which they can implement first protection measures. Items will be selected in consultation with communities and their identified needs. While distributing emphasis will be given to women and girls group to rightly equip them to play vital role in protecting women, girls and of other need groups. Through this process women and girls CPGs will be well accepted in their communities.Form teams of Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteers (RVMVs) from HCs and RRs to map risks and vulnerabilities with special focus on infrastructure and the physical environment, and to draw up plans to reduce risks. It is important not only to mobilize the communities, but to involve all relevant actors and duty bearers in planning and implementation. RVMVs are also trained as rescue volunteers and can thus quickly support their communities in the event of a disaster. Significant numbers of women and girls will be included in the RVMV groups. The groups will be trained and will be supported to skillfully map the community risks and vulnerabilities. Based on the identified risk and vulnerabilities a plan will be developed to address the need of women, girls, elderly people and people in special need.
