Project Summary

ACF is proposing to deliver cash-based for emergency food assistance, support income-generating activities through cash grant and business skills training and development for livelihood, and improve agriculture production through seeds and other inputs, and transformation of skills for food and livelihoods to households in Yobe & Borno States of Nigeria. Social protection and social safety net in the form of cash/food voucher transfers aim to support IDPs and conflict-affected households to meet their food basket needs or the cost of maintaining a nutritious diet. Complimentary sessions on sensitization will focused on good nutrition and hygiene practices for pregnant and lactating women, as well as training on income-generating activities, best agricultural practices aim to strengthen affected populations' food production and nutrition security to support the establishment of their livelihoods and build their resilience to prevent the use of negative coping strategies.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria has endured for over ten years has eroded and destroyed public infrastructure and access to basic services remains inadequate, especially due to the continued influx of internally displaced across Borno and Yobe has placed additional pressure on existing services. The impact of several contributing factors such as hazards and vulnerabilities, food availability, food access, COVID19pandemic, food utilization including water and stability was assessed on these outcomes variables. According the Cadre Harmonize in October 2020, the results indicate that about 10 million (9.8 %) people of the analyzed population require urgent assistance in the current period (October to December 2020). During the projected period (June to August 2021), these figures are expected to increase to 13.8 (12.9%) million people unless resilience driven interventions and humanitarian assistance in conflict affected LGAs is sustained. Farmer-herder conflict across the Middle-Belt region and the arm conflict in northeast Nigeria have severely affected food production systems coupled with the COVID19 pandemic. Most livelihood activities across the states showed deterioration, with several zones/LGAs in emergency arising basically from the global pandemic (COVID-19), insecurity and rise in cost of production inputs. In the projected situation, several households in the some states (Katsina, Sokoto, Kaduna, Yobe, Adamawa and Borno) are likely to resort to emergency coping strategies that may result to further collapse in livelihoods; in the absence of conscious livelihood intervention stimulus packages. Nutrition situation in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States remains a challenge. This has been majorly attributed to gaps in caregiver’s nutritional dietary knowledge and hygiene practices. Stressed to emergency malnutrition thresholds was witnessed in several LGAs of these states (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe) during the current period. Stressed threshold GAM rate of 7.5% was reported in LGAs of Adamawa (North) State. In Borno (North) and Yobe (Central) crisis threshold GAM rate of 14.5% and 14.7% was witnessed; while in Yobe (central) GAM rate is under the emergency threshold of 15.8%. Cereals and cash crop production during the 2020/21 cropping year decreased marginally when compared with the 5year average. This may be owed to the hazard (floods) which submerged several thousands of rice and maize field across the cereal production belts of Kebbi, Jigawa, Niger, Bauchi, Kaduna, Kano States, and the FCT. Furthermore, the COVID-19 lockdown and restriction measures led to spikes in the cost of agricultural inputs and reduced agricultural operators’ investment capacity during the 2020/21 farming season. During the lean season (June-August, 2020) food availability in most states is expected to decline below normal levels, although the decline may be slightly cushioned by harvest from 2020/21 dry season farming. In areas with security challenges (Borno, Adamawa, Yobe, Katsina, Kaduna, Sokoto and Niger States); food availability has continued to be negatively impacted owing to disrupted agricultural livelihood activities and limited access to production assets (land, labour, and farm inputs)

2 - Activities

ACF is proposing cash/food voucher assistance, income-generating activities, all year agricultural round production and social protection activities to support the most vulnerable population communities of Gujba, Nangere, Nguru and Machian of Yobe State and MMC, Jere, Monguno, Nganzai, Magumeri LGAs of Borno State of Nigeria. The objective of the intervention are directly related to the humanitarian situations in the North East Nigeria. In Monguno, Magumeri, and Nganzai, beneficiaries will receive NGN22,000 through food vouchers for 5 months while in Gujba Beneficiaries would receive monthly cash vouchers of NGN18,000 for 5 months. In Nangere, Nguru and Machina LGAs the beneficiaries with will be supported with agriculture and livelihoods interventions. Project will also assist some 2,000 HHs for all round farm activities through including agriculture inputs, income-generating activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

AAH will ensure that at all steps of programming conformity to the ‘Do No Harm’ principle. A conflict sensitivity analysis will be undertaken to understand the evolution of conflict in the project areas, the appropriateness and perception of the interventions, impact of activities on the communities served or not served, and expectations of the same communities and stakeholders. Project staff will apply conflict, gender and protection lenses during project planning, design, and implementation. Assessments will be undertaken to carefully consider if the project’s actions, resources, resource transfers, organization and/or staff behaviors and messages might cause divisions or increase tensions within the implementation area, considering potential conflict between IDPs and host community members. The project will adhere to the partners’ non-discriminatory clauses and endeavor to protect people from further deprivation, prevent them from adopting survival and irreversible coping strategies that expose them to future risks. This includes transparent targeting and selection processes; prioritizing beneficiaries’ rights and protection; humanitarian principles and ensuring a CRM mechanism. Context-specific training will help the beneficiaries to identify potential, dignified, conflict-sensitive and, environment-friendly opportunities that drive the HHs towards sustainable livelihoods. The inclusion of the Porridge mum complementary activities in the Project is to integrate sustainability initiatives within the project to allow for a gradual transition towards stronger recovery programing and, resilient and diversified livelihoods.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACF has designed and implements robust processes to create an effective system that has measures to prevent any misuse or misappropriation of donor funds. Action Against Hunger has developed a robust accountability system with a Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) within the M&E department, which aims to improve the quality of interventions and document key learning outcomes. At the same time, it provides timely information to program staff and senior management to make critical and strategic decisions regarding program interventions. Available channels for feedback and complaints are as follows: the toll-free Line, Complaint Booths, Help Desk, Community Complaints/Feedback Mechanism, Staff field visit and surveys.A key part of the Feedback and Complaints mechanism processes includes; the CRM - Toll free line, community feedback and complaint focal persons (CFCPs), the whistleblower service (email address) and regular sensitization of staff and partners.Banners with a toll free complaint number for Borno and Yobe are displayed at community points, all vendor sites and beneficiary cards. If areas are out of the GSM network, trained community feedback and complaint focal persons (CFCPs) shall be registering any compliant and referring it to designated ACF staff. A WhatsApp group for CVs, Supervisors, CFCPs and vendors shall be created for better coordination and reporting of feedback. As a result, beneficiaries are aware of confidential channels to provide their feedback.
