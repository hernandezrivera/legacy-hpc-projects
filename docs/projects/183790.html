Project Summary

Although the transitional government has taken bold steps to advance the economy, stability and peace in Sudan, these reforms will take time and there is still political instability making the context difficult to predict. In 2021, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance increased compared to 2020, from 9.3M to 13.4M (HNO), due to various conflicts that occurred in the country, the ongoing economic crisis, major floods that occurred in 2020 and 2021, and the measures taken by the government to limit the spread of COVID-19 pandemic . All these events contributed to deteriorate the purchasing power and the living conditions of the Sudanese population across the country . However, an increase of the Sudanese pound stability was observed since the alignment of the official exchange rate with the black market rate in February 2021. Based on the needs identified by TGH and humanitarian sectors, the project aims to respond to the most urgent needs of the population through a multi-sectorial response in WASH, Education, Child Protection and ES/NFI in South Kordofan, West Darfur and Central Darfur.
This project will prioritize activities that ensure multi-sectorial and life-saving assistance to people affected by crisis - which includes conflict-affected people, people at risk of epidemics and floods, and other vulnerable residents - in order to reduce mortality and morbidity in the targeted areas. The WASH component will respond to the most urgent needs through rehabilitation and maintenance of water systems, water treatment, construction of gender-segregated sanitation facilities accessible for persons with specific needs, installation of hand washing facilities, distribution of hygiene kits. This component will be complemented by Education, Child Protection and ES/NFI activities: it will include distribution of emergency shelters and NFI kits for conflict and natural disaster affected communities; distribution of school furniture, student, teacher and recreation kits as well as construction of temporary learning spaces and rehabilitation of schools and WASH facilities in school; provision of children case management, construction and support of child friendly spaces, Psychosocial Support (PSS), capacity reinforcement of the community and stakeholder on child protection issues.
While protection mainstreaming will be integrated during the project through beneficiaries consultation and involvement to mitigate any GBV and protection risks, as well as through a strong accountability for affected people mechanism – including Feedback, Complaints, and Response Mechanism – TGH will also conduct specific child protection activities, including provision of case management and referral services, maintenance and running of child friendly spaces, support of family based PSS services and conduction of community based group activities.
To ensure sustainability of the project and improve resilience of communities, TGH will focus on capacity building of partners, linked with a localization approach. Beneficiaries and local stakeholders will be trained: in WASH, TGH will train committees for the maintenance and operation of water points, as well as hygiene promoters to conduct awareness sessions on safe hygiene and sanitation practices; in Education, teachers and PTA will benefit from training on various subjects, including Psycho-Social Support (PSS); and finally in Child Protection, TGH will train the stakeholders involved in this sector at local level.

1 - Needs

The number of people in need in Sudan has been steadily increasing over the last years. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 5.4 million people were in need of assistance in 2015, reaching 13.4 million at the beginning of 2021, including 7.6 million of women and girls.5.5 million of people have been identified as in need of humanitarian assistance in Darfur, while they were 737,120 identified in South Kordofan (SK) in early 2021 (HNO 2021). These states are highly impacted by population movements, contributing to the vulnerability of the inhabitants: 2.5 million people in Darfur and 364,000 persons in Kordofan States remain displaced (UNHCR, 30/09/2021). TGH will target specific localities affected by major conflicts, population movements and/or natural disasters in West Darfur (WD), Central Darfur (CD) and SK:- In WD, particularly in Geneina and Kreinik localities, the population was severely affected by the conflict that occurred in January and April 2021 between Massalit and Arab tribesmen. In total, 97,000 persons were displaced in January 2021 (IOM, 27/01/2021) and 164,985 by April 2021; 115,525 persons remain displaced at the beginning of September 2021 (IOM, 13/09/2021). 92% of the IDPs are still gathered in schools and other public buildings, and are in dire need of ES/NFI, WASH, Protection and Education support (IOM, 13/09/2021).- In CD, violent clashes occurred in January 2021 between Fallata and Rizeigat tribes leading to the displacement of 19,300 persons (OCHA, 28/01/2021). Attacks by armed militia in Jebel Marra, North Darfur, between 24–31 January displaced a further 22,000 people (OCHA 04/02/2021). The IDPs found refuge in Central Jebel Marrah. In July and August 2021, further clashes in Sortony. At least 30,000 persons were displaced following the violence from North Darfur to Central Jebel Marrah and North Jebel Marrah localities (OCHA, 12/08/2021).WASH, ES/NFI, Education, Protection and FSL needs are observed in Um Dukhun locality while the context remains volatile due to the conflicts that occurred in Central African Republic (CAR). In CAR in December 2020, an upsurge of violence was observed before the presidential elections scheduled on the 27th leading to the displacement of 55,000 persons (OCHA, 24/12/2021). While the situation in Darfur tends to be stabilized in the last few years, returns are observed from CAR to Um Dukhun locality. These returnees are in need of humanitarian assistance and they put additional pressure on the natural resources. - In SK, the population was affected by the years of conflict between governmental forces and the Sudan People Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) following the independence of South Sudan in 2011. High level of needs was identified in 2021: more than 740,000 persons were in need of humanitarian assistance, including 290,000 in severe needs and 220,000 IDPs (HNO). Specific needs concerning WASH and Education were identified by TGH in Tadamon and Rashad localities: the population is facing a high proportion of non-functional water points and must fetch water from unprotected sources; most of the people are practicing open defecation; while schools are facing new arrivals of students following population movement, they are in need of classrooms rehabilitation, teachers training, as well as sanitation facilities construction. According to the Child Protection Cluster, in West Darfur there are significant needs in PSS, alternative care, legal assistance, community engagement, case management. For Central Darfur, the needs are for establish permanent safe spaces, PSS, reintegration activities of children released from armed groups and armed forces, capacity development, case management. In South Kordofan there are significant needs in capacity development, support to unaccompanied children, and placement of children in alternative cares.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

TGH projects are designed to consider and minimize their environmental impact. Their imprint is considered in the development phase, and reviewed during the project cycle to allow for project modifications as needed. TGH actively, in Sudan and globally led from the headquarter, integrates appropriate measures to reduce its environmental impact. TGH procedures in Sudan facilitate reduction in paper usage, utilizing electronic signatures and electronic document validation where possible. In addition, as a phase one TGH has installed solar panels on Um Dukhun base Darfur; with budget and workplan in place for phase two, to ensure all bases within Darfur are supported by Solar panels. Including solar panels in bases outside of Darfur is phase 3 of this planning. To reduce reliance on long-distance transport options, and thereby reduce fuel consumption, TGH prioritizes localized purchasing procedures. In particular materials for shelter kits, and durable latrines are available locally (sand, wood, stones, mud bricks, etc.). However the focus on these local materials is also not without impact. The use of locally sourced wood, found in nearby forests, can have a considerable impact on deforestation. As such, TGH links WASH with FSL activities to support the planting of trees and regeneration of forests through development of IGA for replanting. Ensuring the quality of the grounds in which we work is essential. TGH WASH procedures use lime when treating fecal sludge; lime being a natural resource itself, it is ideal to minimize the negative impacts of sludge on the community environment. Further, to reduce reliance on access to fuel and reduce pollution, TGH installs solar PV-panel to provide power to water points. These have the added benefit of reducing the fuel consumption rate for the water points, as well as reducing the water fee for community members. TGH installed data logger in the well to monitor the ground water. The data from the logger are used to understand how the ground water resource varies through time which helped to take action before depletion of the resource. Community awareness and engagement is key to the success of responsible environmental practices. TGH conducts community awareness sessions, in parallel with support to solid waste management committees, who can also then carry the messaging forward.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

For mainstreaming Protection, TGH endorses the principles defined by the Global Protection Cluster guidance on Protection Mainstreaming also endorsed by Sudan HCT Protection Strategy 2019 – 2021, namely:1/ Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks.2/ Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Maintain focus on individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.3) Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.4) Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.“Do no harm” is a core principle of TGH strategy, interwoven within accountability, inclusion and protection mainstreaming. This ensures all programs are designed to ensure participation and accessibility for communities without discrimination. As such, TGH undertakes a community based approach to all program design and at all stages of the project cycle. All projects commence with community consultation (FGD), with a focus on consultation with women’s groups (facilitated by women) and persons with special needs; along with local authorities. This ensures consideration of specific risks and accessibility for all community members. TGH can then adapt methodology to encompass these needs, to be able to target response to meet the needs of the most vulnerable, and facilitate inclusion without discrimination.An example of this flexibility is seen through TGH distribution methodology. Persons who have been selected as a beneficiary but cannot attend the distribution site, can identify this to TGH team during beneficiary verification process. As such, TGH team then makes arrangements for direct household delivery for that particular person.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As demonstrated by TGH membership in the AAP/CEA Working Group, accountability is key in TGH activities. The beneficiaries are involved in the all stages of the project. Putting people at the center of decision making by promoting their participation and community consultation is part of the development of the communities’ self-protection capacities advocated by TGH. The WASH activities will involve the community to participate in decision making such as during selection of construction site, HKPs or WUCs selection. In Education and Child Protection, PTAs, teachers and the community will be involved to select the relevant topics they want to receive training on, building on the necessary child protection basic training required. This Bottom-up and inclusive approach promoting community ideas and solutions will be encouraged through cooperation with community members participating in these structures important of Do No Harm strategy. Age, gender, vulnerability or disability are taken into account while identifying the beneficiaries and implementing the activities.At the beginning of the project, TGH will present to the beneficiaries the different contact points for the Feedback, Complaint and Response Mechanism (FCRM). When feedback is received, it is recorded by the MEAL manager and transmitted to sector managers or Country Director (according to the degree of severity) for review. TGH then ensures to close the feedback loop and provide the community member or group (if the feedback is not anonymous) with a clear response and outcome. Where a complaint has been received on TGH processes or quality of materials provided, this is considered either immediately as a program adaption and used to adapt and improve future interventions. PSEA policy is a key component of TGH accountability strategy in Sudan with TGH ensuring regular attendance at National PSEA meetings. Staff training and awareness campaigns are key to ensure teams maintain a ‘do no harm’ approach particularly with highly vulnerable groups such as women, girls, elderly or PWD. As part of International-National sustainability of action approach, TGH has ensured a PSEA referent both at headquarters and mission level, supporting implementation of the policy. All recruited staff participate to PSEA training at the beginning of their contract; along with refresher after 12 months. Linked with its FCRM, TGH informs all beneficiaries of the PSEA policy, reporting procedures, and awareness raising on their rights. Any PSEA-related report would be immediately considered by TGH CD, followed by an investigation at HQ level if criminal behavior is potentially involved and donor would be informed of the risk. TGH priority is transparency and an assurance of safety and security for staff and beneficiaries alike. TGH PSEA policy was validated by UNICEF through their annual accreditation.
