Project Summary

The "Resilient Futures" project is being implemented in the town of Mora, in the Far North of Cameroon. In the first year of implementation, the project will reach 100 young people between the ages of 16 and 35. The overall objective of the project is to ensure that vulnerable refugee youths, IDPs, and host community members affected by displacement crises in urban settings have access to livelihoods that enable them to meet their basic needs and generate income and assets in a safe and sustainable manner and contribute positively to their communities

1 - Besoins / Needs

As global humanitarian crises are leading to growing displacement and mass movements of populations, the vulnerable situation in which many displacement-affected populations find themselves is becoming less sustainable. As more and more populations flee conflict, disaster, and insecurity, refugees and internally displaced people (IDPs) are forgoing traditional camp-based resettlement settings in lieu of greater perceived economic and social opportunities in cities and urban areas. The majority of the world’s refugees and internally displaced people are now living outside of camps. Furthermore, displaced people often resettle in neighboring countries that are already struggling economically; 80% of displaced populations resettle in developing countries. Alternatively, some displaced populations resettle in developed countries such as in Europe, where cities, too, have struggled to maintain economic growth amid large influxes of new populations. Urban centers across all these contexts represent both opportunity and difficulty for vulnerable populations, in particular youth, where with sufficient access, knowledge, and support, economic integration is possible. Without due support, however, youth continue to struggle with some of the highest rates of unemployment, social stigmatization, and an inability to integrate positively in their new markets and communities. The Far North is one of the most vulnerable region in Cameroon is particularly vulnerable to frequent shocks that include epidemics, floods, and violent conflict which escalated in 2020 and led to the displacement of over 560,000 people, including 117,826 refugees. Despite the presence of several state and regional armed forces, the security situation remains precarious and continues to severely impact the population. The ongoing crisis has also negatively impacted livelihoods and local economies in a region already characterized by endemic poverty (74.3% of the region’s population is living below the poverty line, compared to 37.5% nationally), and lack of natural and financial resources and market opportunities. The proposed activities will reinforce the resilience of youth people through the provision of multiple services including: entrepreneurship skills training (provided at the three existing training centers in Mora), internship, business mentorship, and small-business grants to start or grow existing businesses. The selection of beneficiaries will be made through a participatory process and IDPs/refugees will be included. The IRC will organize several discussions sessions with the communities and authorities to inform them about the project and get their feedback. These sessions will consolidate social cohesion and improve community participation in the project implementation. The project will also facilitate the placement of clients with existing entrepreneurs, as part of the internship and mentorship programs or for employment, where possible.
