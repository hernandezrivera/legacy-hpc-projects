Project Summary

Principal Objective: Fulfillment of fundamental of human right to water by ensure equitable, sufficient and sustainable access to basic quality WASH services, available when needed in accordance with safety and dignity of the unserved, under served and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank
Specific Objective: Ensuring acutely vulnerable Bedouin and non-Bedouin herders’ communities in Jericho, Ramallah and the Jordan Valley with access to essential WASH services

Needs

The design of this intervention has been built on the needs identified in Area C where vulnerable communities are affected by substandard water quality, lack of access to water supply, proper treatment and disposable of waste, hygiene awareness, water storage facilities and inability to pay for water in 2014 by CISP and after that several updated are done with stakeholders, of which the last have been conducted in 2018. Data have been collected in the communities of Jericho and Ramallah districts. Bedouins (especially women and children) and herders living in Jericho/Ramallah area face serious difficulty to gather water to access to water network (if present), they cannot build any water infrastructures because of the consequently high probability of demolition by ISF. 690 families were mapped, only 218 families are connected to the water network (Fasayil, part of Al Auja, An Nuweima, Ein ad Duyuk al Fauqa and Ein ad Duyuk at Tahta and Fasayil al-Fauqa). Furthermore the storage capacity of the communities is very limited: permeable and friable soil does not allow for digging of underground cisterns and mainly plastic tanks are in use. Among the families not linked to water network (472), 67% has 1,5m3 storage capacity (for both human and animal consumption). Almost 84% of individuals consume less than 30l/day and less than 9l/day is allocated to each livestock head. Water distribution through trucks is commonly a resort for Bedouin herders’ communities even if this increases the water cost. More than 400 families (located in At Tayba, Muarrajat Center, Muarajaat caravan side and Muarrajat East, Ras Ein al Auja) need water through trucks transportation and they are not covered by any NGO. The opened channel starting from the spring of Ras Ein al Auja and the communities of Deir Haijla and Muarrajat East are the most contaminated places by human fecal coli according to CISP water tests study done in 2018 in collaboration with PWA and Jericho Governorate. Some Bedouin families get water from unconventional sources, such as open and unprotected channels and irregular connection. The lack of access to drinking water makes these communities very vulnerable. Exposing them at risk of harassment from IDF during transportation and provision, for all communities is very hard to live in the area. The problem becomes acute in summer when temperature rises over 40 degrees. Targeting especially women and children, CISP promotes approaches to redressing gender inequality and improving the well-being of Bedouins women and children since 2005. CISP opened a Bedouin cooperative in Jericho in which women were involved in dairy productions (2010-14). In 2017-18, CISP increased the storage water for 180 families and built 13 new latrines with shower in Jericho Area C. Women have been involved in the water issues by dealing with the general health activities of the family, taking care of the children hygiene and cleaning latrines; whereas men handle the transportation of the water from the filling points and the tanks’ and latrine maintenance. The lack of hygiene awareness is very high: 95.2% of the interviewees don’t disinfect water and don’t separate water for human consumption from animal consumption. Lack of access to clean water and living in contaminated environment are known factors to have also severe impact on pregnancy outcomes. Bedouins women do not have access to sanitation facilities after childbirth. During 2017-2018, 150 Bedouins families participated at awareness hygiene sessions of which 64% were female Bedouins. Field assessment shows that 385 families do not have an appropriate latrine but use open spaces for their needs. Women due to the lack of privacy wait until dark to do their needs outside and these cause urinary infections. All proposed activities are identified by CISP and PHG in according with PWA.

Activities

Carrying out a fast assessment in order to prioritize the needs and interventions based on the objectives, including updated security situation by bilateral meetings and field visits with stakeholders (PWA, Municipalities and WASH cluster members). Field visits and female and male focus groups will be organized in order to collect their feedback and suggestions about how to solve problems according their different point of view. R1 - Ensure access to basic WASH services in accordance with equity and safety of the most vulnerable Palestinians Bedouins communities under occupation in the Area C. Mechanisms distribution, criteria and guidelines will be defined in coordination with PWA, local authorities and Bedouins communities according an inclusive approach and community participation avoiding marginalization of the most vulnerable in aligning with PWA and WASH cluster criteria/framework. The program has to seek the values of local experiences and viewpoints to understand what women and men want and how it could be sustained. All partners must be raised awareness about gender and vulnerable conditions. A1.1 – Purchase of 1 tractor with mobile water tank (capacity of 10.000m3) to distribute water for the communities. Its use will be coordinated by PWA and to be aligned with PWA and WASH cluster criteria/framework. The activity will facilitate the access to the water for 3,560 people, of which 543 are women and 1,631 children under 12 years old. A coupon management system will be installed according to PWA criteria and within WASH framework. A1.2 - Rehabilitation of pipes and connection with the water drinking storage tanks. The pipes will bring the water close to the shelters responding to the needs especially of women for domestic use and for the animals.A1.3 - The project will provide 2,190 people with 238 water plastic tanks and 25 mobile tankers to enlarge the storage capacity of the communities from 1,5 m3 to 4 m3. A1.4 - Provide affected populations access to adequate sanitation facilities for 112 people (67 female and 45 male) through installation of 20 culturally accepted and sustainable latrines with shower. The installation will be coordinate with PWA, Bedouins’ cooperative/associations, Jericho governorate and Mukhtars of the communities.R2 - Strengthen WASH response capacity to cope with new and protracted threats A 2.1 - Distribution of 260 chlorination kitsA 2.2 - Hygiene promotion campaigns will focus on water treatment, water tanks maintenance, latrine maintenance and cleaning, water borne diseases, excreta disposal and ways of saving water from contamination. The participation of women in these activities will be a focal issue. Women will be also selected per village and will participate in Training of Trainers (ToT). Production and distribution of didactic material for awareness and training (IEC).R3 - Increase national WASH sector governance and coordination capacityA 3.1 - Coordinate and collaborate with PWA staff at local and national levels in monitoring the distribution system among the selected communitiesA3.2 - M&E manager will assist the field implementation tin, the systematic collection and analysis of information. The activities will be monitored through daily visits to the sites by the M&E, project staff and water expert. The M&E will operate within the matrix of the logical framework and will be based on indicators as outlined in this project proposal. The information collected will serve as a basis for any correction.
