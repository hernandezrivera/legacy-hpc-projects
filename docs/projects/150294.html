Project Summary

The aim of this project is to respond to emergency shelter needs of vulnerable households, mainly in Area C of the Jordan Valley, with provision of timely and adequate shelter and NFI solutions through a comprehensive integrated approach that supports building resilience, reinforcing protection, and supporting the steadfastness of the most poor and vulnerable households at risk of displacement or exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns.
The intervention will addresses immediate needs, as well as the underlying conditions that give rise to those needs through a multi- pronged approach that includes; (i) Establishment of community based protection committees and provision of tailored capacity building to empower affected people to cope with sudden and protracted shocks and stresses, increase accountability, information dissemination and transparency specifically to report protection concerns, disseminate DRP practices with the inclusion of training on SPHERE standards and monitoring of intervention. (ii-a) provision of durable climate protection shelter materials and technical rehabilitation support and oversight to the most vulnerable households whom are exposed to harsh weather or protection concerns or at risk of displacement. (ii-b) Prioritized Shelter rehabilitation with full technical support and oversight for Persons with disabilities and widowed, separated or abandoned female headed households through MA’ANs local technical team. (iii) Stockpiling and prepositioning of adequate shelter/NFIs stocks, and protective shelter material for HHs under risk of displacement and/ or at risk of exposure to natural or manmade hazards or settler violence and increase the capacity to respond to natural and manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner.
The intervention is built on MA’AN and ACTEDs Community Protection and emergency response approaches, & previous shelter rehabilitation experience in the area, placing emphasis on the themes of (1) Engagement at the community level with a focus on community ownership and responsiveness (2) Community Driven Resilience focusing on Protection mechanisms for vulnerable groups in communities. To ensure effectiveness of intervention, targeted communities and neighborhoods have been coordinated with the shelter cluster, other partner organizations and actors working in the area to avoid overlap of assistance and ensure best reach in emergencies. MA'AN has prior experience and good understanding about the current legal situation for the construction in Area C and employs strategies in employing a low profile including: Rehabilitating structures from the inside of shacks and tents, Carrying out work at night, Staggering assistance over a prolonged period of time, coordinating with households and key communities members to stay updated with IOF activity in the area. Moreover, As the communities are subject to ongoing threats, and harassment, Project Management will also supervise the promotion and public announcement of the project among the inhabitants of the targeted communities to ensure an update of priority households in the database at the project start. Women in these communities will play an integral part in the selection of beneficiaries as part of the established CBPC , other members include village council heads and representatives, Mokhtar ( village head), and community members. Project Management unit will ensure that CBPCs and selected households are well informed about their rights, obligations and potential risks involved regarding housing, land and property issues and provide their consent to all proposed project activities.
The intervention contributes the Country Strategic Objective 2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.” For beneficiaries for whom a full upgrade is not possible, the action will provide emergency winterization and transitional support, this contributing to the Country Strategic Objective 3 “the ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”.
The Intervention design endorses all of the Shelter Cluster recommendations, in particular by providing a tailored combination of modalities to maximize impact on beneficiaries while fostering ownership and resilience through a demanding community based self implementation approach. Through its upgrade component, the action will contribute to the Shelter Cluster objective 1 “Support to displaced populations to mitigate immediate harm and ensure adequate and safe temporary shelter solutions.” Likewise, the transitional assistance / winterization component will contribute the Shelter cluster objective 2 “Support to vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns.”

Needs

The approximate 300,000 Palestinians living in the West Bank’s so called ‘Area C’ are especially vulnerable as a result of Israeli occupation policies. The dire living conditions of these communities makes them at risk of forced displacement due to the lack of basic necessities for dignified humane living conditions. Many of the residents live in poor housing conditions where they are not protected from extreme weather conditions. The 2018-2020 HNO revealed that there are 13,000 demolition orders in Area C of the WB affecting 15,000 ppl , and over 10,000 people live in 63 communities in Area C of the WB at heightened risk of forcible transfer. The high risk of house demolitions and forced evictions further contributes to the insecurity of their lives and livelihoods. As a result, the inhabitants suffer from high rates of poverty and unemployment. The impact of these conditions are further exacerbated with marginalized groups within these communities such as Persons with Disabilities, the elderly, and female headed households as they are affected disproportionately. Based on coordination with the Substandard Shelter assessment in Area C by Shelter Cluster and partners and on in house data on communities, MA’AN and ACTED have pre identified 12 communities in need of house rehabilitation in the Jordan Valley, encompassing over 6,800 individuals (around 1,140 households) presenting medium to high shelter vulnerability and in need of structures’ improvement and subject to settler harassment, and at risk of forced displacement. These 12 communities were selected after a rapid community sample assessment in Oct 2017, and updated in Apr & Sep 2018 and are considered highly vulnerable in shelter under OCHA’s Vulnerability Profile Project with structure mainly being make shift shelters in the form of a shack, tent, and substandard brick or mud structures and were often overcrowded with insufficient space for gender privacy and safety. The structures suffer from water infiltration, moisture damaged wall plaster, improper ventilation, collapsing and damaged ceilings and roofs and make shift walls providing no insulation A significant number of the Bedouin communities houses have no cement flooring, this is coupled with poor utilities and inadequate sanitary units. Furthermore, a number of the households in these communities have, in past decade, experienced contained harassment that included settler intimidation, demolition of livelihood and residential infrastructure, and demolition orders. The assessment conducted further confirmed lessons from both partners previous experience in the sector concerning the impact of substandard shelter on vulnerable household members. Particularly, the lack of appropriate WASH structures in homes pose a burden for managing daily household tasks for women. Moreover, in households with multiple wives, women suffer from lack of privacy comfort and lack of decent living spaces. Large household size and small shelter also poses a challenge for the privacy and safety of boys and girls as in some cases over 9 children occupied only one room. Moreover, female headed households, the elderly and disabled have less capacity to respond to emergencies and ensuring protection of their children due to limited access to resources and restricted mobility and human resources to upgrade their shelters. These groups are in dire need of comprehensive support with shelter upgrade to ensuring adequate climate protection, safety, and security, privacy and dignity and improved wellbeing for occupants. Moreover, as these groups are less prone to have timely access to health centers in cases of emergencies, distribution of protective assets including first aid kits, fire extinguishers..etc should be prioritized. These factors coupled with economic disadvantages of households, and lack of weather protecting structures further contribute to the vulnerability of these groups.

Activities

Act1: Establishment & CB of Community based protection committees:(CBPC) will be established and trained on SPHERE standards and protection principles. These committees will be involved throughout the whole project to liaise with local communities and ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. Further they will mainstream protection principles and guarantee better compliance and inclusion of protection criteria (safety, respect of privacy , consideration for gender and age constraints/specificities) in the activities’ design and work implementation. Women will be particularly encouraged to join the CBPC as active members. To ensure DRP & shelter related protection 3 committees will be established with members elected by local authorities and communities a min of 30% of women. Each committee will be trained & continuous capacity building activities will be implemented through monitoring visits and mentorship by ACTED’s Protection Officer. Committees will mainstream protection, report protection concerns, and raise awareness among communities on disaster risk preparedness. Act2: Rapid damage baseline assessment: The project will reference MA'ANs JV shelter rehabilitation database and community infrastructure assessments. Listed HHs are ranked based on a comprehensive analysis according to a set of criteria that falls in alignment with the shelter cluster technical survey and analysis including: socio economic conditions HHs, housing conditions, type of g shelter, gender make up of HH, female headed HHs , persons with disabilities, & health conditions. Individual assessments of shelters and community structures will be conducted with males and females separately to ensure their shelter and protection needs are expressed and met.Act3: Substandard Shelter Rehabilitation/ winterization upgrades for the most vulnerable and at risk groups: (a3.1) provision of durable climate protection shelter materials and technical rehabilitation support and oversight to the most vulnerable households whom are exposed to harsh weather or protection concerns or at risk of displacement. (ii-b) Prioritized Shelter rehabilitation with full technical support and oversight for Persons with disabilities and widowed, separated or abandoned female headed households through MA’ANs local technical team. Can include creation of internal walls and installation of doors for privacy and separation for genders, possible expansion with homeowner consent and legal precautions for overcrowding, partitions and windows to neighboring structures for privacy, access to WASH facilities, and disabilities access. All renovation work will be based on the individual technical assessment of the targeted shelter to ensure privacy, safety and gender sensitivity within the same household and with neighboring households. For substandard temporary shelter that cannot be upgraded up to standard (makeshift shelter, caravan, tent etc.) transitional shelter assistance by ACTED will be provided to reduce protection risks and increased seasonal displacement.Act5: Provision&Stockpiling shelter/NFIs stocks, and protective shelter material for HHs under risk of displacement and/ or at risk of exposure to natural or manmade hazards or settler violence and increase the capacity to respond to natural and manmade hazards. Several communities have limited access to health clinic in emergencies, thus, first aid kits along will be distributed to most vulnerable households, trainings will be coordinated through other MA’AN projects. 100 NFI kits and 100 Protective material packages will be prepared, to be provided in case of emergency. These kits will be prepared in line with Shelter Cluster NFI/shelter material and protective assists standards to include blankets, mattresses, tarpaulin, bed sheets, gallon water, kitchen and hygiene set.

Indicators

The M&E Units will be in charge of gathering and analyzing all monitoring data in order to provide the following information products, in addition to measuring progress against indicators and provide clusters and OCHA with all data requested:1) Baseline report and assessment: following the baseline assessment, a report will be drafted to provide recommendations regarding project implementation and setting up baseline data. This report will be shared to the intervention’s donor. A technical appraisal will be conducted in identified communities to evaluate current conditions, detailed needs, and vulnerabilities. Beneficiaries (at least one male and one female per household) will be involved in the process to ensure they can express their concerns and most pressing needs. Criteria of vulnerability will include: basic NFI needs, damage repairs/renovation needs, exposure to harsh weather, exposure to domestic violence/GBV cases, safety risk (fire and child safety), adequateness with special needs. A detailed Bill of Quantities (BoQ) pricing all necessary shelter interventions and a work plan for the self upgrade works will be established for 200 beneficiary households. 2) Protection monitoring: ACTED’s protection officer will support committees in visiting regularly beneficiaries, providing them with guidance and identifying cases in needs of referral. 3) Endline report Upon the completion of the action, the M&E Units will produce a comprehensive final monitoring report the project’s effects, beneficiaries satisfaction, as well as lessons learned and recommendation in terms of response. This report will be shared among humanitarian actors, in particular OCHA and the Shelter Cluster. This will aim at contributing to the improvement of the TSCA knowledge base.4) Complaint Response Mechanism: The intervention will be supported by a Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) compliant CRM accessible to beneficiaries and non beneficiaries through several channels, including a phone hotline. The CRM will ensure that all complaints are recorded, responded to and handled according to their sensitivity. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on its function, process and usage, and will be encouraged to rely on it without fear of any adverse consequences.
