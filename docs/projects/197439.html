Project Summary

HIHFAD seeks to continue integrating nutrition-related activities into the healthcare facilities it is currently operating through screening all children under 5 years and PLW who visit the clinics for nutrition services or other services (RH, or IMCI clinics, etc.), select beneficiaries through MUAC screening and/or Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score), provide SAM treatment for children with complicated SAM using therapeutic milk (for use exclusively in hospitalized patients) f75, F100, and RTUF beside clinical management of common complications, provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated SAM using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW with MAM using Plumpy sup, provide micronutrient supplementation to pregnant women and children, Provide one-on-one, and one to group counseling on WHO-recommended (IYCF) and maternal nutrition practices, disseminate IYCF messages.As well as HIHFAD will conduct training to build the capacity of frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19. HIHFAD will receive the needed nutrition supplies for this project from UNICEF through PAC. Targeting 77638 (IDPs 62110, Host communities 15528) including PLWs women of reproductive age 23890: Female (15-17) 1935 Female (18-19) 430, Female (19-49) 21525, children under five 53748: Male 25799, female 27949. approximately 3.7% (2872) of GAM cases were children with disabilities according to the 2022 SMART survey. The project will provide the appropriate nutrition services to all age and sex groups; boys and girls, women, and men, with attention to people with special needs, without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities while ensuring that inclusion is mainstreamed throughout these activities to avoid exclusion of the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities targeting IDPs, Host communities including PLWs, children under 5, caregivers and men, the number of targeted children under 5 years is 53748 and 23890 PLWs.
Project data is collected on a monthly basis from the field, and reviewed by technical and administrative staff to ensure quality control, and adherence to the project proposal and sent as 4Ws for Nutrition Cluster on monthly basis, also HIHFAD follows the financial and administrative guidelines for financial control.
HIHFAD's M&E team will monitor these project activities based on the agreed log-frame, A detailed monitoring plan will be prepared according to the final version of the log-frame, HiHFAD will ensure its MEAL plan to be implemented fully compliant with ‘Do No Harm’ principles. The MEAL team will be guided and equipped with protection-sensitive interview skills and approaches in order not to damage the dignity of the beneficiaries. The MEAL plan will apply the stratified sampling method in order to obtain sex and gender-segregated data, ensuring the equal participation of women and youth who are quite often under-represented in Syria response. Gender and age-appropriate survey tools and methods will be developed and the MEAL team will ensure appropriate gender balance among its staff, ensuring its access to both gender groups. Throughout the project, the beneficiaries are encouraged to provide their negative or honest feedback in order to improve the project performance as well as to collect lessons learned for future planning and their feedback will be incorporated during the review exercises.
The monitoring and evaluation team will implement the following plan:
1- Field visits: to observe the overall progress of the project and compare the work plan with exact implementation.
2- Monthly reports: To check programmatic/financial progress, identify obstacles
3- Patient Exit Interviews via patients, parents, and companion feedback. (Monthly)
4. Receiving and reviewing all complaints with the project focal point through the WhatsApp complaint line and the complaints box

3 - Needs

NWSyria showed a chronic malnutrition rate was found at 24.5% (20.1- 29.5 95% C.I). Thus,220,000 children under5 in Northwest Syria are stunted increasing the risk of death, disability, and poor cognition and educational performance and the combined GAM rate showed a prevalence of 5.0% (3.3 - 7.5 95% C.I) with a combined SAM rate of 1.7% (0.6 – 4.4 95% C.I). The prevalence of Anemia among children 6 – 59 months was found at 36.18% (32.12 – 40.24 95% C.I), This is classified as serious according to the global nutrition classification of severity ranking criteria., with 40% being classified as critical.Approximately 50.000 children will suffer from severe wasting in 2023.1 out of 10 (11%) PLW (140,000) suffer from acute malnutrition. The prevalence of Anemia among Women of the Reproduction Age in NWS is classified as “catastrophic”. the anemia rate was higher among pregnant women 58.59%. For the IYCF, the results showed a clear deterioration in the complementary feeding indicators. Only 56.26 (42.22 – 70.28 95% C.I) i.e.,1 in 2 infants under 6 is exclusively breastfed and at risk of compromised growth and development. Diarrhea is a leading cause of malnutrition.1 in 2 (51%) children with malnutrition suffer from AWD. The ongoing cholera outbreak in NWS compounds the severity of severe wasting and it extremely detrimental to the nutrition status of young children.In conclusion, the overall nutrition status in NWS continues to be concerning, SMART Survey 2022

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Nutrition is a human right and underpins success in education, poverty alleviation,and the empowerment of women and girls.Some women and adolescent girls,especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated,such as divorced women and widows,may not be able to access basic services. Likelihood: Medium,Impact:High Mitigation measure: more female CHWs will be allocated to provide services at the community level and will access all beneficiaries especially new IDPs to services and refer cases to receive appropriate and specialized care, in this project female staff is about 85%, that increases access of women and girls to health services. Risk related to GBV on CHWs,Staff during outreach visits, and IDPs sites, also risks of GBV on beneficiaries and health and nutrition staff in health facilitiesLikelihood: Medium, Impact: High, Mitigation measure: each two CHWs will work together,training of health staff on providing first-line support, identification of GBV cases,safe referrals,and ensuring access of the affected population to the Minimum Initial Service Package for reproductive health, identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services,Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers of women and girls,Likelihood:Medium,Impact:High,Mitigation measure: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, including victim assistance, trainings.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Prior the project implementation, staff will undergo training on safeguarding aspects including GBV, PSEA, Child protection to mitigate the risk when delivering the nutrition assistance. To ensure that accountability mechanisms are based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy, at the beginning of the project implementation, MEAL team will conduct an accountability assessment in the project locations including the communication channels of feedback and complaints and information and identify their priority information needs, paying particular attention to the diversity of people including vulnerable groups (women, girls, people with disabilities, children,…,etc.) to design the accountability plan accordingly.In addition, HiHFAD staff has been trained on gender-specific intervention protocols in health care (GBV) as well as on response protocols for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and psychological first aid. If concerns are identified during home visits, information is forwarded to a contact point for further processing.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Attacks on the civilian population and health infrastructure, which may result in the destruction of the facility or the death of the project staff/Management: Review and adjust security measures on a daily basis Participation in the UN de-confection mechanism in order to prevent bombing by international actors. Emphasis on neutrality, independence, and adherence to humanitarian principles and IHL 2-Intensification of the conflict, leading to delays in implementation,Possibility:Low/Management: Temporary suspension of project activities and minimization of staff movement Adjustment of time and the activity plan 3-Drastic changes in the context of the Syrian conflict,such as extensive changes in the front lines, leading to mass expulsions and/or access restrictions Possibility: Low/ Management: Provision of emergency plans e.g. through the deployment of personnel in mobile clinics in the event of a sudden IDP influx - Constant and accurate context analysis and situation assessment by the security focal point of the project partner HIHFAD. 4-Potential Cholera outbreak,:Medium,Management/mitigation:WHO established TWG that will be active on 13-Sep and HiHFAD is part of it, all recommendations will be followed, Additional messaging will be added to the messaging package delivered by CNWs 5-nonrenewal cross border resolution risk mitigation : make sure of availability of stocks ,Coordination with INGOs and Turkish authorities for alternative mechanism of supplies delivery
