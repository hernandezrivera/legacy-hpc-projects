Project Summary

The project will strengthen the protection system, inclusive child protection, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and general protection through technical capacity building of communities, partners and government agencies to prevent and respond to violence against girls, boys and their families/caregivers, empowering children and their carers with improved access to quality services to restore their wellbeing.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The conflict in Cabo Delgado has displaced over 700,000 people, half of whom are children, many experiencing repeated displacements. This situation has significantly increased the vulnerability of women and children to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), disrupted education, and left them with limited options for safety and survival. Thousands of girls have been abducted by armed groups, while boys have been recruited as child soldiers. Girls subjected to sexual violence, including those who became pregnant, often face rejection by their families and communities, leading to early, exploitative marriages as a survival strategy. UNICEF (2021) reports that 61% of girls in Cabo Delgado marry or live with a partner before 18. A 2023 Save the Children study revealed that 74% of displaced people in Macomia district live in constant fear. Urgent protection and support are needed to break cycles of violence, abuse, and exploitation.Over the past two years, grave violations against children, such as killing, maiming, recruitment, abduction, sexual violence, and attacks on hospitals and schools, have risen sharply. Non-state armed groups (NSAGs) are identified as the primary perpetrators.General ProtectionSave the Children will undertake a comprehensive needs assessment and GBV safety audit to identify risks and vulnerabilities among community members, including children. Strengthening referral mechanisms in collaboration with other protection agencies will ensure access to essential protection services and information. In partnership with the Ministry of Information and the Ministry of Justice, Save the Children will facilitate mobile brigades to provide civil documentation for adults and birth registration for children in targeted districts.Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) interventions will include psychological first aid, individual counseling, group activities such as positive parenting sessions (Levels 2 & 3), and specialized support (Level 4). Community mechanisms will be strengthened through awareness-raising, training on protection risks, and social cohesion initiatives to foster community-based protection mechanisms.Save the Children will also focus on capacity building and advocacy for local authorities, humanitarian actors, and stakeholders to promote a sustainable, community-led response to protection needs.Child ProtectionSave the Children, in collaboration with humanitarian actors and government agencies, will enhance children's access to safe, inclusive, and gender-responsive child protection services. Case management and referral services will be provided for the most vulnerable children, including family tracing and reunification (FTR) and interim or foster care for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC).Structured, age, and gender-responsive MHPSS services (Levels 2 & 3) will be delivered to UASC and other at-risk children, their families, and communities through home-based care and child-friendly spaces (CFS). Specialized psychological support (Level 4) will be provided by psychologists.Support for Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG) will be prioritized, along with unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) for families of at-risk children to address economic drivers of negative coping mechanisms and reduce barriers to accessing protection services.Community-based child protection mechanisms will be strengthened in collaboration with local structures and community members, particularly through partnerships with Social Action. Advocacy efforts will focus on reducing violence against children, with particular attention to child marriages linked to the conflict. Monitoring child rights violations will be central to these efforts.Save the Children will enhance the capacity of civil society organizations (CSOs) to advocate against child early and forced marriages and unions (CEFMU) while empowering adolescents. 5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will ensure that the most vulnerable boys and girls, women and men will be identified and reached. Guided by Save the Children’s Gender Equality Policy and leveraging the Global IASC standards for gender equality, the project will conduct gender assessments in the communities using the gender equality marker. Gender safety audits will enable the identification of deep-rooted gender inequalities including harmful traditional practices to girls, boys, women and men. By using participatory community appraisals and risk mapping exercises, Save the Children will empower the community to recognize harmful traditional practices like child marriage, sex preference among others, that are a barrier to gender equality.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will work with relevant protection agencies including the UN, Protection Cluster, CPAoR, PSEA network and OCHA, and the government to build the capacity of girls, women and the elderly through leadership and equality training, mentorship and empowerment programme. Additionally, Save the Children will also work with boys and men champions to create awareness on gender equality and its positive impact on families and the community. The project will develop and utilize standardized protection and PSEA tools, e.g. code of conduct, SOPs, checklists, and case referral pathways including information, education and communication materials (IEC) and behavioral change and communication (BCC) materials that will enable all sectors and programmes to identify and mitigate risks to women, children and the elderly in the project locations. Specifically, it will allow staff, partners and community-based protection structures to safeguard women and children from the risk of GBV, including the risk of SEA and other forms of abuse and harm, such as physical abuse, neglect and emotional harm. The protection tools will support safe programming and a safe environment for women and children, including those who have disabilities to speak out.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure access to services is guided and based on a vulnerability assessment that is inclusive and gender-sensitive considering age and ability. Assessments, registration and inclusion criteria will be guided by the Washington Group Questions ensuring persons with disability are consulted and included in the project. Save the Children will collaborate with DPOs including FAMOD, AIFO and UN family for strategic planning, advocacy and implementation of activities. Save the Children will work with DPOs to ensure the capacity strengthening of partners, communities and government on disability and inclusion standards, tools and approaches and general child protection. A mentorship and coaching approach will be employed for quality assurance, reaching higher numbers of children with disabilities (at least 5% of the total target) improving accessibility and responding to needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project is not expected to have major impacts on the environment. To address any unintended impacts of the project on the environment, project staff will be encouraged to prioritize the use of nature-based solutions. The project will also apply a climate change lens to the choice and design of all activities, mitigating the impact of activities through environmental risk screenings. Save the Children's environment and social sustainability screening tool will be adopted by the project.
