Project Summary

South Sudan continues to grapple with food insecurity crisis; between October and November 2020, 34 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Greater Upper Nile region had 20 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), Greater Bahr el Ghazal region had 10, and Greater Equatoria region had 4 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Of the remaining counties, 51 were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), and 12 were classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2). In the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, 22 counties were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), with the number likely to increase to 33 counties in the second projection period of April to July 2021.
In Northern Bahr el Ghazal state, Aweil south county was projected to further deteriorate to IPC phase 5 with an estimated population of about 13,849 people likely to face famine between April and August 2021.
The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests (including the desert locust that affected parts of East Africa), the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods.
The continuing currency depreciation and associated impacts on commodity prices and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets to purchase food and other basic needs. Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020 (with limited recovery from the previous floods and Aweil South localised cyclical foods from River Wathmouk that bursts its banks ), and the much lower than required humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season.
In line with the FSL cluster priorities, Help restore Youth (HeRY) is proposing an integrated emergency food security and livelihoods intervention in Aweil south county covering all the 8 payams; Tiaraliet, Gakrol, Tarweng, Panthou, Nyieth, Watmouk, Ayai and Nyocawany.
The project targets 8,079 HHs with interventions including; distribution of emergency livelihood kits; vegetable, crop, fishing kits and farming tools intended to stimulate food production to save lives while building resilience.
Agricultural production shall be integrated with nutritional education focusing on food preparation and utilisation demonstrations to support households improve nutrition especially for expectant, lactating mothers and children under five years of age.

1 - Needs

In the assessment period of October to November 2020, an estimated 6.35 millionpeople (52.6% of the population) in South Sudan faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.102 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. An estimated 24,000 people were classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, in Pibor County (11,000) in Pibor Administration Area, and in Tonj North County (13,000) in Warrap State. The most food insecure states are Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal where more than 50% of their respective populations are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. The situation was projected to further deteriorate between December 2020 and March 2021, where an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with about 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County in Pibor Administrative Area. In the April to July 2021 projection, an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 31,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo County (11,000) in Jonglei State, Aweil South County 13849 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, and Tonj North County (13,000) in Warrap State. During this period, an estimated 2.47 million are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. (http://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1153003/?iso3=SSD) In Northern Barh El Ghazal state, an estimated 481,000 people (52.7% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 330,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 151,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in the assessment period of October to November 2020. During this period, all counties were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), except for Aweil South County which was classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). In the projection period of April to July 2021, Aweil South County is projected to progress to IPC phase 5 with about 13849 persons likely to face famine. The key drivers of food insecurity include; restrictions put in place to reduce the spread of COVID-19 which continue to negatively impact movement of goods and people to and from the State, crops were affected by pests (including the desert locusts that affected parts of East Africa) as well as climatic shocks including a long dry spell and subsequent flooding that also inundated shelters and displaced households. Decreased road access also continues to access to markets and services. Aweil South County is also classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 and it is projected to further deteriorate in the lean season of April to August 2021. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include; high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%). Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in the county also contribute to acute malnutrition. In line with cluster priorities, HeRY proposes to support Agricultural food production (own consumption & sale) and access to food; targeting vulnerable households including women, girls, men & boys, elderly, PWDs, children U5, HIV & chronically sick persons and contribute in reduction in post-harvest losses. FSL HNO 2021 ranking puts Aweil South affected population at and in Phase 5. In this proposal, HeRY is targeting 100% of the population under IPC Phase 4 and 5. Our focus areas will be aligned with CO2: Targeting 100% of vulnerable HHs. These beneficiaries will be supported with livelihood kits, fishing kits and capacity building of contact farmers in appropriate agronomic practices in Aweil South, NBeG state.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

A range of avenues for information sharing on PSEA shall include during inception meetings and throughout the project. General information shall be shared during all community engagements such as sensitizations, committee meetings among others. Project support structures including the project management committees (PMCs), AAPs and local government structures including the local police force shall undergo training in PSEA aimed at increasing awareness and enhancing buy in. Given the sensitive nature of this topic community dialogues shall be held to enable in-depth engagement on the issue. The media via use of talk shows and IEC pictorials on PSEA shall be used for wide dissemination. Referral pathways shall be made known to all shared and linkages to support organisations and services shared

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

From inception of the project HeRY will undertake mass community education on the feedback pathways of the project. HeRY will support formation and establishment of representative structures such as AAP committees at boma level. These and the project Management committees will be educated on the reception, transmission and sharing of feedback from and to the most vulnerable. HeRY will push for and ensure representation of vulnerable members (women, PWDs, Youth among others) in at least 50% of the project management and AAP committees. Local government structures and leadership structures will also undergo pre-implementation trainings aimed promoting the case for recognition and inclusion of the marginalised groups.Standard practices such as confidentiality shall be adhered to

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HeRY will largely on a variety of avenues to raise awareness to the beneficiaries about the project, feedback mechanism and or complaint mechanism. These will be done during community level sensitisation meetings (such as pre distribution address) and engagements with local leaders, through the local media and state level government structures. The project support structures such as AAP and PMCs shall also be tasked to share this information as one of their performance indicators.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Avenues/pathways for beneficiary feedback include community and sensitisation meetings, monitoring reports, complaints desks, AAP structures, local government structures and dedicated hotlines among others. Formal avenues and channels for reporting and providing feedback on project performance shall be made known to the beneficiaries. Overall complaints and response procedures shall be made known to all in which aspects such as: • To whom should the complaint be forwarded? • Guidance on distinguishing and sorting of sensitive complaints versus non-sensitive complaints, e.g. ‘sensitive’ could include sexual exploitation and abuse. ‘Non-sensitive’ could include late delivery of non-food items. • Which level of management needs to be informed and when? • What is the timeframe for responding to complaints? • Who will communicate the response to the complainant and how? Biweekly and Monthly reports on all related feedback received from the beneficiaries shall be documented with details on present status and corrective actions taken. A critical review of this made will be undertaken taken at management level to ensure all related adjustments are made in the project going forward.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

HeRY has a risk log that is updated from time to time. The project and program teams proactively identify, assess, measure, mitigate and report on any risk exposures that are likely to affect the project results and continuously mitigate and monitor them especially on the most vulnerable groups such as women, men, girls, boys, elderly and persons with disabilities.During agricultural inputs registration and distribution; HeRY uses the FAO/WFP beneficiary guide to share the targeting with involvement of all representative groups such as women, men, girls, boys, elderly and persons with disabilities; they are provided information on the eligibility criteria in the project; the communities themselves pre-select the beneficiaries with the help of local leadership and HeRY only participates in validating this information. Where pre-selection guide is not followed, HeRY will engage with all the priority stakeholders for a joint amicable solution. All the agricultural service provision, HeRY is aware aspects of GBV and child exploitation are likely to materialize in the most dominated patriarch communities we have worked in. Our Staff code of conduct and PSEA documents clearly direct HeRY to advocate in eliminating these vices; our staff and service providers we engage sign commitment for a strict zero tolerance to GBV/PSEA risks and are expected to share this information to all stakeholders. In the course of our programming, where these risks materialize, we offer referral services for survivors within the inter-cluster network especially by working with protection specialists to address the concerns while upholding confidentiality in the entire process; any data obtained during the course of protection service will only be privy to designated protection teams and survivor identity is protected and HeRY will also monitor GBV/other protection risks by regularly undertaking safety audit and also protection tip sheet to proactively identify and create awareness on the negative on the human rights and dignity of the most vulnerable in our project intervention areas. HeRY is also aware some of the protection risks emerge as a result of lack of information and involvement of priority vulnerable persons like [women, girls, PWDs, and Youths]; in this regard, this project has already provided for inclusion of all representative groups of the most vulnerable persons in being part of the project implementation team; in community group leadership and information sharing through help desks, meetings, peer to peer talk, pictorial awareness information on GBV/PSEA related risks during critical service delivery points. This will minimize vulnerabilities in information access and decision making within the project and thus improve overall outcomes of the ongoing interventions. At project inception/initiation meetings, HeRY will establish communicate all the project information to all stakeholders [women, girls, boys, men, PWDs and Elderly] and every gender group will have leadership representation. This approach will eliminate risks of leaving out some vulnerable groups and improve active participation by all. Accountability by HeRY to the beneficiaries and the beneficiairies to HeRY will be improved through active participation and involving of priority vulnerable persons who are always left out in programming and service delivery . In addition, land is a critical resource for productive food security and livelihood activities, so with the help of local leadership [paramount chiefs, village/boma/county administrators, executive directors and RRC], HeRY will influence access to agricultural and settlement land for especially returnees, and IDPs and encourage local leadership to peacefully arbitrate in any land disputes for harmonious co-existance and above all for the vulnerable project beneficiaries to contribute to their own food and livelihood baskets.For explosives when detected, HeRY will report to local authorities for clearance
