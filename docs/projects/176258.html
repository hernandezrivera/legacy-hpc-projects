Project Summary

Through the effective management of South Sudan's S-NFI core pipeline, IOM will strengthen the S-NFI cluster's capacity to respond to protracted and emerging humanitarian needs in locations with the greatest needs. The complex operating environment indicates that the provision of in-kind supplies will continue to constitute a key response modality for lifesaving responses in areas affected by emergencies, such as sub-national violence and the heavy seasonal flooding of the past two years. Under the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, IOM will continue its commitment to support the S-NFI Cluster Pipeline through the provision of key shelter and NFI supplies to benefit a total of 819,000 (75% of the total target of 1,085,000) people in need. Procured supplies will be prepositioned in various logistic hubs throughout the country. The distribution will be prioritized to directly benefit vulnerable people affected by displacement, conflict, natural disaster, disease outbreak, famine/food insecurity, and GBV risks.

1 - Needs

Despite gains in the peace process, South Sudan is still facing devastating human and financial costs due to its complex, protracted crisis. The country remains a complex humanitarian operating environment, with deteriorating infrastructure, the cumulative effect of long and heavy rainy seasons, and spurts of recurrent sub-national, localized violence in various parts of the country, in addition to the COVID-19 pandemic and other disease outbreaks endemic to the country, such as malaria. In 2021, according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview of South Sudan, the need for emergency shelter and non-food items will affect the living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 2.1 million people. The lack of sufficient shelters and weak social support networks makes women and children especially vulnerable. Their health, wellbeing, safety, and security are at increased risk. Although the number of IDPs in the protection of civilian (PoC) sites and collective centres reduced in 2020, the number of people in need increased by 100,000 compared to the previous year due to an increase in the number of returnees. It is estimated that more than 70 percent of newly displaced people outside PoC sites and collective centres are unable to meet their basic household needs and will require humanitarian interventions. More than 50 percent of returnees also need emergency shelter and non-food item (S-NFI) support. Newly displaced populations and those in protracted displacement will require timely and effective access to lifesaving S-NFI assistance. In addition, heavy rains have caused a massive scale of flooding across most states in the country, which has exacerbated the demand for humanitarian assistance, with shelter materials as one of the most critical needs of the affected population. IOM manages 100% of the SNFI core pipeline and supports the SNFI Cluster in providing partner organizations with rapid access to lifesaving materials to meet the population's emergency needs. The most vulnerable and underserved populations will be targeted, specifically, those displaced (including protracted and new displacements, inside and outside of PoCs), at risk of GBV, and those affected by environmental disasters. Depending on the local context and severity of needs, the Shelter-NFI Cluster and pipeline teams will maintain a flexible approach to responses. The contents of packages provided to beneficiaries are determined through assessments of the needs of target communities and will include prioritized key lifesaving items. IOM's extensive experience in pipeline management has built its capacity and expertise to meet partner needs in a complex environment characterized by major logistical and accessibility challenges, including surges of insecurity and a lack of basic infrastructure. South Sudan has a very poor road network with less than 100 kilometers of paved road. Most markets have collapsed as a result of the protracted conflict, and the affected population cannot access the most basic of needs. About 70 percent of the country's road network becomes inaccessible during the rainy season, which has increasingly lengthened from July to December for the past two years. In general, airstrips within South Sudan are of poor quality, with less than 10 airstrips used continuously in the wet and dry seasons. During the dry season, insecurity along the main supply routes is a significant challenge restricting movement and increasing transportation costs due to a high-risk operational environment for transporters. Transportation by road where/when possible remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. The preposition of supplies in key-hubs locations across the country is important as part of SNFI Cluster's crucial effort to ensure swift access to emergency relief items in a coordinated approach.

2 - Project activities

IOM core pipeline will provide critical S-NFI supplies to implementing partners so that the most vulnerable have improved access to emergency shelters and lifesaving non-food items. The following activities are proposed: - Procure emergency S-NFI supplies for the humanitarian response following assessed needs, targeting 933,000 individuals- Administer and process partners' approved request for distribution- Coordinate with the S-NFI cluster, partners, Logistics cluster, state focal points, and all other stakeholders relevant for pipeline operations- Transport procured SNFI pipeline supplies to six IOM logistic hubs outside Juba (Kajo-keji, Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek) and three partner warehouses across the country for prepositioning of stocks- Transport approved SNFI supplies to partner response locations. Appropriate storage and management of the shelter and NFI emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu- Regular monitoring of SNFI core pipeline supply delivery, supplies quality control, storage management, and supplies utilization- Conduct post-distribution monitoring of select SNFI partner response to gather beneficiary feedback on the quality of the items distributed, assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of the response, and assess mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues- Conduct pipeline survey with partners for the efficiency, appropriateness, and coverage of the pipeline service The activities are in line with: 1. HRP 2021 Strategic Objective 2 and Strategic Objective 3 2. HRP 2021 Shelter and NFI Cluster Specific Objectives 2.2, 2.3, 3.1, 3.33. HRP 2021 Sectoral Objectives 1, 2, 3, and 4.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

All S-NFI approved requests from partners go through a rigorous review process by the SNFI cluster and its State Focal Points, through which assessment reports and response/project documents are required for approval. The review considers Accountability to Affected Populations and gauges how partner organizations target beneficiaries with priority given to the population's most vulnerable segments. The SNFI cluster also has a S/NFI Needs Assessment toolkit and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) toolkit that partners use as a guideline in implementing their humanitarian responses. This includes tools and templates that categorically incorporate questions on inclusion, participation, and protection to measure the effectiveness of the response in addressing the affected population's needs. Findings and recommendations are given to ensure that issues are raised and addressed for future response. IOM has a zero-tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IOM staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IOM Contractors. Extensive training and workshop on protection against SEA have been rolled out, and all IOM Core Pipeline staff has undergone at least one training in the past year. IOM Core Pipeline also requires its SNFI partners to sign a memorandum of understanding to promote and strengthen accountability. This agreement includes clauses on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse that partners are also obligated to strictly enforce since they directly interact and deal with beneficiaries In any humanitarian emergency, the partner agencies conduct an inclusive consultation process with the community leaders and the representatives of all groups within the community to assess the affected population's needs. Planning of the response starts here wherein the location of distribution centres and areas of assistance and services are discussed and agreed upon so that not only community leaders decide on important matters. Access and security of beneficiaries are the most important factors that are considered. Community members also take ownership of the process, and tasks such as off-loading, guarding during the distribution, enumeration, and such are given to qualified community members. During the actual response, security is also monitored by the response team. PDM surveys have questions directly related to beneficiaries' safety during and after distribution, and results are taken into consideration so that the principle of "Do No Harm" is continuously ensured across all contexts of humanitarian response.
