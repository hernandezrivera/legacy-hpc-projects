Project Summary

By the end of the 2021-2022 academic year, it is estimated that 6.9 million school-aged children 6-18 years old are out of school, which is 35 percent of the total school-aged population. Of the 12.4 million children who are able to attend school, their learning is severely hampered by poor school infrastructure, teacher strikes, and lack of teaching and learning materials. As indicated in the Sudan Education Cluster’s Joint Education Needs Assessment of 2021, the most common factors preventing children from fulfilling their right to education include financial barriers such as school-related fees and negative economic coping mechanisms such as child labour or child marriage. Other factors include long distances to school in areas without affordable transportation options or without fuel for transport and an insufficient number of schools to accommodate all children, particularly in areas affected by conflict (most notably in the Darfur and Kordofan regions) in which many schools have been damaged. To ensure the most vulnerable children in Darfur have access to quality education, there is an urgent need for emergency interventions such as temporary learning spaces to accommodate displaced, nomadic, and previously out-of-school children and to reduce class sizes, as well as interventions that provide children with access to nutritious food and safe drinking water at school. These emergency interventions should be complemented by longer-term demand and supply interventions, such as the construction of new classrooms, investments in teacher training and strengthening of the education system, and alternative learning modalities including e-learning and distance learning to support the hardest-to-reach and most vulnerable girls and boys to fulfil their potential in an increasingly complex and digital world. The population groups most in need of humanitarian assistance to continue or resume their education include IDPs, refugees and returnees, host community members facing severe food insecurity, nomadic populations, adolescents and youth, children with disabilities, and, in some communities, girls. Conflict-affected girls and boys need psycho-social support, a safe, protective, and stable learning environment, and the normality of a school routine. However, due to large influxes of IDPs and frequent displacements in some localities, many of the learning spaces are overcrowded and are not adequately functioning. South Darfur State is facing main challenges in child protection and education issues therefore Trust Organization is proposing a project to address this challenge, and there was a humanitarian effort have been made targeting mainly displaced persons and people home affected by floods. However, major gaps still remain in the provision of basic services, Education and protection (CP) It is in this context that TPDO proposed the use of an integrated intervention aimed at maximizing impact while targeting vulnerable communities with essential service provision within the sectors of education related interventions, protection initiatives, the project interventions will enable TPDO to achieve the key objective of enhancing the accessibility to essential services through integrated interventions targeting vulnerable communities within belil, Nyala Shimal,and Nyala janob localities. Trust Organization will contribute to the three CP Sub -Sector objectives:
1-Acutely vulnerable children and their caregivers are outreached, and their needs addressed through specialized child protection interventions including multi-sectoral case management services
2- Child protection risk factors are addressed and protective factors that bolster the well-being of children, families, and communities are promoted using primary and secondary prevention approaches
3- Improved well-being, resilience, and protective environment of girls and boys through the provision of psychosocial support, life skills.

1 - Needs

Educational needs are projected to remain high throughout all of 2023, with a slight peak in September-October due to the annual floods which directly impact schools and schoolchildren at the beginning of the new academic year. And beside child protection, up to 60 per cent of the population in need of protection services in Sudan are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats of recruitment, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence. About 3.5 million children (51 per cent female, 49 per cent male), including 15 per cent of children with disabilities, need immediate child protection services. In 2023, 361,080 million children are in extreme need (10 per cent), about 2,061,920 in severe need (58 per cent), and about 939,650 in stress (27 per cent) and require humanitarian child protection assistance. Children with disabilities are among the most vulnerable. In South Darfur state specifically in belil, Nyala Janob, and Nyala Shimal localities, and According to an interagency assessment Led by OCHA, (within the above-mentioned locations) in 1 September 2022, for Nyala Shimal IDPs Camp( Otash , Dreige ), and they identified the needs for protection and education therefore The proposed intervention will fall in line with the strategy allocation paper's goal of providing lifesaving interventions to provide vulnerable communities with a safe environment under which they can live in. Further, the focus on adopting an integrated approach throughout the project is aimed at ensuring comprehensive coverage and delivery of essential services to vulnerable community’s children within belil,Nyala Janob, and Nyala Shimal, localities that has been earmarked under the 2nd allocation by SHF. With a lack of existing education infrastructure within the target localities, majority of the children are missing out on essential basic education and remain exposed to vulnerable outcomes including child labour, recruitment into armed conflict as well as physical and sexual violence. it is against this backdrop that the proposed intervention within the education sector of constructing classrooms as well as equipping and providing other essential support services will go a long way into reversing this trend. This intervention will also contribute towards SHF's goal through its timing which coincides with the beginning of the school year, thereby ensuring maximum intended results through increased enrolment rates. Similarly, targeting community structures in improving safety within the communities as envisioned under the project will help address the underlying protection related challenges that face vulnerable communities living within belil, Nyala Janob, and Nyala Shimal localities. Working closely with and building their capacity to identify and provide much needed assistance to children in need of protection related services and referrals so that all can access the essential services that will be provided. In efforts to address tensions emerging between host communities and IDPs.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

TPDO will ensure that child protection and education mainstreaming through all phases of project from the designing phase up to the implementation by applying the protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity. - Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. - Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -without any barriers. Throughout 2023, child protection needs will continue to be monitored through existing systems, including the 5Ws, Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism on Grave Child Rights Violations (MRM+), the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS+), service mapping, safety audits, protection monitoring reports, and, in the refugee context, Progress database. The CP AOR, together with UNICEF, the South Darfur Council for Child Welfare (SCCW) and partners, launched Primero - a mobile application for case management. The launch represents another key milestone in the efforts to provide accountable and professional case management and social services and it will help to ensure no child, even in the most challenging circumstances, is left behind. Quarterly reporting through the Who, What, Where, When and for Whom (5Ws) tool monitors the progress of the Education Cluster and its partners to achieve the objectives defined in the response plan. Data is disaggregated by sex, age, and disability to ensure the most vulnerable children are reached with targeted Education in Emergencies (EiE) support. Education needs are also monitored annually using the Secondary Data Review (SDR), MSNA and the JENA. The Education Cluster monitors the humanitarian situation and effectiveness of response through several feedback mechanisms and post-distribution surveys. These enable children, parents, and teachers to tell the cluster if they are satisfied with the humanitarian education interventions and how they might be improved.
