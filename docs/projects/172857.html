Project Summary

The proposed action aims to restore the access of boys and girls to quality education and a protective learning environment through capacity building of teachers, the provision of catch-up classes and remedial classes, PSS and recreational activities for children, life skills trainings, awareness-raising and sensitization sessions and the rehabilitation and repairs of damaged schools in conflict-affected areas in Derna and Benghazi. ACTED will enhance access to formal and non-formal education as well as seek to bridge the learning gaps for vulnerable school-aged children affected by protracted crisis (Sector Objective 2) and the closure schools due to the ongoing COVID-19 outbreak by providing catch-up and remedial classes for vulnerable children in both cities. These classes will address the priority needs of vulnerable children who are out of school, who are enrolled in school but have fallen behind because of the conflict or COVID-19, from displaced families, as well from disadvantaged groups (girls, children with disabilities and additional needs, associated with armed groups, from minorities, from poorer socio-economic groups, etc.). ACTED will also design and deliver life-skills training programs specifically in Benghazi which will focus on delivering essential skills trainings.
ACTED will also aim to address the physical and infrastructural barriers to safe and protective learning environments through the rehabilitation and repairs of educational facilities in Derna. The rehabilitation of the selected schools aims to create a safe and inclusive leaning spaces for children, teachers and school’s personnel of all backgrounds and abilities. The works will consist in providing cleaning materials and rehabilitation works and repair that address specific physical and infrastructural barriers, as well as protection barriers related to accessing the educational facilities. Also under Sector Objective 1, ACTED will engage with local communities to enhance access to education in Derna and Benghazi by conducting awareness-raising sessions for parents and caregivers and children on key issues pertaining to education, to COVID-19 and safe return to school, as well as to support building acceptance of the project and participation of beneficiaries. Awareness-raising sessions will also focus on topics related to children enrolment in schools and the importance of prioritizing children’s education.
To strengthen the capacity of teachers, education personnel and sector members to deliver a timely and coordinated response (Sector Objective 3), ACTED will conduct capacity-building of teachers and school personnel on education, PSS and classroom management skills in Derna. The training topics will notably cover: child development and well-being (including PSS), teacher’s role and well-being, pedagogy, curriculum and planning and engaging with parents. These trainings will help strengthen teacher’s resilience and help them better engage with parents and children. A focus will be provided on lesson planning and remote learning delivery as a result of COVID-19. ACTED will also address the question of caregivers’ wellbeing and the impact of teachers’ emotional and psychosocial condition on the effective provision of education to children in Derna. In particular, ACTED will deliver dedicated sessions on stress management to cope with teachers that are experiencing high levels of stress as a result of COVID-19 and the conflict’s impact on their personal wellbeing and their work environment.
ACTED will also conduct psychosocial support and recreational activities (Sector Objective 2) in Derna, responding to the children’s wellbeing needs through an integrated approach combining education and protection interventions in schools. The activities will also seek the participation of parents and community members to contribute to reinforce family and community support systems and foster an enabling and supportive environment for young people

1 - Needs

The scale and intensity of the protracted humanitarian crisis continues to increase in Libya, substantially affecting the well-being and mental health of children exposed to severe protection risks. Although there has been a return to formal education following the end of the conflict in Derna and in Benghazi in Eastern Libya, the low quality of public education, the lack of recreational activities, and limited availability of psycho-social support have compounded the problem and hindered the development of conflict-affected children. These conditions are exacerbated by the ongoing COVID-19 outbreak, which reached Libya in March 2020 and lead to far-reaching containment measures, leading to a complete disruption to education due to the temporary closure of schools. The 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview reports that 35% of enrolled children in Libya face challenges to consistent school attendance. It also demonstrates that 160,000 children in Libya face severe protection needs, with more than half being IDPs and 26% being refugees and migrants. IDP, refugee and migrant children continue to face barriers in accessing basic services such as education due to lack of documents, discrimination and language barriers. In Benghazi, ACTED’s 2019 Factsheet highlights that education (20%) was reported as the top second need and while there has been limited infrastructural investment by authorities and international actors in health and education infrastructures, actual service provision remains low (reported also in Derna). Furthermore, 46% of interviewed households reported that children 6-17 years old were attending school in ACTED’s 2019 Protection Monitoring. Public schools were reported to be overcrowded with children facing risks such as violence in and out of schools, bullying, drug abuse, proliferation of small arms and travel routes to/from schools. ACTED’s EiE Needs Assessment conducted in December 2019 reveals clear gaps in education service provision as well as child protection needs in the wake of the armed conflict in Derna. A lack of qualified teaching staff, overcrowded classrooms and poor classroom management negatively affect the attainment of students. This was also highlighted during ACTED’s Education Assessment conducted between August and September 2020 along with teacher’s concerns on schools re-opening due to COVID-19. The closure of schools for eight or more months during the conflict in 2018-2019 left children in Derna behind the curriculum for their age. 22% of households had one or more school-aged child out of education, with the cost of education constituting the most important barrier to education. Both the assessments of 2019 and 2020 addressed that children with disabilities and non-Libyan children face stigma and bullying in schools. Moreover, teachers reported that children with disabilities face bullying, isolation and hostility from other students and acknowledged that teachers do not give students with disabilities specific consideration. ACTED’s EiE assessment of 2019 also reported that teachers identified that transportation for girls to schools can be a protection risk and this can lead to families preventing girls from attending school given the perceived risk of public transport to reach schools. Female students reported feeling unsafe when travelling to or from school on foot in the absence of available transport and being exposed to harassment. Safety concerns for children attending schools was also reported by HHs (14%) in the recent 2020 MSNA for both cities. Moreover, due to the current COVID-19 outbreak, the 2020 MSNA findings highlighted that 87% of households (HHs) reported that children did not have access to any kind of distance learning since the start of the outbreak, while in Benghazi 52% of HHs believe that safety and security risks have increased since the outbreak.

2 - Activities

In Derna, ACTED will provide a tailored in-service capacity-building program aligned with priority classroom needs for teachers and school personnel by providing sessions on education and classroom management skills (including remote management), on child protection and child-centered pedagogy. In Benghazi and Derna, ACTED will also provide vulnerable children, including out-of-school and displaced boys and girls with catch-up and remedial classes, seeking to bridge the learning gaps created by conflict and COVID19-related disrupted access to education. The content of the classes will be identified by the schoolteachers (Derna) or through outreach events (Benghazi) in line with the curriculum taught in schools and adapted to the age range of the participating children. ACTED will conduct awareness raising sessions with parents, caregivers and children in both cities and in cooperation with local CSOs to sensitize beneficiaries on key issues pertaining to education and child protection, COVID-19 and safe return-back to school measures as well as to support building acceptance of the project and participation of beneficiaries. They will also address topics such as, children with disabilities’ integration in formal education, gender-sensitive education, positive coping mechanisms by both parents and teachers, health, and nutrition. Access psychosocial support and recreational activities will be provided in Derna, by establishing a range of appropriate, structured, age and gender-specific recreational activities directed to vulnerable children, in which psychosocial support (PSS) group-based activities will be integrated. In Benghazi, ACTED will carry out life-skills training programs which will focus on delivering essential skills trainings. Finally, in Derna, ACTED will provide cleaning material and rehabilitate damaged schools in conflict-affected areas to address the institutional, physical and infrastructural barriers to learning environments

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

ACTED dedicated security staff continuously monitor conflict developments to anticipate access concerns and modify procedures in advance. ACTED has in place standard operating procedures designed to minimize the exposure of staff and beneficiaries to security threats. Should changes in conflict dynamics cause a deterioration of the security situation, threatening the safety of staff or beneficiaries, ACTED will potentially adapt the program if needed in coordination (whether delivering assistance through partners, proxies, or by shifting the area of activities to more secure locations). Risk 4.4: ACTED will ensure the provision of PPE materials throughout the entire implementation of project activities. ACTED staff will utilize and distribute PPE materials such as hand sanitizers and masks to all beneficiaries participating to in-person activities, such as, children, ACTED staff, community members, parents and caregivers. Furthermore, in other to mitigate the spread of the COVID-19 virus and abide to its preventive measures, ACTED will conduct small in- person group activities and ensure adequate space for social distancing between beneficiaries. Risk 4.6: Access to all sensitive specific data on beneficiaries will be strictly limited to those staff members directly involved in processing it and working copies will be deleted once no longer needed. Access to data will be limited using password-protected documents. ACTED will utilize red flag forms when dealing with more sensitive cases. ACTED will also ensure trainings to all staff team on confidentiality and data management.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED’s AME Unit will conduct Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with parents, caregivers, and community members in order to receive feedback and suggestions and improve the project activities accordingly to ensure that none of the activities are indirectly harming any of the beneficiaries. Consultations will be held in the start-up phase of the project to adapt the project design and the implementation methodology to the priorities of beneficiaries and communities. ACTED will also continuously monitor the quality and effectiveness of project deliveries including by collecting feedback from beneficiaries directly and through the complaints and response mechanism (CRM), ensuring that the proposed action remains relevant to the needs of beneficiaries throughout the project implementation. The CRM is managed by the AME staff to ensure independence and for which confidentiality is a fundamental principle. Complaints are received via a dedicated CRM hotline and are then recorded and transferred to the CRM focal point confidentially and registered in a password-protected database, categorized based on the level of sensitivity. Information is shared on a strictly need-to-know basis and restricted as much as possible. The CRM Focal Point responds to all individuals who have made a complaint to inform them of the outcome of their complaint. Within this project, ACTED will actively work to ensure that the CRM is able to be accessed by any parent or caregiver regardless of their level of literacy, to ensure that marginalized groups can access it. This served as an active line of communication for ACTED to receive any grievances from community members regarding the implementation of the project activities or any feedback and suggestions for changes. These calls therefore directly translate into recommendations to programs for improvement of programming, and the resolution of such complaints communicated directly back to the beneficiaries.
