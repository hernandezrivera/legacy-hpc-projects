Project Summary

Al-Hakeem project is aimed to ensure access of all boys and girls under five years, including children with disability, and women in 50 communities in Northwest of Syria (25 communities in Al Ma'ra district-Idlib and 25 communities in Afrin district- Aleppo) through two health facilities, one stabilization center, four mobile clinics, and 24 CHWs, to the comprehensive quality CMAM services, disseminate and support C-IYCF practices and promote health and nutrition seeking and basic hygienic behaviors within the population in need. In addition, the project is going to continue providing harmonized emergency community-based services for all children and women of the population in need by applying integrated health, nutrition, child protection, and WaSH approaches.
The total number of targeted beneficiaries is 83100(IDPs 16000, Host communities 64100, Returnees 3000), the number of targeted children under five years is 36634 and PLWs is 18000.
Gender mainstreaming in this project will be supported by tools and expertise developed to address gender equality in the specific context of the Arab world and Syrian culture conditions. During designing the project we made sure that gender issue is included in the needs assessment, activities, outcomes and even indicators, the highest priority of the project will be providing the appropriate services to all boys and girls under five years, including children with disability, women in reproductive age and PLWs, without any sexual discrimination, among the populations, IDP last resort sites, overburdened communities or areas affected by a high intensity of hostilities as well as recently displaced IDP or returnees, In addition, the host communities will be targeted.
We've already collected enough information about vulnerable groups and according to our experience in health and nutrition services provision, we always get help by volunteers from the community.
This project is intended to contribute significantly to gender equality as it follows a participatory and inclusive process at all stages of planning, implementation, and monitoring, and aims at empowering vulnerable beneficiaries to enhance resilience.
Provision of management of acute malnutrition will be ensured at the health facility and community level and in integration with IYCF services and primary health care services
The project aims to improve the life-saving WHO recommended IYCF practices.
The capacity of stakeholders such as health practitioners, nutrition staff, and community workers will be strengthened to facilitate critical one-to-one IYCF counseling support, group sessions, and community outreach activities.
Micronutrient deficiency prevention and control initiatives will be promoted.
Quality, life-saving, curative activities will focus on community- and facility-based screening for acute malnutrition among girls and boys under five and PLW, followed by referral for treatment of acutely malnourished cases with a focus on programme quality performance
indicators.

Needs

4.7 million girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant andlactating women are at risk of under-nutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services in 2019. 3.13 million girls and boys under 5 years of age require appropriate nourishment for adequate nutrition status while 1.59 million pregnant and lactating women require nutrition services. Around 91,811 girls and boys aged 6-59 months are acutely undernourished and if malnutrition is not prevented additional 146,898 children under the age of five years willbecome acutely malnourished during 2019. Nutrition survey data shows that across Syria there are 19,263 severely malnourished children who are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. If not treated appropriately half of the severely malnourished children could die due to complications related to severe acute malnutrition. Source(HNO2019). SMART surveys and nutrition surveillance in sub-districts throughoutSyria in 2018 generally shows that global acute malnutrition (GAM) is mostly at acceptable levels. However, in areas with newly displaced populations such as in Idlib and rural Aleppo, acute malnutrition of children 6-59 months rapidly increased in a space of 6 months (from January 1.5% to June 4%) in 2018. PLWs are particularly vulnerable due to their specific nutritional needs and health risks. Reporting data on nutrition screening in 2018 confirmed that MAM rates of PLWs increased by 134 percent. Lack of access to health services and negative coping mechanisms such as meal reduction are some of the causes linked to deteriorating maternal nutrition. Source(HNO2019).The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life, repeated infections such as diarrhea and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency. IYCF practices remain poor in Syria, with exclusive breastfeeding rates below pre-crisis levels at 24% in 11 governorates. Barriers to appropriate feeding of young children include breastfeeding difficulties, illness of the mother, affordability, and lack of support from family. Source(HNO2019).Coverage of IYCF services are largely awareness-raising messages rather than the needed specialized services such as one-to-one counseling to better support pregnant women and caregivers onmaternal nutrition and to safely feed their young children during the critical window of 0-24 months of age.High acute and chronic malnutrition rates might be related to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, quality health care, andWASH services. Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services including nutrition. Access may be further limited by safety concerns and/or custom and traditions that restrict women from leaving the home in order to access nutrition support. Continued conflict, displacement, shifts in control, and increasing returnees together withdepletion of livelihoods, limited financial and physical access to food,unexploded hazard contamination has exacerbated the vulnerability of the population in Syria. Combined, these factors have the potential to further jeopardize the nutrition status of girls and boys under five PLWs, increasing the overall risk of under-nutrition as well as the short and long-term consequences impacting nutrition and health status such as stunting, growth faltering, morbidity, and mortality (source HRP 2019).

Implementation of activities

SDI will implement integrated CMAM program included all pillars (community mobilization, TSFP, OTP, Inpatient care (SC)) and IYCF program for people in need in 50 communities (25 communities in Al Ma'ra district-Idlib and 25 communities in Afrin district- Aleppo) through two fixed health facilities, one stabilization center and four mobile clinics.First HF is PHC center located in Marrat Al-Numan and the second is PHC unit will be established in Mabatli sub-district.SDI coordinates with local governorate and health directorate to facilitate project implementation, get the agreement for running the mobile clinics in targeted communities, this agreement will facilitate the movement of mobile teams and CHWs.At the fixed HFs, SDI will screen all children under 5 years and PLW who visit the clinics for nutrition services or other services (RH, or IMCI clinics), select beneficiaries through MUAC screening of children under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women and Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score) screening of children under five years of age, choose the worse value, and classify SAM and MAM cases, provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW with MAM using Plumpy sup, SDI will treat complicated severe acute malnutrition according to inpatient management protocol in the stabilization center (SC).In the fixed center, the nutrition staff is one doctor, one nutritionist, and one nurse trained very well on CMAM, they will be responsible for MAM and SAM treatment according to the CMAM protocol, IYCF appropriate counseling will provide by the same staff.The four mobile clinics will work in the same activities. Each mobile clinic has one doctor, one nutritionist, one nurse, and four CHWs. Each mobile clinic will cover 12 communities, visit each targeted community biweekly (one day per week), the duration of coverage for each community is one year. 24 CHWs will provide services at the community level, visit homes and camps and will provide the following activities:1. MUAC screening for acute malnutrition of all children (6-59 months of age) and PLWs.2. Bilateral pitting edema (caused by malnutrition) screening of all children (6-59 months of age). 3. Referring identified malnourished cases to receive the treatment. (CHWs will give referral card to patients). 4. Providing appropriate IYCF counseling.5. Distributions IEC materials about IYCF and CMAM programs. 6. Distribution of micronutrient supplementation.7. Following up the SAM and MAM patients at their homes. SDI will conduct two training on CMAM and C-IYCF to 40 of non-health and health nutrition service providers.Registering and reporting:SDI will use nutrition cluster criteria and forms to register the beneficiaries, all beneficiaries will be registered; every identified malnourished case has a special record, which helps to document the periodic follow-ups.All data will be registered (gender, age, residence, nutrition situation, the number of commodities, outcome).SDI will comply with nutrition cluster reporting requirements, including:4W matrixMonthly reports on performance indicators (i.e. cured rate, death rate, default rate etc.)SDI will do 12 FUAT and supportive supervision missions (CMAM and C-IYCF) to the nutrition service facilities, and share consolidated reports with recommendations with all partners for knowledge exchange exercise.SDI will rent two warehouses, in a safe place; it will have a specific standard to save the supplies. The food will keep in a clean, dry place. In addition, will stack at least six inches from the floor and away from the wall and the ceiling.The project is effectively and efficiently will manage, document and report by a good administrative staff.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Attacks on health workers moving between camps and houses Likelihood: medium, Impact: High Mitigation measure: The CHWs will be chosen from the same-targeted communities and each two CHWs will work together, coordination with local authorities to facilitate the movement of staff and work as a team 2- Potential looting of nutrition supplies, Likelihood: Low, Impact: High MM: Stock prepositioning in multiple, safe, and close locations to delivery sites 3-Attacks on health staff and beneficiariesin the facilities, and losing follows up of beneficiaries.Likelihood: Low, Impact: HighMM: SDI will set up health facilities in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries, and the project includes 4 mobile clinics and 24 CHWs to facilitate access to services and mitigation risks of movement with the prospect to relocate health facilities in relatively safe areas. In addition; applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, continuous reporting on attacks against health, and sustained advocacy for respect of (IHL).4- Gender-inequitable access to nutrition services. Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services. Access may be further limited by safety concerns and/or custom and traditions that restrict women from leaving the home in order to access health care.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMM: more female CHWs will be trained to provide services at the community level and mobile clinics will visit the targeted communities to facilitate access to services and refer cases to receive appropriate and specialized care should beneficiaries’ access to be constrained at the health facility level In addition; promotion awareness about necessary for visiting the health care center to receive basic health care and nutrition services.5- Risk related to GBV on CHWs when visiting the homes, also risks of GBV on beneficiaries and health staff in health facilities Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High, MM: The CHWs will be chosen from the same-targeted communities and each two CHWs will work together, coordination with local councils to facilitate CHWs works, training of health staff on providing first-line support, identification of GBV cases, safe referrals, and ensuring access of the affected population to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health, including clinical management rape,Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor, and the female staff is about 80% of projects staff, that increases access of women and girls to health services 6- Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers of women and girls at the facility and community levels.Likelihood: Low, Impact: High MM: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, including victim assistance, train staff on the code of conduct, including the code of conduct of all staff contracts and address any SEA complaints.7- Risk of discrimination or if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Likelihood: Low, Impact: HighMM: Ensuring that nutrition project design encompasses accessibility of services by vulnerable groups (physical accessibility, disaggregated areas for women and girls, availability of female staff, Signboards) with effective community-based complaints mechanisms.In addition; to mitigate these risks and ensure adherence to humanitarian principles, the project adopted a “Do No Harm” strategy while providing nutrition services. SDI will regularly review risks and mitigation measures make necessary adjustments to programming modalities, and methods of implementation will depend on the dynamics on the ground

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk and assumptions:Assumption1: Regular and timely provision of specialized nutritious foods and available to targeted beneficiaries. Risk1: Access to targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure. Mitigation measure1: Stock prepositioning in multiple, safe, and close locations to delivery sites and secure enough supplies to avoid stop the treatment of patients Assumption2: Technical skills are available. Risk2: shortage in the specialized technical staff in the targeted areas. Mitigation measure2: More non-health and health workers will be trained to provide appropriate services. Assumption3: No further deterioration of the security situation. Risk3: Attacks on health facilities and health workers during moving between camps, communities, and houses. Mitigation measure3: SDI will set up CMAM centers in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries. Moving the center to another safe area to avoid the risk of bombing.
