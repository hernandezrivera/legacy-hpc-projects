Project Summary

GOAL proposes livelihoods support to 72,500 vulnerable individuals in Twic county (Warrap State), Ulang and Renk counties (Upper Nile State) and Kajo Keji County (Central Equatoria state). This intervention will leverage GOAL’s previous interventions and continue to provide sustainable and innovative early responses as well as durable solutions. In the three states, GOAL works with the FSL cluster through active participation in the bi-weekly meetings and supporting food security assessments. GOAL also leads Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNA) in the event of disasters and when called upon by deep field OCHA coordination. Nationally, GOAL participates actively in FSL meetings, HRP workshops and meetings and any food security related events. GOAL is also an active member of the Cash Working Group (CWG) and is a cooperating partner of both WFP and FAO. In these partnerships, GOAL updates its programming based on Humanitarian Response Plans and food and nutrition security assessments. GOAL also complements the efforts of UN agencies and humanitarian actors implementing cluster objective 1 to avoid duplication and to transition beneficiaries from emergency to resilience support. In all the three states proposed for response, health and nutrition will be integrated with FSL through inclusion of market-based and livelihoods interventions in the treatment and prevention of malnutrition and disease prevention. Mother to mother support groups (MTMSGs) and Male Peer Educators (MPE) will be supported to produce crops and vegetables for home consumption and for sale of surplus to improve household food and nutrition security and to generate income for meeting other household needs. This will contribute to prevention of the root causes of malnutrition and to reduce relapse of malnutrition. GOAL also proposes rehabilitation of livelihoods infrastructure and systems through conditional cash distribution. This will provide relief to households affected by floods, drought, the Sudan Crisis and other disasters in South Sudan while ensuring access to critical facilities. In Counties where fish is a major source of livelihood, GOAL will support fish production and value addition while ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of nutrition benefits of fish as well as post-harvest practices. In the event of floods and drought, access to fuel wood for cooking is not only limited but also a risk to women and girls as they travel long distances to fetch it. To reduce reliance community’s reliance on fuelwood, GOAL will pilot production and distribution of locally made energy saving stoves through capacity building of community structures to ensure durable solutions. For sustainability, GOAL will train the community members to make energy saving stoves and utilize or sell for income generation. All livelihood interventions will be done in close collaboration with state and county government especially State and County Agriculture, Environment and Forestry department.

1. Needs

According to IPC report for September 2023, an estimated 5.83 million people (46.3 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). The analysis also found out that most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61.3 percent), Unity State (57.9 percent), Upper Nile State (56.1 percent) and Lakes State (52.8 percent). The situation is worsened by the Sudan crisis that erupted in April 2023 and caused huge population displacement from Sudan to many surrounding countries. As of November 1st, over 356,000 people had entered South Sudan. These returnees were mainly from Sudan, though significant numbers have also returned from Ethiopia, accounting for approximately 65,714 South Sudanese. 14,000 of these returnees are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) and more than 82% of these are in Renk.South Sudan’s vulnerability to climate change and natural disasters compounds the country’s humanitarian crisis, jeopardizing recovery, and undermining development efforts. Since its independence in 2011, the country has suffered from severe droughts (2011, 2015) and floods (2014, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021, and 2022), resulting in high numbers of fatalities, displacements and loss of livestock which have severely impacted people’s livelihoods. In 2022 for instance, an estimated 130,000 hectares of cultivated land were damaged by floods, with an estimated loss of 65,000 tonnes of cereals (CFSAM, December 2022). Over 1 million people were reportedly affected in nine of the ten states in August to late October, mainly impacting Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap and Unity (OCHA, November 2022). Atypical rainfall from October to mid-December, combined with elevated water levels in the Nile River and its tributaries, slowed the expected recession of the floodwaters in the Sudd Wetland and White Nile River Basin (FEWS NET, December 2022). In Upper Nile State, rain resulted in floods that destroyed houses, household properties and displaced an estimated 2,800 people in Renk town alone. There is a strong linkage between the severity of acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition during the lean season when households face depleted food stocks, limited access to and reduced functionality of markets because of the rainy season, high food prices, and reduced income insecurity and lack of access to healthy diets. Preliminary results from the FSNMS Round 28 and the 2022 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) surveys show that children between 6 and 59 months suffering from acute malnutrition remain at an average of 16 per cent in the lean season, surpassing the emergency threshold of 15 per cent and thus considered very high. Three of the 4 counties targeted counties have a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate above the WHO 15% threshold (Renk= 25.1%, Ulang= 26.4% and Twic 25.3%). This is due to poor access to health and nutrition services, high levels of disease and inadequate child-feeding practices, in tandem with persistently high acute food insecurity. It is noted that vulnerable people and children leaving the health and nutrition facilities below do not have reliable access to food and nutrition and end up being readmitted to the facilities for treatment. Having established a base in Renk in January of 2023, GOAL has been responding to the crisis in Sudan which has resulted in returnees, refugees and third country nationals entering South Sudan mainly through Joda entry point and settling in Renk as they wait for transport to their onward destinations. Onward Transportation Assistance is implemented by IOM and started well in the beginning. However, after June, the influx of returnees increased and OTA has been unable to keep up with the inflow of new arrivals across the border.

2. Project activities

1. Conditional cash distribution to vulnerable returnees and host communitiesGOAL will select 2,000 households in 4 counties through community-based targeting criteria to be supported with Asset Creation and livelihoods activity. 2: Distribution of dry season agricultural kitsIn the areas targeted, a total of 1,600 households including Mother to Mother Support Groups (MTMSGs) and Male Peer Educators (MPE) will be selected and supported with vegetable kits following the seasonal calendar.3. Distribution of main season agricultural kits to 6,000 householdsThe farmers supported with vegetable kits will also be supported with crop kits at the onset of rain to improve their food security status during the lean season and reduce their negative coping strategies.4. Support to 1,500 fish farmersThe project will distribute fishing kits to vulnerable households with the objective of emergency lifesaving and livelihoods protection support. Additional canoes will be provided to 10 fishing groups to facilitate transportation and increase the fish yield by reducing time taken to fish.5. Establish/Re-establish kitchen garden sites at nutrition facilities.GOAL will strive to improve integration of food security activities in nutrition through vegetable production support to 20 nutrition facilities.6. Post distribution monitoring.GOAL will conduct post-distribution monitoring surveys in targeted counties within 2-3 weeks after distribution of inputs to understand the impact of the project and assess the appropriateness of the response, how it met the identified needs, accountability to the targeted communities, and beneficiary satisfaction with the response. It will strive to identify any risks or perceived negative consequences, as well as lessons learned and recommendations for future projects. GOAL will ensure meaningful participation of all groups, including men, women, people with disability in the PDM survey, to ensure voices are heard from all sections of the community. 7. Good agronomic practices (e.g., farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management etc.).GOAL will provide training with a focus on technical elements of production, post-harvest handling, and marketing of vegetables. In an integrated approach, the promotion of vegetable production will be complemented by a strong awareness-raising and training component to enhance the consumption of nutrient-rich vegetables and to increase dietary diversity.8. Post harvest management.To improve pests and disease management of harvests, GOAL will support vulnerable households with 8 locally made but innovative granaries, 8 general purpose oil processing equipment and grain storage materials. Post harvest survey will be conducted to understand the yield.9. Strengthening of Village Savings/Loans Associations (VSLA).VSLAs will be introduced to 30 groups of vegetable producers to enable them to save surplus income from the sale of vegetables. The timing of this intervention will be carefully planned to make profits available in the last quarter of 2024. This will support farmers to engage in post-harvest activities and buy inputs for dry season vegetable production. 10. Promoting energy saving stoves.GOAL proposes to support the production and distribution of 2,500 fuel efficient stoves. 100 fuel stoves producers will be trained through the TOT approach.
