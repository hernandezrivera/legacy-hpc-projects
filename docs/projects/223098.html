Project Summary

The proposed project aims to increase the resilience and income of fisherfolk community who are impacted by fishing ban in the Naf River, Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar. The beneficiaries will be provided skill-building trainings with supports of small grants, business management trainings, monitoring of established businesses and market linkage workshops.
The COAST has significant experience in implementing livelihoods activities in Cox’s Bazar areas for both for the refugees and host communities. The selected activities have been piloted by COAST with other funding streams and the scaling up of the activities with a specific focus on sustainability seem promising for achieving long term results for improving the self-reliance of fisherfolk population.
COAST Foundation will be the implementing organization. COAST has operation in the geographical areas and Rohingya response since very beginning of the influx. The project will increase economic productivity and income of fisherfolk women, men and youth who have been impacted on fishing ban in the Naf River. For these women, it will achieve this through:
• Strengthening the resilience of fisher communities through capacity building, knowledge transfer, and providing smart and climate-adaptive technologies for livelihood through alternative Income-Generating Activity (IGA);
• Stabling their sustainable livelihoods through financial and start-up support, creating market linkage to groups and households, and
• Empowerment of women, men and youth support for children back to school and primary healthcare through the engagement of Government and Non-Government efforts.
The project will be implemented Teknaf Upazila (sub district), Cox’s Bazar district of Bangladesh. The principal target group of the project is 1000 HHs women, men and youth aged 18-60 years 100 Self Reliant Groups (SRG).
The project will provide 400 beneficiaries on different technical training, provide livelihoods support to 300 beneficiaries of Host Communities based on gained skills and 300 beneficiaries will receive vocational skill development training from the project. The project will address the need of diverse groups of people including to promote disability inclusion, identification, participation, and input support. The project will take account of disability inclusion to demonstrate compliance with JRP cross-cutting guidelines.
The project will follow the MAAP (Mechanism of Accountability to the Affected Population) tool ensuing the Accountability to the Affected Population. Complaint Response and Feedback mechanism will be ensured. Confidential, safe and accessible reporting channel will be ensured in all project site.
The project will provide training on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) or staff and volunteers and create awareness activities, and safeguards for beneficiaries.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the Teknaf Fishery Office, there are 10,683 registered fishermen, and over 3,000 of them rely exclusively on fishing in the Naf River. Bangladesh government has restricted fishing and other movement in the Naf River since 25 August 2017, due to the Rohingya crisis and to stop drug trafficking, and it is still in force. The ban on fishing in the Naf River and the unavailability of other work due to the Rohingya influx brought the livelihoods of 3,000 fishermen to an end, and it is hardly possible for them to manage their three meals daily. Their income has reduced (even stopped), impacting food intake, child education, mental health, and nutrition. The situation has aggravated over time as the locals struggle to find casual labor works in hard competition with cheap Rohingya labor. Their situation is miserable, and urgent humanitarian assistance is required to stop the silent tears. It is noted that the total population of Cox’s Bazar is 2,823,265, along with 976,507 Rohingya refugees, as of 29 February 2024. Considering all the circumstances, Cox’s Bazar has become one of Bangladesh’s most vulnerable districts now-a-days. COAST conducted study on the profound impact of the fishing ban on the Naf River, explicitly focusing on the challenges faced by over 3,000 fishing families. The study reveals that 84% of them depend exclusively on the Naf River for their livelihoods. Only 23% of respondents acknowledged receiving assistance from the government and local authorities, while a staggering 90% reported a substantial decline in income ranging from 50%-60% since the fishing ban. (Sources: https://coastbd.org/wp-content/uploads/Reclaiming-Rights_-unreveal-the-impacts-of-the-fishing-ban-on-the-Naf-River.pdf) Notably, the absence of fisherwomen surfaced during sampling interviews, underscoring a significant gender disparity within the fishing community. Only 23% of respondents acknowledged receiving assistance from the government and local authorities, while a staggering 90% reported a substantial decline in income ranging from 50-60% since the implementation of the fishing ban. The report also sheds light on the complex issues surrounding identification cards. The study also reveals that 70% of fishermen got some day labor, driving auto-rickshaws, farming on others’ lands, initiating small enterprises, and venturing into small-scale fishing businesses. Confronted with a dwindling source of earnings, many started borrowing money with high interest to sustain their livelihoods. Some of them sold their household assets for survival. The deteriorating state of their once-valuable fishing equipment, including damaged boats and rotten nets, not only signifies the financial loss of the fishing community but also reflects the faded quality of life they might endure for an indefinite period.Besides, COAST has consulted and taken feedback from the affected community on their problems and how to overcome the situation. Due to economic constraints, the fisher communities have been suffering a lot in every aspect of their life, including food, health, and education. They mentioned that their livelihood is worse and more humanitarian than Rohingya life as they are getting regular humanitarian assistance. The 2023 J-MSNA confirmed that unemployment remains high in the host communities, particularly for youth and women. The project will complement the vulnerable Bangladeshi households in affected areas through their skills training. Institutional mechanisms and the skills of training providers will also be enhanced. Livelihood opportunities for the host communities will include bolstering access to economic opportunities, strengthening food systems, localizing production, and improving market linkages and sustainability. Source: https://rohingyaresponse.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/REACH_BGD2301_Host-community-Factsheet_2023-JMSNA_16012024_v2_opt-1.pdf

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The activity has been under pressure with a ban on fishing in the river Naf. Prohibition has put significant pressure on the livelihoods of an estimated 30,000-35,000 fishermen. More support for alternative livelihoods is required.” So, this project activity is aligned and linked with in the host community link to development initiatives.
