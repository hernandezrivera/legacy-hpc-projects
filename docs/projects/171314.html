Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to contribute to the Health Cluster Objective # 1, aligned to the HNO Strategic Objective # 2;
More precisely, the project will emphasize on the following speciﬁc objectives:
1- Promote psycho-education and public health awareness in conﬂict-affected communities about youth vulnerability with regards to self-harm, risky behaviors, and suicide.
2- Support the responding capacity of the 'Emergency SAWA Hotline 121' in oPt- for an extended period beyond the current December, 2020 timeframe- in detecting, supporting, and referring youths presenting self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide vulnerability
3- Strengthen Mobile Clinics (MCs) capacity in Area C& EJ (Health Work Committees and SAWA) in responding to GBV, medical, and MHPSS needs for youth.
4- Integrate MHPSS at ER department at general hospitals in both the WB and Gaza for better detection, response and referral for youth risky behaviors.
5- Enhance the specialized MHPSS provision of care to vulnerable youths (especially youth with YLD) at CMHCs level.
The project will mainly target Palestinian youth- aged from 15 to 29 years old- presenting MHPSS concerns, due to chronic exposure to violence related to the protracted conﬂict and/or due to C-19 infection, social stigmatization, and distress imposed by the C-19 pandemic on patients in most vulnerable communities. A 2014 Birzeit University study conducted in both West Bank and Gaza, on the psychosocial health of adolescents, showed that 16% of them show moderate to severe level of distress, while the 2016 UNFPA study highlighted higher rates of psychological distress in young women as age increases, due to the heaviness of social norms in the Palestinian society.
A recent WHO survey on C-19 impact shows that people with pre-existing mental health issues become more susceptible to virus infections, and their infections are more often severe and can lead to death (WHO, 2020). Moreover, and based on a mixed study conducted by MdM-CH during 2019 on the risk factors of attempted suicide patients and prevention strategies in Palestine, it was found that 52% out of the total attempted suicide patients was youth amidst 16-25 years old. C-19 enlarges this vulnerable group to include infected patients, as the virus causes various mental and neurological complications, including delirium, agitation, and strokes (WHO, 2020). As such, the project will coordinate with WHO partners working on C-19 response to consolidate efforts and complement the work already implemented. We have also coordinated specifically with MdM-France and UNFPA, who have complementing activities but with different partners (for mobile clinics)/ and different hospitals (we will target ER departments with a focus on attempted/ suicide).
The project activities will be implemented in:
West Bank (Area A-B, & C, H2, & EJ) and the Gaza Strip, in an effort to reach people the most in need of MHPSS interventions, including the most vulnerable and marginalized communities.
The project proposes activities based on the 5 speciﬁc objectives above mentioned, aiming to:
1. Decrease stigma related to Self-Harm and Suicide, as well as stigma related to disability caused by attempted suicide, in Palestine and increase detection and referral of vulnerable cases to specialized MHPSS care, through speciﬁc actions at institutional (MoH hospitals) and community level (MCs and CMHCs).
2. Enhance the SAWA Emergency hotline in detecting, supporting, and referring youths presenting self-harm and risky behaviors.
3. Support HWC and SAWA MCs in front-line screening and detection of youth at risk of self-harm and/or suicide.
4. Integrate MHPSS at the ER department of general hospitals in all oPt to better detect, respond, & refer risky behaviors to save lives and decrease YLD (years lived with disability), by emphasizing on mhGap intervention.
5. Enhance specialized MHPSS provision of care for youth at CMHC's and specialized hospital level.

1 - Needs

Mental Health and Psychosocial issues represent one of the most signiﬁcant public health challenges across the oPt. Although the precise extent of mental disorders in Palestine is unknown, the ﬁndings of a study published in 2017 (The Burden of Mental Disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, 1990-2013) indicates that Palestine has the largest burden of mental disorders among the Eastern Mediterranean Region. The previously issued '2019 WHO new estimates of mental disorders in conﬂict settings' shows that more than one in ﬁve people (22·1%) in post-conﬂict settings has depression, anxiety disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia. Adapted to the Palestinian Population, and considering the ﬂare-up of confrontation zones in the country (e.g. Gaza, the East Jerusalem Neighborhood hotspots, H2, Seam Zone, etc.) as potential theaters of risk behaviors for individuals fragilized by long- standing mental health problems, it represents 250,026 individuals presenting life-saving MHPSS related need of intervention. The C-19 pandemic, in addition to living in protracted conflict, are great mental health stressors and are risk factors for "the development, exacerbation, and relapse of a range of mental health conditions" (WHO Global Humanitarian Response Plan COVID-19, 2020). C-19 leads to neurological and mental health issues, such as delirium, agitation, stroke, insomnia, anxiety, and depression (ibid). Moreover, social isolation, due to lockdown and disease prevention, has caused reduced physical activity, and reduced cognitive stimulation; which in turn may cause an increase in the risks of cognitive decline and dementia (ibid). The pandemic has caused a disruption of the healthcare system in general, but more specifically for mental, neurological and substance abuse conditions, which according to WHO 2020, can be life threatening, particularly of treatment for unaddressed suicide risk and harm reduction services (ibid). In the time of a large-scale need, "mental health and psychosocial support services in all humanitarian settings suffer from low investment in human and financial resources as well as stigma and limitations to access due to services disruption" (ibid). More so, C-19 has further exacerbated the situation globally according to a recent 2020 WHO survey, by causing a widespread disruption to critical mental health services for vulnerable people (72% disruption to services of children and adolescents, and 61% disruptions to women requiring antenatal or postnatal services). This corroborates the results of an RNA conducted just two months into the pandemic by PCC in 14 marginalized Palestinian communities calling for the urgent provision of MHPSS services, as it cites a surge in tension and familial conflict, and increased stress and fear as a result of the pandemic. In the Gaza Strip, the uncertainty of the pandemic and its associated measures and risks have made it very hard for people to "adapt and develop effective coping mechanisms –leading to deterioration in their mental health" (Islamic Relief Palestine). The C-19 pandemic has significantly affected the psychological state of Palestinians in the Gaza Strip, where 81.5% of respondents to a survey conducted by Islamic Relief Palestine revealed that participants suffer from anxiety, tension and stress. Finally, fairly new WHO estimates also highlight that YLDs (years living with disabilities) per 1000 people for depression and post-traumatic stress disorder are in such conﬂict affected contexts, more than 5 times higher than the existing global mean burden of disease estimates. In parallel, ﬁeld workers reported, both in Gaza and West Bank, a sharp increase of -attempted and/or completed- suicide in recent years, whilst consolidated national data still lack on the speciﬁc causes of death due to the associated stigma that leads families and health care professionals to under-report these cases. Worldwide, suicide accounts for 1.4% of all deaths and is the 18th leading cause of death (WHO, 2018). Globally, suicide is the second leading cause of death among youths 15-29 years of age (idem). The Arab police and government records report annual completed suicide rates of 1.1/100,000 to 6.2/100,000 (Karam, Hajjar and Salamoun, 2008). Recent statistics estimate that 79% of suicides occurs in low- and middle-income countries (WHO, 2018). Studies emphasized that understanding the risk factors for suicide and suicide methods is very signiﬁcant towards the development of effective preventive strategies. It is the reason why MdM-CH proceeded with an assessment study in 4 Palestinian Public Hospitals of the Southern West Bank in order to identify speciﬁc risks factors and Suicide prevention strategies in the country. The results highlighted a crucial lack in detection of people at risk, due to severe psychological distress, of risky behaviors and self-harm, as of well as to the paucity of cross-sectoral responses. It recommended consolidated, crosscutting, efforts to build a sustainable system of detection, alert and referral of people in need of urgent life-saving MHPSS interventions, and advocates for further prevention national strategies. Taking into account the study ﬁndings, and WHO’s recommendation to include mental health as an integral component in counties’ C-19 response plans, this joint project between WHO and MdM-CH proposes evidence-based activities tailored to the unique and volatile Palestinian context.

2 - Activities

Based on the speciﬁc objectives described above (see summary) the project proposes the following activities listed below: Awareness and Educational Campaigns:- Organize national media campaign around C-19 MH associated risks, self- harm, risky behaviors and suicide and the needs of early detection and support. - Organize psychoeducation campaigns for community leaders, speciﬁcally religious leaders, on the importance of seeking professional help for youth with risky behaviors such as suicidal attempts and self-harm, and to ﬁght misconception on 'Djin' and evil spirits. - Distribute brochures and leaﬂet about youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, and suicide. Healthcare Providers Trainings: - Desensitization and orientation workshops for healthcare providers at general hospitals centers on mental health; specifically on misconceptions about youth risky behaviors such as self-harm and attempted suicide. - Tele-medicine online training for MoH-103-Helpline healthcare operators in Gaza, to provide timely and proper counseling, detection, and/ or referral to youth & C-19 patients with risky behaviors such as suicidal attempts and self-harm. ER Staff at General Hospitals Level (6 hospitals in WB and 4 in Gaza):- 10 days training on mhGAP Intervention Guideline 2.0 for ER staff in 6 WB hospitals and ER staff in 4 Gaza hospitals (Shifa, Al-Naser, Andonisi, and Al-Aqsa) where coordination with MdM-France will be in place (especially in Al-Aqsa hospital), to complement their efforts by emphasizing work on youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide attempts, years lived with disability (YLD) and GBV.- 2 days training on detection of suicidal risk behavior for ER staff. - Provide a clinical supervisor for 12 months to provide support for ER teams (A total of 3 supervisors for this project will work in WB and 3 in Gaza Strip). - Develop registry and guidelines for ER department to deal with MHPSS cases, specifically cases of youth suffering with disability caused by attempted suicide. CMHCs Level (4 in WB and 4 in Gaza):- 10 days specialized training for CMHCs relevant staff on youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide and specialized MHPSS interventions, especially for youth suffering with disability caused by attempted suicide. - Develop referral protocols between ER departments in general hospitals and Community Mental Health service in MoH.- Ad hoc systematic clinical supervision for self-harm, risky behaviors and/or suicidal cases. One supervision for each CMHCs (A total of 3 supervisors for this project will work in WB and 3 in Gaza Strip). Mobile Clinics:- Provide MHPSS, medical, GBV, legal, and C-19 awareness services to youth with risky behaviors, youth with disability, and P&L women in most vulnerable communities through HWC and SAWA Mobile Clinics.- 10 days training on mhGAP intervention guideline 2.0 for MC teams, emphasizing youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide attempts (including YLD) and GBV. - 2 days training on detection of suicidal risk behavior for MC teams. - Provision of one clinical supervisor for each MC team (A total of 3 supervisors for this project will work in WB and 3 in Gaza Strip). - Sensitization session to the SAWA helpline services available for MC users. SAWA:- Operation of the SAWA helpline (15 operators to be available 24 hours/7 days a week)- beyond Dec. 2020. - Specialized training for all SAWA helpline operators (seniors and juniors) about youth vulnerability to self- harm, risky behaviors, suicide ideation and plans, and YLD due to suicide attempts. - Ad hoc & systematic clinical supervision for 12 months of SAWA helpline operators and 2 case managers for cases of self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management : Diagnosis, Implementation and M&E, protection mainstreaming is a cross-cutting theme for WHO and MdM-CH programs and projects. WHO and MdM-CH understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programs followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS. Both partners remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard a number of steps and measures are adopted to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening PIN capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming, the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines) -Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (following Sphere standards and MdM guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR (Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project will target a highly stigmatized issue (suicide/ attempted suicide) impacting youth across Palestine and including them into groups of most vulnerable populations as a result of the social, economic, and in high-conflict areas, also political stigmatization. Suicide attempters, especially male youth, cannot escape the social/economic stigma in Palestine's highly patriarchal society, or the political harassment associated with their attempt to end their lives (which makes them seem as a "security risk" for Israel). Furthermore, attempted suicide increases the possibility of living years with disability (YLD), further marginalizing youth who attempt suicide and increasing their vulnerability. For this reason, and in partnership with WHO, MoH, and our implementing local partners, all project activities will aim to decrease YLD by fortifying case management of youth exhibiting signs of risky behaviors. All project indicators will also be desegregated by PWD, in addition to sex and gender, as youth with disability will be an integral group of our project beneficiaries. As such, both WHO and MdM-CH are basing their interventions on ethical and humanitarian principles. This project aligns with the principalist approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter (and SPHERE MHPSS). Ethical principles in healthcare include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneﬁcence, the promotion of others’ well-being, commonly known as “do no harm,” as described above (9.1) and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the a second source of values and norms that animate both applicants actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conﬂict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors.
