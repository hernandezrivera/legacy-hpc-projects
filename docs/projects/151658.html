Project Summary

This action will be implemented while mainstreaming protection as a core component in IDP camps in Banadir, Lower Shabelle, Hiraan and Galmudug state. Priorities will be given to most vulnerable IDPs, including evicted and female-headed households. The latter are likely to be subjected to abuse or sexual exploitation in exchange for shelter. Beneficiaries will be mostly female headed households, minor-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, older people, disabled-headed households and households affected by drought and conflict.
Shelter and NFI kits shall be distributed through the modality of flexible vouchers. The list of eligible items is not fixed but gives flexibility to prioritise individual and gender based needs in the household. The shelter voucher includes items like plastic sheeting, timber, tools, nails, binding wire, door-frame, door-blade with hinges, latch and padlock, curtains for privacy protection, mats and mosquito nets. The list of items for NFI includes pots, pan and household utensils and also hygiene and other WASH items such as washable sanitary pads, soap, jerry cans.
A flexible voucher is limited by the fixed amount in USD that cannot be over spent. It is an unconditional and restricted voucher that will be handed out to new IDPs (including newly evicted IDPs). Depending on the sectorial combination the voucher can either be a wallet in an electronic voucher, e.g. from RedRose, or a paper voucher in a stand-alone shelter and NFI project.
The project will target 10500 households or 63000 IDPs (31500f) with emergency shelter kits, transitional shelter kits, NFI kits, and durable solutions where appropriate. There is an estimated proportion of the budget of 64% of distribution of items through restricted vouchers as modality.

Needs

The total number of Somali IDPs is about 2.6 million. During the draught in 2016 & 2017 the camps swell on considerable more than doubling the number of protracted IDPs from before the draught. Assessments concentrate on urban informal settlements hence the number of IDPs in the country side is unknown. Due to conflict and the aftermath of draught, the stream of displacement continues, 49,000 individuals were newly internally-displaced countrywide during September 2018 only (PRMN). Humanitarian conditions in many of the informal settlements and public buildings occupied by the displaced throughout Somalia continue to be dire; and may be further aggravated for communities who are forcibly evicted from their location of settlement or continue reside with insecure tenure. Due to evictions being carried out without prior notice, families had to flee their homes without their belongings and seek shelter in nearby settlements. PRMN figures indicate that during the month of August, 3,596 persons were evicted, with majority 2,544 people a clear focus of eviction in Banadir. The eviction rate in 2018 has been alarming with figures showing that the total number of individuals evicted as of July 2018 is statistically more than the entire caseload for 2017.REACH indicates that the major push factor to displacement was drought, conditions that still exist, and with the upcoming Gu rains projected to be below average, the likelihood of the drought continuing is very high, leading to a further deterioration of the humanitarian situation in 2019. Displacement can aggravate existing inequalities, as well as introduce new dimensions of marginalization and exclusion. A needs assessment conducted by SYPD 27 IDP sites in Afgooye Corridor, Baidoa and Beletweyne revealed that more than 50% of the shelter units were not permanent, 37% are emergency whereas 2% have no shelter.

Activities

1.1: Assess and register new arrivals and protracted IDPs in IDP camps. Electronic questionnaire to include questions about the intentions of the respondents1.2: Provide restricted flexible vouchers to draught and conflict affected IDPs for Emergency Shelter and NFI Kits. Both vouchers together have a ceiling value of 120 USD. Depending on available funding, priority will be given to the most vulnerable IDPs like female headed households (hh), minor headed hh, hh with pregnant and/or lactating females (women and girls), elderly and disabled people in the hh, hh with many children, etc.1.3: Conduct market assessments for shelter materials and NFI in the target area, take appropriate numbers of vendors under cooperation agreements for the redemption of vouchers1.4: Organize demonstration sessions for the construction of one shelter per 100 beneficiaries with the materials listed on the voucher2.1: Support IDPs that wish to integrate and build alternative livelihoods on land tenure and facilitate access to land for shelter and/or livelihood activities. To be done in collaboration with the HLP sub-cluster and the local authorities in the affected areas, with the aim of enhancing durable solutions2.2: Site Planning and settlement layout clearly demarcating access roads, fire breaks, common services areas2.3: Conduct assessment in camps to establish existing gaps among the IDPs. This will inform on the gaps that exist and in coordination with the CCCM mechanisms share with the relevant stakeholders for the services needed by the residents2.4: Conduct Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM): PDM will be carried out after each single distribution

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) undertakes regularly intention surveys in IDP camps based on electronic questionnaires that are analysed through the KOBO platform and that are conducted by DKH amongst the targeted population. As a result, DKH already implements in collaboration with local partners (DBG and CPD) skills training and IGA activities for drop-out pastoralists IDPs that wish to integrate in camps along Afgooye Road. With more complementary funding from development donors, DKH will implement also other projects for alternative livelihoods for IDPs in Hiraan and Galmudug-state. Also early recovery for Pastoralists that lost their existence and displaced to Galkacyo are supported with cash for work to restock in their origin area in Hobyo or to learn new techniques to intensify livestock keeping. Both projects cooperate with local authorities. With restored or alternative livelihoods and sustainable income, IDPs are able to cater for their durable shelter that they can build in compliance with their culture.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The most vulnerable IDPs may not be getting the assistance they need, such as single female-headed households, disabled people, minor headed households or orphans and separated children. Civilians arriving from areas controlled by non-state armed actors risk discrimination simply on the basis of their area of origin and alleged affiliations, with repercussions for their access to assistance and protection services. Shifting or disrupted community dynamics as a result of displacement, as well as power imbalances and marginalization, severely limit IDPs’ access to information about the availability services and entitlements, as well as IDPs’ opportunities to voice their views and concerns. Forced evictions inhibit the ability of partners to improve living conditions at sites, and low levels of hindered community participation inhibit the ability to improve beneficiary targeting to include marginalized populations.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Safety conditions especially for women and girls against SGBV need to be improved. These gender groups are mostly afraid of rape during nights when visiting communal latrines and of lack of privacy. While in the camp lay-out foot paths need to be carefully planned so that they do not provide hide-outs for attackers and can be illuminated, the list of eligible items in shelter and NFI vouchers shall include curtains to improve privacy in the homestead. Lockable doors are also on the list of eligible items that allows beneficiaries to lock the homestead from inside. Female and girls headed households are especially targeted in the distributions to avoid exclusion of these gender and age groups.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The most common threat of violence in the camps is forceful eviction and SGBV. Evictions can be very brutal and life threatening. CMC will be trained to cope with potential evictions through negotiations with landlords to tolerate their stay and if they wish to access their land to give sufficient notice to allow a peaceful withdrawal and moving to other land with agreements of the landlords of the new sites. Protection against SGBV can be improved through the provision of safe doors for the shelters, items that improve privacy, and safe foot paths with lighting during the nights
