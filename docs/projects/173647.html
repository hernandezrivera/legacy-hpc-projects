Project Summary

To mitigate the impact of COVID 19 on the health care of Women, Girls, Adolescents and Youth: This project will increase access to lifesaving basic Sexual & Reproductive Health (SRH) services for vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host communities in the three conflict affected States of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. Comprehensive Sexual reproductive health services including family planning will be provided to women of reproductive age including pregnant women, adolescent’s girls, young people and men in communities and health facilities observing strict COVID 19 Infection Prevention Protocols (IPC), using appropriate Information Education & Communication (IEC) as well as Behavioural Change Communication (BCC). The survivors of obstetric complications will also benefit from supported referral and specialized services. Medical equipment, supplies, Reproductive Health Kits, dignity kits and other items essential for SRH services will be procured and prepositioned to ensure uninterrupted service amidst COVID 19 restrictions to access and attendant utilization of essential Human Resources for Health. To ensure smooth coordination, advocacy and leadership of the sexual and reproductive health (SRH) working group under the health sector is to be strengthened to channel support to the maternal health programme and other specific SRH response; advocate at health sector level to ensure uninterrupted access to maternal health, and non-diversion of healthcare resources at the expense of pregnant and lactating women and adolescent in dire need of SRH services.

1 - Needs

As the COVID-19 pandemic rages on it second wave and as fatigue continues to grow on COVID 19 IPC protocols, the number of women unable to access sexual reproductive health especially family planning, facing unintended pregnancies, gender-based violence and other harmful practices may surge as new infections increase and health facilities may be focused on COVID 19 care and response at the expense of SRH. Assessment reveals the enormous scale of the impact COVID-19 is having on women, girls, adolescents and youth in need of essential SRH services as health systems become overloaded, facilities close or only provide a limited set of services to women and girls, and many choose to skip important medical check-ups through fear of contracting the virus. The pandemic is deepening inequalities, and millions more women and girls now risk losing the ability to plan their families and protect their bodies and their health plunging them towards financial impoverishment and a deleterious cycle of diminishing health. During public health emergencies, human and financial resources are often diverted from various health programs to respond to the infectious disease outbreak. Sexual and reproductive health services are being impacted by the pandemic and must be prioritized. Before the emergence of COVID-19, 35 percent of health facilities in the affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe were damaged as a result of conflict. There have also been significant disruption of vaccination campaigns and other essential health services for children and other vulnerable groups in inaccessible areas. In addition, funding has been a major challenge. In 2019, the health sector received only 25 percent of its funding requirements. The humanitarian situation is expected to worsen due to COVID 19, particularly due to ongoing stressors experienced by the health system including disrupted health system in many inaccessible LGAs, non-functional health facilities, lack of health care staff, overwhelmed response capacity of international humanitarian agencies and lack of resources. A tightening of pre-existing movement restrictions, economic deterioration coupled with strained public services will further increase humanitarian needs and compound existing operational challenges. It is critical that all women have access to birth spacing and contraceptive option by choice, quality safe birth, the continuum of antenatal care to postnatal care, including essential screening tests according to national guidelines and standards, especially in epicentres of the pandemic, where access to services for pregnant women, women in labour and delivery, and lactating women is negatively impacted. Vaccine hesitancy is already a problem in the BAY states of the North East, at such more community level sensitization and awareness on the usefulness of a possibly available COVID19 vaccination towards the end of year is critical to achieve some level of uptake and herd immunity.

2 - Activities

UNFPA is working with governments and partners to prioritize the needs of women and girls of reproductive age and to respond urgently during the challenging public health emergency. Our priorities are focusing on strengthening health systems, procuring and delivering essential supplies to protect health workers, ensuring access to sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence services, and promoting risk communication and community engagement.• Train health workers, particularly Doctors, midwives, Nurses on the essential package of SRH/RMNCAH continuum of care targeted at Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), Adolescents and Young People (AYP) and front line responders.• Protect health workers (in particular midwives, nurses, medical officers, obstetricians and anaesthesiologist’s) by ensuring that basic protection (PPE) is available (gloves and masks, gowns and protective lenses.• Provide orientation to health workers on identification of SRH/RMNACH needs of Women & Pregnant women with respiratory illnesses, facilitation of referrals to be treated with utmost priority due to increased risk of adverse outcomes, ensuring Antenatal, neonatal and maternal health units are segregated from suspected and confirmed COVID-19 cases.• Ensure women’s and girls’ choices and rights to sexual and reproductive health is respected regardless of their COVID-19 status, including access to contraception, emergency contraception and post-abortion care through integrated medical outreaches.• Ensure access to healthcare is maintained, including sexual and reproductive health services with special attention to PLW, AYP among vulnerable populations including persons living with disabilities and persons living with HIV via provision of referral support through mini ambulances, referral pathways and health facility mapping and linkages. • Support the implementation of the key package of care with the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health in Emergencies framework - Comprehensive STI service including HIV/PMTCT, upscaling health response to GBV, Support for EmONC and ensuring the health systems are integrated to provide wholistic and quality SRHR services.• Strengthened Coordination through working with the State Ministries of Health and relevant line ministries, and the implementing partners to ensure availability and access to essential SRHR including maternal and new-born health services. • Support supply chain (modern contraceptives, maternal and new-born life-saving drugs and supplies, maternal health equipment, IPC material and supplies, educational and counselling materials) In humanitarian contexts like COVID 19, this should include the implementation of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health in Emergencies.• Support scale up of COVID 19 testing to essential health workers proving SRHR services, PLW accessing care and adolescents. Galvanize SRH partners support to ensure that the most in need of critical COVID 19 vaccination receive it when it becomes available.
