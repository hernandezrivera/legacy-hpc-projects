Project Summary

Using its rapid response mechanism, White Hands (WH) proposes a 24-month action that uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes emergency and regular food distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind & food voucher assistance modalities, and Bakery Support.
This program is a planned action, submitted to a Donor agency for funding, contributes to the first sector objective of the FSL cluster which is "Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance" and aims to address the immediate food security needs of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and affected host communities with acute needs, living in the districts of Afrin, Jebel Saman, and A'zaz (Aleppo Governorate), and the districts of Ariha, Idleb, Harim, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria, in both the short to medium-term.
In the short-term, WH is planning to reach 10,000 HHs/50,000 individuals with 20,000 in-kind RTERs based on their needs and reported situation. This is designed to be a one-off distribution for exceptional cases in which beneficiaries do not have access to cooking facilities.
Equally, to meet the food security needs in the medium-term, WH aims to assist 16,000 vulnerable internally displaced, returnees, and affected host communities’ HHs (80,000 individuals) that meet the Food Security Program beneficiary eligibility criteria with regular monthly food assistance through the most appropriate modalities. This will include providing 7,000 HHs/35,000 individuals, living in areas where markets are inaccessible, or food is unavailable or unaffordable with in-kind food baskets for eight consecutive months, and restricted food vouchers to a further 9,000 HHs/45,000 individuals with access to functioning markets in 8 distribution rounds, as recommended by the FSL Cluster and WFP.
The Bakery Support component provides approximately 30,000 individuals living in supported bakeries’ catchment areas (mainly IDP sites/camps) with free bread. This is achieved through subsiding up to 100% input (flour, yeast, and operational costs) of bread production thus supporting 5 local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditioned upon Free bread production and distribution to IDPs on a daily basis and to be monitored by WH cash & markets, and monitoring Teams.
In the framework of this project, WH will identify households who do not have the financial means to purchase food to meet their survival basic needs, priority will be given to newly IDP households, households headed by single or widowed women, unaccompanied minors, PWDs, and elderly persons.
Throughout this program, WH will identify the unique needs of men, women, girls, and boys and seek to maximize equality of treatment and access to services for all of these groups.
Moreover, WH M&E systems include accountability frameworks and have a continuously functioning complaints/feedback mechanism (CFMs) inside Syria, that include direct mobile phone hotlines with Field MEAL Officer (including through WhatsApp/telephone/email/Facebook), and use of complaints boxes for submission of written complaints. Through CFMs, beneficiaries can safely provide feedback, report any project issues, and easily express concerns regarding the neutrality of WH.

The proposed targeted districts are currently facing a funding gap in Food Security Program, leaving many people in need without assistance.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 160,000 individuals (50,000 individuals provided with one-time emergency food assistance, 80,000 individuals supported with regular monthly food assistance, and 30,000 individuals supported with free bread distribution).
Note: 50% of this program will be implemented during 2022, while the other 50% are planned for 2023.

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO FSA, the situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated with an estimated 14.6M people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance. Of this, 7.3M (50%) are female & 4.1M (28%) are PWDs. Food is the first top-ranked priority needs for 86% of the respondents.An additional Food Security Sector (FSS) assessment on Newly Displaced IDPs (NIDPs) in NWS, found that 41.5% of all NIDP are food insecure and in need of assistance, 50.1% had experienced hunger, and 41.2% of HHs had unacceptable food consumption.Moreover, according to the RNA conducted by WH in NWS in Dec 2021:oThe most at-risk priority group is HHs headed by women, IDPs living in camps, HHs with PWDs & IDPs living in unfinished buildings.oFor IDPs, 90% of HHs respondents tended to rank food as a top priority followed by the shelter.oThe most commonly reported food items needed for IDPs were cooking oil, bread, sugar, rice, and bulgur.o80% of HHs reported not receiving food support in the month prior to the survey. Nevertheless, most HHs in all communities felt the food support was insufficient.oRegarding the modality, 48% of the HHs prefer in-kind assistance, while 52% of the HHs prefer cash/vouchers. The most commonly reported barriers to physically accessing food markets were lack of transportation to markets, transportation cost, and General safety or security concerns at markets.oFrom this RNA, markets appear to be relatively functional but with relatively high prices.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. WH is planning to reach 10,000 newly displaced HHs/50,000 individuals with RTERs within 72 hours of displacement, to cover the needs of 5- members’ HHs for one month. RTERs will be designed following the SPHERE standards and FSL cluster recommendations and aims to meet 2100 kcal/person/day for 5 days. 2. Provide 7,000 HHs/35,000 individuals with in-kind food baskets for 8 consecutive months, and restricted food vouchers with a value of 60$/HH/month based on SMEB estimates & FSL cluster recommendations to a further 9,000 HHs/45,000 individuals in 8 distribution rounds. Regardless of the modality that will be employed, the ration (food basket) will be designed following the SPHERE standards & FSL cluster recommendations of 70% or1550 of the 2,100 kcal/person/day, with 12% of total energy provided by protein, 29%provided by fat & 59% provided by carbohydrates with the inclusion of required minerals. 3. Support 5 bakeries (wheat flour, yeast, etc.) through appropriate modalities to enable 30,000 IDPs to access free bread. To ensure the most vulnerable HHs are selected, WH conducts a socio-economic vulnerability assessment scoring exercise based on aggregated vulnerability scores. The modality of response is determined by market assessments. The most marginalized beneficiaries will be eligible for additional support (such as home delivery). Each distribution will be followed by PDM. WH will report its activities to the FSL cluster on monthly basis using the 5Ws matrix formats.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). L: M/ I: H. MS: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set up, postponed, or canceled, Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding, Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management, Application of checklist at distribution sites, Ensure that food/vouchers distribution is completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women and Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs. Risk2: For cash and vouchers there is the risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. L: M/I: H. MS: Strengthen over-sight mechanism, develop a range of measures & internal procedures to minimize the risk of corruption. Risk3: Tension between HC, IDPs & returnees. L: H/I: H. MS: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs & not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent & groups are fully informed, engage with the communities to sensitize the population, Make an effort to target a % of the vulnerable HC in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs, Establish feedback mechanism, conduct follow-up visits, and Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: Food distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design, and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of food distribution points remains at a minimum distance as outlined in-country policy document as to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with extra workload in particular.Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the ‎PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries ‎concerning CFMs through informative sessions at the beginning of the activities, making sure that beneficiaries are ‎aware and engaged, if necessary in improving the design, monitoring, and review of the CFMs.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting WH’s ability to operate and access it.L: M/I: H. MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.Assumption2: The project does not create distortions in the local economy.Risk2: As a consequence of the project, there is a shortage of food items in the area and the prices increase. L: M/I: M. MS2: Vendors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information regarding expected sales and the amount to be distributed to allow the supply of sufficient stocks. The price of the food items should be agreed upon with vendors and advertised to the beneficiaries. Risk3: Spread of COVID-19 pandemic cases in NWS. L: M/I: M. MS3: Follow strict preventive measures, building on the FSL guidelines and recommendations. Those will be reflected in each activity, All project teams and staff will receive sensitization on COVID-19 precautions. Risk4: Aid diversion by armed groups, including looting of warehouses. Probability: L/Impact: M MS4: Invest in sound security measures at the warehouses, Train the staff on how to deal with aid diversion, Establish timely reporting mechanisms & info sharing with other actors on the ground, donors, and clusters.
