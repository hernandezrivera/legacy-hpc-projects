Project Summary

The NGO aims to improve access to basic preventive and curative healthcare services for internally displaced and conflict-affected populations in targeted areas in NWS through the provision of life-saving emergency, primary and secondary healthcare services, including sexual and reproductive healthcare, physical rehabilitation and mental health services. Programs will be delivered through support to two maternity and child hospitals, five static primary healthcare clinics, and one mobile medical unit. 10 ambulances will also operate emergency transport services and support accessibility. Four physical rehabilitation static centers and 10 rehabilitation mobile units will operate for physical rehabilitation services, integrated with MHPSS and protection services Centers will be supported through the provision of incentives, running costs, equipment, pharmaceuticals, and other needed supplies. Services will target girls, women, boys and men.
Medical: Basic primary health services provided will include the treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses and injuries, including local endemic diseases; the provision of essential medicines, including contraceptives and treatment for chronic diseases; management of communicable and non-communicable diseases and health education. The PHCs and attached MMUs will also provide basic reproductive health services including antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning, and syndromic management of STIs. MMUs and PHCs will operate a referral system for cases to secondary and tertiary service hospitals. Ambulatory teams will provide emergency services to causalities and patients transportation through ambulances. Health activities will maintain and ensure sustaining and expanding essential, quality health services at community through primary and secondary health care to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups to both IDPs and host communities.

Physical rehabilitation: Rehab static centers will provide physical therapy services integrated with psychosocial support and protection services for people with disabilities (PWDs) and war injuries, including treatment plans, follow-up sessions and the provision of mobility and orthoses devices. Caregivers and family members will be provided with training to support the care of PWDs. Rehab mobile units will provide outreach activities at the community and camp levels for those who may not be able to access the static centers due to mobility conditions, financial limitation or security barriers.
Mental health: The NGO will provide an integrated and holistic approach to MH service delivery for those with mental, neurological and substance abuse (MNS) conditions and/or living with acute and chronic psychological distress. This will include specialized and focused individual MHPSS services, including the assessment and psychotropic treatment via a psychiatrist and mhGAP-trained doctors who are under the supervision of the psychiatrist.

4 - Needs

Displacement has increased disease outbreak, with the expansion of services in facilities needed to cover higher caseloads, new health needs, with strengthened referral systems with reproductive health referrals. The NGO’s needs assessment from June 2019 found that distance, lack of available services and cost of health services were also high on the list of barriers, indicated by both males and females. 37% of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; an estimated 41% of Syria’s adult population requires treatment for one or more non-communicable diseases (NCDs). Health Cluster 2019 4Ws report and HNAP June 2019, identified that physical rehabilitation services are an urgent priority at more than 33% prevalence of physical/mobility-related disabilities among Idleb and Aleppo, where over half of people with disabilities (PwD) report the cause as conflict-related injury. The NGO’s assessment in June 2019 indicated that there is a need for physical rehabilitation services and assistive devices yet 86% had never received physical therapy and 92% had not received ADs. The NGO’s MSNA from June 2019 in Syria indicated the causes of MH needs identified are both acute, resulting from the recent ongoing conflict and displacement, and chronic as a result of the protracted conflict affecting peoples’ lives and psychological wellbeing in many ways, as well as persons who have mental disorders from before the onset of the conflict. The situation reflects the need to provide a range of specialized and tailored Mental Health services targeting the most at risk and vulnerable individuals, training and outreach activities focused on prevention, reducing stigma and increasing awareness.

5 - Implementation of activities

The NGO will support to 5 static PHCs (Atmeh 1 PHC, Atmeh 2 PHC. Sothern Atmeh PHC, Green Idleb PHC, Kafr Yahmol PHC and one MMU attached to Kafr Yahmol in accordance with WHO PHC standards including:•Treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses/injuries/endemic diseases; •Provision of essential medicines, including contraceptives;•Integrated reproductive health services (ANC, PNC,STI and Family Planning)•Referrals from MMU to PHC, secondary and tertiary service hospitals.Specialized Maternity and Child Health services will be provided at 2 hospitals in Afrin and in Idleb, providing CEmONC services 10 ambulances (5 Idleb; 5 Aleppo) to facilitate emergency cases and referrals for more specialized medical services. Physical Rehabilitation sevices through 4 static rehab centers and 10 mobile units in Idleb City, Maaret Temsrin, Aldana and Salqin. Rehab centers provide physical therapy services, integrated with PSS and protection services for PWDs and war injuries including treatment plans, follow up sessions, donation of mobility and orthoses devices, and training for caregivers and service providers. Mobile units will assist with accessibility. Mental health: MH Case Management will be provided in 5 integrated MHPSS clinics in Atmeh, Idleb, Dier Hassan, Afrin and Sujjo. Specialized MH, mhGAP and case management services will be integrated into PHCs, rehab and protection centers for access and referral. This will aim to promote resilience, limit MH and psychological effects of the conflict on the community, reduce stigma, and provide space for host community members and IDPs to collaborate. Monitoring and Evaluation: The NGO monitors activities through regular visits by technical and MEAL staff using standardized technical and MEAL checklists for each department. After the visit the checklist is shared with the technical and MEAL departments for review and follow-up of recommendations. In addition, ad-hoc visits are conducted by senior technical staff.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Risk: Violence against health facility/providers; Mitigation: Training for facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. Installing visual warning system in health facilities, continuing advocacy efforts by UN agencies with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law• R: Gender-based violence: there is still stigma face survivors to seek health services: M: Train health providers on CMR, distribute PEP kits and assign focal point to treat survivors at each facility, good integration of GBV services with health facilities. Patient confidentiality ensured. Enhanced referral system among actors. Presence of female staff in health facilities, separate waiting areas, and offers a safe environment for receiving health services.• R: Duty of care; health care providers and humanitarian workers are amongst first responders and the last to move out of areas witnessing increased conflict activities or change in control: R: All NGOs and stakeholder improve duty of care policies in coordination with OCHA and other UN agencies to be applied and considered in the budget.• R: exclusion of persons with disabilities in health services; females from rehab and other services; patients whom are not able to reach health facilities due to physical or financial barriers: staff trained on inclusion principles, health facilities adjusted. M: all teams made up of male and female workers. Outreach activities and mobile units are core to each program to reach beneficiaries in their homes. • R: Violence/harm toward staff from beneficiaries, community members, persons with mental disorders when providing case management, psychosocial support, community-based activities, group work. M: staff never work alone, either in facilities or in the community via outreach. Security guards will be employed at each PHC/MHPSS Center. Each staff member has a mobile phone and is required to check in with team leaders periodically.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions• NGO continues to have approvals from local authorities to operate in targeted locations• NGO maintains access to targeted locations• Security situation remains stable• Non-interference by groups in control of program locations Risks• Security situation is volatile where destabilization negatively impacts staff and beneficiaries• Delays in procurement and delivery of essential pharmaceuticals and medical consumables and equipment essential for health facility rehabilitation and operations; leading to delay in start-up of services for beneficiaries or cause of stock outs and shortage of medicines• Program locations are taken over by GoT, GoS or allied forces; leading to restrictions in implementation of activities without specific approvals (in the case of GoT) or through cross border operations (GoS) Mitigation Measures• The physical security of NGO facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded as needed. Close security monitoring. Security training for staff which to limit/lessen exposure. Support to evacuation/withdrawal of local staff to alternative locations to continue managing program or revise targeting. The NGO will consider supporting affected staff through Duty of Care, including financial compensation, support for the treatment of injured staff and compensation for families of staff that may be killed as a result of conflict and severance pay if staff can no longer continue• The NGO uses joint efforts of procurement and legal staff to clarify details of hosting countries regulations. The NGO applies for appropriate certifications, licenses and permits to import and/or use bonded transportation and warehouses for humanitarian assistanceThe NGO will explore options to continue support through local partner NGOs registered in Turkey in the case of GoT takeover to maintain support. The NGO will withdraw support in case of GoS takeover to protect the safety of the staff as cross border operations are deemed illegal by GoS.
