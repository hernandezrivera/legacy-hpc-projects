Project Summary

Medair has worked in South Sudan since 1992. Currently its operations consist of the static projects in Renk (former Upper Nile State), Aweil Centre (former Northern Bahr el Ghazal State) and Leer and Mayendit (former Unity State) with the main objective of decreasing preventable morbidity and mortality due to acute malnutrition by treating children and PLWs with GAM and underlying illnesses, building local nutritional treatment and prevention capacity, and increasing preventative infant and young child feeding practices. In all the sites, Medair’s main activity will be implementing the management of acute malnutrition, according to the national CMAM guidelines, targeting the vulnerable children under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women. This will include facility and community level promotion of IYCF-E in line with national MIYCN guidelines as well as training, capacity building and regular supportive supervision at supported Medair sites. In total, the target for 2020 will be of 6,663 SAM new admissions, 20,479 MAM new admissions and 11,869 PLWs.
In Renk, Medair remains the only long-term nutrition partner and one of the only health actors to respond to the nutritional emergency. In May 2018, Medair conducted a SMART survey in Renk County that revealed GAM and SAM rates above the emergency threshold, with the critical level of 24.4% and 5.6% among IDPs and 32.1% and 8.5% among the host community, respectively.
Medair performed a SMART survey in Leer County in April 2018 showing a GAM rate of 14.3% (SAM 3.2%). The household food security situation in the Medair catchment areas of Leer appeared dire, with 2/3 (64%) of the households reporting to rely on food aid as their main source of livelihood for the past 3 months prior to the survey. Household Hunger Scale revealed that more than half of the households surveyed (53%) experienced moderate hunger score, while – worryingly - 33% experiencing severe hunger score. Around 20% of the interviewed households, during the SMART exercise, had already employed crisis coping strategies, while the majority of them (73%) had employed emergency strategies.
In Aweil Centre, the results from the SMART survey of July 2019 showed a prevalence of a GAM at 19.3% (including a SAM prevalence of 1.3%), which is significantly much higher than the GAM rate of 6.3% measured by the SMART survey of July 2018.
The IPC classification for acute food insecurity of August 2019 indicates that Aweil Centre is IPC 3 (Crisis), which is a worsening compared to 1 year ago, when it was projected IPC 2 (Stressed).
In all of these project sites, Medair will ensure the minimum WASH package of safe water and latrine access, including waste management is present and functional. Systematic malaria RDT will be conducted on admission for SAM children, and treatment of malnourished children suffering from malaria will be implemented. Moreover, treatment of co-existing illness will be ensured. Where possible, linkage with the FSL partner will be encouraged, while child friendly spaces and early child development activities will be fostered in order to allow the full neuro-cognitive development of the child and promote the importance of the mother-to-child relationship.
The Emergency Response Team (ERT) responds to nutrition emergencies in South Sudan.
According to the IPC of August 2019, the overall nutrition situation in terms of acute malnutrition during the lean season of 2019 is critical and worse compared to same period in 2018. Based on the IPC AMN, 58 counties (out of the 79 total counties) were classified as IPC AMN Phase 3 and above. Furthermore, all counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Jonglei (except Pibor), Upper Nile (except Maban), Unity and Counties of Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Twic, Warrap, Budi, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Lafon, Eastern Equatoria State, Awerial and Yirol West, Lakes were classified as IPC AMN Phase 4.

1 - Needs

In Renk, following the results from the SMART survey performed in May 2018, showing a proxy nutrition coverage rate of 61.2% in both the rural and urban area, Medair will increase its active screening through a strengthening of the CNV network in hard-to-reach areas and the systematic periodic implementation of exhaustive mass MUAC screening in the whole county screening will be conducted on an at least 6 month-basis). The project will continue to provide nutrition services (both OTP and TSFP) in 13 sites (5 static and 8 mobiles), with possibility to convert mobile sites in/scale-up some static sites (Gerger, Shamidi, Molbuk and Jerbana). Timely referral to the Abayok Stabilisation Centre (run by Medair) will be ensured. Medair will support the MoH to conduct the two Vitamin A and deworming supplementation (VASD) campaigns planned for 2020. In Leer/Mayendit, Medair will continue to run OTP, TSFP activities in 6 static (Padeah, Din Din, Gandor, Guat, Adok in Leer, and Luol in Mayendit), and 2 outreach sites (Meer islands, Bow) (outreach of Meer). Timely identification and referral of SAM cases with medical complications to the SC of Nile Hope in Touch Riek will be facilitated. Exhaustive mass MUAC screening will be performed on a semestrial basis. Medair will support the MoH to conduct the two Vitamin A and deworming supplementation (VASD) campaigns planned for 2020. In Aweil Centre, Medair operates in up to 16 nutrition sites (7 static and 9 outreach sites). The activities will continue in 2020 and will be constantly tailored to address the uncovered nutrition needs in the county. An additional outreach site in Machot (close to Akuangkar) will be temporarily operational during the rainy season period in 2020 to guarantee access to the population living across the river. Maluil Akot nutrition site (in the hard-to-reach area, east of Bau) will be established in 2020. Gender has a prominent role in the evolution of food and nutrition insecurity. Poverty and poor access to resources among women result in widespread food access constraints. The region is characterised by the significant presence of female-headed households (72% of the households according to the SMART 2019), with significantly poorer access to resources and services. For this reason, Medair plans to further expand the mother-to-mother support groups under the form of Care Groups to improve heathy behaviors such as strengthened infant and young child feeding practices and increase resilience of the mothers and the households. Care Groups will be developed in 2020 in the areas of Bau, Bar Mayen and Alok locations (on top of the already developed CG in Aweil town and Akuangkar). Medair will support the MoH to conduct the two Vitamin A and deworming supplementation (VASD) campaigns planned for 2020 and will include, during such exercises, the implementation of county-wide exhaustive mass MUAC screening. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will be deployed for a period of up to 3 months to response to nutritional emergencies - through helping integrate nutrition services in health facilities, building nutrition capacity of local partners or scaling up mobile responses in remote areas with nutrition service coverage gaps.

2 - Activities

Infant Young Child Feeding and Behaviour Change In Renk, Leer, and Aweil, Medair will use the National MIYCN Guidelines to govern the dissemination of IYCF messaging. This will be integrated into each level of contact beneficiaries have with staff throughout the OTP and TSFP programmes to provide sufficient opportunities for knowledge acquisition and to promote behaviour change. Child friendly spaces will be used as an opportunity to support mothers, in particular in the violence-affected areas of Leer County.Medair is actively monitoring and will report to the competent MoH authorities about the detrimental distribution of breast-milk substitute, other milk products, bottles and teats in the areas where it works, adhering to the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes and following the directives of the MIYCN Guidelines of South Sudan. Management of Acute Malnutrition Medair will continue treating children under 5 years with SAM and MAM, through the provision of the outpatient CMAM programme in up to 38 (up to 13 in Renk, up to 16 in Aweil, up to in 8 Leer) locations across Aweil Centre, Leer, Mayendit, and Renk counties. Medair will use the 2016 National MoH approved CMAM Guidelines to govern implementation of the Nutrition Programme. As per standard protocol, all children with complicated severe acute malnutrition will be referred to a SC run by Medair or another implementing agency. OTP and TSFP will either be integrated into existing Medair emergency health services (as is the case in Wunthou and Abayok in Renk), established alongside health activities being implemented by other actors (as is the case across all sites in Leer and Mayendit), or integrated into the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services in Health Pooled Fund/MoH-run health facilities in Aweil Centre. The community mobilisation activities will rely on the development of networks of Community Nutrition Volunteers/Workers (CNVs and CNWs). Medair closely follows the CMAM and MIYCN guidelines for all nutrition programming in South Sudan for both SAM and MAM cases. Integration- Nutrition Sensitive Activities As explained above, energy and resources will be employed to ensure that minimal WASH package, health package (RDT and measles vaccination; provision of amoxicillin, and deworming according to the CMAM protocol, ACT for the treatment of malaria when need; management of co-existing illnesses; referral to the stabilisation center according to the CMAM action protocol for OTP); cooking demonstrations, FSL linkage and ECD activities will be implemented in the best patient’s interest and according to the directives of the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will aim to provide the full CMAM package in all emergency interventions as needed. The actual response will depend on the needs and gaps identified in the needs assessment. New sites when fully established will aim to be in secure and accessible areas, while also ensuring privacy for inpatient caregivers, where possible. Medair will endeavour to recruit qualified staff or mentor existing partner staff to run nutrition activities at the targeted nutrition sites. Emergency nutrition intervention will be rolled out in the following cluster rapid response phased model depending on security and accessibility of target areas• Phase 1: High insecurity location with limited accessibility, day trips: MUAC screening at a central location and BP5 distribution • Phase 2: High GAM/immediate need to respond, limited access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: MUAC screening, provision of 2-week ration for MAM and SAM with deworming; use of expanded criteria if needed; key MIYCN/HHP messaging and follow-up visits as security permits• Phase 3: Secure location with access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: establish full CMAM programme, identify or capacity building of long-term partners for handover

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback is gathered from the Care Groups . In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions.Through a network of community nutrition volunteers/workers who do house-to-house outreach, communities are regularly informed about existing services. When the emergency response team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment as well as the fact that the assessment does not automatically result in an intervention. The same message is consistently shared during the assessment at HH level, as well as during FGDs and other meetings held. Based on the needs found in the assessment, Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome. In case an emergency intervention will be conducted, continuous messaging through a mixture of channels as mentioned above will ensure the communities are well informed—including through the use of CNVs going door-to-door.
