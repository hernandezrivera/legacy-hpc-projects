Project Summary

Through this action, NGO X will address the multiple vulnerabilities that households and communities are facing after ten years of conflict. At the household level, the program design creates links between its multi-donor funded emergency response program to its food security, nutrition, winterization and shelter programming, and complements food assistance programming (food vouchers and kits) with basic needs assistance via Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA). NGO X has included two MPCA components under this submission; the first is the one-off emergency MPCA with full SMEB value and the second is the repeated MPCA to cover the basic needs except the food items. The second MPCA component was deviated from the full SMEB value as it is a complementary approach to the regular food assistance activities. The value per HHs with the repeated MPCA component is $31 per HH for 6 rounds, which is complementing the food assistance activities which is equal to 70% of the food SMEB value which is in line with food security cluster’s recommendations.
The proposed intervention is in line with the objectives and strategic direction of the various Northwest Syria Cluster Response Plans. These activities have been informed by Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSRNAs) conducted in Idleb and North Aleppo during 2020 as well as lessons learned from previous projects, ongoing monitoring, feedback from beneficiaries and other assessments conducted by NGO X.
Overall target:
The proposed activities outlined below will target approximately 69,396 HHs/346,980 vulnerable displaced and resident individuals in North West Syria (in Idleb and North Aleppo).
Project budget across sectors for the one-off MPCA is (based on the SMEB):
· FSL: 70 %
· SNFI: 15%
· WASH: 15%
The cash value of the MPG will be aligned with the SMEB. However, in terms of the regular MPCA for basic needs which is meant to cover 30% of SMEB (except food). The currency applied is USD. The number of households beneficiaries who will be target with one-off emergency MPCA is 17,421 HHs/87,105 individuals. The number of households beneficiaries who will be target with repeated MPCA is 51,975 HHs/259,875 individuals.
Prior to launching any response window/round, NGO X will coordinate response locations with CWG, SNFI, WASH, and FSL cluster partners/members to avoid overlaps and duplications. The sub-districts were included in the response locations are the locations where NGO X have access to and currently responding in. NGO X is following area/location-based coordination. NGO X will also explore the potential for NGO X’s e-platform system to become interoperable with other platforms used by donors’ partners, maintaining the integrity of data protection and sharing key findings with the same donor.
The total budget of this submission is $15,290,431. $12,760,902 is contributing to repeated MPCA component which includes support and HQ costs, and $2,529,529 is contributing to one-off emergency MPCA which also includes support and HQ costs.

4 - Needs

These activities were informed by Multi-Sectoral Rapid Needs Assessments (MSRNAs) conducted in Idleb and North Aleppo in 2020, and lessons learned from previous projects, ongoing monitoring, beneficiary feedback and other assessments conducted by NGO X and other agencies. NGO X carried out MSRNAs in Sharan sub-district in March 2021, noting that: • 84% of interviewed people reported that their shelter is crowded and indicated that between 1 and 10 HHs are sharing the same shelter. 20% reported that their shelter is not protected against weather conditions, and lack of privacy. • 66% reported problems in feeding their infants and children less than 2 years. 40% reported a lack of access to infant formula. • Almost all (98%) of HHs reported they don’t have enough food for 2 weeks. o Most indicated that they do not have basic NFI items and that the most needed items are: Fuel for heating/cooking (27%), Hygiene Items (16%), Bedding (13%), Clothes (12%), Solar lamps (12%), cooking materials (11%), winter kits (5%) and Jerry Cans (4%). • 50% said they secure their clean water through water trucking to houses and 48% reported through water trucking to collection points. 2% secure their clean water through piped water to collection points. • 48% reported a lack of household water storage capacity, 48% reported high price of water as a barrier. It was evident from the need assessment and the monitoring findings that MPCA came in the top priority needs and was request by the affected population across NGO X geographic coverage. NGO X references the two needs assessment above as they were recently conducted with fresh findings, but other assessments were conducted during 2020 and 2021 with similar findings.

5 - Implementation of activities

Coordinated MPCA will be implemented through cash transfer is the preferred modality where markets are accessible and functional and security conditions allow. MSRNAs will be conducted, alongside monthly market monitoring to assess market functionality, capacities and gaps. 69,396 households will be reached. The selection team accept or reject affected people based on the defined response criteria that includes the following:• HHs displaced due to a defined shock (natural disaster/ conflict)• HHs displaced within a defined timeframe (i.e. people displaced less than a month)• Displaced HHs haven’t received similar assistance based on the coordination with LCs/Relief Committee/camp management, other orgs and clusters• Verify affected people’s income level/status as much as possible.• Response criteria is decided in the CIC e-mail for approval.Distribution of regular MPCA for basic needs is alongside regular food security assistance. NGO X conducts Distribution Monitoring (DM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) activities for each round, carried out by the MEL team (PDM approx. 3-4 weeks after each round). Prior to distributions, finance confirms FSPs/hawalas delivery capacity and their ability to source enough appropriate bank notes. FSP Selection is based on a comprehensive, competitive procurement process and must pass the vetting process and security check carried out by NGO X annually and is in line with local regulations. NGO X Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is present in all locations and accessible to all programme participants, community members and stakeholders. Dedicated in-country teams and resources manage CFM and ensures they’re safe, accessible and confidential.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Tension between beneficiaries/non-beneficiaries Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: - Ensure distribution is conducted based on needs and not status.- Selection criteria are transparent, groups fully informed. Distribution partners engage with communities to sensitize the population. - Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that supports IDPs - Apply NGO X’s CFM. - Involve local stakeholders throughout the process Abuse of power by distribution actors Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation:-Ensure all staff sign code of conduct and receive training on NGO X’s anti-fraud, whistleblowing, safeguarding and PSEA policies -Safeguarding Reporting Framework and CFM in place -At least 1 female in each verification team. Risk of physical harm at distribution points Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: - Well-organized distribution to avoid overcrowding. Staff training on distributions and crowded management. Checklists at distribution sites. Priority lines for vulnerable persons. Door-to-door distribution for very vulnerable HHs. Risk of data breaches impacting beneficiaries’ privacy/safety Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation:- Data protection policy in place and staff trained- Beneficiary data stored on a secure platform- Minimum personal data required for programmingDiminished availability of small denomination USD notes of acceptable quality in project locationsMitigation: Give 4 days’ notice to FSP for appropriate, quality notes. Set cash transfer value to an even number to avoid the need for smaller notes. Round up CVA value according to the SMEB value. NGO X distribution team to monitor distribution sites to check note quality.
