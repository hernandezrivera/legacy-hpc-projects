Project Summary

Mercy Corps is currently implementing the Projet d’appui à la sécurité alimentaire, la réconciliation et la résilience (PASERREL) project, and planning for a two-year extension with the goal of building upon and expanding the original PASERREL’s goal of improving the well-being and economic resilience of communities in central Mali through a holistic and conflict-sensitive response. Based on successful approaches implemented by Mercy Corps in central Mali through PASERREL over 2019-2020, as well as long-standing lessons gleaned from seven years of programming in northern Mali, PASERREL II will meet the pressing needs of communities for humanitarian assistance in the sectors of Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS), Agriculture and Food Security, Shelter and Settlements, and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH). It will continue to enhance the work of other humanitarian actors in Mopti by filling gaps in geographic and sectoral coverage, and providing options for communities to meet their own needs beyond the initial infusion of immediate humanitarian assistance. The program will pursue interventions under two primary outcomes: 1) Conflict-affected communities maintain and increase well-being despite ongoing shocks and stresses; and 2) Conflict-affected communities receive a timely and holistic package of humanitarian support in the immediate aftermath of conflict, displacement or health related shocks.
The Shelter and Settlements Non-Food Items sub-sector under both projects has been included to help beneficiaries cover their emergency needs for NFIs, in coordination with the WASH NFIs sub-sector. Potential targeting criteria (to be adjusted or verified with community leaders and representatives) for this sub-sector include: households having fled following an attack or security incident with few of their belongings or otherwise lacking basic household items; elderly (+60 years) headed families, living alone or with children; people with permanent disability or chronic illness; and women or children-headed households. In some cases, it may be determined that the entire caseload of IDPs in a given location should receive assistance under this sub-sector, if their villages of origin were destroyed. In other cases, our assessment found that households left their communities in a more pre-meditated fashion, in which case the need for NFI assistance may be less acute. The Shelter sub-sector has also been added. To respond to these needs, two kinds of assistance will be provided under the Shelter subsector: emergency shelter kits and shelter repair assistance. Emergency shelter kits including materials to construct tarpaulin tents will increase access to temporary housing for newly displaced households with no other housing option, or those already living in makeshift or precarious temporary housing. IDPs living in housing provided by host communities (with host families or in individual houses) that is severely damaged or hazardous, on the other hand, will be provided with shelter repair materials and logistical support. Finally, returnees may also benefit from shelter repair assistance. The initial community entry period will provide an opportunity to assess the need for shelter assistance. Mercy Corps staff will assess the current housing situation of IDPs or returnees, including visiting the site to make observations of the physical space and talk with residents. If shelter assistance is a priority need identified during the process of sectoral targeting, and if authorization of use of land for housing is obtained, Mercy Corps will work with IDPs to determine their specific needs and prepare a package of assistance.
