Project Summary

The project will be aimed at supporting and improving the wellbeing of children who have been affected by GBV and will also focus on mitigating and preventing GBV on children.
The Big Smile Foundation will provide immediate psychological first aid and material assistance, including dignity kits, to 300 children affected by GBV, including children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). 300 survivors of GBV will also be provided with case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up.
Survivors will also be supported with livelihoods support through a tailored package of vocational and business skills training, mentoring and cash grants to enable the survivors to access sustainable income-generating activities and promote their socio-economic reintegration. At the community level, Street
The child will also build community-based protection and response mechanisms. This will include awareness-raising activities and training of community-based committees on GBV prevention and response principles. Community-based GBV monitoring and support groups will be established as forums for peer support and mentoring, empowering community members to facilitate awareness-raising, prevention and response activities.
These will also include establishing men's groups against GBV to engage men in combatting GBV and violence against women and challenging harmful gender attitudes.

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in North East Nigeria is primarily a protection crisis, which has exposedcivilians to violence, exploitation and abuse, including gender-based violence. Gender basedis widespread, and has been aggravated by displacement, limited access toservices, disruption of livelihoods as well as harmful socio-cultural practices. Anestimated 1.3 million people are in need of GBV support across the three worst-affectedstates of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa in 2020. Over 1 million of these are girls and women,and over 756,000 are children. These statistics show the particular vulnerability of female headed households and adolescent girls, in particular, to sexual exploitation and abuse -including survival sex, early marriage and forced prostitution. Women and children whohave been abducted by armed groups may have endured rape, sexual abuse or forcedmarriage, and often face high levels of stigmatisation after their release. Childrenassociated with armed groups (CAAG) and unaccompanied and separated children(UASC) are therefore particularly vulnerable. Stigmatisation can also contribute toeconomic marginalisation, making it difficult for survivors to rebuilt their livelihoods andreintegrate fully into the community. Despite the particular vulnerability of women andgirls, it is important to note that all population groups - girls, boys, women and men fromhost community, IDP and returnee communities are at-risk and in need of support. Allforms of GBV, experienced by all types of survivors, are chronically under-reported due tostigmatisation and marginalisation as well as the lack of effective reporting and responsemechanisms. Hence the importance of awareness creation and community engagementthat encourages prevention and access for support for victims of GBV. There aresignificant gaps in the humanitarian response to GBV. There is a general lack of GBVservices available, and where services are available the quality (including making oftimely referrals and follow-up of referrals made) is often low. Coordination betweendifferent partners tends to be weak, and there is also a need to strengthen coordination atthe LGA level by building the capacity of community actors and stakeholders as well aslocal partners. Borno State is the most affected; the GBV needs are categorised ascatastrophic in the LGAs of Damboa and Dikwa.
