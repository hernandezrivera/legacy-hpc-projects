Project Summary

**Mercy-USA for Aid and Development in this project will support the most vulnerable people in NW Syria in many components:
1-Provision of better dignified shelter solution by piloting innovative shelter approach which comprises steel metal frame and sandwich panels (total shelter unit size21.8 sq. meter) in camps in Idleb sub-district to accommodate 335 HH, 1710 individuals (Men 203- Women 301- Boys 586- Girls 620) with ramp in each shelter unit ensure accessibility for people with disabilities in all shelter units and facilities, privacy, and safety for all vulnerable groups.
Gravelling will be provided to the shelter ground with thickness of 15 cm with compaction and humidification for appropriate installation for shelter units
Furthermore, Mercy-USA will install 16 special ceramic latrines from the Turkish type with handrails (8 for males and 8 for females) for persons with disability to access the facilities.
Shelter units will be purchased from Turkish Red Crescent (TRC) and will be delivered in packs to Syria. The shelter units will be installed by daily workers using cash for work. On the other hand, infrastructure preparations including gravelling, ramps, PWD latrines and handrails will be done through contractor
2-Cash for winter (150 USD) for each HH one-off during January and February 2022 in Afrin sub-district in addition to new shelter approach beneficiaries in the same camp in Idleb sub-district.
Number of beneficiaries for cash for winter who will be targeted will be 2500 HH - 13,618 individuals (Men 1613- Women 2400- Boys 4668- Girls 4937)
3-Provision of in-kind eco-friendly biofuel, and stove kits (in line with SNFI activity 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance) in Maarrat Tmasrin, Idleb, Dana, Jandairis sub-districts from December 2022 until February 2023
Number of beneficiaries for in-kind fuel: 10,000 HH - 50,000 individuals (Men 6000- Women 9000- Boys 17000- Girls 18000)
Total number of beneficiaries who will be targeted in 2022 is 12,500HH - 63,618 individuals (Men 7,613- Women 11,400- Boys 21,668- Girls 22,937) from them 10,000 HH who received one round of in-kind fuel in December 2022 will receive additional two rounds during January and February 2023

4 - Needs

Over 2 million IDPs live in last resort sites, the vast majority in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates, in particularly inadequate, temporary shelters such as tents and makeshift shelters which provide limited protection against the elements and require frequent replacement and/or repair (Syria HNO 2022 draft) According to Mobility and Needs Monitoring report which was launched by HNAP in October 2021:**Total population in NW Syria 4.5 million people from them 2.8 million IDPs**The priority is shelter for 57% of IDPs in Idleb sub-district**The priority is NFI for 51% of IDPs in Afrin sub-district Based on Daily Emergency Needs Tracking weekly bulletin: 8 November-14 November 2021:73% of Households without sufficient fuel for heating95% in 61 communities, KIs reported that the preferred modality of assistance for newly-arrived IDPs was cash assistance. Based on IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM): As of December 2021, the CCCM database includes 1,398 IDP sites that host 1,694,712 IDPs or 321,085 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 45,997 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA Risks:•Overcrowded during the Fuel distributions/ redemption which may cause SEA•Mixed distribution lines for males and females•All distribution staff are males•The distribution is not accessible to all gender groups•No shared clear information about timing and content of distribution•No clear procedure regarding loss of card/voucher•Taxation on the assistance•No complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in place Risk mitigation:•Ensure extend distribution days to avoid overcrowding•Separate lines for males and females in all distribution/redemption process •Ensure gender balance in the hiring process, ideally to be 50%of the team are female•The distribution sites will be chosen to be accessible to all gender groups especially female HH and disables•Clear information about timing, contents, and procedures about loss of cards/vouchers of the distribution will be shared with all beneficiaries beforehand and will be existed in the banners in the distribution sites•Sensitization about the scope of the project to all communities with highlighting “This assistance is Free” and this statement will be printed on the beneficiary cards.•Different CFM channels will be in place such as WhatsApp, hotline, email, phone as well as physically by MEAL team.•PSEA awareness materials (such as posters and pamphlets) and reporting hotline will be visible to beneficiaries at the distribution site and/or are included in distribution kits.•Shelters with doorways ramps for PWD
