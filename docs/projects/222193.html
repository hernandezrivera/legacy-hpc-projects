Project Summary

In this project, Reach Africa Organization (RAO) will conduct in-kind and conditional Cash Based Interventions to meet shelter and nonfood items needs of internally displaced persons, vulnerable host communities and returnees in Ayod, Uror, Twic East and Duk Counties in Jonglei State. The four counties are classified as severity 1 or 2 in terms of acute humanitarian needs. The overall aim of the intervention is to increase access to safe, appropriate, protective and lifesaving shelter and non-food items to the affected persons, and create pathways to local shelter and nonfood items durable solutions in the longer term. A total of 37,303 individuals (6,217 Households) will benefit through the intervention in 12 months in the four counties (Ayod 4,849 individuals, Duk 18,278 individuals, Twic East 6,342 individuals and Uror 7,834 individuals). Due to availability of functional markets in some of the areas in the four counties, especially for communities living close to county headquarters, RAO will map and collaborate with local vendors to implement Cash Voucher Assistance to stimulate local markets. As such, the assistance will be provided as follows: 70% in-kind assistance and 30% conditional cash. Reach Africa Organization is an SNFIs partner for Twic East, a focal point in Uror and has conducted SNFIs interventions courtesy of Rapid Response Fund in Uror and Ayod Counties. RAO is conducting Peace Building, Vocational and Business skills training and livelihood support in Uror, Duk and Ayod Counties. This allows the organization to integrate the response with other sectors to ensure a holistic response. In sectors where the organization has no programs in the targeted counties, it will work with other partners in Nutrition, Health, WASH, CCM, Food Security, Mine Action, Housing Land and Property, protection and food security to maximize the integration. To ensure accountability to affected communities, all activities will be implemented, monitored and evaluated closely with the Shelter and Non Food Items Cluster (at national, state and county level), Internally Displaced Persons, returnees and host community representatives, community leaders and local authorities and Cash Working Group for technical guidance, quality assurance, community support and accountability to affected persons. Before and during the response, Reach Africa Organization will observe and adhere to the rights and protection of the participants, its safeguarding (PSEA, Gender, Child Protection, conflict of interest and antifraud) policies, Do No Harm Principle and its commitment to core humanitarian standards. All project activities and reports will be coordinated through the S-NFIs focal points at State and National levels, including 5Ws reporting. Lesson learnt and success stories will be compiled and disseminated among stakeholders to inform current and future interventions and improve on impact.

1. Needs

Floods, poverty, inflow of returnees, and conflict induced crisis have triggered food and SNFIs needs in Jonglei State. According to IPC report for South Sudan Published on 18 November 2024, 727,000 and 349,000 people are projected to remain in crisis and emergency food insecurity or worse between December 2024 and March 2025 respectively. Between April and July 2025, 775,000 and 511,000 people will face crisis and emergency food insecurity respectively. During the same period 22,000 people are projected to slide to humanitarian catastrophe. In Ayod, 80,000 and, 20,000 people respectively are projected to be in crisis and emergency food insecurity between December 2024 and March 2025. Food situation in the county will worsen in April and July 2025 with 90,000 and 40,000 people facing crisis and emergency food insecurity. Cumulatively, Uror, Duk and Twic East will have 534,000 in crisis and emergency phase in September 2024 to July 2025, with Uror County projected to register 10,000 individuals in Humanitarian Catastrophe. Beyond food insecurity, floods have wreaked havoc in the four counties in the months of August to December 2024. As at October 2024, Twic East County had registered 55,300 individuals who were negatively impacted by the floods, out of which 27,994 and 16,007 were women and children respectively this is according to an IRNA conducted by RAO and partners on 19-20 Sept 2024. During the same period, Duk and Uror counties registered 17,000 and 22,300 individuals affected by floods respectively, while Ayod had 26,700 persons based on IRNA conducted by RAO and partners on the 3rd to 6th September 2024. The same IRNAs reported that in Ayod, Uror and Twic East IDPs and returnees residing in the counties identified top three immediate needs to include access to food, shelter and non-food items to re-establish themselves. For example, due to prevalence and risks of malaria during and after the rainy season, returnees have prioritized mosquito nets as an important item for lactating and pregnant mothers, elderly and sick persons. Assessment teams observed overcrowded shelters due to shortage of shelters with five to seven members occupying a single shelter. Males, Females and children were observed sharing the same shelter that were not dis-aggregated, compromising safety and privacy and protection of family members, more so women, girls and children. Those without shelters were observed sleeping with no blankets and exposing themselves to elements of weather and mosquito bites. Without shelter materials, the risks associated with overcrowding will continue to persist in the longer term. Due to floods, women and girls were observed walking long distances in search of dry land and where they can harvest grass and poles. The women and girls raised protection concerns every time they went to the forest to fetch construction materials. According to the feedback received from women and girls, providing shelter materials such as plastic sheets will not only meet immediate shelter needs of families but will also help in reducing protection risks associated with sleeping in the open, overcrowded shelters and walking long distances into the forest in search of construction materials. Further, IDPs and returnees who lost basic cooking items were observed waiting on others to finish cooking so that they can borrow the items. Reports of delayed cooking and conflict resulting from delayed return of the items were reported in more than one instance during the assessments. In their current status, flood, conflict and food insecurity affected IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities have no sources of income they can use to buy shelter and nonfood items. They are forced to rely on humanitarian actors to acquire the materials. In light of these acute needs, there is need to provide in kind as well as cash assistance for the most affected populations to access SNFIs materials in Uror, Ayod, Duk and Twic East Counties.

2. Project activities

Activity 1.0: Inception, midterm and end of project meetings. RAO will conduct an inception meeting with community leaders and members from all the four counties of Ayod, Uror, Twic East and Duk counties to provide accurate information about the project objectives, aims, activities, selection criteria, modality of intervention and the kind of assistance to be provided. Midterm meeting with the same participants will be conducted to review progress on implementation and address challenges. A close out meeting will be conducted to end the project. A PIC comprising of 15 individuals in each of the counties will be formed to collaborate closely with the staff to efficiently execute the SNFIs response. A CRFM will be activated to facilitate safe and timely submission of complaints and provision of feedback. 2.0: Selection of participants RAO, PIC and locally hired enumerators will conduct identification, registration, and selection of 37,303 participants across 4 select counties using a pre-agreed selection criterion, 70% in-kind assistance and 30% conditional cash. Therefore, the proposed project activities are as follows: Enumerators and project implementation team will jointly conduct verification of needs and selection of the participants. Selection criteria will include the most vulnerable returnees, IDPs and host community with priority being given to elderly persons, Un accompanied and Separated children, severely sick persons, PLWDs and women headed HHS and youths. 3.0. Collection and distribution of SNFIs In kind items will be collected from the Core Pipeline and transported by road, air and rivers to RAO’s warehouses in Duk, Uror, Ayod and Twic East awaiting distribution. During distribution, locally hired enumerators will conduct communication and sensitization on amount, type of assistance, distribution days and locations where the assistance will be delivered from to ensure no one misses on SNFIs. A register developed during registration and selection of participants plus the tokens issued to selected participants will be utilized to verify participants during distribution. RAO’s Safeguarding officer will ensure that distribution sites are selected in consultation with community members. Distribution will be done during light hours and access to shade, water and latrine points will be ensured. Activity 4.0: Cash Voucher AssistanceRAO in consultation with project committee and the cluster will conduct safety audit to identify safety concerns associated with Cash Assistance. Thereafter, consultations on the most appropriate cash and voucher delivery mechanism will be conducted. And if use of intermediaries (vendors) will be agreed upon RAO will conduct a mapping exercise to identify vendors who will participate in the CVA. Fraud and tamper proof Cash Vouchers with a transfer value of USD34 will be generated and distributed to selected participants for redemption in the markets. This cash will allow project participants to build and or rehabilitate their own homes with materials bought from the local community. Reach Africa Organization will conduct a mapping exercise in the targeted disaster-prone counties with the purpose of helping RAO identify and map key local and regional markets, vendors and mobile money agents. RAO will also select representatives of project participants mainly the youth, leaders and local constructors to be trained in Resilient Construction Techniques that are context specific5.0: PDM RAO will conduct a PDM 14 days after in-kind distribution and Cash Voucher Assistance to gather information on timeliness, quality control, participation of participants, distribution methods, safeguarding and impact. A random sample of 20% of total targeted HHs/Individuals will be employed during the PDM. Data collected will be segregated according to age, sex and disability status.
