Project Summary

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of South Sudan, combined with effects of floods, lack of food security and livelihood opportunities result in the displacement of communities and the economic downturn and have exacerbated protection needs. 5.6 million people are in need of protection services across the country.
PAH is an experienced organization with broad portfolio of successfully implemented projects in various sectors. PAH will use its expertise in Protection, team of specialists and well-established cooperation with stakeholders to further provide assistance to vulnerable communities across the country. PAH approach to assistance is integrated, multi-sectoral responses meeting priority needs of the beneficiaries and mainstreaming protection operating both in mobile- and static modalities depending on the context. In frame of protection sector PAH plans to implement GP, CP and GBV activities.
PAH will engage in strengthening the monthly protection monitoring and conduct needs assessments and safety audits at the beginning of every intervention. In frame of General Protection AoR, CVA for the returnees will be granted for better resilience following the spontaneous returns along with PSS activities for the most vulnerable community members and awareness raising on broad protection issues.
In created women and girls-friendly spaces, PAH will conduct PSS activities for women and girls, including persons with disabilities as well as other activities adjusted to needs of the groups, including trainings, recreation, etc. PAH will also provide a range of prevention activities, focusing on community-based solutions, involving men, boys and youth. Capacity building on protection topics will be conducted along IGA trainings for GBV survivors and persons at high risk, including fuel efficient stoves. Dignity kits will be provided to women and girls. For GBV survivors PAH will ensure access to information, effective referral and transport services to access health care and psycho-social services. Case Management services will be provided to both GBV survivors and persons at high risk and CVA or in-kind support will be granted.
Child Protection activities will comprise PSS support in Child Friendly Spaces, encourage learning and play along with individual counseling as well as support for parents and schools by trainings and provision of recreation materials. Case Management is another activity that will support the most vulnerable children, including family tracing and reunification and cash- or in-kind support where appropriate. Referral pathways will be updated and children referred to specialized services. Community based structures will be at the center of the intervention with capacity building and awareness sessions as well as establishment of help-desk for children and caregivers.
Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of Protection, PAH combines the described activities with other sectors in frame of own multi-sectoral responses or in close cooperation with other partners. Especially Education, GBV and Child Protection will be integrated. PAH mainstreams protection across Education, Shelter NFIs, FSL and WASH and integrates approach to cash programming across all clusters. PAH will ensure meaningful access to services for men and women, including people with disabilities and other special needs, the elderly and children. PAH team will encourage community participation, establish safe and accessible feedback mechanism and promote resilient approaches. Safety and dignity of the beneficiaries will be at the center of interventions.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1 - Needs

According to the latest HNO - 2022, more than 8.9 million people, including 4.7 million children, need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance in South Sudan with 5 counties being classified as catastrophic inter-sectoral severity level. 5.6m people require protection assistance. 2.9m children need critical child protection support and 2.6m people risk gender-based violence. Persons with disabilities in need of assistant account to 1.3m. The protection crises in South Sudan is multi-layered with armed conflict, heavy flooding, sub-national violence, and others. According to 2023 Intercluster Severity Scales for Humanitarian Needs Overview vast majority of the country (67 out of 78 counties) was classified as extreme severity plus 1 county was classified as catastrophe. Limited capacity of local governments, including lack of access to resources, further exacerbates the situation of the vulnerable communities as violence is widespread. The community safety nets are weakening and thus children, persons with special needs and disabilities, women and other most vulnerable groups remain unheard. Strengthening the community-based mechanisms is of utmost importance. Floods have imposed additional protection risks, especially on women and children, caused displacement of over 616,000 people as of 30 September 2022 and destroyed essential infrastructure and livelihood opportunities. Dire food insecurity and economic crises makes people refer to the negative coping mechanisms, including intimate partner violence (IPV), forced child marriage and child labor. Based on the food security analysis (IPC Acute Food Insecurity & Malnutrition Snapshot South Sudan) 7.8 million people, are expected to experience severe food insecurity in the first half of 2023.The magnitude and severity of food insecurity is is worsening gradually in the country as in the months of September - December the figures changed as follows: 2019 4.5 million in crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above), 2020 - 6.3 million, 2022 - 6.6 million. Children are in dire need of psycho-social support, having experienced trauma and lacking proper support system. Families strained by the daily struggle have lower capacity to create a protective environment for children to develop. The case management services are crucial. In 2022 till 11 November CP partners identified and responded in 37490 cases (unaccompanied children, separated, missing and others cases). The number of child survivors of gender-based violence, indicates a strong need for integrated approach of GBV and CP. Around half of the girls get married before reaching adulthood.Gender-based violence is one of the most critical threats, especially to women and girls, however, man are also affected, especially by forced recruitment, revenge killing or abductions. Almost half of the households expressed concern about GBV and the needs are reported. Collecting firewood in the bush, conducting livelihood activities outside the settlement or accessing WASH facilities were reported among the most common situations in which people felt unsafe. Studies indicate that ca. 65% of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, and some 51% have suffered IPV. The majority of girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18. Flooding, armed conflicts and sub-national violence are escalating the risk of GBV. Yet only 6% of the community members are aware of GBV response services available. The need for more reporting mechanisms, medical support, and other services was expressed by the communities. Women and girls with disabilities are being sexually abused due to their vulnerabilities and in exchange for support leading to pregnancies and sexually-transmitted diseases with no later support from the social networks. Exact needs and existing protection gaps will be identified at the needs assessment level prior to each intervention.

3 - Project indicators

Selected indicators for each of the AoRs will allow PAH to track progress against targets and key results on a daily basis and will allow proper project management. Additionally, MEAL plan will be developed to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency and tools for data collections and responsible person. MEAL team will support the development of data collection methodologies and takes part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings will be incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. Persons with disabilities and other special needs will be captured for proper reporting. PAH will utilize sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and/or Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: # of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialised PSS and other related general protection services (Psychosocial support and access to documentation), # of people of concern to the PC receiving protective cash-based assistance in connection with safe, dignified, and voluntary return, # of identified girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities at risk who receive individual and specialized case management services that meets their unique needs, # of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities). PAH will conduct the end-line survey to compare the data from baseline and collect the feedback from the targeted population and have the lessons learnt for next activities. Despite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. PAH conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, including clusters. Post-intervention monitoring (PIM) will be done by independent PAH MEAL team across the project, as long as the location is still accessible and secure. PIMs gives a clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will give clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve the overall quality of PAH projects. Regular evaluation of the projects will ensure accountability.PAH will contribute to the protection monitoring exercise by conducting monthly monitoring in selected payams with the use of KIIs and FGDs where needed. The indicator used to track this activity is # of Payams covered by protection monitoring.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In an effort to inform cash-based interventions and better understand market dynamics PAH will monthly conduct the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) in accessible locations and will use the data to inform the project activities. Additionally PAH will conduct monthly price monitoring using cluster unified tools. PAH will conduct protection risk analysis as part of market functionality assessment. PAH is a member of the Inter-agency Cash working group.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The specific and different needs of GBV survivors and persons at high risk require individual approach, which is possible when the intervention is cash-based as well as allows payment for services, e.g. transportation to the specialized services. Similarly, for the children supported by case management, the needs will be very individual. The cash or voucher assistance will be used only after careful assessment of the market functionality. Additionally, an in-depth protection risk assessment will be done prior to the decision for either cash, voucher or in-kind assistance. In frame of CVA for returnees, PAH will conduct the market functionality assessment, including protection risk analysis, to give information on cost-effectiveness of the activity and determine the delivery mechanism. CVA supports local economy and empower the beneficiaries to purchase items of their choice. The vouchers are planned to be used as part of dignity kits support for women and girls.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In case of children with disabilities, with limited mobility, PAH officers will visit them directly to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate parents, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons. People with disability will be integrated in all the activities of the projects. The main examples are: In the Protection monitoring it will be checked whether the risk is felt by certain groups such as people with disabilities; community awareness sessions will be conducted in places accessible for people with disabilities, while conducting safety audits, FGDs etc an emphasis will be put on the inclusion of people living with specific needs such as people living with a different disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through a institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. As the GBV cases are often unreported, PAH officers will engage the caseworkers from the community and will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage effective psychosocial support. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based caseworkers in GBV referrals, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge. To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project - volunteers. PAH has a separate document - PAH Policy concerning Protection against Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Sexual Harassment. PSEA Policy is not just an internal policy of the Polish Humanitarian Action, but that, first of all, its principles are known to our beneficiaries. Heads of Mission together with MEAL Managers / Coordinators are responsible for creating a mechanism providing feedback from beneficiaries and the local community, so that they are informed about the threats, their rights and the possibility of filing a complaint in any case of sexual exploitation and abuse. It is done in parallel with the handling of other types of complaints by MEAL. Anyone can report abuse, incident or concern regarding what s/he experienced or witnessed via email complaints@pah.org.pl, by phone or directly to the PAH Management Board or Compliance Manager. Any form of complaint and/or information is suitable. Every PAH employee/volunteer is obliged to report an incident verbally or in writing to his immediate supervisor, HR Team or Compliance Manager at PAH to complaints@pah.org.pl. A complaint about sexual abuse may be made personally or anonymously. Each will be examined but it is obvious that an anonymous complaint and the lack of direct access to the complainant will significantly complicate our proceedings and may lead to unsatisfactory solutions. Each complaint or concern on sexual abuse, exploitation or intrusion submitted to PAH will be treated very seriously. Actions should be taken immediately, without undue delay, and steps which were taken should be announced within 3 working days. If someone’s life is in danger or the matter relates in any way to a child at risk, a decision may be taken by the Polish Humanitarian Action to report the case to local authorities and/or implement its security procedures (crisis management).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback is kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of Senior Management, if necessary, conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. Protection risks are most often higher for marginalized/minority groups’ members, so they will be the main focus in the project. All vulnerable groups will be targeted with attention to their needs and capacities. PAH has the flexibility to use innovative solutions to meet those needs, to ensure inclusion and empowerment. Complaint and feedback mechanisms are always in place to find out about any gaps and address them. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, inc. logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards.
