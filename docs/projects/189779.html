Project Summary

Communities in Uror and Nyirol live in temporary mud-thatched houses with tarpaulin roofs, few others put up in iron sheets houses. Efforts to access basic needs including WASH have been on slow progress before and after December 2013 crisis. Uror and Nyirol communities have been accessing clean water only from one borehole established that has already broken down. According to recent field visit by CHO staff, the borehole has however become dysfunctional due to mechanical breakdown has remained unrepaired till now. During CHO’s consultative meeting with Uror and Nyirol leaders and representatives, communities’ representatives suggested provision of water as their first priority. Health and education facilities fall in the second priority. The leaders and representatives pledged to contribute land space for borehole drilling. The combination of Host communities, internal displacement, increasing number of returnees within an austerity environment, is putting tremendous burden on already overstretched service delivery systems. Whereas majority of returnees and IDPs are integrated into the host communities all over the area, a significant portion are still leaving in churches requiring additional and significant resources to be put in place for a comprehensive minimum package of services. According to local authority in the areas, there is an estimated 1,500HH meaning the existing borehole serves approximately 10,000 persons, majority are women and children.
Approximately 10,000 men, women and children from the Host communities, IDPs and returnees will benefit directly and indirectly; communities in surrounding areas will benefit from the reduced pressure on water points. An adapted PHAST process will also target IDPs, Host Communities and returning communities where a borehole will be situated. CHO will create awareness and support construction of household latrines as demand grows. Approximately 10,000 individuals will benefit as it is expected that up to 3 households would share each latrine. Benefiting communities will be selected in coordination with local authorities and leaders. With consultation of each benefiting Community, the members of the community will choose borehole location. CHO will facilitate those decisions to ensure consideration of constraints such as accessibility for the elderly, physically disabled and protection for women and children is incorporated. Technical team will be engaged to provide technical advice on community contributed land spaces for boreholes drilling in term of space likelihood to strike portable water. CHO will use technical advice to guide community in the process of determining final location of borehole in each community.

1 - Needs

Communities in Uror and Nyirol live in temporary mud-thatched houses with tarpaulin roofs, few others put up in iron sheets houses. Efforts to access basic needs including WASH have been on slow progress before and after December 2013 crisis. Uror and Nyirol communities have been accessing clean water only from one borehole established that has already broken down. According to recent field visit by CHO staff, the borehole has however become dysfunctional due to mechanical breakdown has remained unrepaired till now. During CHO’s consultative meeting with Uror and Nyirol leaders and representatives, communities’ representatives suggested provision of water as their first priority. Health and education facilities fall in the second priority. The leaders and representatives pledged to contribute land space for borehole drilling. The combination of Host communities, internal displacement, increasing number of returnees within an austerity environment, is putting tremendous burden on already overstretched service delivery systems. Whereas majority of returnees and IDPs are integrated into the host communities all over the area, a significant portion are still leaving in churches requiring additional and significant resources to be put in place for a comprehensive minimum package of services. According to local authority in the areas, there is an estimated 1,500HH meaning the existing borehole serves approximately 10,000 persons, majority are women and children. This indicates that HH do not have access to sufficient water due to both distances from the water point and long waiting hours at the water points. This long hours waiting at water point exposes women sometimes at risk of punishment from their partnership for returning home empty- handed or returning late after waiting in line for hours. In addition, school-age girls who must spend long time collecting water are at a higher risk of missing and/or not attending school, which limit their future opportunity. Apart from the single and dysfunctional boreholes, there are small ponds and seasonal rivers that dry within one month after the end of the rains. The other mean of getting water in Uror and Nyirol is through trucked water that is not regular in the area. Many families do not afford water from the trucks and this discouraged truck water sellers from regular delivery of this essential commodity in the area. In addition to this, trucked water is drawn directly from River Nile and delivers to consumers without any form of treatment. There is need to provide households with water purifiers to help them prevent or mitigate water related disease outbreak such as cholera and acute water diarrhea (AWD) among others. In an effort to promote sanitation and hygiene in the community, One quarter of Uror and Nyirol families dug latrine pits as an attempt to make latrine for household use but these families could not afford latrine equipment at the markets due to high inflation. During rainy season of 2015, the pits collapsed (see picture at right on the next page). Currently, the entire community uses open defecation. This practice expose members of the families especially children at high risks of consuming contaminated food and water from poor sanitary environment. The use of open defecation also affects women directly as it affects their dignity and privacy; Women and girls to travel unsafe areas or wait until nightfall to relieve themselves. Child mortality rates and poor maternal health is experience in the area due to malnutrition and diseases related to poor water and sanitation management. According to report from local authorities in Uror and Nyirol, two children were born in 2015 with abnormalities. Both children died in the same year of birth. Several others children suffer from chronicle illness (falling sick frequently, becoming malnourished. Other common diseases include skin diseases, eye infections and whooping cough among others).

2 - Project activities

Activity 1.1: Dissemination of WASH IEC Materials. CHO proposed to disseminate of hygiene and sanitation promotion information to reduce scale of potential outbreak of WASH related diseases e.g Acute water Diarrhea, skin infection disease, and cholera mitigation. CHO will obtain adaptable WASH IEC materials from National WASH cluster and stick the posters on wall/fence/trees at public gathering point. Simple interpretation of the illustrated images will be done to help targeted communities understand images messages. Activity 1.2: Distribution of Aqua tabs. As Uror and Nyirol still lacks functional boreholes, there is need to continue targeted distribution of water purification tablets to vulnerable HH for the treatment of unpurified water drawn directly from ponds/seasonal rivers and the river Nile. Activity 1.3: Drill boreholes to create water point. With support from partner, CHO intend to facilitate drilling of at least three boreholes to provide access to safe water for vulnerable individuals and families. There is also high need to incorporate converting of motorized borehole to solar power system/hand pump for easy sustainability and constantly making water available at in all communities of Uror and Nyirol. Activity 1.4: Establish and train 48 members WMC. CHO will establish and train WMC members from the 3 communities to operate, maintain and manage boreholes. The selection of the WMC members will be the responsibility of the local leaders and community as CHO will provide guidelines for the selection process with gender consideration. Training will include safe water, sanitation and hygiene, operation and maintenance of WASH facilities and management including cost recovery of the water points that does-no-harm to the beneficiaries. The WMC will select maximum 0f twelve members to be trained as pump/plumber mechanics. Mechanics will be provided with standard toolkits after the training to carry on their roles after CHO exit. Activity 1.5: Develop Community Water Safety Plans with WMC. CHO will adapt Water Safety Plan training that follows the PHAST process. The aim is to encourage the practice of maintaining safe water for drinking from source, through transport, to point of use is appropriately apply. CHO will also seek to provide HH with water purification tablets and provide awareness on the use. Activity 1.6: Support and monitor trained WMC throughout project period. CHO will provide continued support and monitoring to the trained WMC through regular visits and meetings, discussion and record progress against action plans, address specific issues and problems and provide short refresher training courses.
