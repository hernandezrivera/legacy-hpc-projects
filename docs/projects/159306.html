Project Summary

The aim of the project is to provide quality and inclusive basic education to conflict/crisis
affected children in Somalia. Focus will be made on improving the enrollment and
retention of affected children through the provision of quality and inclusive basic
education and this includes having access to a safe and protective learning environment.
Conflict / crisis affected children are often subject to drop and remain out school as they
face difficulty to rejoining school because livelihood becoming a pressing priority. The
project will adopt an integrated approach to promoting safe, inclusive and quality learning
opportunities for affected children. This includes the construction of temporary learning
spaces, as well as semi-permanent and permanent classrooms
rehabilitation/construction, lifesaving school meals, and the construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in school.To support in reintegration of returnees and IDPs NRC will rehabilitate and construct permanent classroom in schools, in order to increase the school absorption capacity,and to advance durable solutions.
This includes, the rehabilitation of water storage facilities, construction of water tanks,
drinking water supply, gender segregated latrines and hygiene and water service
promotions. in order to improve the quality of education, the project will provide
pedagogical Skills training, DRR and psychosocial support to teachers, including provision
of incentive for teachers. The project will support a total of 40,946 (20,476 are girls) crisisa ffected
children with scholastic materials and uniforms. Moreover, CECs, head teachers in school’s
management, community members and key staff within the ministries of Education in
Somalia will have their capacity built in in planning and responding to education in
emergencies, as well as resilience building of communities.In total, the project will reach 42,796 direct beneficiaries with 27,379 displaced beneficiaries being reached and 15,417 non displaced beneficiaries being reached.
Beneficiaries will be selected through participatory and predetermined selection criteria
prioritizing the most affected and needy people. Gender will be mainstreamed into the
project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and
girls during the design of activities. Fifty percent of the total beneficiaries will be female.
The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training.
Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.
NRC will ensure adequate participation of the project by the target communities. Besides,
Monitoring, evaluation and reporting plans and tools will be developed. Information will be
collected on regular basis using Koobo toolbox and analyzed so that achievement is
measured and changes made when necessary.

Project Activities

Roughly 2.6 million children and adolescents have enrolled in primary education across Somalia between 2011 and 2018 (UNICEF). While this is impressive progress in enrollment, the national GER for primary education has remained low. According to the 2015/16 UNFPA Population Estimation Survey (PESS) , the Gross Enrollment Rate (GER) for primary school in Somalia is for primary education 30% (55.4% in Puntland, 44.3 in Somaliland and 20% in South Central) and 26.5% for secondary education. Despite efforts to bridge gender gaps with regard to access to education, persistent inequalities remain in several regions and at different levels of education. According to the PESS, gender disparities show that men have more access to education. In primary schools, the ratio of girls to boys enrolled is equal in rural areas (GPI: 1) and in IDP camps (GPI: 1.002). However, among the urban and nomadic populations, the gender disparity is in favour of boys (urban GPI: 0.950 and nomadic population: 0.887). The gender disparities further illustrate that boys generally have more access to education at secondary level and tertiary levels nomadic communities. Another multi-sectoral assessment conducted in 2018 by NRC, Concern and Finchurch Aid in locations across Somalia, the schools assessed were in bad situation and needed immediate assistance. Despite the fact that majority (93%) of them were functioning, at district level, only eight schools were operational which make up 68%. Close to two-thirds of enrolled students were present, attending school at the time of the assessment - Boys 74% and Girls 71%. However, there was general impression that school attendance was strained. 33% of the schools were over-filled from influx of IDP children displaced by the emergencies.More than 2.2 million people face acute levels of food insecurity, 1.8 million school-aged children do not have access to sustained learning, and 2.64 million people are internally displaced (FSNAU).Objective 1: Children and youth affected by emergencies (IDPs, and vulnerable communities) have continued access to inclusive and quality Education in safe, protective learning environment. Mitigation Measures

Communities are forced to leave their place of residence to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor)NRC will employ the following mitigation measures: i. NRC will ensure that children in hard to reach areas have access to education. NRC has mapped out hard to reach areas in Somalia as guided by ACAPS guidelines, and will use the maps to reach more children in hard to reach areas. ii. NRC education team will endeavor to work with other sectors and partners to integrate interventions, and assistance, in order to ensure that communities do not leave in search of humanitarian assistance.iii. NRC will conduct frequent community engagements where possible, hold separate meetings among households clusters to understand the different education needs and priorities for each member of the family. iv. NRC will also set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all.v. NRC will lead in advocating for communities that are marginalized by virtue of conflicts to receive timely and appropriate humanitarian assistance. Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processesi. NRC will ensure the participation and proportionate representation from men, women, boys and girls older persons, persons with disabilities, minority communities in all consultation and planning meeting and that their opinions are represented.ii. NRC will involve women and girls in siting locations for communal facilities considering their security and safety.iii. Regular monitoring and discussions will be made with women and girls to hear their concerns and action will be provided quickly.iv. Gender sensitive complaints, reporting and feedback mechanism will be established. Where necessary, the CRFM processes will be anonymous. v. NRC will conduct frequent individual assesments for women, boys and girls to understand the different education needs and priorities. Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and servicesi. NRC will ensure that amenities are constructed near beneficiaries, to avoid women and girls walking long distances.ii. Conduct in depth consultations to understand factors that could pose security risk to beneficiaries, e.g unwanted visibility on facilities.iii. NRC will lead in advocating for communities that are marginalized by virtue of access to receive timely and appropriate humanitarian assistance. iv. Design of communal amenities such as WASH facilities will be gender sensitive. Women and girls will have separate latrines from men and boys. NRC will also ensure that the design of education facilities preserves the safety and dignity of girls and boys.v. NRC will conduct security risk assessments, and advise on appropriate assistance based on the risks.

Please provide further explanation

The project will contribute to durable solutions efforts particularly in locations where the government has shown keen interest in locally integrating internally displaced people. The support by the government offers a protection environment that if well utilized will see many IDPs reintegrated. Through the integrated approach, NRC will provide responses that are geared towards durable solutions for affected populations. NRC's Education projects will be integrated with NRC's other sectors (WASH, LFS, CCCM, Shelter and Protection) and will adopt area based integrated programming approach to have maximum impact and have more sustainability, resilience and durability. NRC will also advocate through the Education cluster, other available coordination mechanisms and local authorities to mobilize different organizations to work in the same areas to achieve the goal of integration, resilience and durable solutions. Capacity building to teachers and CECs ensure that the national and local authorities have requisite knowledge and capacity to provide services for displacement affected children and host communities. Rehabilitation /construction of existing schools and wash facilities, gender segregated facilities, provision of sanitary kits to girls, scholastic materials, teaching and learning materials, conducting school enrollment campaigns, organizing teachers training supporting teachers’ incentive in the targeted schools, also go towards the realization of durable solutions. Construction and rehabilitation of permanent structures in areas where IDPs are locally integrated will increase the capacity of schools to absorb more learners.
