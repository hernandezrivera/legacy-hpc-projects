Project Summary

Phenylketonuria (commonly known as PKU) is an inherited disorder amongst the group of inborn errors of metabolism. In this disease, blood levels of amino-acid “phenylalanine” which is a building block of protein are increased. It is found in all proteins and in some other food items. If PKU is not early dected and nutritionally managed, phenylalanine can build up to harmful levels in the body, causing permanent intellectual disability and other serious health problems including
The signs and symptoms of PKU vary from mild to severe. The most severe form of this disorder is known as classic PKU. Infants with classic PKU appear normal until they are a few months old, seizures, delayed development, behavioral problems, and psychiatric disorders.
The occurrence of PKU varies among ethnic groups and geographic regions worldwide. In the United States, PKU occurs as 1 in 10,000 to 15,000 newborns. Most cases of PKU are detected shortly after birth by newborn screening, and managment is started promptly. As a result, the severe signs and symptoms of classic PKU are rarely seen.
As a specialized NGO in child’s health, growth and nutrition within Primary Health Care (PHC) approach practiced in the community and in Health & Nutrition Care centers (HNCC) and settings. Since 1984, AEI plays a major role in preventive and therapeutic management of child malnutrition and growth failure besides guidance and mobilization of the Palestinian community towards appropriate road to health.
Ard El Insan provides nutritional and health services to the vulnerable, neediest and marginalized children under age five. Mothers and families of targeted children are also enrolled (as direct or indirect beneficiaries) into those activities through AEI's HNCC based in Gaza and Khanyounis cities and also at community household (HH) activities or other community settings such as Community Based Organizations (CBOs).
Thematic strategic approach is carried out continuously through educational health & nutrition services to beneficiaries in all targeted areas. This has been achieved through adopting consistent technical intervention strategies in; nutrition therapy, medical intervention, counseling, health, psychological support, food security, nutrition education, promotion of breast-feeding, emergency medical relief intervention, capacity building, child protection & advocacy, exchange of information. Within the same context of work AEI adopts evidence based interventions based on researches conducted annually in varieties of health, nutrition, sanitation, hygiene, environment and other subjects.
Furthermore, AEI has developed community outreach program targeting vulnerable areas. The community main interventions include community health and nutrition mobilization, empowerment of local resources, community awareness, child's growth monitoring, referral of eligible cases for management as relevant, networking and capacity building.
Since 1988, AEI through specialty in management of nutritional disorders including nutrition related non communicable diseases such as the PKU, celiac diseases, micronutrient deficiency and others had been providing special care to patients of PKU.

1 - Needs

The project is targeting a priority group for life saving and prevention of intellectual retardation of PKU disease patients in Gaza strip. According to the Ministry of Health, the number of people with PKU in Gaza is approximately 232 and this number increases each year.In the heart of the Gaza Strip, live children born with PKU (phenyl keton uria), a genetic disease that wreaks havoc on their bodies. these children, were diagnosed with PKU about a week after birth. It is a serious condition everywhere, but particularly in Gaza, where specialty medicines and food needs are in short supply..Children with PKU have difficulty metabolizing an amino acid found in protein called phenylalanine. The amino acid is central to the function of the body’s nervous system. Without proper treatment, phenylalanine accumulates, especially around the brain, causing profound damage. The consequences include developmental delays beginning at an early age, becoming more severe with time. Until recently, the test for PKU wasn’t available in Gaza. Some kids suffered mental retardation or died at the age of 10 without being diagnosed.” Now the test is available in Gaza and administered within the first week of a child’s birth. There is no cure for PKU. However, it can be controlled with a protein-restricted diet, low in foods like meat, poultry, fish, eggs, legumes, bread, milk and cheese. They get the nutrients they need from protein by drinking a phenyl-free milk and other such custom foods. Regular blood tests determine whether phenyl levels in the blood are within a safe range.This is when life for PKU children in Gaza becomes tenuous, since the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank has said since March 2017 that it cannot afford to send in phenyl-free foods. Months later, several charities and private institutions began to work together to bring the phenyl-free food into Gaza. However, the amounts were small and the price was high. No matter how old the children become they will never be able to live without it. In fact, as they grow older, they need to drink larger amounts of the milk to make up for the nutrients their diet lacks. . However, the blockade, the lack of financial support from the Palestinian Authority and the rarity of the disorder have remained challenges. Depression, lack of concentration, anxiety, tiredness, mild learning difficulties and problems with behavior are some of the Symptoms if PKU is not managed properly ,not to forget the major impact on the brain development and other severe symptoms . As a result of a focus group discussion with PKU patients care givers , the basic needs of those vulnerable individuals are provision of phenyl free milk and food items , psychological support for the patients and their family members, rising awareness about the disease among health service providers, positions and community members, advocate patients and care givers about proper ways of dealing with the disease. The objectives of the project is to: secure qualitative feeding pattern of infants , children and adults affected with PKU , to enhance better qualitative health and nutritional knowledge among patients and care givers and their improved practices with regards to PKU.to promote and advocate PKU at national level. according to the MOH statistic data there are 227 patient of PKU where 113 are male; 84 < 18 years old ,29 >18 years old , 114 are female; 32<18 years old ,82>18 years old.a focus group was conducted to 10 caregivers of PKU patients (6 women ,4 men ) . they come to a conclusion of the following needs : Medical teams and service providers should know about the PKU . Cooking lessons for the care givers to practice making bakery and other foods from the Gluten free flower. advocate about PKU national wise. sustainable provision of Phenyl free milk to all PKU case within the criteria, noted that 64 cases are less than 5 years old and the project will follow them for growth monitoring and developing.

2 - Activities

1- Follow up 100 % of the already registered 227 PKU cases and open new medical files for the diagnosed new cases for future support if any. Follow up visits are planned as once monthly over 12 months period, during which;• Assessment of general health condition• Assessment of patient's mother knowledge regarding PKU• Assessment of nutritional status and compliance to (Phenylalanine free formula).• Assessment of neurological and developmental milestones.• Monthly provision of Phenylalanine free formula for patients according to daily needs for food intake.• One individual counseling each month over 12 months period.• Sharing in 3 occasions of nutritional health group counselling as once each two months per mother/care giver.• Home visits and contacting the defaulters or non-responding or less improving cases.• Referral when needed to other health specialists\facilities.distribution of handbooks and educational materials to beneficiaries. 2- . capacity building of service providers regarding PKU disease . • training of( 2 )health educators on PKU for 12 hours. • training of Physicians and Nurses(10) at health care centers dealing with PKU patients on PKU for 12 hours.• Training of Physicians and Nurses from primary health care to increase their knowledge about PKU disease 3- provide Psychological and psycho-social support for patients and their families. • conducting psychological support sessions for patients and their care givers 180-227 persons .• conducting psycho- social support sessions to (180--227) patients families .
