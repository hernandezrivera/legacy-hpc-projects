Project Summary

This project was developed as a part of and aligned with the Syrian HRP 2024. It contributes to addressing the escalating needs in the Education sector in Syria, which, as outlined in FSA Sector HNO Chapter 2024, have increased due to the protracted crisis and 2023 earthquakes with 6.9 million children 5-17 (F 46%, M 54%) and 230,000 education personnel (M 52%, F46%) in need for emergency education services, including 2.45m out of school children and over a million at risk of dropping out. NWS governorates are among the highest percentages of out-of-school children OoSC (Idleb 69%, Aleppo 38%). Learning environments and quality are challenged by overcrowding, lack of furniture and supplies, need for rehabilitation and decrease in qualified teachers.
The project targets children and youth, including Early Childhood Care and Development (ECCD), in the subdistricts of Maarat Tamsrin, Idleb, Jandairis and Mhambel in Aleppo and Idleb governorates, NW Syria, which are of high PIN and severity (4 to 5). The project will cover a total of 16 schools with children from 3 to 17 years old, encompassing ECCD, basic, and secondary education, with special attention given to children with disabilities (CwDs), constituting approximately 5-7% of the total beneficiaries. The project will focus on dropouts and those at risk of dropping out of schools and IDPs, especially those residing in camps and newly and frequently displaced. The project aims to provide Formal Education (FE) to 2716 Boys and girls, Non-Formal Education (NFE) to 300 boys and girls, ECCD to 180 boys and girls, with a total of 3,196 students (M1456, F1740, CWD M118, CWD F122), and support 180 educational personnel (M82, F98), with careful consideration of gender balance and the inclusion of CwDs. The project will encompass recreational, PSS and other child protection activities and referral and will be implemented in line with INEE minimum standards.
Efforts will be made to ensure that educational facilities are conducive to learning, taking into account COVID-19 and cholera preventive measures. Additionally, the project will leverage existing resources in targeted schools, such as furniture, fuel tanks, heaters, and textbooks, to maximize cost-effectiveness and provide and missing ones as needed in addition to monthly running costs and incentives.
Specific educational programs will be tailored to address the diverse needs of OOSC, including catch-up programs, ALP, and remedial classes to mitigate risks of recruitment into armed groups, early marriage, and child labor. Collaboration with educational authorities will be sought to facilitate the transition of NFE graduates into formal education.
SDI will coordinate with Education Cluster, educational authorities, local civil authorities, local communities, and parents. The project will offer diverse curricula encompassing both FE and NFE, featuring specialized programs such as SIG and Curriculum B and will ensure achieving learning outcomes for children, and transition from NFE to FE in line with cluster guidance and recommendation.
Significant emphasis will be placed on school rehabilitation and modification efforts to ensure accessibility for all, including children with disabilities, through CWD-friendly adjustments like toilet seats, ramps and handrails. Moreover, training of teachers and staff, specialized support to CwD, recreational activities and referral for specialized services will encourage enrolment of CWDs and reach the target 5-7% of CwDs.
Effective Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) will be established within schools. Robust monitoring and evaluation mechanisms will be in place, including checklists to oversee the educational process and minimum standards in schools and WASH facilities, as well as assessments of school safety and training effectiveness through surveys and training evaluations.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Enrollment initiatives will be accompanied by Back-to-Learning campaigns, provision of recreational activities, ECCD materials, and teacher resources. Winterization package, maintenance and running costs, minimum package WASH & Cholera (enough safe water and soup, cleaning material, hygiene promotion and awareness, child-friendly gender-friendly WASH facilities), and CP (MHPSS and referral for case management) will be provided.Baseline and end-line placement tests will be conducted to gauge student progress and needs accurately. Comprehensive training sessions will be offered to educational staff, covering topics such as Teaching in Crisis Contexts (TICC), Basic Literacy and Numeracy (BLN), Child Safeguarding (CSG), PSS, SLP, Distance Education methodologies, and Safe Learning practices. Active engagement with Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) will be prioritized. School safety protocols, including fire safety measures and first aid provisions, will be implemented, along with regular teacher training and monitoring activities to ensure quality.
