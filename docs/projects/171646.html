Project Summary

In target location a huge percentage of the population still relies on humanitarian health assistance. Majority of women, girls, boys and men in target area need access to emergency health care. Neonatal, infant and child mortality remain high with communicable diseases as leading cause. AWD/cholera is prevalent enhanced by poor WASH (UNICEF 2020). High maternal mortality rate fueled by limited antenatal care (1/4 of women), low proportion of births attended by skilled attendants (1/3), limited facilities for emergency maternal care. Insecurity, poor health seeking practices, poor functioning referral systems, limited access to health services and underlying malnutrition pose major challenges to control and prevention of diseases (UNICEF 2020; FMoH 2020 ).
Kismayo has a rapidly growing population due to immigration in search of aid, therefore straining available Health services, especially in IDP. With concerted efforts, AWD/cholera prevalence has significantly reduced but prevailing poor WASH status threatens to fuel increased prevalence in case of shock. Though great improvement has been noted, significant number of women still deliver at home. Acute malnutrition rates among children under 5 is serious but with high SAM rates among IDP and urban poor.
Hobyo has poor coverage of functional health services and high morbidity rates. Nutrition situation is critical, vaccination coverage low and WASH status very poor. Rural areas are hard to reach with poor basic infrastructure thus causing marginalization
In all proposed areas, sexual violations including rape is rampant, highest cases reported in Hobyo. In rural areas this is further fueled by the distance covered by women and children as they seek health services exposing them to hazards and vulnerabilities.
SAF UK will therefore focus on reducing loss of lives, sustaining lives and centrality of protection, activities that support prevention and treatment of disease and resilience building. This will be done through 4 health centers,1 PHU and 1 mobile clinics.
This will tie in a strong community component ensuring accountability to affected population.
Marginalized groups and the disabled, who are often disregarded and left out, will be participate at all phases of the project cycle. The project will also strive to include all age and gender groups. To ensure staff have capacity to promote these while ensuring protection, all staff will be trained in PSEA, social mobilization and disability inclusion in programs. Strong monitoring will ensure realization of this with all data disaggregated by age, gender and disability.
Project will integrate with Nutrition, WASH, protection and food security and livelihood activities. The project will also seek to improve health seeking behavior though community mobilization. In all sites COVID 19 prevention will remain a key focus. Projects will continue to adhere to containment measures during implementation. All staff will also be provided with PPE.
Significant others to the target beneficiaries, especially Men and grandmothers, will be targeted for inclusion in programs with affirmative action for them. Children, adolescents and the youth who have traditionally been neglected when it comes to programing will fully participate to ensure sustainability and mutual inclusivity. Peer support groups will also be included to promote peer learning.
Access and coverage in these areas will be increased by use of mobile teams and community health workers (CHW). CHW will hail from local communities. They will not only be engaged in primary prevention but cover few aspects of secondary prevention. They will also continue supporting COVID 19 home based care.
Coordination will be enhanced by participating in health and other cluster forum.
The project will adhere to government policies, use government approved national and international guidelines and work closely with MoH at national and state level.

Mitigation Measures

For IDP the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation has maintained adatabase for all bene􀀀ciaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For those inrural Mudug the risk is spontaneous clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this,we have ensured that services are provided for the different clans in their areasequitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect our programs. in all locationthe organisation will seek to provide safety nets by implementing BSFP . Victims ofGBV will also be provided with an organisational GBV focal point who will linksurvivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDp have access tobasic services in an equitable and digni􀀀ed manner with equality also observed in joballocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the risk of exclusion basedon tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain community who found it hardto access services in the recent past based on their tribe and skin color. Theorganisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actually reaches out to allcommunity members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is not based on tribe butprofessional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid of cultures who havelearned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the targetcommunities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women sothat equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably.Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in allprojects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will include allcommunity groups so that an all inclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached.Both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will be part ofcommunity health board as per organisational policy. This will ensure decisions madeare all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helm of needsaffecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's APPframework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for menensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries.Women , especially in IDP face multiple protection related risks, some whose effectsare hard to reverse. The organisation has recently amended relevant policies to putprotection at the center of our interventions. The organisation proposed to bringintegrated services closer to beneficiaries, especially women and children, so that theydo not have to walk far to access services exposing them to multiple hazards. A clearreferral pathway for referring cases of GBV has been established in out APPframework, with a GBV focal point set in every program to facilitate this. Children whoare often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but as part of the community.Their views on service delivery and need will occasionally be sought by a professionalchild specialist so that the whole community participates in program design andimplementation. Community diagnosis,community dialogues, evaluations andassessment will seek to include all community groups to ensure all are reached.
