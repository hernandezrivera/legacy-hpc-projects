Project Summary

Violence against women and girls is alarmingly present in all corners of South Sudan. SGBV– including rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction and other harmful practices is a widespread, chronic and persistent violation of the basic human rights of girls and women and its even worse in women and girls with disabilities. Violence against women and girls is therefore both a human rights and public health issue.
The gender based violence incidences in South Sudan is very high and can be attributed to the prolonged period of violence in the country that occurred in 2013 and 2016. The country still experiences sporadic fighting intercommunal. This left many women and girls seriously traumatized. In the country a study conducted by WHO shows that South Sudan has the highest number of gender based violence with 41% of the women having experienced violence at least once in live time and the study shows raising cases of intimate partner violence currently standing at 49%. (UNFPA 2020).
Cases of forced marriage continue to be extreamly high in the country and the study further shows that that Ruweng Administrative Area (43.1%), Warrap (39.7%), Eastern Equatoria (39.1%), Jonglei (39.1%) and Central Equatoria (38.6%) report the highest rates of physical violence in the country. Similarly, the study documented a high prevalence of child marriage (34.6%, nationally) with Lakes and Jonglei states accounting for the high rates of child marriage in the country - 60.7% and 40.9%, respectively.
Access to gender based violence services in the communities is very low with skilled personnel to offer GBV services very limited, lack of women and girls friendly spaces still remains a major challenge and this women continue to suffer the gender based violence silently. Very many women and girls don’t know the referral pathways to access this essential services.
All these protection concerns calls for efforts to reduce the cases of GBV in the communities and also raise awareness in the communities on the need to reduce this vice with male involvement in reducing toxic masculinity and improve positive masculinity having male champions for the reduction of gender based violence.
Women Agency for Resilience and Transformation (WART) will improve the protection situation in the country through provision of psychosocial support to the victims of GBV. The organization will have Psychosocial support desks to ensure that women and girls who need support are given the support in good time.
The organization will build and equip disability inclusive women and girls friendly spaces that will support victims of gender based violence and also provide them with skills needed to improve their livelihood. There will be awareness rising on the importance of reducing GBV that will be done in the community.
The organization will establish referral pathways for victims of GBV to ensure there is immediate access to legal aid, medical services and where possible other key services like psychosocial support and activities to build resilience and trauma recovery.
WART will also focus on activities that greatly reduce exposure to GBV like making of fuel efficient stoves to ensure that women and girls frequency of going to look for firewood and other energy sources is greatly reduced thus reducing the exposure time to risks associated with gender based violence.
WART will conduct trainings to the case workers and other actors on GBV mainstreaming in health, WASH and other programmes so that safety audits can be conducted in construction of key social amenities in the communities. Distribution of dignity kits to women and girls will be done to also support them and empower them on efforts to reduce GBV.
This project will be implemented in Rubkona, Kapoeta East and Abiemnhom Counties in South Sudan targeting a total of 8000 (2000 girls, 1000 boys 3000 women and 2000 men).

1. Needs

South Sudan has one of the highest prevalence of gender based violence in the region with studies showing that over 40% of the population has in some point experienced some form of gender based violence.According to IRNA conducted in Kapoeta, Protection concerns continue to rise with the communities lacking community based protection networks to support victims of gender based violence and other forms of protection issues in the community.(Kapoeta North IRNA March 2023)The country has been in conflict and also has experienced shocks associated with climate change with the country experiencing floods in the last 3 years that caused displacement of populations and exposing them to gender based violence, abductions, forced marriages and other forms of protection concerns associated with displacement and flooding.According to the IPC report December 2022, Kapoeta North County, Abiemnhom County and Abiemnhom county are in IPC 4 classification in an emergency situation that in terms of food security. This increases the risks of gender based violence and sexual abuse in the communities as people may tend to exploit the vulnerable populations in exchange of food or cash to sustain themselves.The conflict in Sudan has led to massive displacement of populations and influx of refugees and returnees. This has also exposed the displaced communities in the country to very high protection vulnerabilities including but not limited to gender based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse by the actors in the conflict, abductions and forced marriages. The populations arriving in the country are in urgent need of protection services like psychosocial support and other key interventions including distribution of protection commodities.Referral pathways to the GBV services are lacking and communities are not aware of the available SGBV services. Rubkona and Abiemnhom are among the counties devastated by conflict in Sudan receiving returnees from the Sudan conflict in large numbers leading to distressed Women, Boys, girls and men due prolonged periods conflict leading to increased prevalence of domestic based violence and other protection risks. The communities do not have awareness on GBV and there are no clear referral pathways to victims of GBV and other protection concerns.This therefore calls for interventions in the communities to establish community based networks on protection, and offer protection and gender based violence to victims of SGBV as well as building the capacity of case workers and other actors to respond to protection needs of the community.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of survivor-centered psycho social support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery.2. Distribution of cash vouchers to victims of gender based violence. 3. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support 4. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support5. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment6. Conduct dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls as part of GBV risk mitigation 7. Conduct community awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle8. Conducting safety audits in for construction of sanitation facilities and other facilities to minimize gender based violence.9. Training humanitarian workers on GBV prevention and mainstreaming in their programming.10. Establishment of referral pathways to legal and health services and also supporting victims with psycho social support.
