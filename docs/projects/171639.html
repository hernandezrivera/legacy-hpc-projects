Project Summary

The most needy and vulnerable host communities and IDPs in target districts will be covered by this project. Kismayo hosts some of the most vulnerable and protracted IDP in Somalia, most facing risk of multiple displacement. It also host a number of urban poor whose poor household food security status constantly drives acute malnutrition. Hobyo and outskirts of Kismayo town host rural communities that are far to reach and marginalized with some not able to access services for several years past (SAF UK 2019;UNOCHA 2020). Most households have limited access to health services and poor WASH status (REACH 2019) exacerbating disease and malnutrition. Though GAM rate has improved in Kismayo, SAM rates are still high. In Hobyo nutrition status for both children and PLW is critical with aggravating factors (FSNAU,2020). IYCF Indicators still poor in these areas especially in Hobyo and rural Kismayo (FSNAU,2020). Food security status in both areas is stressed. Among Kismayo IDP and urban poor, household food security status deteriorated further between April and August 2020 due to COVID 19 containment measures. FSNAU predicts that the situation is predicted to deteriorate as 2020 ends and further deteriorating in 2021 due to predicted la Nina.
SAF UK will therefore focus on reducing loss of lives, sustaining lives, centrality of protection among the most vulnerable population. SAF UK has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy which it will adhere to. Activities that support prevention and treatment of malnutrition and resilience building for affected population will be our primary focus.
This will tie in a strong community component ensuring accountability to affected population guided by the organizations APP framework. All feedback and complains will be collected and responded to through SAF UK mechanisms.
Marginalized groups and the disabled who are often disregarded and left our will be participate at all phases of the project cycle. The project will also strive to include all age and gender groups for full inclusion. To ensure staff have capacity to promote these while ensuring protection, all staff will be trained in PSEA, social mobilization and disability inclusion in programs. Strong monitoring will ensure realization of this with all data disaggregated in age and gender.
Project will provide treatment of acute malnutrition for children 6-59 moths and PLW while engaging in primary prevention interventions.
Programs will integrate with health, WASH, protection and food security and livelihood activities. This will include integration with basic health and maternal services, Expanded program for immunization (EPI), BSFP, hygiene promotion including hygiene kits. The project will also seek to improve health seeking behavior though community mobilization. In all sites COVID 19 prevention will remain an area of focus. Projects will continue to adhere to containment measures during implementation, support IYCF in COVID 19 and advocate for protection of malnourished children from infection. All staff will also be provided with PPE.
Significant others to the target beneficiaries, especially Men and grandmothers, will be targeted for inclusion in programs with affirmative action for them. Children, adolescents and the youth who have traditionally been neglected when it comes to programing will be full included at all stages of the program cycle to ensure sustainability and mutual inclusivity. Peer support groups will also be included to promote peer learning. Multi sector approach that included involvement in multisector coordination forum like cluster coordination will be key to 2020 programs.
The project will adhere to government policies, use government approved national and international guidelines and work closely with MoH at national and state level.
Government officials will take lead in most key decisions and will be involved for sustainability.

Mitigation Measures

For IDP the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation has maintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For those in rural Mudug the risk is spontenious clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the different clans in their areas equitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect our programs. in all location the organisation will seek to provide safety nets by implementing BSFP . Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBV focal point who will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDp have access to basic services in an equitable and dignified manner with equality also observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the risk of exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain community who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribe and skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actually reaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is not based on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid of cultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women so that equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably. Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will include all community groups so that an all conclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached. both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will be part of community health board as per organisational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's APP framework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for menensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries. Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but their views on service delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek to include all community groups to ensure all are reached. Dialogues, evaluations and conversations will include women, children and youth with their own forum so as to ensure their views are considered and used to programing. 2020 programs will seek to improve access to women and girls , who are key beneficiaries of proposed interventions by scaling up services to be within a few kilometers walk. Far and hard to reach areas in rural settings will be served by mobile teams so as to reduce risks and hazards faced by these groups enroute to accessing services.

Please provide further explanation

In all proposed areas the environment is sufficiently safe to enable implementation of resilience activities. All our projects have a good working relationship with the government and other relevant stakeholders. Some of our facilities were handed to the organisation by the government and work under strict supervision of the same. Others run by the organisation are community owned but supervised by MoH, possessing long term agreements witnessed and endorsed by government. In all proposed districts, project involve government entities in all activities and receive several monitoring and supportive supervision visits. The organisation is also not only registered by government to operate in the locations but also have Memorandum of understanding with clear terms of engagement including relationship with government, commitment to serve and deliver, allegiance to total transparency and accountability, and commitment to deliver positive outputs and outcomes. The organisation also has strong working relationship with communities we serve working closely with all age groups who are involved right from needs assessment. In all areas, community health boards have been formed not only to represent the community in matters health but also provide leadership and guidance. Sustainability continues as our focus area in 2020 and the same is being entrachanced in our policies. As part of our policy and APP framework, beneficiaries are informed of their entitlement to quality service delivery and access to utilities as per project requirement. This aspect is currently operational with a strong, non- bias and functional feedback mechanism in place.
