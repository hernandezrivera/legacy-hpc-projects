Project Summary

According to the HNO for 2022, Women/adolescent girls, widows, divorcees, persons with disabilities, older women, and women minority clans to face heightened risks of GBV with increased displacements multiple displacements and forced evictions due to flooding, droughts, and armed conflicts. Intimate Partner Violence, rapes, forced abortion and revenge killings; sexual exploitations, harassment and abuse are rife due to poor living conditions; destruction of homes, water points, latrines, street lights, farmlands, livelihoods, and schools; family separation for children and open defecation. Lack of community and male protection due to male migration; segregation of toilets, travel to markets and service sites; poor lighting of IDP camps represent major GBV risks for women and adolescent girls. Food insecure families experienced a greater risk of intimate partner violence because of the increasing tensions around sharing family resources. Also, women and girls living in minority settlements are more likely to feel unsafe walking around their neighborhoods due to organized gangs and exclusion from community leaderships, consultations and service provision. women and adolescent girls make up a majority of displaced persons due to flooding and ongoing insecurity and conflict. In Mahaday and Jowhar districts of Middle Shebelle region, a total of 66,000 individuals have been totally displaced from their dwellings, out of which 45 per cent are women and girls, while 28 per cent are children386. A recent May 2021 joint assessment of the GBV and Child Protection Clusters.
Berdale and Baidoa displacement found increased incidences of sexual exploitation (37 per cent); sexual harassment and abuse (33 per cent); intimate partner violence (54 per cent); child marriages (64 per cent) and FGM (57 per cent). Adult married women, adolescent girls, widows, divorced women, female headed households, pregnant and lactating women, women and girls living with disabilities are subject to various forms of violence and discrimination emanating from social status and gendered social norms. Female heads of households, divorced women and women living with disabilities are particularly disadvantaged due to lack of male protection and provision, difficulty to report/escape violence or access services. Women and girls from minority clans also report feeling of exclusion and targeting for humanitarian assistance and are unable to assert their right to own land387 Men and boys are also targeted for rape, forceful recruitment and kidnapping and psychological abuse and trauma. Loss of livelihoods and food insecurity due to multiple displacements forced young boys to step into the roles of provision thereby increasing levels of school dropouts. Men’s declining ability to provide for their families adds to low self-esteem and the use of intimate partner violence to reclaim some of the historically privileged position of household head
A Safety Audit assessment conducted by IMC on September 2022 in Baidoa, Jowhar and Mogadishu indicated Gender Based Violence remains a daily occurrence with poor access to gender and latrines and bathing facilities. IDPs settlements of Bay, Middle Shebelle, Banadir and Mudug, face some of the most serious gender-related problems in the whole country. The particularly low awareness of the extremely high rates of violence, low reporting rates and effective limited of treatment facilities as well as insufficient infrastructure, poor WASH facilities, limited access to markets, water points, and health facilities often equate to inadequate protection capacities.
Through this project, IMC aims to support vulnerable populations in drought-affected areas in Banadir (Mogadishu), Bay (Baidoa), Middle Shebelle (Jowhar)Mudug regions (Galkayo), who are exposed to Gender Based Violence (GBV). 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

• Marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Women/children/PLWs with disabilities will be consulted on the Protection risks they face and activities that would make their communities safer for them. During distribution of dignity kits, beneficiaries with disabilities & other special needs, will be identified and given priority. • Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from marginalized groups know how to access timely, confidential Protection response services. Messages will be disseminated through household visits, community gatherings and videos at heath facilities, in consideration of literacy, disability and mobility levels. Protection teams will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, sociodemographic groups, disability). IMC will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Protection specific awareness messaging will involve both girls, boys, women and men and will be timed to t around women and men’s other work schedules. • IMC will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying Protection risk mitigation solutions and the decision-making processes so as to reduce GBV stigma, promote survivor centered care and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • All IMC Protection staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries.• All IMC Protection staff in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to avoid and report Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. • IMC will provide solar powered lamps as part of dignity kit distribution to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls in high risk areas.• Working in coordination with WASH and Nutrition sectors, IMC will put in place measures to prevent physical/sexual violence arising from distributions of Nutrition Supplements and poor WASH infrastructure in camp settings.• Protection staff will be trained on provision of safe, survivor centered case management, including provision of PSS and safe data storage. • In areas where IMC has Women and Girls Safe Spaces, teams will refer beneficiaries to the WGSS group PSS and basic life skills training. The location and the timing of WGSS activities will endeavor to achieve safe proximity to the beneficiaries a well as keeping to hours that allow women and girls to return to their homes before nightfall. • All IMC health centers will have confidential and private spaces, with at least one trained female health worker on duty at all times to ensure 24/7 access to clinical management of rape and GBV services. • IMC will roll out a safe-word based referral protocol at all implementation sites to ensure direct referral of survivors from Health to the GBV teams, without the Survivor needing to repeat her/his story potentially exposing her/him to further traumatic maintains an emergency GBV response number that allows confidential reporting of GBV cases and ambulance callout when necessary.
