Project Summary

The projects intends to provide a multi-faceted response in an integrated and coherent way.
Shafak's proposal is designed to supporting farmers and livestock herders needs of the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and HCs. The project comprises the following Key intervention areas:
ER1: 9500 Targeted households are able to resume agricultural activities, increase household income and improve consumption through: i) provision of agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; olive harvesting and pruning and ii)improving/restoring agricultural infrastructure (for example irrigation / drainage), IV) Cash for harvest, V) extension services, VI) purchasing wheat, VII) forage cultivation
ER2: 5500 Targeted households are able to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through: i) provision of livestock inputs (500 kg of fodder per beneficiary 1500 beneficiaries) ; ii) associated training, iii) vaccination campaigns per health care calendar (4 campaigns, 200,000 head per campaign owned by 4,000 beneficiaries)
ER3: 3000 Targeted households stabilize, maintain or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which build and strengthen community related value chain infrastructure , disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest and vol control and olive trees harvesting, Purification of wheat fields, and afforestation.
ER4: Rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure, irrigation channels, Mills,
bakeries, storage, fodder mixers, feeder roads and silos.
ER5: 4500 HHs to be supported with regular food basket over 8 rounds
ER6: 6000 HHs to be supported with Cash for food Voucher:
ER7: 15000 RTE to be distributed as response to emergencies and shocks
ER8: 5000 MPC to be distributed to affected populations after emergencies.
ER8: 2000 MT of flour to be distributed to bakeries to subsidize prices.
ER9: 500 farmer will be supported with Cash/inkind productive assets

4 - Needs

The ten years of the crisis has impacted peoples’ negative coping mechanisms and led to widespread poverty a poor economic context, low employment levels, limited access to livelihoods and income generating sources. The crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access essential infrastructure and services. These factors have deepened poverty and weakened communities’ resilience to cope with internal and external shocks. The situation in Syria has deteriorated, with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6 per cent of the total population. Furthermore, all 1.7 million310 people living in camps are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 1.8 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity. The results show a sharp increase from last year, with an increase of 4.5 million people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020.(HNO2021). About 216,000 hactares of wheat and other crops such as barley, fruit trees, vegetables and grazing areas (17 percent of national areas planted) were affected by a number of shocks including floods, fires, hailstorms, high temperatures and plant diseases and pests specifically yellow rust. In Northwest Syria, vaccination of 2.4 million smallholder chickens against Newcastle Disease and of smallholder almost 18,000 dairy cattle against Lumpy Skin Disease has prevented a resurgence of these two devastating livestock epidemics .The impact of the economic crisis on food production puts pressure on the smallholder farming sector.

5 - Implementation of activities

• • Provision of agriculture inputs and services: as per the FSL cluster and TWG guidelines, the farmers will be targeted with Agriculture inputs, services through CFW, Cash for harvest, and value addition.• Subsidizing bread: The procured wheat from farmers will be processed into flour to be distributed to bakeries to subsidize bread prices and increased availability and accessibility of bread in the targeted community. And other residues mixed and distributed as fodder• Infrastructures rehabilitation: Wheat, olive, and agriculture infrastructures such as will be rehabilitated, Projects for Infrastructure to be selected based on community assessment. if the selected projects include private facilities, then, a cost-sharing methodology will be applied • Cash for work: Through the implementation of CFW activities, Shafak is seeking to improve or protect agriculture and bread-related infrastructure or assets. Cash-for-Work activities will enable the provision of labor opportunities.• Graduation Kits/grants distribution: The beneficiaries will receive package of intervention starting from training, then grants /in kind assets distribution – Coaching and mentoring with providing the needed support and facilitation • Providing fodder, vaccines and extension: Vaccination campaigns will be conducted c) Compound fodder distribution d) Extension sessions through groups e) Mobile clinic establishment• Regular Food Basket/Cash distribution/RTE/MPG: Shafak has established a robust in-kind/cash distribution SOPs including activities of MPGs. Shafak SOPs are in line with FSL, CTWG, WFP standards and recommendation. All the targeted HH will be supported with minimum 8 rounds of intervention.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

limitations on movement and physical security risk due to conflict activity- Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility- Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Shafak will mitigate the above risks through the community orientation sessions, in which the Shafak staff ensure that equality concepts are conveyed to the community. The project team in coordination with MEAL and protection technical teams will identify and address protection risks caused/perpetuated by the intervention, design activities in-line with protection principles, monitor whether protection mainstreaming is taking place by taking into account the following elements throughout the project life cycle:1. Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention on affected people. This also includes ensuring data protection during registration, referrals, case management, IPA etc. As such, the project team will be following the Media Guidelines developed by the protection cluster which ensure data protection and confidentiality at all stages, information sharing protocols and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries2. Meaningful Access: Arrange for individuals and particularly children to access services in proportion to need and without barriers, while paying special attention to groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance. 3. Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms, through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions,4. Participation and Empowerment: support the development of communities
