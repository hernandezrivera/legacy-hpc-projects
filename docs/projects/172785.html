Project Summary

Children in rural and farming communities have been hard hit by the impact of covid19 and they are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse as well as gender based violence due to the implementation of lockdown measures and banning of public gatherings. Models of support for children previously relied on the school system which required children to move from homes into the school system. The closure of schools has cut supply to PSEA,SRH and GBV information as well as causing disruptions in children’s access to protection services. This project therefore shall combine school and community protection models thus lining protection actors to provide a broad range of services to children in rural communities as schools reopen despite the inadequacy of protective mechanisms. Traditional courts play a central role to children’s access to justice in rural areas as chiefs and herdsmen are the custodians of customary law. These customary law structures are the 1st entry point for children particularly during the covid19 pandemic which has seen traditional justice delivery system being scaled down or in some cases being inaccessible due to transport and other operational logistics.
The innovation shall strengthen the capacity of teachers in selected primary and secondary schools in the targeted districts to use the Channels of Hope model on child protection. The female teachers shall recruit and establish clubs of adolescent girls and boys into protection mentorship clubs to provide SRH information, knowledge on PSEA and GBV. If mentors identify any child rights violations they will initiate referral into the case care management system. A total of 30 primary and secondary schools shall be targeted from Mbire, Mt Darwin and Muzarabani districts of Mashonaland Central Province. Each school shall run children resilience clubs for the purpose of providing psychosocial support, build life skills and initiate referral for SRH and protection services. There shall be provision of NFIs to participating adultescent girls and young women. The final tire of the project shall target the strengthening of tradition courts systems to preside over cases which involves children in rural areas. Active personnel in the traditional court system such as chiefs and herdsmen shall be trained on child friendly and gender sensitive models. SAYWHAT shall support its representatives to conduct traditional court visits and monitor children cases as well as initiate psychosocial support and the provision of NFIs for both families and children involved.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

SAYWHAT implemented a similar model covering Mazowe, Bulawayo ,Gweru and Makoni districts. The organisation consulted extensively with more than 1000 teachers being recruited and holding protection mentorship clubs across 100 schools reaching out to more than 10000 girls. This interaction gave SAYWHAT access to primary data and feedback on protection issues and most of them has been incorporated under this project to improve on layering and multi sector engagement. The SAYWHAT call centre has a high traffic of calls from districts such as Mbire and Mt Darwin with children going through tele counselling or being referred for more protection services. Such interactions increased particularly during the covid19 era and call centre reports attest to extensive consultations. The project has captured community visioning by incorporating district learning cycles whose outcomes can further align and adjust the project. Communities will have access to the SAYWHAT toll free line which can be used to anonymously give feedback. The trained teachers shall host ward committee meetings to give feedback on the progress and efficiency of the intervention at community level. Children has a direct feedback line with their teachers during resilient clubs and sessions. Annual project review meetings shall be hosted at district level.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project has reasonably law risk to impact on the environment in the 3 districts. SAYWHAT has however adopted as a matter of procedure digital offices. This approach minimizes on the use of paper during project and activity implementation. One such example under the project is the procurement of electronic devices for teachers to submit real time digital reports instead of paper reports, which have an indirect impact on the environment. As part of its learning culture, SAYWHAT shall engage representatives at district level from the Environment Management Agency (EMA) to be part of the district learning circles. Their participation provides the project with technical insights on any possibility by the project to harm the environment
