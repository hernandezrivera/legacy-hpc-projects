Project Summary

The project will address root causes of conflict, advocate for promotion of girl child education, prevention of forced marriage, discouraging child recruitment in labor force more especially in army. The project targets 10,000 beneficiaries of which 7000(3500 boys &3500 girls) focus on child protection,3000(2200 females and 800 men) on General protection
It is estimated that 4.77million women, girls, boys are in need of protection in 2021. The project is geared towards alleviating human suffering and promoting the lives and dignity of children, women, disabled and socially excluded groups of people in Nasir, Ulang IDP. It is well aligned with cluster strategic objective of ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistant and specialized services through integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable children, women, boys and girls in Nasir, Ulang IDP camps. The specific objectives of the project include; i)Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience and ii) Enable durable solutions for IDPS and other affected populations. An estimated 2.7 million people, including 2.3 million children are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection in 2021. This has been aligned with the cluster focus of centrality, impartiality in provision of humanitarian services more especially children in IDP camps and asylum seekers
UNKEA has proposed the following activities to be implemented: (1) provision of psychosocial supporting activities to 18000 children in child-friendly spaces, schools and communities. (2) Strengthening family- and community-based protection structures: UNKEA will do this by supporting community-based groups, consisting of key community members and youth representatives, who are mandated to mobilize communities; raise awareness on Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention, response and risk mitigation services; identify, report and refer protection cases, and promote social cohesion in their locality. (3) Establishing/refining referral pathways for different locations for women, men, boys and girls including with disabilities with protection and this will be done through case management and referral services referral services. This will also include mutli-sectoral coordination for consolidated input from health, WASH, FSL, and Nutrition in all earmarked project locations. (4) Organizing awareness trainings on Mine Risk Education (MRE) targeting 500 beneficiaries (5) Identification, documentation, family tracing, reunification and alternative care to unaccompanied and separated children (6) Organizing trainings for youth leaders formerly associated with armed groups on topics encouraging them to go back to school/formal education, vocational and livelihoods skills, psychosocial support, foster care, tracing and family reunification. (7) UNKEA will also continue its current cash for work for protection activities that are on-going in Ulang and hopefully expanding it to Nasir county next season. (8) Provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls and doing orientation on their usage. (9) Provide attendant support services to survivors of GBV and other related protection vices through individual counseling, case management, and referral facilitation to remedial points of service. (10) UNKEA will also conduct advocacy at all levels to influence/encourage the government/opposition to ensure safety and security at pre-displacement homesteads so that IDPs and other target beneficiaries can build confidence and access care and services they need. In ensuring that these activities make relevant impact, the project will engage other actors consistently over the project period to consolidate input for the target communities.

1 - Needs

It is estimated that 4.77million people are in need of protection services of which 310,000 are refugees and asylum seekers. Of this 2.7million children are dire need of protection, 2million require GBVs services(HNO,2020) Insecurity and violence disproportionally affect children, women and those with disability in IDP camps as well as PoCs. In South Sudan, 97% of the GBV cases are reported among women while a huge number of GBV cases are unreported and is silence in communities. The project therefore seeks to address the root causes of the discrimination among girls,boy and disabled people. The project will also advocate for promotion of child well being in the community and reunification of lost children with their parents, access to resources, information dissemination, prevention of forced marriage, child soldiers and child labor. Children (0 to 17 years old) constitute 61 per cent of the refugee population. They continue to face protection risks including neglect, abuse, exploitation, child labour, forced recruitment, SGBV, and lack of access to services. Protection concerns are especially high for girls risks for early marriage, early pregnancies, and denial of education.Though security concerns have generally been low and stable in most counties in Upper Nile State, Luakpiny/Nasir and Ulang counties have been reporting security concerns (Reach, September 2020). The armed conflicts between sub-clans, cattle raiding and now couple with heavy flooding of last year caused a lot of protection needs for most vulnerable groups especially women, children and persons with disabilities. There is also great fear that there may be present of landmines in the surrounding of Nasir town (South Sudan Mine Action, 2017). This will risk the lives of women, girls, boys and some men who have no ideas on how landmines look like since all the former inhabitants of Nasir town are now planning to move there as soon as the county government is formed. The inter-communal violence and cattle raiding in these two counties (Ulang and Luakpiny/Nasir) caused a lot of safety concerns and such concerns were domestic violence on women who are 18 years and above, family separation and abduction. On the side of men who are 18 years and above, the main protection concerns with regards to inter-communal violence and cattle raiding, it was reported at 67% in Ulang and 38% in Nasir respectively (REACH September ,2020). In addition to inter-communal violence, cattle raiding and landmines protection concerns, the heavy flood of last year displaced many people and destroyed most of the livelihoods in these two counties. According to Rapid Joint Assessment September 2020, a total of 10602 persons were forced to seek refuge in high lying areas in Nasir county alone thus, creating serious pressure on the limited resources available between IDPs and host communities. In light of this, protection concerns were raising in those high lying areas especially gender base violence, child labor, and early/forced marriage. Regrettably, UNKEA has only 4 protection plans for Riang and Biel in Ulang while in Nasir county there is no well-established protection plan and referral pathways that provide for emergency assistance to victims of abuse. This proposed intervention provides opportunities for UNKEA to scale up its current protection engagements to deal with the above identified needs and gaps. The project will support both the IDPs and the host communities in vulnerable situations as affected by armed fighting, cattle raiding and floods in the 2 counties. UNKEA will therefore work with humanitarian community to ensure protection is central in their activities through regular coordination, assessment and information sharing. UNKEA work with communities to identify and strengthen the resilience of affected people and to build capacity of local organizations to address child protection issues.

2 - Project activities

Through this project UNKEA will expand its scope and build from its previous protection work by setting up/strengthening the already existing child protection plans in Nasir and Ulang County while benefiting from an integrated sectoral input of WASH, Health, Nutrition, and Food Security and Livelihoods whose funding is already secured. Specifically, the intervention will include: (1) provision of psychosocial supporting activities to 100 children (60 boys and 40 girls) in child-friendly spaces, schools and communities. (2) Strengthening family- and community-based child protection structures: UNKEA will do this by supporting community-based groups, consisting of key community members and youth representatives, who are mandated to mobilize communities; raise awareness on child protection (3) Establishing/refining referral pathways for different locations for boys and girls including with disabilities with protection and this will be done through case management. This will also include mutli-sectoral coordination for consolidated input from health, WASH, FSL, and Nutrition in all earmarked project locations. (5) Identification, documentation, family tracing, reunification and alternative care to unaccompanied and separated children (6) Organizing trainings for youth leaders formerly associated with armed groups on topics encouraging them to go back to school/formal education, vocational and livelihoods skills, psychosocial support, foster care, tracing and family reunification. (7) UNKEA will also continue its current cash for work for child protection activities that are on-going in Ulang and hopefully expanding it to Nasir county next season. (8) Provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls and doing orientation on their usage. (9) UNKEA will also conduct advocacy at all levels to influence/encourage the government/opposition to ensure safety and security at pre-displacement homesteads so that IDPs and other target beneficiaries can build confidence and access care and services they need. In ensuring that these activities make relevant impact, the project will engage other actors consistently over the project period to consolidate input for the target communities. UNKEA will work with the county health department and local authorities throughout the project period to coordinate multi sectoral support to child protection. The project will training the stakeholders on child right, and aware the public on equal education with girls like boys while the project will also address the issue of child soldier with armies barrack by training the organized forces in 2 targeted counties

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This project will establish a well strengthening family- and community-based protection structures: UNKEA will do this by supporting community-based groups, consisting of key community members and youth representatives, who are mandated to mobilize communities; raise awareness on Gender-Based Violence (GBV)/protection against sexual exploitation and abuse.UNKEA will conduct sensitization meetings with beneficiaries on PSEA after ensuring that the project staff are trained on PSEA. The staff will also read and sign the PSEA and safeguarding policies of the organization.UNKEA will provide the names of the PSEA and safeguarding focal persons to the beneficiaries so that the beneficiaries are able to report cases of PSEA and safeguarding nature. This can be done through toll free telephone numbers and suggestion boxes. UNKEA will ensure that reporters are protected and kept safe by ensuring the confidentiality of the feedback received.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback and complaints mechanisms are based on community/group preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. In this project UNKEA will form or establish a working group on Communicating with Communities (CwC) which will comprise of all group representatives from the community with women, girls and those people living with disabilities(PLWD) included and they will ensure that all beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints are captured and all the project activities are communicated to community. There will be feedback and complaints suggestion boxes put in places which can be easily access and safely use by all literate people including those living with disabilities. Women and girls who are members of working group on CwC will take lead in conducting face-to-face interview with illiterate beneficiaries (women, girls and PLWD) and record their feedback and complaints on audio which can be sent to the UNKEA senior management with a lot confidentiality

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will adopt plural communication channels that will include low technology and non-technology options to cater for both sensitive and nonsensitive feedback, and will ensure that the chosen communication channels are relevant and inclusive to even the most vulnerable populations such as women, girls, and people living with disabilities and illiterate people. The Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems (suggestion boxes, notice boards), interpersonal communication through trusted liaison persons, and anonymous calls using the telephone and or email where possible. Feedback will be solicited both in person especially for those people that cannot read and write, but also using other means such as telephone, and writings. In addition, the project will conduct regular systemic assessments of appropriate channels of feedback as guided by community preferences. Regular feedback/ complaint mechanisms will be incorporated through the project life span; at the schools, suggestion boxes and client satisfaction interviews will be used; in the community, quarterly dialogue meetings will be conducted targeting various community members. Project achievement will be posted on notice boards both at the payam and at the county offices. The project in collaboration with the beneficiaries will identify trusted contact persons in the community to act as liaison people. The contact persons will include both men and women and these will be linked with the project contact person both in the field and also at the organization headquarters. All complaints will be investigated in a systematic method ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times. The project M&E system is designed to capture client feedback through the scheduled client interviews and qualitative analysis. The feedback collected from the community will be incorporated into the project review processes and quality improvements during the project implementation period.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All the feedback collected through suggestion boxes and face-to-face interview with illiterate beneficiaries (women, girls and PLWD) conducted by Women and girls who are members of working group will be analyzed by UNKEA senior management and it will be used in guiding the project team on how best they can deliver the assistance to the beneficiaries.
