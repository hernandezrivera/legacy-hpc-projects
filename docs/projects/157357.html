Project Summary

ADRA proposes to work with IDPs and host communities in the following localities: ElKurmuk, Al-Geissan, Bao in White Nile and West Darfur a ADRA’s approach is to compliment the local partners’ food assistance programs through interventions focused in WASH. Intervention 1: WASH remains the most critical area of need for this program. The focus of this intervention will be the prevention of waterborne disease, thus leading to the protection of health. The project will continue to support the Phase 1 villages in Phase 2 by increasing access to safe water sources by simultaneously rehabilitating and treating the existing water sources to improve water quality, making it safe for human consumption. The project will also continue to promote good hygiene and sanitation practices to reduce transmission of waterborne and fecal-oral diseases. It is imperative that disaster-affected people have necessary information, knowledge and understanding to prevent water- and sanitation-related diseases .

Needs

Water Supply: About 60% of existing hand pumps are not functional, and approximately half of water systems need rehabilitation, creating a major gap in the target locations. Conservative estimates suggest a minimum of between 15,000-23,000 people affected by waterborne diseases, with 280-820 deaths as of 2018. The current numbers for 2019 are not clear. The risk of waterborne disease continues to increase, with spikes in cases expected with the onset of the rainy season in May 2019. The unimproved, unprotected and exposed water sources are susceptible to contamination, including by feces, due to the high prevalence of open defecation. The hand pumps, mini water yards, seasonal Wadis and Haffirs are either broken down, non-functional or insufficient to meet the needs of the growing number of IDPs and hosts in the target locations. The insecurity, shortage of trained technicians and spare parts due to challenges in transport and supply chain have also contributed to the insufficiency of functional water sources. Untreated water from rainfed water catchment facilities coupled with the prevalence of open defecation surrounding the haffirs, use of water sources by both humans and animals, and presence of dead animals has been a major factor in the AWD outbreak in these locations. Data derived from FewsNet shows that the State has gone well into IPC 3 (Crisis) with increasing GAM and SAM rates for children U5 and PLW. The increase of AWD as well as the lack of clean water is one of the main underlying factors of malnutrition in the area. Females especially in those areas face added protection risks as they carry water from the water source to their homes for over 1.5 KM per day. Hygiene and sanitation: Open defecation is common in the areas surrounding the water sources. The sanitation situation in the localities of Kurmuk, Bau and Geissan is particularly severe, with large market areas, high population movement, including nomadic pastoralists, and large areas with no latrine coverage. The interruption of education has contributed to a decrease in knowledge and awareness of good hygiene and sanitation practices.

Activities

ADRA proposes the rehabilitation of existing, non-functional, water systems in the targeted localities. In order to rehabilitate the water systems, ADRA will implement the following activities: rehabilitation of fencing around each Haffir, rehabilitation of the existing slow sand filter, provision and fixing of pumping units, construction of new water tap stands, provision and fixing of chlorine injectors, rehabilitation of elevated water tanks, connection of pipes to water tap stands, and excavation for a catchment area.2- Hygiene Promotion: Hygiene promotion will be conducted by program staff in coordination with WUC with technical input and support from the WES and MoH. In each village selected for water supply rehabilitation, the project team and WUC will organize community awareness sessions to focus on hand washing with soap (HWWS) at five critical times: after defecation, before eating, after changing diapers/wiping babies, before food preparation, and before infant feeding. Both male and female promotors will be trained to prompt household level visits with the community and equal interactions with both males and females in households. 3- Community Water User Committees (WUC): The mobilization of affected communities for water point management and cleanliness will be achieved through Water User and Hygiene Committees. Water Use committees will consist of both male and female members in order to ensure that females are equally involved in decision making as well as facilitate equal access to water. 4- WASH NFI: The project will support IDPs and conflict-affected persons with emergency WASH-related NFIs to maximize the benefits derived from improving access to safe water supply and improved hygiene. Each hh will receive 12 bars of soap and 2 x 10 liter jerrycans valued at roughly $21.60 of value. 5- Construction of household latrines in Wester Darfur, as well as training of communities in latrine maintenance.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will highly promote the safe and equal access to water, with a special emphasis on preservation of of resources, and especially ground water. The project will ensure efficient and sustainable taps to avoid water waste. Rehabilitation of water resources will be conducted in consultation with the communities and the environmental consultants to avoid causing harm to neighboring villages. ADRA conducts environmental evaluation prior to all rehabilitative activities, and consults communities on the all rehabilitative activities. Furthermore; Community Water Committees will conduct sessions on safe water usage as well as efficient and safe water storage. Efficient taps will be installed at all water points to avoid water leakage, and community committees will be trained on maintenance of taps. Furthermore, ADRA will conduct a feasibility study on the possibility of solar panel hand pumps in place of fuel powered pumps.
