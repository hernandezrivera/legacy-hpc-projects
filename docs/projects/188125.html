Project Summary

The overarching goal of the Emergency Telecommunications (ETC) is to ensure that humanitarian actors have access to vital telecommunications services that allow them to perform their work in common UN operational areas. Effective communications systems are critical to support and enhance the safety of the humanitarian community.
Telecommunications infrastructure throughout Syria has suffered significant and ongoing damage due to a decade of devastating conflict. While Damascus maintains good coverage, other locations including Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor, Homs, Hana, Qamishli and Tartous require continued upgrades and maintenance to improve their coverage. In 2022, the ETC will maintain and enhance the operational coverage of Communication System by enhancing the existing VHF network and Deploying Push To Talk (PTT) technology in common operational areas.
There is a need for the continued provision of shared connectivity and telecommunications services to facilitate cross border movement and operations in neighboring countries where Syrians have been forced to flee. The ETC will continue to provide dedicated ICT helpdesk support personnel in the inter-agency response area in Gaziantep, Turkey, and provide technical guidance and support to the response community in Turkey, Jordan and Lebanon as required.
Reliable connectivity also remains a major challenge throughout the country. In 2022-23, the ETC will continue to provide data connectivity services in Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor, Hama, Homs and Qamishli and improve ETC data connectivity services in all interagency hubs where these services are provided. The fuel crisis and a lack of mains electricity remain an urgent issue in the whole country and regular power outages impact on the entire humanitarian communications network in Syria. The ETC continue to deploy solar power systems to support and improve ETC data connectivity services in common hubs.
In 2022-23, the ETC will develop the technical capacity of national humanitarian ICT staff through the delivery of two training courses on IT Emergency Management and technical security communications. The ETC will continue to assess the needs on the ground to support and enable humanitarian activities where and when required. Contingency resources may also be required to support new common operational areas or the extension of common services, based on assessments conducted periodically throughout the year

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the context of Syria, the provision of reliable inter-agency communication services is key for the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In the case of underfunding, the ETC will be unable to fulfil its mandate to provide all operational areas with vital communications services, which will seriously hamper humanitarian operations in Syria and will affect staff safety in the field. Capacity development is a critical element in Syria, as some areas where it is difficult to deploy international staff rely entirely on skilled national staff. A shortfall in funding would restrict the ETC’s ability to organize required training for national staff in 2022-23
