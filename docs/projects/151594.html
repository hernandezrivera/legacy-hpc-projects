Project Summary

Through this project, DRC aims at creating an environment that facilitates the delivery services to affected populations and ensure that all displaced persons are enabled to participate meaningfully in the governance and management of the sites, as well as facilitate the engagement of site residents in care and maintenance activities aimed at improving accessibility, reducing hazards and protection and health risks.
This will be done through establishment and strengthening of inclusive community governance structures and thematic committees at each site level, the engagement of these structures in CCCM coordination mechanisms alongside humanitarian partners active in the respective sites and representatives of relevant government and local authorities, as well as through continued support for capacity building, mainly through the delivery of trainings adapted to each type of participation (thematic IDP committee members, site governance members, local authority representatives).
In addition to the strengthening of coordination and information sharing between all stakeholders, the project aims to support community led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites (through cash for work, establishment of site maintenance committees, distribution of tools) and implementation of emergency sites improvement projects (small infrastructure upgrades) that would minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation measures, installation of fire points, construction of foot bridges and walkways for improved accessibility, etc.). These specific interventions will be agreed upon in consultation with community representatives in each site.
Furthermore, the project will enhance community communication and feedback mechanisms through construction of community information centers, employing community outreach mobile teams, establishing feasible and context-based feedback mechanisms and conducting awareness campaigns.
To support opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions, the project will contribute through community based initiatives that can promote community cohesion and recovery and as well as support communities in IDP sites towards self-reliance in locations where prospects of obtaining land rights is achievable. The project aims to reach over 150,000 drought and conflict displaced women, boys, men and girls.

Needs

The impact of the recent drought persists on affected populations in Somalia. There are an estimated 2.6 million displaced people and 2,000 recorded IDP sites across the country (OCHA Somalia: Humanitarian Dashboard - September 2018). The majority of sites are informal settlements on private land in urban areas. Living conditions for displaced families in the sites continue to decline, and returns to the areas of origin is extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services, which are concentrated in urban areas. IDPs live informal sites with limited basic needs either due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support due to poor targeting of sites. Coordination of services in all the settlements continues to be problematic, with yet insufficient information sharing among service providers and weak feedback mechanisms between the local administrations, humanitarian organization and the IDPs. Generally, the living conditions of IDPs remain substandard in Somalia. The sites are poorly maintained, overcrowded, unplanned, and unclean. Most of the sites do not have fencing, lighting, basic site-planning (access roads, fire breaks) or sufficiently accessible and safe facilities, and in some cases are located in severely flood-prone areas. CCCM services are needed to reduce barriers to assistance for displaced people, ensure their access to basic services, enhance the accountability of humanitarian actors to affected populations, and strengthen community participation and empowerment through consistent engagement.

Activities

The following activities will be undertaken, in line with the objectives of the Somalia CCCM Cluster Strategy: co-chairing sub-national CCCM clusters in Bosaso, Galkacyo and Beletweyn; participation in detailed site assessments exercises in 10 Districts in Somalia; conducting three intention surveys (in Mogadishu, Baidao and Kismayo); establishment of 10 mobile site management teams (one in each of the districts targeted) for identification and engagement with community governance structures, improved monitoring, coordination and support for site management; establishment/refining and training on CCCM principles and community engagement for 200 site committees and community governance structures; training on CCCM principles for 150 government officials/local authorities; improvement and maintenance of 200 sites through cash for work, formation of site maintenance committees and provision of 2000 tool kits; installation of 120 solar lights in areas with high protection risks; distribution of 3000 solar torches; construct and furnish 30 community centers; establishment of 30 outreach teams (outreach workers from the IDP communities) for information sharing and sensitization of beneficiaries on services and activities at site level; HLP awareness raising interventions targeting 150,000 individuals; training of duty bearers on HLP (50 committees); development of contingency plans for potential new IDP influxes in each of the targeted districts; advocacy with local authorities for public land for displaced people; mass sensitization through radio programming, sign boards, text campaigns in all targeted districts; sensitize on the use of DRC complaints and feedback mechanism for beneficiaries in targeted communities; support 20 community based initiatives to promote community cohesion and recovery.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Durable solutions is one of the key pillars of DRC Somalia programming, and continuous efforts are directed towards promoting durable solution and self-sufficiency of the displaced and vulnerable people across Somalia. However, DRC recognizes the complexity and long milestones required to reach this vision. This project will contribute towards supporting opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions given people centered response, capacity building for both the government and IDP communities, linking community priorities with national government plans and as well implementing community based initiatives that can promote community cohesion and recovery. IDPs have limited options for durable solutions to their displacement. According to JMCNA findings indicate that the majority of IDPs prefer local integration. This however has so far remained a challenge due to lack of land availability, repeated evictions and minimal livelihoods options. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited, as most IDPs have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian assistance. DRC will make use of the CCCM coordination platforms to advocate Housing, Land and Property rights for the displaced. In the immediate short term, the envisioned relocation sites in Baidoa and Galkayo - both locations in which DRC intends to continue and enhance its CCCM interventions - are directly linked to early solutions in Somalia, therefore falling firmly within DRC’s commitment to further develop solutions programming in a continued effort for bridging the gap from relief to recovery as part of the focus on nexus programming.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

DRC has a comprehensive community entry and targeting package that guides staff. The project was designed in consultation with the targeted communities, and DRC strives to maintain these consultations throughout all phases of the project cycle. The project will be initiated with a familiarization meeting held with relevant authorities and communities in targeted locations, to create awareness on project objectives, activities, target locations, expected outcomes, scope, and type and selection criteria for beneficiaries. During community mobilization sessions, DRC field staff with sound understanding of the community dynamics will provide detailed information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. DRC has a strong framework for advocacy opportunities, on the issue of evictions from IDP settlements and collective centers and promotion of land tenure security for IDPs at the onset of an emergency. DRC will advocate with clan elders, regional and federal governments to provide land with title deeds to enable this category of DACs access to durable solutions. With regards to community interactions and feedback, DRC employs a complaints and feedback platform (hotline) which is free of charge, easily accessible to everyone, and well-known throughout the targeted communities. The system will be further optimized and expanded in view of its use to serve the present CCCM project, and reports will be issued and shared with the CCCM cluster and within the wider humanitarian community, once per semester.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Protection will be mainstreamed throughout all phases of the project cycle and in the implementation of all activities. Implementation of activities, including site maintenance and construction works, will be done allowing full opportunity for engagement to all site residents. DRC will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services through consulting them during the designing of the constructions. Separate side meetings will be convened with women and girls particularly to address their specific needs. Applied vulnerability criteria for prioritization of assistance will include single women, single-headed HH, as well as PLD and elderly, especially if they live alone. DRC will also reinforce activities that focus on women and their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes in conflict affected areas by leveraging women’s roles and leadership in expanding the humanitarian space, ultimately promoting community resilience, and furthering protection of civilians and civilian assets. HLP rights for women will be prioritized whenever title deeds or land rent agreements are signed. Where cash for work will be implemented, activities will be designed to allow participation of men and women, as well as PLD.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Despite the improvements made against insecurity in Somalia and establishment of permanent political institutions, paving the way towards a future with greater peace, this is yet, however, to translate into an improvement of protection needs of the vulnerable people as there are continued conflicts combined with forced evictions. Forced evictions are linked to widespread tenure insecurity, disputes over land ownership and the reclaiming of state property, particularly in urban areas. They usually occur without notice, and often involve violence and the destruction of housing. Most of the people displaced live in temporary housing with no protection and regularly face different threats and violence’s. Wide spread sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), recruitment and use of children, indiscriminate and disproportionate attacks on civilian areas and infrastructure, explosive hazards and forced displacement, remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in Somalia. Most affected groups include women, children, youth and marginalized communities, especially the most vulnerable (older people, persons with disabilities, female-headed households etc.) Through the Protection Cluster DRC is spearheading the protection monitoring initiative – establishing a Somali-wide PM system that will monitor key protection concerns across Somalia to identify trends and patterns of protection risks. DRC’s CCCM will work closely with the Protection cluster to further engage in joint advocacy to address protection concerns.
