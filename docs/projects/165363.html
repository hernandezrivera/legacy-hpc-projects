Project Summary

Targeted groups: Rural households living in southern rural Aleppo (Al-Hader sub-district). The rural households in southern rural Aleppo have suffered from the ongoing conflict which almost entirely displaced the residents of the southern villages and towns. After security was re-established in those villages, the IDPs returned to their original homes which were either partially damaged or destroyed, the returnees also lost the assets which were in their houses, and their farms were adversely affected by the long period of abandonment. For these reasons, the households are unable to afford the minimum basic life-needs, such as basic house furniture, cutlery, clothes, etc. These households lack secure access to market whether because of physical obstacles, lack of means of transportation, or other concerns. Women, children, elderly and people with special needs are the most vulnerable groups. Therefore, we will work through our “NFI Distribution” project to provide them with the basic needs which support both returnees and IDPs by distributing a variety of items that are not available at the UNHCR and can be manufactured as necessary such as: emergency kits (include clothes, shoes, and a firewood stove for heating and cooking) and NFI kits (include solar lamps, mosquito nets, and insect screens)
The main purpose of the project is to ensure the protection of women, men, boys, and girls including those with special needs from the effects of harsh weather through providing life-saving and life-sustaining NFIs. According to the equity principle, it is vital to ensure that all deserving community members are included in the project within the specified geographical area of implementation. As part of case management, the social workers will ensure that neither gender nor age will affect reach to the provided services.
As a complementary activity, the project will also include awareness sessions about various topics of interest for the beneficiaries to provide them with psychological support to alleviate the effects of the crisis. Moreover, the attendees will receive motivators related to the topic of the session.
AL-TA'ALOUF is involved in activities in several sectors including health, protection, WASH and the distribution of non-food items resulting from the Association's belief in the importance of coordination and its role in saving lives and mitigation of protection risks. AL-TA'ALOUF is also highly involved in coordinated planning in health and protection activities.
AL-TA'ALOUF Association has a vast experience in the distribution of non-food items through similar projects in partnership with UNICEF (Winter Clothes Distribution) UNHCR (NFIs Distribution) and WFP (Food Items Distribution).

4 - Needs

AL-TA’ALOUF’s assessment team visited Al-Hader in southern rural Aleppo and contacted the residents (returnees and IDPs) and village mayor to identify their needs, it was clear that there is still a need for the provision of core and essential, seasonal and complementary, life-saving and life-sustaining non-food items, particularly for people who suffered from sudden displacements and those who returned to their homes to find nothing of their assets and who are exposed during winter and summer. As a result, the team concluded that the residents are in a dire need for clothes and furniture, particularly because these groups are of the most vulnerable and poorest. Currently, 11,500 people live in this sub-district, including IDPs. Therefore, our project will provide a variety of items that are not available at the UNHCR and can be manufactured as necessary such as: emergency kits (include clothes, shoes, and a firewood stove for heating and cooking) and NFI kits (include solar lamps, mosquito nets, and insect screens). to support the basic needs for poor and vulnerable households, particularly in winter. There will also be awareness sessions about various topics of interest for the beneficiaries to provide them with psychological support to alleviate the effects of the crisis. Due to the current security situation in Idleb governorate, we have added 1000 beneficiaries in case in sudden displacement movements happen. The overall number will be 12500 beneficiaries.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risks that may occur due to over crowdedness in the distribution points (sexual abuse and harassment). Mitigation measures: separating women and men in the distribution points through arranging separate lanes. Distributing items at separate locations and hours for males and females.2. Risk of sexual abuse by one of the employeesMitigation measures: providing a complaint box and a hotline to report any abuse or harassment. Moreover, the staff are trained on the PSEA that prevents any form of sexual abuse or exploitation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1. Raw materials are not always available in the local market.Mitigation measure: Purchasing the necessary raw materials at the onset of the project to avoid their unavailability in the local market at a later time.Risk 2. Fluctuating exchange rate, which can unexpectedly raises the prices of materials.Mitigation measure: Purchasing the necessary materials at the onset of the project to avoid purchasing it at a higher price later.Risk 3. Scarcity of energy sources, which raises the costs of provided services.Mitigation measure: Relying on generators for power supply.
