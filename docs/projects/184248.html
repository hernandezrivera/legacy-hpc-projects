Project Summary

The population of Somalia consistently experiences some of the worst health outcome indicators in the world; the average life expectancy is estimated to be 51.5 years. Somalia is among the countries with the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates of, respectively, 76.6 and 121.5 per 1,000 live births. The maternal mortality rate (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) and the fertility rate (average of 6.9 children per woman) are also among the highest in the world, 79 per cent of all births are home delivered without skilled assistance. Overall, 99 per cent of Somali women have undergone female genital mutilation, 64 per cent of these having suffered the Pharaonic type. Overall, only 11 per cent of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated, around 19 per cent of children in urban areas have received all basic vaccinations, while this is the case for less than 1 per cent of children in nomadic areas. The inadequate comprehensive and reliable epidemiological and disease information from all districts makes it difficult to ascertain the extent of gaps in service provision and burden of disease across the country and effectively plan for response among actors. The health status indicators include the coverage of DPT3 and Penta3 vaccinations among children less than 1 year old and the coverage of measles vaccination among children less than 5 years old. The health resources are measured by the presence of functional health facilities per population. For the contextual factors, the overall severe and global malnutrition and the IPC phase were used. For each indicator thresholds were established from severity 1 to 5. During the final analysis, health status was weighted at 40 per cent, health resources at 25 per cent and contextual factors at 3 per cent. Based on this methodology, it is estimated that over 6.56 million Somali people require lifesaving essential healthcare in 2022. Excess mortality in Somalia is driven primary by malnutrition, disease outbreaks (e.g., COVID-19, AWD/cholera, measles, malaria) and violence; as well as non-communicable disease including complications of pregnancy and chronic disease. These are compounded by displacement due to conflict and natural disasters and lack of preventative and curative healthcare. Since the onset of COVID-19 in March 2020 until the 4th of October 2021, a total of 19,980 cases were confirmed in Somalia with a case fatality rate of 5.6 per cent. Vaccine availability is limited, and the vaccine coverage is below 2 per cent by the end of September 2021.People most affected by absence of health care services live in inaccessible districts under control of non-state armed groups. There are no known functional health facilities or services in those districts and health needs remain unaddressed. Key informants in 48 per cent of hard-to-reach settlements assessed reported having no access of any kind of health services. Health services for host communities are scarcer in rural areas than in urban settlements. Young children, girls and women of reproductive age and persons with a disability are most at risk of dying because of a lack of access to health services. In 2022, overall health needs are likely to remain insufficiently addressed. Health services have eroded to bare minimum levels in many locations as health partners face severe funding gaps. Health service delivery in Somalia remains dependent on humanitarian partners who finance and deliver a large proportion of health care to the population. In the absence of sufficient funding there is a danger of erosion of minimum standards of care of primary health care services. Aid vision is already on the ground handling 8 health facilities 4 in middle Shabelle region and 4 in Banadir region. These facilities are providing accessible and quality primary health care services to the vulnerable communities including pregnant and lactating women, children and people living with disabilities

1. - Projects activities

Continuation of giving primary health care services at Shangani and Garasbaley health centers in Banadir region, hantiwadaag and xawo taako health centers in Jowhar and balcad districts respectively of middle Shebelle region to solve immediate and long-term health needs of 42,000 persons living in the districts including persons living with disabilities. Provisions of free medical consultations for both under-five (4284 girls and 4116 boys) and over five (16053 Female and 12113 Male) patients attending the outpatient unit and treated for common illness Increase access to quality health care services to 36570 direct beneficiaries in Garasbaley, Shangani of Banadir Region, Jowhar and Balcad district of Middle Shabelle Region Provide Free Antenatal Care services to 3750 pregnant women visiting the facilities Provide effective, safe and quality EMOC interventions in an acceptable and efficient manner to 1680 delivering mothers and provide postnatal services to all mothers and infants after delivery. Train community health worker (20 females and 20 male) on COVID19 awareness Conduct Training for community health workers (20 females and 20 male) on AWD/Cholera Case management Carry out routine immunization services against preventable diseases like measles and Penta3 for six days in a week. Provision of micro nutrient supplementation to 3750 PLW mothers to prevent anaemia Train facility staff (15 females and 15 male) on integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI) and emergency preparedness. Train 6 health staff to provide comprehensive GBV/CMR services to women, girls, boys and men in the target district Train 6 facility staffs on MHPSS and inclusive health care provision in order provide quality and timely MPHSS services at the targeted three facilities.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Aid Vision will ensure all minority and majority as well as people living with disabilities are involved in the implementation of the project and be able to transparent in terms of employment. Doctors, nurses and Community health workers selection and training will also consider gender sensitivity including men and women. All this will ensure gender and equity issues are addressed among the community members. Aid Vision will collect gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to health activities to ensure they have equal access to quality health care services. Aid Vision will address health inequities by improving access to health by all segments of the affected population including pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months and people living with disabilities have equal access to health assistance. Aid Vision will do social mobilization mechanisms seeking behavior change on minority and marginalized communities who are at risk of being neglected/ excluded are also given quality and equal health care services to prevent common illnesses. Aid Vision will also advocate community participation in the implementation of the program. This will help in getting complaints and feedback responses that enhances effective decision making and promote accountability to the affected population. Aid Vision proposed project is designed in way that prioritizes the rights, safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. The target communities are fully involved and aware of the roles of each stakeholder where each sees the other as partners and equals. The proposed project will not discriminate against colour, age, sex and origin against any beneficiary and will enable equal and impartial assistance and services for all. Targeting of vulnerable children and adults will be done on basis of their specific needs. The staff will be inducted on their duties and responsibilities and particularly the rights of the beneficiaries to ensure there are no any form of patient exploitation at the facilities and Aid Vision offices. Watertight mechanisms will be put in place to ensure served populations voices are heard and can file their complaints without being victimized.
