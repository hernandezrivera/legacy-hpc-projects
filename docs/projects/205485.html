Project Summary

Generally the main drivers of WASH needs include but not limited to economic underdevelopment, lack of resources, and insufficient infrastructure which have left the people of South Sudan highly vulnerable to the long-term effects of climate change. The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is as a result of an accumulation of interlocking factors; the conflict, sub-national inter-communal violence, economic underdevelopment, flooding, and climate change. The conflict in neighboring Sudan threatens to destabilize the already fragile situation in the country further.
Based on WASH Cluster interactive dashboards, Cluster response and monitoring framework and IOM displacement tracking matrix. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites.
The Sudan-South Sudan border is actively used by refugees, migrants, and South Sudanese for cross-border trade. This have also increased the WASH needs to the returnee’s arrivals at border crossing points into South Sudan. Aid Support Community Organization HRP 2024 project will focus on providing life-saving water, sanitation, and hygiene services at points of entry and in transit locations for displaced people to access daily survival requirements, enabling health and well-being to prevent WASH related diseases.
The main goal of this WASH project is to improve wellbeing of people affected by disaster and chronically exposed to lack of basic water and sanitation services in the targeted counties. Despite of a typically widespread and severe flooding due to climate change affecting large population of people, the project will contribute to reducing water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality and provide safe, equitable access to water and sanitation basic services.
The intensity of flooding dramatically keep on increasing, affecting more than a million people with unprecedented flooding with severe damages caused to infrastructures, including water and sanitation facilities. Consequences of flooding includes increased risk of WASH-related diseases outbreaks, reduce access to water and sanitation facilities.
Flooding affected different parts of the country, leaving the people more vulnerable with increased humanitarian needs including WASH needs. Currently the living standard and wellbeing of estimated 6.1 million people are negatively impacted by inadequate access to clean water, sanitation and poor hygiene practices. Its estimated 6.3 million people’s living standard and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2024 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation and hygiene.
Conflict is also a factor that has affected water access. Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points both rural and in urban settings and cross border points where women feel unsafe while accessing WASH facilities.
The response will mitigate WASH related GBV with high risk of protections issues by enhancing safety audits at site level to identify GBV concerns and implement mitigation measures where women shall be given equal opportunities in project implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project will ensure WASH facilities are safe, gender appropriate and maintained in secure locations with most focus on women, girls and persons with disabilities.
Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in the focused locations. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases which will be mitigated.
The project will target 11111beneficiaries’ women 3000, girls 2889 men 2555 and boys 2667to undertake different roles and activities of the project. Community approach and vulnerability criteria will be used in beneficiary selection.

1. Needs

The HRP 2024 overall objective is to contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality and prevent outbreaks among affected populations. WASH needs are high among IDPs and returnees in non-camp settings and among their already stretched host communities. While host communities and non-displaced people are in need of WASH items and facilities, increasing needs are expected with recently returned refugees and IDP groups from Sudan.WASH situation is projected to worsen. This outlook is driven by the impact of recurring heavy flooding due to climate change. Limited access to WASH infrastructure such as water points and sanitation facilities. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable groups, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition.Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. The project focused counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water compromising the benefits gained in nutrition programs.WASH differential needs, concerns and priorities of affected population women, men, boys and girls including elderly people and people with disabilities of different gender and age disparities will be addressed basing on the main cluster objectives. Provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees from Sudan.High malnutrition rates are strongly linked to poor WASH conditions exemplified in the diarrhea-malnutrition cycle which is prevalent in vulnerable populations. Ensure nutrition sites has water and soap for washing hands after visiting the latrine, children with SAM in the Nutrition centers will be provided with WASH standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. Soap and hygiene kits for mothers with children of SAM/ MAM cares provided in nutrition sites, no garbage in the nutrition sites and WASH messages provided in the nutrition sites by ensuring water for appetite test is treated with aqua tabs and availability of soap and water in hand washing stations.Based on the needs assessment on WASH Cluster dashboards, the selected counties face multi-context crisis: conflict, floods, food insecurity and the collapse of healthcare and social services are key drivers. WASH needs are also closely linked to Nutrition, protection and health needs. Hygiene and sanitation situation with low standard sphere of WASH. Increases in acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, displacement, poor access to WASH services, high morbidity and mortality as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Widespread lack of safe drinking water, sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices has left a large proportion at risk of preventable WASH related diseases. The consequences of lacks of access to clean drinking water, poor sanitation and inadequate basic health services would likely cause serious water borne diseases and increases the prevalence rate of cholera outbreaks and WASH related diseases. This project will provide will provide life-saving WASH services and durable access to sanitation facilities with locks and design improvement agreed through focus group discussions to be conducted by female staff with women and girls to mitigate WASH related GBV risks, Lack of latrines in many nutrition sites, residential areas, schools, markets health facilities and cross border points have compelled the communities to practice open defecation which is a health concern.
