Project Summary

The proposed intervention will be implemented by Norwegian Church Aid (NCA) in five conflict affected Local Government Areas (LGAs) with high emergency response need Borno state, Nigeria.
The proposed intervention focuses on provision of emergency water, sanitation and hygiene assistance to the most vulnerable populations (women, men, girls and boys) living in conflict affected areas with high need of WASH services, mainly for the new arrivals and populations residing in and in close proximity to the over-congested camp settings, as well as locations where there is limited availability of WASH services including Gwoza-Pulka, Mafa. The proposed project will cater for a total of 55,000 individuals including women, girls,men and boys and capacity building of local stakeholders including women and the formation of WASH committees to ensure active participation and leadership of hygiene promotion, including MHM, and sanitation activities and the effective management of water facilities. The proposed intervention feeds into the Humanitarian Response Plan 2019-2021 (HRP) and is coordinated through respective WASH sector with lead agencies as well as other partners in the target areas. The project will be well coordinated and some field level leadership will be taken by NCA to ensure quality provision of services and avoid duplication of efforts with other actors, hence NCA will take charge of specific locations and camps with full responsibilities of WASH activities.

Needs

3.4 million people need support to access safe water and basic sanitation facilities across the BAY states caused by the conflict, including recurrent, unpredictable and continuous population displacements. In 2018, an estimated 240,000 people arrived from hard-to-reach areas in camps and informal settlements in 43 LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states, further overcrowding existing WASH facilities in these sites. In 73 IDP camps across 11 LGAs in Borno State, the average allocation of water is 14 liters per person per day, below minimum standard of 15 liters, while IPDs are sharing a drop-hole latrine with an average of 72 people, below minimum standard of 50 people per drop-hole latrine . Disease especially cholera outbreak some LGAs in BAY state has been particularly deadly. At least 18 LGAs have been affected with 4,250 cases reported, including 112 associated deaths, with women and children disproportionately affected and 33 per cent of cases in Borno state. Systematic chlorination in affected areas is required along with the promotion of good hygiene practices, and the replenishment of emergency supplies (water containers, aqua tabs for household, water treatments, and soap).The emergency nutrition situation faced by newly-arrived children from hard-to-reach areas is of critical concern for the WASH sector, with an average GAM prevalence of 11 per cent and above in 9 LGAs in Borno State . This includes at least 28,723 children screened in 9 LGAs in Borno State, between October 2017 and August 2018, with GAM rates ranging from 11.4 to 50.1 per cent. Those proxy indicators reveal a poor nutrition status and an urgent need to improve WASH conditions which are key to cutting malnutrition rates, as exemplified in malnutrition-diarrheal diseases cycle.Lack of access to safe water and other WASH facilities poses specific protection risks including sexual violence and harassment. Only 38 per cent of latrines are segregated by sex (39 per cent in Borno State) and 31 per cent of latrines do not have lock inside, which make latrines unsafe or potentially inaccessible for women and girls and most of the sexual abuses happen in latrines with no sex-dis aggregation, and without locks and not well-lit. Specifically and in addition to emphasize the above, NCA WASH rapid assessments (Aug-Sep 2018) in Mafa, Pulka, Dikwa and Monguno LGAs highlighted the specific needs. High influx of IDPs has increased pressure on existing WASH facilities leading to high use ratio, conflicts, facilities breaking down, and increased risk of diseases. Community management structures such as WASH committees are nonexistent or nonfunctional, WASH facilities being abandoned/not maintained. Water supply pumps were not working, and in disrepair (38% water points damaged). Water storage for 27 % of HH is less than 60l/HH (8l/p) and 41% of the containers used to store water are dirty and old. Forty-four percent of the respondents, mainly women and girls, declare that they are not able to collect water when they need to, and 52% percent of water access is too far and 30% of the families have less than 40 l (2 jerry cans) for water collection. Existing latrine blocks (20 in Pulka, 39 in Mongunu, 10 in Ngala, and 30 in Dikwa IDP camps) required immediate desludging to provide hygienic sanitation for 4,950 people. About 90 latrine blocks and showers.

Activities

NCA will implement various activities to address the needs of the vulnerable population and in closed collaboration with the local population as listed below.1. Provision of safe water through new construction and rehabilitation of water systems: NCA will construct drill boreholes that provide everyone with at least 15 liters of safe water per day in IDP camps, host communities where IDPs are settled. This will carried out through adherence to WASH global standards and country/sector specific standards in Nigeria. 2. Provision of safe water and sanitation in institutions/ schools, public places, through new construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure. NCA will carryout this activity as communal response in IDP and host community settlement in conflict affected areas with high humanitarian needs and also through an integrated response with other sectors especially during epidemic and WASH facilities and services will be provided in full compliance with the Nigeria Emergency WASH package in institutions. 3. Provision of sex-segregated sanitation facilities (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) in formal and informal IDPs settlements: NCA will consolidate and expand coverage to reach the sector’s standard of a maximum of 50 people/latrines and 100 people/shower. NCA will also in full compliance with the WASH sector’s Nigeria Emergency Technical guidelines,ensure latrines and shower blocks will be physically separated and pictorially demarcated by sex, in Hausa and Kanuri languages, with a 1:3 ratio for men to women. Each block of latrines and showers will have one compartment built for easy access and use by disabled and elderly people( make it disable friendly). Additionally, locks will be installed on the inside of the latrines to allow users security and privacy and mostly prevent sexual abuse. 4. Maintenance of provided WASH facilities: NCA will collaborate with affected communities and where necessary, trained laborers/technicians and contractors, to ensure the functioning and maintenance of installed/rehabilitated water systems including boreholes, pumps, solar panels, overhead tanks, taps, chlorination, as well as sanitation and hygiene facilities through latrine desludging, fixing latrine doors/locks, and regular restocking of hand washing stations. In addition, NCA will conduct regular safety audits it GBV sector and other protection actors to monitor and address safety concerns related to WASH facilities in participatory way.5. Garbage collection in formal and informal IDPs settlements: In collaboration with CCCM/Shelter/NFI Sector, government agencies and other WASH partners, NCA will mobilize IDP community laborers and committees and provide cleaning/waste management kits to ensure garbage collection and disposal and ensures safe treatment through burial and/or incineration and disposal of removed waste. 9. Promotion of tailored hygiene messages to strengthen hygienic behavior and practices, including MHM: NCA develops and delivers targeted hygiene messages through a variety of mechanisms including focus group discussions, door-to-door visits, speaker campaigns, radio messaging, jingles, panel discussions, theatre, shows, distribution of leaflets and posters. 10. Provision of basic hygiene items including top-ups: NCA will provide hygiene/MHM kits to affected populations in IDP camps and host communities which include water fetching and storage containers, kettles, a child potty with lid, soap for bathing and laundry, reusable sanitary pads, female undergarments, clothes pins and a rope. Replenishment items will also be provided after initial distributions, based on NCA's assessments/monitoring. 13. Strengthening and expanding coordination and partnership: NCA will continue its existing coordination mechanism in Mafa and will aim to expand presence. NCA will also continue to participate in Technical working groups -Hygiene promotion, WASH, Cholera task force, WASH sub sector coordination.
