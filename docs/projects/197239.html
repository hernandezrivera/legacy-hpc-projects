Project Summary

The project of WASH and ES/NFIs Support Project in ‘South Darfur’ state aims to contribute to increased stability and emergency assistances well-being of 44,425 conflict affected Households and vulnerable communities in South Darfur State of Sudan by providing alternative WASH and Protection and improving WASH intervintion . The project will consider the gender issue ( MWGB) in the project cycle, within 46% from the total number of targeted will be Women, 34% will be Boys and Girls and 20% will be men.
The project will target host and returnee agro-pastoralist communities in five localities of Kass, Nyala, Kubum, Ed ElFursan and Rehed al-Birdi.
Also , the project aim to contribute directly to ES/ NFIs Sector:
Affected populations have timely access to basic emergency shelter or NFIs and domestic items to mitigate health threats and to sustain or improve living condition
This programme aims to ‘Contribute to sustainable livelihoods and economic well-being of 44,425 HH.

1 - Needs

Sudan is ranked 147 in the world with an infant mortality rate of 51.2 per 1,000 live births and under-five mortality rate of 76.6 in every 1,000 live births. 53 per cent of Sudanese are in multi-dimensional poverty with 40 per cent being in severe multi-national poverty. 40 per cent of the country’s population of 38.8 million people (i.e. 15.2 million people) lives on degraded land. The protracted conflict in Sudan has increased vulnerabilities associated with the arid environment and harsh climate and contributes significantly to food insecurity. The government of Sudan’s capacity to meet the basic needs of its citizenry suffered a significant setback with the secession of South Sudan causing the economy to shrink by over 14 per cent from 2011. In 2016, some 4.6 million Sudanese were deemed food insecure and in need of assistance. Two (2) million children were suffering from wasting (GAM) while 550,000 were diagnosed as being severely malnourished (SAM). Seven (7) of the 18 States in Sudan had a global acute malnutrition prevalence of over 15 per cent; which is above emergency threshold as per WHO standards. Malnutrition and food insecurity is particularly high in displaced populations.The plateau of Jebel Marra has a high altitude, which is estimated at about 3 thousand meters above sea level, and covers an area of 12,800 square kilometers, and in proportion to the high altitude, water descends from it in large quantities and in a short period extending from one to three months, which enables the local people to benefit from the quantity The water slope during the year, which exceeds 120 million cubic meters, these natural factors exceed the capabilities of the local population to benefit from the seasonal rain water, livelihoods and the development of life and livelihood systemsThe project will working to contribute in developing livelihood opportunities and WASH backed for the most venerable IDPs, Returnees and Host-communities in South Darfur, by enhance and developing of the individual incomes Sudan covers a large protracted humanitarian crisis over the past 19th years ago, the severity and scale of needs remain far-reaching from the actual assistance needs. Although there has been an improvement in the security situation after the signed Juba Peace Agreement (JPA), but new displacement continues both from Returnees and IDPs in some other area in Sudan. Still, there are more than About 9.3 million people live in protracted displacement camps in Sudan, especially in the Darfur States. (Humanitarian Overview 8 September 2022). The ongoing economic crisis continues to be a key driver of deteriorating humanitarian needs, which have been exacerbated by COVID-19. Control and containment measures increase the cost and time taken to deliver the basic humanitarian service. Flooding is a seasonal occurrence in Sudan and contributes to shelter and NFI needs as a protective measure. Heavy rains caused damage to houses, livestock, farmland, infrastructure, and services. Floods-caused damages were recorded in all States in Sudan. Inflation and currency depreciation have led to shortages of fuel and cash, price increases of important basic commodities, thus reducing household purchasing power. There is a need for durable solutions (return and urban integration) for IDPs and returnees alike including a need to provide environmentally friendly transitional shelters, together with community training on building techniques and livelihood opportunities to promote self-reliance and resilience. Such as brick making, a weaving of traditional mats, provision of fuel-efficient stoves, and basic repairs. However, there are also negative coping mechanisms used by people in need of other services, such as WASH health or education and livelihood interventions.Additionally, Returnees in protracted situations require replenishments of NFIs based on protection assessment/findings, as access to livelihoods is limited.
