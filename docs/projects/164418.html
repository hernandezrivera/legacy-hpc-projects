Project Summary

The project aims at maintaining and improving equitable access to formal and non-formal Education for the most vulnerable groups in NW Syria, by targeting 720,000 children aged between (5-17) and Teachers (women and men) years old (360,000 females 360,000 males) with focus on provision of inclusive Education that supports Education of most marginalized girls and boys and considering their needs during the development of intervention’s lo-frame. The project also provides job opportunities for more than 70 educational actors from both genders. The population in the targeted areas are considered to be in a high severity of need of humanitarian assistance because of the high numbers of IDPs and returnees.
QC is aiming at achieving the main pillars below:
1- Conducting BTL campaigns in 20 communities in NW Syria. Activities will target children and adolescents with age tailored activities. Brochures and posters in classrooms will be distributed to advocate the right of education for children focusing on those who are most vulnerable.
2-Maintain and improve access to equitable Formal Education that is inclusive through:
a- Rehabilitation of 100 Schools in NW Syria. Rehab will cover both schools building and WASH facilities and will consider the gender context and encourage disabled children (girls and boys) to enroll in school by providing necessary facilitation to overcome the physical access barriers as much as possible.
b- Support Teachers and other Educational personnel with monthly allowances in order to motivate them to continue teaching in schools.
c- Conduct training for 3,000 Education personnel on (child-centered and protective pedagogy, active learning, self-learning, life skills, Curriculum B, ECCE, New Curriculum, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE minimum standards (MS))
d- Printing School Textbooks for all children (girls and boys) from 1st grade to 12th grade (including scientific and literature)
e- Support children wellbeing during school time by applying PSS and recreational activities.
f- Provision of digital learning opportunities for children in formal school in NW Syria.
g- Distribution of stationery kits, PSS and recreational materials in the supported schools.
h- Provision of running costs for the supported schools to cover schools ongoing expenses.

3- Enhancing access to equitable non-formal education that aims at re-integrating children in the formal Education process after eliminating all the barriers by:
a- Applying non-formal Education modalities (remedial classes, basic literacy and numeracy BLN, accelerated learning program ALP, Catch-up classes and SLP) as per to the real need of the child and his/her vulnerability criteria (dropped-out, OOS, low educational achievement, overage children, etc.)
b- Establishment of TECs with needed rehab and furniture considering all the needs of the children with good understanding of the humanitarian context.
c- Provision of Teachers allowances for Education personnel.
d- Distribution of stationery kits, PSS and recreational materials
e- Support center with running costs, fuel for heating and other logistic needs
f- Provision of stationery kits, PSS and recreational materials in the supported TECs.
g- Support disabled children (girls and boys), and also girls and boys in need with school transportation.
All the planned activities will take into consideration the ‘Do No Harm” principle. Selection process of locations will be need-based with refreshed assessment and analysis of the targeted communities.
QC through its policies and guidelines, will assure that hired staff are qualified, civilians with clean sheet (not integrated in terror action nor mentioned in sanctions lists) through vetting staff names on world-check paid system. QC through the communication with the HQ in Doha will confirm that received and transferred money are traceable and not a part of any money-laundry process.

4 - Needs

As per to the HNO summary 2020, There is a total of 7-million people in need of Education support; (6.8 million people severity 3-6), 86% of these people in need have acute and immediate need of humanitarian education assistance (severity of 4-5-6). As per OCHA’s population numbers there is an overall increase in the school aged population (5-17 years old) of 5% from 2018 to 2019 (7% in Aleppo and 3% in Idleb). The gathered info stated that overaged children access primary schools are about 9%. And about 24% in secondary schools.At the same time that attendance and enrollment declines for adolescences protection risks for adolescents’ increases. More than 68% of surveyed communities confirmed incidence of early marriage (28% adolescent boys, 69% adolescent girls). Also, 84% of surveyed communities confirmed the presence of child labor. Additionally, from January-September this year, the Syria MRM verified 519 incidents of recruitment and use of children (93% boys), with 96% used in combat roles, and 20% under the age of 15.It should be noted that Idleb has the both highest percentage of school aged IDPs/returnees (53%) and the lowest rates of enrollment (40%).On the other hand, Reports shows that attacks on education are increasing. As per to MRM reports that between January-September this year:- 145 attacks on education facilities and personnel were verified, as well as other interferences by parties to the conflict.- 90% of these attacks took place in the northwestern Syria, mainly in Idleb.Moreover, a study of 300 schools in Aleppo, Idleb, Raqqa found that:- 99% of students expressed fear of specific threats. The two most common included verbal and/or physical abuse from teachers (32%) and airstrikes (24%).- 41% of children reported “some” or “all” teachers’ / school personnel hit children; 64% stated they shouted at or threatened children- Parents/caregivers felt that boys were greater targets for verbal and/or corporal abuse by teachers.

5 - Implementation of activities

QC will conduct BTL Campaigns in the communities to advocate for the wright of Education to children in all grades. Also, QC will conduct rehab works for lightly and heavily damaged schools and get them equipped with school furniture (Desks, White boards etc.). Also, to improving Quality of Education, QC will enhance the capacity of educational actors by applying training for teachers and school principles on cluster prioritized topics. Meanwhile, QC will support the monthly allowances for teachers and schools’ admin staff to ensure the continuity of education in addition to supporting the monthly running costs of the schools (i.e. water, hygiene, fuel, stationery, etc.) as well as distributing teaching and learning materials, school bags and stationery to beneficiaries and teachers in the targeted communities. QC will print and distribute the textbooks for children in all grades. The project will encourage a community-based education approach by establishing PTAs. Project will be implemented by well identified hierarchy and structured team inside and outside Syria, monitored through QC M&E team. Satisfaction Surveys that are related to services provided, will be held to target beneficiaries and other stakeholders to measure the quality. A post evaluation for QC project will be done after project’s closure, to analyze the impact of projects’ done activities. Meanwhile, a feedback mechanism with clear steps and response deadline will be installed in all project targeted locations. Project will consider all children to facilitate their access equitably to Education. For this sake, children with disabilities’, girls’ and adolescent’s right to Education will be advocated through BTL campaigns activities and Brochures. School Transportation will be provided, and rehab works will consider the access barriers for the disabled children in both school and WASH facilities. Protection mainstreaming will be considered during implementation of Activities by all QC staff-partners

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. This risk to be mitigated by: - All attacks should be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. - Students, their caregivers, teachers and other education personnel should be provided psychosocial support services. Those services should have the capacity, ability and resources to address basic needs. For needs that are beyond what should be reasonably expected from learning services there should be a referral system to specialized protection services. If the organization is not clear what to do it should contact the Child Protection Sub-Cluster for help. - Preparedness trainings such as self-care, first aid, fire drills, evacuation drills and stay-and-shelter drills should be regularly practiced. These processes and procedures should be appropriate for the age of the involved students. Where relevant, students and education personnel should be trained on unexploded ordinance risk awareness. R2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Students participate in bullying of students. This risk to be mitigated by: - Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying.- Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. The application of learning should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support- Students, their caregivers, teachers and school personnel should be encouraged to report issues and have safe access to a trusted person they can report instances of physical punishment or bullying to.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In term of Access to formal and Non-formal Education, we assume the following risks: • Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. The likelihood of this risk is High to take place during the year 2019 with a high impact on project activities. To mitigate this risk QC will: - If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform internal emergency focal point on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners, cluster and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Decisions are taken in advance whether hard copies will be destroyed or not. - Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed. • Attacks on schools. This risk has a High possibility and High impact comparing with 2018 reported incidents. QCimmediately will: - Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc.) andmethodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks. - Duty of Care policies are in place, and the staff are informed on the responsibilities as well as limitations of the organization. - Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff - Using OCHA’s Deconfliction Mechanism as a method to hold attackers accountable for their actions QC will also Construct and rehabilitate classrooms and provide schools with school furniture and running costs.
