Project Summary

The activities under Sector Objective 1 aim to give access to livelihood opportunities to vulnerable households (HHs), while at the same time improving local infrastructure relevant for Agriculture and, more broadly, the livelihoods sector. Activities include the provision of short-term labour opportunities to skilled and unskilled workers, supporting 214 HHs, small business opportunities for 50 HHs and support to small-medium enterprises for 10 HHs. These activities are complementary to the Agricultural- and Livestock-based activities included in the submission for the FSA sector. As this project continues in 2020, it is expected that 30 HH will be supported in 2019 and 30 HHs in 2020. The budget included in section 5 is only for 2019.
Temporary labour opportunities will involve the completion of essential works, both those prioritised by the local council and benefitting the wider community in the target locations, particularly in relation to agricultural infrastructure (e.g. seed storage, agricultural roads, livestock water points, etc.) as well as providing increased income to the households participating in the Action. It is anticipated that with the reinvigoration of agro-pastoralist livelihoods there will be continued demand for agricultural labour beyond the project. In addition, supporting small-scale income generating activities, and small/medium businesses and market actors to re-start or expand their activities will contribute to business development, generate income and enable them to sustain their operations, contributing to the functioning of the local market. Capacity building and training of local stakeholders in improved and sustainable agricultural practices as well as the involvement of local councils and extension workers throughout project activities will ensure that knowledge and capacity gained will remain within the community with a view to building sustainability at household and community level.
The project will involve both IDPs and host communities, selected based on selection criteria. Eligibility criteria includes the ability to work; however, a key project selection criterion is considerations to increase the inclusion of women and people with unique needs. As actors are facing challenges in identifying projects that can be implemented through CfW and are at the same time beneficial for the community and culturally appropriate for women, SC has raised the concern for discussion within the Cash-Based Response TWG with the objective of obtaining lessons learnt from the international community to achieve higher women’s participation. Vulnerability criteria will prioritize households with pregnant and lactating women and children under 2, who are most at-risk for malnutrition, and will likely include female-headed households, households will high dependency ratios, households with a person with a disability as appropriate.

Needs

High unemployment rates and limited purchasing power are limiting HHs’ ability to meet their food and non-food needs. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. This is further confirmed by a review of local minimum rates for unskilled work carried out in different locations of Aleppo and Idleb Gov. in 2018, where 20 days of unskilled labour at minimum market rates would pay 90-100 USD, an amount below the SMEB. As per ESCWA, an estimated 83.4 percent of Syrians live below the upper (moderate) poverty line which is an increase from 28 percent in 2010. Additionally, the Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) which takes into account health, education and basic needs related indicators showed that the national poverty rate in Syria is at 38.9 percent. Further, with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. According to a need assessment conducted by Syria Relief in northern Aleppo in April 2018, HHs’ purchasing power is negatively impacted by hyperinflation and a lack of complimentary income generation activities and employment opportunities. Debt is by far the most common coping strategy, with 68 percent of people relying on debt to procure basic necessities. Just over half of respondents reported sending their children to engage in labour activities--particularly in agricultural day labour. Finally, 21 percent of respondents said that they have reduced their consumption of key commodities to cope.

Implementation of activities

As the vulnerability of populations in northern Aleppo has increased due to multiple displacements, the importance of temporary labour has increased. While many poorer households rely on agricultural day labour as a primary source of income, this is by definition a seasonal employment opportunity. SC proposes to assist poorer HHs in a two-prong approach: 1) indirectly through Result 1, which provides support to middle households who have land, but require support to cultivate it—and will likely hire day labourers; and 2) through temporary labour opportunities during months with low levels of agricultural labour. Participating households will be provided with up to 3 months of work, depending upon the project and in line with the recommendations of the Cash-Based Response TWG. Labour wages will be set slightly below the labour market rate, in order to not disrupt the casual labour market and attract only the most vulnerable participants. SC will coordinate labour rates with other actors in the area and through the newly established Cash Technical Working Group in Amman. SC will adjust the duration and number of hours worked in order to ensure that beneficiaries earn enough to meet their basic needs, as defined by the amount of REACH’s Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB), to the extent possible. Simultaneously, this activity is designed to contribute to the reconstruction/rehabilitation of key community assets, with a prioritization of infrastructure that directly link to agricultural livelihoods, including agricultural roads, market roads, drainage canals, small-scale gravity oriented irrigation schemes, etc. SC will work member of the local councils and representatives of different community groups to spearhead a participatory process to identify and prioritize community projects for temporary labourers to complete. Prior to the start of consultation, SC and partners will carry out an analysis of power dynamics in the communities where the project is implemented in order to identify which groups in the community may be marginalized in the decision making process. In order to be selected, projects must benefit most or all of the community; not complete work that people would normally do on their own; have a realistic plan in place for sustainable maintenance; be time- and cost-efficient; utilize predominantly unskilled labour; and include considerations to increase inclusion of women and people with unique needs.SC will provide small business support to strengthen economic activity among the target populations. This activity will benefit individuals in peri-urban and urban areas, to balance the support provided in rural areas under this Action. SC will focus on individuals who currently operate a profitable business, and will utilize their grants to expand employment opportunities (e.g. either through hiring more employees or expanding the number of hours/days current employees work), increase purchases from individuals (likely farmers) from rural areas who produce raw inputs (e.g. vegetables), and/or strengthen key components of food security or agricultural value chains. SC will also reserve a smaller number of beneficiaries who have prior experience in business management, and are seeking to re-start after losing their assets due to the crisis; this option is designed to encourage female participation under this Result and will require a cost-sharing component. SC will focus this Result in predominantly peri-urban and urban areas, but will also announce the opportunity to beneficiaries under other Results; this will allow Results to leverage one another. Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Increased tensions among IDP and host populations due to beneficiary targeting Likelihood = Medium; Impact = HighMitigation measures: Community-based targeting with representative group, community mobilisation/sensitisation on objectives of assistance, monitoring and feedback/complaints systems in place. Risk: Increased tensions within the community over prioritization of project and/or barrier to access for marginalized groups due to power dynamics Likelihood = Medium; Impact = MediumMitigation measures: Analysis of power dynamics carried out prior to engage with the community to identify infrastructure to rehabilitate to identify under-represented points of view and ensure their inclusion in the prioritization process Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors in beneficiary targeting Likelihood: Medium; Impact = HighMitigation measures: Community-based targeting with full verification by project staff; functional feedback and complaints mechanism; clear targeting criteria and SOPs, agreed upon with key stakeholders (if feasible), communicated wherever possible at community level; regular post distribution monitoring exercises undertaken. Risk: Increasing prices or declining market functionality leads to reduced ability of cash transfers to cover needs targeted Likelihood: Medium; Impact = HighMitigation measures: Regular market monitoring and analysis, contingency to increase cash transfer value in line with MEB increases if needed Risk: Humanitarian staff and ‘gate keepers’ are mostly male, resulting in women being less encouraged to apply for support, even if they meet the selection criteria.Likelihood: High; Impact = HighMitigation measures: Recruitment processes to encourage gender balance of staff; training of staff on gender sensitive approaches; ensuring women are represented in beneficiary selection committees; and dissemination and sensitization on humanitarian principles to gate keepers at various levels.

Safety & SecurityAll staff movements must be pre-approved and refresher security trainings are regularly conducted. The Hostile Environment Awareness Training is mandatory for NES deployments; local staff have been trained on Advanced Personal S&S. SC’s partners engage with relevant stakeholders and receive timely updates. SC works on a common response to unacceptable conditions. Airstrikes SC supports partners to immediately report security incidents to be analysed and to develop and implement comprehensive safety plans. Loss of access SC and partners conduct thorough security assessments and maintain updated contingency plans. If necessary, SC will suspend programs and work to resume activities. If this is not viable, SC would assess possibilities for relocation. Only in a worst-case scenario would project termination be considered. Work permits Measures are in place to mitigate the Gov. of Turkey (GoT) legislative environment hindering SC’s ability to operate. Shelling No area is immune. Escalation of military activity at Turkey/Syria border SC program locations and contingency plans are under constant review and the Hibernation, Relocation and Evacuation plan is shared with staff. Offices and guesthouses are located away from military bases and have the necessary S&S infrastructure. Attacks by armed groups Security assessments are conducted on potential project sites. Proximity to military locations is monitored and a back-up vehicle is used in high-risk areas. Staff transit must be pre-approved and take place during daylight. Improvised Explosive Devices SC coordinates closely with Tetra Tech, new project locations are cleared by the Mines Advisory Group and follow up trainings in Mine Risk Education are planned for staff. Disruption to cash transfer operations In NES, SC is conducting a review of Hawala delivery to identify mitigation measures/additional suppliers. In NWS, NGOs have seen temporary suspension of Bab Al Hawa and have faced challenges in money transfer due to GoT restrictions.
