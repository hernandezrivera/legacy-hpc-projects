Project Summary

The project will build the ownership and capacity of community-based protection mechanisms and adolescent clubs to identify risk factors for all forms of child abuse and GBV. Referrals for at-risk adolescents and survivors of GBV will be made to case management workers based in PSS centres and in a mobile unit to directly implement extended case management and PSS services in hard to reach areas. Safe spaces centers will be created for women and girls to facilitate confidential discussions, psycho-social support and sexual reproductive health.

Needs

Niger is ranked last (189/189) on both the Human Development Index (HDI) Gender Development Index (GDI) and Gender Inequality Index (GNI). Since 2015, persistent and deadly incursions by non-state armed groups (notably Boko Haram) have contributed to wide-scale deterioration of security and development indicators in Niger, especially in Diffa Region. Embroiled in the Lake Chad Basin crisis, Niger has suffered from ongoing attacks resulting in civilian casualties and routine looting of the population, culminating in mass displacement of Nigeriens. According to the 2018 population in needs update by July 2018, Diffa hosted approximately 249,813 displaced people, among them 118,868 refugees (Nigerian), 104,288 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 25,731 returnees and 940 asylum seekers; all living in difficult conditions under extreme poverty and in need of protection services. According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the majority of persons in need of support are children, estimated at 211,781, among them 108,008 girls (51%). Protection needs, especially amongst children have nearly tripled since last year with higher rates of gender-based violence (GBV) exacerbated by migration flows due to the crisis. Young girls are at risk of early marriage and many children are exposed to rape, family separation, early pregnancy, and are vulnerable to recruitment by armed groups. Access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and psychosocial support (PSS) services are severely limited. Furthermore, female refugees and IDPs, including adolescent girls, face legal barriers preventing them from gaining employment and constraining entrepreneurship. With limited livelihoods options, some adolescent girls and women resort to adverse coping strategies, such as transactional sex, to support themselves and their families. These incidents expose children and adolescents, especially girls to sexually transmitted infections (STI), fistulas, psychosocial distress and other health complications. From April-October 2018, 550 children, mostly girls (61% girls, adolescents) were exposed to various forms of abuse such as sexual and physical violence, child labor and other forms of gender-based violence in Diffa. These incidents are high in the communes of Diffa, Chetemari, and Gueskerou. GBV survivors and other vulnerable children and adolescents have exhibited signs of severe emotional distress including anxiety attacks, insomnia, lack of concentration and exaggerated fears. Vulnerable groups have limited access to services with only a few protection agencies and limited government capacities in case management and service provision. Many existing protection committees are poorly functioning and with severe deficits in materials resulting in low motivation of members who in turn fail to follow-up and address cases thoroughly. The international community recently reiterated the importance of strengthening protection interventions for vulnerable groups, especially girls and women who are most at risk. Plan International Niger (PI-N) is among the few humanitarian actors focused on child protection (CP) and GBV with an established, strong presence in Diffa region. The proposed intervention will primarily work to strengthen protection services for GBV survivors and other vulnerable groups.
