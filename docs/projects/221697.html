Project Summary

HRP 2024 reports that landmines, cluster munitions and other Explosives and Remnants of War (ERW) potentially contaminate an estimated 16 million square meters of land in South Sudan. The highest levels of contamination are located in the Greater Equatoria region (especially on highly productive agricultural land and along main supply routes) and in the Upper Nile and Jonglei states. These hazards severely affect critical activities for communities, inhibiting their access to essential services, including water, education, and health care. HRP rightly states how these remnants limit humanitarian organizations' efforts to provide life-saving assistance and protection, especially in conflict-affected areas. Providing tailored education and awareness to communities to alleviate risks to the most vulnerable groups always go hand in hand with conducting comprehensive surveys and clearing hazardous areas to ensure the safety and security of the population. Mobile Theatre Team (MTT) has been conducting child protection and EORE awareness-raising & prevention messaging activities for more than 15 years for women, men, girls, and boys including children with disabilities. In 2024, MTT is carrying out EORE activities to support safe behaviors amongst women, men, boys, and girls (IDPs and host communities) in Juba County within Lokiril and Lirya Payams. Starting with educating all our Social Workers, the training enables the beneficiaries distinguish between safe and unsafe practices. The proposed populations targeted by the project will actively be involved in peer education across all gender and age groups. The new teams will focus on primary and secondary approaches to reach people with key EORE messages in all areas of operation. Primary approaches will be used for groups identified as the most-at-risk in the community – those living in or close to contaminated areas or other suspected hazardous areas. They will benefit through direct presentations. Secondary approaches will however, involve the most influential stakeholders; people regarded as very close to their population, who act as agencies of change in their communities. Mostly, these constitute religious leaders, teachers, group leaders or representatives who will pass on their acquired Explosive Ordnance Risks education to other members of the population who might not be reached directly with the messages. They will pass on their acquired EORE information to other members of the community who will not be directly reachable. They will also act as agents for passing on field information received from source to local authorities, NMAA and UNMAS. MTT will also coordinate with other sub clusters and clusters such as Child Protection, GBV, such as Education, WASH, Nutrition, and Health to pass on key EORE information. A key aim of the project is to educate the community on how to stay securely in their environments.

1. Needs

Four months ago, on August 3, 2024, an outcome of the ongoing Mobile Theatre Team Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) process in Lirya, Juba, Central Equatoria found an explosive device under the trees and tall grass. The distance was some 20 minutes’ drive between Juba and Lirya close to Ngangla village. In their report entitled, “Survey & Hazard Information for Child Protection Actors”, MTT humanitarian workers reported how villagers came across the explosive device from Ngulere Boma, at a place where they passed to collect firewood, charcoal and to practice open defecation. As an output from their education, the residence identified and reported the incident to the workers who consequently described the device and place on prescribed forms. The people in Western Bahr el Ghazal were, however, not so lucky as The Associated Press (AP) earlier reported how discarded explosives had killed 10 people including kids. AP mentioned how three children were killed when they mistook unexploded ordnance for scrap metal. The commissioner of Jur River County, James Bak said that people in Jebel-Mille area had been gathering mangoes when they came across the unexploded ordnances and assumed it was scrap metal which they started collecting for sale when it exploded on them1. This emphasizes the need to continue EORE, especially at this time of increased return due to the insecurity in Sudan. Although the needs of different population groups are similar, IDPs and returnees are at heightened risk as they may lack local knowledge of contaminated areas and may traverse and settle in marginal and hard-to-reach lands that have previously not been assessed. HRP 2024 reports that at present, nearly 400,000 returnees, primarily South Sudanese, have sought refuge in South Sudan due to conflicts in neighboring Sudan. Eastern Equatoria is also a primary destination for returnees from Uganda. All community members, especially IDPs and returnees, require increased awareness regarding the dangers of ERW through the delivery of EORE. The Mine Risk Cluster has identified Juba, Lainya, Morobo and Terekeka Counties within Central Equatoria as high- risk areas in need of EORE messaging. The four counties will benefit from this proposed project which is tailored to be sensitive to gender, age, and contextual factors, particularly for children who make up the majority of casualties. MTT is currently operating in Juba. The widespread presence and circulation of small arms and light weapons remain a concern, calling for comprehensive awareness programs to mitigate associated risks. Our EORE teams have contributed a lot in the life saving activities as the communities we have educated are now aware of Unexploded Ordinances (UXO) and landmine presence and continue to pass on the knowledge acquired to unreached populations. This has resulted in change on risky behavior, hence reducing the number of victims of UXO and landmines. They have learnt to identify suspicious areas, where they put visible indicators and signs on presence of UXO or landmines for all to see, respect and respond accordingly. One outcome of this project will be to enable the beneficiaries continue to adopt mine-safe behaviours and choose places to live or stay for safe and adopt dignified voluntary returns. 1.
