Project Summary

NGO will implement integrated health and mental health response in Northeast Syria. Primarily the project will support the provision of critical health services in areas where needs and gaps have been identified and the population largely depend on supported facilities for daily health services. Primary and secondary health services will be provided through four hospitals, one COVID-19 center, three primary healthcare clinics (PHCs) in Aleppo (Ain al Arab), Ar Raqqa (Raqqa City, Al Qahtaniyeh, Abo Qobeih), Deir Ez Zor (Kesrah, Jazra Bu Hamayid) and Al Hassekeh (Tal Baydar). Following the donor regulation, available budget and organization capacity; Health services will include the provision of basic primary health services, such as treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses and injuries, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, including locally endemic diseases and health education. It will also provide basic SRH services such as ANC and PNC, family planning services focusing on women and girls of reproductive age, in addition with close coordination with WHO will provide vaccination services for children under five. COVID-19 center will provide isolation, ICU, and services, PHC’s will also provide a referral system to enable the referral of cases that need further services to secondary and tertiary levels. The Organization is using the supported ambulances to make the emergency cases when required to its supported hospitals, or other facilities (not supported by the organization when the services are not available within its own hospitals. The INGO are tracking all the referrals between the facilities to ensure they are completed; the number of completed referrals are recording on daily basis and reporting to the relevant donor. Through the Kidney Centre at Raqqa National Hospital, the project will provide critical lifesaving hemodialysis services to huge catchment areas in NES. The project will increase access to more than one million people including IDPs and host communities.

4 - Needs

As of 2021, an estimated 1.8 million people in NES (up from an estimated 1.2 million in 2019) – more than half the current population of 3.4 million and the highest level ever recorded in NES. Based on the Multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by REACH in February 2021, health is significant challenges for populations across Northeast Syria (NES). The assessment was covered 1,205 communities across Aleppo1 (162 communities), Ar-Raqqa (261 communities), Al-Hasakeh (712 communities), and Deir-ez-Zor (70 communities) governorates. Data was collected between 31 of January -14 of February 2021 from 3,861 KIs (21% female). The findings indicated health as the first top priority for host community/residents and the top three priority for IDP’s. 74% of the communities indicated that households did not have access to health services in the assessed location. Following the Northeast Syria Needs and Response Overview, March 202, NES hospitals system are under extreme stress, and significant investment needed to build hospital systems, plus 24/7 Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (CEMoNC) services for all camps and communities, continuing in shortage of essential medication such as NCDs, pediatric medications, PEP kits, and life-saving drugs across camps and non-camp setting NES population have faced different stressors, which include security and protection risks, lack of access and availability of basic services and resources as well as family, community, and sectarian tensions. National mental health systems tend to be overburdened and inaccessible. More gaps do exist such as limited geographical reach of mental health services, lack of mental health professionals, and limited community and family supports

5 - Implementation of activities

NGO will provide secondary/specialized health care services through all departments in Kobane and Ain Issa, eleven departments in Raqqa, 5 in Kesrah hospitals. Primary health care will be provided at Al Qahtaniyeh, Abo Qobieh in Raqqa; Jazrat Abu Hamayid in Deir Ez Zor; and Tal Bayder in Al Hassekah. Services include treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses/injuries, management of communicable/non-communicable diseases, as well as basic SRH services such as ANC and PNC, family planning services. Support entails essential medicines, consumables, and medical equipment, salaries for health and support staff, health-related training and supportive supervision, and covering running costs. Throughout the implementation, the organization is ensuring to have equal access to its services from all including elderly people, and people with disabilities through providing all needed mobilities, such as wheelchairs, accessing ramps on the facilities building entrances, and priority in consultation. mhGAP doctor will provide consultations to patients. This will involve mental status assessment, screening, diagnosis, and treatment of MH conditions. The patients will be supported to understand their conditions and be offered basic skills to handle their everyday life while adhering to the medication. Frequent reviews will be conducted on the patients to ensure the symptoms of the condition are reduced or alleviated.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of sexual exploitation: The NES context has a high degree of poverty that may make clients resort to commercial sex, sex for food, and other related abuses to security needs. This may have serious ramifications to the programming. Risk of violence/harm toward staff: Certain clients may have mental disturbances that can potentially lead to abuse to staff.: The area also experiences tribal hostilities, external aggressions which can affect project implementation. This can happen as well due to lack of income to most the mental patients who due to their mental status can’t be able to secure meaningful employment and thus demand Financial support from case managers, who are not in a position to help. Risk of work-related, stress, burnout, and vicarious trauma: MHPSS is among the most emotionally and psychologically exhaustive career due to the nature of clients' issues. For this reason, helpers are always faced with a high degree of stress and secondary trauma.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The overall security situation remains stable across NES, The NGO is working closely with the security cluster and other INGO/NGOs security units and maintaining the daily duties accordingly. * Change of military/political control of geographic locations where the NGO implements programs; the risks a mitigated through maintaining strong lines of communication with global and regional security in order to pre-empt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. *Inflation and increased cost of commodities has resulted in a rise in civil unrest, black-market trading and cross-border smuggling have to weekend the self-administration governance in rural areas or Raqqa and Deir ez Zor resulting in a rise in the tribal conflict. As result this came with short-term stops on some programs with tensions became violent. * Operation Peace Springs proves to be challenging for implementing programs as proximity to military targets and continuous fear of a new offensive in the local population has led to short stoppages on humanitarian operations in the area" *COVID-19 has placed restriction of movement, use of public facilities for program implementation and workshops and has required at times for staff to work from home to avoid the risk of infection; per Self Administration decree, medical staff can only visit one medical facility to avoid cross-contamination risks. *inadvertent or unintended engagement with actors listed under international terrorism lists; Bridger Insight (BI) checks are conducted for each vendor/supplier/partner. The NGO has multiple sources for procurement and maintains procurement plans and processes to mitigate delays.
