Project Summary

This project is in line with SDG 6, which is aimed at ensuring availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all. The project will also contribute towards Plan South Sudan’s country strategy 2021 – 2024 core foundation program - FSL, Nutrition and WASH through promoting access to quality WASH information, services and facilities.
This project aims to increase access to potable water from improved water sources for households in Northern Cueibet and nearby communities in order to reduce the risk of water-borne diseases particularly flood-prone areas of Northern Cueibet. The project also aims to improve access to sanitation and hygiene services/items in the communities so as to reduce the prevalence of WASH-related diseases/ diarrhoea and create a healthy environment for quick recovery. The services provided will aim to break the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases such as diarrhoea, skin infections, COVID-19 etc.
Water, sanitation and hygiene improvements within the community will result in:
- Low incidences of diarrhoea within the communities.
- Improved sanitation resulting into prevention of disease and behaviour change particularly at few institutions / facility and within the community through behaviour change communication and distribution of digging tools and latrine slabs within the community.
- Boreholes / improved water sources for the community particularly during the months/duration of flooding.
The project aims to target about 30,000 people in Payams of TiapTiap, Pagor and Citcok of Norther Cueibet Couty with main focus on improving the water and sanitation situation for most vulnerable people including women, children and elderly.
The project will improve the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene status of the area and contribute towards health and nutrition objectives. This will be achieved through the promotion of good hygiene practices for food preparation, stressing the five critical times of hand washing that results into a more hygiene conducive environment for food intake. The project will also restore immediate access to basic and permanent WASH services within the community. Furthermore, availing WASH facilities/services within the community will help in positive behavioural change and ensure quality access to WASH within the community. The capacity of WASH promoters and community mobilisers and Health Workers shall be built to promote WASH key messages within the community. WASH related IEC material shall be provided at all displayable positions in the community and near the water-points. Weekly sessions with Community Resource Persons and Water User Committees shall also strengthen and reinforce key messages earlier provided to communities including conflict sensitivity and resolution techniques at the household level. This will also help to mitigate WASH-related GBV and protection risks that particularly women and children face when spending long hours at the boreholes.
The project will significantly contribute to gender equality, including across age groups. This will be achieved through involving women in all decisions as pertains the direction of the project (eg priority areas of intervention); and having them occupy at least 50% of leadership positions in Water User Committees so that they can improve on their leadership skills.
The specific objective of the project is that: The health and nutrition condition of targeted communities is improved through better access to clean water and increased health promotion and sanitation awareness.

1 - Needs

Through Resolution 64/292, the United Nations General Assembly explicitly recognized the human right to water and sanitation and acknowledged that clean drinking water and sanitation are essential to the realisation of all human rights. The Resolution calls upon States and international organisations to provide financial resources, capacity building and technology transfer to countries, in particular developing countries, to provide safe, clean, accessible and affordable drinking water and sanitation for all. Poverty and the Lack of essential services (i.e., health, education, WASH) are among the principal causes of children's vulnerability (UNOCHA, 2019). Unfortunately, South Sudan has inadequate WASH systems and infrastructure with a high burden of WASH-related diseases and malnutrition. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022, About 8.9 million people are in need, with 24% being women, 53% children and 15% are people with disability. Atelast 69% people are above severe needs classification. Only 39% households reported having enough water to meet their household needs with over 80% of households not having access to a latrine. This has been mainly attributed to long-lasting development challenges and the perception that communities view using a latrine as a taboo. Majority of people within the area lack access to soap and this is considered a main barrier hindering personal hygiene. Taking a multi-sectoral lens, poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has detrimental impact on health of most vulnerable people as can be seen through high prevalence of malnutrition. At the national level, GAM prevalence for children 0-59 months was 11.3 percent while SAM was at 1.7%. The presence of water-borne diseases in children under 5, for example diarrhoea is very indicative of poor WASH conditions. The WASH situation for the first six months of 2022, WASH situation in some locations is projected to worsen. This is driven by impact of heavy flooding seen through the counties. This is projected to cause further disruption to livelihoods, displacement and damage to WASH infrastructure.Access to sanitation remains low in South Sudan, with only 31 percent of people having access to improved water sources for drinking in less than 30 minutes without facing protection concerns. Only 30 percent of house-holds report owning basic WASH items (e.g., buckets/canisters, soap, and mosquito nets) and have improved access to sanitation services and consequently over 70 percent of households reported being affected by water or vector-borne diseases. (FSNMS round 26). Cueibet is one of the 13 counties of extreme concern as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Severity classification; and this has been mainly attributed to flood on top of protracted crisis including subnational violence (FSNMS round 27)County has an estimated population of between 178,800-187,348. The Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2021 reported that 65% of the population were in varying degrees of need with 20,800 described as being in extreme need. Within Northern Cueibet part of Lakes State in particular, the WASH situation is bad. An initial rapid needs assessment done by Inter Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) in January 24-27 2022 shows that more than 80% of 63 boreholes in Northern Cueibet submerged and inaccessible during flooding period in Citcok, Tiaptiap and Pagor, there is lack of hand pump spare parts and maintenance tools in the three Payams, no Water User Committee members formed or reactivated in the 5-years period, inadequate storage facilities like pots and jerry-cans for more than 90% of the households surveyed. This is compounded by flood periods that come from May to November. In addition, there is 100% open defecation, inadequate institutional latrines, inadequate knowledge on most critical moments for handwashing, lack of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits for adolescent girls and young women.

2 - Project activities

1. Construction of 10 New flood –resistant disability inclusive Boreholes in Pagor, Citcok and Tiaptiap. 2. Rehabilitation/Upgrading of 10 existing boreholes in Pagor, Citcok and Tiaptiap and making them accessible to the disabled. Rehabilitation of un-functional water-yard in Pagor. 3. Formation/Reactivation & Graduation of Water User Committee. 4. Training/Mentorship of 21 Water User Committee (Each committee having 5 members) on WASH Management, Revenue Collection, Gender and Protection, Conflict Sensitivity for 2 days per training. 5. Training/Reactivation of 10 hand pump mechanics in some Payams. Procurement of toolkits for hand pump mechanics (Standard and Special toolkits). 6. Construction of 10 flood resistant New Gender segregated and disability inclusive latrines. Provision of latrine digging kits, tarpaulin and latrine slabs.7. Training of 30 Project staffs (Officers, Hygiene Promoters) on WASH, Gender and Child Protection, conflict sensitivity.8. Procurement and distribution of 3 WASH kits / NFIs and safety equipment to Community Resource Persons and most vulnerable women with young children. 9. Printing of IEC visibility items (100T Shirts, 200 WASH IEC materials on WASH Key Messages) for Community Resource Persons. 10. Hygiene Sessions with 30 Community Resource Persons by Hygiene Promoters per week for 4 weeks a month. 11. Quarterly Review Meetings with 30 project staff and stakeholders. Monthly monitoring visits by government staff and stakeholders. Baseline Survey, Endline Survey. External Audits. Production of video coverage. Monitoring visits by Country technical staff on WASH.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Plan International will ensure that a mechanism has continued for the beneficiaries to participate in all processes including the design, implementation, monitoring and close-out stages of all projects. As part of ensuring accountability, communities will have means to provide feedback and complaints about the project continuously through the different mechanisms to be established within the community. Community can inform positive and negative aspects of the project, which can help to improve projects. This will be achieved through use of suggestion book, suggestion boxes, community feedback meeting with selected community leaders/members at each water points during water service provision and afterwards by Monitoring and Evaluation officer. Furthermore, communities can directly notify Plan staff or pass their issues through the community leaders about the project and user satisfaction surveys will also be conducted quarterly to collect feedback. Also, Plan will provide information about services provided near boreholes and institutions and the requirements forgetting services to beneficiaries during the routine monitoring of WASH and nutrition education messages.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

PI will ensure fair representation of different beneficiaries group in the community through including all age and gender groups when assessing where boreholes shall be constructed through Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA); and having special focus groups of different age and gender groups during baseline surveys and monitoring visits. Special consideration will be given for people with different vulnerability criteria to be represented in the community level feedback meeting. This includes always giving them chance to speak and voice their needs during community meetings. The groups are; disability, women and youth groups shall all be consulted when selecting most affected villages within Northern Cueibet to carry out project activities like drilling and sanitation facilities.Targeted actions shall be based on gender and disability inclusion. Barrier analysis shall be done to identify WASH needs related to disability to target about 15% of the targeted beneficiaries living with atleast one disability. This is to ensure that we obtain measures of how feedback can be given to them.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PI has code of conduct on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. Every staff recruited will be given orientation on code of conduct and will sign it to confirm they will abide by it. Furthermore, there will be continuous sensitization. Also, beneficiaries will be oriented about PI code of conduct and they will be notified any concern should they encounter. There are established complaint and feedback mechanisms in place.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

There will be regular meeting with beneficiaries and the beneficiaries will be told about the project and their complaint and feedback will be collected through direct discussion, using suggestion book, suggestion box and complaints will be addressed immediately

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To mitigate the risks identified above, PI will do the following activities 1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services – PI will provide services at the suitable day time and place agreed by beneficiaries, put clear beneficiary targeting criteria and enable local leaders and community know it, work closely with local leaders. Risks of women, girls and boys are exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance - PI will sensitize staff and beneficiaries, ensure reporting mechanisms are in place and are known by staff and beneficiaries. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) - PI will sensitize staff and beneficiaries about services available, establish strong referral linkage with service provider. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms - PI will ensure beneficiaries know and use the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms PI has put in place. This will be achieved through establishing complaints mechanisms jointly with community members during monitoring visits and community review meetings. During distributions of hygiene and menstrual hygiene management kits, gender sensitive help desks shall be put in place; for all gender and age groups to be able to provide feedback to the project staff.
