Project Summary

NRC's project will contribute to Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector HRP objectives 1, 2 and 3.
NRC will provide ERL assistance using, when possible, participatory planning processes and inclusive, context and conflict sensitive approaches that promote social cohesion and local ownership and by implementing Safe and Inclusive Programming throughout the project cycle. Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level and access.
When relevant and feasible, NRC will seek to have integrated/complementary responses that promote synergies with life-saving assistance such as linking livelihoods initiatives with multipurpose cash for basic needs (when critical markets are functional and accessible) and other emergency and sector specific interventions.
NRC will implement the following interventions:
ERL Objective 1: NRC will support the maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services by supporting the rehabilitation of hospitals, bakeries, market system and other infrastructures, training to ensure adequate maintenance. Providing also solar systems support to address the electricity gap. NRC rehabilitation of schools, boreholes, etc. are included under other HRP sectoral projects.
ERL Objective 2:
2.1 Emergency Response: Vulnerable households can meet basic needs and reduce negative coping strategies, as well as to ensure protection of their productive assets and livelihoods
NRC will provide unrestricted cash based assistance, as a primary response modality to empower households to define and attain their own specific needs; Information and Behavior Change: cash will be accompanied by information about the importance of dietary diversity and nutritious dietary choices to increase the impact of CBA in promoting FS outcomes.
2.2 Economic Recovery: Vulnerable households achieve self-reliance through own-food production and/or income generation. To facilitate the transition of displacement affected populations from humanitarian assistance to self-reliance, NRC will support economic recovery of households so they can restore their ability to produce food, while for non-agricultural population NRC will use market-based approaches to facilitate access to demand driven income generation activities. Local Food Production: targeted grants (cash or in-kind) to support local production of food by conflict affected population. Grants will be accompanied by training in food production techniques. Income Generation Activities (IGA): NRC will facilitate access to IGA through: (1) Cash for Work activities (2) Grants (cash/ in-kind) to support income generation and (self) employment, and (3) Basic business and soft skills training.
2.3 Inclusive Markets and employment Households achieve resilience through sustainable livelihoods and inclusive markets. To promote the development and access to inclusive markets that enable affected populations fully restore their sustainable livelihoods, NRC will offer: Market Based Support; Development of livelihoods via provision of targeted grants, supported with training, mentoring, and market access. Market and labour market access support: Employment Facilitation, Entrepreneurship and Local Capacity Strengthening. NRC will maximize opportunities for impact by linking its inclusive market and economic recovery/employment interventions.
ERL objective 3: NRC will strengthen social cohesion by empowering communities to lead early recovery processes together will be supported by engaging in pilot participatory context analysis and capacity building workshops for community representatives to advocate for early recovery needs and to identify solutions to their needs.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, 14.2 million people need humanitarian assistance, with 12.4 million people are considered food insecure, of which 1.3 million are severely food insecure and 1.8 million at risk of food insecurity. Over ten years of continued conflict causing protracted displacement and loss/lack of livelihoods opportunities and wide infrastructure destruction or deterioration, contribute to high socio-economic vulnerability levels, hampering people's access to food. Covid-19, depreciation of local currency, inflation, rising fuel costs and electricity shortages and economic crises have worsened the situation during 2021, further exacerbating food insecurity. Around, 6.7 million people are displaced within Syria, with 4.8 million people relying on monthly food assistance. Access to food, livelihoods and employment opportunities are the top-three priority needs identified by displaced population, regardless of length of their displacement. Over 1.7 million people currently living in last resort camps are among the population groups most affected by the deteriorating food insecurity situation with no access to livelihoods and total reliance on humanitarian assistance. Accordingly, approximately 50 per cent of Syrians spend more than 75 per cent of their expenditure on food.

NRC's project will contribute to Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector objectives 1, 2 and 3 via several interventions addressing the key priorities of displaced population, as follows: 1. Emergency Response: NRC will provide unrestricted Cash Based Assistance (CBA) as a primary response modality to empower households to define and attain their own specific needs, whenever appropriate; 2. Economic Recovery responses (targeted grants (cash or in-kind) to support local production of food by conflict affected population). 3. Income Generation: NRC will facilitate access to income-generation activities through Cash for Work activities, Cash and/or in-kind grants to support income generation and (self) employment, Delivery of basic business skills training. 3.Inclusive Markets (sectoral Market Based Support interventions); 4. Market Access Support (employment Facilitation, Entrepreneurship, Local Capacity Strengthening 5. Infrastructure Rehabilitation and maintenance and access to energy. 6. Participatory context analysis and capacity buildingThe two-year project cycle is more conductive to having a more transformative impact in the target areas, reducing dependency on life saving assistance and strengthening inclusive markets, employments and income generating opportunities and social cohesion. NRC will build on evidence, learning and experiences of the first year to improve and expand its range of ERL activities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (1): Corruption at the level of beneficiary selection, including exclusion of specific people or communities. Mitigation Measures: 1) Complaints/Feedback mechanisms in place and accessible to beneficiaries. 2) Various outreach modalities utilised and own selection criteria is used.3) Post-distribution monitoring includes questions about exchange of aid for services or money. 4) NRC compliance standards and procedures. 5) staff awareness on anti-corruption policies. Protection Risk (2): Requirement of beneficiary vetting by some donors pose a number of protection risks for beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: 1) Institutional policies in place to guarantee confidentiality of beneficiary data. No beneficiary vetting accepted during implementation. 2) Programmatic tools and processes designed in a manner which guarantees impartial selection of beneficiaries. Protection Risk (3): Centralised, large-scale distributions might create security risks for beneficiaries and staff/partners. Also, they may not be physically accessible for people affected by impaired mobility or those unable to leave the house. Mitigation Measures: 1) Prioritisation of household-level distributions where feasible and appropriate. 2) Where centralised distribution sites are required, selection will include risk assessment and alternative distribution methods. Protection Risk (4): Diversion of aid/assets. Mitigation Measures: 1) Activities are predominantly implemented directly.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Potential SEA risks are mainly identified during the beneficiary selection process, the distribution of assistance and while accessing services. NRC communicates clearly with communities on the expected assistance modalities as well as confidential complain and feedback channels available to them and makes sure gender balanced teams register and deliver assistance to communities whenever possible. During post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs), NRC inquires if people were asked to exchange anything for aid or service. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks in PDMs who, according to the recipients of aid, provided the assistance they received, enabling identification of possible areas of concern. All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC's Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and child safeguarding (CSG) policies. Several sessions take place with staff to raise awareness on PSEA and CSG, in addition to sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete mandatory online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and CSG reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams who regularly updates guidance to emerging needs and trends to tailor management and support to cases. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM started and are expected to conclude in 2022.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. NRC staff are injured, killed, or suffer psychological trauma due conflict: Mitigation: Security briefings, HEAT and HEIST trainings, briefings, more support from UNMAS.2. Fraud/corruption/bribery. Mitigation: enforcing NRC's policy of zero tolerance through strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the "whistle-blowing" policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures. 3. NRC is unable to timely operate due to the impact of internationals sanctions. Mitigation: request for derogation, looking for different banking channels, advocacy.4. NRC is unable to timely implement due to delays in approvals from Government/local authorities. Mitigation: Advocacy plan in place, set the lead time for approval submission to be 2 weeks with a close follow.5. NRC is unable to timely deliver the planned activities due to rapid inflation and change of prices. Mitigation: review budget and targets on a quarterly basis, communication with the donors, find more convenient payment terms to suppliers.6. Donor policies, including overly restrictive redlines and perceptions, affect the ability to implement and scale-up programs. Mitigation: Engaging with donor, Joint advocacy efforts with DINGOs, Intensify the negotiations with local partner and MOUs, Long terms programs and reconstructions, media coverage in private and public advocacy.C-19 Restrictions: SOPs for safe implementation during C-19; adapted implementation modalities.
