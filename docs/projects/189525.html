Project Summary

The Humanitarian intervention towards a free and safe environment from land-mines and explosive remnants of war is well positioned in strengthening the community resilience through opportunity of exercising their livelihood in a sustainable manner, the eruptions of December 2013 and 2016 conflict that threaten to thwart all the invested resources in cleaning the socioeconomic sites for enabling the masses to rebuild their livelihoods. The lands are so contaminated such that previously cleared areas are not spared of new explosive ordinance including the globally banned cluster booms reported in Central Equatoria state of which the 2022 report indicate YEI County as the most contaminated given the limited or inadequate intervention in the previous year’s till 2022.
It’s this brief that OSIL aimed at strengthening community resilience for development sustainability (SCORDS) through responsive approach to explosive ordinances in Yei County. The 2-EOD and 2-EORE team will be task by UNMAS and NMAA to survey/cleared and provide awareness raising to 44,435 people, most of whom are children (boys and girls) men, women of the host communities, returnees, Internal Displaced Person (IDPs); - all in variant categories not limited to people with disabilities, old age, children, women & men respectively).
Besides, responsive intervention gearing onto ope-operation on EORE and EOD; - with activities throughout the project life as stakeholders consultation/ Survey (Context), Initial training for the EOD/ CL Teams (02), Deployment and direct EORE and EOD engagements, reporting and documentation and continues review and streamlining the project implementation onto the ultimate goal of free/ reduced existence of mine field for social-economic activities; enhancing better food security for the population in Yei County – in two ways dimensions; domestic and commercial purpose. As such development is improved and geared onto sustainability.

1 - Needs

Right from Six (6) years and yet continuing 2013 and 2016 protracted wars amongst SPLA factions, or and with the over whelming existence or / some new rebel factions; 1 in 3 people in South Sudan have been displaced. More than 4 million citizens have been forced to flee their homes. Around 2.3 million people have escaped to neighboring countries in search of safety, and 1.8 million are trapped inside the warring nation; - generally, leaving the economy in shambles livelihoods severely disrupted, and estimated six (6) million people living in landmines and explosive remnants of war contaminated areas. The World Bank estimates that South Sudanese poverty is rated at about 82% of the total population across the Country; -according to the most recent estimates, based on the $1.90 2011 purchasing power parity poverty line yet with limited access to peace, and Justice compounded by lack of strong institution by 2017, and generally socioeconomic and climatic factors; - substantially, behind the UN’s 2030 target of 45 percent. Arguably, the suggestion of over 82% of the population being below the poverty line, unlikely position South Sudan onto gaining a more favorable position of 187, of the 187 world countries by 2022 rating. Landmine & ERW contamination presents significant risks to human security and exacerbates poverty by creating crippling socioeconomic blockages preventing the productive use of sizeable tracts of land, impacting not only on the provision of mainstream services including health and education but also longer-term rural and economic development. (More than 404 suspected and confirmed minefields remain across South Sudan, including older minefields predominately in Greater Equatoria and Jonglei and more recent Hazardous Areas (HAs) located along the border region of which YEI is inclusive yet over the years there has been no Intervention towards the explosive ordinance of such area (YEI). As a result, this affects much of the population and poses an immediate risk of death and injury to local communities, refugees, IDPs and returnees alike. The UNMASS and NMAA let efforts to rid the country out of land mines and explosive remnants of war were headed for success before the eruptions of December 2013 & 2016 conflict that threaten to thwart all in invests resources in cleaning the socioeconomic sites for enabling the masses to rebuild their livelihoods. The lands are so contaminated so that previously cleared areas are not spared of new explosive ordinance including the globally banned cluster booms reported in Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Central Equatoria, and Eastern Equatoria endure the brute of explosive ordinance more than the rest of the parts in the whole country. This is because of the previous war that ushered independence from Sudan and the ongoing conflict majorly concentrated in these areas. In 2019, OSIL two clearance team unearth several landmines fields, four stockpiles for munitions along Juba - Nimule Road. The finding not surprising in that, the team composition and local communities easily opened-up to OSIL, and institution they call their own. The result was so overwhelming beyond our mandate but a right pointer in the direction of inculcating the key pillars of our mandate that is Ownership, Capacity, and Sustainability. In line with our philosophy that, any project to register significant results, it must interleave purposeful efforts for collective responsibility across stakeholders, processing of enhancing their ability to functions with precision and quality as a guaranteed measure for the lasting impact that is sustainability in line with UNMAS and NMAA strategy.

2 - Project activities

As defined in Mine Action Sub Cluster strategic objectives, OSIL management will deploy 2-EOD teams and 2-EORE teams to carry out the project activities Yei County: - - Mobilization & Training of EOD and EORE Teams. – Teams Accreditation conducted. - Deployment under UNMAS & NMAA supervision.-Conduct NTS and marking of the hazardous areas. - Selection and training of 360 Community Focal Persons (CFPs)- Approximately 44,435 targeted beneficiaries will received EORE with additional 15% will benefit indirectly from the training.- Over 38 Hazard Areas will be cleared and Approximately 320,000 Sqm of land released to the community for use. - Up to maximum of 75% HAs recorded by OSIL CL team and reported to UNMAS are responded to by UNMAS/ NMAA or OSIL Risk Management team(RMT1&2). At least 65% of the HAs areas is released for safe access/development/use. – Maximum of 95% Square meters of contaminated land cordoned and cleared of explosive debris of war. - Post Project Knowledge attitude and practice (KAP) survey conducted. - Monthly, quarterly internal, and external Quality Assurance conducted/ reported.

3 - Project indicators

A total of 44,435 people will benefit from Risk Education activities implemented by OSIL’s 2 operational teams in Yei, Central Equotaria State by the end of the project period. It is anticipated that by the end of the project, a maximum of 65% of direct beneficiaries (approximately 28,883 beneficiaries) will be children; whereas 95% hazard areas will have been marked, cordon or cleared. - OSIL anticipates reaching at least 6,665 individuals indirectly through EORE, peer to peer, social media, and Radio Talk-show.Approximately 95% of land cleared from ERW is released to the community for public use and both the Host community, IDPs and returnees exercise their livelihood in a sustainable manner with significant improvement In the House Hold (HH) economic status.
