Project Summary

This project aims to foster an urgent first emergency and life-saving response, to provide timely multi-cluster life-saving assistance to crisis-affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity and mitigate protection risks and respond to the protection needs through humanitarian action.

1 - Needs

The conflict escalated on April 15th and has been exasperating the already difficult situation of Sudan, causing a large-scale displacement, crumbling of the local market, and shattered access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care, and other basic services for millions of people. In addition, the local population is facing high insecurity levels, shortage of water, disease outbreaks, and increased food insecurity and malnutrition. Over 1 million people have been displaced since the conflict started. As of 18 May 2023, UNOCHA, IOM, and UNHCR have recorded a total of 843, 130 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and 253,591 displaced foreign nationals. The highest numbers of internally displaced people are in West Darfur, White Nile, and Northern states. These new displacements are in addition to the almost 3.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), primarily concentrated in Darfur, prior to the new fighting. Therefore, in 2023, COOPI will continue to address the WASH, ES/NFI, and FSL, protection needs in the most affected localities as follows: WASHThe conflicts between SAF and RSF, caused, among others, severe water shortages caused by blackouts, fuel scarcity, and lack of supplies. Access to clean water remains low, specifically as a result of further exacerbating desertification and deteriorating climate conditions. Among others conflict over resources (including water resources), combined with a tense political transition, continues to lead to localized conflict outbreaks and displacement. Sanitation is not prioritized, and high numbers of PIN continue to practice Open Defecation, which together with the lack of water, exacerbates the outbreak of WASH-related diseases. In Khartoum, Port Sudan, White Nile, North Darfur, and Gedaref, IDPs experience high levels of WASH insecurity across the sub-sectors including latrine inadequacy, poor access to clean water and handwashing facilities and poor access to waste disposal. The continued influx of IDP in Port Sudan has overstretched the limited available functional WASH infrastructure such as boreholes, hand pumps, and latrines posing a high-risk brake down of the infrastructures due to continuous usage with low maintenance. FSLAs of May 2023, the FLS PIN is 9.1, due to the current SAF-RSF conflict resulting in additional displacement, coupled with exiting loss of livelihoods, diminished production capacity (low harvests), and a lack of livelihoods. The already declining purchasing power of the most vulnerable households in Sudan is being exacerbated by the current conflict. People, including existing IDPs, and residents, were forced to migrate from their current domiciles to other locations with virtually little or no resources taken with them. Both IDPs and some residents are unable to engage in productive activities either because they migrated with no productive assets, or they are depleted. Female-headed households are particularly affected and life-saving intervention, (primarily food assistance, NFIs, emergency WASH, and Shelter) is paramount in the immediate term. As the crisis subsides this will be followed by investment in production capacity to ensure better food security, livelihoods, and resilience building for residents and returnee households. SHELTERThe shelter continues to represent a major concern in the affected communities of Sudan. Other risks such as torrential rains and floods are likely to affect communities in target States and as well increase the number of households in need of shelter and assistance, particularly in urban settings (such as in Jabal Awlia, Khartoum, other locations in Red Sea, Kassala, etc) where IDPs and vulnerable communities live in a make-shift shelter, posing high public health and GBV risks.

2 - Activities/Outputs

ES/NFI: Output 1.1: Shelter support is provided to the most vulnerable households living in emergency shelters in the Open Areas, through: - Provision of shelter kits and construction materials for improved emergency shelter in the old and new settlements.- Training of households on shelter rehabilitation and construction.- General site maintenance, including the establishment of safe spaces, embankments, and site rehabilitation to protect communities against the effects of flooding. WASHOutput 2.1: To provide access to a safe water supply which will be achieved through:– Setting up emergency water supply systems and trucking of water to high water demand areas.- rehabilitation of water sources and water points- Operation and maintenance of water sources, particularly water schemes inside IDP camps, including capacity building for WASH committees on chlorination and technical maintenance. Output 2.2: to provide access to adequate, protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services complemented with hygiene promotion, through: - Construction of communal latrines inside institutional facilities, such as Health Centers and schools. - Provision of PHKs and NFIs including, water treatment tables and jerry cans.- Hygiene promotion, including the training of local hygiene promoters and the organization of community-based hygiene campaigns (IEC distribution).- Training of households on water quality, and provision of Point of Use Water Treatment kits for safe use of water. Output 2.3: To promote healthy living conditions by targeting their close environment through disposing of solid waste and supporting its management, intervening to drain stagnant water, and establishing vector control systems, through:- Dispose of Solid waste and support its management.- Do vector control (spraying, draining standing water). FSL: Output 3.1: to provide timely lifesaving food assistance and emergency agricultural assistance for IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable resident (non-displaced) populations affected by the SAF-RSF conflict and to improve their access to basic needs while maintaining pre-SAF-RSF conflict regular life-sustaining activities:-vocational training to vulnerable people and Income generating activities (IGA); - vocational training and life skills training (training of livestock and agricultural packages); - improved seeds distribution and agricultural tools distribution targeting vulnerable farmers;-enhancing and monitoring financial management capabilities through conditional CVA based on development planning, support of Village Saving and Loan Associations; and Cash for work, specifically related to DRR, preparedness, and response;

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

COOPI promotes environmentally friendly and risk avert mechanisms in its WASH and shelter/NFIs response projects and livelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the design phase, implementation and monitoring of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreating, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. The Water User Committees shall be trained on water conservation to promote prudent use of water resources. Additionally, COOPI staff will work closely with the community to ensure that local materials used in construction are sources sustainable and where possible use of by-products of agriculture such as harvested crop stocks and other by products are reused in construction of latrine superstructures. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water users’ committees, hygiene promoters, natural resource management groups, shelter committees where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; COOPI will focus on ﬁnding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, able to scaled, environmentally sustainable and climate smart.The project activities will take into account impact on environment and local resources by attempting to monitor the over-use of material (e.g., construction material, water, etc.) as well as the waste production after kits' distribution and other activities Furthermore, COOPI will continue monitoring markets and opt for purchase of locally available material wherever is possible.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

All described activities will be conducted in accordance with the principles of COOPI Protection Policies through an approach that is Survivor-Centred Approach for Promoting Ethical and Safety Standards, rights-based, community-based, and age-gender-diversity sensitive. Through a protection-informed approach, COOPI will ensure full respect of the rights of all persons in conditions of equality, regardless of their age, gender, religion, ethnic or social origin, or political orientation. Protection will be mainstreamed at every stage of the project cycle ensuring impartial and equal access to the project benefits and services. To this end, COOPI will: 1. Involve women and other at-risk groups as staff and leaders in the siting, design, construction, and maintenance of water infrastructures: 1.1: COOPI will ensure 50% representation of females within the COOPI team and provide women with formal and on-the-job training.1.2: COOPI team will ensure women (and where appropriate, adolescent girls) are actively involved in community-based committees and management groups. Paying attention to potential tensions that may be caused by attempting to change the role of women and girls in communities and, as necessary, engage in dialogue with males to ensure their support (Do No Harm). COOPI will ensure feedback mechanisms are accessible and anonymous for women and girls, and respond timely to any GBV concerns.2: Implement strategies that increase the availability and accessibility of services and support for women, girls and other at-risk groups.2.1: COOPI will endeavor to place constructed water points no more than 500 meters from households, in accordance with Sphere standards. COOPI will also ensure that livelihood activities are adopted to serve the needs of women. Times should be set that are convenient and safe for women, girls and other at-risk groups, and users should be fully informed of when and where water is available.2.2: COOPI will ensure construction designs (WASH, Shelter) are women- and girl-friendly3. Incorporate GBV messages into awareness campaigns and other water-related community outreach activities.3.1: COOPI will operate in accordance with SHPERE and IASC GBV global guidance and will set up GBV risk mitigation measures across sectors of interventions. Though limited Protection / GBV actors are operational in several localities where the project is implemented, COOPI will continue to engage with Protection actors to strengthen the referral system for specialize protection services. COOPI will ensure beneficiaries receive the appropriate information for timely referral through COOPI’s feedback mechanisms which guarantees the privacy of beneficiaries using confidential toll-free lines and complaint boxes accessible only by authorized COOPI personnel. In addition, COOPI will train all staff, contractors and suppliers that are in direct contact with the beneficiaries on PSEA to mitigate risks of harassment and abuse.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Community Participation: The project adapts a community-based approach where communities are involved in the design and implementation of activities. The project will be designed in a manner that humanitarian assistance will be provided in line and in proportion to the needs identified and documented in targeted areas. COOPI will involve the beneficiaries and respond to their feedback when planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating its program. COOPI’ accountability includes four pillars i.e. risk management, capacity assessment and monitoring the performance of implementing partners, planning, monitoring and reporting, and project and partner auditing. COOPI will provide space for communities to shape their own recovery and for COOPI and partners to better deliver on commitments to stakeholders, including the people COOPI serves and the resource partners who make assistance possible. COOPI will ensure timely information sharing among stakeholders regarding targeted communities. COOPI will focus on various approaches to collect information for community-based indicators. Feedback and Complaint Mechanisms: COOPI will establish effective feedback mechanisms and a Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) so that COOPI and partners can incorporate feedback and address problems rapidly, including for sensitive issues like protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian or development workers. COOPI and partner staff will be trained and oriented on SEA. Two community accountability focal persons will be selected from each target community (a woman and man) and trained to facilitate the process. In line with COOPI’ Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policies and procedures, COOPI will consult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give each community the opportunity to identify and report issues on program quality and protection concerns. The system will address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding confidentially and non-retaliation principles. Specifically, COOPI will link project beneficiaries with existing feedback mechanisms in the area for formats like: (1) an existing Hotline establish by COOPI Sudan for all projects, where beneficiaries can make free calls to interact with MEAL Coordinator for any feedback/complaint and receive a response in immediate timeframe; and (2) Active outreach to project beneficiaries by the MEAL Officer, who will take findings from random beneficiary satisfaction visits to regular program management meetings, or (3) suggestion boxes to offer confidential feedback as appropriate. The suggestions boxes are opened on a monthly basis and a database and response mechanism in place according to COOPI FCM SOPs. Remote Project Monitoring System: COOPI will implement its standard Remote Project Monitoring System as per COOPI MEAL Procedures.
