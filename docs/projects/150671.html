Project Summary

Mercy Corps intends to construct a solar energy power source for Al Bakara wastewater pumping station in Gaza City. As the energy crisis in Gaza continues to worsen, and emergency fuel isn’t always guaranteed to be available, all of Gaza’s infrastructure is at risk of failure. As it stands, the current operation scheme for all water and wastewater infrastructure is at a critical condition causing massive shortage in service and severely damaging the environment. Al Bakara WW PS (No. 5) is no exception. Serving over 100,000 people in Gaza City specifically Al Shiekh Radwan, Al Rimal Al Shamali, Al Daraj, and Al Mashahra neighborhoods. This pumping station is a critical utility and is a very high priority location for the Municipality of Gaza due to the fear that its stoppage will cause a flood that will affect public health and the environment. We at Mercy Corps want to ensure that this vital utility keeps running during day peak hour times to mitigate environmental impacts, but more importantly to protect lives and the public health. The proposed project’s Photovoltaic panels will be installed on a land dedicated by the municipality near the pumping station.
This emergency intervention will be conducted in coordination with the WASH Cluster, Municipality of Gaza, and CMWU with the goal of meeting the needs of vulnerable households and sustainably building the capacities of local utility providers. All Project proposed activities, designs, and budget estimations were closely coordinated with CMWU.

Needs

Nearly two million Gazans are facing worsening public health hazards due to a lack of capacity by local water and wastewater utility providers. This lack of capacity is primarily caused by the prolonged, daily electricity blackouts and fuel shortages for local electricity production. In addition to the major challenges faced by the utility companies and local municipalities to complete all required basic equipment to treat available water and handle wastewater, the equipment is electricity dependent and is facing a major reduction in energy and fuel supplies. This combined environmental degradation, lack of wastewater treatment and insufficient electricity production/availability highlights the need for localized alternative energy solutions, like solar power. The electricity shortage in the Gaza Strip reached 80% in July of this year (UNOCHA). Currently, untreated sewage flows into Gaza’s coastal waters (UNOCHA). With demand for electricity increasing and supply worsening due to fuel shortages, the basic WASH needs of Gaza’s residence are not being met, creating an extreme public health hazard. Gaza’s water and sanitation infrastructure is on the brink of total failure which has the potential to result in catastrophic social and environmental impacts that may include public health disasters, irreversible environmental damage, property damage, and even the loss of lives. According to CMWU, the public WASH facilities need approximately 36 MW, and it’s expected to reach 92 MW by year 2020. In September 2018, Mercy Corps conducted a needs assessment in consultation with relevant local WASH experts and other key stakeholders, like the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), to assess the capacity of water delivery, water treatment, and wastewater handling infrastructure throughout the Gaza Strip. Using the WASH Cluster’s priorities as a starting point, Mercy Corps looked for critical gaps in essential WASH service delivery in order to identify interventions to address the basic needs of Gazans and respond to the increasing prevalence of household vulnerability. Through this process, Mercy Corps identified a critical WASH infrastructure that needs urgent alternative sources of energy, including wastewater pumping stations, water booster stations, desalination plants, and access to and delivery of water from municipal water wells. Mercy Corps found that all water and sanitation facilities in the Gaza Strip are at a critical point of failure, threatening to deepen the current humanitarian crisis. Compounding the WASH crisis, the energy crisis and daily electrical blackouts have severely limited the capacities of electricity-dependent infrastructure from meeting the basic water and sanitation needs of Gaza’s residence. With UN funding for emergency fuel being cut and/or eliminated, alternative energy sources are highly needed as the supply of electricity from sources both external and internal to Gaza continues to be highly constrained. However, the issue of lack of electricity means that all water and sanitation facilities do not currently have the capacity to meet the WASH needs of all Gazans, and therefore, an immediate intervention is required to address the electricity supply issue and increase the capacity of this critical WASH infrastructure. Al Bakara area in Gaza City has a large wastewater pumping station that serves well over 100,000 Gaza City residents according to the Municipality of Gaza. It has previously been damaged by the Israeli forces and repaired and is also a highly prone area for flooding. Hence, due to its strategic importance for the utility provider, this pumping station has been listed as a high priority to protect lives and property. Activities

The project activities Mercy Corps intends to carry out are straight forward and to the point. Mercy Corps intends to first consult with the facilities operator (Municipality of Gaza) to define the scope of work to be done and identify the best way to supply the target location with a solar energy solution. Defining the scope of work will entail defining the target number of working hours, the type of solar power technology required to meet minimum standards for electricity output needed, and requirement for installation. Mercy Corps will also conduct a public consultation meeting with the residents of all the target groups to ensure that any missed concern they may have is taken into consideration. Then Mercy Corps will issue a call for bids for local engineering firms to conduct required engineering design work including the production of tendered technical drawings, documents, and bills of quantity (BoQs). Mercy Corps’ procurement team will issue a call for tenders where different local consulting firms will bid to design the required solar systems in detail. The selected engineering firm will also be responsible for the construction supervision and quality assurance of the installation. The designs and decisions will also always involve the operator (MoG) and will also be done in coordination with the WASH cluster, CMWU, and partners to ensure maximum technical benefit by sharing lessons learnt and getting feedback for improvements. Once the designs are complete and reviewed by Mercy Corps technical team, the tender packages will be issued for contractors to submit bids to supply and install all required equipment for the energy solution at the target facility. Mercy Corps, along with our hired technical consultant, will continuously supervise the construction of the solar energy solution at the target location. We will ensure that the contractor adheres to the specifications and we will conduct quality checks throughout the installation process. It is estimated that 1,900 panels of 325 W capacity will be installed to produce a total power of more than 600 kW, which meet the required power according to the Gaza Municipality. Once the contractor has finished their required works, and all items have been installed correctly and thoroughly tested, Mercy Corps will hand over the project to the operator. Mercy Corps’ Monitoring Evaluation and Learning (MEL) unit will conduct a number of quality checks to ensure that works are being completed up to standard. Moreover, daily records, photos, and weekly meetings minutes will be kept on record as part of the project’s indicators mean of verification.
