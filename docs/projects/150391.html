Project Summary

Overall Objective: To Promote the access of women and girls victims and survivors of Violence in Gaza for holistic protection services based on human rights standards, First year expected results:-Enhanced the knowledge of vulnerable groups of women, girls and men such as (IDPs, divorced women, widows, heads of households, (WWD), girls and women in conflict with law, women, men in MR and WVV on women's legal rights at family law.-Improved the awareness of G & WWD and adult men on GBV, Family law and PWD in local and international laws.-Increased access of women and girls especially those victims of GBV (Through legal representation) to their legal rights at family law -Increased the Visibility of Hayat center as multipurpose shelter for women and girls Victims of Violence and at Risk in The Gaza Strip as a result of community outreach workshops.-Improved the awareness of girls, women and men as a result of community outreach workshops on the sexual abuse.-Enhanced the capacity of Hayat center staff on the best practices on case management interventions according to Hayat’s SOPs. -Decreased GBV and psychosocial problems among targeted girls, women and their children.-Improved quality of life, psychological wellbeing, stability and established good relationships among themselves, their families and community. -Positive change in the ability of targeted girls and women in decision-making and problem solving, ability to respond, adapt to and withstand in crisis. -Improved family relationships and reduced tension and problems by creating a space for conversation and overcoming daily problems.Second year expected results:-Economic empowerment of girls and women victims of violence and at risk in The Gaza Strip as a result of interventions conducted by Hayat’s economic empowerment unit.-Girls and women victims of violence and at risk in The Gaza Strip are empowered to use available, accessible and quality essential services and recover from violence.

1 - Needs

HNO (2019) estimated 52,098 persons including 26,049 children<18 years with MHPSS problems associated with GMR demonstrations, exacerbates already acutely vulnerable of 210,000 adults and 295,913 children<18 years at end of 2017. Women>18-59 and >59 years and girls<18 years from families affected by casualties and injuries due to GMR were in high risk of GBV. Mostly affected women were mothers of injured children; widows; women whose husbands have been killed/ injured; girls who lost a father or have a parent with disability. The demand for GBV services has doubled due to electricity crisis, salary cuts, GRM and overcrowdings in service provider facilities. Estimated 148,478 women/ girls need GBV services which place a high pressure on GBV service providers who have inadequate professional human capacity. UNFPA (2018) presented that 40% of GBV survivors were exposed to physiological abuse, 25% to physical abuse and 18% to resource denial. Around 40% of these cases identified husband as alleged perpetrator and 31% other family members. Around 15% of ever-married women have been exposed to sexual violence. Highlights from UNWOMEN (2018) included: one-third of married women>18 years who experienced physical violence were exposed to severe acts like attempt to strangle (3.6%), attacking with a knife (2.3%), and beating resulting in a coma (2.3%); household poverty raises women’s vulnerability to repeated physical violence and loss of a close family member does correlate with women’s greater vulnerability to physical violence. Factors that make women less vulnerable to GBV are higher educational achievement, stopping early marriage and women’s employment. NGO GBV services were used by married women (63.3%), divorced (16.5%) and single (9.5%). (32.3%) of these GBV services were used by women aged 20- 29 years. Since the project funded by OCHA launching on May 2019, till the end of Aug 2019. CWLRCP received a new 200 cases 18-70 provided psychosocial-legal support , and another 70 cases received a legal aid, and 13 women received sheltering services short,medium and long ,800 psycho-legal consultation. CWLRCP conducted 5 FGDs with beneficiaries in julay 2019 under OCHA-funded project and concluded needs to be addressed for women/ men 18-59 and elderly>59 years and girls/ boys<18 years as they have differentiated needs: group and individual psychosocial support, recreational days, mental health treatment, case management, psychotherapy for couples, legal counselling and representation, emergency hotline for legal and psychosocial support, emergency health services, diagnostic and treatment services for women>18 years survivors of cancer, dignity kits, business management training and grants to women entrepreneurs, awareness raising of women, girls, men and boys in risk of GBV,. Findings included: women>>18-59 and >59 years suffer from physical, sexual and economic violence and denied inheritance; psychological trauma; women don’t resort to protection mechanisms; women need MHPSS,sheltering services, legal aid, health care and economic enablement. CWLRCP main serious GBV concern that will be addressed in this intervention is “limited access of marginalized women 18-59 and >59 years and girls<18 years to well-designed multi-sectoral and gender responsive services, protection and economic support”. Other priority concerns are gender-based vulnerabilities; higher incidence of intimate partner violence; sexual abuse and forced marriage; WWDs are victims of two-fold discrimination and violence; elderly women>59 years are more vulnerable to poverty, disease and have limited access to services; adolescents’ sexual and reproductive health; unsafe motherhood health care; lack of diagnostic and treatment services for women survivors of cancer; social norms that hinder psychosocial and mental health service uptake; women’s poverty and lack of livelihoods; lack of access to legal aid; and lack of women’s knowledge of GBV services offered .

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Providing multi sectorial services ( psychosocial support, legal assistance) target 300 women including 160 middle age,125 youth, 15 elderly age and 200 men CWLRCP will provide them case management support after conducting the rapid case assessment to assess their needs, the protection services including structured group, individual psychosocial support, family psychotherapy,group psychological support(entertainment open days, psychodrama support ,legal counselling and representation, and referral to health and other service. Of them 150 women will be provided psycho social support and 150 women will be provided legal representation before the courts, - each women need from 7-12 individual sessions depends on the case. Progress of each case will be evaluated by virtue of a pre and post questionnaire of each case .Activity 1.2. CWLRCP will refer cases at least 70 in need of professional multipurpose protection services as a result of case intervention plans to other service providers such as health, mental health,and child protection actors,etc) identified from among the community outreach target groups or who access at Hayat and CWLRCP headquarters Since CWLRCP has itSOPs and referral system and maintains 25 referral agreements with 25 CSOs,Activity 1.3: Provide sheltering services (long- medium- short term) to hosted 12 women whether who access to Hayat project or referral from other service providers (police, attorney office and GBV service providers) including provision specialize services (food, clothes and health care) in addition to case management support for each case (rehabilitation intervention plan) to integrate them into community. Activity 1.4: Provide training and economic support to 20 women from vulnerable backgrounds to empower them economically (remedial) as following:-Conduct 50 training hours for 20 women of successful cases from vulnerable backgrounds on the handicrafts training and how toStart Your Small Business, Marketing and its Importance / Marketing Management, Pricing And pricing, financial management/ market study, feasibility study, Activity 1.5: Provision of income generating opportunities , including establishing and/or supporting existing small businesses for 8 WSV with severe poverty ,social marginalization and ignorance have leadership skills, self confidence and ability overcome the Challenges they may face. Having feasible proposed businesses or having innovative Business in harmony with market needs in marginalized areas.Motivated women with good .Activity 1.6: provide dignity kit for project's beneficiaries who benefited from multi sectorial services totally 100 women (from vulnerable background with severe poverty and social marginalization and Ignorance.Activity 1.7: The Hayat center will provide child visitation services for at least 20 families (divorced couples) and their children which entails biweekly visits to the center to enable the parent who does not have custody over the children to see them in a supportive child-friendly environment. The visitation services are coupled with counseling for parents on how to deal with the children to minimize the impact of divorce on the wellbeing and psychosocial status of the children.Activity 1.7 Carry out 50 awareness-raising workshops for 1000 RWG, women impacted GMR and WGD. and 250 men. 1.8 Conduct 40- hour training course for hayat team and that may include, but not limited to, safety and security of beneficiaries and Shelters dealing with an emergency situation

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming and GBV are key drivers to maximize positive impact. The proposed project mainstreams protection and GBV including:- CWLRCP follow Code of Ethics and Conduct ensuring that affected women and girls are treated with respect and dignity, have their welfare promoted and receive support in an environment in which every effort is made to promote welfare and to prevent abuse.- CWLRCP follow Rights Based Approach Manual as it deals with beneficiaries as rights holders rather than service recipients and empowering them to protect and claim their rights.- CWLRCP follow also shifting approach via shifting power within, power with and power to vulnerable women and girls’ survivors of GBV.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will be adequately announced in the targeted communities using various outreach tools to give equal opportunities for women, girls, men and boys to benefit from the project.- The targeted groups will be selected based on transparent selection criteria that ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered without bias, and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and vulnerabilities.- The selection of beneficiaries should fulfill the global prioritization criteria for the project to be lifesaving, reducing suffering, maintaining human dignity, time critical or critically enabling as well as the oPt context-specific criteria, such as focus on communities at imminent risk of displacement, or particularly vulnerable demographic groups.- CWLRCP apply Gender-Based Analysis to this humanitarian project to capture the gender differences including the different situations, priorities and needs of women, men, girls and boys at all stages of the planning and implementation processes.measures to improve access of women with disabilities to the r services; To conduct the sessions in an adapted placesTo have a sign language translator in the sessionsTo have sessions in different geographic areas in Gaza Strip to target the women with disabilities who have a limited access out of their living area.
