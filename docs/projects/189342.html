Project Summary

This project aims to provide sustainable and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene to reduce the suffering of displaced persons and vulnerable host communities in Tonj East, Tonj North, and Tonj South Counties of Warrap State. The focus will mainly be on women and children who constitute the bulk of the displaced persons and at-risk host families. The project will provide lifesaving Wash assistance to households who have children suffering from malnutrition and preventable communicable diseases due to poor access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation facilities. These all-malicious impacts are caused by severe flooding and widespread inter-communal violence in the Warrap State particularly in Tonj East, Tonj North, and Tonj South Counties of Warrap. The project will be implemented in underserved and multi-hazards affected areas of the Greater Tonj region of Warrap state. The affected population includes internally displaced persons and vulnerable host communities including but not limited to poor female-headed households, households with persons living with disability, those with low income, and the most food-insecure households suffering from malnutrition cases, particularly pregnant and lactating mothers and under-five children. Targeting will be based on humanitarian principles where fair and impartial targeting will be carried out to achieve impartiality and will provide lifesaving assistance based on need alone and this will materialize the principle of humanity. CAFOD protection and safeguarding framework are central to our work and the framework guides overall program management and development processes to be safe, meaningful access, dignified and inclusive.
A participatory and consultative approach will be used to agree on targeting criteria ensuring the most vulnerable individuals/households' voices are heard and influence targeting processes. This will be implemented by building on past targeting methodology used in previous CAFOD response and recovery interventions. This project will be built on CAFOD's previous work in Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South supported by SSHF and other funding sources. CAFOD in 2021 received funds from SSHF for FSL and Protection to address needs of the affected population with absence of funding for WASH. This year, CAFOD will implement multisectoral response to address the multiple needs of the target communities holistically address the needs. This project will provide WASH support through repair and rehabilitation of critically broken boreholes, house-to-house hygiene promotion through project volunteers, training of water users committees to aid sustainability of access to safe drinking water, and refresher training for handpump mechanics which will be conducted by CAFOD and its partner staffs. To support girl-child education, the project will provide sanitary kits for adolescent girls in primary school to curb menstrual hygiene-related absenteeism from school and mediocre performance. The project will also train women, girls, and boys on the making of reusable menstrual pads (RUMPS). This training, latter, will also serve as a source of income generation for individuals trained on this skill. Children being social agents for change, the project will establish and train school hygiene clubs to pass on good hygiene practices to their peers in school and eventually to the entire community. The main messages would revolve around personal hygiene, food hygiene, environmental sanitation, safe water chain management, and safe disposal of solid and human waste. Hygiene campaigns in public places such as markets, health facilities, village centers, and schools would also be conducted to enlighten the communities on environmental and public hygiene. COVID19 Infection Prevention and Control messaging will be mainstreamed in every activity throughout the project cycle. This project will be complemented with ongoing projects. Beneficiaries will receive multiple support.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021 (HNO 2022). Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. Continued macroeconomic crisis, the low preparedness capacity of national and state authorities, COVID-19 pandemic and consecutive years of flooding are exacerbating existing vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of people, while pushing more people downward spiral into crisis and reliance on humanitarian assistance. People’s access to safe and improved water and sanitation is low. Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. Additional access issues reported by IDPs are taxes imposed by host communities to use WASH facilities (reported in Maban and Tonj South) according to HNO 2022. Approximately 41 per cent of the total population have access to safe water while 10 per cent have access to improved sanitation. Some 61 per cent of the population practice open defecation (UNICEF, WASH, briefing note, November to December 2021). An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 because of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. The findings from the 2021 FSNMS + analysis indicate that access to WASH facilities by women and girls, boys, persons with disabilities and elderly remains a challenge. According to CAFOD KAP survey conducted in Tonj North and Tonj South in September 2022, 24.2% of the population do not have sufficient water for drinking and other domestic purposes. The same survey indicates that 32.21% of the population wait for about an hour to access water from the water source meanwhile, 27.85% walk for an hour or more to reach the nearest safe water source. The long walking distance to the water source reported increased exposure and vulnerabilities of women and girls to protection related risks. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition securities across South Sudan and this contributed to the malicious impacts on children growth and developments. Communities in greater Tonj cited lack of access to water contributes to tension and conflict in the population. Access to sanitation is very low in greater Tonj with only 51% having access to functioning latrine. Community hygiene awareness and practices require continuous improvement to help in gradual behavior change on hygiene practices. Reach assessment conducted between January to March 2022, indicated similar findings. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs which include greater Tonj areas. This project will address the identified WASH needs in close coordination and collaborations with key humanitarian and development actors.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CAFOD carried out a successful voucher assistance program in Tonj North and Tonj South for sorghum, cowpeas, and groundnuts. The modality used is seed fair. The venture into CVA was after comprehensive market assessment and feasibility analysis which informed the decision to undertake CVA in Tonj North and Tonj South Counties. CAFOD has deployed its cash advisor to the area and found that the voucher-based assistance modality is feasible and relevant to the greater Tonj context. The market assessment was to ascertain the market potential to support the approach. Areas considered in the assessment included functionality and access to the market by the affected population, availability, and source of goods and services, and traders’ potential to supply the market. Cash transfer programming (CTP) refers to all programs where cash (or vouchers for goods or services) is directly provided to beneficiaries. It is an approach that builds upon linkages, capacities, incentives, and relationships to encourage effective market recovery. The use of vouchers or cash reduces the overall administrative and management costs of the assistance and thus helps effective and efficient use of limited resources with a riddle effect to the wider market system. The modality also helps to reach more people with limited resources. The engagement of local vendors in supplying items to the beneficiaries is intended to support the local market dynamics. Through this approach, the cash or vouchers given to beneficiaries and the voucher will be exchanged with locally selected vendors for supplies and this would advance financial linkages in the local markets as well as boost the purchasing power and local economy. Cash and vouchers assistance (CVA) is globally felt to be a more dignified form of assistance for humanitarian aid recipients because it promotes the dignity of the participants. Individuals accustomed to supporting themselves and their families suddenly become dependent on aid and charity. While their sense of dignity cannot be easily and fully restored, cash and voucher transfers offer some choice and a small degree of control in a situation where many feel they have none. The affected populations preferred the flexibility that cash offered them and the choice it offered them to prioritize their own needs. We have realized that the use of cash and vouchers empowers participants through ownership of their needs and the opportunity to purchase locally preferred supplies. Since CAFOD intends to provide WASH NFIs through vouchers this will further boost the local economy and potential of local traders and trigger market growth.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CAFOD country programme undertook rigorous preparatory work including sending its cash transfer programming (CTP) focal staff for training CaLP in Kenya in 2019. Additional staff and partner staff were trained on carrying out market analysis for CTP in the field in 2019. This enhanced staff capacity on CTP. The skills in CTP were honed by carrying out an actual market analysis by CAFOD in Gog rial in 2021 in preparation for its food and NFIs distribution project which was successfully carried out involving local traders. Consultations during the market analysis were held with communities, local chambers of commerce who mobilized the traders, other local vendors, consultations with other NGOs, and government officials. This was to dig out and unpack information to streamline processes before it rolled out its cash-based response. As mentioned above, a market assessment and cash feasibility analysis were undertaken which informed CAFOD to embark on the commodity voucher programme. CAFOD will build on lessons learnt from that experience in shaping the CVA approach for this project. In 2021 CAFOD has already embarked on market analysis for the supply of various commodities such as WASH NFIs and crop and vegetable seeds which this project will benefit from this analysis. To update this finding the programme will undertake further mini-market assessment to make sure that the context is still relevant to undertake voucher-based assistance delivery modalities. There is also an ongoing market analysis on the handpump spare parts supply chain in which CAFOD will engage local traders in the supply of handpump parts in the local market for easy access by the community. To ensure beneficiary choice and flexibility, especially as regards the types of WASH, hygiene items and the mode of assistance, there would be consultations with the participating households whose lives are affected. An important secondary aim has been to stimulate local business and inject money into the local economy to strengthen the market system

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will make a deliberate effort to explore, understand and mitigate programme related risks leading to the development and implementation of strategy that minimize and address all ranges of threats and risks both external and internal. In practice, this means that all intervention responses will be guided by Core Humanitarian Standards to which CAFOD is certified and Do-No-Harm and conflict- sensitivity approaches through analysis of risks to programme participants and environment. In relation to risk 8.1 on long distances to distribution centres CAFOD has proactively mitigated this risk in its projects and will do so for this project. In the past there were 7 distribution sites in Yirol, but because of protection risks related to long walking distances, an additional 4 distribution centers were created to reduce distances. In Gogrial distribution points were increased after consultations with traders who were asked to open temporary shops closer to participants. As part of its responsibility of mitigating risk 8.2 and 8.3 on SEA, CAFOD has various policies on PSEA policy, code of behaviour and safeguarding policies which are signed by every staff member, volunteers and contractors before any official engagement to ensure protection of project participants. Project participants are also made aware of these policies through various information sharing ways including setting up of information kiosks and agents in the project locations. CAFOD and its partners have a robust complaint and feedback handling mechanism in place, and hotline to receive complaints and feedback. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistleblower. CAFOD has clearly laid out procedures on handling and responding to feedback and complaints. Complaints are categorised and serious, sensitive complaints such as SEA are handled by senior management with clear investigation and disciplinary protocols it must be resolved within set time limits and feedback provided to affected persons. In relation to risk 8.4 on no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors CAFOD has robust protection response and prevention activities in most of its projects. CAFOD programmes provided community based GBV protection measures such as case management, psychosocial support, creation of women and girls friendly spaces, CAFOD and its partners have mapped out referral pathways trained service providers on their use. There is also training of various service providers (health workers, police, chiefs etc.) in Yirol East and Gogrial to strengthen their capacities on GBV case reporting, survivor centred handling and management of cases. As regards risk 8.8 where the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs- based selection of beneficiaries CAFOD has employed measures to restrict or limit this influence. CAFOD makes use of a targeting committee comprised mostly of women from the community. Targeting criteria are agreed with the community in inception meetings and their inputs incorporated to ensure no vulnerable families are left out.When targeting is completed a public meeting of community members is called forpublic verification and acclamation of selected persons. CAFOD also uses its SADI (Safety Access, Dignified and Inclusivity) framework to mitigate exclusion of marginalised and vulnerable groups in a project. The framework carries out a comprehensive assessment of at-risk groups and how they are affected differently by a disaster or conditions of their vulnerability using such diversity statuses such as sex, age, disability, being a refugee, or IDP, minority status and others. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of groups of people.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The proposed intervention will contribute to the collective outcomes on food insecurity by improving access to basic services in emergency, mitigate GBV and protection and build WASH resilience. It will Improve level of WASH services for both IDP and host community in greater Tonj Counties which falls between severity levels 3 to 4. WASH is a critical complement in enabling reduction in morbidity and mortality. The close correlation between malnutrition and diarrhoea due to poor WASH services is a well-established cause of morbidity and mortality amongst children under five years old. The undernourished are more likely to suffer higher incidences and increased mortality rates from diarrhoeal diseases, while on the other hand those suffering from diarrhoea are more likely to suffer from wasting, stunting, or other complications resulting from abrupt dehydration and loss of valuable nutrients and electrolytes. Furthermore, recurrent parasitism due to faecal-oral contamination associated with poor hygiene and sanitation leads to repeat cyclical infections, diarrhoeal diseases and malnutrition. This intervention will complement CAFOD's ongoing response in the locality and will improve community’s access to safe drinking water as well as increasing adoption of good hygiene practices thus, reducing incidences of diarrhoea and other hygiene and sanitation related diseases and building resilience of communities to shocks and stresses.
