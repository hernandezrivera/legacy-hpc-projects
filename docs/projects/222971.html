Project Summary

The project aims to enhance the resilience and economic empowerment of both Rohingya refugees and host community members in Cox's Bazar through self-reliance and livelihood initiatives. The project focuses on skill-building and income generation, with embedded support for each community.
For Rohingya Refugees
The project will provide Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) and non-formal technical skills training to increase capacity and self-reliance. This will include creating income-generation opportunities within the camp through self-employment initiatives. Participants will receive a 120-hour skills training package over 30 days (4 hours a day), with a training incentive of 150 BDT/day. By leveraging with existing PROTTYASHI managed facilities, we will also use abandoned or unused facilities in the camp by renovating them.
For Host Communities
A non-formal technical and vocational skills training service will be offered to the host community, with a focus on empowering youth and adults for better integration into the labor market. Training will include 120 hours of non-formal technical skills training and 360 hours of vocational skills development, following the skills development framework and BTEB guidelines.
Courses for technical skills training will cover trades such as craft making, dressmaking, carpentry (wooden/bamboo handicrafts), hand stitching, and soap making, selected based on market analysis. This will be applied for both refugee and host communities.
Inclusion and Vulnerability
The project will prioritize vulnerable households in both the refugee and host communities to create an inclusive environment. An estimated 3% of participants will be identified who are facing different type of disabilities, and 80% of beneficiaries will be female to bridge gender equality gaps. Skill development for the intended beneficiaries will be complemented by the inclusion of DRR components in its modules and materials to make the people resilient towards disaster impact.
Post-Training Support and Income Generation
Graduated beneficiaries will be engaged with income generation through creating self-employment with MSME (Micro, Small & Medium Enterprise) in both host and refugee communities with the in-kind supports. The host community graduates will also receive job placement and referral support to engage in the formal sector. A robust market linkages initiatives will be undertaken to support businesses of the host communities and will be linking them with PROTTYASHI’s “GRASSROOTS” social enterprise, integrating both offline (shops, kiosks, fairs, retail) and online (e-commerce platform) strategies to ensure sustainable income generation. This will be monitored and followed up using regular income tracker and trend analysis.
Market Linkages and Economic Reintegration
The refugee skilled graduates will be linked with available product supply chains within the camp, following government guidelines, to secure income generation. This support will also enhance the refugees' confidence and capabilities, aiding their eventual economic reintegration upon repatriation to Myanmar or relocation in other places as guided by the GoB and other international actors.
Long-Term Impact
The project will enhance the capacity of households to meet their nutritional needs and build resilience against future humanitarian crises. By combining skill-building, income generation, and market integration, the project aims to create sustainable economic opportunities, promoting self-sufficiency and strengthening resilience in both refugee and host communities. These supports will improve their capacity of affording and meeting nutritional needs of the households ensuring their overall well-being. In addition, engaging host community youth in the skills development programs will foster peace-building and social cohesion, contributing to a more harmonious relationship between refugees and the host population.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

With around 1,010,298 Rohingya Refugees, marked by 140% rise in Rohingya population in Bangladesh after the arrival of an unprecedented number of Rohingyas in 2017, Cox's Bazar is now the world's biggest refugee settlement. Despite ongoing efforts by numerous national and international organizations to address the issues induced by the situation, these interventions are insufficient to meet the community’s escalating needs. Further complicating the situation, funding for the Rohingya response operation has been decreasing. The Refugee-Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-VII) Report, published in July 2024, highlights that all food security and vulnerability indicators were significantly impacted by ration cuts in the camps from March 2023 where 70 percent of the Rohingya classified as poor to borderline diet conditions for the first time since 2017. At the same time, Bangladeshi households reported a gradual decline in all indicators, with highly vulnerable households increasing to 30 percent from 13 percent in 2017.In the host community, 57 percent of Bangladeshi households urged the need for greater access to fresh food and the continuation of food assistance. As a result, malnutrition & growth challenges have increased alarmingly. According to the UNHCR Bangladesh 2023 Standardized Nutrition Survey, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is above emergency threshold as per the WHO emergency classification, and the worst since the 2017 influx. It is clear that permanent solutions are needed to give income-generating opportunities for both Rohingya and host community populations to arrange basic family needs by taking into account diversified needs, especially in light of budget difficulties that are resulting in decreases in life-saving food vouchers. In order to meet the urgent & contextualized requirements of these communities, solutions that caters to the specific needs of the camp & host community, initiatives such as facilitation of Accelerated adult literacy packages, non-formal technical training, vocational training that leads to skill development, self-employment, creation and expansion of market linkages, job referrals, volunteer engagement, conditional and unconditional cash support can create income generation opportunities that will contribute towards making the communities more resilient to economic shocks and reduce dependency on humanitarian aids.On the other hand, inequality between genders due to harmful social norms and practices have led to making women dependent on men even for basic survival needs while this dependency has a reflection on everything. Women in these communities face more challenging circumstances when it comes to earning a living because, without their empowerment, the community as a whole cannot be empowered. Economic inclusion will be improved by addressing vulnerable groups like women and making them priority participants in skill development programs. Addressing these issues requires a vigorous intervention that includes facilitation of resilient livelihood opportunities to meet the urgent needs of both Rohingya & host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The need for such skills along with special consideration of gender & age groups is critical, given that many refugees lack formal education and vocational training opportunities aligning with national and international frameworks, including the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) set by the United Nations, particularly Goal 1 (No Poverty) and Goal 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth).

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project is committed to ensure that all project support and services are accessible, providing necessary accommodations to address diverse needs aligning with the protection mainstreaming guidance. A protection risk assessment will be conducted to identify the vulnerabilities of the target population, focusing on protection concerns such as gender-based violence (GBV), child protection, exploitation and marginalization. The project will sensitize the staff on protection principles, safeguarding policies, and how to respond to protection concerns, ensuring they are equipped to handle sensitive issues. The project will sensitize the beneficiaries by raising awareness on gender-based violence, exploitation, and child protection, and how these can impact the livelihoods and well-being of individuals. Moreover, a clear and accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established, enabling community members to report issues confidentially and receive timely responses. A strong focus will be placed on women's empowerment, enabling them to advocate for their rights and participate actively in decision-making processes. To safeguard women from any risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, strict protocols and guidelines will be enforced, creating a secure environment for all participants. These measures will include thorough background checks for staff and volunteers, regular monitoring and evaluation to identify vulnerabilities, and the establishment of robust support systems in collaboration with local authorities and organizations. The project will also utilize the referral mechanism in Cox’s Bazar for protection issues wherever required. It will also collaborate with key stakeholders through regular meetings and information-sharing platforms to ensure coordinated efforts, and advocate for stronger protection strategies, heightened risk awareness, and collective actions to enhance livelihoods while protecting vulnerable groups.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project initiatives will be reaching individuals of all ages and those with disabilities, whether directly or indirectly. Aligning with this commitment, the project will ensure 3% persons with disabilities as direct beneficiary. One of the key steps will be identifying people with disabilities, including children, through the use of Washington Group Questions-WGQ. This ensures that no one is overlooked and that the project’s benefits are accessible to everyone.In order to make sure that all groups can equally access the project’s offerings, the intervention strategies will focus on inclusivity and fairness. In this objective, the project will provide all the staffs and volunteers with training on disability inclusion and disability inclusive humanitarian action to ensure the project team is well-sensitized on the persons with disability inclusion programming.The project will ensure accessibility of the facilities and supports for the persons with disabilities through providing different assistive device and tools, adding assistive component like ramp in the training centers and adopting learning methods required as of the type of disability.This will also involve adapting the program to accommodate the varying needs and capabilities of each age group. The project will be mindful of children’s needs, keeping them away from labor-intensive tasks, and ensuring that resources and support are distributed fairly, with a particular focus on increasing food security for children and the elderly.These principles will be reflected throughout the project life cycle, from initiation, planning, selecting beneficiaries to service delivery and impact evaluation.Finally, an accessibility audit will be carried out at all project facilities and service points to ensure that they are fully accessible to individuals with disabilities. This holistic approach aims to ensure that every group is empowered and has the opportunity to thrive from the project.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will be implemented through in-depth community consultations using various tools e.g., Focus Group Discussions-FGDs and Key Informant Interviews-KIIs, to ensure that the unique needs of diverse age and gender groups are addressed. Community input will also guide key decisions in the implementation phase, such as selecting beneficiaries, training venues and times.The project will conduct livelihood counselling to inform the community people about the project interventions and supports. In line with the ISCG guideline, the project will inform the community about the LSDS priorities and plan for the economic empowerment of the refugees through translating the plan into a format accessible to communities in livelihood counselling. The project will conduct participatory evaluation of the intervention and share the results of the evaluation with the communities through local workshops and meetings like camp level meeting, Upazila and District NGO Coordination Meeting, community-based sensitization sessions and the stakeholders through organizing annual lessons learned workshop. To maintain accountability, the project will be supported by PROTTYASHI's MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning) team, responsible for addressing complaints through both proactive (hotline) and reactive (CFM box) channels. The community will also have a role in decisions around item distributions, ensuring that interventions are relevant and effective.Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be integrated into every stage of the project, with continuous feedback and adjustments made based on client satisfaction. A PRR tool will prioritize all-inclusive community concerns and guide necessary modifications. Additionally, the project will strengthen the capacity of targeted populations and ensure they are well-informed about AAP processes, fostering a responsive and inclusive approach throughout the project’s lifecycle.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will implement comprehensive measures to address the risks of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) through mandatory training for staff and beneficiaries, ensuring they are well-informed about prevention and response actions. Structured training sessions will be organized to sensitize staff, volunteers, frontline staff and vendors—on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles, available reporting mechanisms, and the importance of fostering a safe environment. To reinforce these initiatives, a clear reporting mechanism will be established, with guidelines for handling reported incidents and strict confidentiality for complainants. The project will ensure strict confidentiality for individuals who report complaints or concerns and will inform participants about the referral pathway, which connects survivors to vital support services, including counseling, legal aid, and medical assistance, to aid in their recovery and empowerment. The project will distribute informational materials such as posters and flyers to raise awareness within the community and help individuals recognize and report abuse or exploitation. Additionally, the community heads will be actively engaged in advocacy efforts to strengthen commitment to PSEA, cultivating a culture of accountability. The project lead will prioritize SEA prevention, integrating SEA discussions into all coordination meetings, with management actively monitoring compliance and progress. Apart from that, the project will ensure that participants, especially women, girls, and other vulnerable groups, are protected from exploitation while receiving in-kind support. Through actively promoting safeguarding principles and establishing a culture of zero tolerance for any kind of sexual misconduct, the project will create a secure, respectful, and supportive environment that upholds the highest standards of protection and accountability for all involved.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will contribute to the achievement of strategic objective 2 (Restore and Rehabilitate the natural environment and conserve ecosystem) and strategic objective 3 (Strengthen community resilience against climate related and other environmental risks) of the EEN Climate Action Strategy 2023-2025. The project will incorporate climate actions within its processes and methodologies through sensitizing the intended beneficiaries on disaster risks for their assets and resources in the ever-changing climate context and environmental challenges. In this process, the project will provide the beneficiaries with Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) inclusive technical skills training through adding components of DRR within the training modules and materials aligning with the sector guideline. The project will connect the beneficiaries with local level disaster management committees at the host and refugee communities they can take anticipatory actions to safeguard their assets and resources getting early warning information from the committees. Environmental sustainability remains a cornerstone of the project approaches and methodologies and will be ensured through using natural resource based and biodegradable input materials for business development. Besides, the project will utilize solar energy for availing power supply to the training facilities encouraging use of renewable energy within the community.As an outcome, the project increase access to livelihoods for the refugee population that will reduce their financial barrier to LPG access and increase their purchasing power. This will reduce their tendency towards fossil fuel consumption aligning with the common goal of EEN Climate Action Strategy 2023-2025. The project will maintain coordination with Energy and Environment Network (EEN) in implementing the interventions in host and refugee communities through participating in their events, sharing require reports and adopting recommended strategies in implementation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures will be embedded within the project strategies and methodologies to secure protection of resources, facilities, community people as well as structures maintaining a coordinated and collaborative approach. The project will adhere to the sector specific contingency plan/fund, business continuity plan, EPR plan and EPR Working Group workplan to avoid loss of the resources due to disaster situation taking proactive actions utilizing its resources/contingency fund. PROTTYASHI have a emergency response team in Cox’s Bazar which will be ensuring EPR initiatives immediately analyzing the community needs and potentials risks. At the camp and host community level, training facilities will be made strong enough and available to be utilized for temporary relocation making them accessible for all type of population including persons with disability, elderly and other vulnerable groups during any cyclone situation. In these situations, the project will ensure that• Effective communication with camp and host community level disaster management committees is maintained and proactive initiatives are taken• Emergency measures are in place in anticipation of identified risk• Minimum protection standards are maintained for the livelihood beneficiaries• Accountability to affected population (AAP) measures are in place• Zero tolerance approach is secured in response to sexual exploitation and abuse• Recovery actions are in place to attain ‘build back better’ for the livelihood beneficiaries following the contingency planThese strategies will be accomplished in line with the Multi Hazard Response Plan for the Rohingya Refugees in Cox’s Bazar District and National Plan for Disaster Management 2021-2025. Besides, the project will ensure ISCG reporting for any disaster incorporating the updates on impact, response and challenges being observed at the particular targeted locations.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project will be implemented aligning with the IASC Guidelines on “Working with and for young people in humanitarian and protracted crises” and include youth in the design and implementation of activities. PROTTYASHI will Implement Livelihoods programming addressing the needs of young people with aim to build their human, social and financial capital in the form of lifeskills training, vocational training particularly for the host community youths and community engagement activities.The Accelerated Adult Learning Package and Non-Formal Technical Training in the Rohingya community and vocational training in the host community are tailored to address specific needs of youths aged 18-24 years, providing essential education and practical skills that will prepare them for productive futures. The Accelerated Adult Learning Process focuses on delivering foundational skills in literacy, numeracy, and life skills, structured to quickly bridge educational gaps for youth who have missed years of schooling due to displacement or resource limitations. These approaches help Rohingya and host community youth develop essential skills needed for everyday life and work, fostering self-confidence and the ability to participate actively in their communities. Complementing this is the Non-Formal Technical Training, which offers hands-on learning on the skills that are in demand within the local context, the project will integrate the trained youths with different sectors through linkage building within and across sectors in humanitarian response.These technical skills enable youth to seek informal work, become self-employed, or even start small businesses, providing income opportunities where formal employment is limited. Following the IASC guidelines, PROTTYASHI will hold consultations – grouped by age and sex of participants from the youth community with a diverse cross-section of young people to identify needs and barriers to access during the implementation of the programs.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project will promote self-reliance and resilience for the refugees through economically and socially empowering them in the form of increasing their resources and purchasing power to meet the nutritional, household and personal needs. This will reduce their dependency on aids and help them attain self-sufficiency that will make them resilient towards shock of any disaster or humanitarian crisis situation. Such resilience will be strengthened through enhancing their transferrable skills in the form of life skills training which will make them competent to survive and develop assets whenever they will be repatriated. Besides, knowledge on DRR approaches will make them prepared to analyze the disaster situations and help them take instant safeguarding measures to protect their resources and assets letting them acquire disaster resilience.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project will be adopting strategies and methodologies aligning with the objectives of Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Plan to accelerate the economic transformation, jobs and sustainable development for the residents of Cox’s Bazar District. In particular, the project will be strengthening the economic resilience of Cox’s Bazar economy through creating higher financial resilience and better livelihoods in the District, improving human capital and productivity through investing in education for the population of the District and promoting opportunities for vocational training and employment-driven skill development and promoting social cohesion until repatriation through reducing perceived and actual gaps in services, opportunities and benefits received by population residing in Cox’s Bazar including refugees and host communities and strengthening vulnerable people’s engagement mechanisms to ensure protection from extreme deprivation resulting from shocks.
