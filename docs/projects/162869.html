Project Summary

This project aims to improve the food security, nutrition and livelihood status of 94,650 individuals (15,775 HHs) of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Zalengei, West Jabal Marra (Nertiti), Central Jabal Marra (Golo) and NorthJabal Marra (Rokero) Localities in Central Darfur State. According to needs assessment and pre-harvest assessment carried out by VSF-G and partners, post-harvest assessment conducted by the State Ministry of Agriculture and other relevant reports revealed, although there has been an improvement in the rainy season in 2018 from 2017, the targeted localities still experienced rainfall variability and dry spells, as a result, there was extremely poor harvest, affecting mostly vulnerable IDPs, returnees and destitute host communities who have suffered destruction of crops and livestock, loss of livelihood assets & income and market disruptions. High levels of displacement are also placing severe pressure on the host communities, who already face elevated levels of vulnerability and food insecurity. This situation was exacerbated by the volatility of market prices. Deterioration of natural resources, lack of basic infrastructure and services in the targeted localities have affected livestock with poor pastures and disease outbreaks as a result of high concentrations of livestock around limited pastures and water sources. The main objective of this project is to assist vulnerable conflict-affected populations in achieving their own food security and improving their livelihoods by increasing their production and income through integrated FSL and cash interventions. VSF-G will work together with Local Partner (UPO) to provide emergency agric inputs and services to 4,500 farming HHs including: improved seeds of staple cereal crops (22,500 Kg of sorghum and 22,500 Kg of millet), cash crops (45,000 Kg of groundnut) and vegetables (450 Kg of okra, 90 Kg of tomato and 225 Kg of watermelon), hand tools (4,500 rakes and 4,500 hoes) and extension training to 150 farming HHs. The project will also provide emergency animal health services including: provision of refresher training, veterinary drugs and replenishment of equipment to 30 active CAHWs, vaccination of 250,000 animals belonging to 10,000 HHs, voucher-based treatment of animals belonging to 3,525 HHs and extension services to beneficiaries. Animal health interventions through voucher-based treatment contribute to save livestock assets of poor HHs, selection of beneficiary HHs for this activity will be based on prior assessment. Each selected HH will receive veterinary services which include treatment of internal and external parasites and other infectious diseases. This activity will be implemented through the engagement of community members, private pharmacies, CAHWs and the Government. This modality will focus targeting beneficiaries and was selected upon proven VSF-G experience. The project will include supporting vulnerable 225 female-headed HHs from the IDPs and returnees with goats restocking, to provide replacement herd for those who have lost their livestock assets and produce milk for HH consumption. To enhance the productivity of lactating goats concentrated animal feed will be provided for the first three months, additional 300 HHs from DPs and host communities will also be provided with animal feed to improve their milk production. Involvement of the communities in cash for work program (450HHs) to rehabilitate natural resources (rangelands) will help protecting rangelands from further degradation and provide cash injection for vulnerable HHs, while increasing their awareness towards environmental issues through orientation sessions. Extremely poor HHs (300 HHs) who cannot participate in CfW program will be targeted with unconditional cash transfer program to cover part of the HH food basket and improve their food security status. 1 - Needs

VSF-G and partners conducted a rapid needs assessment in Nertiti and Golo in October, 2017, with the pre-harvest assessment conducted by SHF FSL Consortium Project, which is being implemented in North Darfur, Central Darfur and West Darfur States. These assessments revealed urgent needs in terms of food security and livelihoods. The respondents for the assessments were from the IDPs, returnees and host communities in Nertiti and Golo, over 90% of the respondents (out of 204 surveys) indicated that they suffer from food insecurity, this problem was more sever in Nertiti than Golo, which is consistent with the predominant status of the respondents from Nertiti; being IDPs who have lost their production assets. 15% of the respondents from Nertiti stated that they entirely depend on food aid for their HHs’ food basket. Agriculture is the predominant livelihood activity in Central and West Darfur and the main source of food (for 97% of the residents). The majority of respondents (95.9%) confirmed that there are constraints to agricultural activities including accessibility to farmland, availability to improved seeds and tools, in addition to that the fluctuation in rainfall (rainy season, 2017) have caused losses due to sowing and re-sowing by farmers. 85.5% of the communities did not have access to enough seeds in the previous rainy season, 97.2% did not have access to appropriate tools and 60% indicated absence of veterinary services -including animal vaccines- in their areas, hence, they are facing high risk of livestock morbidity and mortality due to vulnerability to disease outbreaks. These findings are in line with the State Ministry needs assessment which they have shared with VSF-G; in addition to agricultural support to vulnerable communities (improved seeds for staple cereal and vegetable crops, locally appropriate agricultural tools and establishment of nursery for production of fruit seedlings in Nertiti), there is urgent need to rehabilitate rangelands which have deteriorated as a result of high concentration of livestock around available and accessible resources, rangelands plays a significant role in limiting tensions between farmers and herders in the region, there is also need to provide supplementary animal feed to improve milk production for children, increase the immunity of animals to fight diseases and eventually prevent losses of key livelihood asset. In Nertit Locality there are about 93,000 IDPs, 21,000 of them are new IDPs in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in all sectors including FSL. According to State HAC, there are 36,757 and 14,000 DPs (returnees) in Rokero and Golo localities respectively, who have recently returned to their villages, and need urgent humanitarian assistance particularly FSL. According to FEWS NET, although food access is improving among IDPs and poor households in Jabal Marra area of Central Darfur, however, restrictions on access to land, limited agricultural labor opportunities, and continued low asset holdings as a result of conflict will continue to limit household productive capacity. Households in these areas will continue to require humanitarian assistance through May 2018. Sorghum and millet prices are expected to remain 15-20 percent higher than the recent five-year average and 10-15 percent higher than during the same period last year, due in part to increased production costs. According to Sudan IPC Technical Working Group, October 2017 all of Central Darfur Localities are in crisis (IPC Phase 3) and targeted communities face high risk of moderate and severe acute malnutrition among children under five.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Gender issues are considered here in terms of roles and responsibilities. The burden on women in the local communities of the targeted locations is very high. In South Kordofan State, the burden of HH food supply, children and household management is entirely placed on women. The polygamy practice as a way of life has reduced the status of women to mere free labor. Traditionally women in these areas are involved in farm and animal production activities. This explains why the targeted beneficiaries of the project are mostly women. The project will focus on training women in animal husbandry, good agricultural practices and manufacturing of fuel efficient stoves. Special consideration will be given to females who head their households and women from households with more than six members, female/children-headed HHs who will benefit from vegetable seeds distribution and goat restocking activities. The livelihood portfolio in the targeted areas varies substantially between male and female, each one of them experience poverty unequally and differently. This necessitates that this proposed project will ensure that both women and men have equal access to and control over resources, decision making and benefit at all stages of the project from the planning, implementation and finalization. The project will contribute to gender awareness among different community members during all the trainings that will be conducted; the project will seek involvement of both men and women putting more emphasis on the participation of women in community-based activities. This will be done carefully to ensure maximum consideration and appreciation of the cultural differences with regards to the different roles of men and women in society. The project will make use of different approaches to equally reach both genders based on needs, roles and responsibilities, especially among the IDPs, pastoralists and agro-pastoralists. 75% of seeds distribution will target women (female-headed HHs), 80% of goats restocking will target vulnerable IDPs and returnees women and the FES training is 100% targeting women

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their tribes and ethnicity or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with communities and the opportunity provided to raise concerns or complaints on the selection process will be in place. Selection criteria for all project activities will focus on female-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, orphans, elders and persons with disabilities; beneficiary selection for goats restocking will focus on female-headed HHs, HHs with malnourished children, vulnerable HHs with children under the age of 5 and elders; tools distribution will focus on vulnerable farmer IDPs and returnees who have lost their production assets due to conflict and displacement; stabilized soil block training will focus on returnees, vulnerable host communities and IDPs men; fuel efficient stoves training will target mainly vulnerable women from IDPs , returnees and host communities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

VSF-G adopts Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership guidelines (HAP) and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards and building capacity, communication, participation in programs, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of a strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. VSF-G’s mainstreaming approach will focus on assessing and addressing the needs of all within communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elderly, those living with disabilities among other vulnerable and minority groups to ensure inclusive community consultation and participation, access to services by all, specific needs of extremely vulnerable groups and the safety of all program participants. To ensure that community engagement is maintained through various stages of implementation, and that key information is shared and collected, VSF-G will undertake the following: - Formulate Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) to be signed with the partners to establish feedback and complaint &response mechanisms.- Sign with its partners a commitment to prevention of all forms of abuse and sexual exploitation of affected population.- Draft AAP guidelines for data collection and reporting. - Monitor the partners to ensure implementation of AAP.- Community meetings will be held at the beginning of the project to gauge acceptance and needs, as well ensure that the project is well understood by targeted communities. - Community committees will be created to organize and mobilize beneficiaries; each established community committee will have three elected representatives i.e., head, secretary and reporting members to provide leadership and participate in the project management cycle.- Organize community consultation meetings to promote participation and involvement of the beneficiaries in formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule.- Participation of community committees in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, identification of assets, distribution of agricultural and livestock inputs and monitoring & evaluation of the interventions.- Getting the community’s feedback during focus group discussion (FGD), individual interviews, and meetings with community committees, community leaders, women, men and youth as a way forward to adjust or revise the project implementation plan.- Conduct routine spot checks for project beneficiaries to get their views/reaction on their livelihood status, consumption score, purchasing power and income levels during the project implementation. - Feedback and complaint & response mechanisms (create hotlines, suggestion boxes in project locations, provide leaflets with office contacts and addresses of sub-offices and HQ) to be provided to community committees to facilitate reporting and management of complaints.
