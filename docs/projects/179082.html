Project Summary

Due to the ongoing 10-year crisis, movement and the corrosion of public services and infrastructure, there is increased risk of disease outbreaks. More than half the population now lives in poverty, with around 8 million people becoming poor since the beginning of the war https://unstats.un.org/unsd/publications/statistical-yearbook
. The basic health status of women and children in Syria remains precarious, with a fragile health sector. This situation has been exacerbated by the disruptions to allied sectors, such as food security, water, sanitation and hygiene which have further compounded the prevailing vulnerabilities. The COVID-19 pandemic also contributed to the increased poverty and vulnerability of children and women due to the lock-down and interrupted social services. The economic crisis and devaluation of the local currency also added to the vulnerability of Syrian children and women who are not able to pay for the essential health and nutrition services. This proposal from UNICEF aims to support the provision of basic lifesaving curative, preventive and promotional health services, while enhancing early recovery and resilience of the sector by contributing to strengthening the service delivery partners, community systems through supporting local NGOs and community volunteers, and support health system strengthening interventions including information system for primary health care and immunization. The project is also conceived with a view to promoting the complementarity of the contributions of other sections within UNICEF (Nutrition, WASH, Education and Child protection) and other key sector partners (UN agencies, NGOs etc).
More than 5 million women and children will directly benefit from the package of services, focusing on the highest severity areas in all governorates of Syria as defined by the health cluster. Programme effectiveness shall be enhanced by applying a conflict and risk informed lens to planned interventions, and the promotion of the participation of affected populations. The accountability towards affected population (APP) is integrated throughout the programme cycle.
UNICEF will work in 262 sub districts, 163 are with severity scale 3 or higher and 99 are in lower severity scale but they are hosting IDPs and they need to be supported for the essential maternal and child health services including immunization.
The support to primary health care and immunization programme will strongly support resilience through building strong health information system, building strong cold chain system for vaccines allover the country, building national capacity on vaccine management and quality vaccination services. In addition to the rehabilitation of health facilities as needed.

4 - Needs

As Syria crisis approaches its 10th year, the health system remains heavily disrupted. COVID-19 pandemic coupled with economic downturn has pushed the health system to the brink. By mid-2020 more than 7.78 million people in 100 sub-districts were living below 3 critical emergency health standards. With just 58 per cent of hospitals and 53 per cent of PHC Centers fully functional, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system. 2020 saw 39,773 COVID-19 cases and over 1,300 deaths reported in Syria. Further strengthening of all pillars of the COVID-19 response must continue in 2021, in addition to planning for vaccine rollout. Essential health services were disrupted as agencies and health workers alike rushed to respond to COVID-19. For example, Immunization Program experienced temporary suspension due to COVID-19 risk. While catch-up efforts regained most of the lost ground, gaps in coverage remain with DPT3 coverage below herd immunity levels in seven of 14 governorates. Due to cumulative effects of concurrent crises, cost of health services; lack of medicines, supplies or health staff; and fear of exposure to COVID-19 are the main self-reported barriers to health access. Compared to 2020, the health sector PiN has increased three per cent. In order to promote quality, there is a need to expand existing efforts for standardized primary health service packages that guarantee comprehensive reproductive health services; child health and routine immunization services; integrated maternal health and nutrition programming; diagnostic and screening services; and availability of essential medicines. Preventive and response to COVID-19 in 2021 must be maintained and strengthened across sectors.

5 - Implementation of activities

Partner NGOs will run 52 fixed centers & 43 mobile medical teams. IPs will provide essential health package, integration of MHPSS, GBV and NCD through coordination with WHO and UNFPA clinics, nutrition minimum package, and referral support (transportation to, and connections with the referral sites) to available public and private hospitals through agreements with those hospitals and follow-up on referred cases. Vaccination prog. is supported through procurement of vaccines, installation and maintenance of cold chain equipment, monitor vaccination coverage and awareness raising for families on EPI.UNICEF is: A) reporting monthly to the sector to ensure coordinated response, avoiding overlap and reaching the most vulnerable groups, B)has a concrete data collection system and verified calculation methods to report on beneficiaries reached at community level. All IPs are trained on PSEA & accountability towards affected population. Female doctors are available to ensure maternal care is accessible to vulnerable women, UNICEF also implement reporting mechanisms to ensure that services are up to the satisfaction of beneficiaries and they can report on misconduct. Third-party monitors are employed for program monitoring, they also conduct situation monitoring. TPMs are usually deployed from their respective areas of origin and have specific experience and a thorough knowledge of the area and trained. UNICEF developed a WOS info management platform operational across the two hubs of Damascus & Gaziantep. UNICEF monitors programs delivery through staff & TPMs. Qualitative studies to collect feedback through FGD with beneficiaries on the adequacy, appropriateness and quality of C4D campaigns for immunization were also conducted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Attacks against health workers: 28 attacks documented in 2020 resulted in 13 killed health workers and 42 injured. Mitigation measures: Reporting on attacks (SSA & MRM), advocacy to respect IHL.COVID-19 exposure: data showed increase in health workers affected by COVID, with 691official cases, including 30 deaths. Mitigation measures: Providing health workers with PPE and adhering to physical distancing. Gender Based Violence: risk of GBV is still elevated. Mitigation measures: Training for health workers in HFs, Synergy with child protection section & UNFPA to ensure availability of the full complement of training, skills & services for health workers. Promote recruitment of female health workers by IPs to improve access & utilization by vulnerable women.Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: risk of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries persists. Mitigation measures: Enforce PSEA policy for IPs with zero tolerance, with a documented commitment from IPs, community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance, awareness-raising on SEA among IP with continuous training & monitoring.Duty of care: It is obligatory to provide physical & mental safety, security & wellbeing to staff. Mitigating measures: Implementation of “duty of care” policies, measures to ensure regular and consistent delivery of salaries, support health workers to re-connect with their agencies in case of displacement. Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups & Equitable Access: This principle is at the core of UNICEF programming principles. UNICEF concentrate on high severity (3-6) areas, and target women & children. Mitigation measures: Using sector approved vulnerability assessments in planning. Deliver integrated preventive & curative care.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries persists in all phases of the project. Mitigation measures: 1. Enforce UNICEF PSEA policy for all implementing partners IPs with zero tolerance, 2. Documented signed commitment from all implementing partners on prevention measures. 3. Establish community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. 4. Awareness-raising on SEA among IP staff with continuous training.5. Continuous monitoring.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Guard against fraud: UNICEF is implementing programmatic visits and financial spot checks on regular bases to all IPs. All partners have signed on UNICEF rules to keep their records & financial documents for 5 years after the completion of the project. All partners are assessed and classified for their "risk", and all high-risk IPs are asked to take corrective action & checked more regularly and reassessed later after complying with the mitigation measures. Ensure humanitarian principles are upheld and services delivery is delivered equally without discrimination through regular monitoring and orientation of all IPs on those principles and continuous follow-up and feedback from beneficiaries. Community volunteers are trained on how to deliver in a professional unbiased way and they are always selected from the same community to avoid any unnecessary contact with check points.Lockdown and restriction of movement: this was implemented in the first half of 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and posed difficulties for beneficiaries to reach the services, and exacerbated by transportation problems between rural areas and the cities. This was also magnified by the fuel crisis. UNICEF is increasing the transportation support to the mobile medical teams to ensure access for the most vulnerable groups. Devaluation of the local currency and price increase: This led to increased cost of all commodities including medicines & medical consumables, this will continue for 2021 due to the economic crisis and the continuous sanction on Syria. Mitigating measures: UNICEF will increase salaries for health workers & specialists working with IPs in coordination with other UN agencies to ensure continuity of services in high-severity areas
