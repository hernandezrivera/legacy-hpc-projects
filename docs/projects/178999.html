Project Summary

The Nutrition preventative programme aims to address urgent and life-saving nutrition needs of mothers and children in Syria, located in high severity areas across the entire country. The programme targets all affected populations, including IDPs, host communities and returnees with high severity food insecurity and nutrition indicators.
The Nutrition preventative activities aim to:
- Support improved and equitable access to life saving nutritional services for food insecure and vulnerable children (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) at community and facilities level to stabilize and/or reduce malnutrition (acute, chronic and micronutrient deficiencies) in the target population.
− To prevent acute, chronic malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in children 6-23 months
− Improve diet diversity amongst pregnant and lactating women within food insecure and vulnerable families.
WFP aims to target boys and girls aged 6- 23months and PLWs, including those with disability, through its preventive programme. The Programme is implemented in coordination with the Ministry of Health and other partners of the nutrition sector in Syria including UN agencies, National and International NGOs. Considering the high needs and limited resources, the project will adopt a context and severity-based approach to prioritize sub-districts identified with high nutritional and food insecurity severity, mainly those with severity level 3,4 &5. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to locations where severity of food security and nutrition of is more than 4 and to newly accessible areas.

4 - Needs

The ten years of protracted conflict in Syria has led to a staggering 4.9 million pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children in need of life-saving nutrition interventions, an increase from 4.6 million in 2020 (as per data from the nutrition sector). Despite overall acute malnutrition levels remaining below the emergency threshold, rates among children and PLWs have witnessed a consistent increase as per nutrition surveillance data during 2020. In addition to the increase in acute malnutrition rates, Nutrition vulnerabilities are mainly manifested in Syria through high levels of chronic malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies. This is largely attributed to the economic downturn, low purchasing power, increased food insecurity, suboptimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and diseases (including impacts of COVID-19). According to recent data from the nutrition sector, an estimated 141,540 children aged 6-59 months face an elevated risk of morbidity and mortality attributed to acute malnutrition, 0.6 million children are projected to suffer from stunting, one million children are facing the adverse impacts of anemia, while over half a million women of child-bearing age that are anemic could die without appropriate support in Syria . Hence, in the current circumstances, the nutrition prevention programmes are of vital importance as they possess an extensive outreach to collectively combat the three forms of undernutrition (wasting, stunting and micronutrient deficiencies), in the most vulnerable populations, tackling both immediate and un especially when implemented through a multi-sectoral integrated approach

5 - Implementation of activities

During 2021, The Nutrition programme will target a maximum of 598,800 children and PLWs with its activities in sub-district which have score of 3 and above on food and nutrition severity scale.The section below describes individual activities to be implemented aims at addressing the various nutrition concerns:• Targeted provision of lipid based nutrient supplements (LNS-MQ) to children 6-23 months of age, from food insecure and vulnerable families receiving WFP general food assistance inside Syria and through cross-border operations, ensuring daily macro and micronutrient requirements are met. • Provision of vouchers redeemable at local shops for fresh foods (fruits, vegetables, dairy, meat) and hygiene items, to Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls (PLWGs) of food insecure and vulnerable families receiving WFP general food distribution inside Syria. This dignified assistance modality puts the beneficiary and their needs at the center of humanitarian programming, enabling them to make informed decisions on the assistance they receive, in addition to receiving nutrition and health awareness, and sexual and reproductive health services • Partnership with UNICEF and UNFPA for complementary nutrition support services to enhance results.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

in order to minimize the potential adverse effects to the targeted communities and humanitarian workers while delivering nutrition interventions to the most vulnerable segments of the community, such as girls and boys under five and PLW, the sector carried out a thorough forecasting of likely risks that may be caused while providing nutrition services, and developed a framework with mitigation measures. Risks related to activities carried out by the nutrition sector are anticipated to be mainly to the health workers moving between camps, potential looting of nutrition supplies, COVID19 spread among health and nutrition workers as well as among beneficiaries, attacks on health workers and facilities as well as beneficiaries accessing the facilities, losing follow up of beneficiaries and creating tension when targeting IDPs for distributions in the host community.In order to mitigate these risks, and ensuring adherence to humanitarian principles, the sector continues to adopt a “Do No Harm” strategy while providing nutrition services to vulnerable groups throughout Syria. These mitigation measures include training of nutrition implementing partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, scaling up community-based approaches to ensure quality access to services. To ensure proper and effective implementation of the Nutrition activities, WFP will maintain regular coordination with MOH, in addition to nutrition actors through the nutrition sector, and various thematic groups developed, especially with UNICEF and UNFPA. The Nutrition programme will seek to incorporate in its design the feedback from beneficiaries received through focus group discussion and meetings with the different stakeholders.
