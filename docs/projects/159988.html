Project Summary

In the SYDF protection overview we find that the main CP issues concerning boys are considered to be Child Labour and Child Recruitment. Even though, both of them are mainly affecting boys, also girls have been reported to be involved in such harmful activities. The main threats for girls keep on being child marriages (reported in over 85% of the assessed sub-districts), sexual violence (especially rapes) was reported to be the main protection concerns for women and girls. Adolescent girls are particularly affected by this kind of problems and protection concerns.
The direct witnessing of atrocities and violence, the continuous experience of forced displacement, family separation, relatives loss and the exposition to CP threats is threatening children well being, both physical and psychological. According to the Somali Child Protection Assessment 2015, 94.2% of sites report behavioural changes in children, including unusual crying and screaming, unwillingness to go to school, isolation and withdrawal, wetting themselves, disrespectful behaviour in the family and sadness (for girls) and unwillingness to go to school, aggression, wetting themselves, disrespect in the family home and wishing to join armed groups (for boys). The report goes on to state that the main stressors include feeling unsafe, attacks, early marriage, not being able to return home and tension in the family (for girls) and feeling unsafe, attacks, lack of food, not being able to return home and tension in the family (for boys). Support is provided by mothers, father and peers, siblings and school teachers (for girls) and respectively by mothers, father, siblings, teachers and peer groups (for boys).
Beside the psychological consequences of the war boys and girls are facing several threats for their lives in the areas where they live. Explosive hazards are considered to be the most threatening issues for people, and especially for children, with evidences showing that boys are more at risk than girls to be harmed from explosive hazards (HRP 2017). Looking at the CP assessment conducted in 2015/2016/2017 by the CP sub-cluster, Explosive Hazards were ranked in the top 5 most significant risk of death or injury for children in Somalia.
This scenario clearly demonstrates how in Somalia an entire generation of youth mainy children is exposed and shaped by traumatic experiences stressing the importance to develop and enhance effective and efficient protection services. Children are among the most vulnerable groups in the Somalia war. Even though most will survive the conflict physically, the immediate and long-term well-being of children remains a serious concern for humanitarian organizations.

Mitigation Measures

Providing Risk Education capacity building and sessions targeting directly children might expose our beneficiaries to a sense of overconfidence and consequently underestimate explosives hazards related risks and threats. For this reason we will relay on qualified and experienced consultants to train our filed staff and we will stick to existing and effective curricula (Unicef and ICRC) in order to deliver adequate risk education messages to our beneficiaries. Thus, a regular monitoring on behavioural changes will be ensured through the entire duration of the programme implementation.
