Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to implement sustainable agriculture, fisheries, livestock and honey processing project for sustainable livelihood for economic recovery and resilience in Western Equatoria State targeting 3,409 IDPs, returnees and vulnerable poorest members of host communities as direct beneficiaries. The households of 3409 direct beneficiaries has 6 indirect beneficiaries each totaling to 20,454 indirect beneficiaries in the project areas in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. These counties were badly affected by the conflict that erupted in Juba in 2013 and 2016 respectively. The targeted three (3) counties are currently hosting high numbers of IDPs and returnees. In accordance with Mid-2019 population (NBS) report/records the populations of the three (3) targeted counties are as follows:-
(1) Mvolo County Total population is 71,586 (14,000 people are at crisis level and 7,000 people at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure
(2) Mundri East County total population is 95,873 (29,000 people are at crisis level and
14,000 people at emergency level). 43,000 people are critically food insecure.
(3) Mundri West County total population is 46,760 (16,000 people are at crisis level and 5,000 people at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure. The average of 60% population in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties are farmers (FAO 2016). The crops grown successfully in these targeted counties include maize, sorghum, beans, pumpkins, okra, tomatoes, groundnuts, and sim-sim. The counties fall within the Green Belt in Western Equatoria State include some of the most fertile lands in the country and farmers in the area can reap two or three harvests per annum from the same plots. In November 2015, Western Equatoria State as a whole experienced one of its worst crop harvests in recent history. The level of food insecurity reached was historical lowest in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties that one person out of four being considered food insecure. The livelihoods inputs/equipment in the conflict affected parts of Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties were looted in several Payams including Bangolo, Medewu, Yeri, Lessi, Dari, Kotobi, Lui, Lozoh Witto Kediba, and Amadi Payams. The signing of the revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS) in September 2018 has relatively brought peace in South Sudan including Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. The IDPs and returnees are returning in their original home land. Unfortunately they lost all their agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs/equipment. There is high need to supply them with agricultural tools, improved seeds, and seed processing equipment, fisheries kits, veterinary drugs, cold chain, and honey processing equipment, sun-drying racks, and solar driers, smoking ovens, post-harvest technology trainings on food packing, handling, hygiene and storage to empower 3409 IDPs/returnees and host communities to produce their own food in their ancestral lands to reduce malnutrition, hunger and gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges. This project will be implemented under COVID 19 protocols:- (1) The awareness of the beneficiaries will be raised to keep social distances (2) Beneficiaries will be mandatory to wear face masks throughout project implementation period (3) beneficiaries will be required to practice frequent hand- washing (4) Beneficiaries will be told to avoid hand-shaking greetings to control and prevent the spread of COVID 19 infections among the project beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

In January 2021, an estimated 1.6 million people remained internally displaced persons in South Sudan and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Dec 18, 2020 the first projection in the period of December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity and 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. In Western Equatoria State; Insecurity, poor roads, lack of basic services prevented the deliveries of lifesaving humanitarian relief assistances. Overall food insecurity worsened and some communities were facing catastrophic needs. More children were acutely malnourished than in the past three years. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced Labor and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards, pushing people to the brinks of mortality especially in urban areas. The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by conflict, related displacement, low crop production, flooding, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19 limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The continuing currency depreciation and associated impacts on commodity prices and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets to purchase food and other basic needs. Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020 (with limited recovery from the previous floods) and humanitarian food assistance much lower than required in the face of increased needs and will certainly result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season. In particular in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties malnutrition and hunger has become the primary causes of many deaths among the IDPs/returnees and host communities in Western Equatoria State. In accordance with Mid-2019 population (NBS) report the populations of the three (3) targeted counties are reported as follows: - (1) Mvolo County Total population is 71,586 (14,000 people are at crisis level and 7,000 people are at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure. (2) Mundri East County total population is 95,873 (29,000 people are at crisis level and 14,000 people are at emergency level). 43,000 people are critically food insecure. (3) Mundri West County total population is 46,760 (16,000 people are at crisis level and 5,000 people are at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure. Lactating/pregnant mothers, children, old aged people and people with disability are vulnerable groups severely affected by food insecurity. There is insufficient distribution of inputs, tools and equipment for IDPs/returnees and host communities to implement livelihood projects. They lack agricultural tools, improved seeds, vegetable irrigation cans, seed processing equipment, fishing kits and veterinary supplies. They also lack post-harvest equipment, tools and inputs such as grinding mills, peanut butter machines, cold chain, solar driers, and honey processing equipment, Sun-drying racks, smoking ovens, milk processing equipment in Mvolo, Mundri East, and Mundri West Counties. The supply of agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs will help beneficiaries to produce their own food and they will gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges. Therefore; this project aims to supply agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs and equipment to enable 3409 IDPs/returnees and host communities to produce their own food.

2 - Project activities

The activities of this project aims to implement Food Security and Livelihoods cluster objective Number 2: (Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19 through the following activities targeting 3409 IDPs, returnees and host communities in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties as follows: - (1) Recruit 3 qualified agricultural officers, 3 fishery officers, 3 livestock officers and 3 Bee-keeping officers as field coordinators (one officer per county). (2) Carryout participatory community mobilization and planning meetings to identify 3409 beneficiaries and to introduce to them overall objectives of the project and also to assess their critical needs, priorities and choices through analyzing technical, financial, environmental and social feasibility to ensure they understand the project to enable them to take ownership of the project to enhance successful implementation and sustainability of the project (3) Conduct mobile field trainings targeting 3409 beneficiaries (men, women, youth and persons with disabilities) to foster peaceful co-existence, cohesion, good governance and accountability among the beneficiaries, to improve their security and business skills in modern food producing, processing, handling, hygiene and storage good practices and also to enable the beneficiaries to form agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing co-operative groups to enable them work together to produce outputs of economic scale that will be sufficient to ensure availability of food supply for households’ consumption and marketing throughout the year; to enhance better health and beneficiaries economic well-being (4) Procure and distribute assorted agricultural tools, improved seeds, and new genetic crops, vegetable irrigation cans to 1966 beneficiaries (men, women, youth and persons with disabilities) to help them to cultivate in their own ancestral agricultural lands and also to set up demonstrative kitchen gardens in 6 nutritional facilities 2 in Mvolo, 2 in Mundri East and 2 in Mundri West Counties. (5) Procure and distribute fishing kits to 238 beneficiaries’ fisher folks (men, women, youth and persons with disabilities) (6) To vaccine and carryout treatment to over 1585 livestock belonging to 317 beneficiaries (men, women, youth and persons with disabilities) who are the vulnerable poorest beneficiaries unable to access and buy veterinary drugs to treat their livestock (7) Procure and distribute post-harvest equipment, tools and inputs (grinding mills, peanut butter machines, cold chains, solar driers, honey processing equipment, sun-dry-racks, smoking oven, milk processing equipment targeting 888 beneficiaries (men, women, youth and persons with disabilities) in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo counties. (8) Conduct continuous project impact monitoring and evaluations. The project will enhance formation of agro-based dealers, fisheries; engage veterinary drugs for livestock vaccination and treatment and training on utilization, operation and maintenance of equipment and tools and intensive technical, hygiene and food safety and different trainings targeting entire beneficiaries 3409 IDPs/returnees and host communities. GREDA had existed in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties since 2012. Between the periods 2015-2018 GREDA constructed 50 low cost houses of two rooms each in Mundri East County for widows and also distributed relief food assistances and vitamins to malnourished IDPs, returnees, pregnant/lactating mothers in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties funded by Al khaier Foundation UK and Vitamin Angels USA. By the end of this project beneficiaries will gain skills to produce their own food to become self-reliant and they will gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges.

GREDA will prepare transparency and accountability measures and tools for reporting. GREDA will produce PSEA brochures for dissemination to staff and project beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees, persons with disabilities) to become aware with the mechanisms for protecting themselves from abuses such as the Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). GREDA will develop a feedback mechanism form and distribute to the beneficiaries to enable them to report back to GREDA management on lack of cooperation, commitment and abuses in project implementation. The tools that will be used for feedback mechanisms are (1) Face-to-face meetings (2) focal group discussions (3) Suggestion boxes to allow beneficiaries to drop their feedbacks (4 ) Key staff in project implementation teams will share their telephone numbers to allow beneficiaries who are aggrieved to report to them for solution or correction.
