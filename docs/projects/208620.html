1 - Needs

To meet the immediate basic needs of newly displaced households, and ensure vulnerable families access survival food needs, shelter, S&S Non-food Items (NFIs) and clean water while receiving health, nutrition and protection services needed to survive and be protected.

2 - Activities

The program will provide services to mothers, fathers, caregivers, and their children under five years of age, pregnant women, and adolescent girls to prevent and treat acute malnutrition in children 0-59 months. Children with acute malnutrition will be treated through community- and facility-based preventive and curative interventions for acute malnutrition. SC will scale up the MAMI Care Pathway to leverage and build on existing support services to support small and nutritionally at-risk infants under 6 months and their mothers. The program will integrate its interventions both within the existing public primary health care (PHC) system and community-based systems. SC will work closely with existing PHCs to identify at risk infants and mothers through existing ANC/PNC/MCH services. On the other hand, SC will also refer mothers and infants to ANC/PNC/MCH clinics to receive essential services like immunization. The program will be implemented jointly by SC and Greencode. Greencode will lead the implementation of community-based MIYCN-E activities and community follow-up of beneficiaries and Tom Brown approach. SC will provide technical support and lead in Mass MUAC screening and referrals and the implementation of facility-based MIYCN-E, inpatient (stabilization centers) and OTP services. - Agriculture: majority of targeted HHs continue to live in IDP settlements with limited access to land to engage in meaningful agriculture. To complement food assistance and improve dietary diversity especially among children 6-59 months and PLWs, SC will support vegetable production through micro gardening. Using an e-voucher system, SC will provide micro gardening kits to caregivers of children in IYCF support groups to encourage HH vegetable production and improve availability of nutritious food.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SC will ensure safe implementation of activities through strict adherence and commitment to uphold the principle of “Do No Harm” in its activities. Suitable sites and locations will be chosen for activities including meetings, focus group discussions and key informant interviews of beneficiaries. Secure sites that are in close proximity to beneficiaries will be given priority to ensure that people can return home before dark to minimize risks. SC and partner staff and enumerators will be trained on safeguarding (child safeguarding and PSEA), gender equality and inclusion, accountability, and Save the Children’s Code of Conduct. All new SC employees are oriented to and required to sign the SC Safeguarding Policy and the Code of Conduct as part of their contract. SC mainstreams protection, gender equality and inclusion across all programming. Older caregivers, child-headed households, persons with disability headed households and female-headed households are referred to the food assistance program as vulnerable households with minors.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

Due to protracted conflict in Northeast Nigeria, majority of HHs in Borno are living as IDPs after multiple displacements; women and children make-up 82% of the total IDP population.9 HHs have not been able to return to their homes of origin and/or to their LGAs of origin due to insecurity. Most of the population is concentrated around Maiduguri town and neighbouring LGAs of Jere. In a recent assessment, IDPs indicated their biggest unmet need is food (76%) followed by NFIs (13%) and shelter (4%)

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will promote transparency and accountability in all its operations and ensure that beneficiaries, community leaders and other community members have access to the feedback and response mechanisms (FRM) and SC will ensure confidentiality to protect beneficiaries. The feedback mechanisms will be established together with and for beneficiaries – including children, adults, persons living with disabilities and other vulnerable groups, and take timely corrective actions to address safety, dignity and access issues raised by users and/or to redefine their assistance. During IYCF sensitization meetings, SC will reiterate messages to beneficiaries, partners, local leaders and other community stakeholders emphasizing that SC assistance is free and impartial, and the right to aid as being unconditional and that no one should ask for any kind of favor in return for aid, particularly sexual and/or monetary. These messages will be reinforced in radio jingles on local radio stations in local languages to provide information to the wider population. SC will use the preferred feedback mechanisms selected by the community, these may include a toll-free mobile phone number, help desk, and community meetings. Within the shopping areas, a FRM desk will be set up and managed by two SC staff (male and female) to ensure beneficiaries can provide feedback on any challenges they face relating to this sector and other components of the program and that they are addressed efficiently. Community feedback meetings to engage with community structures and beneficiaries on the impact of program activities to the community and ways of improving on the support provided.
