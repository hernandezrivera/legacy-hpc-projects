Project Summary

The Education support for refugees and host community project is designed to reach 970 beneficiaries over the period of 12 months. Primary refugee camp will be 1E and Teknaf Upazila, with the expected outcome that target children are able to access basic education, child protection case management and psychosocial support.

Needs

Lack of available learning centers and distance to existing learning centers has been identified as key barrier to education by the Education sector in Cox’s Bazar. Also existing learning centers are running at full-capacity and are unable to enroll additional school-aged children; relevant learning facilities or children aged children 3 -14 are completely absent in the camps. The Government does not officially allow for education activities with the refugees to be undertaken in the Bangla language and actively promotes learning in English and Burmese language. In addition, the Government does not allow the use of their Ministry of Education non-formal education curriculum for new arrivals. Twenty percent (20%) of the refugee and host community population are adolescents and youth between 15 and 24; with less than 2% of this age group accessing education services. Adolescents in this crisis are taking on economic and family responsibilities, which pose barriers to their participation in traditional forms of educational provision - where it does exist for them. Only 32,527 adolescents are accessing education or life skills curricula, of which approximately 70% are girls. Refugees complain that education does not meet their needs, and is not delivered in a way culturally appropriate for girls who reach puberty. In the host community, IRC has identified extreme overcrowding, deteriortating infrastructure (including WASH facilities), lack of school supplies and high drop out rates of both boys and girls. Adolescent boys in particular have a high opportunity cost and are choosing to work; falling wages in the area leave families unable to prioritize paying school fees for girls.

Activities or outputs

IRC is new to the education sector and aims to start up education activities in the coming year, with a small amount of secured funding and a significant amount in a nearly secured. This funding will allow IRC to put a robust feedback mechanism into place, in order to promote accountability to affected populations. IRC also aims to begin working with partners and will be ramping up capacity in order to properly manage and evaluate potential partners in the coming year. Potential activities include several IRC curriculums that are in use world wide, and/or have been developed with IRC's global expertise and are currently being piloted in Banglandesh. These projects are designed to include education, child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) components (inlc case management), and will be run by integrated teams. - Pilot of Safe Healing and Learning Spaces, Through the Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS), IRC will provide opportunities for Social Emotional Learning, basic literacy and numeracy, and parenting skills for caregivers of children and adolescents. These centers will be constructed in 2018 under funding already secured; this project will also make use of them. As noted in the draft JRP, access to more centers with quality services for children and especially 14+ youth are needed. The Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) approach is unique in its approach to reversing the effects of toxic stress in children, through layered Social Emotional Learning (SEL), non-formal education, equipping caregivers/parents with competencies to enhance positive child-parent interaction and reduce violence within the home, and engagement of caregivers and community structures. Evidence shows that a safe, predictable environment with supportive adult relationships helps to mitigate this stress. To support this programming, IRC will also work to strengthen the knowledge and skills of Safe Healing and Learning Spaces staff and parents to through on the job coaching, and support to parents to create a home and learning environment conducive to children’s healthy development and well-being. This is an integrated program with child protection, and will be implemented with a mixed Child Protection/education team, and further backstopped by Child Protection and Gender Based Violence case management. (Girls, boys, 5-14,caregivers, host/refugee) -Start up of non-formal education: IRC will support the non-formal education needs of children through the Safe Healing and Learning Spaces and in the home. Math and literacy skills will be taught by trained facilitators in the SHLS for children who can come there. A mobile model will make use of a tablet-based math curriculum that may be developed through a curriculum pilot . This innovative tablet-based modality will allow for ‘adaptive’ curriculum that responds to the user’s skill level by making each session easier or harder as they move through the material. Trained facilitators will move from location to location to facilitate the use of the tablet curriculum for small groups within the home. This model addresses needs of post-pubescent girls for culturally appropriate education, as noted in the draft JRP. (Boys and girls 5-14 (hopefully 14-19 as well), refugee and possibly host)-IRC will also conduct recreational/sports activities with youth from both communities, including caregivers where possible, in order to promote social cohesion (Boys and girls, 14-19, refugee and host) -Through IRC's Girl Shine program for adolescent girls, IRC will offer basic skills training. (Girls 14-19, refugee, possibly host)
