Project Summary

The project is in line with HRP 2022 objective 1 and 2 and also with cluster/sector specific objectives 1, 2 & 3, Besides, it aligns with the cluster strategy to ‘Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5’ have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs.
This project will improve access to safe, quality and inclusive protective education services to 5, 284 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host communities’ children in Fangak, Ayod, Maiwut and Nasir Counties by 2022. The integrated education project by ADA in hard-to-reach areas aims to ensure that children (boys and girls) have access to quality and inclusive education and those already in schools are retained in Maiwut and Nasir Counties (Upper Nile State), Fangak and Ayod Counties (Jonglei State). The following interventions are envisaged: establishing safe learning and recreational facilities in 12 locations – 3 in each County; teacher training to enhance the teaching/learning function supported in schools; providing monthly stipends to 84 volunteer teachers - 21 teachers per County; campaign for education, encouraging children, especially girls and people with disability to return to school; and provision of school feeding programs to enhance attendance and retention of children at schools. Response priorities will include gender-segregated WASH facilities with much concentration in COVID-19 prevention measures, inclusion of GBV counselling rooms in the schools, provision of learning, teaching and WASH materials, capacity building of School Management Committees (SMCs) and Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs), life-skills and recreational opportunities to support the recovery of children and to promote their overall well-being. Equal access to inclusive education will be prioritized regardless of gender, disability or ethnic background. Through introduction of school gender-sensitive WASH programme, this project will have established strong WASH facilities in schools. The project will seek to support prevention of COVID-19 through rehabilitation/construction of hand-washing facilities and latrines, provision of soap, face masks, sensitizers and maintenance of social distancing. Carry out messaging on coronavirus signs and symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention to children and their parents.
The project will seek to address one of the top causes of school drop-outs (School feeding) that will help increase and maintain school enrolment and attendance, and reduce risks of death, disease and cognitive underdevelopment. School feeding will also improve protection of- in-school and out of school children by keeping them away from potential recruitment into armed groups, child labor, early marriage, exploitation, sexual violence and abduction while on their way to schools and back home. This will help to reduce morbidity and absenteeism, support retention in schools and children to help sensitize their communities. Crisis-affected children will be engaged in basic education and recreational activities in order to create an enabling environment for their critical development and positive engagement with the community. Youths will catch up on primary education through accelerated learning programmes (ALP) to improve their literacy and numeracy, technical and vocational training, and life skills. The project will seek to include physically and mentally challenged children; girls and boys vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups, and child labour; youths; adolescent heads of households; young mothers; and children from different ethnic groups. Teachers, SMCs and PTAs will be trained on life skills and referral pathways to enhance the protective aspect of education in emergency (EiE) intervention.

1 - Needs

The general humanitarian situation in Upper Nile state remain worse and Nasir County in particular has experienced a seasonal flooding. The recent flooding has caused massive internal displacement of populations with their Livestock in most parts of the county posing a serious risk food insecurity,poor health, nutrition status, destruction of shelter and learning facilities in Nasir County borders Ethiopia through main border point Burebiey, has it border to Longechuck county in the East, Akobo County in the South and Ulang county in the West. Its inhabitants are the Eastern Jikany Nuer section These emergencies ranging from: non-natural conflicts to Natural disasters which some arise yearly with extraordinary intensities that divulge communities to complex risk situation and acerbate struggling of children, women and aged. The local cope up mechanisms have grown-up to be very insignificant due to the fact most of groups are crushed with the aid of the frequency and rampant occurrences of lifestyles threatening situations. Base on the assessment team report it was found that, the school infrastructures are all affected in the area visit Like in Makak, Maker, Balok, Kuetrengke, Koat and Northok villages. No School is functioning in the flood affected areas (Makak, Maker, Nyieny-Gok, Nyariew, Wandow, Dhekdhek, Northok and Dingkhar). Learning materials were destroyed by the flood. Lack of schools feeding program for the children in Functioning schools of Mandeng, Wanding and Nasir payams. These communities most of them were displaced from floods affected areas, however, there were other group who were host communities and assessed areas are their ancestral lands. According to local community leaders, they may have people who can volunteers as Teachers, but they have low capacity. The community Leaders reported that, they are ready to work hard to contribute towards the education of their children if they Establish temporary learning spaces for IDPS, distribute scholastic material to students e.g. exercise books, pens, chalkboards, etc. Provision of Schools feeding at the school to allow children participation to attend classes. Training of Teachers in the code of conduct and teaching pedagogy. Training of PTA and teachers on child protection, Protection Mainstreaming, GBV and PSEA principle and child safeguarding at schools. Fangak County remain vulnerable given the prolong flooding for nearly 5 years with encompasses of recent conflict in New Fangak, Tonga and Pigi Canal the humanitarian need remain high. According to Spot-check assessment on (31st August and 2nd September 2022) and IRNA on (22nd August 2022 reports) indicate the additional an influx of IDPs coming from Tonga, Pigi Counties and New Fangak Payam fleeing hotspot areas where violent escalated rapidly. The Local authority (RRC) and humanitarian partners on the ground estimated the arrival of 9,251 individuals in Old Fangak with commercial boats from New Fangak Payam. Majority of IDPs are currently settled at the Women League center in Old Fangak. Phom/ New Fangak has an estimated total population of over 26,000 individuals according to estimates from RRC Fangak, including population that was displaced from Tonga of Panyikang county in the previous conflict between Agwelek and Kitgwang Forces. Out of this population, 3,873 individuals are believed to have remained in locations of Wuntur, Lele, Tongdiok, Pakan, Kuerkan, Wichmoun, Kuerjiel respectively. This population are receiving humanitarian services from WASH, Health, NFIs and other clusters while education needs remain unmated. As such scale-up Provision of school materials for children of IDPs. Provision of school meals so that children may be motivated to attend school and also prevent malnutrition and stunted growth. Create awareness on GBV and strengthening protection mechanisms to reduce incidences of gender-based violence especially amongst young girls.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA through project front line staff and community volunteers will provide awareness and sensitization in the community on Protection against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA). ADA has staff trained on PSEA that will train and guide the front-line staff while carrying out the activities. A strong referral pathway will be built where the survivors of SEA will be referred to the concerned actors in line with cluster data management policies. ADA Juba headquarter will assist its field offices to mainstream inter-agency complaints mechanisms at community level, strengthen communication with communities, support survivors to access services through referral pathways, and related activities. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) through ensuring its employees are trained and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through awareness raising and also provide information on who to report to and how to report preparators. ADA will map out PSEA partners and establish safe referral pathways to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure zero tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including staff dismissal and taking legal steps against preparators. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee will be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The front-line staff will be reaching out to the beneficiaries who are unable to reach to the ADA field offices or complaint desks as part of their daily outreach activities. This will help those with disabilities and others who cannot easily access the feedback and complaint desk to express their feedback on the services provided by ADA in the community. The outreach staff will fill for them and what they recommend to be done to at least satisfy their basic needs. A good practice that ADA has used in the past is to ensure that people with disabilities, different vulnerabilities, girls, elderly etc. are represented in the monitoring teams and specifically gather info from their own category. Special desks, lines, suggestion boxes that suit their needs are provided. Oftentimes, special people who can translate or have special ways of rapport with the various categories are identified to be representatives in a feedback loop on project matters (including complaints). These methods will be employed to ensure the project feedback mechanism is representative and inclusive of the various categories of beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ADA has a policy on, and extensively uses feedback mechanisms in project implementation. This provides for a complete loop of communication between the field, programs and management. In essence, ADA employs four key steps in providing beneficiary feedbacks: design, implement, collect data and analyse; provide feedback through an agreed feedback loop. In this project: 1. ADA will use available procedures in previous projects to adapt to the feedback needs of the current project, by involving beneficiaries on how best they would like their feedbacks to be recorded, analysed, and handled in a manner that is satisfactory to them. This will be area-specific and handled with utmost confidentiality and practicality. The modalities used by ADA include suggestion boxes, phone calls, use of SMS, face-to-face meetings, focus group discussions, project implementation committee appointees, complaints and complements forms, and questionnaires. Beneficiaries make their choice and this informs the course of action. 2. At the implementation level, ADA will agree with the beneficiaries on how to participate in collecting/gather complaints and complements realistically and in a timely, realistic manner, meeting the needs of beneficiaries in specific locations (Ayod, Fangak, and Nasir) 3. ADA will agree with beneficiaries on how data collected (complaints and compliments) will be analysed, by whom and at what frequency to provide needed feedback. This stage includes concrete action points on matters raised and possible redress or way forward 4. ADA will agree in advance on how the actions generated can be shared internally or externally to create the needed change or course of action that was desired by beneficiaries. At this stage, the communities are informed of necessary changes, by when and how. ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. The action points will provide feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, strengthening the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Positive feedback motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct in beneficiary communities. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold ADA and other decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.
