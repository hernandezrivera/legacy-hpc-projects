Project Summary

The intervention aims to improve the nutrition status and reduce the nutrition related mortality and morbidity rates among the most vulnerable groups of the population, especially children under five and pregnant and lactating women in Kassala State through the provision of timely and comprehensive preventive and curative nutritional services. The project will provide SAM services to 1449 U5 children in 7 OTPs, and 217 with medical complications in SCs; provide MAM services to 4000 children 6 - 59 Months, and 783 Pregnant, Lactating women in 7 OTPs. FPDO will provide MNPs to 10550 for 6-59 months, FBPM for (children 6-23) to 1400, FBPM for (PLWs to 1050). In addition, mother to mother and father to father support groups will be established as well as IYCF breast feeding corners.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition in all its forms is a serious public health challenge in Sudan. It is associated with physical and mental well-being because when nutritional needs are not met it is impossible for an individual to achieve optimal physical and mental health. Children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are the main groups of concern as their nutritional needs are relatively high and deficiencies readily manifest when nutritional needs are not met. Globally, 45 per cent of all child deaths are associated with undernutrition this implies, in the context of Sudan, approximately 40, 000 child deaths annually. In 2020, 15 out of the 18 states had high global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates. Around 95 localities reported extremely high levels of malnutrition. Around three million children under 5 suffer from acute malnutrition out of which an estimated 611,000 have severe acute malnutrition (SAM) (HNO 2022 draft). Additionally, in the case of maternal nutrition approximately 905,004 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) suffer from acute malnutrition annually in Sudan. Scaling up maternal nutrition services remains a concern with approximately 647,623 PLW in of need Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) management services to attain adequate diet. Also, one million mothers need appropriate Infant and Young Child Nutrition (IYCN) counseling services. Based on data available (S3M II 2018) the prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was reported at 10.2% in Kassala states. 43.8% of children less than five years of age are stunted in Kassala states. The national S3M survey 2018, revealed low awareness of appropriate child feeding practices in all states. In Nahr Atbra locality the prevalence of SAM is 1.7 MAM is 8.7 and GAM is 10.1 also the prevalence of stunting is 27.7. These above indicators represent huge gaps in nutrition and a need to strengthen the quality, access and coverage of community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) programming to access nutrition through public health facilities, targeted outreach and management interventions are needed.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

There is some consideration of the environment as it relates to biomedical waste of primary health facilities. Some health facilities have adopted negative practices in disposing waste in the open with potential harm to themselves and the community-at-large. The project is supporting safer methods of disposal through the establishment of proper waste pit disposal. Waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods including Sachet and cartons of RUTF/RUSF. Staff training on proper waste disposal will also be carried out.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

FPDO will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. Do no harm analyses will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened to eliminate risks from all, especially women, girls and children. Staff and volunteers will ensure screening of all children boys and girls during the HH visit and nutrition and health facilities; will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In all stages of the project the communities are kept engaged and involved through community dialogue meetings and project disseminated materials as well as posting notices regarding the availability of nutrition services for beneficiaries in all project locations . Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The existing/ newly community committees and community leaders will be actively engaged at all levels of the project including management, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints handling mechanisms will be established to ensure that complaints are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. These committees are expected to be gender and disability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project.
