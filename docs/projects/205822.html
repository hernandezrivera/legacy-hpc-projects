Project Summary

In 2021, the Education Cluster conducted a Needs Assessment to provide an analysis of the functionality of schools in light of the impact of the 7-year conflict and COVID 19 pandemic. This Education Needs Assessment found out that majority of schools in South Sudan are 1-2 hours walking distance from the nearest County capital. This presents a problem in terms of children accessing schools and thus many children not attending regularly. 40.9% of schools were reported not accessible to children with disabilities (CwDs, 50.4% schools reported that their female learners did not receive any dignity kits and there was lack of functioning referral mechanisms in schools. Reasons for dropping out of schools included domestic duties and long distances to school. The Sudan conflict has exacerbated the situation in Raja County. Since the start of the conflict Raja county has received and registered 4070 returnees; 2500 women, 500 men and 1070 children. Raja has also registered 4648; Sudan refugees; 485 women, 350 men and 3811 children (Source RRC office Wau). Many of these returnees are staying with relatives and friends’ families mainly in semi-urban centers with limited or without pit latrines and water sources. The project shall focus on the three specific objectives;
Objective 1: Enabling emergency-affected children and youth to access age appropriate, safe, protective, and inclusive learning Objective 2: Providing emergency affected girls and boys with minimum quality education for improved learning outcomes and to attain the minimum standard stipulated in the national learning competencies.
Objective 3: Building capacity of government and other partners for effective and accountable coordination in preparedness, timely response, recovery and resilience programming.
Therefore, this proposal seeks to address the challenges facing the Education sector in Raja County, Western Bahr el Gazal. The Project will target 3,001 children in Need (CiN) in Raja County (1,500 female and 1,501 male). Out of 3,001 children targeted, 450 (225 female and 225 male) will be children with disabilities (CwDs) and about 500 of them are returnees. The proposed project will be relevant in the following ways:
By constructing of school infrastructure more classrooms will be available and schools will be able to enroll more children to learn in a protective and safe environment. Community mobilization and Back to Learning campaigns will help change the perception of parents about the importance of education, particularly for girls and result in increased enrolment of children in schools. Training teachers, PTA/SMCs and government officials will build the capacity of teachers and school officials with the knowledge and skills needed for effective teaching and better school management. Provision of school materials will relieve parents of the financial burden to buy school supplies and encourage children to go to school and participate in the learning process. During implementation, EFO will adopt the cluster recommended gender sensitive education response. EFO’s first modality to promote gender equity and ensure project sustainability will be to use the social change approach to identify Girls’ Education Champions using the powerful influence of group identity and human desire to conform to standards of a peer group as a strategy to motivate positive outcomes. The approach will transform existing negative and discriminatory social norms amongst men, boys and other key influencers by creating a perception of gender equality and girls’ education as the 'new norm'. The second modality is to work with school heads, teachers and CED to set up Zero-Tolerance Clubs (ZTCs) in the five schools to support school children and young girls and boys who have already married, providing the chance to complete or continue their education, information on their human rights.

1. Needs

This project seeks to address challenges to school functionality in Raja County. The needs this project aims to address include; physical barriers including long distance to school, lack of teaching and learning materials, inadequate learning spaces and untrained teachers, low capacity of the SMCs/PTA. Children are not attending school regularly because of long distances; teachers lack morale and are frequently absent due to low and delayed salaries. Most of the teachers are untrained, leading to poor delivery and low quality of education. In addition, teachers are not trained on how to teach or support Children with Disabilities (CwDs). Children with Disabilities face challenges during the learning process because schools cannot provide learners with adequate conditions to move and study with convenience, for example, they lack ramps, doors are too heavy to open and wash rooms are inaccessible. Schools also lack assistive or mobility devices to support the learning process for CwDs The Covid-19 pandemic also exacerbated educational inequalities leading to learning loss due to national lock down. This had negative impact particularly on girls who became further engaged in domestic responsibilities and faced greater risks to early marriages. Due to the impact of the Sudan war, WBGeS has received a big number of returnees as well as Sudan refugees. Raja County alone has registered 4070 returnees; 2500 women, 500 men and 1070 children. Raja has also registered 4648; Sudan refugees; 485 women, 350 men and 3811 children (Source RRC office Wau). Many of these returnees are staying with relatives and friends’ families mainly in semi-urban centers with limited or without pit latrines and water sources.

2. Project activities

In order to overcome the challenges facing the communities and children in particular, the following activities will be implemented; 1. Construction of 5 Temporally Learning Spaces: these shall be constructed in areas without schools or with schools under trees. Each TLS will have two classrooms. During construction, the guidelines given by MoGEI on the standard size of a classroom shall be strictly considered. Each classroom shall have a ramp to facilitate accessibility by CwDs.2. Construction of 5 Gender Segregated Latrines (4 stances each): For every newly constructed TLS gender segregated latrines shall be constructed. This is aimed at improving on Sanitation and hygiene of the new school. These Latrines shall also have ramps to facilitate accessibility by CwDs. 3. Enroll children in Pre-primary and Primary schools/learning centers. This shall involve mobilization of parents, learners, SMCs, PTAs through drives, door to door campaigns, radio announcements and holding of drives to attract and encourage parents to send their children to school. 4. Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers; 5. Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials, including sports and games equipment, sports jerseys, musical instruments (balls, javelin, team uniforms, and goal post), for both male and female learners, including children with disabilities. 6. Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD and primary school. These shall include classroom consumables such as; chalk, chalkboard mathematical sets, rulers, mathematical sets, pens, pencils, shading colors, and textbooks. 7. Train 50 teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children 8. Train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition 9. Train 56 SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change 10. Support conducting back to learning campaigns.The success of the project shall be measure using the following indicators; 1. # of temporary learning spaces established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2. # of school children who got access to gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls) 3. # of boys, girls and adolescents (3-17) provided with access to quality education services4. # of learners who received sanitation and hygiene materials 5. # of children and youth (3-17 years) who benefited from recreational material supplies. 6. # of teachers trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies. 7. # of teachers trained and updated on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms 8. # of community education committee members and local education officials trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) 9. # of children, teachers and PTA members provided with appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials and information on prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases. 10. # of school campaigns organized on back to school/learning.
