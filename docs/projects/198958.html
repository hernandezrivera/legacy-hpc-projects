Project Summary

CA will implement a complementary feeding porridge mum approach that brings together women to learn and discuss issues related to improved childcaring and IYCF feeding practices while undertaking cooking demonstrations. Participants will benefit from receiving a nutrition meal as the group con-sume the food prepared during the sessions. Among the beneficiaries, PLWs who are identified as vulnerable, qualify for admission into the porridge mum group with the aim to influence a positive out-come of their nutritional status and that of their children who are less than 59 months. The nutritional status of children is not an admission criterion for participating in porridge mum activities. Children of pregnant and lactating mother less than 59 months of age are all eligible to directly benefit from the approach. Women learn about good nutrition and hygiene practices and adopt the porridge mum nu-tritious recipes as a viable means to treat MAM and prevent children with MAM from becoming se-verely acute malnourished (SAM). The nutritional and immunization status of children and PLWs is checked to track their health and well-being, ensuring adequate referral to health facilities for vaccina-tion, deworming and micronutrient supplementation.
Under the proposed intervention plan, 2 activities are proposed to ensure group cooking demonstra-tion. The first being the crediting of Porridge Mum groups members' wallets with NGN 5,000 cash for household purchase of locally available nutritious food items. The second activity is procurement of fuel wood/charcoal, water, food items and condiments worth NGN 50,000 per group through e-voucher modality monthly. An established process where the treasurer finalizes the list of items with the group and shops in the presence of an elected proxy member ensures transparency and group engagement in the shopping process. Under this activity, 1,500 PLWs and caregivers of children U5 will be credited with NGN 5000 through a cash agent/vendor monthly for 11 consecutive months.
The nutritional status of children and PLWs will be checked by CNMs together with community health workers from UNICEF/SPHCDA, ensuring adequate referral to the health facilities (HFs) for vaccina-tion, deworming and micronutrient supplementation. Pregnant mothers will be referred to the HFs for ANC and institutional delivery services. They will receive counselling on early initiation and exclusive breast-feeding. Health education will focus on IYCF practice, child health, reproductive health, gender and social norms on a monthly basis. CHWs, caregivers district officials will be trained on Mother-MUAC approach component of CMAM and orientated to screen their MUAC and that of their children 6-59 months of age MUAC for malnutrition and early identification and treatment. Community Nutri-tion Mobilizers (CNMs) will screen the children for MUAC on a monthly basis through active case find-ings while CHWs will provide such service at the HFs. The project staff will work with catchment area primary HFs to refer children and PLW for maternal, child health and other nutrition services. All iden-tified SAM cases (with and without complications) will be referred to OTP/SC within the HFs, and where necessary supported with transportation.

1 - Needs

Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.Christian Aid had also conducted a malnutrition assessment in Michika LGA by drawing random of 7,962 (3849M, 4113F) children aged from 6 to 59 months. From sample children whose MUAC measurement collected, 779 (350M, 429F) had a reading between 11.5cm and 12.4cm being the MAM category, while 89 (41M, 48F) had a MUAC reading below 11.5 being a proxy for SAM. There were also five (5) oedema cases identified. Out of the 89 cases of SAM/Oedema identified, 49 were enrolled in the program while 40 were not enrolled, giving an enrolment rate of 55%. The estimated proxy GAM and proxy SAM rates were 1.12% and 10.9% respectively (CAID Mass MUAC Screening Report l June 2022) The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) using weight-for-height z-score (WHZ) among children aged 0-59 months was highest in Borno (12.3%) followed by Yobe (10.6%) and Adamawa (7.2%). By domain, acute malnutrition WHZ for GAM and SAM are 8.6 and 8.1 for Southern Adama-wa (Fufore) and 6.1 and 0.5 for Northern Adamawa (Michika and Madagali) respectively. While for Central Borno (Mafa), acute malnutrition WHZ are 8.6 & 8.1 for GAM and SAM respectively (Sum-mary of the findings of Nutrition and food security surveillance, August 2022) The projected food and nutrition insecurity situations in the BAY states indicates that Nganzai will be in phase 4, while Bayo, Mafa, konduga and Monguo will remain in phase 3 in Borno state. Similarly, Madagali, Michika, Fufore LGAs will remain in phase 3 while Mubi North and Mubi south will remain in phase 2 respectively in Adamawa state. (Results of the October/November 2022 analysis and Identification of areas and populations affected by Food and Nutrition Insecurity in 26 States and the FCT NG-FSS MEETING 24th November 2022)

2 - Activities

The project will use existing government system - PHCs at community level to deliver treatment services to nutritional vulnerable groups identified with uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition SAM through outpatient therapeutic feeding programme. In locations that are hardly accessible due to insecurity and infrastructural deficit, Identified SAM children will receive treatment through the outreach approach. Under the proposed intervention plan, 2 activities are proposed to ensure group cooking demonstration. The first being the crediting of Porridge Mum groups members' wallets with NGN 5,000 cash for household purchase of locally available nutritious food items. The second activity is procurement of fuel wood/charcoal, water, food items and condiments worth NGN 50,000 per group through e-voucher modality monthly. An established process where the treasurer finalizes the list of items with the group and shops in the presence of an elected proxy member ensures transparency and group engagement in the shopping process. Under this activity, PLWs and caregivers of children U5 will be credited with NGN 5000 through a cash agent/vendor monthly for 11 consecutive months. Community based promotion of maternal, infant, and young child feeding - MIYCF will be integrated into the nutrition service package both at community and facility level, to ensure that malnutrition prevention is an integral component of service delivery. Similarly, children 6-23 months will receive micro-nutrient supplementation The SBCC activities will focus on improving behaviors on MIYCF and increased demand for consumption of nutrient dense locally available food and integrated with WaSH and hygiene promotion.
