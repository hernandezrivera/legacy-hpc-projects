Project Summary

EIE project is targeting conflict affected school age children from most vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host communities who have been displaced due to a protracted crisis over the past one year or more years. Compounded by inter-clan, inter-tribal violence and cattle raiding which has left most children out of school. The project will reach up to 15,912 targets of which 15,500 (9,010M, 6,490F) will be primary school and ECD pupils from 3-18 years and 412 teachers and SMCs/PTAs. The project will address three key areas of education in emergency with the following objectives:
• Increase access to safe, protective and inclusive learning environment to crises affected boys and girls (3-18yrs) in IDP centre, Returnees area and Host Communities in Uror, Duk and Twic East counties
• To Improve the quality of Education through better education services for school aged children in Uror, Duk and Twic East counties
• Improve the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate impact of emergencies on school aged children in Uror, Duk and Twic East counties

Needs

The South is faced with countless challenges, some of which arise from historical marginalization, poverty and under-development, while others are driven by more recent events, including the protracted conflict and conflict which began in December 2013 between SPLM_IG and SPLM-IO. Peace Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan was signed in August 2015 and Transitional Government formed in April 2016; however the clash between SPLA-IG and SPLA-IO in Juba in July 2016 has resulted in various consequences throughout the country. Due to the compounding effects of widespread violence and instability, agricultural production reduced tremendously, the macro‐economy is deteriorated, hyperinflation realized (According to South Sudan Bureau of Statistic, the cereal price increase more that 600% since 2017 and still the inflation is not managed) and the shortage of foreign currency has eroded people’s purchasing power, especially that of the most vulnerable, curtailing their ability to access food for consumption through the markets. Jonglei is hosting the second highest IDP numbers, estimated at 378,821 people. Furthermore, the September 2018 IPC classified indicate that 62.5% of the population in Jonglei are in emergency, crisis and catastrophe level. For instance 535,000 people are in IPC 2 - stress, 810,000 are in IPC 3 - crisis, 275,000 are IPC 4 -emergency, 5000 are in IPC 5 catastrophe. Twic East, Bor South and Duk counties felt crisis (IPC 3); Twic East fall will fall under emergency IPC 4 by March 2019. There are an estimated 53,033 IDPs in Twic East and Duk alone in addition to those under the UN Protection of Civilian (POC) in Bor town. Many households in Bor South (Jalle), Duk and Twic East depend on gathering wild foods, borrowing and catching fish as their main source of survival including dependence on humanitarian assistance for their survival. When this situation is not reverted, then its impact on school going age children would be huge.The 2018 figure from Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), indicates that in Duk County; a total of 26033 IDPs individuals mainly women and children (boys and girls), and the elderly i.e. 4,339 households. Most of the IDPs were from Uror, and Ayod Counties of Jonglei State, and from within the deserted Payams of Duk County i.e. the Payuel payam which was attacked by trial raider in November 2018, were over 40 lives were lost. According to the assessment report by interagency in 2017, the influx of the IDPs was majorly due to a clash between the government and opposition forces in Uror County. Other reasons were cattle raiding and hunger. Similarly Due clash between government and Opposition forces in 2017 , Uror county (which was under opposition control from December 2013 to February 2017), divide into two , the western part that included three payams ( Yuai, Padeik, and Wiekol) fall under government and the Eastern parts includes 3 Payams (Pathai, Peri and Motot) are under opposition. Due to the clash the people moved to Duk and Akobo areas. The Primary Net Enrolment rate in South Sudan decreased from 42% in 2013 to 35% in 2017, due to disruption of educational activities in the most crisis affected States. In Duk, Uror and Twic East Counties of Jonglei State, the education needs still remain dire. Of the 26 primary schools operational in Duk County prior to the 2013 conflict, only 15 schools reopened by 2016-18 with 11 schools remain deserted of which 6 schools were destroyed during the conflict. While only two Early Childhood Development centres are operational. In Uror County, of 21 primary schools operational prior to 2013 conflict, only 11 schools are reopened by 2017/2018. There are two Early Childhood Development Centres functional in 2016/17. In 2018 the Education activity in Uror did not showed any change. In Twic East County all 38 primary schools operational since 2017.

Activities

Objective 1: Increase access to safe, protective and inclusive learning environment to crises affected boys and girls (3-18yrs) in IDP centre, Returnees area and Host Communities in Uror, Duk and Twic East counties • Establish four temporary ECD centres in the three counties (connected to primary schools and Church)• Rehabilitate three temporary learning space of 4 classes each in three primary schools • Establish gender segregated 4 Pit latrines with Washing facilities in four ECD centres• Establish gender segregated 5 Pit latrines with Washing facilities in 5 primary schools • Train pupil on sanitation and hygiene• Support 4 ECD Centres with ECD kits that includes teaching and learning educational materials (textbooks, exercise books, pens, pencils, chalks, blackboards etc)• Provision Dignity kits for 1200 school girls (Afripads)• Establish 3 women advocacy groups to create awareness and advocate for the girl children to stay in school• Provision of teaching learning materials (Scholastic materials) for 12 schools Objective 2: To Improve the quality of Education through better education services for school aged children in Uror, Duk and Twic East counties • Support 4 ECD centre pupil psychomotor growth by provision of recreational materials• Training of 92 teachers (12 ECD teachers and 80 primary school teachers) on pedagogy, and life skill, and handling of children with psychological support• Identification and support of 12 incentive ECD teachers with salaries • Support 8 support staff (cleaners and security guards) in five ECD centre with salaries Objective 3: Improve the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate impact of emergencies on school aged children in in Uror, Duk and Twic East counties• Train SMCs/PTAs from 16 Primary Schools and 4 ECDS on School Management and Governance• Train SMCs/PTAs from 16 Schools and 4 ECDS child safe guarding and psychosocial support, GBV, Sanitation and hygiene, and environment • Train all 12 ECD teachers, 80 primary teachers, 8 support security guards on code of conduct• Establish child help desks in all the targeted primary schools to report and refer cases of SGBV and other forms of violence and abuse of children.
