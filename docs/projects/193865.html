Project Summary

The project aims at strengthening the prevention of and response to GBV by enhancing knowledge and awareness of vulnerable groups of women and girls, men and boys in the affected communities on gender and GBV issues and promoting a newly developed referral pathway for East Jerusalem. The project is mainly designed to provide and expand safe and equal access to adequate multisectoral services for East Jerusalem women, girls, men, and boys survivors of GBV, including women with disabilities, through UNFPA partner GBV service providers. It will build on three existing safe spaces and increase high quality GBV case management, referral, and access to GBV services through capacity building of GBV case managers and services. In addition, UNFPA’s implementing partners will promote the GBV referral pathway application (Tamkeen) to targeted beneficiary communities. In 2022, UNFPA established a Jerusalem specific referral system, to provide GBV survivors both inside the Separation Barrier, with full access to GBV multisectoral services. UNFPA will continue to work on this mechanism by developing associated Standard Operating Procedures in addition to strengthening the capacity of the HEMAYA Network partner organizations. In addition, the project will focus on communication and outreach targeting GBV survivors in vulnerable communities within the Separation Barrier, by promoting the referral pathway. Through its awareness and outreach activities, it will ensure that all targeted communities have a better knowledge of available GBV services, and where and when to access services.
Additionally, the project and its interventions will aim to strengthen and empower women and girls in Jerusalem, focusing on girls and women with disabilities who are at elevated risk of GBV, to enable them to live in dignity, safety, and security within their families and communities.
The project will target the GBV survivors as well as the providers to match the scaling up of emergency GBV services with building the capacity of non-civil society government actors to provide quality, survivor-centered GBV services. Through the enhancement of new approaches, technologies, and platforms, the project will continue to use the multisectoral approach in providing GBV services, building on past UNFPA achievements. The project will target women and girls GBV survivors (and women and girls at risk of GBV) in Jerusalem (Old City and Isawyeh, Abu Dis, Anata and Sufat, Al Tur, Silwan, and Kufr Aqab suburb.)
In line with the HRP 2023 protection indicators, the project has two main outcomes:
1. Strengthen and empower the women and girls of Jerusalem, especially the most vulnerable and those exposed to gender-based violence, to enable them to live with dignity and peace within their families and communities.
2. Enhance the knowledge and awareness of vulnerable groups of women and girls, men and boys in the affected communities on gender and GBV issues.

1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains in a protracted protection crisis, characterized by more than 54 years of Israeli military occupation, a lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities. Palestinians continue to face a number of protection threats that hamper access to livelihoods and essential services and result in continued erosion of resilience and preparedness. In 2022, millions of Palestinians were estimated as highly vulnerable to humanitarian shocks, as a result of multiple long-lasting political and socio-economic factors from previous years. In the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, Israeli settlers’ violence, destruction of property, movement restrictions, as well as restricted access to basic services and livelihoods persist, which negatively leads to a coercive environment denying many Palestinians their basic rights. As in most crises, the COVID-19 pandemic has disrupted access to critical sexual and reproductive health services and hampered authorities’ ability to respond to gender-based violence, at a time when women and girls need these services the most. The pandemic had the greatest impact on the most vulnerable groups, including women and girls, the elderly, persons with disabilities, cancer patients in Gaza, Bedouin communities in Area C, households isolated by the West Bank Barrier, and undocumented residents of East Jerusalem.Palestinian women and adolescent girls are entitled to the protection of their rights, safety, and dignity. Yet they pay a heavy price in the humanitarian crisis and face exacerbated pre-existing risks of gender-based violence (GBV), including early/forced marriage, intimate partner and family violence, sexual harassment and sexual violence including rape and incest, denial of resources, psychological violence, and physical abuse. In 2022, gender-based violence continues to be of great protection and health concern in the oPt. Based on the GBV SC SDR 2022, a number of factors contributed to and in some cases increased the risk of GBV for women and girls. These factors include Israeli occupation and conflict-related violence, the May escalation, prevailing traditional cultural norms in Palestinian society, an increase in economic and social deterioration, and the COVID-19 pandemic. Women and girls face a number of risks, including discrimination and various forms of GBV such as early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, psychological abuse, and sexual exploitation and abuse. 31 percent of households in the West Bank including East Jerusalem and 19 percent in Gaza are concerned about the safety and security of girls, while 24 percent and 17 percent in the two areas respectively are concerned about the safety and security of women. According to HNO 2022, the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection crisis, where too many Palestinians struggle to meet their most basic needs and live in dignity. The crisis is characterized by more than 54 years of Israeli military occupation, a lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent Israeli escalations in Gaza. COVID-19 has exacerbated the crises in the past two years. Rates of gender-based violence (GBV) are significant according to the 2019 Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistic (PCBS) survey on violence, which was updated in July 2022, 59.3 percent of (married or previously married) women between the ages of 15 and 64 experienced violence in the 12 months preceding the survey – 70.4 percent in Gaza and 52.3.3 percent in the West Bank. Of those, 57.2 percent experienced psychological violence, 18.5 percent experienced physical violence, 9.4 percent experienced sexual violence, and 9.6 percent experienced the emerging issue of cyber violence. The recurring and frequently increased risk of violence, and military conflict exacerbates pre-existing risks of GBV, including early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Lack of accessible services in some parts of East Jerusalem due to the absence of the Palestinian national authority providing services remains one of the challenges faced by women in Jerusalem especially those who hold a West Bank ID and live in Jerusalem who often lack accessible services due to their legal status. The occupation is therefore a real obstacle to the Palestinian National Authorities due diligence obligation to provide services to GBV survivors in areas where it does not have full jurisdiction. Working to protect women and girls in areas under full Israeli control is very difficult for civil society organizations and community organizations and even more difficult for the Palestinian National Authority, especially in relation to law enforcement. The Ministry of Social Development is not able to operationalize its protection policies, procedures and service delivery in the vulnerable areas of East Jerusalem under the implementation of the National Referral System. Moreover, a lack of acceptable services has also been identified especially for women who are exposed to violence in Jerusalem awho often refuse to receive services under Israeli law due to the social stigma these women face. Most importantly, the SDR highlighted the 2022 key priorities which include: Harmonizeand unify women protection legislations; Reform and strengthen legal protection to women and girls; Introduce laws that criminalize domestic violence, sexual harassment, and marital rape; Reform the male guardianship laws, Address political factors that hinder implementation and enforcement of women protection; and Mainstream GBV as a crosscutting priority in the oPt humanitarian response. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all partners of GBV service providers know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. UNFPA IPs service providers and GBV SC members received in September 2022 a case management session with UNFPA consultant about the global standards of case management, to protect the identity, personal information, and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honor. Additionally, UNFPA targets men and boys in most of its interventions to balance the male-to-female dynamics in the family and community, and to solve any risky challenges through the male counseling units and the couple therapy. Also, unrestricted cash assistance in case management is basically designed to contribute to the safety, dignity, and resilience of women and girls in humanitarian contexts as it offers discretion and flexibility compared to in-kind assistance. Additionally, UNFPA conducted an unrestricted cash assistance risk assessment in October 2022 to get feedback from beneficiaries and case managers about the potential risks linked to unrestricted cash assistance and the responsive mechanisms that are adopted by women in different communities, and how to mitigate the negative mechanisms such as early marriage, forced marriage, selling their assets and jewelry, and beggary from the community. On the one hand, UNFPA IPs case managers received two induction sessions from UNFPA international humanitarian team about unrestricted cash assistance in case management and risk mitigation. UNFPA conducted different capacity-building training sessions for GBV SC and other clusters’ members on PSEA and established safe and confidential pathways such as the SAWA hotline that covers Gaza and West Bank, where all women and girls and other target groups are advised to use in any suspected PSEA case.
