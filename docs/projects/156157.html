Project Summary

Fondation Caritas and its implementing partner SARD are committed to assist affected people most in need in North West Syria, despite the unpredictability and the risks of working in areas controlled by radical opposition armed groups,
The action with a budget of 2,659,776 € will address the needs of more than 20,320 people over 12 months, addressing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance.
The multi-sectoral assessment conducted at the end of 2018 cuts across the Food, Non-Food Items, shelter, education and livelihood sectors, 465 households were interviewed.. Respondents included internally displaced persons, returnees as well as host households. Most vulnerable families, as previously identified and assisted by SARD were consulted, as well as households in non-assisted communities.
The findings of the assessments show that food and NFI/hygiene items ranked as households’ main priorities for the next 3 to 6 months. 99% of households ranked food as their main priority. With 98% of the households unable or barely able to meet their current food and NFI needs, emergency relief remains a crucial and life-saving need.. Despite 67% relying on food assistance, households spend large portions of their revenue on food expenses often forcing many to resort to harmful coping strategies. Assistance should be particularly attentive to geographical variations as some communities tend to be more vulnerable than others.
Multi-sector assistance will range from emergency food support to restoring food production livelihood (described in the present document). It will also include provision of necessary non food items, shelter intervention (described in the SNFI document).
FCL and SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have improved access to life-saving assistance through an effective and efficient response mechanism (target of minimum 10,000 individuals) that include provision of emergency food through the delivery of cooked meals (CMs) with bread for one month in reception centers and communities, in close coordination with FSL, CCCM and reception centres (RC) managers.
In addition to the life-saving first line emergency assistance, FCL and its partner SARD are committed to also provide life-sustaining food support through life-sustaining multi-purpose assistance
The action provides up to 8-months multi-sector assistance with food for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs and host communities. Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism.
Identification is based on a set of eligibility criteria such as household vulnerability, shelter conditions, possession of productive assets and food security level (livelihood coping strategies).
FCL and SARD will mainstream protection principles throughout the action, ensuring that assistance is provided safely in secured locations, responding to people's needs, and reinforcing their dignity.

Needs

The comprehensive needs assessment conducted by FCL and SARD in December 2018 which is in line with Humanitarian Needs Overview (OCHA 2019), as well as the analysis of the continuous assessments of needs done by field teams during the implementation of the 2018's projects. The needs assessment conducted by the M&E department in December 2018, targeted 465 households in Idleb and Aleppo governorates (Daret Azza, Azaz, Atareb, Teftnaz, Qourqeena, Ariha sub-districts). Respondents included internally displaced persons, returnees as well as host households. The assessment targeted both beneficiaries (e.g. most vulnerable households assisted in 2018) and non-beneficiary households.The findings of the assessments show that food and NFI/hygiene items ranked as households’ main priorities for the next 3 to 6 months with 99% of households ranking food as their main priority and 98% of the households are unable or barely able to meet their current food and NFI needs.Assessed governorates, as per the HNO 2019 and Whole of Syria Food Security and Livelihood Assessment, total over 2 million people in need of food assistance, with 27% and 39% of the population being food insecure in Idleb and Aleppo governorate respectively. IDPs and returnees, in particular, are more at risk of food insecurity. In addition, the HNO 2019 estimates that 478,283 people currently living in last resort camps in the North West and North East Syria have very limited opportunity to access a diversified diet, thus further increasing their food insecurity level.Corroborating with the HNO 2019 findings, the needs assessment showed that food expenditures represent about 64% of households’ budget and 51% of their total expenditures. Consumption levels tend to be stable, as reported by 49% of the households, although 27% declared having reduced their portion in the last month. Lack of financial resources remains households’ main constraint in accessing food. The findings of livelihoods’ coping strategies show that a large proportion of household’s resort to harmful coping strategies to survive and to meet their daily food needs:• 86% of the HHs, for instance, reported borrowing, sharing or purchasing food on credit, corroborating information on households’ debt level,• 43% declared having sold household assets,• 18% declared having sold productive assets or means of transport,• Almost 62% reported reducing their expenditures on non-food essential items to buy food.Shortages of employment opportunities, lack of sustainable livelihood activities deeply affect households’ ability to support their needs. Thus, households’ resort to harmful coping strategies that further deepen their vulnerability. Livelihood assistance is essential in supporting households to overcome poverty and dependence on external assistance while providing dignified means to sustain their basic needs.In May 2019, hostilities increased in Northern Hama southern Idleb, resulting in the displacement of 397,437 individuals moved to different community in NW of Syria between 1 May and 15 June 2019, increasing drastically the needs to the already pressing situation. To better understand the situation and address the needs , FCL-SARD conducted a Rapid Need assessment (RNA) in May 2019 on the newly arrived IDPs. As a result, Food was considered as the top lifesaving priority for the 66% newly displaced families, with 80% of the families reported difficulties to secure their food, while 20% are totally unable to secure their food needs. In 75% of the cases, people reported decreasing the food consumption in order to cope with the current situation, while only 21% of the people would have access to the same quantity of food. The lack of financial resources, high cost of the market price and the difficulties to reach the market were the 3 top challenges families are facing for accessing food.

Implementation of activities

To address the multi-sectoral needs described above, FCL and its implementing partner SARD are committed to ensuring that the critical, multi-sectoral needs of the most vulnerable households are met and their resilience strengthened through both life-saving and life sustaining multi-purpose assistance. Multi-sector assistance will range from emergency food support to restoring food production livelihood (described in the present document). It will also include provision of necessary non-food items, shelter intervention (described in the SNFI document). FCL and SARD will ensure that newly-displaced families have improved access to life-saving assistance through an effective and eﬃcient response mechanism (target of minimum 55,000 individuals) that includes provision of emergency food through the delivery of cooked meals (CMs) with bread for one month in reception centres and communities, in close coordination with FSL, CCCM and reception centres (RC) managers. For the emergency food distribution, following activities will take place : • Identiﬁcation and targeting of newly arrived IDPs • Registration and distributions in close coordination with stakeholders (FSL + CCCM + Camp Managers, relevant local authorities) • Monitoring and post monitoring of the emergency support • Close coordination with relevant clusters (FSL+CCCM ) including 4Ws matrix sharing In addition to the life-saving ﬁrst line emergency assistance, FCL and its partner SARD are committed to also provide life-sustaining food support through life-sustaining multi-purpose assistance The action provides 8-months multi-sector assistance with food and NFI for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs and host communities. Vulnerability is identiﬁed and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism. Identiﬁcation is based on a set of eligibility criteria such as household vulnerability, shelter conditions, possession of productive assets and food security level (livelihood coping strategies). The following activities will take place : • Assessment on the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable to determine the modality of the response (cash, voucher or in-kind); • Family needs assessment conﬁrms needs of each household. • Multi-purpose assistance includes food voucher, hygiene and NFI kits (bedding unit, kitchen set and clothes set) and is provided according to the identified needs of the familiy. Among the MVF , FCL-SARD team will identify families eligible for small-scale food production opportunities (gardening), in order to offer them a sustainable exit strategy from food assistance. • The primary assessment is done at the household level, during the multipurpose assistance activities to assess feasibility and family’s capacity. Some family could be potentially referred to the livelihood’s activities. • The selection will be based on a set of eligibility criteria that will include an assessment of the family’s capacity and access to land (using the HLP SNFI cluster guideline) • SARD team will then establish a Bill of Quantities for a tailor-made package for each case, making sure to address small scale food production opportunities, with an emphasis to small vegetable garden, chickens and other light livestock • Based on the BoQ, the value of the grant will be agreed with the family • Distribution of cash grants • After the action, review and satisfaction survey are conducted by the M&E team to verify implementation and measure the impact of the action.

Protection Risk Analysis

FCL-SARD is taking proactive steps to minimise any negative impact of its intervention on targeted communities as well as on its own staff. All activities are implemented with a protection lens, ensuring that safety, dignity and meaningful access are provided in all activities. All of SARD Turkey and Syria teams have been trained on protection principles. In a bid to mitigate any harmful effects, SARD has put in place a strong complaint response mechanism (CRM) regularly advertised to local communities and offering multiple channels for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike to raise any concerns or feedback. CRM include the availability of a focal point in SARD’s local office and at the desk office in Turkey, face to face field visits and WhatsApp. Regular field visits are also conducted by programme teams and M&E departments. 1. Intimidation and exploitation: Risk: Medium; Impact: HighMitigation measures: All of SARD’s staff has been trained on protection principles, and are requested (as per their contract) to follow SARD’s protection policies. Trainings include awareness about disciplinary procedures. At the community-level, Memorandums of Understanding signed with local authorities include a section on sanctions related to the intimidation and exploitation of SARD’s beneficiaries. SARD has put in place a strong complaint response mechanism (CRM) regularly advertised to local communities and offering multiple channels (telephone and WhatsApp complaint hotline, CRM desk in SARD’s office and face-to-face field visits) for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike to raise any issues or concerns over intimidation or exploitation from SARD staff, local authorities or any other stakeholders. 2. Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV): Risk: Medium; Impact: HighMitigation measures: SARD’s CRM manual offers clear instructions on how to handle any sensitivecase, such as SGBV ones, including how to keep details of the case confidential and when/how to refer the case to a protection actor (if agreed by the SGBV survivor). Finally, all staff has been trained on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). SARD is currently revising its PSEA policy and is in the midst of appointing PSEAsafeguarding focal points at Turkey and Syria levels. 3. Child protection risks: Risk: Low; Impact: HighMitigation measures: Apart for its education activities, SARD does not implement activities with children under 16. In rare cases, where the household is headed by a child (under 16), SARD’s staff takes precautionary measures such as offering door-to-door distribution, having at least one female staff present (especially in case of female minor heads). All staff have been trained on Child Protection and safeguarding. SARD is currently revising its child protection policy and is in the midst of appointing child protectionsafeguarding focal points at Turkey and Syria levels. 4. Inflation of staple items: Risk: Low; Impact: LowMitigation measures: Should SARD intervention negatively impact prices on the local market, the programme team is ready to adjust activities and modalities accordingly. SARD often does market surveys. 5. Food related protection risks associated with cooked meals/Food poisoning: Risk: Low; Impact: HighMitigation measures: In reception centers (emergency response) and schools, cooked meals are distributed in a separate refectory. Households are not allowed to bring food back to their accommodation to avoid contamination. In addition, SARD kitchen teams constantly monitor the quality of their food, ensuring that only fresh food of good quality is procured. Kitchen staff respects basic hygiene practices (e.g. wearing mesh cap, washing hands, etc.). Hygiene conditions of the mobile kitchen and of the kitchen buildings apply minimum safety and hygiene standards.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

FCL partner SARD is a member of several safety and security platforms (INSO, HAT and others). The daily internal security reports from the ﬁeld security management committee, about the situation along the border and relevant trend-analysis, are shared. However, the security conditions allow daily access to affected communities and population for assessments, distributions and monitoring. Access to settlements and communities for the staff as well as direct monitoring of distributions and post distributions is facilitated and ensured by IDP and community leaders and local authorities. Food, NFI and shelter kits materials are available on the local market for the ﬁeld kitchen, food vouchers, NFI and Shelter intervention. Armed factions do not get involved in decision-making processes for human resources, logistics procurement or beneﬁciary selection and distributions. Risk of robbery on FB , NFI and shelter kits materials shipments from Turkey and inside Syria remains low; all shipments are coordinated with border authorities in both Turkish and Syrian sides. Coordination with other actors for FB and NFI distributions. Cross-border operations and transactions for money, goods and staff from Turkey to Syria remain possible and staff crossing is not restrained. Staff care policy allows to train staff on safety and security, on evacuation, and staff is funded (by all back-donors) in case of evacuation or stand-by of activities. In case the security situation deteriorates and the access to sites or settlements is not possible, SARD may suspend activities or move activities closer to the border (set up a kitchen production unit close to the border for the production of 5,000 meals per day). SARD has a minimum of 2 weeks stock to cover the kitchen production in case of market disruption. In case of continuous market disruptions, imports from Turkey will be made for dry food, and NFIs. In case of a large inﬂux, SARD will increase response capacity and will use its contingency stock to: Increase the kitchen production of its CM production to up to 16,000 food rations daily; set up a mobile kitchen within communities for the production of 3,000 meals per day and set up a new kitchen on the border area with a capacity of 5,000 to 10,000 CMs a day; provide FBs from stock to 2,500 families; increase food voucher capacity from 1,000 to 2,000 families; use NFI stock. In the event that speciﬁc community leaders prevent SARD staff from conducting direct distribution and monitoring, SARD will suspend food and NFI deliveries to the speciﬁc communities until full access and control is guaranteed. To avoid any interference in the local purchasing process, SARD may, as far as possible, switch to voucher mechanisms in independent local shops or SARD may favour the import of products from Turkey.
