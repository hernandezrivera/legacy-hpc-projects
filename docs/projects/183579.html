Project Summary

This project aims to address the urgent needs of Limb Reconstruction patients in Gaza, especially those who suffered from complex LR injuries as a result of the May 2021 assault on Gaza, but also those who are still under ongoing treatment at the LR unit at Naser hospital.
This project is in rhythm with Strategic Objective #2 and Health Cluster Objective #1.
The complex limb injuries has placed high demands of the Ministry of Health to deal with, as these cases require years of treatment and are financially overwhelming. Plus, the skills' set available in Gaza are only able to meet a tiny proportion of these numbers and complexity of injuries.
This project will aim to reduce the suffering of the LR patients via bringing medical missions from the UK (with flexibility in time related to COVID-19 restrictions), tele-medicine support, HIS support, and also via the supporting the local teams in Naser with the needed supplies to perform the LR interventions locally. The intervention is coordinated with other key stakeholders serving LR in Gaza such as MoH, WHO, MSF-F, MSF-B, MDM Spain and others.

1 - Needs

This project is in rhythm with Strategic Objective #2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.” and Health Cluster Objective #1 “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”.The nature of the protracted emergency in Gaza forces complex limb injuries on the patients. This was evident in all the assaults that were waged against Gaza with the most recent one being in May 2021 in which it was anticipated that almost 10% of the injuries from the assault would need Limb Reconstruction (LR) services according to Dr. Mahmoud Matar, the head of the LR unit. This amounts to almost 220 LR injuries. The HNO for 2022, with the efforts of all partners, including MAP’s rounded the number of the patients in need for 2022 to around 400. This is due to the fact that there are still patients being treated for LR at the MoH facilities and other partners’ and the fact that internal conflicts, RTAs and other forms of injuries in Gaza produce LR injuries. On 28 September and 6 October, WHO held two meetings for LR stakeholders (MAP, IDEALS, MoH, WHO, MDM Spain, PUI, MSF-F and MSF-B) to discuss the current gaps in service. All the partners agreed on: lack of specialized HR, lack of supplies in terms of implants, fixators, machinery, drugs and disposables, lack of HIS coordination, and the need for external support in terms of tele-medicine and missions. This all has to be added up to the drastic financial and timely needs of LR patients as for example, WHO indicated that 81% of the current admitted patients at Naser are unemployed which means that they will not have the financial support to obtain such treatment outside MoH facilities if it existed in the first place. All the above was faced by a new reality that is the COVID-19 pandemic. Currently, the MoH has started mixing LR patients with “normal” ortho patients in the LR unit at Naser just to be able to face the surge of COVID-19 cases and the lack of space which emits danger of the LR unit’s patients. As for the number of new patients added to the HIS system that MAP manages, please note that in the second quarter of 2022, around 41 new cases were added to the database which gives an approx., number of 160 new cases of LR patients per year (this is before the May assault). It must also be mentioned that the treatment journey for patients is long, WHO’s data of the LR unit indicates that only 2% of the LR patients at the Naser unit were completely recovered while, 94% are still under treatment and partially recovered (OCT, 2021). MAP’s database echoes this number with only 27 patients fully recovered vs. 304 patients being treated since start of 2021. All the reasons mentioned previously are strong evidences of why MAP and all other stakeholders must continue supporting the LR patients in Gaza to try and enhance the patient safety and prevent long-term disabilities. MAP coordinates its efforts with all relevant stakeholders, and it should be mentioned that currently, amid the suspension of missions, MAP coordinates for a biweekly online meeting between MoH, IDEALS, MSF-Belgium, MSF-France (with its branches in Jordan and Iraq too), and WHO to discuss clinical cases and provide tele-medicine support to local teams and organizations dealing with LR injuries in Gaza. Our experts indicated at these tele medicine meetings that there is a great need to sustain clinical and surgical services for patients with LR to avoid imminent complications as amputations, osteomyelitis and eventually disability. This comes in line with the pandemic which already delays service provision for patients with LR since the suffering of LR patients is regarded now as a compounded humanitarian suffering. New activities for the LR project will include providing local training courses and providing online learning methods for ortho residents in Gaza related to LR in specific. For missions, MAP and its volunteers are now flexible and will be able to accommodate missions that would range from the normal 1-week duration to the COVID-19 restriction forced 2 weeks missions. Support towards procuring more items to enable the local team to conduct this work will also be continued. In addition, the aforementioned biweekly online meetings will help setup treatment plans for the local team to enable them better. GAM: MAP provides the LR treatment to both male and female patients and all ages according to their specific needs with no discrimination. MAP also is very active on CHS and is part of the alliance globally. Having said all of the above, it must be said that the needs are still greater than all the stakeholders’ capacities combined, and that donors support is key to addressing the needs of LR patients in Gaza.

2 - Activities

Overall, the activities of this project will target 160 unique patients. Medical expats in addition to the local team will deliver approx. 650 consultations through the outpatient clinics and will conduct 145 complex limb reconstructive surgeries. Furthermore, the educational component of the project will help build the capacity of 50 healthcare workers from across the LR system of surgeons, nurses and physios. The activities are summarized as below:- Conduct 6-8 medical missions (this depends on the quarantine situation in Gaza and worldwide but 6-8 are expected), - Procure the needed LR items and donate these to LR units at Naser - enhance the emergency preparedness of the LR services via the prepositioning and stocking of special ortho items needed during emergencies (such as external fixators) at the MAP stores to be able to address the needs during any emergency rapidly - Conduct medical MDT 6-8 symposiums for 30 attendees of ortho/plastics related health care providers - Provide local trainings for 14 ward and theatre nurses and 6 physios staff at Naser unit (after coordination with WHO and MoH and the relevant staff at the LR unit) - Provide online learning platforms for the orthopaedic residents in Gaza (25 residents) - MAP to follow up with the cases using EQ-5D-5L assessment form - Maintain the project’s database - Conduct bi-weekly online clinical discussions with MoH and other relevant partners to address 1-2 cases every meeting. 2*2*12 = 48 cases,. All the activities of the LR intervention have already been shared with the relevant stakeholders: MoH, WHO, MSF-F, MSF-B, MDM Spain, HI and HC. MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. But MAP’s core MDT team that has been trained by our partner expats includes male and female physios that help in the examination of female patients and ensure their dignity. In addition, the visiting team have a reasonable balance of male and female practitioners which takes into consideration cultural differences and social variances. At least every quarter, a pediatrician LR specialist examines the children’s cases. As for protection, please note that protection of data and beneficiaries has been one of the founding pillars to the LR services in Gaza. Extra care is taken into consideration treating patients’ data, soft copies are stored in an online safe place controlled by our London IT manager with access limited to few MAP staff only, hard copies are secured in metal cabinets with keys made to only two staff members, data is shared online without any unique identifiers such as ID or names but via unique codes (we are currently drafting an agreement with other stakeholders such as MSF-F to unify these codes), no data is taken from patients unless they provide full written consent and patients can even ask MAP to alter their names and not to publish photos. Needless to mention that the project ensures the full inclusion of people with disability especially that most of the limb reconstruction patients suffer one form of disability. MAP, with its’ partners, ensure that the LR services are accessible and the place for consultation is adapted for PWDs. Accountability:In terms of accountability to beneficiaries, please note that MAP has a dedicated team combined of in-country and at headquarter staff dedicated to Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism and Accountability. We hold weekly meetings and discuss the BFM in all the projects implemented with community partners and the MoH. We have recently held meetings with MoH staff and agreed that it would be very important to have BFM tools in projects where we have direct interaction with beneficiaries such as the LR project where our programme officer and program assistant visit the OPC at Naser weekly and interact with patients face to face. The tools that we agreed on are a mix of WhatsApp number, FGDs, handing mini leaflets with data about BFM and giving the beneficiaries a mini scoring tool to measure satisfaction of services provided. The tools were discussed and chosen carefully to make sure that we reach as many people as possible regardless of the literacy level and also to make sure that there are secure ways of reporting without feeling exposed. MAP has also a page on our website dedicated to feedback and complaints in both Arabic and English with a generic e-mail to use if needed. The algorithm at the organisation has a triage system that makes sure that complaints are all handled in complete confidentiality but also ensuring that serious complaints would reach the highest levels of management in the organisation to examine and investigate.As for the MoH, as a partner and beneficiary, we make sure that they are involved with us in all aspects of the project from the design phase, during the implementation and after conclusion of the project. We also make sure that we inform them of the donors (if any) and how to provide feedback to us or our donors at the start of each project.

MAP is present weekly in the LR unit to collect patients’ data and this data is used to update medical records but also attain health outcome scores for patients via the EQ-5D-5L which informs the project team of the progress in health of patients but also informs the medical teams of any alarming conditions that they should be aware of. To add, MAP has a dedicated LR database, the only one with LR patients’ data since 2015, and this database is key to establishing the best M&E tools and baselines for the data to be collected. The data MAP collects include patients’ data segregated by gender and age, mission data, numbers of surgeries by local teams, surgeries by missions, MDT calls conducted, cases discussed, attendees in all events, etc. As stated, the data is used to create logframes, M&E plans and implantation plans. To add, MAP collects success stories, and will be conducting 3 FGDs with patients one at the start of the project to influence the early design, middle of project to check on the status of the project and the patient’s satisfaction, and the last one at the end of the project to evaluate the project as part of the internal review that MAP does after each project is finished. We also create field visit reports that include action points and notes, and this informs the quarterly reports that also hold financial data about the project. As for symposiums and trainings the MAP does, we make sure that we have pre and post tests to measure the knowledge differences and we collect a mini satisfaction survey at the end of the session which informs MAP about any adjustments that need to me made about the training like language, materials, venue, and even food!. All the documents created are in accordance to the PMEAL strategy of MAP and are shared with the program manager in the UK who is MAP’s expert in M&E. As for BFM, more will be added under the relevant section in the concept note.

10.1 - Do No Harm

Gender: MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. But MAP’s core MDT team that has been trained by our partner expats includes men and women physios that help in the examination of female patients and ensure their dignity. In addition, the visiting team have a reasonable balance of male and female practitioners which takes into consideration cultural differences and social variances. At least every quarter, a pediatrician LR specialist examines the children’s cases. Data protection: MAP is committed to GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), this means that all patients’ data saved by MAP are encrypted and restricted. Not all MAP staff have access to the data. Also, MAP is utilizing a specific consent form for every patient and avoid any photos showing the patient face at all to provide them with some protection if these materials were to be used online or publicly. MAP also is very active on CHS and is part of the alliance globally. The provision of the surgical items will make sure that availability and accessibility are guaranteed for the targeted population. In addition, we make sure that the quality of the items procured is top notch as we only procure the best ortho items for LR and this is evident from the testimonies of the MoH and the missions we bring. As for acceptability, MAP always seeks feedback from the beneficiaries and the partner and will make sure that the intervention is tailored to the need and fills a critical gap. MAP will also make sure to coordinate the response with the MoH, WHO, MSF F & B, MDm Spain, IDEALS, health cluster, other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication of services which in a way is considered as a harm being brought on the health system in general and the beneficiaries in specific.Safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults. MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS). They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in. MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. MAP is an independent organisation and is not affected by commercial powers. MAP is humanitarian and provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. We help MoH which in treats all vulnerable groups regardless of their cause of injury and this important to mention as some organizations only focus on war related injuries while we treat all LR injuries that are complex regardless of the cause of the injury. In addition, the BFM in place will make sure that any breaches to the above principles are to be addressed immediately by the organisation in order to avoid any partial and un-equal access to the services.
