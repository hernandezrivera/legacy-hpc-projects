Project Summary


H4BF aims at improving safe and dignified access to quality Child Protection and MHPSS services addressing the needs of displaced and vulnerable children and host community girls, boys, men and women. The project will be implemented in within 19 host communities in Donga Mantung, Mezam in the North West and 6 host communities in Mifi and Noun in the west region.
The project is in response to clear and evident deterioration of Cameroon complex humanitarian crisis. The intervention will protect 6,000 girls and boys, aged 1 to 18, in Donga Mantung, Mezam in the North West region of Cameroon, from violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect through prevention interventions and referral support. This will be achieved through 1) creation of child-friendly space for which children can attend and grow without fear, and 2) establishment of community-based child protection mechanism, in which community members will be capacitated and aware of child protection concerns and work to protect their own children. It will include provision of individual Case Management services and provision of structured MHPSS tailored to the needs and interests of girls, boys and adolescents. Additionally, recognizing the importance of nurturing family environments, H4BF will be providing family support and strengthening services and PM+ counselling for female and male caregivers. The community-based protective environments will be supported through integrated community outreach messaging, incorporating Child Protection, Health, WASH and Nutrition. The barriers, which prevent adolescent girls, boys and children with different abilities from accessing the services, will be assessed through community-based FGDs and acted upon accordingly. Moreover, recognizing the importance of peaceful co-existence,
H4BF will capacitate and empower adolescents and young men and women of Donga Mantung and Mezam and host communities. In peaceful conflict resolution and implementation of youth-led initiatives for a peaceful co-existence. H4BF intervention is guided by the four-humanitarian principle, humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. At H4BF gender-based issues are considered in the design of policy, strategy, plan or program and gender equality is promoted in its intervention. GBV caseworkers, supervisors and interpreters whom will be 80% female will be on GBV Case Management based on the Case Management Guidelines. The project build confidence, ensure proper confidentiality and safety in all the steps involve, H4BF will set-up/accommodate a safe and confidential space for service provision that women and girls can access without stigma or fear of being identified. With the project, the survivor is at the center of every decision making and has a right to tell us what he/she want, may accept or reject our proposals.
H4BF will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures during crisis, natural disasters, including measures to prevent children family separations as well as the activation of LCCMPs. Implementation will strickly follow COVID-19 safe practices measures. H4BF will carryout sensitization on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, ensure the strict implementation of Do No Harm Principle and child safeguarding.
H4BF already has well-established internal mechanism to prevent and respond to PSEA and child safeguarding through local and international PSEA focal points and safe and confidential reporting channels.
H4BF will also continue its ongoing inter-agency representation at and contributions to the Cameroon CP sub cluster and GBV case management task force. H4BF is an active participant of the GBV and CP Sector Cluster and project activities will be reported monthly with use of a 5Ws and refers to the dashboard of partner presence. Case management data management will adopt the GBVIMS tools for easy universal data collection, storage, analysis and share within concern clusters. H4BF is an active member of GBV referral pathways.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Three years of violence and instability in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon have left more than 855,000 children out of school according to UNICEF. Within the 3rd year of crisis in the NW/SW, following their forced displacement, over 3 million people are affected in NWSW, among these children, almost 150,000 have been displaced from their homes, which increases their vulnerability and trauma. The tension between the host community and displaced persons is increasing as a result of scarce resources. The key drivers of tension also have a negative impact on intra-community relations leading community members to resort to violent conflict resolution measures. Both Displaced persons and host community manifest deeply rooted socio-cultural norms and traditional practices that disproportionately discriminate against women, girls and marginalized community members preventing their access to services and resources. Those discriminatory norms and practices promote unequal power relationship, which prevent women and girls from actively contributing to the wellbeing of their families and communities. Harmful traditional practices such as early marriage and a commonly accepted social norm of domestic violence are on the rise in both communities due to the lack of livelihood opportunities, overstretched resources and support systems being further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Men are power holders in key decision making at the household level in both communities and both boys and girls are particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to a strong stigma attached to disclosing abusive experiences and a lack of tailored services. Those structural factors have negative implications on the safety and wellbeing of children, adolescents and young people as the violent measures of conflict resolution are further perpetuated at the household level. Displaced Adolescents and youths and host communities are particularly vulnerable groups having limited educational and livelihood opportunities. Displaced female adolescents, due to numerous threats to their safety and the need to negotiate intra-household decision making channels for accessing services, often prefer staying indoors, participating in very few interactions outside their shelters. Although displaced male adolescents report being freer to move in and out of their shelters, they report high levels of risk of exploitation (including trafficking, kidnapping, going missing), physical abuse, illegal activities, substance abuse, imprisonment, physical injuries while performing heavy works. “Kidnapping” by armed groups is ranked as the highest risk affecting girls and boys in both NW/SW and highlighted as a top concern in the host community. Importantly, children with disabilities are identified as the most vulnerable group in the host community assessment. As for protective factors, child protection community mechanisms are weak in the host community. Parents are reported to be the main source of protection for children.
