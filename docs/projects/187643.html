Project Summary

Based on community participation, the project is designed based on the following: reduce hunger by providing access to food to the population affected by the crisis especially people living inside camps to have access to life-saving food to meet their basic food needs, all year round in Idlib and Aleppo Governorates.
Key interventions will include:
- 5000 food baskets in Harim district and Jarablus District for 23 months.
- 3500 food vouchers in Dana, Al-Bab, and Maaretmasrin districts for 23 months in line with FSL cluster recommendation.
- Moreover, there will be 28000 RTEs that will be distributed in emergency situations.
- Free bread for the families and supporting the bakeries 2325 MT for 15,000 families.
ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the vulnerability selection criteria:
• Women headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
• Large families (more than 5).
• Families with disabled members.
• Families with low income (less than 50 USD)
• Families with elderly (men-women).
The total number of beneficiaries will be 202,500.
ATAA will remain AAP through open dialogue, that accurate and timely information(including goals, outcomes, and any vulnerability criteria to participate) will share with them. We have now $4,157,655 as a secure fund and ATAA will make efforts to secure funds to carry out the project.

4 - Needs

Entering the tenth year of Syria crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across NWS remain overwhelming. At least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. (HNO 2021).The main drivers to food insecurity remain to be conflict and protracted displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity and economic sanctions on the country. 2020_SHRP.There is significant overcrowding in several areas in northwest Syria. Food needs in Aleppo has been soaring with no additional capacity to accommodate further arrivals. In addition, host communities face significant pressures in terms of food supply and adequacy. Humanitarian groups fear catastrophic consequences if the coronavirus numbers increase in IDPs overcrowded areas in opposition-held areas in north Syria, as in those communities there are a large number of displaced people still live in either camps or sharing temporary housing, and both of them lack food, care, health and hygiene services. Severe food insecurity and all forms of malnutrition, if not addressed, will have a devastating impact on hundreds of thousands of Syrians, both in the short (increased mortality) and long term (irreversible physical and mental health impacts).

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA last updated PSEA policy in December 2021 and was shared with donors, and set a dedicated PSEA committee including (PSEA focal point, HR focal point, MEAL focal point, Grants and partnership focal point).ATAA adopted a core concept in all interventions to make PSEA and CSG mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers, and contractors will receive orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conduct.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels, in addition, to ATAA feedback and complaint system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to the investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of the investigation.ATAA is a PSEA network member participates in all regular network meetings, in addition to submitting monthly reports to the network, conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.Gender balance will be a core point in the project within all teams (programs, media, MEAL) and locations will be accessed by women and girls safely.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Relative Stability in Jarabulus, Maaret Tamsrin and Dana sub-districts and access to people in need. No disruption in the supply chain, transport and distribution of commodities. Adequate humanitarian access is secured. There is social acceptance for the project and the people are prepared to interact with it. An analysis of all risks related to the project was conducted where risk has been classified into protection, environmental and security risks as well as COVID-19 risk. Protection and GBV risk: When selecting the beneficiaries, some people and communities or dominant groups in the project area do not accept the idea of participation of women in project activities. This risk will be mitigated by external awareness-raising sessions in the community on women's rights and the importance of their participation in the project and signing MOUs with local councils which include the participation of women in the project. The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in. This risk will be mitigated by transparency with local authorities and the community and by informing them very clearly of the selection criteria regarding women. Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse.
