Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to achieve the SO2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives by supporting livestock owners, and most vulnerable rural women in the target area.
The support will be through:
1- Extension service by establishing mobile clinic, a committee composed of the M&E team and veterinarians will be set to select 500 BNFs according to specific criteria.
livestock (cows, sheep, goat) of selected beneficiaries will receive health, and nutritional care by field visits and provision of necessary vaccinations against endemic viral diseases to prevent their occurrence , treatments (Antibiotics, anti-inflammatory and other medications are provided as needed), and nutritional supplements such as vitamins, minerals and others, and provision of food supporting by providing the livestock owners with fodder voucher of 100$.
2- 100 rural women will be selected from the most vulnerable families according to specific criteria, and they will be provided with milk at a reduced cost for 3 months, in addition to a kitchen set to start dairy production.
The kitchen set consists of (50 Plastic pails 2 kg, 4 Plastic container 25 liter, Pot stainless steel capacity 50 liter, stove unit (Gaz, cylinder, burner, connectors), 2 Large spoon, 10 Milk filter)
The project will target most vulnerable households, according to a specific criteria:
Households headed by women.
Households with disabled persons, elderly and a big number of children.
Households with breadwinner who has difficulties in securing income.
Households who have left the camps.
The selection process will be in direct coordination with the Local Authority of target communities
Milk and dairy products are essential sources of food because they contain the essential components that the body needs to build from protein, fats, sugars, minerals and vitamins that must be present in the individual's daily diet. Milk is one of the cheapest types of animal protein and is an important source of related manufacturing and income sources for a large segment of small rural and urban producers (animal breeding, milk processing).
The planned project will target two locations through two phases:
1- Atareb in Jebel Saman district of Aleppo during 2022.
2- Mare in Azaz district of Aleppo during 2023.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022 14.6M people need some form of food and agriculture assistance this includes 13.2M people are estimated to be food insecure. The protracted crisis in Syria, the knock-on effects of COVID19, and major losses in food production sector due to climate change have intensified needs.The lack of financial and physical access to food is the result of a big drop in food production, lack of income and low purchasing power, high prices, high fodder prices, insufficient coverage of veterinary services. Therefore, people have resorted to harmful strategies include spending savings, borrowing money, depleting productive assets. IAC team conducted RNA and market assessment in target areas, major findings were: 1-70% of livestock owners said livestock is the main source of their income. 2-59% said they don't periodically examine health of their livestock. 3-62% said that there is no dispensary or veterinarian in their town. 4-35% said that livestock health care is a burden on them. 5-90% said they needed support to secure feed for livestock. 6-66% said they consume 1 kg of yogurt per day per household, 19% said 2 kg, and rest reported don't consume it. 7- 33% said they consume 1 kg of cheese per day per household, and the rest reported don't consume it at all. 8- 58% of said that the prices of milk and cheese and other dairy is high and don't compatible with their weak income. 9- 80% agreed that establishing dairy production will decrease price and enhance availability

5 - Implementation of activities

The main activities of each phase are:1. Establishing Mobile clinic to provide vaccinations and treatments for all available Livestock.2. Supporting 500 HHs with fodder voucher value of 100$.3. Establishing women association from the most vulnerable HHs (100 women), MOU will be signed with supported Livestock owners to sell their products to the association with reduced cost for 3 months as support to the association to start producing and selling their products, the association will provide them with required items to start food processing (dairy products, and other food processing available).In each area one of the people roams the farmers and buys milk from them cheaply, as the livestock breeders sell the milk produced for this person for these reasons:• Tradition in the area.• Milk produced is a food fast corruption and cannot be stored in normal weather conditions without the use of refrigeration is not available currently due to the lack of electricity availability.• High transport costs (raw milk - milk factory) which leads to low competitiveness of the product in the market because of the high price compared to others.• Producers of milk (small producers) are always looking for stability in the marketing of their product, which is achieved by the person who comes to their villages on a daily basis and committed to buy the milk permanently despite low price paid to them.4. 100 women will be provided with kitchen sets for dairy production and food processing.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Partial access to Project services; IAC Team may not reach out one of the most vulnerable groups within the population i.e., women.Likelihood: medium Impact: highMitigation: IAC will be able to reach both genders (male and female) within targeted communities, through recruiting gender-mixed Project staff.Risk 2: Dignity of BNFs may not be respected which might cause tension between IAC and local communities. A member of IAC team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations.Likelihood: low Impact: highMitigation: The staff will sign IAC CoC, which includes adherence to humanitarian principles. Dignity of the affected population will be maintained and Project staff will not abuse or exploit the BNFs.Risk 3: IAC staff member applies sexual exploitation or abuse towards a BNFs. This might result in hurting the staff member or the beneficiary.Likelihood: low Impact: HighMitigation: IAC will train all Project staff on PSEA and CSG. Then they will sign IAC related policies before reaching out BNFs.Risk 4: Project Beneficiaries cannot share their feedbacks, concerns, complaints due to lack of reporting/complaint mechanism or fear from reactions/revenge.Likelihood: medium Impact: High Mitigation: IAC will share information about Project activities, locations and selection criteria with local communities. A safe, accessible reporting/feedback mechanism will be established and shared with local communities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff are in direct contact with the beneficiaries. All field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: 1- IAC will make sure that All staff, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct. 2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA training 3- IAC will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms 4- Activating complaints mechanisms to protect PSEA in particular, placing them in easily accessible places, designing awareness-raising banners, placing boxes for complaints and suggestions, and activating the hotline number (WhatsApp).5 - provide awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA and methods for reporting them and gaining the trust of society and spreading humanitarian principles and stick to them.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: -Access to specific Sub-districts remains open. -Availability of fodder and medicines at an appropriate price.-Availability of the necessary quantities of milk for the project.Risk1: Implementation of the durable activities is prevented/ stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping.Mitigation: IAC will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/ violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/ violence is noted, alternative routes will be used and additional warehouses will be rented, or consider alternative areas/ activities under the planned intervention.Risk2: A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the organization and negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria.Mitigation: As a registered NGO in Turkey, IAC is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate.If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, IAC will adapt its modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure a continued assistance.Risk3: Some of the beneficiaries might not engage in the production or sell their inputs.Mitigation3: the farmers will receive a voucher with allocated amount for redeiming on regular bases and not one time only to make sure that all vouchers will be redeimed with required materials and used in the proper time.
