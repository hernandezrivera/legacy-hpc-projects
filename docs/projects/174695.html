Project Summary

Populations in NWSW continue to suffer severe consequences of the ongoing-armed conflict with the situation exacerbated by COVID-19. NW is 5th most affected region with 18/19 Health Districts affected by COVID (OCHA, 2020). About 3 million people have been affected- 705,800 IDPs and 360,500 Returnees with 1,968,578 people affected in NW, 782,535 in need of assistance with 232,300 IDPs and 204,298 returnees (Humanitarian Response Plan, 2021). The conflict characterized by the destruction of the already fragile economy, massive displacements due to continuous arm battles coupled with COVID 19, is having disastrous consequences on the populations of the embattled regions with women and children suffering the most. In addition, people affected by crises are more susceptible to illness and death from disease, particularly diarrhoeal and infectious diseases especially the vaccine preventable diseases. This is so as such diseases are strongly related to inadequate sanitation, water supply and poor hygiene. The health system in the Northwest and South West regions have been hard hit by the crisis with many health facilities in remote hard-to-reach areas destroyed and most healthcare personnel have vacated their areas of work due to insecurity. In most localities now, the few surviving facilities are mostly ran by Community Health Workers (CHWs), who for the most part are unskilled, thus unable to adequately respond to health needs in an emergency and humanitarian context especially vaccinations and deliveries. In addition, as is the case with massive displacements in crisis times, IDPs live in crowded, unhygienic conditions in host communities, bushes, or hard-to-reach areas in make shift settlements that is a breeding ground for diarrheal and common childhood diseases like malaria and acute respiratory infections and vaccine preventable diseases like measles. Because access to safe drinking water remains problematic in such settings, an outbreak of any of the WASH related diarrhoeal and infectious diseases in such a fragile health infrastructure could be more difficult to contain thus, necessitating prevention. The displacements and COVID 19 have equally led to many people losing their means of livelihood, reduced farming which constituted the main means of survival for most people; explaining the observed increase of GBV especially towards women and children. In such situations, PwDs especially women and children face a triple jeopardy resulting from their gender, economic situation and disability.
To respond, the project will improve the health of children and women especially pregnant women and GBV survivors by carrying out mobile clinic consultations, Measles vaccination of children between 6 months to 15 years, support pregnant women to access ANC services, training and deploy BeMONC nurses increase the percentage of deliveries attended to by skilled birth attendants. GBV survivors will be supported to access healthcare and referrals and counter-referrals to the protection cluster sector will be supported.
Targets
- 2500 people consulted through mobile clinics
- Support the vaccination of children against measles
- 100 CHWs trained to raise awareness of good practices to reduce the risk of epidemics
- 100% of GBV survivors received health care
- 100% GBV survivors referred or counter-referred to the protection sector
- 95% of births carried out by skilled health personnel
- 2500 pregnant women received in antenatal consultation (ANC 1)

OCHA 2020 reported that the number of reported GBV cases remains very high with 669 cases reported in October. Sexual violence represents 35 percent of the reported cases of GBV while survivors’ access to a multi sectoral response remains a challenge. Survivors of GBV incidents are mostly women (85 percent), 11 percent of which are living with disabilities, and 49 percent are children. There is a critical need to scale up lifesaving GBV services including prevention activities, treatment and social support. In such situations, Persons with disabilities especially women and children face a triple jeopardy resulting from their gender, economic situation and disability. OCHA had postulated in 2019 that if the funding situation does not improve 64 % of the 1.1 million people in need of lifesaving GBV services will receive no assistance in 2020. Many of these victims end of getting pregnant if medical care is not sought early. Many of such victims report the lack of financial resources to seek care and lack of information about where to seek care and the unavailability of the needed services in most communities. UNFPA in 2019 reported that these most vulnerable populations are also at high risk of complications of pregnancy and delivery.
