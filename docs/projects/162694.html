Project Summary

The main objective is providing access to life-saving and resilient WASH-IWRM, Education and Protection services and advocacy including gender and age concerns, protection guarantees, and environmental enhancement for 80,000 (WASH), 3,000 (Protection), and 3,550 (Education) beneficiaries who are traumatized IDPS, refugees, returnees, host communities, those affected by unsafe water borne disease threats, conflict and/or natural recurring disasters in South Kordofan, South Darfur, West Darfur, Red Sea states. There will be a special emphasis in targeting vulnerable populations such as women, children, and the elderly. THe WASH is linked as a platform for protection and education activities. Women lead in
initiation, design, planning, execution, and close-out activities, is a key objective that will not only save more lives, but improve the quality of services that alleviate suffering for the present situation, add valuable input, help mitigate negative impacts of future life threatening occurrences, and affect truly inclusive decision-making beyond the water maintenance committees. Again, gender, protection and environment will be major priorities. This will set the stage for newly emerging resilient, sustainable IWRM approach to overall water usage and conservation and all that this most basic natural resource impacts.
In order to reach this objective IAS will utilize an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) approach. IAS has developed a practical field manual on IWRM that serves as guide in the implementation of its WASH programs. Practical examples include: Following hydro geological studies and community consultation, the most sustainable method for water provision will be selected. Drilling of new boreholes equipped with hand pumps will include school usage. In areas with high demand and adequate water supply solar powered mini water yards will be introduced. In the areas with rare to limited availability of ground water the best option will be solutions related to the rainfall capture schemes. Every water point implementation or rehabilitation will include beneficiary sanitation and hygiene promotion and training, including hardware mechanic training which will include women and girls, for recruited water maintenance committees, where we will communicate that the best prevention to disaster is maintaining resilient, accessible and safe water points.
Closeout activities will include helping beneficiary water maintenance committees determine the financial cost for actual spare parts so that they can target necessary budgets for ensuring continuous water availability when breakdowns occur.
IAS interventions in education sector will be in Sirba- West Darfur ,Rashad - South Kordofan and Haya in the red sea targeting 3000 boy and girls , as more areas became accessible. IAS interventions in the targeted localities aims to improve quality of education environment through activities prioritized in the HRP such as the provision of teaching, learning and recreational materials targeting 3000 in the 3 localities, capacity-building for teachers and PTAs members targeting 550 individuals in Sirba and Rashad to support community engagement and interaction with schools through training on school management , psychosocial support and disaster risk reduction in order to enhance their skills. TOT training will also be conducted for 80 teachers in Sirba and Rashad. More over IAS will contributes in improving of the healthy environment in the schools through the conduction of awareness campaigns in hygiene promotion, gender sensitive and child protection for 3000 in the 3 targeted localities.
Protection is ensured through hygiene awareness sessions to targeted communities and students at schools including children and people with disabilities. IAS will use multi-sector approaches to increase the effectiveness of programming and meet multiple needs of the targeted affected populations in the targeted localities

1 - Needs

According to IAS' recent Needs Assessments in Red Sea State and South Kordofan and ongoing assessments in West and South Darfur, along with statistics published in OCHA's 05, December 2019 Sudan Situation Report that there are 9.3 million people in Sudan in need of humanitarian services. In the first three quarters of 2019 IOM has registered 14,500 IDPs and 111,500 Returnees across 6 states. Also, the authorities are reporting newly emerging areas in South Kordofan where access has not been available for agencies to provide basic necessity services. Thousand of people from the SPLA N areas are streaming into Kadugli locality seeking basic needs. Many from these localities are wandering search for water, food and shelter. Nearly 50 local NGOs have had their registration cancelled in recent days so there are less humanitarian implementing partners to intervene. The implication is a tremendous need to help the Sudanese Government provide even the most basic life-saving services to these traumatized IDPs, refugees and returnees, along with the increasingly over-stressed host communities. They cited the need for safe drinking water and water borne disease mitigation training and convenient sanitation locations as key priorities. WASH urgent needs: 1. Rehabilitation and upgrading of existing water supplies, drilling new boreholes, quickly developing catchment areas to harvest rainwater through sand-dams which replenish existing wells in needy areas, and rehabilitate and build new hafirs for livestock. 2. Establishment of monitoring of water quality and treatment systems 3. Comprehensive disease prevention by improvement of sanitation facilities supporting health/Nutrition sector. 4. Systemic hygiene promotion 5. Establishment of solid waste management program 6. Ensure schools and marketplaces have hand washing facilities and adequate latrines. As IAS is one of the very few organizations that have our own drilling rigs, geologists, survey equipment and CDOs to meet the needs of all targeted beneficiaries in WASH, protection, environment, and all age/gender needs. Girls are walking many hours in unprotected areas in order to wait in long lines to fetch, often, contaminated water for their families. This leaves no time to attend school. Women and the aged are marginalized and prevented from assuming more influential roles in beneficiary societies. Needs in the education sector continue to increase mainly in North and West Darfur. As more areas become accessible, the need for quality education is increasing across all areas with humanitarian. The most vulnerable children and adults are those with special needs. Children of displaced communities are adding pressure to existing education facilities which cannot accommodate new students due to limited space and resources. As a result, children are in risk of dropping out of school, becoming exposed to additional risks and violence and child labor. The physical safety and psycho-social wellbeing of of girls and boys affected by emergencies in Sudan remains greatly compromised due to the protracted exposure to violence, disruption of social support systems, increasing poverty and the persistent lack of access to quality social services and necessities.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Due to our experience and lessons learned in the multi-year DFID funded IWRM projects in Darfur and eastern Sudan, IAS uses IWRM principles to enhance the environment in the design, implementation and closing of our WASH humanitarian projects. For 2020, IAS will be implementing sand-dams using the community involved catchment approach. The beneficiaries make up the catchment committees and they will help determine while coordinating with WES the decisions governing all things related to the intervention including environmental actions. Rather than continuing to use up existing precious water by drilling more boreholes, this approach guides rain water (90% of which is normally lost) through the use of sand dams and other subsurface dams to replenish existing wells. The environmental impact for safe water provision is naturally very positive in terms of supporting a healthier lifestyles, better quality of lives, and supporting plants and agricultural projects, etc, but with the use of cement and construction materials, great care will be given to cleaning up after each project site; that each water point intervention area will be cleaner than before IAS commenced the project. Inclusive community/ main actors project design, initiation, and planning meetings will be the stage for environmental protection and planning advocacy. Beneficiaries will be tasked with developing a long-term environmental plan and will sign-off and be accountable to the authorities on their progress. For every water point, IAS will conduct Environment Impact Assessments (EIAs). Taking the data from these surveys, we educate targeted beneficiaries to take the necessary steps to improve the environment. Communities will be involved in cleaning up water points and latrine construction sites. Fences and shrubs will be set up around the area. Conservation of natural resources, especially water, will be central themes in WMC meetings throughout the project, including close-out activities which will also include inspection ensuring that the environment activities were carried out and the environment enhancement plan was completed for the future in each beneficiary community where IAS is intervening. As well as budgeting for water point maintenance needs, WMCs will be requested to work with the community to budget environmental enhancement activities. Finally, those receiving environmental advocacy and planning training will be encouraged to bring their knowledge into the local schools.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In the midst of emergency WASH responses, IAS will continue to facilitate meaningful participation and engagement of affected communities including training and messaging throughout the programme cycle including during child protection needs assessment, response planning and monitoring. In carrying out community-based protection services, IAS will pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner, conducting community safety audit and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programing and advocacy. IAS will maintain transparent, inclusive and adequate communication with communities on available services; and by consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selection of services, feedback after service provision. Partners will also be guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability boys and girls, children without adult care,ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to the needs of all communities. Child Protection services and response interventions will continue to be based on a quality and integrated approach including coordination and capacity building with other humanitarian actors to support protection mainstreaming and a do-no-harm approach as well as principled response. In addition, IAS will build on and integrate wherever possible, feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and support the interagency coordination mechanism to respond to relevant complaints. IAS will continue working closely with partners to monitor beneficiaries’feedback, share information and conduct community consultations, ensuring adequate accountability toaffected populations (AAP). Child Protection training for partners will often include sessions on AAP and Child Protection response.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IAS will follow the WASH cluster approach by continuing to promote the minimum commitments to safety and dignity for affected people, following the guidance of the Global WASH Cluster and the HCT. The minimum commitments are operationalized in the accountability framework developed by WASH partners, which guides partners to 1) provide minimum information to the public, 2) involve community in decision making, 3) learn from feedback and complaints, 4) encourage appropriate staff attitude and behaviors. 5) use information from project learning, and 6) develop programs based on participatory assessments. The WASH cluster will continue to guide partners in adopting the framework through sharing of best practices and peer learning. The implementation of the framework will be monitored through a self-reporting mechanism, while the cluster will also advise partners on relevant indicators they can include in their monitoring efforts (including post distribution monitoring).As part of its community-based response, IAS will engage and involve affected populations through, among others, establishing community based protection networks and committees, including IDPs, women and youth, in order to foster two-way communication and feedback with the humanitarian community and mobilize capacities toward the protection of all members of the community, particularly the vulnerable. Community protection initiatives also involve the participation of the community to raise awareness on key protection issues relevant to women, girls, boys and men to prevent or resolve conflicts and foster peaceful co-existence. IAS will also established feedback and complaint mechanisms, such as protection hotlines that provide information on services, suggestions boxes in child friendly spaces, safe spaces for women and regular trends analysis. Other accountability mechanisms include the Accountability and Impact Assessment Framework of the GBV Sub-Cluster, as well as Child Protection Minimum Standards, which supports accountability between partners and meaningful child participation in programme implementation. Every WASH intervention will include SEA mechanisms.
