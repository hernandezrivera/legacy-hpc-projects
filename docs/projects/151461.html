Project Summary

This project aims to target locations with newly displaced populations that have limited or no access to educational facilities in order to support the learning requirements of the internally displaced families. Children between 7 and 10 years old will be targeted to access primary education, while those aged 11-14 who have never attended or have dropped out of formal education will be targeted through non-formal education activities. The project will target 4,000 children (2,100 girls and 1,900 boys), 100 teachers and 30 Community Education Committees (CEC) members. Eligible categories of vulnerability and beneficiary selection criteria are expected to include young mothers, former militias, girls, children with disabilities, dropouts and children at risk of dropping out of school, pastoralist youth with no access to education and vulnerable children and youth from the host communities. The education programme will target duty bearers as means of fulfilling the education rights of the target group. This will be achieved by supporting the Ministry of Education, which has limited means to supervise the quality of learning. This will also enhance the mainstreaming of education in emergencies in the formal education sector.

Needs

Shocks of the prolonged drought, conflict and the latest floods have led to very low (30%) Gross Enrolment Rate (GER) for primary schools in Somalia. Over 1.7 million children of school age are out of school. According to a recent Education Cluster report, only 17% of children living in rural areas or in IDP settlements are enrolled in primary school. In a recent needs assessment conducted by Concern in target IDP camps (Afgoye, Garasballey in Lower Shebelle and Benadir respectively), parents interviewed indicated that large number of children out of school was partly due to children engaging in income generating activities to supplement the family income. They therefore do not attend school regularly and finally drop out. Boys are sent for shoe shining and girls are sent to earn money from washing clothes in other households as well as help their mothers on home activities. Such families cannot afford the cost of learning such as purchasing learning materials for their children.Even for children already enrolled in the EiE learning centres, a good number are at risk of dropping out of school due to the same costs coupled with limited support from parents. The learning centres and schools where a majority of the IDP children are enrolled lack teaching and learning materials, teachers with inadequate capacity to deliver quality lessons and provide psychosocial first aid services. There is acute shortage of female teachers. Community Education Comities have limited capacity to manage schools/ centres and resources. The learning centres/ schools have limited WASH facilities appropriate for children, especially girls. The gender parity index of 0.81 in South Central, largely due to traces of cultural beliefs that prioritize education boys' education, especially when resources are scarce. As a result, many children especially girls, are denied education, especially among IDPs. New IDPs settlements lack access to schools. Benadir, Bay and Lower Shebelle are among others

Activities

OC 1: Increased Access to Learning opportunities for vulnerable Boys and Girls from Displacement Affected communities: As a priority, expand learning spaces (build extra classrooms) in existing formal schools to accommodate out of school children. In areas where formal/ non- formal schools are inaccessible to children in IDP camps, Concern will build Temporary Learning Spaces (made of tents and/or iron sheets including furniture) to set up Emergency Learning Centers, children enrolled in schools prior to being displaced will continue learning while in displacement; Support Mobilization and awareness raising in communities on enrolment for all children, especially girls, equality, importance of parental participation in learning of their children, protection and prevention of School Related Gender Based Violence (SRGBV). OC 2: Improved Quality and Safe Learning for Boys and Girls from Displacement Affected communities. : Support for education needs, teaching and learning material which include reading and writing material;Emergency incentives for teachers who have been displaced;Provide teacher training opportunities aiming to improve capacity to deliver relevant and quality lessons.; subject mastery (especially Literacy and Numeracy), pedagogical skills appropriate for crisis affected children. Have MoECHE master trainers to support teachers on a continuous basis; training for teachers on psychosocial support, child protection;Provide life-saving assistance,distribution of hygiene kits to school boys and girls, rehabilitation of school water sources and construction of gender-segregated latrines. Adolescent girls will receive reusable dignity kits to improve menstruation hygiene; distribution of recreational materials.OC3. Strengthened school system to respond to emergency preparedness& Risk Reduction: Work with (MoE), (CEC) and Education cluster members on emergency preparedness and Risk Reduction plans for the schools;Joint monitoring and coordination.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will ensure that the protection environment is sufficient and stable by creating awareness and training on safety and protection among teachers, CEC members and children (boys and girls). These trainings will be conducted in collaboration with Ministry of Education staff to ensure skills transfer and sustained capacity within the Ministry. Furthermore, CEC members and MOE staff will be trained on emergency response and resilience. Through this interventions Emergency Education will be largely integrated in the broader plans of the Ministry of Education. At the school level, implementation of the approved national curriculum in all the schools and Education Emergency centers will facilitate easy transition of children from the Emergency Education centers (with Accelerated learning) to formal schools, ensuring some level of continuity in learning for children. The classrooms built in the formal schools to accommodate displaced children will be officially handed over to the CEC and theministry of Education at the district level. This project will be linked to the Global Partnership for Education, which is a long-term project implemented by Concern in collaboration with the Ministry of Education at the Federal and state levels.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Displaced communities in Somalia face the risk of evictions from IDP camps and as a result, children's learning is interrupted. Concern thus plans to work with the Ministry of Education officials to develop written agreements for land owners to give adequate notice for eviction and to ensure that education materials are not destroyed. Conflict between IDPs and the host communities constitute another risk. As such, Concern will facilitate discussions between the host community and IDPs for safe social integration and sharing of school resources. Minority groups being excluded from beneficiary list constitutes a protection risk and thus Concern will facilitate a comprehensive and participatory beneficiary identification process to include the vulnerable and excluded groups within the community.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Working children and youth who have never been to school risk being left out since during day time they are at work and not at school. Working with community leaders (men and women) to identify IDP children involved in child labor and raising awareness of the consequences of child labour will mitigate the risks Adolescent girls also risk being left out due to cultural and traditional beliefs that de-prioritize education of girls when resources are scarce. Sensitization of parents on the right to education for all children, especially girls will support in mitigating this risk. The voices of children are not heard in identification of issues, and monitoring of project progress. As such, children (girls and boys) mobilization and sensitization sessions will be conducted throughout the project to ensure the project is accountable to the children it aims to serve. Children with disabilities are likely to be excluded due to the limited participation space they are given culturally. Concern will thus work with community leaders (men and women) to identify IDP children living with disabilities and measures are taken to ensure their participation in project activities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Families moving away from project areas due to armed conflict. Concern will therefore engage with government and community leaders to identify areas that are at high risk of being attacked by armed groups to avoid these areas during location identification. Community jealousy & disharmony leads to violence. This will be managed by building on already established networks with the authorities and communities and developing access strategy based on the recruitment of staff from all clans in project area and investing in sensitization at community level. Localized clan conflict. Regular early warning information collection from UNDSS and field teams will mitigate this risk. Such information will help define the remedial measures while also ensuring engagement with legitimate local, district and regional authorities in programme implementation areas. Threats from insurgent groups. The programme will keep at its core the principle of neutrality while balancing staff safety and programme quality.
