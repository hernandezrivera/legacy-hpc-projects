Project Summary

The fragile food security and nutrition situation in Somalia requires close monitoring in order to ensure timely interventions by all stakeholders. Without this crucial information generated by FSNAU, response agencies would lack the evidence to effectively design the appropriate food security, nutrition and livelihoods interventions; the government would not be able to advocate for resources and coordinate responses; and resource partners would lack the evidence to make informed decisions to fund the interventions.
The project objective is to support emergency and longer-term policy and programme interventions through the provision of timely information and analysis on the current and emerging food, nutrition, and livelihood security situation in Somalia.

Needs

Given the persistently high level of food insecurity and malnutrition in Somalia, a rigorous food security and nutrition information system is needed to provide timely, credible and independent analysis. Since its launch in the mid-1990s, the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) has been providing a broad range of information users with timely information and analysis to support evidence-based decision-making related to humanitarian assistance, including short-term food insecurity and malnutrition. Without this crucial information generated by FSNAU, response agencies would lack the evidence to effectively design the appropriate food security, nutrition and livelihoods interventions; the government would not be able to advocate for resources and coordinate responses; and resource partners would lack the evidence to make informed decisions to fund the interventions. FSNAU regularly monitors rainfall and vegetation conditions, river levels, crop and livestock performance, food and livestock prices, wage rates, conducts integrated food security and nutrition surveys covering displaced, urban and rural population; conducts food security and livelihood assessments which are based on quantitative household surveys (data collection is fully gender disaggregated; results are also reported by sex of children, gender of head of household, etc. as appropriate).

Activities

• Seasonal food security and nutrition assessments will be conducted across Somalia with the active participation of government line ministries and partners, including follow up assessments in the most affected areas• Key risk factors related to food security and nutrition will be monitored and disseminated through the FSNAU Early warning-early action database and dashboard in order to ensure both data sharing and use by humanitarian actors, government and other stakeholders• Seasonal and regular food security, nutrition, market and livelihoods related information and analysis products will be prepared and disseminated to users via briefing presentations, subscribed mailing lists, web posting and hard copies, including translation of key messages and/or documents into Somali language• Provision of IPC and related food security and nutrition training to government and partner staff• Participation of trained staff in planning, data collection, assessment and analyses for seasonal food security and nutrition assessments

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Most project activities are to be carried out in accessible areas. The protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable to enable durable solutions as well as resilience. In hard to reach areas (due to insecurity), FSNAU collects data through local partners and locally recruited enumerators. Information and analysis generated by FSNAU over the years, including data that will be generated under this project in 2019, are critically relevant to inform recovery, resilience and long-term interventions. FSNAU does not have direct beneficiaries in the conventional use of the term and project activities do not involve the provision of services and utilities to beneficiaries. The primary beneficiaries of the information and analysis generated by FSNAU under this project will be government line ministries, UN agencies, local and international NGOs and donors. Ultimate beneficiaries of the project will be the 4.6 million food insecure people (FSNAU August 2018).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

FSNAU established practice of providing information and analysis disaggregated by IDP, urban and rural, which are further disaggregated by district and sub-district level will help reduce risks of exclusion based on displacement status.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

FSNAU assessments involve representative household surveys, which by their very nature ensure inclusion of representative households regardless of gender, age or head of household. In the absence of the head of the household, he/she can be represented by any adult member of the household present at the time of the interview. Attention is required and will be paid to reduce gender and age-based exclusion during the composition of participants for focus group discussions and key informant interviews. Where appropriate, FSNAU information and analysis are disaggregated by gender and age in order highlight gender and age-related differences in food security and nutrition outcomes. This information can be used to reduce gender and age-related exclusion risks during programme design and intervention.
