Project Summary

During 2021, the main driver of displacement in Ethiopia was conflict. According to the Shelter&NFI annual report, the number of people in displacement situation rose by 100% compared to 2020, reaching 4.2M, as result of regional tensions, inter-regional dynamics, and localized tensions in several areas of the country.
The conflict in Tigray started in November 2020; the tensions in this region spilled over to Ahmara and Afar since August 2021. OCHA reports that 9.4m of target people for humanitarian assistance in Tigray, Ahmara and Afar. The situation in these regions remains tense and volatile. However, while in Tigray and Amhara regions is remaining relatively calm, Afar Region reported fightings in Abala, Berhale, Dalol, Erebti, Koneba and Megale woredas , along the border with Tigray. These recent events, beside increasing further the humanitarian needs in Afar, resulted also in the suspension of the delivery of humanitarian supplies into Tigray via Semera-Abala-Mekelle road since 15 December 2021. The closure of the Abala Corridor, is an additional factor shrinking the humanitarian response operations in Tigray, heavily hampered by: insecurity and bureaucratic impediments (severe difficulties in bringing sufficient supplies, fuel, and cash to the region), the low access to telecommunications, the high number of people in need with restriction to access people in hard-to-reach areas due to insecurity. OCHA reports that the prices of basic goods continue to increase due to inflation as shortage of supplies in the local markets. On 24 of March 2022, The Federal authorities have promised to facilitate aid access into Tigray; the TPLF declared that the acceptance of the truce would be linked to the delivery of humanitarian aid in the Region. Out of 4.2m people in Need, the Shelter&NFI has a target of 2.4 m. From January to March 2022, the Cluster reached nearly 982,045 people, including 500,843 women and girls reached. The preliminary figures for target Woredas, show that out of 38 of those, 9 are categorized as in severe and 29 in extreme severity conditions. To give continuity to the Shelter&NFI assistance planned by COOPI in Central Zone, the project targets the following woredas in extreme conditions: Adwa, Axum, Laelay Maychew, Tahtay Mayechew. Globally, the Cluster targets 215, 928 IDPs. The activities planned in the project include: delivery of NFI (4147 households), in cash or in kind emergency Shelter (3000 households), emergency shelter & NFI (3400 HHs). Furthermore, the projects include the delivery of 1338 ESNFI kits for those households living with a member with disability. In total the project will cover 59426 individuals, (22% male; 23% female; 26%boys; 25% girls; 2% elderly men; 2% elderly female). Finally, the proposal includes cross-cutting activities related to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitationand Abuse (PSEA); the support to referral mechanism to specialized agencies. COOPI will mainstream gender& age throughout theimplementation of the project by ensuring the participation of women and men and including gender sensitive selection criteria.

1. - Projects activities

A1. Preliminary activities- COOPI has already in place an agreement with EDRMC; briefing on safety and security protocols for staff, including ESNFI guidelines for distribution in COVID-19 context; training and briefing on PSEA based on the Ethiopia Network Training Package; protection mainstreaming approach.-Sites ‘selection in the target woredas, prioritizing those with higher number of IDPs, access to basic services; Shelter and NFI needs/gaps and protection threats. -Procurement of the materials/items according to the type of response and the technical specifications and quantities as designed by the Cluster-Beneficiaries‘ identification, selection, verification and registration considering the following criteria: displaced households who have not received any Shelter/NFI support; female-headed and child-headed HHs, HHs with 6 children or more, with member(s) part of the vulnerability groups (infant, children, elders etc.) or households with specific needs (a member with disability, pregnant women, persons with critical medical conditions, among others). The criteria stated above will be revised and agreed according to the feedback of the main stakeholders in line with humanitarian principles (neutrality,independence, humanity and impartiality) with the Shelter&NFI cluster at Federal, regional and sub-regional level. -Set up of a feedback and complaints mechanisms aimed at sharing information on the project and entitlements, implementing organizations and donor; reporting mechanisms, installation of suggestion boxes and notice boards at site level, the identification of formal and informal leaders/community committees in charge of delivering feedbacks/complaints to COOPI. -Set-up of selection committees made of village elders; religious leaders; local authorities, IDPs/Returnees and hosting community representatives and COOPI Staff. The project will ensure equal participation of women and men.COOPI will ensure that data will be disaggregated by gender and age and, when it applies, the type of disability (neurological, mental, sensorial and physical). 3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

COOPI adhere to the humanitarian accountability principles and all COOPI Ethiopia staff is trained of the 5 commitments of the Accountability of the Affected Population.COOPI will make sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, where and how to obtain it.Following COOPI Standard Operating Procedures, to ensure informed decisions COOPI staff will involve partners, communities, local authorities, beneficiaries and especially vulnerable groups in the planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating of this project. This includes engaging beneficiaries at the design stage by conducting focus group discussions and individual interviews with both men and women in various age groups, and ensure that assessments include the most vulnerable, including elders and people with disabilities. During the action, beneficiaries at all levels will take a leading role in the formation/consolidation of the committees described in the proposal (selection committees). These committees, jointly with local authorities and project staff, will co-lead in the selection of the recipients and in the monitoring of the action.COOPI will provide opportunities for women, people with disabilities and elder people to hold positions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committees including as community focal points for assessment teams. An accessible, well understood and confidential mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions andcomplaints, with a clear follow-up system on all reports.COOPI’s feedback andcomplaint mechanism includes information sharing on:• Project objectives, outcomesand outputs, implementing agency and donor.• Selection criteria and mechanisms toreport issues for benefi ciaries;• Composition of the ES-NFIs kit/entitlements. The validation and agreement with the main stakeholders on the selection of sites and beneficiaries of distributions will take place through meetings with community members, local authorities and cluster representatives.COOPI Project management team will strengthen local capacities, foster local knowledge and community coping strategies, as well as enhance protection mechanisms providing where possible psycho-social services to the community and beneficiaries.COOPI management team will make sure that field teams are sensitized and trained on the importance of making assessments tools age-friendly, by including questions on the needs and capacities of older and vulnerable people.Particular attention will be provided on PSEA. COOPI will provide training on its internal policies to the project staff and main stakeholders. The policy includes the concepts related with PSEA, preventive measures, referral mechanisms as well as feedback and complaints mechanisms. Besides that, COOPI will brief the staff also using the Ethiopia PSEA Network Training Package.COOPI will set up a feedback and complain mechanism to allow beneficiaries’ participation,improving monitoring and accountability reporting and promptly adjust theimplementation of the project. Mechanisms to report issues will be done through:•Provision to local stakeholders of main contacts for COOPI staff (phone and email), especially for the SMT program staff • Provision of help-desk contact (help@coopi.org) • Establishment of complaints and feedback box and notice panel at the field (depending on level of literacy of beneficiaries)• Participatory selection of focal points at community level, for oral complaints/feedbackCOOPI will record all possible complaints and feedbacks. According to the severity of the issue COOPI SMT will respond, in at least 4 days, involving different staff at different level:• Minor complaints/ request of information: social promoters/project manager/emergency lead.•Major complaints: Programme Manager/Emergency Response Lead/CountryAdministrator/Head of Mission/HQ
