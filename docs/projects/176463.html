Project Summary

In this project, Reach Africa Organization (RAO) will ensure provision of shelter and non-food items to newly displaced Internally Displaced Persons and individuals facing new vulnerabilities in the counties of Ayod, Uror, Akobo and Nyirol. In twelve months, shelter and non-food items assistance will be provided to an estimated 30,000 displaced and vulnerable persons to be distributed as follows: Ayod: 8300 individuals); Nyirol: 7300, Akobo: 8300 and Uror: 6100 individuals. The proposed project will seek to aid in form of Kanga, blankets, cooking sets, non-food item bags, reusable masks, solar lamps and mosquito nets to the most affected persons. The assistance will cover newly internally displaced persons and most vulnerable host community members who fall under the following categories: elderly (given their advanced age, poor economic status coupled with inability to actively engage in income generating activities); pregnant and lactating women (they are exposed to various risks such as sickness, malnutrition and protection thus making them more vulnerable compared to any other group); children; unintegrated persons; people with no community links; severely sick persons; and individuals living with disabilities. A complaint mechanism will be put in place from the onset to ensure beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit complaints and receive feedback on time. RAO will utilize IOM Housing, Land and Property due diligence standard as a guide during the implementation process to mitigate the risk that the construction of shelter contributes to increasing tensions and conflicts around land and establish a process that can anticipate and respond to land and property issues ensuring HLP assets are not disputed to avoid eviction of beneficiaries and other affected populations. HLP messaging will be conducted among staffs and local community on connections between land rights and shelter and will ensure legal certainty about land rights through obtaining general information about land rights and clarifying tenure arrangement and authentication of ownership documents. RAO will adhere to WHO, CDC, Ministry of Health Guidelines and Protocols and Shelter, Non-food Items guidelines and requirements and RAO do-no-harm principle while delivering assistance to vulnerable populations. RAO coordinate with local authorities, S/NFIs National cluster and focal point, humanitarian partners in Uror, Nyirol, Ayod and Akobo to avoid duplication of activities and efforts. The intervention is integrated and will seek to link activities and beneficiaries to nutrition, health, education, protection, FSL and WASH. Protection partners will be engaged during GBV awareness and referrals, rapid assessments, safety audits, registration and verification of beneficiaries and distribution to ensure the exercises are conducted in the most conflict sensitive manner. Nutrition partners will be engaged when conducting trainings and distribution of NFIs such as mosquito nets to pregnant and lactating mothers. Joint messaging on COVID 19, Cholera, Malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea prevention and proper usage of mosquito nets will be conducted together with health partners. FSL partners will be engaged in joint information sharing with fishermen and farmers groups to reduce incidences of mosquito nets being used for fencing gardens and fishing. For durable solutions, community based S/NFIs committees will be trained and supported on effective, sustainable and equitable utilization of local resources to ensure community based shelter solutions, RAO will establish and train four Housing, Property and Land Committees to advocate for non-violent resolution of housing, property and land issues facing targeted communities and access right. RAO will facilitate in the formation, training and support to county’s disaster and risk reduction committees to conduct awareness at household and community level on disaster risk reduction

1 - Needs

The population of Jonglei State have been left in highly vulnerable conditions due to four underlying factors including Covid-19, inter communal violence, floods, scarcity of food and the economic crisis. According to South Sudan’s Humanitarian Snapshot Report (October, 2020), an estimated 207,000 were affected by flooding in the State in the last quarter of 2020. The impact of the flooding is still being felt in Akobo, Uror, Ayod and Nyirol Counties. Flooding devastated livelihoods by way of crop failure and livestock death, destroyed houses and household assets and damaged roads leading to restricted movement of goods thus disrupting market functionality. Scarcity of essential household goods has resulted in high prices and inability of displaced and vulnerable host communities from accessing essential items such as mosquito nets, blankets and cooking pots at the local level. Beyond the flooding, the four counties experienced intercommunal violence in May and October 2020, which erupted between youth residing in the four counties and youth from Greater Pibor. The violence left many homesteads burnt and household items looted. Those affected by violence are still displaced, lost household assets or food and animal stocks. In the latest Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) findings, an estimated 252,050 individuals (Ayod: 75,718; Uror: 57342; Nyirol: 41633 and Akobo: 77357) are classified as IPC4 (emergency) food insecurity or worse and IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4 (critical) or above between January and July 2021. In Akobo County, 11,051 persons are projected to slide to catastrophe (IPC5) between by July 2021. Due to worsening humanitarian situation, negative coping mechanisms include going to bed earlier due to mosquito infestation, reducing meals per day due to lack of sufficient food and adequate cooking items, staying under trees during day and seeking shelter at night from neighbors due to lack of shelter materials, and using animal hides to cover children and elderly persons at night due to lack of blankets. In the prevailing acute food security situation, vulnerable internally displaced persons and host community who have lost household assets as a result of conflict and flooding require in-kind NFIs support. Such support will enable them dedicate their remaining recourses to buy food and medicine instead of using the money to replenish lost household items. Also, providing the affected population with non-food items will enable them to reduce protection and Covid-19 risks associated with overcrowding. Considering these glaring needs and gaps, Reach Africa Organization seeks to provide shelter and non-food items assistance to an estimated 30000 displaced and vulnerable host community individuals to be distributed as follows: estimated 30,000 displaced and vulnerable persons to be distributed as follows: Ayod: 8300 individuals); Nyirol: 7300, Akobo: 8300 and Uror: 6100 individuals.Ayod. 2 - Project activities

ACTIVITY 1: Conduct Inception meetings: Inception meeting will be conducted with IDPs, women leaders, youth and people with disabilities. The purpose being is to provide information on project objective, duration, anticipated results, kind of assistance to be provided, category of beneficiaries targeted in the project, modality of implementation, and role of community and project staff in the implementation process. During the meeting, an Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) mechanism will be established comprised of fifteen (15) individuals (8 women) in each of the targeted counties. The AAP will be oriented on the project, and trained on the Feedback and Complaint Mechanism, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), inclusion, selection and targeting criteria. The AAP will participate in mobilization, sensitization, and selection of beneficiaries and during distribution of Shelter NFIs. ACTIVITY 2: Selection of 90 (47 Females/ 43 Males) local enumerators: Enumerators will conduct mobilization of beneficiaries, communication and sensitization, registration, setting up of distribution points, distribution of non-food items and post distribution monitoring. They will play the role of initiating community level engagements, mobilization of beneficiaries and stakeholders during planning, meetings, trainings, and project monitoring and evaluation. ACTIVITY 3: Selection of beneficiaries: RAO’s project staff will work closely with AAP committee, chiefs, local authorities, site managers, enumerators and existing partners to select 30000 most vulnerable households among IDPs and host community who will benefit through distribution of S/NFIs. The selection criteria to be applied will include: Individuals classified as IPC 4 and 5, women headed households, and children headed households, elderly persons, severely sick persons, pregnant and lactating women, care givers of orphans, separated and unaccompanied children. ACTIVITY 4: Distribution of S/NFIs: RAO project staff will supervise setting up of distribution venues two days prior to distribution exercise. Each distribution point will be accessible by elderly persons, those living with disability and considered safe for women and girls. The venues will have gender disaggregated latrines, water points and safe spaces where pregnant and lactating mothers can be able to rest, feed their children. Public announcement will be made using public address systems in all distribution locations. On the day of distribution, enumerators will conduct demonstration on appropriate use of the SNFIs prior to handing over the items to beneficiaries. RAO will work closely with health partners who will conduct COVID-19 messaging during distribution days. All distributions will be conducted during light hours to allow beneficiaries sufficient time to walk to distribution centres and return home during daytime. ACTIVITY 5: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring: The PDM will be carried out using S/NFI’s cluster PDM household questionnaires which contains the question that assesses the appropriateness as well as beneficiary satisfaction with amount & quality of items distributed registration and verification processes. Respondents will be selected randomly among the beneficiary HHs with focus on the IDPs and vulnerable host communities. The project will employ a sample size of 20% (1200 individuals) of the HHs who will benefit directly through the intervention. ACTIVITY 6: Conduct HLP analysis and awareness: Due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RAO is committed to the protection of human rights, therefore it has established mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). RAO is dedicated to upholding IASC 6 core principles on PSEA and will integrate PSEA sessions in all its activities, RAO will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with mainstreaming protection and creating awareness on PSEA, training consultants, field staffs and enumerators on PSEA and their policies pertaining to PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries, during distribution of shelter and NFIs. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard their protection concerns. RAO gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in Uror, Nyirol, Ayod and Akobo. Necessary PSEA referrals will be done if and when they arise by the gender officer.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RAO will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, RAO will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, RAO will seek to engage women in leadership positions so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports, post distribution monitoring reports and end of project reports will be shared with community representatives, Shelter and NFIs cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in Akobo, Uror, Ayod and Nyirol Counties. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by Reach Organization’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries and at distribution venues. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, Reach Africa Organization will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer and Protection Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at registration sites as well as distribution sites. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries and distribution of shelter and non-food items.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RAO employs evidence-based programming in all its projects which will apply to this project through constant information sharing and feedback from the community. Project feedback and suggestions from the community will be gathered through multiple channels, especially hygiene promoters during hygiene promotion activities, monitoring activities e.g. post-distribution monitoring (PDM), safety audits (on protection and GBV). The information gathered will be used to inform future programming on the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance, preferred quality and quantity of S/NFIs and protection concerns associated S/NFI in the targeted locations. The feedback/information gathered will also be used in advocacy for further actions from relevant parties in situation where RAO is unable to respond to the needs. Positive feedback will form part of documentation of the project success stories.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

On risks of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, RAO will conduct a safety audit on all registration and distribution points to map existing and potential risks. Safety Audit Findings will be embedded in project workplan and safety matrix to ensure all project activities are conducted in locations and sites where no risk is posed to staff, project supplies or beneficiaries. RAO will ensure registration and distribution points are near settlement sites to reduce on distance travelled by beneficiaries to collect their shelter and non-food items. On risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, RAO has a robust PSEA policy with all the staff properly trained on PSEA. RAO will conduct PSEA training targeting enumerators, community mobilizers and AAPs committees. Also, RAO’S gender and protection officers will conduct sensitization targeting women and girls, community leaders and local authorities on PSEA mitigation and reporting. On risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, RAO’S Child Protection policy prohibits any form of child labour and child exploitation. Gender Officer and the Protection Officer will conduct community sensitization and awareness to ensure child protection concerns are addressed and mitigated before they occur. Cases of Child Protection will be referred to appropriate child protection actors at project locations. On risks that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The project targeting criteria is clear and targets these category of beneficiaries. Community communication and sensitization will be conducted targeting beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in order to minimize of tensions arising from sections of the community who do not receive the assistance.To mitigate HLP related disputes and conflict, RAO through local authorities, protection partners and chiefs will conduct due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. Staff, local authorities and AAPs will assist to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership prior to distribution of Shelter materials to beneficiaries.
