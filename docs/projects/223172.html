Project Summary

The El-Kanemi Memorial Foundation is proposing a comprehensive education initiative to address the urgent learning needs of 5,000 conflict-affected children in Ngala, Dikwa, and Nganzai Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Borno State. These LGAs have been severely impacted by the ongoing insurgency in Northeast Nigeria, which has disrupted education, destroyed infrastructure, and displaced thousands of families. This project seeks to restore access to quality education by providing essential learning materials, rehabilitating schools, training teachers, and offering psychosocial support to children and educators alike.
Through this initiative, the El-Kanemi Memorial Foundation aims to bridge immediate gaps in education access while fostering long-term recovery and resilience within these communities. The project will prioritize marginalized groups, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), girls, and children with disabilities, ensuring inclusivity and equity in its approach.
Key activities include:
Distribution of educational materials to 5,000 children across the three LGAs.
Training 100 teachers on trauma-informed and inclusive pedagogy.
Rehabilitation and equipping of 30 child-friendly, gender-sensitive learning spaces.
Provision of psychosocial support services to students and teachers.
Community mobilization to promote school enrollment and retention.
At $40 USD per child and a total budget of $200,000 USD, this project will directly benefit children, educators, and communities in Ngala, Dikwa, and Nganzai. By addressing critical educational needs and building community capacity, this initiative will contribute to the broader goal of restoring stability, hope, and opportunities for conflict-affected populations.
This proposal aligns with Sustainable Development Goal 4 (Quality Education) and the Nigerian Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), reaffirming the commitment of the El-Kanemi Memorial Foundation to empower communities through education and create pathways for sustainable development.
Purpose of the Intervention:
Immediate Relief: Provide essential educational materials and psychosocial support to children and teachers to address urgent needs.
System Strengthening: Enhance teacher capacity and rehabilitate school facilities to create safe, inclusive, and conducive learning environments.
Community Engagement: Mobilize communities to overcome cultural, social, and economic barriers to education, with a focus on increasing school enrollment and retention, especially for girls and marginalized groups.
Target Population:
Primary Beneficiaries:
5,000 conflict-affected children, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), girls, and children with disabilities.
100 teachers in need of training on trauma-informed and inclusive teaching practices.
Secondary Beneficiaries:
Families and community members in Ngala, Dikwa, and Nganzai LGAs, who will benefit from improved educational opportunities and strengthened community cohesion.

1 - Needs

The education sector in Ngala, Dikwa and Nganzai LGAs faces significant challenges stemming from the protracted insurgency in Northeast Nigeria. A thorough assessment conducted by El-Kanemi Memorial Foundation (EMF) in collaboration with local stakeholders has identified the following critical needs that the proposed project seeks to address:1. Lack of Access to Learning Materials• Many children lack basic educational resources, including textbooks, notebooks, pencils, and school uniforms, making it difficult for them to engage effectively in learning.• Teachers also lack adequate teaching aids and materials, further hindering the delivery of quality education.Activity to Address Need:• Provision of essential learning materials for 5,000 children to facilitate effective learning and classroom participation. 2. Limited Teacher Capacity• Teachers in the region often have minimal training, especially in trauma-informed teaching and inclusive education methods.• Many teachers are unable to address the complex needs of children who have experienced trauma or displacement.Activity to Address Need:• Training 100 teachers in trauma-informed and inclusive teaching practices, enabling them to provide better support for students. 3. Inadequate and Unsafe Learning Environments• School buildings in many areas have been damaged or destroyed due to conflict, leaving children to study in overcrowded or makeshift spaces.• A lack of gender-sensitive facilities, such as separate latrines, discourages attendance, especially among girls.Activity to Address Need:• Rehabilitation and equipping of 30 learning spaces to create safe, child-friendly, and gender-sensitive environments. 4. Psychosocial and Emotional Support• Many children have experienced trauma from conflict, displacement, and the loss of loved ones, which affects their ability to focus and learn.• Teachers also face emotional burnout and stress, impacting their teaching effectiveness.Activity to Address Need:• Provision of psychosocial support services to children and teachers through trained counselors, including group therapy sessions and individual counseling. 5. Low Enrollment and Retention Rates• Cultural barriers, poverty, and a lack of awareness about the importance of education contribute to low school enrollment and high dropout rates, especially among girls.• Many parents prioritize short-term survival needs over education, seeing little value in formal schooling.Activity to Address Need:• Community sensitization campaigns to raise awareness about the importance of education, with a focus on girls’ education.• Formation of Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) to engage parents and guardians in school activities and decision-making. 6. Marginalized Populations (IDPs and Children with Disabilities)• Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and children with disabilities face significant barriers to accessing education, including discrimination and lack of tailored support.Activity to Address Need:• Ensuring inclusivity in all project activities by prioritizing IDPs and children with disabilities in the distribution of resources and school enrollment campaigns. ConclusionThe proposed project directly addresses these critical needs through targeted activities that not only resolve immediate gaps but also strengthen the education system in Ngala, Dikwa and Nganzai LGAs for long-term sustainability. By aligning with the specific challenges identified during assessments, the EMF ensures that this initiative is both impactful and responsive to the unique context of the region.

2 - Activities

The El-Kanemi Memorial Foundation proposes a comprehensive education initiative to restore access to quality education for 5,000 conflict-affected children in Ngala, Dikwa, and Nganzai LGAs. This project aligns with the Education in Emergencies (EiE) Working Group Sector’s priorities and aims to address critical gaps in access, infrastructure, teacher capacity, and psychosocial support. Key Activities and Justifications:1. Distribution of Educational Materials:• Activity: Provide textbooks, notebooks, uniforms, and teaching aids.• Target: Equip 5,000 children and teachers with essential learning materials.• Justification: Lack of resources hinders learning; this activity ensures students are better prepared.2. Teacher Training on Trauma-Informed Pedagogy:• Activity: Train 100 teachers on trauma-sensitive and inclusive teaching methods.• Target: Ensure 80% of teachers are equipped to support children affected by trauma.• Justification: Builds teacher capacity to handle complex needs, improving classroom outcomes.3. Rehabilitation of Learning Spaces:• Activity: Repair 30 classrooms with child-friendly furniture and gender-sensitive facilities.• Target: Create safe, inclusive environments for 5,000 children.• Justification: Damaged infrastructure reduces attendance; improved spaces encourage learning.4. Provision of Psychosocial Support Services:• Activity: Deliver group therapy, counseling, and recreational activities.• Target: Improve emotional well-being for 60% of students and teachers.• Justification: Trauma impacts learning; psychosocial support fosters resilience.5. Community Mobilization for Enrollment:• Activity: Conduct awareness campaigns to promote education, especially for girls.• Target: Achieve a 20% increase in enrollment and reduce dropout rates by 15%.• Justification: Addresses cultural and economic barriers to education, promoting inclusion. Modality and Innovation:Activities will use community-based approaches, leveraging local stakeholders for implementation. Technology, such as e-resources and mobile apps, will support teacher training and real-time monitoring. Rehabilitation will prioritize gender-sensitive and child-friendly designs. Convincing Details:This intervention aligns with SDG 4 and the Nigerian Humanitarian Response Plan. It ensures inclusivity, prioritizing girls, IDPs, and children with disabilities, while building long-term resilience through local capacity development. The cost-effective design ($40 per child) provides immediate relief and lays a foundation for sustainable education recovery in Ngala, Dikwa, and Nganzai.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming throughout the project, the following modalities will be implemented: 1. Safe and Inclusive Access • Schools and learning spaces will be rehabilitated to be child-friendly, gender-sensitive, and disability-inclusive, ensuring all children, including girls and children with disabilities, can access education safely. • Project activities will prioritize marginalized groups, such as internally displaced persons (IDPs), girls, and children with disabilities, using vulnerability-based selection criteria. 2. Do No Harm Approach • All interventions will be designed and implemented to avoid exacerbating existing vulnerabilities or creating new risks, particularly for women, children, and other at-risk groups. • Security assessments will be conducted in collaboration with local authorities to ensure that project sites are safe for staff, beneficiaries, and communities. 3. Community Engagement and Feedback Mechanisms • Community members, including women, youth, and vulnerable groups, will be actively involved in planning, decision-making, and monitoring to promote ownership and accountability. • Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be established, ensuring that concerns related to protection risks are addressed promptly. 4. Training and Capacity Building • Teachers will be trained in trauma-informed and inclusive pedagogy to ensure they can respond appropriately to the psychosocial and protection needs of children. • Project staff and partners will receive training on protection principles, including child safeguarding, gender-based violence (GBV) prevention, and how to handle sensitive cases. 5. Referral Pathways • Strong referral mechanisms will be established to connect children and community members to appropriate services for psychosocial support, GBV, or other protection-related needs. 6. Risk Mitigation in Implementation • Activities will be timed and conducted in a manner that minimizes risks, such as avoiding long travel distances for children and ensuring security measures during distributions and school activities. These modalities will align with global protection standards and integrate the principles of safety, dignity, accountability, and non-discrimination in all project phases.
