Project Summary

WFP Zimbabwe, in partnership with UNHCR and jointly-selected partner Terre Des Hommes (TDH), is currently supporting more than 13,000 refugees in Tongogara Refugee Camp (TRC) with monthly food assistance. The refugees are mainly from DRC (72.3%), Mozambique (13.7%), Burundi (6.04%) and Rwanda (5%), with others from the Horn of Africa including Somalia, Ethiopia and South Sudan. TRC the only refugee camp in Zimbabwe, located in Chipangayi, Chipinge District in Manicaland Province, about 500 kilometres South East of the capital, Harare and approximately 80km west of Chipinge town. The camp was established in 1981 to cater for an influx of refugees from Mozambique during its civil war. The number of refugees residing at TRC has consistently increased, with a sharp rise from 5,000 in 2015 to the current 13,000. The population is expected to grow further in 2019, considering the rising tensions in DRC.
The camp measures approximately 800ha, with a possible maximum of 1,000ha available for current use and planning requirements, according to Government of Zimbabwe (GOZ).The camp is in natural region 5, characterized by temperatures that in mid-summer can average more than 40 degrees Celsius, with mean annual rainfall of less than 450mm/year and of a sporadic nature that is not suitable for growing the staple maize crop. The camp experiences cycles of floods and droughts and can go through an entire season without rain; the nearby Save River becomes an important water resource for agricultural-based livelihoods.
The GOZ and UNHCR manage the camp, and work with other partners to ensure basic needs and protection of rights of concerned population. In line with the global agreement, WFP is the main partner managing and providing food assistance for refugees at the camp since 2015; accordingly, it has built up substantial capacity to run the programme in collaboration with partners. The refugees are fully reliant on WFP’s food assistance for survival.
WFP provides monthly food assistance of the resident refugees, through cash-based transfers, sufficient to meet 100 percent of the daily energy requirements of an adult person. Chronically ill people also receive a top-up of Super Cereal through camp health facilities, enabling them to consume up to 2,380 kcal per day. The new arrivals at the camp receive an in-kind transfer for the first three months to cover the gap between their arrival at the camp and the completion of their registration, after which they are entitled to the regular cash-based assistance.
WFP has been implementing cash transfers at the camp since 2016. The cash-based assistance has empowered the persons of concern to buy food commodities of their choice furthering their dignity and opportunity to access food, increasing diversity of food consumption and stimulating local markets. It has also empowered some of them towards pursuing other livelihoods options.
The refugee population is continuously increasing by an average of 150 persons every four weeks. This is in addition to the already existing 450 caseload of people seeking asylum. Following this trend, Zimbabwe is likely to experience a wave of new asylum seekers, with the numbers reaching 20,000 by December 2019. WFP immediately requires US$1,765,248.00, to provide the much-needed food assistance to the refugees for approximately six months, from January to June 2019.
WFP proposes to respond through its CSP Activity 1.2 Support to Refugees, to ensure that the food-insecure refugees in TRC are enabled to meet their basic food and nutrition requirements. Support to refugees contributes to SDG target 2.1 (ending hunger and ensuring access to food) as well as SDG target 2.2 (ending all forms of malnutrition). This contribution will assist WFP to pursue the objective to improve food access to vulnerable refugees through the provision of cash-based transfers and in-kind commodities.

Needs

In order to ensure food assistance to the refugees at TRC, WFP requires $1,765,248.00. With the requested resources, WFP will provide assistance to an estimated 14,650 (maximum planned) refugees for six months through a combination of cash transfers and in-kind food assistance. Of the total amount requested, $1,718,226.00 will be channelled through cash assistance with cash transfers valued at $13 per person per month, equivalent to a complete food basket consisting of maize meal, pulses, vegetable oil, sugar, and salt to meet a person’s full daily energy requirements based on 2,100kcal per day diet. Furthermore, approximately 250 chronically ill people will also receive a top-up of Super Cereal through camp health facilities, enabling them to consume up to 2,380 kcal per day. In addition, an average of about 600 new arrivals per month will continue to receive in-kind assistance made up of cereals, pulses and vegetable oil until they acquire refugee status – a process which takes three months– while the chronically ill will as previously noted receive an additional Super Cereal ration. The following quantities will be procured for the new arrivals: 43 MT cereals, 2.7 MT vegetable oil and 7.2 MT pulses and for the chronically ill 4.5 MT Super Cereal will be procured. Accordingly, $47,022.00 of the requested funding will be utilized to procure the in-kind commodities. Activities

WFP will cater for different needs of the refugees based on their vulnerability, expectation of return and existing social/human capital. The major activities include the following: • Prevention of malnutrition for an average of 250 of the most vulnerable refugees per month. Chronically ill refugees receive highly nutritious Super Cereal rations on a monthly basis through the health clinic in the camp. • Provision of food rations for an average of 600 newly arriving refugees per month. These new arrivals receive in-kind rations during the first three months of their stay at the camp consisting of 2,100 kcal, which includes 12 kg of cereal, 2 kg of pulses, 0.75 kg of vegetable oil. After three months individuals transition into the cash-based assistance.• Provision of cash transfers to an average of 14,050 refugees per month with each receiving US$13.00 per month/person. Furthermore, WFP on a monthly basis will carry out independent and joint monitoring activities to ensure compliance with set guidelines and provide technical support in the operational areas. Mobile vulnerability analysis and mapping will also be used to collect food security data from households. TDH in consultation with Refugees Leadership at the camp collects food prices and other market-related data. Both allow access to high frequency, near real-time data to monitor household food security and market trends, with the view of informing effective, targeted responses. WFP’s M&E system will ensure efficient programming is tailored to capture progress in terms of outcome and output achievements. It further tracks consumption of commodities and use of CBT, outputs and outcomes of key indicators revealing the food and nutrition status of individuals and communities. WFP will comply with the corporately agreed-upon reporting obligations using the Annual Country Report. WFP has built a strong strategic partnership with the Government of Zimbabwe (DSW), UNHCR, TDH and service provider SECURICO to help with food assistance through cash transfers. Despite the elevated economic challenges compounded by poor liquidity in the country which has resulted in increases in inflation, prices in TRC have remained relatively stable because the cash is distributed in USD. The camp has operational markets and shops where a wide range of commodities are available. WFP, UNHCR and TDH continuously monitor prices at the camp to determine the transfer value and ensure that it remains relevant. WFP remains committed to ensure that the Refugees Programme is safe, dignified and accountable to the people.
