Project Summary

GOAL will support the provision of comprenhensive health & nutrition services in 21 Primary health
facilities across South Kordofan (Diling, Habila, Abukarshola, Rashad, and Talodi localities inlcuidng support for Stabilization Center in Talodi and Habila) and 18 clinics in North Darfur (Kutum, Al Waha, and Umbaro localities), in areas with high numbers of con􀂧ict-affected households (including long-term IDPs, vulnerable host communities, and some returnees. GOAL will conitnue support to rural and hard-to-reach facilities supported during 2020 and expand in North Darfur to provide some services in Umbaro locality. Services include Reproductive Health, EPI, early warning and
outbreak response, support and training for health staff, clinic running costs, provision of
drugs, medical supplies and equipment, and support for the Locality Health Department,
including the Health Information System. GOAL also provides community health through
Care Groups, community health promotion, defaulter tracing for RH and OTP services, and
the Nutrition Impact and Positive Practice (NIPP) approach to prevent and treat Moderate
Acute Malnutrition. GOAL will ensure comprehensive care in supported facilities, and GOAL will continue to
closely coordinate through the Ministry of Health and the Health and Nutrition Sectors in El Fashir,
Kadugli and Khartoum, and will also engage in ongoing coordination with other partners
in each locality to ensure no overlap. Nutrition services aimed at preventing and treating
MAM will also be expanded to Kassala & Gedarif states through the Nipp Technical
Advisory Services (NTAS).

1 - Needs

Kutum & Alwaha localities remains a fragile context with recent history of conflict, remaining estimated 48,000 IDPs living in the locality, and according to the HeRAMS in 2018, 74% of the functional health facilities are those supported by GOAL. GOAL is the only international healthcare, nutrition and WASH service provider in Kutum and Al Waha localities, providing health care services to 56% of the population of Kutum and Al Waha, and ensuring over 80% of the EWARN sites. The 2020 HIS data from Kutum & Al Waha show that communicable diseases (malaria, diarrhea, and ARI) make up 66% of total morbidities consulted. The persistence of poor nutrition, sanitation and hygiene contributes to these morbidities and to malnutrition. A 2019 MICS survey found only 34% of respondents washing hands at critical times, and only 27.6% of children age 6-24 months ate 4+ food groups (WHO's minimum standard for this age). Findings from a Barrier Analysis that was conducted among both Doers (people washing hands at appropriate times) and Non-Doers showed that lack of water and soap as an obstacle for 54% Non-Doers and `washing hands before preparing food' is the most difficult key moment to remember or do. Perception concerning the severity or risk of family members becoming ill with diarrhoea if the respondent does not wash hands with soap or ash and water at all of the key times defined as before eating, after defecation, before cooking, after handling child faeces, before child feeding came up as a determining factor for practicing the behaviour. In early 2019, GOAL-supported clinics and EWARN reporting identified and responded to a potential measles outbreak, with six suspected (3 confirmed) measles cases in Goba clinic. These cases were caught early and any spread stopped, but other areas of North Darfur suffered at least 38 confirmed cases. For EPI, the coverage for Penta 3 is 76.41% which is an improvement from 62% in 2017 while that of measles was 74.97% up from 65% in 2017 in Kutum and Al Waha localities; despite this improvement the rates still fall short of the 90% target that is set by the MoH in Sudan.The health status of the 30,600 population in Rashad remains critical according to data collected by GOAL in March 2020. The HIS at the locality health department indicates high levels of morbidity with ARI at 40%, Malaria 20% and diarrheal diseases at an equal high of 20% hence leading to very low health outcomes. These three categories constitute the highest burden of morbidity in Rashad. Immunization coverage and RH indicators remain low. At 63.8% for example, measles vaccinations remain significantly below the 95% level required to confer herd immunity on the population and set as the SPHERE minimum standard, leaving communities vulnerable to outbreaks. Talodi hosts 13,968 IDPs among a population of 48,862. An additional 30,000 gold miners travel and live there for work, and mistrust between the population, mining companies, and government remains high as evidenced by strikes there in 2019. IDPs and vulnerable populations are concentrated in often unsanitary conditions near Talodi town. The catchment population where GOAL supports 3 health facilities and a Stabilization Center is 21,327 people. Data from the locality health department indicated that just like Rashad, Malaria, diarrhea and ARI were the main morbidities, and children under 5 are particularly affected. Among all diarrhea cases, under-5 children represented 63.1%; for malaria cases 48.8%, and for ARI cases 52.3%. With a population of 27,928, Habila locality was severely lacking in health care services and staff, with primary health facilities barely functional, without basic staff or drugs prior to GOAL’s intervention. ARI, Malaria and diarrhea constitute the highest burden of morbidity in Habila comprising 36%, 33.7% and 33.3% respectively of morbidities reported.

2 - Activities/Outputs

- Comprehensive primary health service provision at 18 clinics in North Darfur and 21 inSouth Kordofan - Ensure provision of RH services at all supported clinics - Training andsupport for health staff in all supported - Support vaccination campaigns in all supportedclinics - Ensure EWARN reports are submitted and that supported localities haveemergency response plans - Provide drugs and medical supplies for supported healthfacilities - Conduct community health promotion and defaulter tracing for EPI, OTP andANC - Implement Care Group and School Health Club approaches to improve communityhealth-seeking behavior especially among families with young children, and reachingadolescent boys and girls with health messages. - Provide CMAM services by supportingOTPs in 17 facilities in North Darfur and 24 in South Kordofan for SAM cases, includingcovering staff costs as needed - Support rehabilitation, equipment, and supplies for OTPsites - Support the Stabiliation Center in Talodi town to be fully functional - ImplementNIPP Circles to prevent and treat MAM as part of CMAM, integrating male and femalecircles to improve gender roles in CMAM - MUAC screenings and rolling out the "MUAC byMothers" approach - Support referrals of SAM with Complications and EMoC (RH) toKutum Hospital - Support LHD for monitoring and supervision visits to clinics and OTPsites - Training, equipment, and supplies for 9 localities in North Darfur to implement DHIS- Training of State Ministry of Health on NIPP approach and support for NIPP scale-up -GOAL also integrates WASH into its health & nutrition programme - COVID-19 prevention measures and in clinics and communities, and ongoing support to some rural isolation centers, referral of cases, transportation of samples, surveillance and RRT logistical support
