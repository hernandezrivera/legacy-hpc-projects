Project Summary

The project specific objectives are as follows:
Support the productive capacity of farmers in Eastern Khan Younis and East Gaza through the provision of sustainable means of accessing water
Improve access of farmers households (HH’s) to food security
The project aims to contribute to the Country Strategic Objective 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
More specifically, the project aims to relieve vulnerable farmers from their losses due to the negative impact of the electricity crisis by providing immediate cash assistance to farmer Households (HHs) in east Khan Younis and east Gaza, install solar panel power systems for 15 water wells in order for farmers to effectively irrigate their vegetable crops. It also aims to increase farmers’ (women and men) capacities in improving adaptation strategies with the obstacles facing the sector, in addition to increasing awareness on women rights and women engagement in the agricultural sector. The targeted area is selected based on a detailed needs assessment conducted by Oxfam’s partner MA’AN Development Centre, the area being the primary source of vegetables and the area containing the most affected farmers.
The project contributes to Food Security Sector objectives 1 and 3 by enhancing the productive capacity of farmers, restoring and helping sustain their livelihoods and ensuring and sustaining their food security access. The project has been designed to ensure environmental sustainability, gender protection, and other protection concerns that are accounted for and addressed within the socio economic context of Gaza.

Needs

The poverty level in Gaza is four times higher than in the West Bank and female headed HH’s in Gaza experience a poverty rate of 1.3 times higher than those of males. Adding to the already deteriorating situation in Gaza, the funding cuts imposed on the PA and UNRWA by the US Government will affect the delivery of emergency assistance to vulnerable families. The Great March of Return demonstrations taking place in Gaza have also contributed to higher food insecurity due to the damage of 870 dunums and the large number of injuries and deaths. In October 2017 MA’AN conducted a detailed assessment in the areas targeted to identify irrigation water wells to be operated by solar energy. The assessment targeted agricultural areas that are most affected and in coordination with the MoA and farmers’ associations 15 wells were nominated depending on technical factors and socio-economic vulnerabilities. 824 farmers and 4512 dunums in these areas are completely reliant on water pumped from these 15 wells. The power outage disrupted the irrigation process by affecting the water quantities pumped from these wells which are allocated for irrigation, causing a drop from 6400 m3 per day to 3200 m3. Many farmers reported that they could not afford the irrigation water which led to the destruction of some of their plants and a total of 544 dunums of farmland were abandoned as a result. This affected the ability of farmers to access basic food needs, which is exacerbated for women and girls due to deep gender discrimination such as limited access to productive resources (land, labour, fertilizer, credit, technology, extension and markets), which negatively affects the food availability of female headed HH;s and gender discrimination in allocation of HH resources. For example, it was reported that women in the HH tend to eat less in order to prioritise children and elderly. Although women and men have complementary yet different roles in agriculture, men have more access to the benefits emerging from those activities. Women play a fundamental role in agriculture, representing 65% of these activities, yet 48% of these women do not receive paid salaries for their work. Moreover, women’s role is seen as a supportive one to their husbands and merit is often to denied to those women due to prevailing norms and attitudes. In addition to working in the agricultural sector, women carry the additional burden of domestic and reproductive duties. Vulnerabilities, food insecurity and agricultural work are therefore deeply gendered in Gaza, generating a devastating impact for women and girls. In September 2018 Oxfam carried out a Vulnerability and Risk Assessment (VRA) in Beit Lahia and Beit Hanoun areas. In addition to unanimously identifying the overarching root problem; the Israeli blockade, many of the issues prioritized by the community group representing the two areas including Um El Nasser were related to the agricultural sector. The findings from the assessment reflect the wider agricultural situation in Gaza, as affirmed by the MoA. The issues were debt, lack of compensation to affected farmers, agricultural land bulldozing, urbanisation and increase of population density, and water aquifer and seawater pollution, the lack of accountability of local actors and finally and the PA salary cuts. The respondents proposed cash generating activities for youth, women and men to protect their HHs from negative coping strategies. Also, the MoA confirmed the need to further invest in rehabilitation of lands and greenhouses, improving access to water and injecting cash among farmers.Furthermore, according to Oxfam’s 2018 Post-Crisis Market Analysis, multiple layers of overwhelming debt in a system of credit were identified with the average debt standing between 2000-5000 ILS / 540-1360 USD with an average monthly salary of 410 NIS. Borrowing money or food is hence one of the only ways that many HH’s in Gaza are able to survive.

Activities

The project will target male farmers and female headed HH’s in order to address the gendered impact of food insecurity and poverty by reducing negative coping strategies among different age groups and women. The project will improve the water access to vulnerable farmers by providing alternative source of energy and will target beneficiaries through capacity building on women’s rights with a specifi c focus on division of labour and care duties, taking into consideration age groups who perpetuate negative perceptions of female engagement in agriculture, based on Oxfam’s resilience theory of change. Activity 1: Provision of unconditional cash transfers to 250 vulnerable farmers HHs which will benefi t approximately 1400 individuals. This will target those who are most affected by the crisis and are vulnerable to crop devastation and acute income loss. Cash will support farmers to effectively irrigate their vegetable crops. 1.1 Conduct vulnerability assessment on a pool of farmers nominated through MoA in east Khan Younis and east Gaza 1.2 Select 250 farmers (50 of them women) based on food insecurity criteria that includes scores of CSI and HFIAS, socio economic indicators, protection concerns, prioritizing female headed HH and female farmers in coordination with MoA and cross checking with the FSS. Female beneficiaries can be farmers, laborers or food processors. 1.3 Hold orientation sessions with 250 farmers to make them aware of the activities and the feedback mechanisms in place 1.4 Transfer unconditional cash to 250 farmer HHs over three months installments, the transfer methodology will be consulted with Cash WG/FSS including MoA Activity 2: Installation of solar energy systems in 15 water wells east of Khanyounis and east of Gaza City directly bene ting at least 824 farmers, approximately 4614 individuals. 2.1 Conduct technical assessment and a bill of quantities for the 15 water wells nominated through MoA 2.2 Tender solar energy systems 2.3 Wells solar energy pumping works implemented 2.4 Carry out baseline and end line surveys ( first 2 months and last two months) 2.5 Handover to community and operation of wells based on the newly installed system Activity 3: Considering MoA resources and plans, capacity building for farmers on safe agricultural practices, adaptation practices and women rights will be delivered. Activity 3.1 800 farmers register for training (to include 100 women) Activity 3.2 Hold 10 training sessions on adaptation practices, safe agriculture and women’s rights provided for 800 farmers The 250 farmers and their HH members may benefit from the water wells rehabilitation process in addition to the cash assistance but are not accounted for to avoid double counting. Activity 1& 3 BNFs will be selected from a pool of farmers identified by MA’AN's assessment and in coordination with MoA and the FSS to select the most affected and vulnerable farmers. BNFs of Activity 2 are identified by MA’AN’s assessment where 15 water wells were identified with their respective owners and users.

Indicators

Oxfam is committed to gender-sensitive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) across all its programs and projects which ensure equal access to Oxfam project services for males and females from all age groups. Oxfam also developed a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability which forms the basis of M&E systems for all Oxfam projects. In the case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies will be applied. The CAMSA approach comprises how gender-relevant needs and issues are monitored during the implementation and how project reviews and evaluations take gender sensitive issues into serious consideration. All tools, data gathering, data analysis, reporting, recommendations and action points are gender and age disaggregated where required. Throughout the project, MEAL will be undertaken in accordance with a comprehensive M&E plan which will track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe, and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by age and using different tools such as questionnaires, Key Informative Interviews (KII), stories of change, and focus group discussions (FGDs). In terms of accountability, Oxfam launched its Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) in September 2018 which provides wider access for diverse and vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts and concerns, provide feedback and submit complaints in a safe and private manner. This system uses ICT for receiving feedback either by an online link, verbal feedback or recording complaints received by other channels such as the hotline and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers the received feedback to the relevant program staff and will be monitored by Oxfam MEAL Officer.
