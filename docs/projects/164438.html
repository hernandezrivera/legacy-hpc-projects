Project Summary

BINAA will provide humanitarian life-saving shelter and NFI assistance to IDPs and host community HHs in Afrin, Al Bab, A'zaz, Jarablus, Jebel Saman, Harim, Idleb and Ras Al Ain districts. The locations are prioritized as per the HNO 2020 data and according to prioritization of BINAA to the location as per the field team’s communication with the local councils and their field visits to some villages. BINAA will provide shelter repair and functional upgrades valued at an average of $650for 1300 HHs (7,800 individuals) (1673 Men, 1759 Women, 2143 Boy, 2225 Girl). The repair works will enable the target communities to use local materials and work through local contractors to improve their living conditions, fostering a sense of ownership, responsibility, and resilience while supporting local markets. We will also rehabilitate 25 collective centers accommodated by 416 HHs (2,500 individuals) (537 Men, 564 Women, 687 Boy, 713 Girl) and providing safe and dignified housing conditions. valued at an average of $12,000 for each CC
BINAA will provide emergency winter NFI assistance by Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs includes winter clothes kit to 2000 HHs (12,000 individuals) (2574 Men, 2706 Women, 3297 Boy, 3423 Girl) in the targeted locations, and Provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs to 6000 HHs (36,000 individuals) (7722 Men, 8118 Women, 9891 Boy, 10269 Girl) in the targeted locations, and In line with the Do-No-Harm principle, BINAA will include IDPs and host community HHs as beneficiaries where and when applicable.

4 - Needs

With continuing conflict creating repeated displacements and reducing coping mechanisms the shelter needs are great. The HNO 2019 states that there are now 5.65 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the capacity and remit of the SNFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. The shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental wellbeing and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria , with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5. 65 million people. 1 Of this, 3.8 million (69 percent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Raqqa city and camps in the north east. Up to one million IDPs reside in last -resort informal and spontaneous settlements. The NFI Pin has declined by 22 per cent from 2019 to 3.42 million people in 2020. This reflects both a continuing but slow improvement in market functionality and the successful efforts of the sector to distribute assistance across the country. However, this progress is fragile as many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inflation and low wages, while populations routinely report need for items outside the core NFI packages. There is still ongoing displacement, with roughly one million people expected to be displaced in 2020. This is exacerbated by the deterioration of purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their needs during harsh weather with fuel and power sources remaining in high demand.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1.1 Provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs e.g. mattress, plastic sheeting, blankets, jerry cans, kitchen sets, solar lamps; including market-based interventions for these items (6,000 Kits), BINAA will prioritize as per vulnerability criteria include length of displacement/hosting period, with families displaced/hosting for a longer period of time receiving priority; number of times of displacement and households who have not received prior NFI assistance in the previous 6 months.1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including winterization, fuel, and summerization, e.g. winter clothing for these items (2,000 kit for 2,000 families), BINAA will take into consideration the coldest area and targeted it based on SNFI priority, 1.4.1 Rehabilitation of collective centres and transitional shelters (including: in-kind, cash, voucher, physical repair, etc.) Rehabilitation of 25 collective centres, BINAA will prioritize the overcrowded centres and unfinished buildings with the intervention. BINAA will ensure that HLP guidance are considered and utilized. 2.1.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.) BINAA will repair/rehabilitate housing for 1,300 HHs will be for IDP and host community families living in an inadequate shelter that does not meet minimum SPHERE minimum standards and has inadequate privacy or protection from the elements, settlement planning and WASH facilities. We will target HHs that have light to medium damage but remain structurally sound. Works include doors and windows repair or replacement partition. BINAA will ensure that HLP guidance are considered and utilized.

Assumptions: - Locations where the project activities will be conducted are secure and accessible. - Materials/labor are/is available. Risks: 1. Losing Access to targeted location Interference from armed groups or the local councils outside the specified context. BINAA will mitigate the risk of access limitation through: • Regular monitoring for INSO security and incidents reports. • If it’s during the implementation, will look for other accessible location with beneficiaries meeting the criteria. • BINAA has regular access to the proposed locations. 2. Attacks could affect rehabilitation works. BINAA will mitigate the risk of the attacks by the following:Temporary suspension in the targeted location and re assess the security situation. • Change the targeted communities/locations in coordination with Shelter cluster and HF. 3. Community acceptance for the project. BINAA will mitigate the risk of community unacceptance by the following: • BINAA built the intervention based on needs assessments and consultations with local communities for their needs and priorities. • Visits will be held to describe the project and type of intervention and criteria before starting of implementation. • MoUs will be signed with local councils to define roles and responsibilities of the stakeholders. 4. Rareness in qualified contractors. BINAA secured qualified engineering team to ensure the quality of implementation and well definition for the requested works in the BoQ of each house. 5. The landlord will eviction the IDP from shelter after completion the rehabilitation works (HLP component ) BINAA will mitigate the risk by the following: • sign contract between the landlord and tenant •Ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of PSEA by integrating PSEA in BINAA policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA or GBV.
