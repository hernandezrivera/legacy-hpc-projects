Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen and establish a protection system through which County governmental, non-governmental, and for-profit sectors deliver sustainable, integrated, quality Gender-Based Violence (GBV) services to all host communities, IDPs and returnees targeting women, girls, men and boys in the priority 1, 2 and 3 affected location of; Pibor and Pochalla County in GPAA, Jonglei, state, Aweil North, East, South Center and Aweil West in Northern Bahr El Ghazal and Gogrial West and Twic Warrap State. Further, it will make deliberate efforts to prioritize the needs of and services provision to Persons with Disability (PWDs) and persons with extreme vulnerabilities.
ForAfrika has a long-standing footprint with established field operations in the proposed states and it will leverage other sector programs to contribute to improved safety net outcomes. Through strategic partnerships ForAfrika will capitalize on existing opportunities and platforms to make contributions to the improved safety net and mental health outcomes with full county ownership focusing on: the protection system's building, local skills strengthening and support sustainability; improving prevention, risk mitigation, and specialized GBV response service demand, access, and quality, reducing vulnerabilities created by systemic and socially reinforced barriers for most vulnerable groups including PDWs leveraging social determinants of health and FSL for improve lifesaving outcomes. Key activities will include strengthening GBV, Child protection, and PSEA referral pathways and community-based support mechanisms for identification and reporting. GBV and PSEA awareness raising for prevention, response, and risk mitigation. Provision of GBV mental health and psychosocial support services. Provision of GBV and PSEA case management and response services. Provision of dignity kits for survivors. Engagement of men and boys to address social and cultural biases that promote and facilitate GBV and child abuse. Capacity building for community-based structures for GBV and PSEA.
To ensure Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) ForAfrika will work with community based Structures to provide feedback and raise complaints during project implementation. ForAfrika already has an existing AAP mechanism including suggestion boxes, telephone line for reporting, a Help desk in existing programmes and staff designated to address AAP concerns. The project will strengthen existing platforms and establish new ones in new areas.
The team will work in partnership with the Local Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare (MGCSW) at the County and Payam levels; Non-governmental organizations providing GBV services, Allied sectors, including Justice (Women and children justice unit) and prison services; Health (particularly specific units/ services addressing violence against women & children); Police (Special Protection Units – SPUs); Defense (DRR, conflict-related violence); Education (prevention & response to violence and abuse in schools); Ministry of Labor (Child Labor)structures at County, Payam, facility, and community levels in payams in concert with Cluster and sector level strategies and investments to offer the highest quality essential services including integration into Education, nutrition, clean water, and sanitation interventions – while addressing the social determinants of health and lifesaving outcome. The project will integrate innovative interventions for effective service delivery through the public, NGO, FBO, and for-profit private sectors. The project will continue to actively promote synergy between the County and community-level service delivery, county-level Cluster coordination, and national-level technical assistance components of the Protection Implementation strategy at the cluster.

1. Needs

Humanitarian needs in South Sudan continue to rise, driven by cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflict, sub-national violence, food insecurity, climate crisis, public health challenges, and the ongoing conflict in Sudan. An estimated 9.4 million people, including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children, and 337,000 refugees, are projected to need humanitarian assistance and protection services in 2023 reflecting 76 percent of the country’s population and a 5 percent increase from 2022. The humanitarian crisis is further exacerbated by the ongoing conflict in Sudan that prompted an escalation in the cost of commodities, including food, consequently diminishing the purchasing power of the population. According to OCHA’s update of the 29, 09 2023, about 291,224 people were registered crossing into South Sudan. The influx of new arrivals; - returnees, refugees, and asylum-seekers is projected to intensify the humanitarian need by stretching the already limited resources. As of 2022 South Sudan had an index of 0.385, ranked 191 out of 191 countries recorded on HDI. The UNDP, Gender Development Index (2020) ranked South Sudan at 185/189 on gender parity. Women and girls bear the brunt of some of the most brutal gendered manifestations of the physical and food insecurity crisis. In 2024, the protection cluster projects that 5.53 million people will be in need of assistance. A 2013 Human Rights Watch report identified dowry in the form of cattle payment as being one of the drivers of child marriage – as young girls fetch a higher number of cows in exchange for marriage, increasing the asset base and overall food security of their household. Traditional gender norms prescribe women and girls the responsibility to gather food, collect water, and firewood for use, which has exposed women and girls to rape and violent abuse. GBV against women and girls is commonplace, with about 65 percent of women and girls reporting some form of physical or sexual assault in their lifetime. Women and girls continue to face threats to their safety and security arising from early marriage and sexual exploitation/GBV as they lose livelihoods and access to education, which is not prioritized by their families. About 15% of the women population particularly women, girls, and PWDs face a more severe threat to GBV. 11 % of girls/boys/women are at risk of GBV (sexual violence and forced marriage/reproduction) according to the GBV 5Ws / FSMNS+ Annual; 13 % of school-aged children dropped out in the previous school year (CP CPIMS/ FSNMS+ Annual), the majority being girls. Within the country 17 % of girls/boys/women/men do not have access to core GBV services (MA FSNMS+ Annual) at all, thus they deal with the impact of GBV on their own without any critical support. Key factors influencing GBV include underlying norms and dynamics that underpin gender inequality broadly and acceptability of the use of violence against women and girls, the legacy of conflict and violence, a breakdown of the rule of law and key security institutions to protect against violence, increases in opportunistic crime linked to high levels of poverty (World Bank, 2021). Key perpetrators are indicated as men and thus a need for male engagement in addressing GBV, promoting women’s rights, and supporting women’s access to critical reproductive health and GBV services. Children face constant exposure to violence, exploitation, neglect, and abuse takes a toll on their wellbeing and development. They are subject to instrumentalization, such as gang violence and other forms of negative coping strategies, in the absence of viable alternatives. Children and adolescents are targets of abduction, conscripted to armed groups, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence HNO 2023, youth face severe livelihood challenges due to limited income-generating activities and protection risks, including revenge killings, which limit their mobility-HNO 2023.

2. Project activities

• Train staff on GBV identification, referral, and case management. • Conduct community awareness-raising sessions, meetings, and dialogues on GBV and protection risks, available response services, and timely reporting of GBV incidences for women, men, girls, and boys. • Engagement of women, men, girls, and boys through GBV prevention initiatives (e.g. SASA, EMAP, community care programming) • Provision of specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming) to women, men, boys, and girls are • Provision of Psychosocial support (PSS) services including recreational activities to women, men, boys, and girls as individuals and groups. • Procurement and distribution of dignity kits to GBV survivors Empower GBV survivors (women and girls) with income-generating activities and fuel-efficient stoves. • Establishment of a functional GBV referral pathway in counties• Development and printing of GBV referral pathways and GBV IEC material for awareness-raising • Conduct training for non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response
