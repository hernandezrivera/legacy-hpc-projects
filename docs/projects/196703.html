Project Summary

The project aims to reach the most vulnerable group of people including Pregnant and Lactating Women and Children Under the age of Five with rapid nutrition and nutrition-sensitive lifesaving interventions of early detection, referral, and treatment of malnourished through preventative and curative activities of Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, Infant and Young Children Feeding in Emergencies, Integrated Management of Childhood illnesses, in a close linkage to the nearby Health and Nutrition centers providing Targeted Supplementary Food Program, Outpatient Therapeutic Program, and Inpatient Care services to the Acutely Malnourished cases. Moreover, a tightened involvement of awareness raising of maternal and children nutrition and health will be scaled up by the dissemination of integrated key messages of WaSH, Health education, COVID-19, and optimal feeding during the first 1000 days of the child's life throughout the deployed Nutrition BINAA Organization for Development will support the Nutrition Programs throughout the deployment of 16 Rapid Response Teams in 5 Districts, 10 Sub-Districts, 71 Communities, and 272 Camps from the high-level severity areas.
in addition to supporting 3 PHCs in 3 subdistricts. the teams will follow the MAM and SAM cases and coordinate the treatment or transportation related to these cases.
BINAA Organization through RRTs and PHCs will provide nutrition services for PLW and CU-5 as follow:
 Direct population: 170,000 individuals (PLWs and CU5)
 Children under five: 110,000 (50,000 boys, and 60,000 girls)
 Pregnant and lactating women: 60,000.
 Disabilities: 25500 individuals
the project will also include distribution of CVA assistance to 3000 families who have children under 2 years old to enhance the nutrition of the children and the mother( from households who are receiving in-kind food assistance through integrated project under FSL sector)
BINAA's previous experience in CVA activities:
BINAA has implemented many CVA projects under many sectors through cash and E-voucher modalities that include the following (providing cash for rent to IDP families, providing vulnerable families with CVA assistance against hygiene items, providing farmers with vouchers against agricultural inputs, CFW activities under SNFI, WASH, and ER cluster)
the mentioned projects were funded by SCHF, IOM, JIZ, UNICEF, UNDP, GC, and SAMS.

3 - Needs

Based on the smart survey 2022, the Chronic malnutrition (stunting) rate was found at 24.5%. Thus, 220,000 children under 5 in Northwest Syria are stunted increasing the risk of death, disability and poor cognition and educational performance-The combined GAM rate showed a prevalence of 5% with a combined SAM rate of 1.7% Approximately 50.000 children will suffer from severe wasting in 2023. If not timely identified and treated, this children will likely die especially if compounded by medical complications including cholera-Maternal Wasting is of serious public health concern among Pregnant and Lactating Women. 1 out of 10 (11%) PLW (140,000) suffer from acute malnutrition-The prevalence of Anaemia among Women of Reproduction Age in NWS is classified as “catastrophic”. 1 out 2 (53%) women (413,990) is anemic. 1 out of 3 (36%) children (689,638 is also anemic Syria is faced with a triple burden of malnutrition- high chronic malnutrition depicted by high prevalence of stunting, 277,403 children aged below 5 years suffer from acute malnutrition. The number of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases among aged children 0-59 months increased by 45.6% - from 51,013 in 2022 to 74,296 in 2023. 138,572 children will suffer from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases (children 6-59 months) in 2023. This is a reduction of 40% from 194,188 in 2022. Approximately 356,680 (20%) Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), an increase of 34.6% in 2022 are in need of MAM treatment in 2023

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

During the implementation of project activities, BINAA staff will ensure compliance with ve key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Feedback mechanisms will also be established and announced to the targeted populations. Gender and age disaggregated data from project coverage and impact will be collected, analyzed and routinely reported on, while project activities are monitored for improvements in self-reliance as well as beneficiaries satisfaction and resilience of both women and men. The targeted population will be involved in different stages of the project management cycle, either by contacting the Key informative persons like Local council or camp management or by direct communication with beneficiaries through the ME questionnaires during baseline, end line, PDM, PIM, and the implementation and distribution monitoring activities, and through the focused meeting which will discuss the reaching consensus about the proposed activities and laying the foundations for active participation and involvement during implementation of the project and to prevent any harm may the intervention cause.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas-Mitigation: -Regular monitoring for INSO security and incident reports. -Assess the access security records for each targeted location. -BINAA has regular access to the proposed locations. Exploring different distribution modalities and plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible. -BINAA works toward a comprehensive model of security risk management based on how we enable the programs. Moving from a structure of when to leave in a security situation to how can we stay and continue programming methodology.2-Attacks may affect the vendor shops-Mitigation: -Implemented works will be reported daily by BINAA filed team -Temporary suspension in the targeted location and reassess the security situation. -Change the targeted communities/locations in coordination with clusters.3-Fraud in redeeming vouchers-Mitigation: -Using the E-voucher system will limit this risk, -Conduct daily monitoring through the dashboard and the control panel. -monitor the markets through the field team on a daily basis. -publishing the complaint posters and the complaint. -The MEAL team acts as a focal point.4-COVID19 and CholeraLiklihood: M impact: H-Mitigation: organizing the distribution to avoid gatherings and consider social distancing. Put awareness posters against COVID-19 and Cholera and conduct awareness sessions.
