Project Summary

The project will increase the resilience of IDPs and HC in areas with a large bread shortage by supplying wheat flour from Turkey to areas that suffer from the scarcity of the flour and high prices.
For two years, the SADAD organization has been distributing free bread to camps and communities in Jandairis SD, nevertheless that the distributed quantities do not meet the growing need in the area as a result of high influx of IDPs to the area, 90 % of the total population are considered IDPs, the decreased flour quantities in NWS as all and the high inflated of the SYP in the past 6 months.
The designed project aims to support bakeries with 2000 tons of local wheat flour to bake, and distribute free wheat flour bread to the most vulnerable IDPs in camps and IDPs and host residents in communities in Jandairis SD on a daily basis of 6 days a week for 8 consecutive months with an average of 250 ton per month.
The process of baking will be based on a public tender to select the bakeries with proven experience, capacity, and good reputation in the area. Besides selecting the flour supplier based on another public tender to be announced on the public newspaper, and social media.
The project will support an estimated ~7600households average of 5 persons per family estimated (36404individual) in Jandairis SD in 10 communities (Bayada-Deir Ballut-Haj Eskandar -Jalma- Kafr Safra-Mhamadia-Nisriyeh-Tal Slur-Upper Diwan-Western Eshkab-Zanda) besides 11 camps (Aljamiaa Altawniah -Alwadi- Masrat Kafar Safra- Kherbit Aloush-Tareek Alhamam-Janadirsi Entrance-Yalnquz Road-Al Muhamadyeh2- Ashkan-Sundianki-Mamal Blook Kafar Safrah)
and 12camps in AL Harim SD (Act and Impact -Ain Jaloot-Al Aasy -Al-Bayan (Qah)-Al Fateh Al Araby-Al Qalaa -Al Nawaeer(Qah)-Al Ikhlas wa Al Taqua-Al Hurriya wa Adalah-Al Fateh Al Araby-Act and Impact Extension-Farajak Yarab)
The project will target those most in need of food support and highly at risk of food insecurity and persons with disabilities regardless of their ethnic background and based on specific selection criteria, typology of the affected population (IDPs, Returnees, Host, etc) and based on vulnerability (unaccompanied children, separated children, families of children with special needs, single-headed household, pregnant and lactating women, older persons at risk, and extremely food insecure individuals)
This project will be implemented directly by Sadad staff and will provide ~7600HHs from the IDPs and Vulnerable host residents with free wheat flour bread for 8 months, Sadad will conduct the distribution through 33small distribution points at each village and camp.
The process of free bread distribution will be based on specific criteria, where families under 5 persons to receive 1 Kg of bread (1 bundle), families over 5 to receive 2 Kg of bread (2 bundles) and families over 10 will receive 3 Kg (3 bundles)

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021, the situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6% of the total population. Furthermore, all 1.7 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100% food insecure.The estimated sector PiN for 2021 - of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 9.8 million to 14.2 million. The evidence highlights an alarming deterioration, caused by a combination of factors including currency depreciation and associated loss of purchasing power, considerable hikes in food prices, and the Covid-19 pandemic.The results of the national assessment showed that 50% of Syrian households reported spending at least 75% of their income on food, while 28% are adopting crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies.Food prices in Syria have increased dramatically in 2020. WFP’s national standard reference food basket price was at SYP 99,243 (i.e. USD 79) in November 2020, which was 251% higher when compared to November 2019.Subsidized bread prices have continued to increase throughout 2020. The increase in the price of bread has been caused by the Syrian currency depreciation as well as the wheat shortage in the country. In NWS, prices of bread have also tripled for a bundle of bread and only 58% of the population’s minimum bread needs were met in 2020.Debts and credits are also a common coping strategy with 52.9% of households have taken on debt. Over 1.7 million people currently living in last resort camps are among the population groups most affected by the deteriorating food insecurity situation, where 80% of 1.5 million IDPs living in the camps are women and children.
