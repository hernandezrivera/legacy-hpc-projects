Needs

1. Notwithstanding the signing of a peace agreement in June 2015 by the different parties to the conflict, there is a continued need for humanitarian assistance in northern regions due to limited government presence and limited capacity to provide basic social services. Since mid-2016 clashes between armed groups have led to new population displacements. Insecurity has also extended into central Mali, leading to limited Government presence and control – including the ability to provide security and other basic services in several areas in the central Mopti and Segou regions.2. Despite the crucial need for humanitarian assistance, it is difficult for humanitarian personnel to provide adequate assistance to vulnerable people given the precarious security situation in central and northern Mali. While the intensity of violence is lower than at the peak of the insurgency in 2012, record levels of criminality, terrorism and armed conflict continue to hamper access. There is a continuing upward trend in security incidents affecting humanitarian workers, with 76 incidents recorded between January and April 2018 alone, almost double the number of incidents compared to the same period in 2017. The majority of incidents are reported in Timbuktu, Gao and Kidal, and increasingly in the central regions of Mopti and Ségou.3. In addition, road travel in Mali remains highly risky. Traffic to and from northern Mali is frequently affected by landmines and Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs). Since the signing of the peace agreement, access constraints in the north arising from armed group attacks and acts of banditry have increased, and several roads are controlled by armed groups, hampering access to affected communities. 4. Due to the volatile security situation as well as the vast distances and poor road conditions in northern Mali, air travel plays a critical role in facilitating humanitarian access to beneficiary populations. Currently, there are no commercial air operators in Mali that would be able to transport aid workers to remote project sites. UNHAS services play a key role in supporting the humanitarian community’s ability to implement and monitor a wide range of relief activities in some of the most remote and distressed areas of the country, and demand for UNHAS services to key destinations in Mali remains high. 5. Activities

1) Operating out of Bamako, UNHAS Mali provides scheduled flights to enable 148 NGOs, UN agencies, and donor organizations to implement and monitor projects.2) Special flights will be conducted for high level missions and in response to other specific requests3) Undertake airstrip assessments secondary airfield and other possible airfield that could be opened in the future.4) As part of efforts to further improve its service delivery, UNHAS will conduct a Provision of Access Satisfaction Survey (needs assessment) and a Passenger satisfaction survey.
