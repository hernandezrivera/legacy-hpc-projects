Project Summary

As part of ALL TOGETHER Gender-based Violence Program (GBV) in 201 9.
ALL TOGETHER plans to respond to the growing needs of conflict-affected and most
vulnerable families (women, adolescent girls, men, adolescent boys, and people with
disability) within northwest Syria by providing GBV services projects.
The projects' activities will respond to the Gender-Based Violent (GBV) survivors by
providing free GBV services within women and girls safe space (WGSS) Centers in afrin & alraee&jarablus and Azaz One center in each area & MHPSS
Centers. The response services will target (women, adolescent girls, and women and girls
with disabilities) GBV survivors.
The WGSS centers will provide GBV case management, as well as multi protection
support activities, such as recreational activities, psychosocial support, life skills, and
razing awareness sessions for the center beneficiaries.
Furthermore, the centers will allocate specific activities to target young mothers (age
under 1 8). The activities will aim to raise the young mothers aware of GBV risks, RH, and
CP issues, by using young mother club curriculum developed by UNFPA.
Moreover, the GBV outreach’s teams will work on GBV prevention activities by conducting
raising awareness sessions and GBV campaigns for men, and adolescent boys, in the
community, to increase their role in preventing GBV in the community.
Also, the outreach teams will use the awareness raising toolkits; which developed by GBV
sub-cluster by targeting men and adolescent boys in GBV awareness raising sessions.
The GBV program teams will ensure in delivering the protection information to survivors in
an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to access, using
the GBV sub-cluster SOPs.
The GBV program teams will ensure in delivering the protection information to survivors in
an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to access, using
the GBV sub-cluster SOPs.
ALL TOGETHER's GBV program will work on increasing the capacity of humanitarian
actors, as well as the protection workers, in GBV and protection topics. The GBV capacity
building component in the program will include GBV, PSS, and PFA training.

Needs

Communities in northwest Syria continued to experience volatile and unpredictablesecurity situations and displacement. According to PMTF quarterly report April-Jun1 8,indicates that communities continue to experience challenges in accessing basic needsand services, and face a variety of, and often inter-linked protection risks. Also, theGBVfocused analysis of FGDs, as well as the KI interviews conducted in 201 7 for theHumanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), identified this to be a common issue, mainlyaffecting women and girls who are trying to access aid (Voices from Syria, 201 8).Moreover, the questionnaire (MSNA); where focus group discussions were conducted with(men, women, adolescent boys and adolescent girls) has indicated that girls and boys ofall ages still face violence in multiple life situations: at home, at school, at work, and in thecommunity. Adolescence, in particular, is a time when exposure to new forms of violenceincreases. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detained and recruitedby parties to the conflict or to be involved in child labor, while girls are increasingly at riskof being recruited and used by parties to the conflict in some locations. Adolescent girlsare particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of gender-based violence,including sexual violence. As is the case globally, women and girls are disproportionatelyaffected by all forms of gender-based violence in Syria, in both the public and privatespheres. In addition, women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and womenand girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination, aswell as forced marriages. Men are particularly affected by restrictions of freedom ofmovement and forms of violence directly associated to the conduct of hostilities.Qualitative sources indicate that during situations of hostilities and displacement, personswith a disability are at increased risk of separation from their families or carers, or theassistive products which support their independence. Under the assessments part inProtection Sector DRAFT HNO 201 9 chapter (201 9) v.3 – 09-1 2.201 8; thedisproportionate impact of Gender-Based Violence on women and girls the assessmentshave indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forcedmarriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms offamily violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women andgirls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women andgirls feel safe. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, atdistribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is aconcern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psychosocial stress andlimits their movements. Additionally, various means of technology—e.g. texting and socialmedia—are used to sexually harass and facilitate early/forced marriages. The restrictionon freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services,humanitarian aid and ultimately curtails their rights, with widows who have experienceddivorce and widowhood and adolescent girls disproportionately impacted. The shame andstigma surrounding sexual violence mean that survivors often do not talk about violencewhen it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them andease the financial burden on the family. Where GBV services exist, women and girls haveconfirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with women and girls’ safespaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services(health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational andskill training).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one ofthe major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conflict in northernSyria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures:1 - Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordination andcontinuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure thesafety of staff and beneficiaries. 3- Store relief items in small quantities and multiplelocations. 4- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensified shelling 5- Daily RiskAssessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowdingof beneficiaries during activities. B-Low Quality of Programming/ This includes limitationson direct monitoring, statistical data, percentages, samples and tight data related togender, age or protection issues etc. This is the setting for a decline in program qualityunless remote management tools are accurately employed.
