Project Summary

The needs of IDPs and host communities in the BAY states are severe. Essential services including health interventions for communicable and non-communicable diseases, WASH access, supply, and hygiene, access to nutritional support for the food insecure, and protection from the risks of living in an insecure environment, among other services, are vital to save lives in Northeast Nigeria. Furthermore, the elderly and chronically sick, persons with disabilities (PWD), women and child-headed households, unaccompanied or separated children, adolescent boys and pregnant and/or lactating women (PLW) are among those facing a particular lack of access to essential services caused by protection issues in Northeast Nigeria. Pre-existing patriarchal social structures and practices make women and girls more vulnerable than their male counterparts. They face increased exposure to GBV while performing daily tasks, such as traveling to water sources, collecting firewood, and using communal latrines. This in turn makes them more vulnerable to SRH risks, including sexually transmitted infections (STIs), HIV, unwanted and unsafe pregnancies. COVID-19 and associated lockdowns, closures of schools, and restriction of movement measures have placed women, girls, and children at heightened risk of violence. Children also face serious protection concerns including physical abuse, child neglect and child labor. Moreover, many IDPs and returnees faced added vulnerabilities as they are denied free movement between states and access to basic services and employment opportunities because of a lack of civil documentation. The IRC will target all of these populations with key health, nutrition, WASH and protection services that address their emergency needs.

1 - Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict continues to exacerbate the negative effects of gender inequalities across Northeast Nigeria, with women and girls including children being disproportionately affected by the conflict and incidents of GBV. According the 2021 HNO, more than 1.5 million people—IDPs, members of the host communities, and returnees—need GBV prevention and response services across north-east Nigeria. All forms of GBV continued to be underreported. Reporting an incident of sexual violence (rape or sexual assault) is hindered by the risk of stigmatization, retaliation, shame or marginalization and oppression from the community, as well as lack of survivor-centered service provision after referrals. Physical assault, sexual violence, denial of resources opportunities and services, and psychological and emotional abuse are among the violations most frequently reported, recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners (domestic violence). Girls and boys under the age of 18 years are also at risk of GBV, mainly occurring within the context of child sexual abuse and child/early marriage. The GBVIMS 2021 mid-year report shows that women and girls were the victims of more than 99% of reported GBV incidents between January and June 2021. 70% of the survivors are adults, whilst 21% are children. Additionally, 2% of the survivors are women and girls with disabilities. On average, one in five reports of GBV alleges child survivor, and three in five reports concern the context of intimate partner violence. Overall, women, girls, children, and persons with disabilities (PWDs) remain the demographic groups most at risk of GBV in Northeast Nigeria. According to the MSNA, at least 1% of the surveyed households report having children (either gender) under 17 married off in the three BAY states. Adolescent girls, female or child-headed households, and orphaned girls living with foster carers are at particular risk of GBV. Nigeria ranks 139th out of 156 countries in the Global Gender Gap Report 2021, clearly showing the magnitude of gender-based disparities that continue to place women and girls and children in the crisis-affected region at risk. For many survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), reaching available services such as the police, the judiciary and healthcare requires an untenable investment of time, resources, and determination to overcome socio-cultural barriers.

2 - Activities

The IRC WPE team will continue to provide case management services to women and girls adolescents in need of comprehensive GBV services and referred for other services such as health, legal and livelihood services, as needed by the survivor and based on her consent. Information dissemination will be provided as well in all BAY states. IRC will continue to encourage the utilization of the GBV screening tools, at our comprehensive women centers, this is to ensure timely access to GBV services. With the support of our community structure members, the WPE team will continue to raise awareness in communities and carry out safety audits, community safety action plans, as well as referral of cases. Through Targeted Individual Assistance (TIA), IRC WPE will continue to ensure that GBV survivors are supported to receive adequate services they need which are not accessible in the community. The in-kind support can be utilized for survivors’ meals, transport, and other immediate needs. The WPE teams will ensure that existing structures and spaces meet minimum safety standards. Existing group such as WAGs, activists, Engagement Men through Accountable Practices (EMAP) group, and Discussion Group Series (DGS) group will encourage inclusion of PWDs. Start Awareness Support Action together six (6) groups will be formed using (SASA! Together) methodology which is a community mobilization approach that engages different circles of influence (Local activist-could be males or females, community leadership-could be religious or traditional leaders, youth, or women leaders, and selected institutional allies) within communities to bring about sustainable changes for preventing violence against women and girls, this methodology will require training of identified allies across communities and institutions after which they will be engaging other allies at different level step by step while measuring the impact of their engagement towards achieving sustainable change for the benefit of the community, this process is required to be implemented in phases. WPE services will ensure that minority language speaking women and girls have access to IRC services through translation and dedicated resources wherever possible. Joint activities such as community sensitizations, international events and information sharing will be strengthened and sustained. The IRC WPE will improve beneficiaries’ access to feedback mechanisms through frequent listening up sessions and from community structures engagement. Quarterly safety audit will be conducted by IRC staff through FGD, KII, and an observation method at the community as well as within the WGSS to ascertain different risks women and girls are facing in their community. Report of the audit will be included in the protection monitoring report for adequate action plans, which includes provision of risk reduction materials to women and girls base on context across the BAY state. GBV Response & Case ManagementThe IRC will work with relevant ministries (Women Affairs, Justice, and Health), local organizations and community structures in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe to ensure that women and girl survivors have safe access to quality and comprehensive case management services and if desired, psychosocial support. The WPE team will continue to work with the Health and Child Protection teams to support the needs of male adult and child survivors, in addition to women and girls. To address the gap in health services for advanced medical services for GBV, the IRC has a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) to provide referrals for these services to IRC-supported hospitals and equipped health facilities. The IRC will make referrals based on survivors’ consent to partners who have signed the GBV information management protocol and are providing the needed services. The IRC also developed a Targeted Individual Assistance (TIA) Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to provide support to GBV survivors seeking advanced medical services

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC will use listening sessions to ensure barriers preventing women and girls from accessing services and how to minimize them are discussed with women, men, boys, and girls. The IRC will identify safe and unsafe places in communities through safety audits and engage communities at the local level to identify and execute risk reduction activities. In all personal information sharing, consultation and counselling, the IRC will ensure that confidentiality and privacy are always respected in all forms in responding to GBV. All IRC existing counselling rooms have soundproof material and are set aside from WGSS meeting spaces with an alternative exit point. Both survivors and non-GBV survivors will be encouraged to visit WGSSs and CWCs to reduce stigmatization
