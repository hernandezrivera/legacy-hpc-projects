Project Summary

The AHP Bangladesh Consortium is designed to contribute to the overarching DFAT Bangladesh Rohingya and Host Community Humanitarian Package. Partners will work towards all DFAT package high-level outcomes: basic needs, self-reliance, resilience and reform. In line with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2020, RDRS Bangladesh will focus on protection and WASH, which are two of the fundamental and basic needs for refugee communities. In addition, RDRS Bangladesh also identifies a growing need to support refugees and the nearby host communities to increase their self-reliance through livelihoods activities. RDRS Bangladesh will enhance resilience of communities through environmental mitigation and Disaster Risk Reduction, social cohesion and peacebuilding initiatives. Affected communities and local partners will have increased power to make decisions, lead on activities and have a stronger voice through RDRS Bangladesh Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), which will lead to sustainable reform.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Apart from providing humanitarian support to refugees, a growing need identified to support refugees and near by host communities to increase their self-reliance through livelihoods activities. The influx of refugees has; inceased living expenses, depleted water sources, and caused massive deforestation. leading to recurrent landslides and a lack of protecttion. The projects identify rising living expenses is the most important factors fuelling the tension between the host communities and the refugees. By collaboration with Plan International and oxfam to conduct a joint need assessment to come up with preferred livelihood option from the affected population and determine livelihoods capacity building needs and also to conduct joint market analysis on the preferred livelihoods options in Teknaf and Ukhiya upazillas thus identifying feasible/profitable trade options in these areas. Through these assessment, the project identified the needs as grants support size for livelihoods options and relevant vocational, social and life-skill training. As part of rationalization process, livelihoods support grants size will be harmonized and coordinated with livelihood working group which will be formed with the participation of implementing agencies.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Before the start of the project, RDRS Bangladesh has been working in Camps 12, 2 East, and 18 with another donor-funded project that aims at expanding into the dire need for a similar intervention in that camp as conceptualized from the needs assessment with the inhabiting refugees and Camp in Charge (CIC) authority there, and also because of the close proximity of the camp to the Camp # 12 and 18 from logistics perspectives. In the project working area intervened only Moheshkhali Upazila with Cox’s Bazar (CXB) Sadar where the need for livelihood development is still very high. This Upazila is identified as the poorest one with a 40.2% poverty rate compared to the district average of 26.2%. This is the Island Upazila and most of its inhabitants depend on harvesting fish, crabs, and salts from the sea. By 2020 October, the Inter-sector Coordination Group (ISCG) completed the Joint Multi-sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) both for the Rohingya and host communities. Some of the major findings of needs from this assessment are summarized below: • Compared to 2019, access to foods and income-generating options (cash) has been mentioned more frequently by the Rohingya people in 2020; more than 50% of the households reported their access to cash as a priority needs in 2020. • More than 40% of the Rohingya households mentioned Non-food Items (NFIs) and Shelters materials as their priority • Only around 12% of pregnant/ lactating women in the camps were reportedly enrolled in nutrition-feeding programs. • Households without adult males/females of working age as well as small households and households with people with disabilities were significantly more likely to report adopting emergency/crisis coping strategies. This indicates a greater vulnerability toward livelihood shocks. • Protection concerns were very much frequent for single female-headed households and people with disability. (ISCG, 2020). 3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

RDRS Bangladesh shares the principles of “zero tolerance” and “zero tolerance to inaction” to any kind of Sexual Exploitation, abuse, or harassment against anyone, especially children and vulnerable people and this will be socialized to all staff and communities. RDRS works together with communities to develop a comprehensive risk assessment building on the detailed AHP (Australian Humanitarian Partners) Design Risk Matrix and contextualizing it to partners and specific activities, during project delivery. The risk assessment and monitoring of mitigation strategies will be reviewed regularly. The risk assessment will consider the Protection risks & safeguarding risk. These measures will also focus on: - Recruitment and screening processes for all RDRS Bangladesh staff and stakeholders. - All staffs are oriented on Code of Conduct, safeguarding, protection, gender, CFRM and CHS. - A designated safeguarding focal person is available receiving complaint or have questions.

3.2 - Gender

RDRS Bangladesh will prioritize gender equality and disability inclusion during implementation and for looking at this specialized part there assign a female-positioned Project Officer (Gender and Disability Inclusion) who will regularly follow up on the project interventions to align with gender and disability inclusion and participate in the gender inclusion-related joint working group of AHP led by Plan International to ensure that a common gender equality framework is mainstreamed. To focus on the gender theme, RDRS will ensure more than 50% of the direct participants of the project are expected to be women and will provide training on leadership and women empowerment. RDRS will advocate for Community Committees with equal gender representation from the community. RDRS Bangladesh believes that; the above issues come through gender discrimination and lack of knowledge and awareness of people. So, we also plan to improve the knowledge and awareness through different relevant event.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

This project will ensure impartiality giving priority to the most vulnerable people and making no distinctions on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class, or political opinions. The project will be considered Child Protection Guiding Principles as organizational policies. Those are- • Zero tolerance of child exploitation and abuse • Recognition of the best interest of the child • Sharing responsibility for child protection • Risk management approach • Procedural fairness The project age groups of participants as Youth (Male/ Female): 18-35, Women: 36-60, and persons with disability with no specific age limit. To assure, the staff and community safeguarding all will be liable under organizational safeguarding and PSHAE policies. Besides, the project will encourage the participation of PWD beneficiaries specially women for identifying their livelihoods options, vocational and leadership skills training and provide cash grants or IG tools/assistive device.

3.4 - Diversity

RDRS Bangladesh will be trained in disability inclusion, with CBM Australia engagement through World Vision as the disability inclusion lead. RDRS Bangladesh will share approaches, particularly through the Age and Disability Working Group. The PO (GDI) will provide technical support to partners to prioritize Persons with Disabilities (PWD) across all activities and ensure that the minimum standards for the Age and Disability Inclusion in Humanitarian Action will be maintained. There will be a particular focus on disability inclusion in the livelihood component of the RDRS Bangladesh program that, based on social demography (disability disaggregated data) analysis, is targeting up to 30% of its direct beneficiaries as PWD. RDRS Bangladesh will use the Washington Group Set of questions for disability disaggregated data collection and briefing for Community leaders, counsellors and program staff; on how to address the unique needs of children, including those with disabilities.
