Project Summary

• Cost per beneficiary: $616
• UNRWA reached 50,143 through educational activities.
In 2020, UNRWA will continue to provide quality, inclusive and equitable education services to up to 51,000 Palestine refugee children enrolled in 103 UNRWA schools located in Damascus, Rif Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Latakia and Dera’a. A number of schools still operate on a double-shift basis. Since the beginning of the conflict, the number of UNRWA-managed schools has decreased from 118 to 103 in the 2019 / 2020 scholastic year, due to access constraints and damage or destruction creating a severe for the rehabilitation of premises. UNRWA will employ approximately 1,700 teachers, including daily paid teachers to cover possible gaps created by the conflict. The Agency will also continue its Education in Emergencies (EiE) interventions, including the implementation of recreational activities, the provision of Summer Learning activities; the engagement of parents through awareness-raising sessions on violence / GBV, ERWs, and career guidance for their children; and the strengthening of the identification and management of Psychosocial Support (PSS) cases in schools; and continued capacity-building for education staff. To address the anxiety and stress generated by the trauma of conflict, group and individual PSS counselling sessions facilitated by professional counsellors will be available throughout the academic year to students in UNRWA schools. UNRWA will be executing awareness-raising sessions for parents and children on disability inclusion, violence against children, GBV and ERW; career guidance for students; and capacity development for education staff using other funding streams.
In 2020, UNRWA will work towards the resumption of educational services in newly accessible areas or areas of increased returns, including Yarmouk/Yalda should conditions allow, and Dera’a and Ein el Tal camps. Construction works will be undertaken depending on the availability of funding to ensure students, including children with disabilities, have access to safe and secure UNRWA premises. With the authorization received to carry out works in Dera’a camp, UNRWA will prioritize the reconstruction of schools in this area to ensure the provision of education services to the increasing number of returning Palestine refugees.

4 - Needs

Ensuring that UNRWA classrooms provide a safe, inclusive and enabling environment for children is imperative and requires continued efforts to build the capacity of staff on a range of topics related to education in emergency, protection, and safety and security. School supplies are needed to ensure the delivery of quality education services to Palestine refugee children, particularly since the majority of families are struggling to make ends meet. A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Depreciation of the Syrian pound, rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities. Furthermore, the conﬂict has had a devastating impact on UNRWA educational facilities. 40 per cent of UNRWA schools are currently unusable due to the conflict making essential rehabilitation and construction works on UNRWA installations a priority. Palestine refugee children have been highly affected by the conflict. Some have missed years of education due to displacement and security issues, and have been exposed to violence, loss of property, displacement and in certain cases to injury and death. This increased cases of anxiety, stress, and trauma, prompting the need for inclusive and accessible psychosocial support at the school level. It also necessitates activities such as summer learning and the provision of catch up classes.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Agency will achieve its objectives through the performance of the following activities: •Direct provision of educational services to up to 51,000 Palestine refugee students, through 103 schools (some in double shifts), including in newly accessible areas; •Provision of psychosocial support for students and staff by counsellors in all UNRWA schools; •Rehabilitation, reconstruction or maintenance of schools located in newly accessible areas and in areas severely affected by conﬂict (such as Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal camps)ensuring accessibility for children with disabilities; •Provision of support and catch up classes to students, in addition to summer learning activities, following the success of these activities in previous years; •Offering students the supplies and materials they need for learning. ;•Delivery of training to school principals, teacher and students on safety and security and protection, particularly in areas affected by the conﬂict. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has different structures in place to monitor all its programmes, and specific structures within the Education programme. There is a dedicated Area Support Team and an SFO based Programme Support Team (PSO). The PSO has a Monitoring and Evaluation system in place that closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects. The Education Programme has staff that monitor the quality of the education it provides. Monitoring is guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing male and female staff based in the field and their respective programme. The Education programme has an online database that gathers students’ data. In addition, it has established PTAs in its schools that allow the collection of feedback from parents.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Exposure to Explosive remnants of war (ERWs) as children access UNRWA schools in various locations. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. ToT training has been provided to staff including to psychosocial counsellors and teachers and widespread awareness-raising activities are being conducted with children to increase their knowledge and reduce their exposure to these risks. 2. All UNRWA school children (approximately 51,000 ) have been reached with ERW awareness sessions. 3. UNRWA also monitors the movements of Palestine refugee children and has been opening new schools based on those movements and gatherings of the said children. Protection risk: Violence against children in and around UNRWA schools. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA has been delivering training sessions to staff to address violence against children (AVAC) and to decrease violence in and around UNRWA installations, including peer to peer violence. 2. Self-care sessions are conducted with the UNRWA education staff and counselling and psychosocial support is provided to the students through UNRWA school counsellors and UNRWA PSS workers. 3. UNRWA addresses cases of violence against children from staff members using available investigative and sanctions regimes. Protection risk: Pressure from caregivers and the community to rely on potential negative coping mechanisms occasioned by the crisis like child marriage, child labour leading to drop out of school. Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigating measures: UNRWA conducts targeted activities and awareness campaigns on disability inclusion, child labour and child marriage along with a variety of activities aimed at fostering a better understanding on the rights of children, including the right to education, and also aimed at reducing the longer-term impact that the crisis has had on the mental health of children and the adult community members.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A mix of humanitarian and post-conflict interventions: Whilst Damascus, Rural Damascus and most of the major urban concentrations in Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Dera’a experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities continued in other areas of the country and the overall situation remained tense and unstable. In 2020, the Agency anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conflict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the different needs of Palestine refugees across Syria. High levels of humanitarian needs especially amongst the most vulnerable Palestine refugees: A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Depreciation of the Syrian pound, rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities, particularly for those most vulnerable.Increase in returns; a large proportion of Palestine refugees in Syria will remain displaced: the levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high among Palestine refugees, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted and the level of destruction there is significant. Returns to Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, which have sustained major destruction, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns from out of Syria in 2020 will increase, as will HLP and CD needs. Secondary displacements is also anticipated among Palestine refugees due to their legal status. Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to face serious protection challenges: It is anticipated that protection risks will persist and may intensify in newly accessible areas where spontaneous returns, both from within and outside of Syria, are expected to increase.
