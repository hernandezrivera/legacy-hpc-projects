Project Summary

This project targets IDPs, community groups and enterprises founded in Idleb and Aleppo governorate as AlBab, Azaz, Afrin sub-districts in Aleppo and Idleb, Salqin, Maraat Tamasrin,Dana, and Ariha sub-districts in Idleb . The project’s main objective is to strengthening resilience of them against exacerbating living conditions related to both conflicts and floods in where economic deterioration and its effects such as income loss, reduced purchasing power are severely experienced. Through the provision of livelihood opportunities consisting of integrated interventions, the project aims to support ER efforts and community revitalization; It will contribute to rehabilitation and restoration of disrupted livelihoods, providing skills, income opportunities through three main interventions.
By pursuing the main objective referred above, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
1. Local small and home-based enterprises and related activities are built up with financial, in-kind supports and restorations
2. Access to given term livelihoods opportunities involving CFW both in restoration works
3. Support livelihood& empowerment embedded Local small businesses and entrepreneurs

4 - Needs

In NWS, the number of people living in IDPs sites reached 1.5 million . In 2021, heavy rainfall and flooding further increased the needs of communities where CCCM Cluster reported, more than 152,700 IDPs were affected, with 27,700 tents destroyed or damaged. The cluster estimates that 65% of HHs reporting insufficient income in Aleppo and 80% in Idleb. According to HNAP in January 2021; %98 of individuals is in extreme poverty, %83 of HHs reported their income is insufficient , %66 of HH suffer lack of employment opportunities. MWL conducted RNA in Dana sub-district using 3 main methodologies: Personal Interviews with Key informants (KII), and FGDs on voluntary basis. MWL targeted 23 camps and their surrounding communities (16). Based on RNA, 76% of adults are unemployed and 75% of the families earn less 100,000 SYR monthly. 80% of KI said that people can only afford about less than 25% of their basic needs. Recent floods and pandemic affected the local manufacturing where 39% suffered from reduction in profits, 26% experience difficulties in securing raw materials, 23% are unable to sell products, & 21% had to shut down their businesses. In addition, 81% of participants have ideas for micro, small, and home-based enterprises. Based on RNA, MWL identified facilities that need rehabilitations such centeral markets, mills, slaughterhouses, local shops. In terms of entrepreneurial , 93% of enterprises owners said that will receive trainees and in need support in 41% logistics, 30% wages where the average wage for unskilled worker is 18 TL and skilled is 26 TL. For Vocational Trainings (VT), 93% said that trainings are not available, and the most important VT are handicraft, sewing, and foodstuff production including dairy.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will address the needs identified in the previous section through an interconnected and integrated set of activities, as below. -Provide CfW activities for local population and IDPs, which will contribute to the rehabilitation of essential socioeconomic infrastructures and services, and thereby also promote social cohesion between IDP and local populations as a step towards recovery. - Rehabilitate community and public infrastructures, destroyed markets, mills, slaughterhouses as a means to help recover the local economy/businesses which also paves way for sustainable supply of livelihood opportunities for local communities. - Provide for technical/entrepreneurial capacity-building and small grants to businesses, both startups and existing ones, to help them initiate and restore their livelihoods. As mentioned above, the lack of livelihoods is one of the biggest challenges in northwest Syria. In order to restore and create livelihood opportunities, the proposal aims at strengthening the purchasing power of the individuals in the targeted locations by a tailor-made set of activities defined by the needs and specificities of each population group, focusing on the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities to enhance both affordability and access to NFI/health/water related goods and services.The project will focus on supporting small businesses and beneficiaries’ economic empowerment, that have been affected by the conflict, COVID-19 crisis and recent floods.
