Project Summary

PIN plans to respond to WASH needs in Northern Syria by delivering humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most the vulnerable people. PIN will focus on both distributions of essential WASH NFIs and hygiene promotion and promoting nature-based and environmental solutions under WASH like wastewater treatment plants and improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/emergency WASH facilities and services. Interventions will primarily target newly displaced/returned populations in informal settlements, camps, and host communities, based upon coordination with relevant partners to maximize response coverage and minimize gaps. Technical design and proposed level of services provided through these interventions will follow standards such as SPHERE and those established by the WG and will consider varying needs among different groups in the community, such as women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Capacity building for local councils and water departments will be provided by PIN staff through field coaching sessions focusing on operational aspects and management of water systems and supporting water departments with maintenance and spare parts for water stations and rehabilitating networks. PIN is planning to implement WASH activities in schools and health facilities, taking into consideration locations with WASH infrastructure damaged by war, locations with high IDP populations, locations with non-functioning WASH infrastructure indirectly affected by war, locations with unstable electricity supply, as well as locations with lack of maintenance and lack of skilled staff for maintenance. In addition, PIN will carefully consider locations where non-existent or malfunctioning WASH infrastructure presents a significant risk to the environment and public health, as well as locations related to medical health facilities that require WASH interventions. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups through employing a wide spectrum of different activities, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable, and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PIN’s programs are coordinated with local authorities at various levels from cross-border, to hub, to district and village levels. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at projects' planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages. Solar power plants will reduce the operational support cost for centralized water supply systems off-camps, connecting water/sanitation networks in camps to nearby systems will save the cost currently spent on the water trucking and septic tank desludging. In addition to cost recovery activities the establishment of a Tarifa system will reduce the operational support needed even when the solar power plants are not sufficient. In 2023, PIN will continue to us use solar panels in Menbij reservoir to generate electricity.

3 - Needs

With the Syrian conflict verging on its twelfth year, the humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate, impacting all aspects of life for Syrian families and further increasing their struggles to meet their basic needs. As indicated in the HNO 2023, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance is 13.6M, a 2.6% increase from 2022. In comparison to 2021, 10% more subdistricts are now in severe need. Syrians face continued challenges accessing safe water and affordable water, sanitation, solid waste management and hygiene supplies. Based on a PIN needs assessments conducted across 200 locations, the cost of operation is a barrier to the sustainability of water provision in centralized systems, and investment in sanitation infrastructure, including the treatment of wastewater. So far, under this project, PIN has improved WASH and general rehabilitation needs, particularly in Manbij and Raqqa. PIN's WASH service targeted schools have seen enhanced health practices among students. Also, health facilities in NES are better able to meet their WASH needs through PIN's help. In addition, in 2022, PIN covered maintenance needs of water departments in Manbij and Raqqa and delivered technical training courses to aid technical staff and operators in water departments. Overcrowding and lack of adequate WASH facilities put people in camps at higher risk of cholera, which massively spread in 2022.

4 - Implementation of activities

PIN plans to implement activities such as distributing essential WASH NFIs and hygiene promotion kits. PIN will also undertake hygiene awareness activities tailored to the context. The targeting criteria include vulnerable communities, schools, and health facilities; to improve access to WASH services with rehabilitation of latrines, hand washing stations, water tanks, and solar heating systems to save energy in health facilities. PIN will provide training/mentoring to institutions and assist in maintenance and technical/management support to promote the sustainability of services. PIN will also install solar power systems for water stations, water stations connections to electricity grid, water networks rehabilitation, ensuring long-term sustainable and environmentally friendly solutions, extension of sanitation networks to minimize flood risks, water stations operational support, and establish a sustainable fee collection system.In addition to nature-based solutions, PIN will establish a wastewater treatment plant to protect the groundwater and improve public health. The treated water will also be available as a safe, low-cost, and sustainable water source for irrigation. After completion, PIN and respective LC will conduct follow-up monitoring visits to ensure fulfillment of operations and maintenance duties. PIN actively responds to the cholera outbreak by ensuring correct chlorination and treatment of water, cholera kit distribution, water quality testing etc.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

According to PIN protection mainstreaming policy and the WASH cluster recommendations, The following risks (R) and respective mitigation measures(MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)(MM): needs based targeting criteria communicated with communities/LCs; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the LCs. FGDs with representatives from a crosssection of community, advertisements and communication channels for CFRM, 2)R: Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosions, human casualty and asset destruction.; 2)MM: comply with specifications outlined in the supplies on delivery, storage and distribution3)R: PSEA, 3)MM: Staff training; gender-balanced teams, PDM contains protection questions, clear process to address PSEA cases swiftly, posted signs in project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Potential exposure to explosive hazards (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructure (WWTP, sewer networks)., 4)MM: Cross-check with Mine Action actors on explosive hazard contamination and infrastructure clearance. 5)R: Children below 18 years old conduct work on behalf of humanitarian organizations, partners, or contractors.5)MM: Monitor employees documentation and attendance sheets for daily workers


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA considerations are integral part of PIN protection mainstreaming strategy. PSEA trainings are conducted to all new staff as part of the protection mainstreaming trainings. PSEA is also integral part of the Code of conducted signed with staff, local authorities, daily workers, and other stakeholders involved in PIN projects. Protection risks and mitigations matrix is developed for all the activities implemented by PIN in order to ensure that PIN has well-thought identification of possible gaps and standardize the precautionary and preventive measures required.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): Security situation in target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN's staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occur, PIN temporarily suspends activities; conduct internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with actors, inform counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of incidents; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN relocates resources to other areas. (A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitates safe humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with UNOCHA, NGO forums, Working Groups) to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift to Cash programing
