Project Summary

The Northeast of Syria is an important agricultural area due to its abundance of fertile land, extensive countryside and favorable climate compared to the rest of the country. During the past few cropping seasons, there was a sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity, which has been driven by weather anomalies, but also a result of issues with water scarcity and availability, damaged infrastructure, and unproductive assets. AFH is supporting the resilience of the population of Hajin Deir-ez-Zor with livelihood activities primarily targeting agriculture workers, as most of the population of NES relies on agriculture or their income.
Action for Humanity, partnered with World Vision Syria Response and World Vision Germany, is implementing a project to support communities in Hajin subdistrict with Livelihood, WASH and Peacebuilding interventions. The project targets a total of 48,458 beneficiaries and is being implemented over a period of 36 months. The project is being supported by BMZ.
In terms of Livelihood, 7,508 stakeholders will benefit from agriculture support, livestock breeding support, beekeeping support, the introduction of new farming techniques, vocational training accompanied by the provision of work toolkits, and a range of capacity building training courses. These activities will focus on providing beneficiaries with the support needed for them to build their businesses or start new businesses. The objective of these activities is to increase the income generating opportunities for the people of Hajin. In doing so, AFH is building the resilience of the community while supporting the economic growth of the area.
In terms of WASH, AFH is rehabilitating two drinking water stations and damaged water pipelines to ensure Hajin residents are supplied with adequate amounts of clean drinking water. Cumulatively, the WASH activities will increase the access of a total of 34,050 beneficiaries to clean drinking water.
In terms of Peacebuilding, a total of 6,900 beneficiaries will be supported with peacebuilding activities which include training on peacebuilding modules and PFA (psychological first aid), community connector projects, and social and cultural events. The objective is to strengthen social cohesion and bring the communities together.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project in Hajin aims to benefit 48,458 individuals, with 30% IDPs and 70% host communities or returnees, including 1,453 Persons with Disabilities (PWDs). It targets farmers, ranchers, and vulnerable groups such as female-headed households (FHHs) and children/adolescents. FHHs, comprising one-third of Syrian households, face significant challenges meeting basic needs, exacerbated among IDP households. The project prioritizes FHHs, aiming for at least 50% of beneficiaries to be from this group. Additionally, it addresses the needs of children and adolescents, focusing on food security to combat negative coping mechanisms like child labor and early marriages. To ensure effectiveness, a participatory assessment will precede project initiation, engaging beneficiaries to identify their specific needs, challenges, and aspirations. This approach fosters inclusivity and tailors interventions to meet the diverse needs of the community. Through targeted efforts and resource allocation, the project aims to provide support and address the unique challenges faced by vulnerable groups, including PWDs, FHHs, and youth, contributing to long-term resilience and stability in the region. WVSR and AFH prioritize robust monitoring and evaluation (MEAL) with a comprehensive framework. Gender-responsive baselines and endline evaluations are conducted, supplemented by routine monitoring (RM) and beneficiary surveys measuring household well-being. Annual Post Implementation Monitoring (PIM) ensures continuous assessment of achievements and interventions' effects.Accountability mechanisms are integral, with transparent information sharing and responsive feedback channels. Beneficiaries, including vulnerable groups like PWDs and women, are encouraged to provide feedback through various channels such as suggestion boxes and hotlines. Separate focus group discussions cater to diverse voices, ensuring their needs are heard. Participatory events involve partners and stakeholders, reflecting on evaluation findings to foster learning. WVSR supports AFH to enhance accountability within targeted communities, promoting sustainability. Risks:- Soaring prices and shortages of materials that need to be procured - Market analysis, coordination with clusters and other NGOs- Breakages in supply chains affects procurement and hinders delivery of assistance - pre-qualified suppliers- Operational challenges - AFH is on the ground and well aware of the local context- Political instability or government position - AFH has the needed approvals from local authorities and acceptance from local community - Potential cases of corruption/Fraud - Requirements, Whistle blowers, Monitoring- Logistical challenges - Security Risk Management (SRM) system- Tension between host communities - Local stakeholders are actively involved - Hiring staff from different provinces creates tensions - transparency with local communities by explaining the recruitment process
