Project Summary

"Rohingya camp: UNICEF will continue implementing components of community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) through its 26 integrated nutrition facilities (INFs) in the 19 camps. The project will combine facility, community and household-based approaches to facilitate improved access for the most at-risk groups. At the INF level, UNICEF and partners will target to admit 7,100 children with SAM for treatment. All nutrition centers will be equipped with disability friendly facilities including access during monsoon. Caregivers of children under 2 will be screened to identify needs for breastfeeding support and will be provided IYCF counselling. The project will strive to achieve optimal IYCF practices by involving men and other family members taking an active role in infant feeding and family activities and influence women on decision making. Besides, UNICEF will support the implementation of community engagement activities such as cooking demonstration sessions and Mukhe Bhaat ceremonies that help to improve mothers’ and caregivers’ knowledge and practice of optimal IYCF practices. All staff and volunteer will be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, and PSEA awareness messages will be displayed in the nutrition facilities. Furthermore, a comprehensive complaint and feedback handling mechanism will be established, and a service referral linkage will be established to the protection agency. Monthly Growth Monitoring and Promotion sessions will be conducted focusing on 0-23 months of children. Considering the COVID-19 situation, all activities will be adapted ensuring protection of beneficiaries and service providers from COVID-19. All community mobilization activities will be conducted through community nutrition volunteers. These volunteers will do follow up visits with an emphasis on absentees, defaulters and non-responders from treatment. Strategies for behaviour change communication will include mother-to-mother support groups, peer groups for adolescent girls as well as courtyard sessions targeting community leaders, male caregivers and other stakeholders. Pregnant and lactating women and adolescents will be given iron folic acid to prevent anaemia. Early childhood development services will be mainstreamed as part of essential basic services of health and nutrition since early childhood development is seen as a holistic approach including protection, stimulation, health and nutrition. In addition to that, the attitude of parents, male caregivers, service providers and community people on nurturing care will be promoted as well. The existing contingency plan will be revised according to the need, and partners will be trained to tackle any unanticipated weather incidents including fire outbreaks.
Host Community: To ensure quality nutrition services at the facility-level, upazilla nutrition supervisors and nutrition counsellors will be deployed in the union health and family welfare centers, and upazilla health complexes. Frontline health and nutrition service providers will be trained on a comprehensive package of nutrition and essential nutrition supplies will be procured and distributed to the targeted facilities. The services in the host community will involve community engagement and mobilization by trained multipurpose health volunteers (MHVs) and Community Support group members. The community engagement activities will mainly focus on practical demonstrations for mothers and caregivers on improving the quality of complementary foods through cooking demonstrations and mukhe bhaat ceremonies. Furthermore, UNICEF will collaborate with the department of agricultural extension and the department of livestock services and local NGOs to build the capacities of beneficiary households on sustainable homestead gardening and backyard poultry rearing to improve household level food security."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Refugee Camp: A Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) conducted in December 2021 showed the level of acute malnutrition situation remained critical at 11.7 per cent (in terms of GAM), having slightly increased from 11.3 per cent since the previous SMART survey in December 2020. Survey results further reveal malnutrition aggravating factors such poor dietary diversity and anemia continue to persist among young children. In December 2021, one-third of all Rohingya children under five years were found stunted which lies above the emergency threshold of 30 per cent. Over half of children (aged 6-59 months) are anaemic which is also a major concern alongside rising malnutrition prevalence. As of 2019, more than a third of children were not exclusively breastfed and only 26.4 per cent of children aged 6-23 months were receiving a minimum acceptable diet. Inappropriate IYCF-E practices indicating a serious concern among the Rohingya population. The pandemic has exacerbated pressures on local food pantries, leading to a potential elevation in the food insecurity condition. The 2020 Refugee Emergency Vulnerable Assessment (REVA 4) revealed the prevalence of population below the accepted food consumption increased from 42 per cent in 2019 to 50 per cent in 2020. Considering that it is increasingly important to scale-up and strengthen preventive services while continuing acute malnutrition treatment. Besides, strengthening effectiveness and quality of service provision is also a priority, requiring hard and valid evidence to be used for a gradual adjustment to the programme in a changing environment. Host Community: A February 2021 SMART survey in Teknaf and Ukhia identified a GAM prevalence of 9.3 per cent with approximately 8,879 children under five suffer from acute malnutrition. The same survey estimated the rate of SAM to be 0.7 per cent in both upazilas, with 2,229 children are affected, is a serious concern. Level of stunting (20.7 per cent in Ukhia and 25.8 per cent in Teknaf) indicates a poor socioeconomic conditions and possible early exposure to illnesses. The same survey revealed 73.5 and 60.0 per cent of mothers respectively in Ukhia and Teknaf exclusively breastfed their children which is an improvement compared to around 50 per cent EBF rate in 2018. Survey findings indicated a minimum acceptable diet of 36.8 per cent in Ukhia and 19.3 per cent in Teknaf, very low in the Teknaf upazila, as did the score for minimum dietary diversity which is 41.7 and 25.2 per cent respectively in Ukhia and Teknaf. The pandemic has worsened the food security situation: the 2020 REVA saw acceptable food consumption decrease from 79 to 67 per cent both in Ukhia and Teknaf. In the host community, nutrition services are delivered through the health care system: community clinics, Upazilla Health Complexes and home visits by the community nutrition workers. COVID-19 affected the accessibility and availability of nutrition services due to lockdowns. Corresponding to that improving the quality of nutrition services through strengthening health system is a priority. At community level creating awareness on optimal IYCF practices will require community engagement activities therefore develop caregivers and mother’s capacity as they will ‘learn by doing’."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will follow the principle of the rationalization exercise carried out by the Nutrition Sector, specific IP will be the only nutrition services provider in the targeted camps with the support from UNICEF and WFP. All nutrition services will be provided through INFs, operated by a single partner in each camp to avoid duplication and to optimize the utilization of resources. For the implementation of CMAM and IYCF activities, costs will be shared equally between UNICEF and WFP. To implement the activities, the selected IP will maintain good communication and effective collaboration with different agencies and other local, national, and international organizations. Integration with other sectors such as health and WASH will be ensured and referral paths will be established. Furthermore, UNICEF and its IP will work very closely with Health Sector to ensure the referral of SAM children with complications and without complications and the promotion of IYCF among pregnant and lactation women visiting health facilities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

UNICEF will works with children, families and communities to prevent and address a wide range of protection issues specially focus on children in humanitarian settings. UNICEF will works to strengthen protection systems to prevent and respond to abuse and exploitation in humanitarian situations, including by supporting families, equipping community-based groups to monitor child protection concerns and strengthening the social service workforce and case management services. UNICEF will also address gender-based violence by ensuring quality, multi-sectoral response services are accessible for all survivors of gender-based violence and improving the safety and resilience of women and girls, including by identifying and mitigating GBV risks across all sectors. Besides that, will promote mental health and psychosocial support, by providing community-led, multi-layered services focused on mobilizing family, social, and community support and strengthening MHPSS systems.

3.2 - Gender

The project will target girls and boys under 5 equally, along with pregnant and lactating women and adolescents. UNICEF and it's IP will take gender into account during programme design, addressing the specific nutritional needs of pregnant and lactating women through IYCF-E counselling and promotion with the provision of screening for the need for breastfeeding support. IPs will ensure systematic age, sex and disability disaggregated data in its programmes. The special needs of adolescent girls will be addressed through multiple interventions, i.e. IFA supplementation and deworming targeted to reduced anaemia, increasing nutrition awareness and promote diversified food intake and hygiene practice.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

UNICEF will ensure that the right of all children are protected, nurtured and free from all forms of violence, abuse, neglect, maltreatment, and exploitation as set out in the Convention on the Rights of the Child. This will be ensured by enforcing Child Safeguarding /Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures which must be adhered to by all UNICEF and partner staff. There will be zero-tolerance for violation of this. UNICEF will ensure that all frontline workers will be trained on gender equality and PSEA. Any PSEA-related concerns found in the complaints boxes or brought to the help desks at the INFs, will be immediately referred to the PSEA network and/or protection sector for prompt action. To ensure that partners will follow their approved PSEA action plans, UNICEF will follow up the implementation of the action plan systematically and timely through regular field monitoring visits and required reporting.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity and Inclusion is fundamental to achieving UNICEF's vision. UNICEF are for every child without discrimination whatever their ethnicity, sex, religion, language, abilities, or any other status. UNICEF will ensure that sex-, age-, disability- and other context-specific disaggregated data are collected, analyzed and used and systematically include a gender and diversity analysis. UNICEF will also ensure humanitarian responses are based on a gender and diversity analysis and recognize and respond to the specific vulnerabilities due to diversity and gender norms and cultural practices.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF will reinforce the complaints response mechanism at all integrated nutrition facilities (INFs) and communities through integrating AAP sessions in training, community sessions and sensitization meetings. Each INF will maintain a designated helpdesk where beneficiaries can give complaint, seek information, share their views on topics of concern. The participation of women will be ensured by positioning male and female volunteers at helpdesks and posting the complaints box in a secure location and a hotline number will be posted at INFs through which mother/women can provide feedback anonymously. Complaints related to service provision will be raised at the appropriate staff level and addressed promptly. Moreover, UNICEF will continue to train its partners to demonstrate their capacity to analyze the feedback routinely and integrate AAP aspects into the program design and implementation to ensure the availability of a functional, efficient and accessible complaints system.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"Yes. Cox’s Bazar District is vulnerable to natural disasters, including cyclones, landslides and flooding during the monsoon season and fire during the dry season. To mitigate this, UNICEF and partners will employ disaster risk reduction measures as guided by a Multi-hazard Preparedness and Response Plan. This includes the pre-positioning of a buffer stock of supplies and medicines in the nutrition facilities for rapid deployment in the event of an emergency due to floods and cyclones. "
