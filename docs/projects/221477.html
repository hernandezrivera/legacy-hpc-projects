Project Summary

For more than eight years now, NW and SW regions have been the center of violence that has resulted in the displacement of over 630,000 individuals (UNICEF, July 2024). The conflict has aggravated the humanitarian situation with needs ranging from immediate food assistance, livelihoods enhancement, protection, shelter/NFI, water and sanitation as well as access to health and education. The majority of the displaced persons especially youths tend to engage in negative coping strategies such as survival sex, child marriage, street hawking and petty theft. The prolonged crises has caused adverse impacts that have accumulated by not allowing affected households to rebuild their livelihoods and recover. The accumulated negative impacts of protracted shocks on household livelihoods have long-term effects and reinforce intergenerational transmission of poverty and pushing poor households further into greater vulnerability. As much attention is given to saving lives, equal attention must be given to saving livelihoods in order to achieve household and community resilience. In the two affected regions, damage to livelihoods has resulted not only in already limited income sources becoming further diminished, but also in households losing one or more of their food sources. This project aims to provide integrated food and nutritional assistance to crisis-affected populations (men, women, girls, boys, PLHIV and PWD) in Boyo and Momo divisions of the North West Region of Cameroon as a means to support their self-reliance and recovery needs. The project will implement Emergency General Food Assistance (in kind), strengthen and support livelihoods capacities as well provide Blanket Supplementary Feeding to vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in target area. In this while providing food assistance, COMINSUD plans to enhance capacities for at least 300 households (50% women headed, 4 % persons with a disability, 2%single mothers) in the area of livelihoods enhancement. To achieve this COMINSUD will identify from the targeted food recipient households critical cases that it will train on at least one out of 5 clusters of livelihoods activities (livestock – poultry, piggery, gardening) and provide a seeds, inputs plus technical follow ups to render these supported households self-sufficient by the end of the year.
This project alignments with Specific Outcome 1 (SO1) of WFP’s Country Strategic Plan (2022-2026): Crisis-affected populations including refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host populations in Cameroon have safe access to adequate and nutritious food during and in the aftermath of crises. The project will contribute to vision 2030 (i.e., the world has eradicated food insecurity and malnutrition) and SDG 2 (i.e., Zero Hunger).
Specifically, the project will ensure continued and regular access to adequate and nutritious food through general food assistance (In kind) to 30,000 crisis-affected women, men, girls, and boys in Mezam and Momo divisions (North-West region) by the end of December 2025 as well as enhance livelihoods for 300 households by rehabilitating farmlands for agricultural production, Provide seeds, tools, and training boost livestock production through improved feeding, and restocking within the targeted beneficiaries communities while promoting inclusion of women, men, girls, boys, PWD, PLHIV in all aspects of the project and ensure everyone has equal opportunities, rights, and obligations to benefit. The project will as well enhance improvement in nutrition-related practices through social and behavioral change communication (SBCC) to 80,000 people in the target area.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The ongoing crisis in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon has severely impacted food security and nutrition, with nearly 1.6 million people in need of assistance. In the North West region alone, an estimated 775,000 people face food insecurity, exacerbated by violence, displacement, and limited humanitarian access. Specific areas such as Mezam, Momo, Ngoketujia and Bui Divisions are particularly affected, with displaced populations struggling to access basic food supplies and nutrition services.Assessed Needs:In the area of Food Security, approximately 60% of households in the region report inadequate food consumption. Disruptions to farming activities and market access have heightened dependency on humanitarian aid.In the area of Nutrition, over 10,000 children under five years suffer from severe acute malnutrition (SAM), with limited access to therapeutic feeding programs. Pregnant and lactating women are at high risk of malnutrition due to food shortages and health service disruptions.The planned activities include: Emergency Food Assistance interventions that will distribute in-kind food, cash transfers, and vouchers to 30,000 individuals Mezam and Momo Divisions. Support local markets by integrating cash-based assistance wherever feasible.Nutrition Support to expand community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) programs to treat 1,000 MAM and SAM cases. Provide fortified supplementary foods to 5,000 children and 3,000 pregnant and lactating women.Livelihood Support interventions that will provide agricultural training to enhance resilience for 1500 farming households as well as distribute seeds and tools to them to rebuild local food production.This intervention is expected to produce the following results:Improved food security for 60% of targeted households, reducing reliance on emergency aid.Reduction in malnutrition rates among children under five by at least 20% in targeted communities.Enhanced self-sufficiency through revitalized farming activities and market engagement.This intervention aligns with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan
