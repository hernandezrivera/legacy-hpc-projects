Project Summary

In line with strategic cluster objectives 2: CLO2 Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support and objective 3, SO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks; RuCAPD seeks to help the populations in Panyikang , Manyo, Fashoda, Baliet, Akoka, Malakal and Melut in Upper Nile state, Juba(Wonduruba), Lainya and Morobo in Central Equatorial as well as Canal Pigi in Jonglei state move through recovery and resilience toward development through own food production livelihood support and livestock health services. This project is designed to effectively contribute to the protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods of the most vulnerable and affected population in Greater Upper Nile and Central Equatoria States. The project is a 12-month project targeting 68,929 HH equal to 413,570 vulnerable individuals IDPs – 41,357, returnees 248,142 and host community 124,071; of whom 90,985 are Men, 95,121 are women, 111,664 are boys and 115,800 are girls. The targeting will be done with gender lens and consideration of incorporation of Accountability to affected population. We expect to train a total of 300 HH on different agronomy practices as well as 140 Community Animal Health Workers (CAWHs) for the livestock aspect of the project. Vouchers will be used in the seed fair project sites. The beneficiaries will use them to get the seeds they want and the traders will then redeem them for cash. The program implementation will ensure that Do No Harm principle is adhered to as transparency and accountability to the affected population is placed right at the centre of beneficiary targeting and provision of support. RuCAPD extensive presence and history in Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Jonglei coupled with an in-depth understanding of the community conflict dynamics and context tailored approaches to specific communities will help identify, analyze and manage risks as well as reduce any potential negative impacts of the project and any protection issues.
The project will receive pipeline supplies from FAO whilst the requested funding from SSHF will be used to provide front line services. The project will use direct distribution to the targeted groups through a transparent process of identification and distribution. In case of security hitches, RuCAPD has extensive network of how to reach the targeted beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

According to August, 2019 IPC analysis, 6.35M people, about 54% of the total population, are in IPC Phase 3/worse acute food insecurity at the lean season of Aug2018 (Aug2019 IPC). Acute malnutrition is faced by about 1.30M children and about 0.35M pregnant and lactating mothers (Aug2019 IPC). There has been a slight improvement in food security in 2019 compared to the same time last year because of the relative security, opening markets and improved humanitarian access attributed to the revitalized peace agreement. However, food insecurity continues to persist due to localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality (Aug2019 IPC). The protracted economic crisis, soaring stable food prices, a high cereal crop deficit, pests and diseases as well as drought or floods in parts of the country have led to further deterioration of food security (FSNMS24). According to IGAD ICPAC July 2019 Monthly Climate Bulletin Climate Review and Forecasts for September 2019 and forward, Most of GHA recorded near average rainfall including Central, northern and southern SS, where RuCAPD is proposing. Upper Nile has the second highest number of people in IPC phase 3 and above after Jonglei (Aug2019 IPC). FEWSNET October 14th 2019, SS crop production could be equal/ better than last year. Nut there’s no harvest estimates to quantify potential production compared to short term or pre-crisis average. The high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country, poor quality and diversity of food and high prevalence of diseases has caused alarming levels of acute malnutrition (Aug2019 IPC). All these underlying causes must be addressed if malnutrition is to be controlled (WFP/FAO/UNICEF Oct2019). The food security is expected to improve in the harvest and post-harvest season, but these stocks will rapidly deplete in 2020 due to a generally poor harvest this year that has been caused by inadequate farming and fishing inputs, pests and diseases such as Fall Army Worm, unfavourable weather conditions and disease burdens at the households (FSNMS24). Livestock is one of the main contributors of food security in rural areas of South Sudan. It faces threat due to lack of veterinary services, livestock disease outbreaks, armed groups, intercommunal raiding and adverse weather conditions that affect availability of water, pasture and abundance of diseases (FSNMS 24). The conflict has continued to disrupt availability of animal health services to the communities. The government has not been able to provide veterinary service to most of the areas. Closure of trade routes has resulted in unavailability of livestock drugs. Normal mobility and migration of livestock has been disrupted, livestock being concentrated in small areas and migrating through previously not used routes. Creating favourable conditions for development and rapid spread of diseases even from endemic to non-endemic Areas. The remoteness calls for RuCAPD veterinarians to intervene. Exposing communities to zoonosis. The livestock of the most of the community members at risk of death due to outbreak of diseases and lack of access to veterinary services. RuCAPD has other projects in the area thus will integrate and build on the so much needed uninterrupted multisectoral humanitarian assistance. Crop, vegetable, fisheries production, livestock treatment and vaccination increase HH diversification and access to food within their communities. They are able to sell the surplus in the local markets (FAO news release, July 11 2019). RuCAPD also seeks climate finance (GEF and GCF )to see how we can practice climate smart agriculture since climate change is here with us.

Cluster Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support: Increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through agricultural seeds and fishing inputs  Emergency food production through distribution of main season agriculture seeds & tools  Emergency food & livelihood support through distribution of fishing kits, cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds  Reducing pest and crop disease through surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication with focus on FAW  Livestock disease prevention through vaccination and livestock keepers sensitization & treatment Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks: Prevent and reverse the erosion of vulnerable rural & urban households and community assets through livelihood & conditional cash based support for more sustainable livelihoods.  Strengthening seed security and multiplication through establishment of seed multiplication sites  Strengthening seed security and multiplication through seed fairs using locally sourced seed  Training & capacity building of farmers through the farmer field schools approach  Support value chain addition of fish by preservation & marketing through establishment of fish processing sites and fisher folk training  Diversification & restoration of livelihoods through reviving of veterinary pharmacies.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This intervention is well intertwined with the IPC and food security cluster objectives which are in line with the government policies on agriculture, livestock and fisheries as well as based on the needs of the community that we serve. We work in collaboration with the community and government system because we deal with the line ministries. For example our livestock, agriculture and fisheries interventions are always monitored by the line ministries in our areas of operation. The county ministry offices are aware of all that we do and approve. We share some of the work too because no one can operate alone. We need the community and the government line ministries to achieve the sustainable development goal of zero hunger. We, therefore, will leave no one behind.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. This project is in tandem with the national framework for return, reintegration and relocation of displaced persons- achieving durable solutions in South Sudan. July 2019 version. Which among others reckons that the food insecurity in the country can be tackled by supporting the resumption of agricultural activities for the affected populations. The priority actions recommended in line with our proposed activities include improving access to seeds and tools, local seed production, capacity building of farmers, protecting and rebuilding livestock assets, strengthening fish production and fish processing and supporting vocational training.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

RuCAPD understands that it is important for the participating population, staff members and all stakeholders to feel and be safe.This project ,therefore, is designed in such a way that inequalities and protection issues are mainstreamed. Inequality and protection threats in all aspects of life and so much so this project is an important issue. This can be based on one’s ethnicity, gender, age or social/ economic status. To deal with this, RuCAPD first looks at creating awareness to all stakeholders of the project that there is problem exists not only in our project areas but also globally. RuCAPD adheres to the “Do No Harm” humanitarian principle and the 2030 agenda clarion call of “leave no one behind”. Exposing actions of inequality and protection threats is the other way of dealing with it. Community coping mechanism is an important protection strategy. RuCAPD does emphasize the importance of stakeholders understanding &supporting strategies employed by the at-risk populations to protect themselves and their loved ones from threats to their safety and dignity. This is because they know the internal communication systems very well. We understand well that the civilian strategies are essential to determining their own safety. Views by the women, girls, elderly and the community at large are taken seriously- for example time and venue of distribution, training etc. This is through the feedback mechanism which remains important in our work as we seek to always closes the loop. Clear, open and transparent communication with all stakeholders in the intervention help create the so much desired safety ad stable protection environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. This is an important aspect f the intervention because we desire to cultivate sustainable, resilient and self governed food security and livelihood communities. own production and ploughing back of the seeds received as well as the treatment and vaccination services is key to our intervention. This will go a long way to create resilience, ownership and sustainability. Our people will stop depending on food aid thus boosting their esteem ad dignity.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback, (positive and negative) from the community we serve is invaluable. RuCAPD, has mechanisms in place to help with this. We have community communication agents at all the project sites. Their main function is to collect all feedback from the community and channel it to the concerned personnel for further communication and action as well as receiving feedback on the community concerns and closing the loop by reporting back to the community on the way forward. We speak the local language and also hold chiefs barazas to deal with matters arising as well as use the public address systems to announce matters At the current stage, all the communities in our areas of operation have asked for early pre positioning of inputs in respect with seasonality- we forward this to donor and core pipeline managers. The other concern is the communities in Greater Upper Nile project sites require malodas instead of hoes while those in Central Equatorial wont mind the hoes..Our good rapport with the community is a plus as we use local language in the field to effectively communicate about services and the support available. Arabic and English are also used but not as much as mother tongue. We also have suggestion boxes at our distribution sites to help people communicate their concerns.
