Project Summary


This project aims at improving the protection environment and providing life-saving assistance and basic services to Persons of Concern (PoCs) that is, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs),People Living with Disabilities ( PLWD), Returnees, Vulnerable Host population living in the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions of Cameroon.
This will be done through a community based participatory approach.
Out Puts
• Conduct Vulnerability Mapping: capacitates the POCs to identify their vulnerability and help them to find opportunities to solve the issues. This will be done by training enumerators in the community to collect data using various tools and methods on the subject under study.
• Re-enforce Community-based Protection monitoring: With the help of Community-based Protection monitoring team, communities will be monitored and community-level concerns will identified and referred for further assessment; This will be done by training the various identities in the community on Gender transformation, PSEA, , Core Humanitarian standard/principles.
• Strengthen Systematic Protection incident reporting: Protection incidents will be systematically documented and reported. This will help in generating evidence-base analysis on the protection environment and related risks to inform advocacy, resource mobilization and communication with all relevant stakeholders on the protection situation of persons of concern in Cameroon. GBV data will be collected to contribute to the analysis and evidence monitoring provided through GBVIMS where operational and other case monitoring tools such as UNHCR database. Data will also be collected on risks factors e.g. loss of civil documentation, incidents on Housing, Land and Property and freedom of movement.
* Case Management: we will provide comprehensive and inclusive case management services to survivors of GBV as well as protection case management to the Persons Of concern(POCs)
• Raise Awareness and set up Community Protection Mechanisms: Awareness raising will be done in the communities on protection: Gender- Based Violence, Child protection, human rights and PSEA. Set up Community-Based Protection Mechanisms that will act as channels to report protection incidents and response systems. Effective feedback mediums that are age appropriate, disability and gender sensitive will be established in communities with the aim of encouraging people to share feedback/complaints without anonymity towards Plan International being compromised.
• Advocacy: Plan International will advocate to UNHCR to seek interventions through other partners, stakeholders, and agencies.
• Facilitate Strategic Assistance and Referral: Persons of Concern(PoCs): unaccompanied and separated children, people with disabilities, elderly, GBV survivors, single women on the move/ heads of household, will be identified, and supported with relevant kits(dignity, mama, WASH and referred for further assessment and assistance. Cases of PoCs with nutritional, and shelter needs will be referred to other partners and this will go a long way to strengthen partner relationships.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the Northwest and Southwest regions (NWSW) where a violent crisis broke out in 2016, the conflict has produced 532,290 IDPs (August 2022 statistics) as well as 417,516 returnees while exacerbated the protection environment. As a result, the protection situation is marked by systematic violence inflicted on civilians by the parts in conflict (State forces and non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and increased gender-based violence, attacks on property, illegal or arbitrary arrests, recruitment of children into armed forces and groups, attacks on life, physical integrity are among the most recurrent violations reported in the regions. From January to August 2022, 3252 protection incidents were reported through the protection monitoring while in the same period 7594 GBV incidents were recorded (31% Emotional abuse cases, 22% Denial of resources/opportunities; Physical assault 21%; Sexual assault12%; Rape 8%). With an increase of IDPs, living conditions are particularly precarious and IDPs are exposed to regular abuses especially women and girls. Without access to decent jobs and effective support to pursue their studies, some unemployed women and young girls who have not attended school have turned to survival sex to meet their basic needs such as nutrition needs, dignity needs, communication, shelter needs amongst others. Many of them are victims of exploitation and physical violence. Some of them have lost their civil documents, including birth certificates and identity cards. This limits their access to services and the enjoyment of their rights. The civilian population continues to be the victim of attacks from non-state armed groups (NSAGs). As such, the security situation remains volatile, especially in localities along the borders with Nigeria, as well as the West Region. This situation is basically characterized by incursions, abductions, including of children, threats, looting, theft, and destruction of property and infrastructures. Plan International Cameroon also works on ending gender-based violence through various campaigns/movements such as the ‘Because I am a Girl’. In addition, partnership is built with corporate organization (e.g. Orange) to conduct campaigns and sensitizations during specific international days, such as, the International Days of the Girl Child, International Women’s Day, 16-day Activism Against Gender Based Violence (GBV) with messages linked to the themes of the events. In addition, Plan International Cameroon facilitated and supported a feasibility study for the setting up of Child helpline in Cameroon. Furthermore, we are a member of the committee which review the states reports on the CEDAW and the UNCRC in addition to being a member of the protection cluster and working groups. We support the national child right coalition in developing alternative and UPR reports.
