Project Summary

Fifteen years into insurgency, the conflict, further, the flooding and insecurity, and the humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria remains profound and widespread. Over 7.9 million people face severe protection concerns, extreme deprivation beyond their existing poverty levels, and daily threats to their well-being, with women and children severely impacted (HNO 2024).

The UNICEF Child Protection project will continue to focus on strengthening access to quality preventive and responsive child protection services that address the needs, rights and dignity of children and their caregivers in the conflict affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY). The response will prioritize the most vulnerable including at-risk children and their families. UNICEF’s efforts to address child protection and gender-based violence in emergencies is part of its over-all strategy on prevention and response to all forms of violence against children (VAC) through adopting a nexus approach that addresses the urgent protection concerns facing children affected by crises; reinforce existing local and national capacities and systems for children’s protection; transitional justice and promoting peaceful coexistence including a focus on climate change mitigation where possible.
The present project module is part of ongoing efforts to provide a comprehensive child protection and gender-based violence program that aims to support the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict affected children and caregivers, quality case management services for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and survivors of GBV, and community-based reintegration of children and adolescents formerly associated with Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs). Girls, boys, and women survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), including conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) will also have access to safe spaces and benefit from case management, information, and referral systems to specialized services. In addition, project actions will further focus on social norms change, child protection risk education and child protection system strengthening through the roll out of the child protection law in the BAY states, registering under-5 child births, and enhancing the capacity of a professional social service workforce to improve the quality, coordination and sustaining the response. The response will be integrated with the work of other sectors, such as education, WASH, health, and nutrition, for increased and comprehensive delivery of services.
Results will be achieved by providing integrated child protection case management services for children including family tracing and reunification for UASC; delivering quality and sustainable socio-economic reintegration services to children formerly associated with armed groups as well as other vulnerable children and their families. Additionally, conflict-affected children will have access to community-based mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) and GBV prevention and response services. Explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) will be provided in schools, child friendly spaces, transit, and displaced camps with the support of Mine Action sub sector.

1 - Needs

 According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs overview (HNO), over 7.9 million people face severe protection concerns, extreme deprivation beyond their existing poverty levels, and daily threats to their well-being. Women and children, particularly girls, remain the most affected, comprising 83 per cent of those in need. Child protection needs have increased and are expected to rise in 2025 due to ongoing conflict and insecurity, recent flooding, displacement, camp closures, and other socioeconomic factors. Approximately 2 million children require child protection services (33 per cent IDPs, 44 per cent returnees and 23 per cent host communities) due to the conflict and insecurity. As of 30th September 2024, the Country Taskforce on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified 473 grave violations perpetrated against 205 children (134 girls and 71 boys) in north-east Nigeria. Grave violations against children’s rights is profound and on the rise. Most violations involved killings and maiming, abduction, recruitment, and rape and sexual violence are the most prevalent violations by the Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP) and Jamā'at Ahl as-Sunnah lid-Da'wah wa'l-Jihād (JAS). Both armed groups and security forces continue to commit crimes under international humanitarian law and international human rights law that have a severe impact on the protection of children. Gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence and forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity, and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. Approximately 1,829,002 million individuals are requiring GBV prevention and response services and assistance (HNO 2024). Further, forced displacement and returns impose high risk on separated and unaccompanied children. Boys and adolescent male risk forcible recruitment by armed groups, or suspicion of association with armed groups by authorities. Civilians continue to suffer death and injury from explosive ordnance, including the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. About 1.5 million IDPs, returnees and host community populations are affected by the indiscriminate use of explosive ordnances (EOs), resulting in deaths and injuries, and impacting access to basic services and socio-economic activities (HNO 2024). UNMAS has identified a significant rise in the number of children affected by EO incidents in 2022, 2023 and 2024, in respect of previous years. In 2024 so far, 16 recorded EO casualties were children, which represent 4% of the total direct civilian victims. Between January and October 2024, UNMAS recorded 230 EO incidents (including 174 IED incidents) with 614 casualties of which 280 were killed and 313 injured. This is more than the casualties registered in 2021, 2022 and 2023. As of 30 November 2024, a total of 6,830 individuals (34% women and 37% children) were living in three transit camps (Hajj, Shokari and Bulumkutu) in Maiduguri. The effective reintegration of children is hindered by ongoing and persistent armed conflict and it’s on societies, such as the absence of livelihood opportunities, weak economies and markets, and a breakdown of social infrastructure, including schools and health facilities. Community resilience to protect children has been weakened, resulting in children and families adopting negative coping mechanisms.

2 - Activities

Child protection: Sector strategic objective (SSO) 1: Ensure boys and girls, including those with disabilities, those who have experienced grave violations of child rights and children at risk of abuse, discrimination, exploitation, neglect, and violence receive meaningful support through community-based reintegration services, comprehensive preventive programs, and high-quality response services that prioritize their safety, well-being, and resilience. Key activities under SSO1: 1. Support Family tracing and reunification of separated and unaccompanied children.; 2. Place children affected by protection risks into an appropriate community based alternative care; 3. Provide children at risk with specialized care and support; 4. Provide children with disabilities with appropriate need-based child protection services, care, and support; 5. Provide comprehensive multi-sectorial case management support (abuse, neglect, exploitation, child labor & child marriage etc.) to children at risk/vulnerable children; 6. Register children under 5 years and support the issue of birth certificates; 7. Referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services; 8. Support age appropriate structured recreational and creative activities for children in CFS; 8. Provide Community-based social and economic reintegration support to children formally associated with armed groups. SSO2: Empower parents, caregivers, children, and communities to create a protective environment that supports resilience, safety, and dignity for all, by fostering positive social norms and behaviors aimed at eliminating violence, abuse, and harmful traditional practices. Key activities under SSO2: 1. Support community mobilization, engagement and awareness raising to address social norms that affect the rights of children etc.; 2. Provide life skills education for adolescent girls and boys; 3. Provide psychosocial services/ positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children. SSO3: Strengthen the capacity of child protection partners and the social welfare workforce to deliver comprehensive, high-quality prevention and response services, thereby reinforcing the resilience and effectiveness of the child protection system. Key activities under SSO3: 1. Build capacity of Committee Based Child Protection committees, Community volunteers, child protection partners, social welfare staff of government on child protection issues and best practices; 2. Strengthen legal and child protection system. GBV AOR: Strategic Objective (SO) 2025: Improved access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated GBV response services for survivors and individuals at risk. Key activities under SO 2025: 1. Provision of GBV response / specialized services: GBV case management, mental and access to psychological support, livelihood, medical care, legal assistance, safety, and security support; 2. Procurement/prepositioning and distribution of dignity kits; 3. Establishing Women and Girls Safe Spaces and Integrated Facilities for providing lifesaving GBV services; 4. Organizing trainings and building capacity on GBV Service Provision (GBV/Gender mainstreaming, PSEA Training) and development and dissemination of IEC materials.Mine Action Sub Sector. Sector Strategic Objective (SSO) 1: Enhance civilians’ protection from harm and respond to specific needs and risks with timely and comprehensive protection services. Key activities under SO 2025: Provide community and school-based risk education for IDP’s and host communities in highly contaminated areas.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

UNICEF project will empower affected communities to break the culture of silence and bring about significant change in social norms through awareness raising on child protection issues and GBV education. In addition, the project will further focus on child protection system mapping, system strengthening such as the domestication of federal Child Rights Act (CRA) and support implementation of the law and enhancement of the capacity of the social service workforce to improve the quality and coordination of response and sustain the outcomes. The System building approach will complement the humanitarian-developmental nexus approach of UNICEF and the Resilience Integrated Child Protection Programming for Children in north-east Nigeria. In addition, the project will provide opportunity for adolescents and women to access livelihood opportunities through vocational training in established and functional government enterprises and training centers will the goal to improve livelihood capacity of conflict affected adolescents and women that would subsequently lead to improved capacity to participate in community development. The project to transitional justice for children’s activities with aim to strengthen justice for children and enhance their participation in peacebuilding, reconciliation, and social cohesion.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

UNICEF’s response strategies will continue to focus promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian service provision and promoting the rights children, their families, and communities. Target population will be engaged at all stages, and the participation of all in the design and targeting of interventions, vulnerable and marginalized groups, women, girls, boys, men, people with disabilities, old age, women headed household (HH), child headed HH and pregnant, lactating and mothers married girl children. All implementing partner staff, will undergo gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training before they begin working in the program. This is to both help prevent GBV and PSEA but also to spot potential warning signs of gender exploitation and abuse in targeted communities. UNICEF and its partners will establish safe and accountable GBV reporting and feedback mechanism in their targeted communities. UNICEF and its partner will collaborate with other agencies, communities, and stakeholders to develop a more holistic environment of protection for vulnerable groups, displaced populations, and at-risk children.UNICEF will strengthen collaboration with other agencies, communities, and stakeholders to develop a more holistic environment of protection for vulnerable groups, displaced populations, and at-risk children. UNICEF will ensure all implementing partner staff will undergo gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training before they begin working in the program. This is to both help prevent GBV and PSEA but also to spot potential warning signs of gender exploitation and abuse in targeted communities. UNICEF and its partners will establish safe and accountable GBV reporting and feedback mechanism in their targeted communities. UNICEF will deploy the principle of “Do no harm” and employ appropriate measures to avoid exposing people to further harm because of project actions and ensure that the environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people to further hazards, violence or human rights abuses or violations. UNICEF and its partners will manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized and will ensure that the affected population have access to humanitarian assistance against any form of direct or indirect discrimination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF has established several safe and accessible community-based reporting mechanisms in targeted communities to report against PSEA and other violations. UNICEF also uses its short text messages (sms) based U-Report tool to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback or complaints. The U-Report 24x7 Hub receives complaints, reports, and feedback from communities, analyzes the data and shares with relevant sections in UNICEF and its partners for action. As reports and complaints regarding other services handled by different agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to act. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints. UNICEF will continue to strengthen these mechanisms and expand collaboration with other key stakeholders to ensure effective use and maintenance of mechanisms.
