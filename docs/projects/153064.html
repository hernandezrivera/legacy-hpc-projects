Project Summary

Provision of Primary health care through Rapid Response Mechanism, Mobile outreach, PHCU and PHCC to people in need and selected host community in Nyirol and Torit. Save the Children is looking to implement this project in Nyirol as a continuation of the current SSHF 2018 activities. The total population is 181,159, while people in need are 127,569. Targeted beneficiaries are 21,428 (IDPs in host community and 60% of the host community). The services will be delivered through 1 mobile outreach, 1 PHCU, 1 PHCC and 1 Hospital.
Total population in Torit is 160,818 (IDP 9,743 & host community 151,075) of which 47% (70,005) was identified as vulnerable community falling within the 8 objectives priority areas. Further 60% was identified from the 70,005 as direct beneficiary from host community. This project will support target beneficiary of 21,428 (men- 10,714, F-10,714, <5yrs 4,287 and < 15yrs 17,142) as people in need. The project will support 0ne PHCC and PHCU through provision of comprehensive clinical packages. Of the PIN 25% have been considered at risk of Ebola outreach- the population along the border with Uganda and Yei. The health facilities will be IPC comply with staff trained and equipped to respond to the hemorrhagic outbreaks.

Needs

There is widespread shortages of essential medicines, and seasonal floods exacerbating displacements. NGOS are managing 4 out of 5 health facilities with continuity of services reliant upon ad-hoc funding. In Jonglei and Upper Nile only 211 out of the listed 318 facilities currently have some sustainable funding, with no real surety of funding into 2019, even if funding is availed it will target only 150 facilities, (RRHP 2018). Worse still the mentioned facilities have deplorable infrastructure as buildings are made of grass mad single rooms-very old and wasted. Lack of space to provide clinical package of health at PHCU/C is a major concern. Predictions from IPC findings show that 8 counties may go into catastrophic levels which might further trigger diseases. High risk of Ebola in 12 counties demands stringent health securities, failing which, the disease spread may lead to a country wide epidemic. Torit County is among the Counties at high risk of EVD outbreaks. The unmanned entry points and the free entry points without EVD screening points increase the EVD risk in South Sudan. Worst is the level of which health facilities are not ready for such outbreak. The waste management, IPC and case identification remain wanting in almost all the PHCC in Torit and along the borders. SCI will conduct training to the Health staff in 2 facilities supported with this fund plus all the 150 CBDs providing ICCM in Torit. The CBDs and their supervisor will conduct risk communication awareness and surveillance tracking in their areas of coverage in Torit. CBDs are the most and highly at risk of contracting EVD. The children <5 with fever, vomiting etc. are taken to CBDs as the first contact. If not trained well the CBD will be a conduit for infection transmission. Early in September 2018 SCI was the first IP to establish Screening point at River Port in Nimule (EVD TASK FORCE request). SCI has a good experience in EVD management having actively been involved in West Africa (Sierra Leon, Liberia and Guinea) responses. Currently SCI is actively involved in DRC EVD outbreak and is also conducting preparedness in Uganda and Rwanda. South Sudan Country office is actively involved at the National task force, Cluster Task force and TWG (case management and risk Communication). SCI based on previous experience has established in country RRM team of qualified medical personnel to respond to outbreaks. Three of the RRM team members will undergo IPC training. SCI has Emergency Humanitarian Unit with 72hr-response lead-time for deployment in times of need. Recommendations from inter-cluster needs analysis denotes that 10 counties are at risk of high GAM rates (>15%), displacements (>5000), IPC (Phase5) along with disease convergence. In the recent SMART survey conducted by SCI (April 2018) in Nyirol showed the GAM and SAM was 25.7% (21.9↔30.0 95% CI) and 6.6% (4.6↔9.3 95% CI) respectively, which is beyond the WHO thresh hold. Outbreaks of endemic diseases such as malaria can extensively strain the already depleted health system in South Sudan especially in opposition (IO) controlled areas and hard to reach locations (Nyirol). The target population for health response has been prioritized along 8 alarming public health parameters: malaria endemicity, cholera hotspots. Penta 3 coverage, IPC, health service functionality, cold chain capacity, active conflict, flood prone areas, including counties in risk of Ebola resulting in a minimum county PIN of which Nyirol scored 47%. Poor infrastructure bad roads, terrains and prolonged rainy season make it difficult for transportation and deliveries of goods. The air flights remain the reliable and timely transport but expensive. Lack of women participation due to cultural issues, low education level and discrimination have disadvantaged women against men. SCI will ensure women issues are considered in the project and their involvement and opinion incorporated in decision making and project improvement.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Save the Children South Sudan has a robust accountability system that enables beneficiaries to provide continuous feedback in a safe and accessible way and where the feedback is acted upon to improve the quality of our programming. The complaints and response mechanism will sensitize beneficiaries at project initiation stage through kick off meetings and community consultation workshops on SC accountability standards. Posters and banners containing information about SC, the project objectives, beneficiary selection criteria, complaint mechanisms and how community will be participating through the process cycles will be printed and erected at community centers and service centers. During distributions the project team will first be sharing project information to the beneficiaries on objectives and targets for the project and reminding them of different channels they can use to give feedback to the program.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Save the children enforces Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by adhering to four commitments in order to ensure the safety and well-being of adults and children. Save the Children prevents and responds to PSEA by implementing and enforcing our child safeguarding and PSEA policy of zero tolerance; assigning focal point for reporting and training in each field office and location we operate in that feeds to the national system for further investigation and action; raising awareness and engaging communities we work with; conducting risk assessment of our activities; ensuring safe reporting mechanisms are in place and responding to internal and external reports to SEA through different internal and external steps and channels. The four commitments, awareness, prevention, reporting and response, stipulates that:• Awareness: All staff, partners, representatives, volunteers are aware of issues of child abuse and sexual exploitation and the risks to both adult and children. Children and families (beneficiaries) are aware of standards of behavior they can expect from SC staff and representatives.• Prevention: We will ensure that staff partners, representatives, and volunteers minimize the risks to children. We will create an environment where children’s rights are respected and child abuse and sexual exploitation of both adults and children is prevented. • Reporting: We will ensure that staff partners, representatives, and volunteers are clearer as to what steps to take when concerns arise regarding the abuse or exploitation of adults and children. • Response: We will ensure that action is taken to support, safeguard and protect adults and children where concerns arise regarding their possible abuse and exploitation.

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The full participation of communities in the transformation of their own life will be part of the focus of the program. Community leaders, community committees, community groups and other social partners will be mobilized in raising awareness and creating demand for services. During the initial months of the project, SC will conduct a series of meetings with key decision makers and community leaders in the project area as part of efforts to mobilize awareness and support for the health, and and nutrition interventions. Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Multiple channels for providing feedback such as the toll-free hotline (subject to service availability for those locations), suggestion boxes, appeal committees, help desks will be used at all project meetings and distribution sites. Save the Children will also conduct focus group discussions, sampled feedback collection, onsite and post distribution monitoring surveys with different groups; men, women, children and elderly to get their feedback on the program performance. The first post-distribution survey will also collect information from non-beneficiaries to get information on their perception on the fairness of targeting process. The feedback will be compiled in a database developed and maintained by the MEAL team which alerts and escalate for every outstanding action on the feedback provided. All the feedback will be collected and analysed by our MEAL staff for monthly analysis and feed into program review meetings to be discussed with the program staff. For issues that are related to service quality (rather than project design), an “action tracker” maintained by MEAL will be utilized for deciding actions and tracking progress in implementing the required actions. From our MEAL database, we have observed that beneficiaries are more likely to provide feedback through focus group discussion and feedback collection sample surveys. Save the Children provides multiple channels to make to make sure beneficiaries have a context based choice of the preferred method based on social and cultural sensitivities as well as literacy level of the community members. All information received from all channels will not have names of the person (feedback provider) to make sure confidentiality is maintained. Suggestion boxes will put at a place that would be of comfort to the community members after consultations. MEAL staff also conducts sample feedback survey periodically to beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As an organization we aim to minimize risk and prevent harm to all adults and children associated with SCI programs, by putting in place reporting mechanisms and guidelines to ensure that cases of suspected child abuse and exploitation of adults and children are immediately reported and followed up on, through our clear guidelines for reporting. Any allegation of abuse or exploitation of an adult or child associated with a Save the Children program will lead to the immediate suspension and removal from the location of the accused individuals. SCI would start an immediate internal investigation and report the case to local authorities. SCI will respond appropriately to PSEA issues and concerns and child safeguarding concerns as they arise. In all cases, the identities of the victim and the reporter if different will be protected.
