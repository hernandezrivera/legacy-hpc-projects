Project Summary

As the protection crisis in South Sudan continues, VOP will continue to work with populations affected by conflict and displacement and other stakeholders, to enhance the protective environment of individuals and communities, and identify, prevent and address protection risks. The project objective is aligned with the Protection Cluster and HRP objectives by providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas, and enhancing their resilience and preparedness. The project will target a total of 51191 people (19191 internally displaced persons, 8,000 host communities, 15,000refugees and 9,000returnees. And will focus on three components which includes: Analysis, response and advocacy. VOP will collect protection data and conduct analysis for the purpose of ensuring an in-depth understanding of the protection environment, allowing for appropriate prevention and response activities. In parallel, VOP will ensure protection concerns are captured in protection information products, such as assessment reports, allowing for appropriate and relevant prevention risk mitigation, response and advocacy with the clusters, humanitarian partners, and other main stakeholders. The initial key findings will be communicated to the cluster within 72 hours of return. A full assessment report will then be shared within a week of mission completion. VOP will ensure evidence-based interventions, at the individual and community levels, ensuring appropriate and principled action is taken to prevent and respond to protection risks. While this is a static project that will target urgent humanitarian needs,
VOP focuses its intervention on Pibor County, Jonglei State.
.
VOP will continue to work closely with communities, particularly existing community structures, vulnerable groups and individuals like the elderly and people with disabilities, to identify protection risks, coping strategies, intentions and resilience among the affected population, to ensure preparedness and resilience of communities and at-risk individuals are enhanced. VOP will work with existing community-based protection networks, facilitate focus group discussions, conduct information dissemination, awareness raising, training on human rights, aiming to increase populations’ awareness and, where possible, prevent and mitigate identified protection risks. VOP provides timely, coordinated, tailored and complementary protection prevention, risk mitigation advocacy and responses based on the
Identified protection needs. Protection will be central to all interventions which include service mapping, prevention activities, protection mainstreaming in to other sectors like education, health, among others safe referrals, individual protection assistance for at-risk individuals, support to people with specific Needs (PSNs), information sharing and awareness raising on fundamental rights. VOP adopts an integrated, multi-sector approach, linking Protection with other sectors including Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFIs), Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) education, health and Camp Coordination, HLP and Camp Management (CCCM). VOP will continue to engage with other humanitarian partners and the relevant clusters and coordinating bodies to ensure proper coordination and aid effectiveness. VOP will put focus on the centrality of protection, ensuring the safety and dignity of persons of communities affected by displacement are prioritized and a do-no-harm approach is mainstreamed during all aspects of the project cycle. If the team identifies needs beyond protection, a wider multi-sector assessment and response team could be flexible to be deployed by VOP, through separate funding, with WASH, CCCM and S/NFI response teams, to provide immediate assistance to the most vulnerable and at-risk cases or share with partners of multi sectors to respond to the needs of the people of concern

1 - Needs

In Jonglei (GPAA) like other former states affected by the decades of conflicts in South Sudan, Rape and sexual violence among other protection types have been perpetrated on women and girls during crisis yet opportunities to access basic life-saving services have been limited. The need for multi-sectoral humanitarian response to address the multiple needs of the population and scaling up of existing services to meet the growing demand of most vulnerable and conflict affected population therefore becomes eminent. Pibor counties of former Jonglei State have experienced multiple displacements and insecurity due to internal conflicts and flooding. Key drivers of conflict in Jonglei, specifically in the above-mentioned counties were identified as food insecurity, flooding, cattle raiding/theft for commercial purposes and rising bride prices. COVID-19 affected 1.6 million people who are internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. According to HNO and VOP assessment findings of 2020, Clashes in Pibor also resulted in widespread displacement, livelihood collapse and disruptions to the delivery of HFA throughout the reporting period. According to PLAN International, around 34,000 individuals (11,443 households) were registered at the United Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) Adjacent Area (AA) and several spontaneous displacement sites throughout Pibor Town in early August, 2020. Many people from Pibor Town, Gumuruk, Lekuangole, and Verteth/Durren were also displaced to IDP camps in Labrab, remote villages South East of Pibor town and rural areas as far as Marowa Hills and Boma. Nearly all households displaced to Pibor Town were reported to have returned to Lekuangole and Gumuruk by the end of the August, despite reports that these areas had sustained significant damage. Findings from across the country, South Sudan 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) protection monitoring by static colleagues, as well as assessments conducted by VOP, show how protection risks continue to affect the South Sudanese population and especially in Jonglei state Pibor County. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Pibor County in Jonglei which was classified as the only county in catastrophic need. As a holistic protection intervention, the project will directly support 51191 (20,000 Women, 15,000 girls, 8096 boys and 8094 boys) in Jonglei State, Pibor County. Early/forced marriage is often used as a coping strategy by communities, forcing girls to marry at a younger age. Girls are the first to be deprived of the right to education. Not only boys, but also girls, are often recruited by armed groups, either as soldiers or as domestic servants. Sexual violence is a daily threat for women and girls across the country, especially in Jonglei, Pibor. Services are often unavailable and survivors do not have access to justice. Health care, including reproductive health care, is largely non-existent in hard-to-reach areas. In some areas, health services are available, but people face multiple protection risks when trying to access them. Similarly, children are often required to walk for hours to reach the nearest school, often crossing swamps or streams on their own. Areas of Jonglei, have been affected by clashes recently and as the context is rapidly evolving, there is a need to continuously monitor and analyses the situation, to guide responses and ensure adherence to ‘do no harm’ and protection principles in all interventions.

2 - Project activities

To Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs, VOP will; 1. Train and support medical staff on clinical management of rape (CMR) and survivor centered approach, 2. Conduct Comprehensive case management to GBV survivors, other vulnerable children, persons and survivors. These activities are linked to protection cluster objective and HRP 2021 Strategic Objective. They aim at addressing immediate lifesaving protection needs of the most vulnerable women, and girls in Pibor and Pochalla. By the end of the project, a total of 400 most venerable women, and girls especially GBV survivors, other vulnerable children, persons and survivors are expected to benefit from comprehensive case management services. To Enhance prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience, VOP will; establish Women and Girls friendly spaces to enhance accessing of GBV services to women and girls, 4.Train front line service providers on GBV, referral pathways, survivor centered approaches and psycho social first aid, 5. Establish community based protection networks to provide protection assistance and psycho social support to women, girls and vulnerable people affected by the conflict and flooding, 6. conduct Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services, women’s rights, 7. Conduct door-to-door outreach activities and awareness raising campaigns on protection risks, referral pathway, and key GBV messages and other cross cutting issues. Conduct regular safety audits and develop community based protection safety committees, and strengthen participation of women groups in humanitarian and other community meetings. Procure and distribute Dignity Kits including hygiene and protection items (reusable menstrual pads, underwear, multi purposed cloth, Torch and Soap), 10. Participate in protection needs assessments and Coordination of Protection meetings as a Lead partner for Protection in Pibor. These activities are linked to protection cluster objective and HRP 2021 Strategic Objective 2. They aim at preventing, mitigating, and responding to protection risks including people with disabilities/ GBV of the most vulnerable women, and girls in Pibor and Pochalla. By the end of the project, 20,000 women and girls are expected to benefit from GBV preventive and resilience building services and messaging. To Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations, VOP will; 11. conduct peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence activities including; building capacity of local women groups, women individuals, and women leaders on participation in meetings, mediation and peaceful resolution of conflicts, dispute resolutions, conflict mitigation and so on. Engage the women, and girls on Income Generating Activities (IGA) and livelihood for protective purposes for persons of concern including GBV survivors, other vulnerable persons and survivors, and youth using Cash-Based interventions (CBi) for example embroidery of bed sheets and beads, bakery, fishing, distribution of Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES). These activities are linked to protection cluster objective HRP 2021 Strategic Objective 3. They aim at providing durable solutions to GBV survivors, other vulnerable persons and survivors, and youth and support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks in Pibor.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

VOP has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the VOP. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the VOP to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at WGSS and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter.Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men,women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. VOP’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as sexual exploitation and abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the VOP will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold VOP accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.
