Project Summary

The 2022 Emergency WASH Response in some communities within MEME and NDIAN Divisions of the SouthWest Region within the context of COVID-19 will go a long way to save lives and restore dignity within IDPs and host community members. This project will run for a period of 1 year. Since the beginning of the Socio-political crisis in the North West and South West Regions, many communities within the South West Region has experienced a growth in their population as a result of the movement of people ( IDPs) in to these communities which are relatively safe.
As a result of the increase in population because of the presence of IDPs the communities, there is an urgent need for a WASH response, since the basic WASH facilities can no longer support the population of the IDPs and host community population.
With fundings from partners, the different WASH and COVID-19 activities will be implemented to alleviate suffering and save lives. The activitives are as follows;
-There is a need for Awareness raising on good Hygiene and Sanitation Practices along side COVID-19 preventive measures. This will improve personal hygiene at household level and proper handling of domestic waste and will reduce the risk of contracting infections related to WASH such as cholera, Diarrhoea etc and at the ame time the beneficiaries will be sensitizedon the COVID-19 preventive measures.
-There is also a need for access to safe improved Water sources by Harnessing Springs. Harnessed springs will help to improve the quality and the quantity of the water , building/rehabilitation of catchments and also expanding already existing water systems in these communities will provide adequate quantity of portable water and reduce long waiting time at water points,and also bringing the water close to the communities to ease accessibility for both the elderly people and children hence preventing possible risk of been attacked due to long distances covered to get portable water .
- Provision of WASH Kits including Aquatabs to very vulnerable households and individuals, so that water from the source could be transfered to the house uncontaminated.
- There is the need to construct a gender sensitive ventilated improved Sanitation facilities including pottis for children under 5 years, taken into considerations persons living with disabilities, with handwashing facilities stationed nearby, to avoid open defecation within the communities. this is going to resotre dignity especially in young Girls and Women.
- Provision of Menstrual Hygiene Management ( MHM) Kits to Women and Girls of menstrual age. As a result of limited supplies of MHM Kits in the market in some communities in Meme and Ndian ,the cost of the MHM Kits is high,so there is a need to assist Girls and women to restore their dignity and maintain personal hygiene during their menstrual flow.
During the implementation of this project, we shall be targeting 40000 beneficiaries (Children under the age of 5, the Youths and the elderly persons especially Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and very vulnerable host community members), taken into consideration the sex.
During targeting we shall be selecting beneficiaries base on their vulnerability ,ie both the communities and individual, since the funds are always limited, bearing in mind that assistance is provided based on needs alone.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The needs assessment found significant WASH needs amongst assessed populations, regardless of displacement status or location. Access to water is a key challenge across the area, both in terms of quantity and quality. Overall, KIs reported that only a minority of people manages to secure enough water to meet their needs and that one fifth of the communities rely exclusively on unimproved sources. Limited access to water is mostly due to the fact that people do not have enough containers to fetch and store water, and that existing water points are not sufficient, or are too far. The situation particularly concerns certain strata of the population, such as people living in the bush and, to a lesser extent, in rural villages, likely due to the limited coverage of WASH infrastructure in remote areas. Access to hygiene is very limited. KIs reported that only few people have soap at home and even less have hand washing facilities. This, along with factors linked to knowledge and attitude, contribute to poor hygiene practices, with the overwhelming majority of KIs reporting that only few people or nobody usually washed hands with soap, especially for communities living in the bush and in rural areas, as well as displaced populations. In addition, one out of ten female KIs reported “none” as preferred menstrual material, which is a concern, especially if KIs understood the question more in terms of current practice, rather than preference. The overall sanitation situation is poor. Even though KIs reported latrines as a common place of defecation, half of them said that only few or nobody have access to them. Major issues reportedly limiting access to sanitation are unavailability of latrines, and the fact that the existing ones often do not ensure privacy and gender separation. As a result, open defecation is a widespread practice, especially among communities living in the bush and rural areas.Ownership of key, basic WASH NFIs was found to be the exception, rather than the rule. Only few affected families have enough water containers, mosquito nets and soap. This can be explained by the fact that access to market is limited, especially outside urban centres, and in conflict affected regions especially in the SW. Even when markets are functional, key WASH NFIs, when available, are often unaffordable. The precarious WASH situation is even more concerning considering the health conditions faced by affected populations and their access to health care. Water-related diseases are widespread, with more than half of the KIs reporting that malaria, respiratory diseases and watery diarrhea are the main health issues affecting children. Access to health care remains limited, with more than one third of KIs reporting that people do not visit any health service provider when children are sick, especially those living in the bush or in rural villages.
