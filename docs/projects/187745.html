Project Summary

In 2021, SARD’s conducted a YNA (Yearly Needs Assessment) which observed the needs in various sectors including Food, Non-Food Items, Shelter, and Livelihood sectors. Findings showed that 25% of households reported that their food consumption decreased in the last month. Furthermore, the main challenge relating to food access pertained to the high price of food items in local markets, as reported by 78% of households, the low quality of available food (36%), and the lack of variety of goods in the local market (14%). Additionally, safety and security concerns in markets, particularly for women, were reported as an impediment to access.
On average, 68% of interviewed households rely on income-generating activities (IGA) for subsistence while 16% have no cash sources available. However, preliminary findings show variations based on households’ displacement status with 63% of IDPs having IGA and 17% with no cash sources compared to 87% and 10% respectively for host households. Lack of job opportunities in the local market, lack of financial means to start a business along lack of technical qualifications are the main challenges faced by conflict-affected households. Cash remains the most preferred aid modality for livelihood support, as reported by 55% of households.
To address the wide range of multi-sectoral needs, SARD’s intervention is comprised of the following: emergency and life-sustaining food assistance, NFI and shelter assistance (described in the SNFI document), and ultimately livelihood restoration for affected families.
With a budget of $13,159,765, this intervention will address the immediate needs of approximately 32.500 individuals, (75,000 in 2022, and 75,000 in 2023) over the course of a 24- month period through the provision of lifesaving and life-sustaining food and resilience assistance.
Through a protection lens, SARD’s intervention is designed to ensure that newly displaced and vulnerable families have access to dignified and timely assistance through a robust response mechanism. To ensure that marginalized groups such as female-headed households, the elderly, and persons with disability (PwD) have safe and secure access to assistance, specialized and gender-sensitive activities have formed an integral part of SARD’s response strategy,” the proposal is integrated between FSL and ERL cluster”.

4 - Needs

In SARD’s recent needs assessment, 78% of households interviewed reported the high price of food items in local markets as a significant impediment in their access to food. In order to cope with the lack of food / money to purchase food, households tend to resort to stress and crisis coping strategies such as decreasing spending on non-food essential items and on productive assets, purchasing food on credit, borrowing / sharing food, or selling assets. For example, 25% of households reported a decrease in food consumption in the last month. Additional barriers of access were reported for women or persons with disabilities were cited, as the security and safety in the markets remain a concern. Thus, food continues to rank as the main priority for affected households, followed by access to basic services (fuel, electricity, etc.), hygiene items, shelter, NFI/household items, health care, education, PPE (mask, gloves, etc.), and income-generating activities.households. On average, 68% of households interviewed relied on income-generating activities for sustenance while 16% had no cash sources available.The main driver of food insecurity was the lack of purchasing power at the household level and inability to afford many basic food items due to soaring prices. As a result, access to food has become an increasing concern (according to HNO).

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the multi-sectoral needs as described above, This assistance will be provided and range from emergency food assistance to restoring food production livelihoods. SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have dignified access to life-saving assistance through provision of emergency food through the delivery of cooked meals with bread for one month or RTEs to populations in needs, in close coordination with the FSL and CCCM clusters. Emergency activities will take place as follows: Identification and targeting of newly arrived IDPs, registration and distributions , monitoring and post- monitoring of the emergency support, and close coordination with the relevant clusters including the sharing of the 4Ws. SARD is committed to also provide life-sustaining food to most vulnerable families. This action provides 8-months food assistance for selected MVF, including IDPs and host communities. Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism. Assessment on the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response (cash, voucher, or in-kind). Among the most vulnerable families, SARD will identify families eligible for small-scale food production opportunities, to offer them a sustainable exit strategy from food assistance. The selection will be based on eligibility criteria to gauge the family’s capacity. A BoQ will be signed with selected families and followed by cash grant dispersion.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women headed HH, PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to the socio-cultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures. a) SARD increase visibility for self-referral to ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categories them according to their vulnerabilities. Protection Risk: The high target of the project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEL teams constantly monitors the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities, currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via SARD’s WhatsApp number. Protection Risk: The blanket approach of the program limits the possibility of providing tailored service to person in Need.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: SARD coordinate with protection actors to enhance referral pathways and ensure providing the service based on the needs of beneficiaries.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Due the nature of the project, household visits are required to assess the needs and deliver the services which may expose the most vulnerable individual to the sexual exploitation and abuse.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Through SARD's a) MEL team, and established accountability and complaint mechanism, SARD insure a quick response in case on any incident. B) constantly check the efficiency of the reporting pathways and reviewing SARD’s PSEA policy; c) Employs staff aware of the PSEA policies and run regular refreshing training; d) conduct awareness raising activities to the beneficiaries of the program. Risk: The socio-cultural context in the area may impact the relationship between SARD employees which may results in abuse of power or sexual harassment.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD value a divers and transparent working environment, therefore, it ensure all staff are aware of the PSEA and CoC; b) review the CoC and PSEA on regular basis and updates them accordingly; c) review the complaint mechanism and ensure all staff are aware of them by running refreshing training on regular basis.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: -Locations of project activities are secure and accessible. -Markets are functioning with the availability of food on the market -People of concern (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) are aware of selection and prioritization criteria implemented by SARD -No Covid-19 cases reported in the IDP sites/camps. -SARD staff and targeted communities are aware of Covid-19 threats, risk, and mitigation measures. Risks and Mitigation: -Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries: To prevent these risks, safety precautions will be taken such as alternate routes, changing the warehouse and office locations. Should the situation become volatile, SARD can use a mobile kitchen. -Lack of supplies. To prevent any shortages, SARD will identify alternative options in nearby areas. SARD's warehouse in Syria has 1-2 weeks food provision for the kitchen, and SARD has secured access for additional food supply in Turkey. -Implementation of gender and protection-sensitive measures during activities, including additional technical support when required. - Risk of tension due to selection criteria for the livelihood component. SARD will assess in advance and adopt mitigation measures aiming at adequate distributions of chances - Activities in the context of the Covid-19 pandemic: Standard operation procedures were developed and being implemented by SARD’s teams.
