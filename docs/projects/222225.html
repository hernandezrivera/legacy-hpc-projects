Project Summary

With a goal to facilitate access to education services, improving wellbeing and strengthening protection capacity of students and teachers in the districts of Capo Delgado Province, WeWorld will focus on the following activities:
a) A range of trainings to teachers at the schools hosting children and adolescent from the host communities as well as returnee and IDP. TiCC (Teaching in Crisis Contexts) package will include trainings on MHPSS (Mental Health and Psycho-social Support), GBV (Gender-based Violence), VAC (Violence against Children), Inclusive Education, DRR (Disaster Risk Reduction). MHPSS training will include measures that aim to preserve or promote psychological wellbeing and prevent and counteract mental illness. It will give the tools to minimize stress and strengthen resilience. GBV training will cover GBV key concepts and will focus on mainstreaming GBV risk mitigation. VAC will be part of Child Rights and Child Protection training covering the topics of children’s protection from abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence and right to education. Training on inclusiveness will equip teachers with ability to create a truly inclusive classroom. The learning objectives will cover diversifying/differentiating the curriculum, reflection on attitudes to individual learning and the curriculum, linking learning style to individual student, challenge to attitudes and mindsets. Through DRR training teachers and students will acquire knowledge on fundamental disaster risk concepts and the most globally adopted DRR tools and protocols.
b) Alternative education which includes life-skills training aiming at strengthening students’ ability to meet the challenges of everyday life, to deal with stress and frustration, to develop tools of problem solving and self-confidence building. Alternative education will also include vocational training aimed at youth empowerment and improving their employability.
c) Accelerated education services will assist children through remedial and catch-up classes, as well as ALP (Accelerated Learning program). In remedial classes, children with learning difficulties will be enrolled, in catch-up classes children who are out of school less than 3 months and in ALP - children who are out of school more than 6 months.
d) Teaching and learning materials will be distributed to teachers and students which are vital components of the teaching and learning process. The kits are tools to support learning and aid a learner in concretizing a learning experience and make learning more exciting, interesting and interactive.
e) School-based MHPSS is vital in helping children affected by emergencies continue to learn and thrive. These interventions are essential for promoting and safeguarding students’ and teachers’ mental health and psychosocial wellbeing.
f) The adolescent girls and boys will be targeted through awareness raising campaigns on personal hygiene and reproductive health which will contribute to the enhancement of their physical and mental well-being. This activity will help in educating every youth about sexual and reproductive health, safe sexual practices, preventing sexually transmitted infections and birth control methods.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will target children and adolescents as well as schoolteachers from host communities, IDP (Internally Displaced People) and returnees with specific focus on girls and children with specific needs. The project will aim at improving access to education, formal as well as informal, through remedial and catch-up classes and ALP (Accelerated Learning Program). Besides, MHPSS and artistic activities will be implemented to improve the wellbeing of the students and at the same time to develop their creativity. 5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WeWorld conducted protection risk analysis using PAF (Protection Analytical Framework) developed by GPC (Global Protection Cluster) based on the protection risk equation which considers the vulnerability and capacity associated with identified threat. The protection analysis followed four pillars of PAF: context, current threat to population, effect of threats to population and existing capacities to address threats. The presence of OAG (Organized Armed Groups) in the Northern parts of the province and their unpredictable movements and actions cause frequent displacement of population and disruption of everyday life. This makes displaced families extremely vulnerable, puts them in the condition of extreme poverty and inability for children to attend schools and to continue studies. Institutional analysis showed the scarce capacity of district education authorities to accommodate an increased number of school age children into existing school system and dire necessity of support to ensure access to schooling. Besides lack of access to education, there is a high likelihood that protection risks will increase, and negative coping mechanisms will be adopted to overcome trauma and distress caused by displacement such as substance abuse, avoidance and denial, isolation, physical and psychological violence in general and especially against women and girls, negligence and lack of care towards children. Unstable security environment remains a main threat, and it makes impossible for the families to take long term decisions, to settle down and to improve their socio-economic conditions. Children also remain under constant threat of not having access to education and not being able to pursue their studies. WeWorld applies "Do No Harm" principle to all projects aiming at ensuring that aid and interventions do not unintentionally cause harm to the communities they intend to help. To mitigate risk, WeWord takes proactive measures to prevent and alleviate adverse consequences for the affected population. The project will equally provide education and education related services to the school age children of host communities, returnees and IDP as well as teachers. Egalitarian approach to different groups of the communities reduces the risk of tension and conflict among different communities and community members. Community Engagement is an approach that WeWorld actively applies at each phase of project cycle management. WeWorld establishes AAP (Accountability to Affected Population) mechanisms such as feedback and complaint systems, helping ensure that affected communities can voice their concerns and that they can respond appropriately, ensure that aid is delivered effectively, ethically, and transparently. Local communities are fully involved and engaged in the planning and implementation of projects to ensure that their needs and perspectives are considered. They are part of decision-making processes, which ensures that the projects are culturally appropriate and respectful. WeWorld is also conscious and sensible about protection mainstreaming. It integrates protection principles into all aspects of project management and prioritizes the safety, dignity, and rights of affected populations.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

To ensure meaningful access to assistance WeWorld applies principles which strengthen participation and empower people and communities ensuring that all children regardless of their abilities, backgrounds, or language proficiency, can effectively access and benefit from the education services and programs. Language consideration – despite Portuguese being a language of education, mother tongues of the students are taken into consideration, the teachers knowing these languages support the students ensuring they can understand and use education services effectively. Non-Discrimination - ensuring that access to education is based on equity and fairness, no individual or group is excluded and every child, has opportunity to enjoy the education services with specific focus on girls’ access to education services.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

WeWorld applies disability inclusion principle to ensure that students with disabilities have equal access to education and are fully integrated into the school community. WeWorld pays attention to accessible Infrastructure of schools ensuring that schools have accessible facilities, such as ramps to accommodate students with physical disabilities. An inclusive curriculum will be designed allowing students with disabilities to participate fully in all learning activities. This might involve using assistive technologies, providing materials in accessible formats, and adapting teaching methods. Teachers’ Training is one of the key components of the project and specifically training on inclusive education practices. This includes understanding different types of disabilities and developing strategies to support diverse learning needs. Community and parental involvements are a crucial component of inclusion of children with disabilities into the school system. This can help create a supportive environment for students with disabilities and ensure that their needs are met both at school and at home. A range of awareness raising campaigns on inclusiveness and disabilities will take place for students, teachers, parents and wider communities. This will promote inclusive education and ensure that schools have the necessary resources and support to implement inclusive practices.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Including environmental considerations into education projects is essential for fostering sustainability and environmental awareness among students. WeWorld will integrate environmental topics into the students’ curriculum as an additional subject and will conduct awareness sessions on environment such as climate change and DRR (Disaster Risk Preparedness). Students and teachers will get familiar with the strategies and practices aimed at minimizing the damage caused by natural and human-induced hazards. Knowledge of DRR will make them more resilient and less vulnerable to disasters. DRR curriculum will involve a range of activities, including a) risk assessment: identifying and analyzing potential hazards and vulnerabilities. b) mitigation: implementing measures to reduce the impact of disasters, such as building flood defenses or enforcing buildings. c) preparedness: ensuring students and teachers and communities are ready to respond to disasters through early warning systems and emergency planning. d) response: providing immediate assistance during and after a disaster to save lives and reduce suffering. e) recovery: supporting communities to rebuild and recover, aiming to restore normalcy and improve resilience against future disasters. DRR curriculum will also include project-based learning and will engage students and teachers in hands-on projects that address real-world environmental challenges, for example, developing recycling programs. Outdoor education methodology will be applied during the DRR course. Outdoor spaces will be utilized for learning to help students and teachers to develop a direct connection with nature. Field trips, nature walks, and outdoor classrooms can enhance students' appreciation and understanding of the environment. DRR activity not only will target students and teachers but also will involve the entire communities through awareness raising campaigns, sessions and workshops.
