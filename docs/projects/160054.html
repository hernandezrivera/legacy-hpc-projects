Project Activities

UNHCR supports returnees with access to basic needs and essential services through cash assistance. UNHCR provides two direct multipurpose cash grants to returnees to re-establish their livelihoods and accelerate their re-integration. Each returnee receives a one-time reinstallation grant during the return travel to Somalia (this cash grant is covered of the refugee response as it is part of the assistance provided at the way stations). In addition, each returnee household receives subsistence allowance in six instalments with first instalment released one month after their arrival. The support depends on the Household size as follows:• HH size 1-3 persons: $100/month• HH size 3-6 pers.: $120/month• HH size 7-9 pers.: $150/month• HH size 10-11 pers.: $180/month• HH size 11+ pers.: $200/month• PSNs: an extra $20-50/monthThis is aligned to the MEB average of $100 per month for HH size of 6 persons, excluding rental subsidies. The subsistence allowance will be up to 6 months and will be based on a vulnerability assessment. This allowance is regarded multi sectorial support for non-refugee as the returnees are no longer refugees and their assistance is covered as part of the cluster response. UNHCR will continue to foster the achievement of protection outcomes through cash assistance, based on the evidence that cash inherently reduces protection risks.

Mitigation Measures

UNHCR is reaching out to better understand protection challenges and to strengthen the community’s “protection networks” so that they can respond to protection problems and facilitate access to institutions and available services. Building trust with the local authorities, service providers and the international community will require continuous investment, both in areas of displacement and in return areas. The participation of the displaced population needs to be ensured through the establishment of committees coupled with the presence of community mobilisers, including for the provision of information on essential services, available assistance and returning process. Participation of women in community-based committees is rare and needs to be reinforced to guarantee a truly participatory approach and give voice to the whole community. In some areas progress has been made, and UNHCR will further strengthen community structures and engage with all persons of concern through age and gender sensitive approaches. Although it is not mandatory, UNHCR will promote that assistance recipients be female heads of households, or at least be involved in the decision making of how assistance is utilized at household level. • Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence (2000 characters max.).Returnees and IDPs are affected in line with the general population by the ongoing insecurity and fragile political environment in Somalia. The insecurity results in restrictions in humanitarian access and restraints for persons of concern to move freely and in safety, further exacerbated by inter-clan conflicts. Threats targeted at beneficiary communities often force humanitarian organizations to postpone the implementation of affected projects until they receive security assurances for their staff and the beneficiary community. Due to practical considerations related to humanitarian access, concrete reintegration support and assistance can only be provided in areas of the country with relative stability and where UNHCR and partners are currently present and/or can establish operations. • UNHCR will continue to monitor closely all protection incidents occurred within the context of forced displacement in Somalia. By advocating and putting in place response systems, every protection incident should get a referral or direct assistance. Additionally, the information gathered through a thorough monitoring system feeds into the wider system to better analyze main protection incidents in the country and better tailor programming to needs. • Influenced by the HCT’s Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will pilot monitoring in areas which are occupied by armed non-state actors. Especially in South West State and Jubbaland State, individuals will be identified with strong connections in areas which are currently not included in humanitarian programming and assessments. Expected is that these Independent Monitors will feed protection related information to UNHCR and the wider humanitarian community on specific protection needs among these communities.

Please provide further explanation

The protection space in Somalia is fragile, but Somali refugees continues to return and they still require support to reintegrate and start investing in livelihoods and durable solutions. UNHCR, will work with government and other partners to create bridges to bring displacement on to the development agenda and find permanent solutions through local integration. Market surveys will be conducted to determine the sustainability and viability of livelihood opportunities. In the mid-term and longer term UNHCR aims to focus less on individual assistance and increase community-based assistance. In the shorter term, individual support is required to ensure returnees can pay their expenses, until they find opportunities, in order for the return to be sustainable. UNHCR will continue to advocate for returnees to be part of any future government-led social protection initiatives and other recovery to development programmes being implemented in areas of return.
