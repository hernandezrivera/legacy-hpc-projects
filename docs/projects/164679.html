Project Summary

The project will help to address the education community needs in 4 camps in Sarmada (Rfie’in, AlBarri, Abu Nawas, and Alyousuf Camps) and in Daret Azza SD in addition to Afrin in Northern rural of Aleppo.
In Sarmad and Daret Azza,the project aims to:
1.Reach out to 5000 school aged children with BTL campaign in the two areas and conducting placement test during the campaign to identify children students levels and grades.
2. Raising community awareness of importance of education process and participating in declining child labour by encouraging parents to resume sending their children to schools.
3. Establishment of 4 non-formal education centers in 4 Samrada camps and one centre in Daret Azza
4.Provide 1840 children (900 boys and 940 girls) (8-15 yeas) in four camps of Sarmada and 1100 children (600 girls and 500 boys) (8-15 years) in Daret Azza with the below activities:
a. Provision of remedial classes to support the children in restoring missing knowledge and level up with their peers. The curriculum to be used for the remedial classes is Short version of Syrian Interim Government SIG curriculum.
b. Providing them with life skills and citizenship education programs in non-formal settings.
c. Enlighten children students with "I DEAL" courses as supportive materials to provide them with practical and discovery interest.
5. Providing 130 teachers and education personnel with incentives, and providing them with teaching resources, kits and guides.
6.Building the capacity of the teachers , education personnel in the schools ,in addition to 70 education actors from the local authorities and humanitarian workers on in crisis-sensitive data collection and analysis and dissemination, relevant standards, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and INEE Minimum Standards, and/or advocacy on the needs and rights of children in crisis contexts.
6.Equip the learning centres with Child Protection specialists to provide needed services and referrals.
7.Establishment of gender-sensitive school wash facilities which are taking into consideration the people with disabilities as well.
And in Afrin , we are going to :
1.Reach out to 2000 school aged children with BTL campaign in the two areas and conducting placement test during the campaign to identify children students’ levels and grades.
2. Raising community awareness of importance of education process and participating in declining child labor by encouraging parents to resume sending their children to schools.
3. Supporting two learning centres in Afrin
4.Provide 900 children (400 boys and 500 girls) (8-15 yeas) with the below activities:
a. Provision of remedial classes to support the children in restoring missing knowledge and level up with their peers. The curriculum to be used for the remedial classes is Short version of Syrian Interim Government SIG curriculum.
b. Providing them with life skills and citizenship education programs in non-formal settings.
c. Enlighten children students with "I DEAL" courses as supportive materials to provide them with practical and discovery interest.
5. Providing 70 teachers and education personnel with incentives. and providing them with teaching resources, kits and guides.
6.Building the capacity of the teachers , education personnel in the schools ,in addition to 30 education actors from the local authorities and humanitarian workers on in crisis-sensitive data collection and analysis and dissemination, relevant standards, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and INEE Minimum Standards, and/or advocacy on the needs and rights of children in crisis contexts.
7.Equip the learning centres with Child Protection specialists to provide needed services and referrals.
8.Rehabilitation of wash facilities taking into consideration the gender marker and the people with disabilities.
9.Maintenance and running costs of 7 schools and provision of safety and security equipment.

4 - Needs

An estimated 2.1 million children are out-of-school knowing that 31.3% of the entire 20m population in Syria are school-age children (5-17 years) according to HNO 2019. UNICEF, in a 2018 report indicated that over 3.3 million children inside Syria have been exposed to explosive hazards which threaten serious injury, loss of limbs or death. These impacts are made worse by limited access to essential services. The HNO 2019 states that there are 14,205 school-age children (5-17 years) in Daret Azza with only 296 teachers;and there are 24,540 school-age children (5-17 years) - in Sarmada with only 511 teachers.In Afrin and as of the EDU Severity documents , 25,901 Children in Need (11752 Boys and 14149 girls) and it is hosting 863 teachers and education personnel (392 Male and 472 Female) This project is designed on community-based approach by involving beneficiaries in design phase, as POINT has conducted RNA to know the actual and updated needs of the areas and then in involving community in awareness campaigns of education importance.most of the experienced teachers have left the country and the one who stayed there ,as per POINT RNA in the three mentioned areas, are in serious need of being supported with training courses that can help them to deal with children in this situation of crisis. Teachers and education personnel salaries are not regularly covered and many schools lack of funding.Big number of children are not going to school du to the bad economic situation of their families who are not able to secure the educational needs for them.So we are going to provide them with the needed learning kits and stationaries to enable them to re-join their formal education. The schools available in the areas are crowded and can’t receive more children from the IDPs who are with average of 45% of the populations in the three areas , So we need to establish four schools in Samrmada , and one in Daret Azza and we will support one non-supported school in Afrin.

5 - Implementation of activities

POINT is going to conduct the following activities in Saraqeb in Idlib and in Daret Azza and Afrin in Aleppo Governorate.POINT will -Provide children and youth with non-formal education programs (including catch-up classes, self-learning program (SLP), remedial education, literacy and numeracy classes) -Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns, and establish Parent Teacher Associations.-Provide professional development to teaching staff and education personnel (child-centred and protective pedagogy, active learning, self-learning, life skills, Curriculum B, ECCE, New Curriculum, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE minimum standards (MS)).-Provide teachers and education personnel with incentives.-Provide teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides in formal and non-formal settings.-Provide children with life skills and citizenship education programs in formal and non-formal settings.-Provide children with supplementary learning materials for children attending formal and non-formal education.-Build the capacity of humanitarian actors in crisis-sensitive data collection and analysis and dissemination, relevant standards, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and INEE Minimum Standards, and/or advocacy on the needs and rights of children in crisis contexts.-Support or establish school-based governance and accountability mechanisms to support school operations.- Establishment of gender-sensitive school wash facilities which are taking into consideration the people with disabilities as well in the 7 schools. In addition to the safety and security equipment.-Equip 7 Schools with referral mechanisms.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Main Protection key risks to the project are: 1- Children students are not interested in attending remedial classes Likelihood: Medium level of impact: high Ways we could reduce the risk or weaken its impact: Conduct awareness sessions for parents and children on importance of remedial classes and make students, parents and teachers meetings to explain remedial classes approach. monitor for this risk: Include indicator and awareness sessions in monitoring in M&E. What resources, if any, would be required to mitigate and/or monitor the risk: Teachers and project field coordinators Is this an acceptable level of risk? Do we go forward with the activity? It is acceptable, yes. 2- Child labor prevents children from attending remedial classes. Likelihood: Medium level of impact: High Ways we could reduce the risk or weaken its impact: Coordinate with organization providing livelihoods and protection working in the project locations monitor for this risk: Include high coordination plan within the project. What resources, if any, would be required to mitigate and/or monitor the risk: POINT field staff and management will conduct meetings Is this an acceptable level of risk? Do we go forward with the activity? It is acceptable, yes. 3- Volatility of the security situation, conflict and clashes: especially in a conflict zone as Daret Azzah Likelihood: Medium level of impact: High Ways we could reduce the risk or weaken its impact: Redesign work-plan conduct activities that might not cause any harm while in conflict monitor for this risk: Risk assessment What resources, if any, would be required to mitigate and/or monitor the risk: Project manager and safety and security coordinator Is this an acceptable level of risk? Do we go forward with the activity? It is acceptable, yes.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: military clashes within Daret Azzah SD and/or Sarmada preventing students access to learning centers Mitigation measures: - coordinate with Parent Teachers Associations PTAs - Suspend activities till peace is recovered. - coordinate with POINT program team to resume providing services in nearby safe areas once safety is taking long time to be restored Risk 2: Risk of long distance and young children walking alone. Children may get lost, be attacked or lose interest in learning Mitigation measures: - establishment of complaint mechanisms in school - Training of partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools specific child safety policies POINT has experience in successfully delivering various kinds of project despite ongoing conflict. Additionally, we have designed security plans for the team and participants according to different scenarios. Our primary priority is the safety and security of the beneficiaries; therefore, we will conduct safety and security assessments prior to implementation and update the safety plans accordingly. And accessible accountability mechanism will be used in the project, known communication lines as well. Do no harm Standards will be applied at all stages of implementation and the project life cycle. In order to do that, POINT will conduct baseline assessments to ensure that none of the project activities will harm the community in any way. Additionally, POINT will conduct security risk assessments and safety and security reports in order to ensure the safety and security of project beneficiaries
