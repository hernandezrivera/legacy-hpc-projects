Project Summary

Established in 2006 and an active member of S-NFI Cluster since 2011, SSUDA has long experience in emergency S-NFIs response in hard-to reach locations. Populations in Upper Nile, Jonglei and other areas targeted by this project are facing life threatening situation as a result unprecedented flooding, consequences of conflict and outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic. With most counties classified in IPC Phase 4 with possibility of sliding to IPC Phase 5 the affected population faced with multiple displacements, while prerequisite conditions for returning populations remains unbearable. The IDP hosting communities are equally vulnerable. The presence of IDPs further undermines the coping abilities of host communities by increasing demand and competition for already strained natural resources, food and basic services such as health and education and often leading to tension and HLP issues. Through this project seeks to support the S-NFI Cluster Objectives 1-5 and contribute to 2021 HRP SO 1 & 2 by providing safe and appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to 6,500 HHs – 40,000 individuals (14,800); women (17,296); children (5,600) and PWD (2,400) from IDPs, Returnees and Host community households from 7 counties in the Upper Nile (Nasir, Fashoda, Baliet, Melut and Manyo) Jonglei (Pibor) and Eastern Equatoria State (Kapoeta East). The project will support 40,000 newly displaced men, women and children include people with disabilities in emergency or transitional situation with NFIs, Other target groups will be protracted IDPs, returnees and Hots communities. The project will also inculcate a recovery and disaster resilience components through Community-Managed Disaster Risk Reduction Approach which SSUDA has successfully adopted from EU and Cordaid funded PRO-Resilience projects. SSUDA has static (projects) presence or planned to undertake new projects in the target locations, therefore will ensure an integration approach with other sectors such as Food Security and Livelihoods, WASH, Nutrition, CCCM, and Protection (HLP). SSUDA will use both in-kind and cash-based approaches to support the vulnerable with shelter and non-food-item needs using Cluster approved methodologies including assessment of functionality of markets in affected areas. Women owned businesses will be targeted with the CBI approaches which SSUDA has previously used successfully. For in-kind approach, emergency shelter and NFIs supplies will be acquired from IOM (Core-Pipeline) and distributed bases on assessed needs. With experienced S-NFIs staff and in Juba and field, SSUDA will have capacity to conduct mobile interventions in the target locations. In all stages of the project planning, implementation and M&E, SSUDA will ensure gender and age consideration including in the disaggregation of data and the targeting of female ¬headed households, incorporating gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs assessments and response recommendations. SSUDA will ensure S-NFIs are effectively and sufficiently distributed to the most people in need and that feedback mechanism is well in place including carrying out age and gender-sensitive Post Distribution Monitoring Assessment. While responding to immediate needs of the affected population, SSUDA will ensure that the local capacities and resilience of the affected population are strengthened to enable them bounce back from the crisis and further withstand future shocks through formation, training and awareness raising of local committees on HLP, natural resource management, Disaster resilience, Peace building, Climate Change Adaptation and prevention and response to Gender-based violence COVID-19 pandemic. As a State Co-Focal Point SSUDA will work closely with SFP to coordinate and support the Cluster at State level by participating in Cluster meeting, conduct joint assessments, provide relevant information to partners and support capacity strengthening of new partners.

1 - Needs

Compounded by vicious cycle of inter/intra-communal conflicts, influx of returnees, annual floods and the outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic, majority of people in South Sudan are not only facing devastation of livelihoods and assets but the situation has also left approximately 8.3 million people (out of country’s 12.1 million) majority women and children in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection from violence, abuse, exploitation, human rights violation and impacts of Covid-19 (HNO, 2021). Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei are three of the 10 states with highest number of affected population with large numbers of population experiencing multiple displacements while other are living in dilapidated conditions in protracted IDP camps include POCs UNMISS, 2020). Based on the statistics from the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis conducted in October – November 2020, an estimated 7.37 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity during the period of April to July 2021 and require urgent assistance include Emergency Shelter and lifesaving NFIs. With already compromised conditions, 1.4 million children (under 5) who will likely suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 are at more risk of life-threatening diseases such as Malaria cholera, diarrhea unless they are provided by emergency Shelter and lifesaving NFIs. While this happens, new displacement continues to occur as a result of vicious cycle of inter-communal conflict and flooding in most parts of Upper Nile. From July 2020, nearly 1.3 million people in South Sudan include vulnerable children and women in 43 counties have been affected by floods that has caused damage on shelters, livelihoods and community infrastructure such as water points, markets, schools, hospitals, bridges and roads (SSUDA, Flood Assessment Report, January 2021). Whenever the displacement occurs IDPs are left with no chance to carry their belonging to the new IDP settlements. In many cases, the IDPs are already so vulnerable and cannot access shelter and NFIs. Returnees are already walked long distances on foot back to their villages where there is nothing to kick start their lives. The IDP hosting areas are equally vulnerable communities. The presence of IDPs and returnees further undermines the coping abilities for the host communities by increasing demand and competition for natural resources, food and basic services such as health and education and often leads to tension and HLP issues (IOM, 2021). GBVIMS reports indicate that 96% of 502,434 GBV survivors’ cases are women and girls and are caused by general violence and intimate partners at home. Women and girls living with disabilities are worst affected by violence who are also exposed to SGBV in their homes due to various factors including lack of sufficient shelter as well as when they are undertaking life-sustaining activities such as water and collecting firewood (UNMISS Report, 2020). According UNHCR, despite self-sponsored return of 345,00 refugees since 2017, conditions are not yet considered conducive for the safe, dignified and sustainable returns (UNHCR, October 2020). In some areas in Upper Nile conflict between returnees, IDPs and host communities over land and property ownership have threatened peaceful coexistence and further stagnates the much anticipated Return, Resettlement and Reintegration programme (UNMISS, 2020). The integration of Peace building efforts into humanitarian interventions in South Sudan have been recommended by recent assessment reports.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SSUDA Cash Based Interventions is intended to contribute to the recovery of local markets and financial infrastructure following disruptions caused by flood, conflict and COVID-19 pandemic. Throughout the country, people have been facing price inflation and a shortage of hard cash, which has reduced their purchasing power and further put vulnerable households at risk. SSUDA has considered options to scale up cash-based interventions as an effective means of assisting vulnerable households. In 2015- 2019 SSUDA has been using CBI approach in its main responses include S-NFIs identified critical information in terms of market functionality and prices of basic goods and thus making the CBI an integral part of intervention. A key milestone is the integration of CBI at field levels where teams provide regular and accurate information on the prices and availability of basic food and non-food items. The field officers collect and analyze data on the prices of basic items and assess the market in their respective locations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

After careful market assessment, analysis and based on recommendations of Market functionality, SSUDA will apply Cash-Based Interventions (or CBIs) including cash assistance (unrestricted or restricted) where beneficiaries (households) will be provided with vouchers or cash to directly purchase and access NFIs and Shelter materials to specific suppliers, specific items and a set of determined value. Based on the assessed context SSUDA will use most appropriate modalities electronic cash transfer; hard cash payments; electronic vouchers; and paper vouchers. Cash and voucher system has become the most preferred approach of supporting communities affected by emergencies. It provides affected population with greater choice, flexibility and transparency through their ability to choose what basic items their household needs and when to shop. CBI empowers beneficiaries, particularly women, reduce negative coping mechanisms and have broader multiplier effects for both IDPs/returnee and host community populations than traditional in-kind assistance. AS SSUDA, vouchers will allow for more flexible provision of NFIs and reduce pressure on logistics chains. Site monitoring and post distribution monitoring will also allow SSUDA to understand beneficiaries’ shopping preferences and needs and identify vulnerable households for a follow up support. CBI will have wider benefits for host communities especially the small local business include those owned by youth and women groups through Village Savings and Loans Association. SSUDA will collaborate with local business to implement cash and voucher in the affected areas with market functionalities. Replacing a range of in-kind items with cash in any form also reduces the resale of distributed items by beneficiaries which immediately decreases the value and impact of the provided assistance, tends to distorted local markets and negatively impacts neighboring host communities.

Further, while developing the beneficiary targeting list and the registration system, SSUDA will allow female and child-headed households priority access during distribution times and have all safety arrangement and equitable access for these vulnerable groups including people living with disabilities. In addition, SSUDA will hire and train volunteers and temporary workers (men and women) who will work daily in collaboration with AAP committee members to help with security, data collection and distribution of Shelter NFIs materials. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSUDA recognizes that women, girls, boys and men all have very different needs, interests and vulnerabilities. Vulnerable groups such as women, girls, people with disabilities and elderly can be taken advantage of and stigmatized. SSUDA will target and ensure that perspectives from these vulnerable groups are included in the consultation and decision making process as well as implementing a system for people to submit complaints and issues that arise. This will allow the participation of women and other vulnerable groups to get prioritized and ensure their access to shelter and NFIs and other humanitarian assistance. Through needs assessment, registration, monitoring and evaluation and Post distribution monitoring, SSUDA will be able to collect sex and age disaggregated data and monitor whether these vulnerable groups are receiving adequate assistance.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SSUDA will communicate and provide information for all stakeholders including cluster and the beneficiaries about the interventions through various means and channels. The organization will first conduct initial community consultative meetings with the community leaders and representative of the beneficiaries to solicit their opinions and manage expectation. The meetings will provide opportunity to identify AAP committees and to review the project plans. AAP committee will be the connection between SSUDA and community and a channel for communicating information and feedback from the community. Other means include complaint feedback during distribution and Post-distribution monitoring. SSUDA understand that IDPs hosting areas are equally vulnerable communities. the presence of IDPs may further undermine the copying abilities of host communities by increasing demand and competition for natural resources including land, food and basic services such as education and health care leading to tension and conflict. At the same time, displacement could be source of conflict and generation for fear of loss land as well as IDP's inability to return to return home. In order to address HLP and conflict that may arise between IDPs and Host Communities, SSUDA will 1) Ensure that Host communities and displaced persons are engaged at initial stage to understand that emergency shelters are not permanent residency but temporary support during crisis; 2) Mutual agreement on the locations of IDP shelters which are environment friendly and accessible to basic services such as schools, health centre and water sources; 3) establish and train HLP committees comprising of the host community and IDPs representatives to plan, monitor, report and address issues related to HLP and conflict; 4) Create linkage with Protection Cluster partners to promote human rights of displaced persons include working with traditional justice system to ensure HLP Rights are addressed; 5) SSUDA to continue consultation with affected population to develop preferred transition and long term shelter strategies to be included in developmental project proposals for long-term shelter solution.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The reasons most often given for establishing a feedback mechanism are to support accountability, transparency, empowerment, monitoring and evaluation, and programme improvement, and to provide early warning of impending problems. Handling and investigations of sensitive complaints from individuals or affected communities (e.g. fraud, corruption, abusive behavior, sexual exploitation or child abuse) require specific expertise and must be managed according to the specific procedures and standards. SSUDA has developed different channels of Feedback and Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM). Whilst there are occasions where complaints are handled well by field staff, there are many examples when the beneficiaries are not satisfied and AAP Committee is tasked to handle such cases. At times, staff, already overwhelmed with day to day emergency activities, may find it difficult to manage the informal feedback and complaint they receive, might not prioritize complaints, or might forget or lose complaint. SSUDA’s CRM involves multiple approaches such as Static and collective channel by SSUDA staff at field and Coordination office in Malakal and include appropriate face-to-face discussions with complaints, Help desk at office and distribution sites. With support from FAO, SSUDA is in the process of setting up Hotline Feedback system with MTN telephone service provider. AAP Committee members are trained and has a role and responsibility to consult, plan and participate in CRM activities. Other channels of receiving complaints are feedback surveys and individual and HHS questionnaires during monitoring and evaluation visits and PDM. To support SSUDAs Feedback and CRM, a 15-member county level accountability to affected population (AAP) committees will be established in each county comprising of the equal number of women and men to work with SSUDA to deliver the project effectively. The membership of the AAP committees will represent community leaders, local authority, women, youth and vulnerable and PLWD.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Shelter solutions will be provided to ensure the affected vulnerable population has adequate space for physical protection from severe extreme weather conditions as well as privacy and dignity for family life. SSUDA will coordinate with the protection Cluster to ensure housing, land and property issues are considered in site selection, (in coordination with State, county, local authorities and community leaders), to avoid not only discrimination but future conflicts and forced evictions.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Multiple displacements weaken the coping strategies of IDPs, returnees and host communities. By supporting the displaced and other vulnerable populations with shelter and NFI assistance, SSUDA will ensure that the communities’ resilience is strengthened (especially amongst the most vulnerable populations). Shelter and NFIs provides the bedrock of recovery from extreme shock of displacement and relief from severe food insecurity, economic meltdown and impact of COVID-19 pandemic. In addition, by providing appropriate shelter SSUDA will be safeguarding vulnerable people against common diseases. Specifically, Vector-borne diseases such as malaria can be controlled through a variety of initiatives, including appropriate site selection and shelter provision.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The timely provision of emergency shelter and NFIs are life-saving assistance to people displaced by conflicts and natural disasters. SSUDA will coordinate with other Cluster partners (FSL, WASH, Protection, Health etc) to ensure the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, include people with Disabilities, female-headed households and unaccompanied minors, on arriving at their place of displacement or return, have the same access to shelter and basic services monitoring and other interventions as necessary include access to information on COVID-19.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Over the past decade a consensus has emerged amongst global health analysts that there exists a crucial link between shelter and the prevention of epidemics. The most pressing concern relates to the link between hypothermia and inadequate shelter. Malnourished children under five living in inadequate shelter are at serious risk during cold weather. Without the foundation that Shelter and NFIs provide to the affected population, the ability to cope with poor Nutrition is widely diminished and it is more difficult to improve Sanitation in dilapidated conditions. The provision of non-food items such as cooking sets will be considered so as to improve the food utilization of the households.
