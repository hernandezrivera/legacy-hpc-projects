Project Summary

The project aims to provide cost-efficient, safe, dignified and climate-resilient access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services to IDPs, returnees and host communities living in extreme and catastrophic conditions in South Sudan.
For that, IOM’s interventions will focus on locations identified by the WASH Cluster as intersectoral priority 1 counties (Rubkona, Malakal, Renk) and priority 2 counties (Abyei and Aweil). IOM will continue being the sole provider of integrated WASH services for over 188,235 people in South Sudan’s two largest IDP sites in Bentiu and Malakal. Additionally, IOM will continue to be the main water supply provider for returnees in transit centers in Renk during 2025. With a focus on cholera and flood response, IOM will extend its operation to support high-impact WASH interventions with a focus on water supply in response to acute needs in Rubkona, Bentiu, and Malakal towns of Unity State and Upper Nile, and Northern Bhar el Gazal and Abye Administrative Area.
IOM’s HRP project contributes to the 5 WASH Cluster Strategic Objectives, focusing on integrating WASH programming with Nutrition, providing access to basic WASH services in PoC/Camps, whilst mitigating WASH GBV and reducing community’s exposure to environmental hazards. In total, IOM plans to reach approximately 366,578 individuals (88,096 women, 83,388 men, 95,967 girls, 99,127 boys) by deploying a range of activity packages that combine hardware and software interventions, adapted to the context at play and the needs of the most affected population. IOM will put emphasis on the reduction of aid dependence by minimizing operation and maintenance costs across the entire operational cycle. Emphasis will also be given to self-governance and effective capacitation of local authorities and local communities to take over existing WASH infrastructure and services in Bentiu and Malakal. These hardware interventions will be complemented with in-kind and/or cash-based distribution of essential hygiene items, like soap and sanitary pads. All activities will contribute towards mitigating protection risks and to improve accessibility to people with special needs.
Where needed, activities will be adjusted to be climate resilient and/or to build the capacity of communities to respond quicker to natural disasters. Where possible, IOM will also prioritize market-based programming approaches, using local markets to support WASH responses. A special emphasis will be given to the localization of high-impact activities with a focus on risk communication, hygiene promotion and community engagement, and the construction of essential sanitation infrastructure (latrines and showers), wherever possible.

As the sole WASH service provider for the protracted displaced individuals in Malakal, and the IDP Sites and Camp in Bentiu. IOM will provide WASH services through the operation and maintenance of safe and accessible water supply systems, care and maintenance of accessible and secure sanitation and handwashing facilities, as well as management of solid and liquid waste. IOM will also continue investing in cost-effective, and climate-resilient means of ensuring adequate service provision within these settings with the focus on the minimization of O&M costs by retrofitting existing water supply and sanitation infrastructure and services, and the monetization of essential WASH services to generate revenue to effectively facilitate the takeover by local authorities. IOM will put special emphasis on engaging local authorities, local businesses and local communities to build their capacity for the operation and maintenance of water supply, sanitation, faecal sludge and solid waste management infrastructure and services in the Bentiu, Malakal PoC IDP camps, Rubkona, Bentiu and Malakal towns. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) measures will also be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle.

1. Needs

South Sudan's remains a protracted crisis context, affected by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints, and public health challenges, including the impact of climate change resulting in extraordinary flooding. A big part of South Sudan's population (9 million people) will need humanitarian assistance in 2025 (only 2.02 million are IDPs, and 2.3 million are returnees) – 5.7 million of which will have inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions are predicted to be the most severe across Upper Nile, Unity, Abyei Region and Ghazal states – all areas where IOM plans to intervene through HRP. WASH conditions will continue to be dire due to results in 2024. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. Approximately 59 percent of the total population lack access to safe water while 90 percent lack access to improved sanitation. Some 60 percent of the population practice open defecation. The impact of floods, linked to the effects of climate change, the ongoing Sudan crisis, and high levels of food insecurity have increased water and sanitation needs. The WASH Cluster further predicts that the combination between the poor access to WASH services describe above, combined with elevated levels of food insecurity, will continue to have a detrimental impact on children's health, leading to malnutrition and stunting. – all states where Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold. PoCs camps and IDP sites will continue to be at risk of disease outbreaks, climate change side effects and conflict. Several exogenous drivers, such as conflict, climate change, outbreaks, funding gaps, Sudan crisis, as well as chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure and service provision, will also continue to hamper access to WASH services; which, combined with the high number of people in need, will only add more pressure to already scarce WASH infrastructure. Due to ongoing flood levels in 2024 it is also likely that there will be an increase in waterborne diseases and added WASH needs in the most affected/at-risk states. The country is still prone to ethnic and politically motivated conflict coupled with localized violence and disputes over land and cattle, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH infrastructures.

2. Project activities

IOM’s activity package per each WASH Cluster SO: SO 1: • Development of water resources in response to acute and protracted emergencies at institutional facilities (health and nutrition sites). In response to acute emergency needs (cholera outbreaks, floods, etc.), borehole chlorination and/or bucket chlorination at water collection points, and/or household water treatment and storage supplies to cover at least for two months.• Sanitation infrastructure, including integrated faecal sludge management, institutional facilities (health and nutrition sites).• Environmental health infrastructure, including solid waste management, institutional facilities (health and nutrition sites).• Distribution of essential household water treatment and hygiene items, i institutional facilities (health and nutrition sites). SO 2.1: • Provision of water supply, sanitation, faecal sludge management, environmental health, and distribution of essential household water treatment and hygiene items in response to protracted and acute emergency needs for displaced individuals. • Provision of essential water supply, and sanitation services across points of entries (PoEs) and transit centers in response to Sudan crisis returnees. • In protracted IDP Sites, to operationalize the IOM’s Self-Reliance, Resilience and Livelihoods Strategy to Deliver WASH Services that revolve around the minimization of O&M costs by retrofitting existing infrastructure and services, and the monetization of essential WASH services to generate revenue in order to effectively facilitate the takeover by local authorities. • In response to acute needs, IOM will implement high-impact water supply activities in Unity State, Upper Nile, Western and Northern Bahr El Ghazal and Abyei regions. • In response to acute needs , IOM will promote community engagement in sanitation, by constructing of emergency and durables latrines at the institutional level and household level. SO 2.2:• Triangulate conflict sensitivity and protection considerations. • IOM will distribute essential hygiene items with a particular focus on soap, reusable sanitary pads, and water storage containers, to most vulnerable individuals in the target locations. • Promoting women's participation and leadership through skills-building opportunities in WASH. SO 3: • Construction/Rehabilitation of WASH flood-resilient infrastructures while maintaining access to PSNs (water points: elevated platform, protection of the sanitary seal; Latrine: pit lining, increase the lining above ground level to avoid rainwater filtration; PSN: ramps). • Engaging local authorities, local businesses, and local communities to build their capacity for the operation and maintenance of water supply, sanitation, faecal sludge and solid waste management infrastructure and services in the targeted locations.• WASH-related Disaster Risk Reduction activities, including WASH preparedness and response plans for potential shocks (diseases outbreaks, floods, displacement).
