Project Summary

During the last two years, Education in Lebanon continues to be negatively affected by the multiple and complex crises and shocks the country is going through. The country is witnessing its worst economic crisis, which has resulted in increasing poverty and exacerbating vulnerabilities among Lebanese families and communities. Shortage in basic resources, including fuel and electricity, has had a direct impact on the student access to education, on teachers, and on the functioning of schools. Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic has caused major disruption of education, and the implementation of remote and hybrid learning has not gone without observing major challenges on ensuring the continuity and quality of learning. The tragic Beirut blast of August 4th 2020 caused additional strain on the education system, where more than 220 public and private schools have reported damages in physical infrastructure and teaching and learning equipment. This alone has directly affected more than 85,000 school-aged children and youth.
Through the proposed project, and building on its previous work, UNESCO aims to support the public formal secondary education sector, in order to ensure that secondary school students stay in school and continue their education, so that they can later move to higher grades and ultimately complete their education cycle, and be able to continue their higher education. This will be done through supporting families with education-related costs from one side, and supporting secondary schools directly, in order to be able to host additional students coming from the private sector, and to keep their schools running in light of the increasing costs of resources and facilities in the country. With respect to first area of support, UNESCO will provide all vulnerable Lebanese secondary school students in formal education with the basic stationery needed op engage in learning. The most vulnerable students will also be provided with connectivity devices (internet bundles and/ or battery banks for phones, tablets, or laptops) to facilitate their participation in remote learning, should the health situation in the country require the implementation of distance or hybrid learning. Targeting for this item will be done with MEHE, giving priority to the most vulnerable, and those sitting for high stake exams.
The proposed intervention will complement other initiatives that UNESCO is implementing with MEHE in public secondary education, including developing the capacities of public school teachers in delivering quality distance learning, and providing psychosocial support to families and teachers that were affected by the Beirut blasts.
In terms of implementation strategy, UNESCO will build on its previous work with the secondary education directorate at MEHE in providing access to formal secondary education for Syrian refugees and other non-Lebanese in Lebanon, and will use the already established mechanisms with secondary schools, such as transferring money to the school funds, and working with the technical team at the directorate to collect the required data, conduct needs assessments, and coordinate the implementation of the intervention.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under SO1, UNESCO will provide vulnerable Lebanese secondary school students with connectivity devices (internet bundles and/ or battery banks for phones, tablets, or laptops) to facilitate their participation in remote learning. Targeting for this activity will prioritize the most vulnerable (based on a needs assessment) and Grade 12 students who have to sit for high stake. In this activity, UNESCO will apply its financial and procurement rules and regulations to guarantee cost-effective and quality items.Under SO2, UNESCO will support families with education-related costs and schools to help them keep operating. This will be done by providing students with basic stationery in the form of a ‘School in a Bag’ to alleviate financial pressures on families, which would support their retention in education.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The project will be based on an inclusive approach that considers dimensions of gender, disability, and vulnerability. The gendered repercussions of the multiple crises on educational attainment such as household responsibilities, economic participation, and increased domestic violence incidents will be addressed. To ensure a gender-sensitive approach, UNESCO will obtain disaggregated data of beneficiaries by gender, disability, socio-economic status to identify pressing vulnerabilities and ensure adequate analysis, tracking and monitoring. Provision of stationery will also consider the needs of students with disabilities. The project will employ the principles of:-Equity: Pay attention to equity to benefit the most vulnerable-Gender Equality: Assess gender-specific needs and adapt interventions accordingly-Inclusion: Provide children with disabilities with materials tailored to their needsUNESCO will follow protection and referral mechanisms to prevent and respond to PSEA risks

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

[The proposed project will ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP) through the following approaches: - Integrating AAP in needs assessments to be conducted during the planning phase of the project;- Engage AAP in developing selection criteria of beneficiaries;- Form a joint team between MEHE, UNESCO and the beneficiaries to oversee and monitor the provision of learning materials and transfer of funds to the beneficiaries; - Ensure ongoing communication and monitoring with AAP (and its documentation) to confirm the reach and impact of the provided support;- Use feedback mechanisms to gather feedback on the quality and accountability of the intervention, and review intervention according to feedback;- Report back to and share information with AAP and partners
