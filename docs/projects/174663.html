Project Summary

The project aims to support the reinsertion of children who have dropped out of school into formal education and to enable them to complete their formal basic education. Against high drop-out rates in CAR crisis-affected areas of East Cameroon, this intervention will strive to address the root causes through the provision of alternative education options, including accelerated education, catchup classes, and remedial classes. To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The project results will be achieved through activities aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents. To ensure a consistent and comprehensive response, the project will be developed through the lenses of gender and conflict mitigation, which are two concomitant perspectives to the dissemination of the principles of protection in the project.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon hosts the largest refugee population from CAR, of over 290,000 refugees, who are mainly located in the East and Adamaoua regions. Many have been displaced for several years, including 45,000 people who have been in Cameroon for more than 10 years. Over 70% of CAR refugees are staying in host communities, putting significant pressure on already limited resources and services. Tensions between refugees and the host community for access to drinking water, land and social services such as education and health are reported. Refugees from CAR are dependent on humanitarian assistance, with education, health, food, WASH, protection, shelter and livelihoods reported as priority needs. An intention survey carried out amongst refugees from CAR indicated that more than 70% do not intend to return to CAR. According to these households, their safety is not yet guaranteed there. This means that the pressure over access to social and natural resources in eastern Cameroon will continue.As concerns education, although there is no reliable data on the rate of school dropouts in the East, information from field visits suggests a high rate of out of school children. The closer a settlement is to a mining site, higher the rate of non-enrolment in school. While parents point at economic poverty, social ills such as discrimination in support between boys and girls, child exploitation and early marriages are also factors. The average number of out-of-school children in the zone shows high trends: in the host households, 5 girls out of 10 and 4 boys out of 10 school aged children do not attend school. In refugee households, 2 out of 10 girls and 2 out of 10 boys are out of school. Also, schools suffer from lack of qualified teacher, absenteeism of both pupils and teachers, high dropout rates and poor results and capacities of learners. It is likely that the closure of schools due to the risks of COVID-19 will lead to more students dropping out in the 2020-21 school year. In line with NRC and Global Partnership for Education Orientations, the following response is envisaged:Accelerated education: over-aged out-of-school-children are invisible and the number of these forgotten children increases every year as more children drop out of school. NRC, the Ministry of Basic Education and Plan International developed a specific curriculum in 2019, which is being piloted in the Far North of Cameroon. An expansion of this initiative to East region will permit out-of-school children who are willing to continue their schooling to be supported until the end of primary school. Under this initiative, temporary learning spaces will be built, and materials provided for both teachers and pupils recruited. For children from economically vulnerable households, NRC will cover the costs of registration for formal exams.Catch up classes: for out-of-school children who missed less than 2 years of schooling, they will be offered a bridge to formal education reintegration through a six weeks’ education program during school holidays. For this, NRC will work with communities, namely the PTAs, to identify and register out-of-school children focusing on vulnerability and gender inclusion. The Ministry of Basic Education will select and train the teachers who will be mobilized for this initiative. Activities include teacher training, payment of an incentive to teachers and supervisors, provision of materials to pupils and teachers and support to reinsertion to formal education. After the course, NRC, PTAs and Ministry of Education will monitor and support enrollment and retention of beneficiary children in local schools.Remedial classes: targeting boys and girls of class 5 and 6 who are facing difficulties in achieving learning in a specific subject, the project will offer learning opportunities that are complementary to their normal studies. These classes will be offered outside normal school hours by qualified teachers.
