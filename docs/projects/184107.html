Project Summary

The project intends to provide integrated Protection, Child protection, FSL, Education, WASH, Environment & Energy, and NFI services to a total number of 45,000 South Sudanese refugees and host communities in White Nile, and 20,000 refugees and host community members in Gedaref. Plan international seeks to provide timely emergency services to the mentioned refugees in the areas where it is already operational and providing refugee assistance. The project is intended to start on 01/01/2022 and end on 31/12/2022.

1 - Needs

The open-door policy adopted by the Government of Sudan has encouraged significant number of refugees to consider Sudan as a destination and transit point for refugees from several neighboring countries. The recent war in South Sudan compounded by food insecurity has forced at least 800,000 South Sudanese to flee to the neighboring Sudan, among them 260,000 reside in Kostis , Jebalain and ELsalam localities in White Nile state . In addition, following armed confrontations in the Tigray region of Ethiopia between federal and regional forces in November 2020 high numbers of refugees have crossed into Kassala, Gadaref and Blue Nile States in Eastern Sudan leading to an emergency that has overwhelmed existing response capacities. On May 2021 the UNHCR High Commissioner revised the Inter-Agency Refugee Emergency Response Plan – Refugee Influx from Ethiopia which asks for USD 181.6 million to provide lifesaving assistance for 120,000 refugees in Eastern Sudan until December 2021. In June 2021 UNHCR reported that more than 71,000 Ethiopian refugees were accommodated in Um Rakuba and Tunaydba refugee camps. According to the UNHCR Ethiopia Emergency Situation Profile (as of July 1st 2021), 18,522 refugees are registered in Um Rakuba Camp and 19,466 in Tunaydbha Camp. In the Um Rakuba and Tunaydbah camps, the refugees are suffering from dire living conditions and poor access to basic services, as most of them are living in overcrowded temporary shelter (tents) following the destruction of tents and latrines by strong wind and rains in May. Heavy rains and winds destroyed refugee tents and latrines during the week beginning 10 May 2021, which left about 5600 of people without proper shelter. The collapse of toilets are further risking the refuges to water born diseases and other related communicable diseases. Open defecation is observed in both camps. There are 137 separated children, of these 65 live in groups and there are 28 groups. The other 72 separated children live with either relatives or neighbours but not foster families. Hepatitis B out break has also been reported in both camps. Cases of sexual assaults have also been reported in Um Rakuba refugee camp.In Um Rakuba camp there is a static health facility run by MSF while in Tunaydba there is a mobile clinic. There is also a police post constructed in Tunaydba refugee camp. Refugees are not allowed to move outside the refugee camp and aid agencies are providing different assistance. For a complete overview, the UNHCR Plan of Action in Response to the Ethiopian Emergency Situation (dating August 13th) can be consulted. The response includes Reception and Relocation, Flood Preparedness, Shelter, WASH and Health, Protection and Food Security. Plan International Sudan is providing Protection awareness and services to refugees, including CP activities and NFI distributions
