Project Summary

AMAN has developed this project with objectives to ensure safe and equitable availability and access to formal, non-formal and in-formal Education for Rohingya refugees/ FDMN (including the Myanmar Curriculum) and Host community children, adolescent and youth; Enhanced quality of both formal, non-formal and informal Education for Rohingya refugees/FDMN (including Myanmar Curriculum) and host community children, adolescent, and youth; Enhance and support the community engagement and capacity strengthening of Education partners to ensure quality and accountable implementation of Education services for Rohingya refugee/FDMN and host community children, adolescent, and youth through the implementation of following activities.
Construction/ Rehabilitation of Learning Facilities in Camp 8E & 15 along with WASH facilities (toilet) with sex segregated and functional DRR plan, and having health Hygiene kits (MHM) addressing gender demand; Providing education on Myanmar Curriculum among 6-10 Rohingya children; Providing informal education on LCFA (Level 3) among 11 -16 Rohingya children; Providing training among teachers and facilitators on thematic education principles and pedagogy, and Learners Assessment in line with the Teacher Professional Development Framework; Distribution of Educational materials (MHM kit, ECD Kits Box, Teaching learning material for LF, Learning Materials for Learners, COVID-19 protective material for LFs and learners, and Others material for learners); Capacity building for Staffs, and Community stakeholders (SMC and CESG members, Parents /Caregiver, community leaders and community members) with Engagement/Sensitization in monthly basis while increasing awareness raising of beneficiaries across gender and age on COVID and the gender impact with protection concerns (child safeguarding, reporting mechanism, gender responsive behavior etc.); Community Engagement through observing days and planned for learning circles to enhance the gender sensitivity and learning sharing activity within the education sector partners and other stakeholders.
The project proposed location is Camp 8E (Block B) and Camp 15 (Block D). Total 3 Learning Centers will be operated where 367 learners (147 learners in Camp 8E and 220 learners in Camp 15) targeting the most marginalized including 3% learners with disability and LGBTI will have enrolled. Total budget for the project is US$ 61,896 for 2023. The proposed project will be implementing following education sectors policy and guidance where Multi-Year Operational Plan till 31 December 2025 has been designed.
AMAN will ensure safety and security of all concerned following Education sector minimum standards along with Child safeguarding policy and PSEA policy for effective implementation of the project. The project will keep provision of different mechanisms for participation by different specific needs groups including women, men, girls, boys, and other at-risk groups (LGBTQI, elderly, persons with disabilities, orphan, unaccompanied and separated children, etc.). There will be SOPs or guidance on the establishment and operation of feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection, GBV, and PSEA issues. In addition, collaboration with other stakeholders such as peer agencies, sectors, partners, local authorities, and involvement of camps leaders and SMC groups to fulfil this commitment will also strengthen the program. all activities will be implemented under Inclusive Education, and Gender to continue quality education strategy, and that will directly address gender discrimination and promote gender equity in order to ensure that no harm comes to children.
AMAN has skilled manpower to run the assigned project and is a member of ISCG PSEA network and has necessary policy, DRM strategy and guidelines specially DRR plan that will be followed for the implementation of the project efficiently.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Key findings in Education, ref. to Needs and priorities of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar since 2017: what has changed (ACAPS 8/30/2022) - Access to education for Rohingya children in the camps remains challenging. COVID-19 mitigation measures resulted in the closure of LCs in camps, for more than 18 months, halving the number of children able to access learning in 2021. Access to education has improved in 2022 but remains difficult for some Rohingya children, particularly older children. Barriers for older Rohingya students persisted, as they faced a less structured curriculum and significantly fewer opportunities to have an education facility in their camp. Only 3% of the LCs offered services for children above 14 years of age. The main barriers to education reported by boys included irrelevant or age inappropriate learning (reported by 52% of boys). The barriers reported by girls were those related to cultural norms and families’ tendency to withdraw girls from public life after puberty. Factors affecting the latter involved concerns around girls bringing shame to the family or ruining their chances of marriage, perceptions that there is limited utility to educating girls in terms of the low likelihood of them getting jobs, and concerns about being harassed by men and boys.Involvement in livelihoods and domestic work are additional impediments to educational quality for older children (REVA 2017; ISCG 12/11/2020 b; REACH et al. 29/03/2021). Remote learning has been challenging for children in Rohingya camps given limited resources. Impediments include limited internet and mobile network connection, a lack of learning materials, the unavailability of teachers, a lack of guidance from teachers, and the unavailability of educated household members to support children with their studies (REACH et al. 29/03/2021; J-MSNA Rohingya 2020). In 2020, 62% of households reported facing challenges in supporting their children’s remote study (J-MSNA Rohingya 2020). Households also reported older children getting involved in child labour and child marriage, putting many (both boys and girls) at risk of not returning to school at all (REVA 2020; J-MSNA Rohingya 2021). In 2021, Rohingya refugees expressed discontent with the LCs, specifically about the poor quality of education, disinterested Bangladeshi teachers, and a lack of progressively increasing difficulty levels upon finishing each year of study (ACAPS/IOM 28/04/2021). It is difficult to accurately understand the proportion of eligible children covered because of differences in the age groups used in the demographic breakdown of refugee population figures. To give an approximate idea, UNHCR figures indicate that there were 64,415 girls and 69,504 boys in the 12–17 age group in the camps as at 31 July 2022 (UNHCR 13/08/2022). While the introduction of the Learning Competencies Framework and Approach and the Myanmar curriculum are likely to improve education quality for all children regardless of age and prepare them for future employment, opportunities for them to work and improve their quality of life remain severely restricted (J-MSNA Rohingya 2020). LGBTI group are common in the Rohingya community; at the same time, they frequently experience harassment and discrimination. They have few, if any, spaces to gather and be themselves and meet others like them. They involved in the pilot reported feeling unsafe in their communities and were subjected to harassment (The Women’s Refugee Commission/Addressing Sexual Violence against Men, Boys, and LGBTQ+ Refugees: Learnings from Pilot Projects in Bangladesh.../July 2021).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

AMAN has designed this project based on the Principles of Rationalization for the Rohingya Refugee response in Bangladesh, prepared by the ISCG in Cox’s Bazar. Necessary activities and strategies have been adapted ensuring that targeted Rohingya Refugees in Cox’s Bazar (367 learners including learners with disabilities & LGBTI aged 6-16 under 3 LFs at Camp 8E & 15) have equitable access to basic education services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, through a standardized and cost-effective approach to providing equitable education services to refugees where 3% learners will be CWD, while "Program Staff and Teachers' Guidance on Learner's Transition: LCFA to MC" prepared by education sector will be followed. AMAN will take necessary steps on inclusive education which will be appropriate for the CWD learners and LGBTI. In addition, AMAN has been operating 3 LFs (one LF at camp 15 and two LFs at Camp 8E) since 2019 and also under the approved project for JRP 2021-22, and these LFs have been planned to run in 2023 following the multiyear planning till 2025. While AMAN is delivering the quality education (MC & LCFA) along with other need based supports to the targeted Refugee children in light of Child safeguarding policy and PSEA maintaining necessary coordination with peer agencies, sectors, partners, SMC and CESG members, Parents /Caregiver, community leaders and community members, CiCs and above all, following the education sector prescribed guideline and principles. AMAN is also providing monthly 5w reports and other necessary reports as required by the sector and other stakeholders regularly. AMAN has experienced manpower who have received different training by Education sector and will be involved in capacity building initiatives by the sector to ensure a minimum standard of service delivery.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

AMAN will ensure that the planned activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation in light of Safeguarding and PSEA policy for effective implementation of the project. There will be SOPs or guidance on the establishment and operation of feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection, GBV, and PSEA issues. In addition, collaboration with other stakeholders, such as peer agencies, sectors, partners, local authorities, and involvement of camps leaders’ and SMC groups to fulfil this commitment will also strengthen the program.

3.2 - Gender

The project will keep provision of different mechanisms for participation by different specific needs groups including men, women, children, older persons, minority cultural groups, LGBTI, and people living with disabilities. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection, GBV, and PSEA issues. To fulfil gender equality commitments and mainstream gender in the Rohingya humanitarian response, AMAN must bring attention and resources to critical issues of gender equality, women’s rights, and empowerment. Accordingly, in all stages of emergency preparedness and response, gender issues will be systematically and explicitly identified and addressed.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

AMAN will ensure safety and security of all concerned in light of Safeguarding and PSEA policy for effective implementation of the project as well as to ensure that every child, adolescent and youth regardless of their age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex, or sexual orientation, has a right to equal protection from harm. The project will keep provision of different mechanisms for participation by different specific needs groups including men, women, children, older persons, minority cultural groups, LGBTI, and people living with disabilities through smooth cooperation with site management, RRRC/CiC, sector focal and other stake holder for children are safe from adults and other children who might pose a risk. This includes learning facility, voluntary and community organizations, faith groups as well as private sector providers. AMAN Safeguarding and PSEA policy is in action that is taken to promote the welfare of children and protect them from harm.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity program will have followed by AMAN during project implementation period which involves three major steps: (1) Building a corporate culture that values diversity. (2) Changing structures, policies, and systems to support diversity. (3) Provide diversity awareness training to learners and facilitators to enhance and understanding of and sensitivity to cultural and other difference by building awareness and understanding. AMAN will follow transparent, fair and equitable hiring practices, establish pay equity, provide support that helps each team member succeed and give all team members a voice. AMAN will focus on different types of diversity such as cultural, racial, gender, physical Disabilities and diversity in interest during implementation of project. The activities will be performed through involvement of education sector, site management team, proper communication with CiC, and other stakeholders who are related to implement the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by AMAN to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people they seek to assist. AMAN will consider following suggestions while designing their humanitarian projects for JRP 2023 and beyond: Information, Communication, and Transparency; Participation, Engagement, and Inclusion; Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism; Learning and Adaptation. Information will be provided routinely to the community by both the organization and its stakeholders (Parents, care givers, facilitator) and on the organization’s vision, mission, mandate, accountability commitments, code of conduct, feedback, complaints procedure, and relevant contact details. The Do No Harm framework or equivalent approach will be employed to AMAN staff as well as the participation of beneficiaries.
