Project Summary

The proposed one year’s project will be implemented in two States namely, Northern Bahr el Ghazal which is categorized under priority 1 State and Upper Nile State which is categorized under priority 2 State (FSL Cluster Geographical Prioritization-2025 HNRP). The proposed project targeted areas include Aweil East County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Nasir, Ulang and Longechuk Counties in Upper Nile. Four Payams will be targeted in each of the selected Counties. Aweil East County (Baach, Madhol, Mangar-tong I and Mangok). Longochuk County (Dajo, Malual, Pamach and Wudier). Luakpiny/Nasir County (Kiech Kuon, Kuerenge-Ke, Mading and Maker). Ulang County (Doma, Kurmuot, Ulang and Yomding). A total of 30,000 people (12,000 male and 18,000 female) will be targeted to benefit from the proposed project. This target will be drawn from 5,000 most vulnerable households comprised of returnees, IDPs and host communities with specific focus on single-headed Households, child headed households, PwDs, GBV survivors and persons with special needs who will be prioritized in the selection processes. The selection tools will be jointly developed and applied by UNKEA’s project management teams and relevant stakeholders within the project locations. UNKEA will work closely with South Sudan Clusters; FSL, Health, Nutrition, Protection and WASH to which UNKEA subscribes as a member to offer specialize expertise, skills, knowledge, and information lacking within the organization for the project beneficiaries. Standard operating procedures developed by the clusters technical working group will be followed to guide the implementation process. The proposed interventions will be coordinated closely with State Ministry of Agriculture, County production department, community leaders, youth and women groups including CSOs present in the project locations to help support selection of project beneficiaries including resource sharing, joint planning, monitoring and evaluation of the proposed project interventions. The proposed project will be implemented through FSL Cluster prioritized activities that contributes to the set objectives as below: Objective1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, shing and livestock. Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. At the end of this project, the households are expected to be economically empowered (with improved incomes) and food secure in terms of accessibility, sufficiency (quantities and quality), balanced and nutritious enough that will guarantee a healthy living. Fewer cases of malnutrition and diseases associated with lack of dietary diversity is expected to be reported.

1. Needs

In the twelfth year of independence, the people of South Sudan are confronted by formidable challenges marked by conflict in 2013 and 2016, intensifying humanitarian, economic, social and political crises. The 2018 R-ARCSS continues to provide hope for peace. However, sub-national violence persists in some areas, leading to the displacement of people and loss of lives and livelihoods (HNRP 2024). Upper Nile State. December 2024 – March 2025 (Projection 1): During the harvest/post-harvest period, the food security situation will slightly improve in Upper Nile State with an estimated 795,000 people (50.6 percent of the state’s population) likely to be in Phase 3 or above, of which 206,000 people will likely be in Phase 4, and 589,000 people will likely be in Phase 3. During this period, Nasir will be in Phase 4, and the remaining 10 counties will be in Phase 3. April – July 2025 (Projection 2): In the peak of the lean season, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate in Upper Nile State with an estimated 1.02 million people (65.1 percent of the state’s population) likely to be in Phase 3 or above, of which 10,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), 330,000 people will likely be in Phase 4, and 683,000 people will likely be in Phase 3. During this period, Longochuk, Luakpiny/ Nasir and Ulang) will likely be in Phase 4. The state is considered the main entry for majority of returnees from Sudan, and this is likely to put more burden on the local communities’ resources. Northern Bahr el Ghazal. December 2024 – March 2025 (Projection 1): During the harvest/post-harvest projection period. the food security situation will improve, with an estimated 393,000 people (40.8 percent of the state’s population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 126,000 people (13.1 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 267,000 people (27.7 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. April – July 2025 (Projection 2): During the lean-season projection period, the food security will deteriorate, with an estimated 600,000 people (62.1 percent of the state’s population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 232,000 people (24.0 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 368,000 people (38.1 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this lean season period, Aweil East counties is classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The deterioration of the food security situation is seasonal and is largely attributed to the persistence of high food prices because of low own stocks, the economic crisis and associated currency depreciation and low availability of hard currency, disrupted supply chains occasioned by the crisis in Sudan, displacements caused by floods, and disruption of trade routes because of the deteriorating road conditions during the rainy season. The likelihood of cattle-related conflicts and intercommunal violence is also a factor that would contribute to the deterioration of the food security situation. Other factors aggravating food security include crop and livestock pests and diseases, and the influx of returnees and refugees into the State that would increase competition for resources and livelihood options. Therefore, there is urgent need to increase access to food and livelihood assets for the most-in need population in Nasir, Ulang and Longechuk Counties in Upper Nile and Aweil East County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal through cash transfers, provision of agricultural crop seeds and vegetable seed kits , tools, market access, fishing kits and community engagement in viable IGAs such as VSLAs and vocational businesses for the short and medium term ((IPC Nov 2024-July 2025)

2. Project activities

UNKEA will support the provision of FSL interventions that saves lives, alleviates suffering of the communities and improves their coping mechanisms to all forms of shocks that they experience. These shall be done through the implementation of the Cluster prioritized activities that contributes to the set objectives as below: Objective1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). This will be done through provision of unconditional food assistance to the most vulnerable population such as cash transfers and food assistance. Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, shing and livestock. This will be done through provision of emergency livelihood support to the most vulnerable population, they will include training of farmers on improved farming practices, livestock management practices and fisheries. Other support will include establishments of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites/schools/households, provision of dry and main season agricultural crop kits, vegetable kits, fishing kits, agricultural tools, animal restocking and vaccinations.Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. This will be done through improving livelihood productivity, market access and value chains, non-farm livelihoods and anticipatory actions (DRR, Climate change adaptation). They will include provision of post-harvest handling equipment, pre-position grain and seed protection kits, including hermetic bags, tilted buckets, and grain bags, vocational skills building, promoting IGAs (VSLAs etc), provision of cash for work (for youth). Others include training farmers on post-harvest handling and agricultural value chains, training local population to make tools by blacksmithing, promoting establishments of seed banks and storage facilities, promoting vegetable and seed production & multiplication for markets, establishing or strengthening community-based early warning systems (EWS) by providing necessary equipment such as rain gauges and disaster forecasting tools, facilitate the dissemination of downscaled climate and flood early warning information through radio programs, including radio shows and solar radios, training farmers on climate-smart agricultural practices and promoting acquisition of household assets among others.
