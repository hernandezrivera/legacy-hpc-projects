Project Summary

This ARC food security and livelihoods (FS&L) project aims to provide emergency FS&L services for IDPs, Returnees and host communities in East and South Darfur so as to improve their well-being and promote self-reliance. 15,000 people will be targeted by the project in East and South Darfur states.
The project will be a 12 months’ intervention that will run from 1st April 2020 to 30th March 2021. ARC envisions the establishment of a long-term comprehensive livelihoods and economic recovery program to support Sudan’s transition to longer-term development through: a) reducing dependency on humanitarian relief support; b) increasing self-reliance and resilient economies; and c) promoting peace and cohesion between Refugees and Sudanese populations. This intervention will target total populations 558,137 in the locaties of El Daein, Adilla , Alferdous , Assaliya , Yassin , Shariya,Abukarinka and Abu Jabra in East Darfur and Bielel , Gereida ,Elsalam , Kalma camp , and Dimsu localities in South Darfur base on FSL ranking by Humanitarian need overview for 2019 .
In East Darfur ARC continuing providing food security and livelihoods services for refugees out of camp , returnees and host communities in Adilla , Elferdous , Assaliya and Abujabra localities for over 5000 people .
The project interventions aim and focus on :
(i) reduce acute food and livelihoods insecurity of newly displaced and affected by crises and natural disasters
(ii) restore and sustain the food security and livelihoods of vulnerable people in protracted displacement
(iii) build the resilience of the food and livelihood insecure by reducing vulnerabilities to conflict and disaster risks; and
(iv) strengthen the capacity of national partners to address food and livelihoods insecurity and improve coordination in FSL core functions at national and state levels.
The intervention will focus on mobilize communities (IDPs, returnees, host communities) to participate in programming interventions that address their needs and mainstreaming gender diversity of women (widows, orphans, divorce, female headed house hold and Survivor of SGBV), men, people with special needs and disables through setting appropriate activities that meet their needs and choices while the proposed activities will be market aware to promote longer-term self-sufficiency. The proposed intervention will contribute to key area of collective outcome for Sudan (Economic development and Sustainable Livelihoods) indicated that by 2022 communities and households benefit from increased production and productivity that lead to sustainable livelihoods, sustainable access to food systems and improved nutrition status with emphasis on the agriculture sector as a driver. ARC committee to coordinate with the humanitarian actors working at the field to avoid duplication of resources and efforts and maintain the core principles

1 - Needs

According to fews net east Africa report for November 2019 Food security outcomes among IDPs and poor households in conflict and non-conflict affected areas continued to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes due to high staple of food prices. although high transportation costs have prevented prices from falling as much as in a typical year during the harvest. For example, sorghum food prices usually fall by 10-15 percent between October and November in most markets, but this year they have only fallen by 5-10 percent in some markets and or remained unchanged. The Sudanese Pound has continued to depreciate on the parallel market, reaching 82 SDG/USD in November, compared to 69 SDG/USD in September and 76.5 SDG/USG in October This continues to drive increases in prices for essential, imported commodities, such as wheat flour, medicine, fuel, and agricultural inputs. In November 2019, the price of wheat flour has reached 48 SDG/kg, compared to 45 SDG/kg in October. Overall, wheat flour prices are 68 percent higher than last year. ARC aims to improve the wellbeing of IDPs, returnees and host communities in South and East Darfur states through improving food security and livelihoods among war affected households, particularly female-headed households, Widows, Orphans, divorcee, disables, people with special needs and men. The project will focus on activities that improve food security and livelihoods through the provision of agricultural tools and inputs, capacity building for farmers through trainings on extension services and vegetable gardens, provision of income generating activities include trainings, provision of cash base assistance to IDPs groups etc. ARC will also continue to maintain a smaller scale seed vouchers and tools assistance to individuals that are identified as in extremely precarious food situations. This program will be integrated with the nutrition intervention whereby families with malnourished children will form part of the beneficiary selection criteria. The program will assure that a proper balance of support is provided to vulnerable individuals, small businesses, farmers, and other key members of the local economy so that assistance is complimentary and supports full-scale economic resilience.

2 - Activities / Outputs

• Support house hold farmers with agricultural tools and seeds to improve food production among IDPS, Returnees and host communities. many farmers are struggling to provide cropping inputs and to prepare for the next planting season which will create a risk of food security with support and provision of tools and improve seeds from FAO ARC will distribute total 678 metric tons of various seeds to total target of 1000 house hold, peanut seed 650 MT, Sorghum 15 MT, millet 5 MT and Seas am 8 MT. in addition ARC will provide 1000 ploughs (one per household) and basic farm tools such as hoes (2000) and weeder as well . • Improved farming skills through Pre & post-harvest training for targets farmer’s. This will be through extension workers • Support mothers income generating activities for 200 women's whom their children are part of nutrition program SAM /MAM children this will be through integration services , nutrition program database for 2019 /2020 in ARC nutrition centers as guidance to reach the most vulnerable families and assess their needs in order to provide assistance .• Support small scale farmers with agricultural tools: Most IDPs and host communities have a tradition of producing their own food, but lost their tools during displacement. Those with no access to land in the camps, move long distances outside the camp to land that they are renting under formal agreements with the land owner (which have been in existence for several years) during the rainy season. They lack means of transport to ferry their tools and farming materials during pre and post-harvest periods. ARC proposes to provide 500 donkey or ox-drawn carts to 500 households to be used for transportation to access the farmlands- Support women with home gardens and vegetable growth: ARC will integrate this approach with their nutrition data, and target mothers or caregivers with children with SAM and MAM for home vegetable garden activities in order to improve their nutritious food at the household level.Vegetables promote diet diversity and strengthen households’ food security. ARC will purchase and distribute seeds of green leafy vegetables to caregivers of severely malnourished children without medical complications. Caregivers will also be trained on kitchen garden establishment and irrigation and encouraged to consume the produce at their households. As a result, more micro-nutrients will be available, resulting in a more sustainable prevention of micro nutrient deficiencies and stunting and improve production .-Vocational training and start-up kits for 200 vulnerable youth. Youth who do not have any opportunities for work or income generation are particularly vulnerable to crime and violence. However, youth also present an opportunity for economic growth in these communities if they have the support to learn new skills that are in market demand and provided with materials to start their own business. ARC will work with their local partner Alsawid Al khrada to identify 200 youth to enroll in vocational skills training from 3-6 months depending on the skills training. Upon completion of their training, youth will be provided with start-up kits containing whatever tools or equipment is required to begin working. The trainings to be offered will be decided following analysis of the labor market assessment which will be conducted prior to implementation. Trainings currently offered through VTC include food processing, mechanics, welding, mobile phone repair and computer skills.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ARC will mainstream protection in all proposed activities under food security and livelihoods to ensure Women, girls, boys, men and persons with specific needs will be consulted in all assessments and situation analyses to address their FSL specific highlighted needs would be included in project cycle. Also engaging community leaders, local authorities during selection of beneficiaries for livelihood projects to ensure right beneficiaries were selected. ARC protection team will sensitize sector staff to respond to the needs of all beneficiaries including the elderly, women, children and persons with specific needs, and will make staff aware on key humanitarian principles (Do No harm, providing services without discrimination and respect human rights and dignity ).ARC protection team will work with health and Nutrition and livelihood team to ensure all services are accessible to the all beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, and ensure our services are tailored to the needs of the people we served.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ARC will work with the camp governance structures and representatives of the affected and at risk families on a participatory hazard assessment to position shelters and select beneficiaries per IASC principles of Accountability to Affected Populations. ARC will capture the requests from those most affected and living in unsafe and undignified conditions, including PSNs, the elderly, female-headed households with several children, and unaccompanied minors. This will be done in conjunction with the FSL needs findings done by ARC FSL team in accordance with the SPHERE Handbook and the FSL Sector guidelines . While observing these guidelines, ARC will encourage user-driven approaches through co-creation with the ARC team, and desirability of intervention from the PSN perspective.
