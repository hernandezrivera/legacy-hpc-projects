Project Summary

The Project aims to continue to provide protection to vulnerable girls and boys in camps and in the host community through specialized child protection services, case management, strengthening Community-based Child Protection Mechanisms and capacity building of protection actors. Focus remains on the vulnerable children at risk or survivor of child abuse, child labour, child marriage and trafficking and on particular groups (e.g. unaccompanied and separated boys and girls, children with disabilities and/or child survivors of sexual abuse).
Services will be provided through implementing partner Terre des hommes (Tdh) in camp 13, 20, 20 extension and in the host community in Ukhia/Palong Khali, and by direct implementation of IOM in camp 9 and 23. In camp 7,9,17, 18, 20 extension and camp 23 IOM Child Protection runs Child Friendly Corners in Women and Girls Safe Spaces. The CP team also engages with IOM GBV, Counter-Trafficking and General Protection Sub-units as part of integrated protection approaches. With a cross-cutting approach IOM CP cooperates with other sectors (e.g. MHPSS/ Health, Shelter, WASH, Site Management, Transition and Recovery Division, ) to mainstream child protection, disability and to promote peaceful co-existence and to strengthen the PSEA and Feedback mechanisms.
The prevention and response closely interlinked with Community-based protection to enhance sustainability, accountability and community participation and especially to enable children to be instrumental in improving the quality of their life and mitigating risks and dangers in their community and living environment. The Project also has a strong emphasis on supporting children and their caregivers/families with Mental Health and structured Psychosocial Support services and with comprehensive Case Management services, utilizing the safe spaces run by IOM or its partners as access points to the beneficiaries
IOM is committed to monitor the situation and activities in the camps to ensure safe multi-sectoral programming, including monitoring to mitigate the risks of exploitation and child labor and to protect beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). IOM provided training on PSEA to staff and appointed PSEA Focal Points within each camp where complaint and feedback mechanism, as well as referral mechanism are in place for reporting.
IOM ensure accountability towards the Rohingya community and continuously investing and developing accessible, reliable and data-protected electronic Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM). IOM Site Management currently is piloting a new inter-agency CFM system for collecting, referring, analyzing, and replying to community feedback. Child Protection is working closely with IOM Site Management to reach out to generally underrepresented groups like boys and girls in different age groups (with special focus to adolescents) in a child-friendly way through organizing FGDs periodically to give a voice for the younger population about their concerns and feedback.
Coordination: IOM has been strongly committed to child protection inter-agency efforts and has contributed to the development of several CP technical documents, key message campaigns and adaptation of materials for inter-agency child protection trainings. IOM CP contributes to the CP Sub-Sector, CP Case Management Technical Working Group, GBV Sub-Sector and co-chairs the Counter Trafficking Technical Working Group and PSEA Network.
IOM Child Protection programme aims to establish durable and sustainable solution through strengthening community child protection mechanism through inclusive and diverse community participation (including boys, girls, and children with disabilities). IOM also contribute to institutional capacity strengthening building of NGOs, local authorities and law enforcement agencies and to the advocacy to promote children’s rights to have access for informal and formal structures and services of Government of Bangladesh.

Needs

The humanitarian situation for Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh remains dire, with a total of 914.000 Rohingya refugees who have fled systematic violence in Myanmar settled in the camp and around host communities in Cox’s Bazar. Children represent 55 per cent of the refugee population. Congested and difficult terrain conditions coupled with restriction of movement, lack of livelihood and educational opportunities has contributed to increase the vulnerability of children to violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse. Increased number of incidents reported on security and safety concerns in the camp in 2019 had further negative impact on social cohesion as well as on the well-being of both refugee and host communities, especially children and women. Furthermore, environmental hazards including cyclone and monsoon conditions put children at further risk of harm. Due to the prolonged period of uncertainty about the situation of Rohingya community, the physically and mentally challenging living environment, new protection risks emerged and further needs has been identified. Child marriage, child labour, human trafficking and violence against children are key child protection issues prevalent in both refugee settlements and host communities for vulnerable boys and girls, including for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). Marriage continues to be the primary means of attaining social and economic security for Rohingya women and girls, who are traditionally discouraged from working and focusing on caring about the family and house chores . Married girls are at increased risk of a range of physical and psychological consequences including child labour, early pregnancy intimate partner violence. According to IOM Case Data Analyses on Trafficking cases (2019) children are particularly vulnerable to trafficking due to socio-economic reasons. There were a high number of girl victims identified as demand for girls as domestic workers are generally high. Concrete interventions targeting adolescent boys are few in number compared to the scale; consequently this may contribute to their engagement in hazardous work and forced labour. Due to the increasing protection needs, security fears of community and unresolved traumatic issues there is an increasing need for more specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services. The majority of the MHPSS services available are community level and non-specialized interventions and there is a significant gap in the provision targeting high risk groups as adolescents and youth. In addition, services to “invisible” children, including children with disabilities, Hijra children (with third gender identity) , adolescent girls restricted to stay home and male survivor of sexual abuse needs to be better targeted.Unaccompanied and separated boys and girls remains in focus due to the prolonged stay of 2 years in Bangladesh and their changing needs. 8,596 UASC has been identified until September 2019 by CP actors based on the 5W. More durable solution needs to be identified (e.g. family placements with long term capacity and commitments) or continued to be monitored existing family care arrangements. In many cases elderly relatives or unrelated adults provide care for UASC, and carers who are victims or witness of violence in Myanmar, therefore specialized support is needed for caregivers (MHPSS, positive parenting etc.). Activities or outputs

2.1: Strengthening Community-based Child Protection Mechanism (CBCPM) to enhance the capacity of families and communities to care for and protect children through child participation IOM will provide support and capacity building through regular meetings to Community-based Child Protection Committees (CBCPCs), Adolescents’ Committees (AC) and Peer-to-Peer Network, Parent Groups, Religious leaders’ and Teachers’ networks empowering them to raise awareness, identify risks, gaps, barriers to access to services, promote positive social and behavior change to address child protection issues. Adolescent committees (age 14-17) Children committees (age 10-13) and Adolescent Peer-to-Peer Network, (age 10-17) will be formed separately for boys and girls and ensure participation of children with disabilities. In the host community, IOM will support CBCPCs, ACs and Teachers’ Network to improve the learning environment in schools.2.2: Community-based cross-sectoral Child Protection (CP) mainstreamingIOM continue CP mainstreaming and child safeguarding to strengthen the protective environment through multi-sectoral and participatory approach, involving the community, children and caregivers. Regular monitoring and safety walks are carried out with boys and girls in different groups (incl. persons with disabilities). Risk mitigation plans are developed, inclusive to disaster risk reduction in monsoon and cyclone season. 3.1: System strengthening to promote specialized child-friendly, survivor-focused services to child victims of trafficking and other forms of exploitationIOM will provide technical support and capacity building to Camp-in-Charges, local authorities, law enforcement agencies, military and NGOs, promoting specialized child-friendly prevention and response in camp and in host communities. Trainings and sensitization sessions will be provided on CP principles and practices to enhance victims’ safe access to protection, (e.g. of Do No Harm principle to avoid further traumatization in the process). IOM launched a joint Community Security pilot project to improve the dialogue between the Police and Rohingya communities, and to strengthen social cohesion and security through Community Policing Forums. CBCPM will be tapped in to mitigate and identify CP risks and promote community crime watch. 4.1: Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services with an integrated approach to support vulnerable children and their familiesMHPSS will be provided for at-risk children and their families through integrated approach in existing structures. PSS support is provided in Child-friendly Corners in the Women and Girls Safe Spaces to children (age 2-7) while the mother/carer participates in activities. Existing static and mobile Child-friendly Spaces (CFS) continue to provide safe access to age-appropriate, disability and gender-sensitive structured psychosocial, recreational and cultural activities (e.g. Mobile activities for adolescent girls with restricted movement). Counselling services will be provided for distressed individuals, with a focus on caregivers to promote positive parenting and reducing harmful practices. Boys and girls with disabilities, their carers and families will receive comprehensive support through case management and MHPSS services. 4.2: Quality Case Management (CM) services are provided to children at risk and survivors of child abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation.IOM provides integrated CM services to vulnerable girls and boys at-risk of harm and to child survivors of abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation with specialized services in Caring for Child Survivors (GBV) and Counter Trafficking, promoting multi-sectoral approach. The service include alternative care considering age and gender appropriate solutions, services for female and male child survivor of sexual abuse, safe shelter for emergency and rehabilitation purposes for girl survivors and family tracing and reunificatio
