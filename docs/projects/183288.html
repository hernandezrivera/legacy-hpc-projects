Project Summary

This project is part of the LHF-funded food security and nutrition project entitled “Food security for 665 migrant households and nutrition prevention for 1000 migrant children in Beirut and Mount Lebanon”, under the 2021 first standard allocation. This is a summary of the nutrition component only.
The food security component targets 665 migrant households in Beirut and Mount Lebanon (collectively considered to be ‘Greater Beirut’) with monthly dry food rations as well as local produced, seasonal and long shelf-life fresh vegetables in order to ensure the best nutrition outcomes.
All migrant households with children who are in the 665 households targeted by the food security component will automatically be eligible for nutrition education (roughly 366 children below the age of 5); an additional set of households with 634 children will be identified through migrant-led initiatives to reach the target of 1000 migrant children for nutrition education.
Malnutrition of 1000 migrant children aged 6-59 months will be prevented through the provision of micro-nutrient supplements to ensure adequate nutrition, whether or not they are also receiving fresh and dry food through food distributions.
Early detection and referral for treatment of moderate or severe malnutrition, with strong referral pathways to referent malnutrition treatment centres in nearby PHCs (Bourj Hammoud and Mazraa) will be done through the family MUAC approach; targeting the mothers and caregivers of 1000 migrant children aged 6-59 months.
IYCF awareness raising will be provided to pregnant and lactating women and the mothers and caregivers of children under 23 months, in close coordination with IOCC for both training and accessing lactation specialists.
All households with children aged 6-59 months that receive food through the food security component will also receive cooking demonstrations tailored to cultural food and cuisine preferences, bearing in mind that the targeted households come from 22 different nationalities. Cooking demonstrations will be delivered by migrants from these diverse nationalities.
Information, Education and Communications materials that underpin nutrition education, such as IYCF counselling cards, flyers, recipe booklets and general information on malnutrition and how to screen for it will be translated into selected migrant languages. All are already available in Arabic, most in English, and some in other languages; Concern will ensure they are all translated into Bengali, Singhalese, Amharic, English and French. Additional IEC materials on vaccinations, COVID-19, Sexual and Reproductive Health and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse will also be translated into these languages. All translated materials will be made available to any organisation who need them, trough the relevant working group.
UNICEF will provide the micro-nutrient supplements, MUAC tapes and IEC materials. The project is closely coordinated with the nutrition working group, IOCC and the Ministry of Public Health
This project is embedded within Concern’s strategic approach to working with migrant workers in Greater Beirut, which combines addressing immediate and basic needs with long term efforts to address the underlying and structural causes of migrant vulnerability, and works with and through migrant-led initiatives in a migrant-community based, bottom-up approach.
The project will work with 9 members of three Migrant-Led Initiatives (Mesewet, Together Forever and African United Communities in Lebanon), and focus on building their capacity in nutrition education, accountability to affected populations and referrals; ensuring benefits that last beyond the duration of the project.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Adequate and nutritionally balanced diets: fresh vegetables distributed alongside dry food rations for 665 migrant households; cooking demonstrations following cultural food preferences to food recipients to enhance nutritional impact; distribution of micro-nutrient supplements to 1000 migrant children aged 6-59 months. IYCF support: translate IEC materials for IYCF, family MUAC and nutrition; receive training in IYCF from IOCC; raise awareness of IYCF for mothers/caregivers of 596 infants aged 0-23 months; referral of PLW to IOCC/MoPH lactation specialists; baseline-endline surveys. Identification and referrals of malnutrition: receive training in family MUAC from IOCC; train mothers/caregivers of 1000 migrant children aged 6-59 months in how to use MUAC tapes, including for PLW; assist MoPH in outreach and communications; referrals of any malnourished PLW or children to nutrition treatment centres in Bourj Hammoud or Mazraa with appropriate follow-up; referrals of other identified needs to relevant service provider.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Concern globally adheres to its internal standards for protection, with policies in place. The majority of interaction between project staff and beneficiaries will be through majority female members of 3 migrant-led initiatives. This ensures that barriers related to trust, language and culture are minimised, and close working relationships forged. Migrant-led initiative members will receive safe identification and referral protection training from Concern's Senior Protection Officer, which includes PSEA training to all personnel, including drivers (contracted).Concern's Migrant Protection Team maintains a service map and close relationships with service providers. Concern's referrals procedures follow all inter-agency guidelines. Concern's Outreach Officers will assist the migrant-led initiative members in making referrals, receiving feedback and addressing complaints.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Concern globally adheres to internal policies on safeguarding. Concern Lebanon has an established Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) to ensure accountability towards all beneficiaries. It collects any complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and ensure genuine beneficiaries’ complaints are addressed.Dedicated communication lines are managed by Concern staff to receive concerns and provide responses on timely basis. Projectparticipants are also encouraged to give verbal feedback, especially through Concern’s outreach officers who, as members of migrant communities themselves, are an additional mechanism of cross-community communication.
