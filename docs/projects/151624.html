Project Summary

This project will contribute to the improved living conditions of newly and protracted displaced IDPs through provision of emergency shelter and NFI kits. The project also seeks to contribute to the protection and improving the living conditions of newly displaced people and of the protracted internally displaced persons through transitional shelters and NFIs. NoFYL intends to implement the shelter and NFI project through a community driven approaches that maximise on beneficiary participation and enhancing local Opportunities. Selection of beneficiaries will involve IDPs settlement and community leaders and focal persons with close collaboration with local authorities.
In addition, community workers teams will continue to assess, verify, and deliver assistance according to identified needs to affected populations wherever they are, and most likely in the most affected regions (Banadir, Lower Shebelle and Gedo regions). The community teams will carefully consider and tailor each response to the verified needs on the ground, paying particular attention to the most vulnerable and women, and with an overarching focus on protecting the safety, security and dignity of populations targeted with assistance.
In all stages of the NFI and emergency shelter response, NoFYL will ensure gender and age awareness including in the disaggregation of data and the targeting of female headed households, incorporating gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs assessments and response recommendations.
To maximise efficiency, this project will be carried out in consultation with the Shelter Cluster lead. This will ensure solid impact, avoid duplication, and promote sustainability where possible.

Needs

According to the 2018 Somalia humanitarian need overview, shelter is ranked among the top three priority needs of the newly displaced IDPs; the other priority needs being food and water. A considerable number of households is reported to live in emergency shelters that will soon be in need of replacement, or upgrade to more durable shelters due to their limited lifespans. A total of 87 per cent of the households indicated having no floor covering material which tends to worsen situations in shelters during the rain seasons. In addition, only one per cent of the households had an acceptable NFI score, as a result of lacking basic non-food items such as sleeping mats, jerry cans and plastic sheeting. Most shelters found in the settlements do not to meet the minimum standards of space size, cover and privacy. They thus do not provide sufficient protection from harmful elements and physical safety and security; nor do they ensure sufficient privacy and dignity for the groups, families, and individuals living in them, increasing the risks of SGBV especially for girls and women. The newly displaced are in immediate need of life-saving emergency/temporary shelters and NFIs, whereas protracted IDPs are in need of more durable assistance which involves local reintegration or return, sustainable transitional shelter or permanent shelter solutions. Overcrowded settlements are in need of support to decongest and reduce the risks of fire and spread of easily communicable diseases.

Activities

Activity 1.1: Timely provision of Emergency Assistance Packages including Non food Item and Emergency shelter kits as per Shelter Cluster guidelines to the newly displaced populations, and Returnees in Banadir, Lower Shebelle and Gedo Regions. (3000 NFIs for 3000 for new displaced families (18000 individuals), 2000 NFIs for protracted IDPs, comprising of women, girls, boys and men and people with special needs) Activity 1.2: Provide provide newly displaced IDPs and returnees with 2000 ESKs for 2000 families (12,000 individuals) among men, women, boys and girls through continuous situation analysis under the cluster’s guideline. Each family will have one package of ESKs as per the shelter cluster guidelines. Activity 1.3: Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) using a standardised methodology to those that have Emergency Assistance Packages to ensure accountability and to improve programming and receive feedback from beneficiaries on quality and relevance of the kits, this will also provide provide a complaint and feedback mechanisms. The PDM will target target around 80% of the total beneficiaries. Activity 1.4: Training of protracted IDPs and returnee beneficiaries on shelter construction, repair and maintenance using cultural and locally available materials following a standard design and the spatial plan developed for this purpose, with interventions done in accordance with the sphere standards. This in turn will promote employability skills of trained beneficiaries thus promoting durable solutions. Activity 1.5: Train community leaders on DRR and conflict resolution in regard to housing, land and property (HLP) & linking to other sectors for guided installation of these services at the same sites. Activity 1.6: Base line survey assessment to observe locations and status of project beneficiaries of 39,000 of women, men, girls and boys including protracted, newly displaced, refugee returnees and host community.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Sustainability of the project will be ensured through community ownership of the project. For all activities reaching the communities, IDP community leaders and host community leaders, including women and youth leaders will be consulted in order to adapt the activity to the context and ensured community participation, Where possible community members will be encourage to volunteer and participate in the project. Collaboration with both host community and IDPs will ensure the project adhere to do no harm principles. Using community participation leverage will ensure a longer term impact of the project. Establishment of community structures and its capacity building aims at ensuring the community focal persons have capacity to continue support referrals from the communities to other service providers beyond the project durations, established service mapping will also provide a long term network between the communities and service providers thus ensuring sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The focus of the present intervention will be on reaching vulnerable groups of individuals, such as extremely vulnerable individuals, children and adolescents at risk, female HHs, elderly, disabled, etc. Active participation of individuals with special needs will be pursued to identify, support and strengthen the coping mechanisms that beneficiaries often create to protect themselves against threats to their safety and dignity, and also foster beneficiaries’ self-esteem and development of skills. Participation of beneficiaries will also ensure that the proposed intervention is appropriate and effective. The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries will be prioritised by ensuring confidentiality, prioritising the best interests of the child and respecting the wishes of parents/caregivers, where they do not endanger the child further. All measures to respect and adhere to the Do No Harm approach will be taken during the process of beneficiary selection, service provision and data collection and management. Specialised services and useful information about service providers will be provided to all beneficiaries that meet the vulnerability criteria, including both IDP and host communities. Hence, services will be provided purely on the basis of need, in line with humanitarian principles.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with female beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through NoFYL female staff accessing them and identifying their needs.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risks: The volatile security and political situation might limit or even preclude access to the target areas and regular implementation of the project activities. A shortage of adequately skilled staff especially around CMR and response to child survivors, capable to positively interact with extremely vulnerable people, as well as the lack of specialised services in the target areas, might affect the quality and the impact of the project activities. Mitigations: - Continuously assess and closely monitor the evolvement of the security and political situation in the target areas, and have the flexibility to quickly adapt the implementation methodology as needed in order to guarantee the achievement of the project objective and the most effective impact. In this regard, NoFYL will maintain regular information flow and constantly consult with the cluster leads and donor. On the staff skills continuously train, coach and follow up the frontline workers to ensure timely and quality services is provided to beneficiaries.
