Project Summary

The project will be implemented from January 2018 to December 30th 2018 in Ménaka commune. The project will be
implemented for 12 months and will support 1518 very poor households. The overall objective of the project is to "contribute to the lasting improvement of the vulnerable households' food and nutrition security in northern Mali".

Needs

The present project is part of the ARC program which is a multi year initiative financed by USAID/Food for Peace and the European Union Emergency Trust Fund (EUTF). ARC is composed of HI, ACTED, Action Contre la Faim, DRC, IRC, NRC and Solidarités International. All the partners except NRC will implement cash transfers; in addition, ACTED will implement the disaster risk reduction component of the program and NRC will manage activities related to civil documentation and land related issues. Since 2012, northern Mali has been facing a complex multidimensional crisis and has been destabilized by an important political and military crisis that significantly deteriorated the state of the social cohesion and unity of the country. Mali is facing a structural food and nutritional crisis affecting hundreds of thousands of vulnerable households annually leading to the deterioration of households’ economies due to the reduced purchasing power and limited production capacities. Beyond accentuating important humanitarian needs, the conflict in the Northern regions has reinforced ethnic, geographic and political tensions undermining the Malian social fabric. This security and political instability has led to the displacement of massive populations (more than 520,000 displaced since 2013) and increased humanitarian needs in a country already facing major structural difficulties. 36,690 people (24% women and 54% children) are still displaced in Mali and 138,811 Malian people are still refugees in the border countries (Mauritania, Burkina Faso and Niger).In the districts targeted by the intervention, nearly 145,500 people are in phases 3 to 5 in terms of food insecurity on a global population of 752,000 inhabitants, or 19.3% of the population of these circles.Even though there has been a good amount of rain in 2017, it has been unevenly distributed in Mali, both geographically and over time, which has had a negative impact on agricultural production and pasture (because of pockets of drought and floods, which damaged pastures). Thus, an early agricultural and pastoral lean period is expected in 2018. This information was confirmed during the last Harmonized Framework workshop held last November 2017. Projected Analysis (June to August 2018) shows that 794,743 people (4.21%) will be in need of food assistance with 775,171 in the crisis phase and 19,572 in the emergency phase (17.47% in Phase 2). Amongst them, 60,833 individuals will be between Phase 3 to 5 in Gao region, representing 8.6% of the total population (the second highest rate with Mopti) and67,354 individuals will be between Phase 3 and 5 in Timbuktu region.According to ENSAN, throughout Mali, 11 circles are in a situation where the overall food insecurity is higher or equal to 40%. In the circles of intervention, the global rates of households suffering from food insecurity are the following:Ansongo (40.2%), Rharous (54.4%), Goundam (19.3%), Dire (38.7%), Menaka (57%), Douentza (38.8%). The circle of Menaka is the only one with only 15 to 20% of the people being in severe food insecurity (17.9%).This situation could be aggravated by the increasingly complex intervention context and the absence of state authoritiesin certain regions that contributes to a significant increase in crime, localized conflicts between armed groups and inter community tensions; particularly in the North and the Center of the country where 224 jihadist attacks were made during 2018 and 41 people have been killed during only the fourth week of January 2018. This situation could be worsened throughout 2018 due to the upcoming local, regional and presidential elections. Thus, the communities already affected by the conflict also face difficulties in terms of access to food, economic security and other needs such as civil documentation and access to land.

Activities

1 518 households are identified and benefit from unconditional cash transfers for a total of 120 000 FCFA (218USD) per households in the commune of Ménaka. The year 1 of the ARC program implementation as well as previous experiences of partners helped find that single cash transfers would provide an improvement in the household situation at the time of distribution, especially if distributed at key moments of the agricultural/pastoral calendar. The cash aims at enabling the beneficiaries to cope with different periods of needs related to the lean season (pastoral and agricultural). Thus, the distribution period will take into account these two key moments namely pre lean and lean period.Thus, cash transfers will ensure greater monetary availability within the household, especially to: (i) limit credits in the pre lean and lean periods and thus reduce the pressure on the immediate sale of crops for their repayment; ii) facilitate the repayment of loans that may have already been contracted earlier and limit the impact of repayments on the livelihoods of households. Partners will implement these transfers through different methods (direct cash transfers with service providers, electronic vouchers and paper vouchers) depending on the security context and the state of markets. Gender integration: The targeting process and more specifically targeting criteria were adapted in order to integrate the higher vulnerability of women including women head of households. Specific needs of lactating/pregnant women and children under 5 years old are integrated; at the same level of poverty a household with at least one child under 5 or a lactating/pregnant women will be prioritized due to their higher vulnerability to nutritional insecurity. Moreover, at least 30% of the member of selecting committees will be women and IRC will ensure that a sex and age diversity is ensured; youths will also be integrated. Moreover, IRC will also support the establishment of activities aiming at reducing malnutrition from one hand (though community sensitization sessions) and at increasing resilience of households on the other hand (through the implementation of VSLA, development of individuals accompanying measures, funding of community project aiming at reducing malnutrition). The training of mothers, in screening at BP (Brachial Perimeter) will aim at positively transforming the traditional role of women (ensuring food and household hygiene) by improving the skills of women and placing them as essential links for screening children in communities.Accountability and feedback mechanism:During the implementation of the project, a management committee (accountability committee) will be set up during the confrontation of the pre-lists at the general assembly in each village. Composed of 5 members, this committee will be responsible for receiving and forwarding complaints to IRC for consideration and management and feedback / solution to complainants.In addition, IRC recognizes the limitations of traditional complaint management and feedback systems in the Malian context. The monitoring and evaluation system will be strengthened to give voice to each group of the population, including women. Accountability (quantitative approach) surveys as well as focus group (qualitative approach) by gender will be organized as far as possible by another team (M & E or project) independent of the intervention, in order to better understand the perceptions, opinions and information protection risks (inside households, in villages or on journeys). The collected data will be systematically disaggregated according to the gender of the head of the household in order to identify possible specificities of practices, attitudes, perception according to gender.
