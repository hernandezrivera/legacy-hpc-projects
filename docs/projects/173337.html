Project Summary

In 2021, PIB will continue to support the Cox's Bazar (CXB) Education Sector's JRP in both camp and crisis-affected host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf .
Building on the current and prior experience, PIB will continue to ensure and expand education access at all levels, with an extended focus to early childhood education, secondary education, and education for adolescents and youth both in camp and HC. In camps, PIB will sustain its community-based learning facilities (CBLF) and deliver strengthened caregiver-led education approaches in response to the new challenges of COVID-19.
Capacity building trainings for the relevant education staffs, learning facilitators and teachers both in camps and HC, will aim to strengthen the quality of teaching and learning process through a blended approach across the age spectrum targeted by Education Sector. Regular monitoring of the progress will be ensured through a continuous support of on-the-job coaching and mentoring. In 2021, education staffs and respective Govt. education officials will be further supported to provide better mentoring support to learning facilitators and teachers for inclusive, quality education.
PIB will continue to ensure community engagement and ownership in education by building the capacities of Learning Facility Committees in the camps to support equitable access to education, safeguarding and protection, DRM including stablishing effective accountability mechanism. Adolescents and youth will be supported to be the champion of the change, to lead social cohesion initiatives to promote values of equality, tolerance and inclusion within the camps and across the HC. PIB has a unique and sustained focus on caregiver engagement in ECD, having rolled out a Parenting Education package in 2021
Finally, PIB will extend support to localization through coordination and capacity building of local partners and Government counterparts with the aim to move to a sustainable quality of education goal and strengthen the respective system.

1 - Identified Needs

In the design of this project, PIB has considered community and local partners’ input, lessons learned from current projects, and the available needs assessments and field data to identify project activities that will best address the current needs within the Education Sector for 2021. In camp settings, education has been deeply impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic. According to the 2020 MSNA, only 64% of household who attended a learning center pre-COVID reported having spoken to a teacher since the centers closed in March. While 86% of individuals attending learning pre-COVID reported continuing learning remotely, with a much lower percentage reporting engaging with a teacher even one time, we can assume that the quality of remote learning is mediocre at best. PIB’s approach to caregiver-led education during COVID has been to conduct follow-up home visits to all learners, following social distance guidelines, to try to combat this challenge; PIB intends to continue and adapt this approach in 2021 as needed if learning centers do not reopen. When learning centers do reopen, the relationship formed between caregivers and facilitators during COVID will be reused to ensure the quality of the regular shift-wise learning throughout the year. Additionally, with 27% of households reporting loss of access to education, PIB is targeting the continuation and expansion of their programming through local partners to ensure that, where needed, additional education services are being offered, particularly at the ECD and youth/adolescent levels. In the host community, COVID-19 has also resulted in significant learning losses for Bangladeshi children, the extent of which is not yet known. Only 39% of households who were enrolled in education pre-COVID reported having spoken to a teacher since the schools closed in March, a much wider gap than in the camps. Pre-existing barriers to education for children and youth in the host including deteriorating access to quality schools and the increasing demand for income-generation are only likely to increase in 2021. The economy in Cox’s Bazar district suffered immensely during COVID-19, as extended lockdown resulted in job losses, business closures, and reduced incomes. These factors, combined with sustained school closures and limited access to remote learning solutions, have already resulted in an alarming increase in child labor (49% increase) and child marriage (20% increase) in the last six months that will make extreme harder for children and youth to return to school in 2021, specially the girls will be affected to a large extend. Further, the need for quality education, including catch-up programs to address learning loss, has never been greater, at a time when the quality of host community schools is already low. PIB plans to target these challenges by continuing and further strengthening the engagement with host community schools and education officials in Ukhiya and Teknaf. PIB’s projected youth programming in Teknaf, providing both youth education in the camps and youth livelihoods support in the host community, will target acute needs for youth support, while programming in Ukhiya will continue to work with both primary and secondary schools to support in their response to COVID-19 through 2021.

2 - Activities or outputs

Output 1: Access - Continued immediate access to equitable, gender-responsive learning opportunities in an enabling, protective environment for crisis-affected children (girls and boys aged 3 to 24 years old) despite COVID 19.1.1. Learning facilities for refugee 3-24 G & B will be established (LCs), identified (new CBLFs), or sustained (existing CBLFs) in line with Edu Sector standards 1.2. Learning facilities in the camp and host community will be equipped with gender segregated accessible WASH facilities, hand washing stations and COVID 19 hygiene kits based on requirement 1.3. Learning centres will be rehabilitated to ensure the accessibility for CWD in line with sector guideline1.4. Learning faculties will be organized in line with gender diversity and inclusion specially for adolescent girls. 1.5. ECCD learning facilities for 3-5 in the camps and 4-5 in HC will be identified and equipped.1.6. ECCD programmes will be delivered for 3-5 G & B in camps and 4-5 G & B HC, adopting PIB’s Shishu Bikash Kendra (SBK) approaches for play-based learning, basic PSS and education readiness.1.7. Learning programmes in the camps will be delivered for 6-24 G & B according to Education sector LCFA and GIEP1.8. Myanmar curriculum Piloting will be continued and move forward to scale up in the camps for the 6-14 G&B1.9. Learning facilities (3-24 G & B), and schools (4-18 G & B) in camps and HC respectively will be equipped with relevant and adequate materials for quality learning in a safe and protective environment, incl. T-L, DRM, H&H and/or recreational materials, aligned with relevant Edu Sector standards, and coordinated with GoB where and as relevant (particularly for HC).1.10. HC 4-18 G & B will be supported for equitable learning opportunities through remedial classes; life skills and resilience programmes; or awareness raising activities on inclusion and equitable education and protection along with COVID 19 health and hygiene issues.1.11. All facilitators and other education support personnel, e.g. HC Mentors, Education FPs and Aunties (volunteers to support girls’ participation and retention) will receive incentives, aligned with Edu Sector set rates.1.12. ASER+ assessment will be continued to register new refugee learners into level-appropriate sessions according to Edu Sector standards laid out in the Guideline on Informal Education Programme (GIEP).1.13. Caregiver led education will be focused to build capacity of the caregivers to enhance support to quality education. 1.14. Caregiver led education will be integrated with the gender transformative approach for the caregivers to build up understanding of the role of gender transformation in education.
