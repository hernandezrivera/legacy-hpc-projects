Project Summary

The Somalia Humanitarian Fund (SHF) is a multi-donor country-based pooled fund that was established in 2010 to support the timely allocation and disbursement of donor resources to address the most urgent humanitarian needs in Somalia. The SHF has been essential in enabling timely, coordinated and effective humanitarian action due to its distinct comparative advantages – the unearmarked nature of the Fund; the established and functioning accountability systems; integration within the existing coordination systems; and ﬂexibility.

Project Activities

1. Advocate for resource mobilization: Conduct regular bilateral and multilateral donor briefings and outreach to ensure that the SHF is well resourced 2. Produce and disseminate appropriate information products providing updates on analysis of humanitarian financing for Somalia for decision making 3. Increase donor confidence through effective fund management including implementation of SHF accountability framework for effective risk management 4. Provide guidance and support to the Humanitarian Coordinator to ensure the strategic and efficient use of the fund in line with allocation principles taking into consideration complementary donor and CERF funding. 5. Make pooled funding decisions in consultation with the Advisory Board, ICCG and HCT to support strategic response to high priority humanitarian needs and in alignment with HRP 6. Strategic management of SHF reserve to ensure t is responsive to emergencies 7. Support localization in humanitarian response through progressive increments in fund allocations to local NGOs 8. Provide of guidance and support to partners and clusters throughout all segments of the allocation cycle – strategic prioritization, project selection, disbursements, implementation of activities and accountability measures, project closure – in close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders.

Mitigation Measures

N/A. While this project is an administrative project for the SHF secretariat, the projects funded under the SHF must include the following:1. Gender: Implementing partners will include gender sensitive indicators and activities as well as ensure gender considerations in needs analysis and outputs. The project specific GAM reference code must be included into proposals.2. Disability inclusion: All projects must include breakdown of beneficiaries to include sex-disaggregated people with disabilities targeted.3. AAP: Partners are asked to describe:- how beneficiaries and affected populations have been and will be involved in the different stages of the project management cycle: needs assessment, project design, implementation.- what mechanisms are put in place to enable beneficiaries and affected populations to provide feedback and complaints, and how these will feed into management decisions- what mechanisms have been and will be put to ensure adequate information sharing and communication to beneficiaries and affected populations- Considerations made for the principles of Do No Harm4. Protection mainstreaming and GBV: Partners asked to describe:- how partners have prioritized the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and considered the principles of Do No Harm in the proposed project.- how the proposed project enables equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs.- what mechanisms are put in place to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist affected population’s to claim their rights. All funded projects must properly address the above, and these are vetted through the clusters and review committees. Partners are given guidance documents on the above to support their proposal preparation. In addition, a Programme Manual is being prepared ahead of the next allocation to include guidance on all the above to be used as a supporting reference to ensure proposals are of high quality.
