Project Summary

This project aims to provide adequate and safe shelter solutions and improve the living conditions of vulnerable households affected by displacements in the Southwest and Northwest regions of Cameroon. This will be achieved through the provision of shelters, the rehabilitation of community and public infrastructures and the distribution of essential households items to 36,000 individuals.
The latest multisectoral needs assessment (IOM, 2023) highlighted that approximately 464,146 individuals are self-settled in the bushes and in acute need of shelter and household items in the Northwest and Southwest regions. Furthermore, most accommodations across all displaced populations are critically below standards in terms of location (official, informal/bush, and collective shelter); type of shelter; square meter per person; structural damages, land tenure, etc.
To respond to this need, and ensure the project reaches the most vulnerable groups, NRC will mainstream gender, age, and disability into its programming, ensuring groups with specific needs and challenges have meaningful access and can actively participate in the project. Consultations with communities will ensure the designs of the shelter are culturally appropriate and meet the specific needs of groups with specific needs. Lastly, NRC will actively communicate with targeted communities, ensuring their feedbacks are captured and contribute to the adaptation of its programming.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The situation In the North-West and South-West regions remained tense in 2023, with continued violence, including intercommunal clashes, and targeted attacks, abductions, kidnappings, killings, unlawful arrests, and destruction of property. As the crisis has grown more protracted, the number of returnees to the region has slowly increased. Upon their return, an estimated third of returnee households found their homes damaged due to conflict (approximatively 8,200 households), forcing them to stay with acquaintance in overcrowded conditions. As noted by the Shelter Cluster, families hosting returnees are also living in inadequate housing due to damages, overcrowding and lack of NFI. Access to land tenure and housing continues to be challenging, especially for women. As co-lead of the HLP AoR, NRC will continue advocating for secure access to HLP before delivering any type of shelter assistance to people in need. According to 2024 HNO, the OCHA Multisector Needs Assessment conducted by the International Organization for Migration, a total number of 464,146 of people need of adequate shelter and NFIs, including 207,010 IDPs, 198,919 Returnees and 58,235 Host and Non-Displaced affected by the crisis. The project will target 36,000 individuals in Southwest (Fako, Meme, Kupe Muanenguba, Manyu and Ndian Divisions) and Northwest (Mezam, Donga Matung, Momo, Bui, Mechum, Boyo and Ngoketunjia Divisions) with: 1. Provision of life-saving shelter and NFI support NRC will provide life-saving shelter and non-food items kits to the most vulnerable households. The composition of kits will align with the Shelter cluster guidance and confirmed by community consultation to ensure they are adapted and meet the specific needs of the communities. Items included in the kit have been selected to ensure they are mobile, which is important considering the risk of secondary displacement. 2. Provision of sustainable shelters. The project will support the rehabilitation/repair or construction of transitional/permanent shelter; NRC will ensure that people of all gender, ages and abilities receive humanitarian assistance that respond their specific vulnerabilities, needs, and capacities. 3. Support an enabling protection environment / social cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. NRC will encourage traditional construction methods to promote cultural and climatic adequacy and to reduce the dependency on imported materials, particularly in hard-to-reach areas in Cameroon. The project will actively adopt measures to reduce adverse impact on the environment and ecosystems by building shelters from sustainable, locally available materials and work with local partners whose technical capacity has been built
