Project Name

Supporting the vulnerable IDP, returnee, and host communities in Jonglei, Lakes, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Eastern Equatoria and hard-to-reach areas across South Sudan with emergency SNFI through a combination of static and mobile responses and with disaster risk reduction.

Start Date

01/01/2023

End Date

31/12/2023

Project Summary

In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, through static and mobile interventions, ACTED will utilize in-kind and cash-based modalities to cover SNFI gaps for IDPs, returnees, and host communities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups and people with special needs (PSNs).The activities will include distribution of in-kind NFI and Shelter kits, cash/voucher assistance for emergency shelter and NFIs, cash for work activities for shelter construction, and construction of communal shelters to respond to large scale emergency influx of displaced populations
The interventions will contribute to the following cluster objectives:
Sectoral objective 1: Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities.
Sectoral objective 2: Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs.
Sectoral objective 3: Support the most vulnerable returnees and host communities/affected non-displaced with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions.
Sectoral objective 4: Support recovery, resilience building and access to land, housing, and property.
Sectoral objective 5: Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills.
For all its activities, ACTED will implement COVID-19 and Ebola prevention measures. This will include increasing engagement with local authorities by explaining in detail mitigation strategies in place for each activity. ACTED teams will receive trainings on both Ebola and COVID-19 preventive measures and a full set of PPE stock will be used during project activities. During distributions, ACTED will ensure the beneficiaries and ACTED staff follow social distancing measures and temperature testing. Moreover, ACTED will install handwashing stations at distribution sites for beneficiaries to wash their hands before and after the receipt of the items.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is characterized by long-lasting conflicts, economic instability and frequent and increasing climate change-related hazards/shocks, leading to chronic food insecurity, disruptions of livelihoods and displacements. The latest South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO) stresses the enormous needs of 2.4 million people for emergency shelter and non-food items assistance, in addition to 329 000 refugees and asylum seekers in a particularly concerning situation of vulnerability and requiring similar humanitarian aid. Indeed, be it inter-ethnic violence, poverty, food insecurity or climate-change, every year tens of thousands of people are forcibly displaced, leaving behind their home and belongings. The rainy seasons observed in 2022 had catastrophic consequences, damaging shelters and infrastructure, destroying assets and livelihoods, and forcing more than 900,000 people (UN info, October 2022) to displace in order to seek refuge, food and safety – which is becoming a yearly phenomenon. This situation exacerbates the vulnerability for women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities, representing more than half of the affected population. Thus, people urgently need the support from humanitarian actors to provide a safe environment and to prevent the further deterioration of their living conditions and exposure to protection risks and food insecurity. As per the HNO 2022, in 23 counties, more than half of the affected people require emergency shelter support – particularly in the target regions of Jonglei, Lakes, Northern Bahr El-Ghazal, and the Upper Nile States where there is the highest concentration of displaced people. Needs vary significantly depending on the location, availability of resources and proximity to a conflict zone, which can generate a ripple effect: such as damage to natural resources used for construction work such as timber and grass, damage to roads and bridges, jeopardizing humanitarian accessibility and leading to delays in the provision of emergency assistance etc. Hence, the limited resources and precarity of the situation may cause competition between communities and ultimately conflict, precipitating further crises and displacements. Figures for internally displaced peoples have been steadily increasing over the past years as environmental shocks and political instability continue to worsen. Therefore, in 2023, ACTED will continue implementing S/NFI interventions in line with the following Cluster’s objectives: CO 1: Percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition -- New displacement CO 2: Percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition – Protracted CO 3: Enhance community dignity and engagement in the provision of S/NFI services CO 4: Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures

2 - Project activities

In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, ACTED will utilize in-kind and cash-based modalities to cover SNFI gaps for IDPs, returnees, and host communities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups and PSNs. This will be done though distributions in areas where ACTED has a static presence, such as Juba and Bor former PoCs or through mobile interventions in hard-to-reach areas. ACTED has an established emergency response mechanism to ensure a timely response and effective coordination as part of its mobile responses which include SNFI, WASH, CCCM and protection components. A shock is flagged through clusters, working groups, ICCGs, and/or humanitarian coordination fora. After a shock, ACTED is able to deploy a team in 3-5 days to conduct a scoping assessment, which will last apprx. 3 days to complete. Tools such as site profiles and site monitoring matrix 3Ws are shared with partners. In line with pipeline availability, ACTED will immediately launch a multisectoral response as per the identified needs of the targeted communities. The intervention duration will usually last between 4-6 weeks. ACTED will coordinate with all the clusters, working groups and humanitarian coordination fora as well as reaching out to ECHO and RRM partners to avoid duplication. ACTED will also coordinate with RRC, local partners, mobile partners, and cluster state focal points. In addition to activities surrounding service monitoring, ACTED will train local partners on coordination mechanisms to avoid duplication of response. Apprx.,2-3 months after an intervention is complete, ACTED will return to the location to conduct a post intervention/distribution monitoring and will share the results with the relevant clusters.The activities will include distribution of in-kind NFI and Shelter kits, cash/voucher assistance for emergency shelter and NFIs, cash for work activities for shelter construction, and construction of communal shelters to respond to large scale emergency influx. Moreover, ACTED will support communities in locations prone to man-made or natural disasters with DRR preparedness activities. The DRR activities to be conducted by ACTED are the following:1- Establishment community managed disaster risk reduction committees and centers 2- Establishment of Participatory Disaster Risk Assessment process 3- Training of staff and government partners in DRR related prevention and mitigation measures 3-Community Action Plan validation and linkages workshop, implementation of DRR works by communities. As part of the mobile interventions, ACTED will ensure protection mainstreaming and follow a do no harm approach. This will be done through the inclusion of at least 1 Protection Officer to ensure protection mainstreaming measures are incorporated into response. Protection Officers will do awareness raising on HLP and SGBV. For mobile interventions, a protection scoping report will be prepared and shared with other partners for advocacy. These will be supported with the beneficiary selection process which includes suitability and vulnerability criteria and is supported by PMCs, which are composed of 50% women. The mobile responses will establish a protection pathway through its CCCM components which is handed over to the community at the end of the intervention. ACTED mobile responses will entail service monitoring and will where possible establish an advisable pathway through which community can make referrals to mitigate cases, HLP issues, and legal assistance.ACTED will prioritize to minimize any HLP issues arising from its shelter interventions, including eviction. At the start of each intervention, ACTED will conduct FGDs and KIIs with boma chiefs and local authorities to discuss any issues related to HLP. Moreover, the assessment period looks at HLP issues and informs the design of the intervention.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market analysis is a crucial part of ACTED’s cash and voucher activities and are incorporated into the response. Before undertaking any cash or voucher activities, ACTED will conduct a detailed market analysis and routinely monitor prices to ensure the market is functional and has the sufficient capacity to meet the needs

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher programming is used as complementary to the in-kind SNFI items provision in ACTED’s programming. Cash and voucher modalities will be preferred where possible. This includes preference from beneficiaries and availability of items in local markets as well as capacity of the market to absorb such transfers as in areas directly affected by disaster, functioning markets are either not available or slow to recover from the shock with limited availability of goods due to hampered access or damaged infrastructure. ACTED will implement both conditional (cash for work) and unconditional (emergency needs for the most vulnerable households). The conditional modality, which is cash for work for emergency shelter construction, will have a two-fold benefit; supporting the households financially while addressing shelter needs. The unconditional modality will facilitate access to a wider range of items and empower the beneficiaries to choose the specific items that their household needs, in locations where the markets are functional.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As per its standard operating procedures for CRM, throughout the project, ACTED will ensure that multiple channels of communication (e.g.: help desk, staff registering complaints, hotline etc,) are available for ACTED to receive feedback from communities including the most vulnerable members such as individuals living with disabilities and individuals who are not literate. Furthermore, ACTED will have at least one women staff member in help desks to encourage women to register their feedback to ACTED.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the implementation project activities, beneficiaries will be sensitized on the complaints and response mechanism in camps, and on how to submit concerns or complaints to ACTED. Moreover, help desks at project sites will be installed at central locations and equipped with visibility materials. Multiple avenues will be used to spread the awareness of the CRM from key messaging using megaphones during activities, to engagements at community meetings, house-to-house visits conducted by outreach workers, radio and visibility material (banners, stickers, business cards…).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During sensitization of beneficiaries on CRM mechanism, ACTED informs beneficiaries about PSEA and provides clear instructions on how to report such cases utilizing CRM present at the site of implementation. ACTED explains how such sensitive cases are handled to encourage reporting.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ACTED’s independent AMEU will continuously register the complaints and feedback of the target population and insert them into ACTED South Sudan’s learning database. The findings will be discussed at least on a monthly basis with the project implementation and project development teams to ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the ongoing project as well as for the design of upcoming projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ACTED mainstreams protection in all its interventions. Throughout its sectoral programming in shelter and settlements, ACTED assesses and monitor protection and GBV-related safety risks. ACTED follows the guidance of the Global Protection cluster and its protection mainstreaming toolkit to incorporate the four key elements of protection mainstreaming throughout the project cycle. For its programming, in order to ensure safety, dignity and avoid causing harm in its programming, ACTED examines and monitor risks of GBV in the communities’ daily activities, at high-risk security zones (eg. around water collection points) and at, to and from any distribution point. ACTED also regularly monitors and discusses with beneficiaries to inform any tension between different ethnic, religious or other groups and consider cultural practices within the affected community. In terms of accountability, participation and empowerment ACTED makes sure that up-to-date referral mechanisms are in place to provide an appropriate response and specialized assistance to PSNs and survivors. Feedback and complaints mechanism at community level are set-up/strengthened in parallel to ACTED’s CRM, taking into consideration confidentiality issues and accessibility. Any ACTED staff and related personnel are trained on the Code of Conduct and PSEA. ACTED will additionally strive to have gender representation across its field teams and ensure adequate trainings on protection, gender and GBV prevention mainstreaming. ACTED coordinates with specialized protection/GBV to ensure risk mitigation and appropriate mediation. ACTED additionally engages with women to ensure GBV prevention and risk mitigation to risks associated with firewood collection. Daily activities are monitored with protection partners to carefully examine potential rise of violence (including GBV) to boys, girls, women and men, and identify preventive measures and responses. The establishment of community-based gender-responsive early warning systems and the strengthening of community-based support/recovery mechanisms is another point of attention to coordinate with the GBV sub-cluster. In order to address the gender imbalance and respond to associated challenges, ACTED’s activities are designed to ensure that there is balanced gender representation to ensure the female perspective is put forward in the activities of these groups and the target communities at large. ACTED additionally always ensures to include representatives of marginalized groups into any community-based structure established and ensures their rights and wishes to participate are understood by decision-makers in the communities. As women in South Sudan face denial of access to rights, lands and properties, their inclusion is of paramount importance to safely access shelters. Registration and distribution times, durations and sites are organized by consultation with women, men, girls and boys, and distributions happen during daylight hours to allow safe travel home with the final information provided 3 days in advance ACTED ensures that the needs of vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly, are prioritized and addressed during distributions in all locations. Fast-tracked, caretaker’s systems of collection and home deliveries are implemented accordingly.Finally, ACTED will also take every necessary precaution to mitigate the spread of both Ebola and COVID-19. This will be done through a combination of activities such as hygiene messaging, installing washing stations and checking temperatures before the commencement of activities. Likewise, social distancing will be enforced and beneficiaries will receive frequent safety messaging at all activity sites. ACTED is strongly aware that illnesses such as Ebola and COVID-19 disproportionately affect PSN and at-risk groups and will mitigate this threat through all available means.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climate change is one of the major issues in South-Sudan as flooding massively affect populations. Floods exacerbate the vulnerability of communities and force people to flee their house, increasing their basic needs. In view of the climatic situation, ACTED will ensure in each of its intervention to strengthen the resilience and self-reliance of communities in order to reduce the impacts of recurrent shocks. As an example in this project, ACTED will strengthen the communities to adopt strategies to increase their resilience to climate and disaster related shocks/stressors, included flooding, through participatory, community-driven planning and resource management.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The proposed action will incorporate gender, age and diversity into the full project management cycle in order to prevent and mitigate protection risks and ensure all activities are inclusive. Where possible, gender, age and (to some extent) diversity disaggregated information will be collected to further inform the programming and advocacy. The integrated protection response proposed adopts a twin-track approach by ensuring protection is mainstreamed throughout the project cycle (awareness, attitude change, integration and inclusion) and by aiming at empowering communities. ACTED will focus on the empowerment of affected populations and particularly women in its approach. Prevention and mitigation of risks related to GBV and gender inequalities will be integrated into the interventions through GBV/Gender mainstreaming. The incorporation and monitoring of the protection principles in all project activities and sites’ locations will be assessed in terms of safety and accessibility.
