Project Summary

Under this project, Dorcas Aid International will be providing comprehensive protection activities through various community centers located in Aleppo, Homs, and Rural Damascus. These centers serve as vital hubs for protection projects targeting beneficiaries in specific areas, including Hanano, Hellok, and Maskameh in Aleppo; Al Waer and Tal Kalakh in Homs; and Al Tal and Harassta in Rural Damascus. These projects will be implemented either directly by Dorcas or in partnership with local organizations.
The project aims to significantly enhance awareness and knowledge on key protection subjects, including children's rights and non-violent communication. Moreover, it seeks to bolster resilience and enable effective responses to gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection (CP) issues within the Syrian community, with a particular focus on women, girls, and boys residing in Aleppo, Homs, and Rural Damascus.
The overarching objective is to fortify the resilience and protection of conflict-affected children, adolescents, and their families in Syria by fostering a protective environment conducive to their well-being and positive coping mechanisms. To achieve this goal, the project will deliver a range of crucial services, including case management, psychosocial support (PSS) sessions, parenting without violence sessions, and life skills training. These interventions will cater to vulnerable displaced and returnee populations, including older persons and individuals with disabilities, assisting families in identifying suitable protection measures to ensure their safety and security within their communities.
To address the immediate protection needs of vulnerable Syrians, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and displaced individuals residing in Aleppo, Homs, and Rural Damascus, Dorcas will engage in targeted outreach efforts. Identification of beneficiaries will be conducted through a well-established network of community center staff, comprising experienced social workers and community workers who will conduct thorough individual needs assessments.
Furthermore, with regard to GBV case management and PSS services provided at the community centers, Dorcas will prioritize the provision of specialized support tailored to the unique needs of survivors. GBV case management will involve a comprehensive approach encompassing risk assessment, safety planning, referrals to essential services (such as medical care and legal assistance), and ongoing monitoring and follow-up to ensure survivors' well-being. PSS sessions will offer a safe and supportive space for individuals to express themselves, process trauma, and build coping skills through various therapeutic techniques and activities. These sessions will be facilitated by trained professionals adept at addressing the psychosocial needs of survivors in a sensitive and empowering manner.
The project aims to significantly enhance awareness and knowledge on key protection subjects, including children's rights, non-violent communication, and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention, within the Syrian community. Through a series of targeted awareness sessions, individuals, especially women, girls, and boys residing in Aleppo, Homs, and Rural Damascus, will gain valuable insights and practical tools to navigate challenging situations and safeguard their rights. These sessions will cover topics such as identifying signs of abuse, understanding resources available for survivors, and promoting healthy relationships and conflict resolution skills.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Additionally, Dorcas is committed to upholding the principles of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) throughout the project implementation process. Robust risk analysis and management strategies will be integrated into all project activities to identify and mitigate potential risks related to safeguarding and protection issues. Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) will also be established to ensure meaningful engagement with beneficiaries and promote transparency and accountability in project delivery. Through these measures, Dorcas aims to create a safe and empowering environment where vulnerable individuals can access quality protection services and support to rebuild their lives with dignity and resilience. Dorcas will uphold strict guidelines and procedures to ensure the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries and staff. This includes conducting thorough background checks on personnel, implementing codes of conduct, providing training on PSEA principles and reporting mechanisms, establishing confidential complaints mechanisms, and promptly investigating and addressing any allegations of misconduct. Dorcas has also a well established AAP focuses on ensuring that affected communities are actively engaged, informed, and empowered throughout the project cycle. Dorcas will prioritize AAP by establishing clear communication channels with beneficiaries, soliciting their feedback and input on project activities, involving them in decision-making processes, and transparently sharing information about project progress, outcomes, and challenges. Dorcas will conduct participatory risk assessments to systematically identify and prioritize risks, assess their potential impact, and develop tailored risk management strategies. By involving relevant stakeholders, including community members, local authorities, and partner organizations, Dorcas ensures that risks are comprehensively addressed and mitigated to safeguard project outcomes and the well-being of beneficiaries. Dorcas will establish robust M&E systems to monitor the implementation of project activities, measure progress towards objectives and outcomes, and evaluate the impact of interventions on beneficiaries and communities. This includes setting clear indicators, collecting relevant data at regular intervals, conducting qualitative and quantitative assessments, and using findings to adapt and improve project strategies over time.
