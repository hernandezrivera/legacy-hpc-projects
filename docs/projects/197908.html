Project Summary

The North West and South West make up 30% of the total population of Cameroon. The population of the North West region is 1969000 while that of the South West region is 1553000 giving a total population of over 3522000 (2015 population statistics-Cameroon). This population is at the brink of jumping into worse living conditions as living standards keep dropping by the day due to the ongoing crisis. The Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism (IRRM) is therefore crucial at this point to savage the population in these regions from worse situations; the people face difficulties accessing WASH, health, food security, education, protection
Every day in a remote rural village in South West and North West Region, hundreds of mothers and babies die at childbirth. Many moms in villages give birth at home, some in the bushes, which is a risk to them and their babies, a big part of the problem is the lack of access to sterile supplies in rural communities where a big number of women live and work as well as the broken healthcare chain affecting those areas due to the ongoing crisis that have destroyed many villages and has cause many to fly in to the bushes. The traditional birth attendants severing umbilical cords with rusty blades infecting babies with tenants, midwives using their mouth to suck out mucus from the nostrils of a baby etc. Having identified these challenges CADEF is seeking support to procure mama kits from Teheca (customized prepared mama bag) to be provided free to all mothers to promote a safe delivery.
After years of watching midwives suck out mica from the nostrils of newborns to prevent birth asphyxia, women giving birth on bare floors and birth attendants using rusty blades to severe umbilical cord, the project will equip birth attendants in their communities and IDP camps with skills & exposure through training, address the challenge of accessing sterile supplies at childbirth in rural communities. These kits mean more moms will have a healthy and safe delivery and the supplies they need to care for their precious newborns.
The project seek to train 650 tradition birth attendance (TBA’s), 3000 community health workers (CHW’s), distribution of 5000 Maama kits to expectant mothers to improve maternal health among rural young girls and women, targeting pregnant women and girls living in IDP Camps, in bushes, hard to reach areas, secondary school, providing a sterilized ultra-affordable delivery kit containing essential supplies a woman requires at childbirth to ensure she has a clean, safe and hygienic delivery. We will work to ensure every baby gets an opportunity to live his or her potential and every mother gets the opportunity to watch her baby grow. The mama kit, also known as a clean delivery kit, is a regular kit, aimed at having mothers undergo safe and protective delivery. The kit contains 500 grams of cotton wool, baby soap, two razor blades, two polythene sheets, two gauze pieces, 3 surgical gloves and an immunization card. Teheca designed mama kit come in a bag which contains the manual that the whole family can read to know the precautions in case the baby comes before they reach the hospital. The mama bag will play a big role in improving the maternal reproductive health in rural communities by inclusion of men.
Main Aim Of The Project
To contribute on the reduction of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality
(illness and deaths) through clean and safe delivery.
Objectives Of The Project
• To provide information to women, men and communities on the importance of delivering under a clean environment.
• To provide Maama Kits to all women who deliver in camps, far pushes, health units, and in the community.
• Training of Traditional Birth Attendance(TBA’s) and Community Health Workers (CHWs) for proper hygiene and clean environment at delivery and improved Extended Program for Immunization (EPI) activities to camps and hard to reach areas affected by the ongoing crisis in NW and SW.
