Project Summary

Since the conflict between the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the Sudanese Army Forces (SAF) broke out on 15th April 2023, over 1,042,1114 individuals have been internally displaced. Of this number, it is estimated that 404,028 children of school age have equally been displaced. The situation is fast evolving, and the numbers are expected to change. 319,194 people are estimated to have fled the country to neighboring countries. The Revised HRP 2023 indicates that 8.6 million children have been affected by the conflict. This war has compounded the challenges facing the education sector in Sudan. Prior, schools had remained closed in January and February, adding further to the entrenched loss of learning for Sudanese children. Through this proposal, WFP attempts to contribute towards mitigation of the impact of the conflict on school children by integrating school feeding into a comprehensive package of support falling under access and protection by ensuring that at risk boys and girls are protected from completely falling out of education. WFP proposes provision of emergency school meals to support both formal and informal learning. WFP will work with other core partners such as UNICEF to create conducive child friendly spaces for children affected by war as part of a comprehensive education sector response to the crisis which has affected millions of Sudanese with over a million people being internally displaced and hundreds of thousands others fleeing the country. While efforts are being made for internally displaced children to access education, it is still not clear how those that have fled the country will be integrated into those countries’ education systems.
Inter-communal conflicts between the Masalit and the Arabs in West Darfur have also broken out, a possible indication that the regular tribal conflicts may resume, which may further increase insecurity and place extra burden on communities hosting the IDPs.
The education sector like other sectors, has been hit by the ongoing war. School infrastructure has been damaged, or classes occupied by either forces or indeed IDPs. Schools in Darfurs, Khartoum, Northern, North Kordofan and West Kordofan were forced shut six weeks before end of the school calendar. Teachers and students alike have fled schools and are displaced. Ongoing assessments indicate deaths of children who have been caught in the cross-fire.
There are real concerns that children will be more affected and an increase in drop-out rates is expected. Children from affected states and poor families, girls and those in hard-to-reach areas are the most vulnerable. Further, children due to write grade six examinations are at real risk of failing to do so, whilst those that have fled to other countries may not have the opportunity to access learning. Stakeholders are worried about access to basic services for children such as education, nutrition and health. Sudan has among the highest number of food insecure people in the world. Looting, lack of access to communication, the impending rainy season which may result in floods will further affect both access and quality of education. Further, the traumatic experiences that children and teachers have been exposed to since the conflict started will remain a scar in their minds, unless stakeholders mobilise a comprehensive support package to help children refocus their minds away from the war.
The provision of complementary feeding will support all other activities as both an enabler of access and retention through provision of meals for children. The meals will attract children to safer spaces to be created by UNICEF and protect them from further mental and physical harm.
This project targets the eastern part of the country that have had minimal disruption from the war, in particular, Blue Nile, Kassala, Gedaref, Red Sea and South Kordofan targeting 365,717 children (296,321 vulnerable residents and 69,396 IDPs) in the first phase of the project.

1 - Needs

the war that broke out on 15th April 2023 has led to an exodus of hundreds of thousands of citizens from conflict affected areas of Khartoum, Ormudsman, Darfur regions and Al Jazera to safer states. Among the most affected, are school age children and school children in particular of around 404,028 school-age-children.The conflict has left a trail of destruction of cross-sectoral infrastructure and services including classrooms, health, water and other facilities. In addition, the influx of refugee seekers has increased pressure on resources in hosting communities and households including food, water and others.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

this activity is an emergency response to the escalated conflict in Sudan which has seen hundreds of school children displaced. WFP is working towards integrating climate smart technologies in its broader school meals programme where efficient cooking energy and meal preparation techniques to reduce on firewood consumption, time of cooking while improving children's diets. Fuel efficient stoves are being evaluated for possible use. However, as this is an emergency response, these best practices will be applied as and when feasible.In addition, WFP will pursue use of High-Energy Biscuits to the extent possible, taking into account lead time in procurement.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In alignment with the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy, WFP remains committed to ensure that the assistance does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the people it serves. In the design phase, WFP, in collaboration with other education stakeholders, will conducts consultations on community needs and inform beneficiaries, school children and other relevant individuals of their entitlements (e.g. what to be served at what time) to ensure that everyone is aware of their entitlement. In the implementation phase, monitoring of project sites will be a key component, to the extent possible ensuring that any potential protection issues emerging are addressed promptly without affecting children's entitlement. WFP has positioned its gender and protection staff in Port Sudan with a Hub in Nairobi to provide ongoing project support.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

WFP is part of the Sector Advisory Group and Education Clusters at Area Office levels. Utilising these forums and other stakeholder engagement processes, WFP will explain project objectives, targets and modus operandi. Quarterly updates will be provided to the stakeholders through established groups as well as ad hoc one to ensure that issues emerging from communities, schools and being experienced elsewhere that may hurt the project integrity are addressed. The project will also work closely with other projects focusing on child protection to ensure that the rights of children are preserved and protected. In addition, inter-agency collaboration in Community Feedback Mechanism has been established with other UN agencies including UNHCR, which enables to ensure wider coverage and a smooth coordination. Also, WFP has a dedicated PSEA advisor and actively contributes to inter-agency fora such as PSEA network at national and state levels. Regular participation in these forums enhanced collaboration and coordination to strengthen WFP’s approach to PSEA throughout the project.
