Project Summary

South Sudan underwent series of humans suffering before and after the attainment of Independence all brought by civil wars, communal conflicts and natural calamities such as flooding and COVID-19 pandemic and these hampers people's sources of livelihood and deteriorate the economy. In 2022, Humanitarian community in South Sudan estimated that two third (2/3) of South Sudan population which is approximately 8.9 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. This includes the 2 million in which 55% of them are women and girls with an additional 2.3 million residing in the neighboring countries as refugees. All these negative factors will only aggravate humanitarian conditions in South Sudan. The lands for farming will be limited by flooding and drought and this increases the risk of vulnerability rates and increase in the number of IDPs in the Country. Yirol East recently hosted a huge number of IDPs with 9473 HHs registered and verified by WFP through according to Yirol East Relief and Rehabilitation Commission office in Yirol East County with another 160 HHs that later arrived after the verification and this will only exacerbate the humanitarian situation Yirol East County as the resources of the host communities are shared and soon will be depleted. In Yirol East County of Lakes State, Help-Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe has been implementing Food security and Livelihood project alongside WASH and nutrition. Major support under this this sector falls under distribution of farm inputs (seeds, tools and implements) and training (Climate Smart Agriculture), conservation agriculture and distribution of fishing gears. Help is scaling up to promote the enterprise farming that will boost other sectors of livelihood such as agribusiness. So far, Help previously supported and trained several groups in Yirol East and West Counties and many of those groups had progressed expectedly. Under the project “Multisector emergency aid for displaced persons and host communities in Eastern Lakes State”, Help is currently implementing a three (3) years project that is targeting 10,000 Households in Lakes and part of Jonglei states to benefit from seeds and tools distribution and agronomic trainings such as kitchen gardening, perm gardening, aerial gardening especially in the flood affected population places. Another 9500 HHs to benefit for unconditional Cash/food voucher where 4000 HHs already distributed food items and other essential things like washing soaps and another 5000 HHs to benefit from fishing gears. The proposed intervention is designed to contribute to Help Interim Country Strategic Plans of 2022-2024. The combination of these interventions will contribute to its work in realizing Sustainable Development Goal 2 to “end hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture” in South Sudan. As food insecurity problems in Lakes State are complex and interrelated, it requires multifaceted and integrated responses both at household and individual levels in addressing immediate and underlying causes of food insecurity and malnutrition to enhance their capacities and reduce vulnerabilities to disaster risks. The supplementary coping mechanism is acquired through consumption of wild fruits and greens. The IDPs and host community households prepare smaller portion of less nourishing food for survival and the number of meals per a day reduces to one. The project will also incorporate GBV, HIV/AIDS, accountability to affected populations, and gender equality during the implementation. In our intervention, Help implements hand in hand with the community stakeholders Chiefs, Women representatives, church leaders, youth representatives, and government representatives (boma and Payams administrators) during the identification of the most vulnerable households and the distribution of humanitarian assistance and agricultural inputs. The targeting criteria include women headed HHs, Child headed HHs, destroyed HHs.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in South Sudan is exacerbated by climate extremes, with environmental impacts such as land degradation and deforestation entailing further consequences for biodiversity. The implications in terms of human and economic costs are significant, with immediate as well as long-lasting effects on livelihoods and food security. Pre-harvest SMART survey in June 2018 in Yirol East and the result showed increased food insecurity at the household level. Help-Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe proposes Multi-Dimensional Nutrition sensitive programming integration of WASH, Nutrition and FSL. South Sudan Combined IPC Results - October 2020 to July 2021 projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity.Food security levels are not expected to rapidly improve in the coming years, as structural constraints will continue to severely affect trade and livestock and farming-based livelihoods. The long-term effects of the conflict, market failures, high food prices, depleted livelihood options and limited humanitarian access pressure household food security. Main shocks and drivers of risk Economic The economy of South Sudan has been adversely affected as well and is now characterized by very low investment, declining revenues (oil production has dropped significantly), a decrease in GDP, disrupted market functionality and limited employment opportunities. Insecurity and conflict The conflict has affected women, children and young people in particular. A lack of strong, effective institutions, the proliferation of arms, politicization of ethnicity, and weak property rights have all contributed to the continuation and deterioration of the situation. Climatic shocks Climate variability is a major characteristic of South Sudan. However, in recent times the country has experienced an increased frequency, intensity, unpredictability and magnitude of climate and weather extremes such as drought, heavy rainfall and flooding. Besides their direct impact on agricultural production, these also affect the spread of crop pests and animal diseases. Animal diseases and crop pests and diseases in South Sudan have had a greater impact due to limited vaccination coverage, knowledge or early warning systems on anticipated threats. Degradation of natural resources The crisis has had a heavy impact on the country’s natural resources: changing livestock migration patterns have led to fierce competition over land, grazing and water sources, not only between pastoralists but also among farmers and herders. This has resulted in further degradation and taken a toll on biodiversity. Help approach will be geared towards:- Change in production compared to previous year’s harvest by commodity- Change in ability to meet survival and livelihoods protection thresholds- Number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers as % of planned- Quantity of food/value of cash/voucher received by beneficiary HH (and proportion in relation to food basket)- Frequency of food/cash assistance to beneficiary HH (months)- Total value of cash and vouchers for livelihood recovery distributed to targeted beneficiaries as % of planned- Number of assets built, restored or maintained to targeted beneficiaries,

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The following are some points that can be used to support the choice for CTP. Remember though that all of the benefits listed below hinge on the contextual appropriateness for a cash-based intervention. - Flexibility and choice: One size does not fit all! Using cash or vouchers recognizes that beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their households need, and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities. - Dignity: In allowing beneficiaries to control what goods and services their households need, cash transfers are considered by many as more dignified than receiving goods in kind, and recognize beneficiaries as active participants in providing for the welfare of their families after a disaster. - Empowerment: The availability of cash gives households a sense of restored power over their immediate situation. In addition, there is evidence that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household. Access to financial structures such as bank accounts may empower beneficiaries, in particular women, in the longer-term as well. - Supports or revitalizes local trade: Injections of cash or the use of commodity vouchers with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation can potentially also provide a link between relief and longer-term economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savings. - Reduces negative coping strategies: In the modern world, most households depend on cash to some extent. When aid is given in kind, households are often forced to seek cash in ways that may be detrimental to longer-term livelihoods, for example selling productive assets, or giving children to the care of relatives. Evidence shows that cash transfers reduce the negative coping strategies used by beneficiaries. - Multiplier effects: By stimulating local markets, cash based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend.- Time and cost efficient for agencies and participants: When goods are available and accessible locally, cash or voucher-based interventions have the potential to be more rapid than bringing supply from out of country. When beneficiaries use local markets, agencies also eliminate logistical and import costs. However, the additional staff, security and logistical resources sometimes required to distribute cash or manage a voucher programme must also be factored in. • Explain how the project design considered market functionality Help-Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe: South Sudan Program typically, this can be achieved by carrying out a simple, rapidmarket assessment. The process can be as basic as a two-hour conversation with vendors at the localmarket: Are shops open? Are they stocked with items? How easy has it been for vendors to resupply?Are the roads to markets accessible and safe? • A functioning market for essential goods and services existed prior to the emergency. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

To ensure confidentiality of any critical or sensitive protection issues, including all GBV and CP cases reported, Help – Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe ensures all project staff are trained in Protection, CP and GBV guiding principles with emphasis on confidentiality when reporting and responding to issues that arise. This will be extended to partner agencies, as well as community-based actors and local authorities. Confidential spaces are accorded in each project location to facilitate confidential discussion with the survivor or party involved. Help – Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe will collect information utilizing coding and non-identifying data, store hardcopy information in a locked, secure cabinet, and store softcopy data using password protection. Information will be shared only according to CP and GBV information sharing protocols for South Sudan. Response to critical or sensitive protection issues will be conducted by qualified Protection and / or social worker staff. Note, “Help – Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe” has also a guiding document “Whistleblowing policy” that gives right to report about malpractice or misconduct or an imminent threat within the organization. The beneficiaries will also be informed that “Help – Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe” has validated the 6 core principles of the Inter‐Agency Standing Committee on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. 1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers constitute acts of gross misconduct and are therefore any staff involve is grounds for termination of employment. 2. Sexual activity with children (persons under the age of 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief regarding the age of a child is not a defense. 3. Exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior is prohibited. This includes exchange of assistance that is due to beneficiaries. 4. Sexual relationships between humanitarian workers and beneficiaries are strongly discouraged since they are based on inherently unequal power dynamics. Such relationships undermine the credibility and integrity of humanitarian aid work. 5. Where a humanitarian worker develops concerns or suspicions regarding sexual abuse or exploitation by a fellow worker, whether in the same agency or not, he or she must report such concerns via established agency reporting mechanisms. 6. Humanitarian workers are obliged to create and maintain an environment which prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and promotes the implementation of their code of conduct. Managers at all levels have particular responsibilities to support and develop systems which maintain this environment. CoC: SEA Policy Final Version January 2017

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will ensure protection mainstreaming through the implementation of its activities across all sectors. Special attention will be provided on the identification and referral of the most vulnerable and at risk. Some parts of the population (Persons with disabilities, older persons, women and children) face additional challenges to access humanitarian support and are “invisible”, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE teams will be trained in order to identify and prevent potential risks while responding to the specific needs of the most vulnerable. All HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE staff in South Sudan are required to sign the code of conduct (See Annex E) and its additional policies pertaining to 1) Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), 2) Prevention of Fraud and Corruption and 3) Child Protection. In addition, briefings are provided ensuring a good understanding of core humanitarian values and beneficiary protection. As a whole, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE has made a particular effort to ensure a balanced recruitment process in terms of gender and ethnicity, particularly for health and protection teams in order to ensure accessibility of services. o Furthermore, all members of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will received protection mainstreaming training including a strong component of Psychological First Aid (PFA) allowing them to provide good listening, identify potential risks and either internally or externally refer. HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE is aware of the importance of including gender equality to increase the efficiency of the humanitarian assistance and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. In NE South Sudan, Displaced Women and Girls have been particularly exposed to violence, for this a particular referral pathway ensuring confidentiality and protection of the beneficiary will be put in place and explain to relevant staff. o With strong protection and outreach community component, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will foster the protection environment by enhancing awareness, information and identifying individuals at risks requiring an immediate response. Protection Outreach teams will be specifically trained to assess basic and specific needs (with a health approach) and to analyze, with a holistic vision, the vulnerability of the individual or Household and to refer to the relevant HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE department in order to receive an adequate response. For instance, in link with the food security department of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE (financed by USAID-FFP), particularly vulnerable households identified as facing protection risks because of the lack of sufficient food will be referred to FSL teams for a sectorial assessment to establish the need and the response o In parallel, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE is ensuring that complaint mechanisms are in place. In Yirol East a hotline has been available for beneficiaries since the beginning of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFEs intervention. Through this project, teams will enhance the diffusion of the hotline number to ensure beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries have access to it. In Yirol East, with low telephone coverage, a complaint box will be available for beneficiaries to communicate with HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE and receive treatment to their complaints.
