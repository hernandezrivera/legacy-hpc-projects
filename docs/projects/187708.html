Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the overall efforts in Idleb and Aleppo governorates to reduce mortality
and morbidity rates and increase the resilience of the target communities by maintaining the ongoing health services within the targeted locations and facilities in order not to overburden the existing health system in the area in case of closing any of these facilities.This project aims to continue supporting two secondary health care facilities and specialized PT center in addition to 2 thalassemia clinics and related blood banks as well as 20 PHCs (level 2) and 2 MCs for 12 months by assuming the running costs, staff salaries and providing medicines and consumables. The PHCs and MCs:
Provide services including general medicine, Pediatric, Pre- and post-natal examinations(ANC, PNC) in addition to the general SRH counseling, further services include management of NCD and communicable diseases, mental health and PSS in addition to community health and integral services including malnutrition screening, management and referral.

Hope hospital will provide specialized pediatric services including outpatient consultations, inpatient and neonatal care.
The blood banks and thalassemia clinics will provide the diagnosis and management of thalassemia patients in addition to the collection and distribution of blood components covering several health facilities in the targeted areas.

4 - Needs

According to OCHA, situation in NWS remains grim, with 3.4 million persons in need of humanitarian aid. Out of the total 4.4 million persons in NWS, over 70% are considered people in acute need of life-saving assistance. With health facilities already stretched thin given the high level of needs in NWS, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic has added further strain to an already fragile healthcare system. By the end of 2021, over 4 million people were in need of basic and life-saving medical services. Lack of fuel safe drinking water low level of public health awareness, and suspension of many health facilities led to increased morbidity and mortality rates, consequently further jeopardizing the resilience of affected people in NWS, especially vulnerable people. Provision of basic health services with focus on vulnerability of host and IDP populations, is high importance in the winter season, particularly the most vulnerable people such as children, elderly and people suffering from chronic diseases, including asthma and other upper respiratory infections to avoid the harsh winter weather has a serious negative impact on the health of over 4 million vulnerable people. Equitable access to health care services, including medical consultations, medicine, referral services, and reproductive and child health services is essential to avoid an increase in morbidity rates, especially during winter months.there is a significant gap in secondary healthcare services in northern Aleppo.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. To provide IDPs and host communities with high-quality and free primary health services in 20 PHCs and 2 MCs by covering the running costs and providing medicines and consumables. 2. Introduction of a quality management framework, training and coaching (on the job) of health personnel based on current needs. 3. Collection of health and epidemiological data for the Early Warning and Response Network (EWARN) and activation of emergency plans for epidemics. 4. Provide essential secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral. 5. provide antenatal care. 6. Provide specialized health care services (blood bank and thalassemia services) in Azaz and AlAtareb sub-districts. 7. Run one ICU department in M. Wassim Maaz Hospital in A'zazdistrict by covering the staff salaries, medication, consumables, and running costs needed to operate. 8. Cover the salaries of medical staff and running costs of the internal department in M.W.Maazhospital. 9. Support the staff salaries and running cost of 1Physiotherapy center in A'zaz district. 10. support Pediatric department in Hope hospital(Ghandura hospital). The MEAL team will conduct frequent field visits to collect data and monitor the activities implementation using checklists, observation, and interviews with beneficiaries and staff and share notes with project management for corrective action and follow-up.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Raising awareness through messages about PSEA topics in addition to having a complaint mechanism in place and sensitizing beneficiaries on how to use it. Also, service providers will be periodically trained on such topics.Risk 2: Risk: safe closure of facilities and protecting data. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Electronic data and information are properly protected and backed up according to IDA SOPs - IDA has develope its own exit strategy guidance to exit in emergencies.Risk 3: Spread of COVID-19 in the project facilities.(Likelihood: high, impact: high)Mitigation: COVID-19 mitigation procedure will be in place during the project and will include social distance between beneficiaries and the regulation of the patients flow in a way that keeps all of them safe where the crowding will not be allowed at all at the entry of the facility or within the waitin hall or at the clinics. The patient's companions will not be allowed to enter and at least there will be10 minutes between each patient and another when they are registered b the data entry. In addition to triage and isolation tents at the entry of each facility, where all beneficiaries will be examined and measured temperature before entering the facilitie. 11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The ever-changing security situation may badly affect the response and result in a mass influx of new IDPs including children and children with disabilities. (likelihood: weak, impact:high) Mitigation: Safety and security measures and emergency plans will be set and considered before and during the implementation period. Project activities will be designed flexibly to be modified appropriately with the changeable security situation.Implementation sites could be located in safer places as needed in coordination with the Health Cluster. Risk 2: the UN cross-border resolution is not extended (Likelihood: mid, impact: high) Mitigation: Intensive advocacy, in addition to coordination with the international NGOs to secure medicine and medical consumables. Risk 3: The local authorities decide to prevent IDA from implementing the project or part of it. (likelihood:weak, impact: mid)Mitigation: Intensive advocacy, negotiation, and coordination with local councils, stakeholders. Risk 4: The lack of well-qualified specialties in the targeted location (likelihood: mid, impact: high) Mitigation: IDA will communicate with the health directorates to ensure that the required specialties are recruited. Risk 5: Corruption and abuse use of cash (Likelihood: mid, impact: high) Mitigation: IDA will assure that all accounting and financial management practices are in line with IDA FinancialInstructions.
