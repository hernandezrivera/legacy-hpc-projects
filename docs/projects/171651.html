Project Summary

The triple threat of floods, cyclic droughts and the effects of the current COVID-19 pandemic compounded by the protracted crisis in Somalia have led to massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene
The proposed action aims to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality attributable to the consumption and use of unsafe water, the lack of or inadequate sanitation facilities during emergency situations and inadequate hygiene practices.
Medair proposes to implement an integrated WASH response in partnership with four local partner organizations namely; DAWA, SAACID, Somali Aid and ZAMZAM and the WASH interventions will be in Mogadishu- Karaan, Hawl Wadag and Daynille districts, in Shabelle region- Cadale and Balcad districts, in South West State- Afgooye District, and in Jubaland State- Kismaayo and Badhadhe districts targeting 20,000 beneficiaries.
The proposed project will provide water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services to internally displaced people (IDPs) as well as host communities in the target districts and will be integrated with health and nutrition. UN OCHA estimates that close to 4.6 million Somalis are in need due to insufficient access to potable water and inadequate sanitation services. Lack of adequate WASH services t is a main cause of the spread of Acute Diarrhoeal Diseases (ADD), leading to malnutrition and contributing to the existing stunting rate of 25.3% among children under 5 years and ultimately to poor health status among the affected communities.
In February 2020, Medair conducted a WASH Gaps assessment which highlighted lack of access to adequate and safe water in almost all Somalia project areas.3 In July 2020, the WASH Cluster reported significant WASH gaps throughout Somalia and especially in Lower Juba and Shabelle Regions. The needs included provision of emergency safe water supply, sanitation services, hygiene kits and hygiene promotion.
Lack of clean water, poor hygiene and environmental sanitation contribute significantly to high rates of illnesses especially, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and cholera. The IDP population lacks access to basic services, especially clean water; in many areas this has heightened the risk of outbreaks of water-borne diseases
The WASH priority for Medair remains to provide lifesaving assistance and improve the well-being of people with a view to preventing excess morbidity and mortality focussing on children, women and internally displaced groups.
Medair proposes to provide safe p water to the affected populations (e.g. IDPs) within international standards such as SPHERE. Where national guidelines exceed international standards the national standard will be targeted. Sanitation facilities will be provided in order to minimize the risk of the spread of acute diarrhoeal diseases. Hygiene promotion will be conducted in order to enhance practices that promote good health. Hygiene promotion is a key component of Medair interventions and currently supports over 1,300 community volunteers who are trained on behavior change communication (BCC) approaches using the Care Group model approach. Information is first collected from the community about cultural ideas, attitudes and practices around hygiene to identify the behavioral aspects that impede carrying out required healthy practices. Each Care Group volunteer is assigned 15 immediate neighborhood houses where (s)he will be visiting regularly promoting appropriate hygiene practices in order to secure positive behaviour change.The Care Group volunteers are capacity-built with training focused on the importance of hand washing with soap or ash during the five key moments or critical times. The trainings involve examples of the hygiene risks and how behaviour change can prevent sickness.
Medair has developed accountability mechanisms that reach across all stakeholders, including beneficiaries. Complaints, compliments and other propositions are routinely received, reviewed and addressed

Project Activities

1. Provide safe and sufficient water supply to the affected populations (IDPs and the host community with the inclusion of PwDs) ensuring adherence to international standards including SPHERE2. Establish and train water user committees on management and monitoring of water points 3. Procure and preposition emergency WASH supplies (Aqua tabs, point of use filters, water storage facilities e.g. water filter, hygiene kits…..). (Sourcing from UNICEF and the WASH Cluster in the form of gifts-in-kind (GIK) may be considered as an option).4. Construction of sanitation facilities, e.g. latrines, ensuring gender and disability sensitivity5. Establishment of a rapid response team (RRT) to respond to emergencies (e.g. AWD/ cholera outbreaks)6. Training of the RRT and community health volunteers (CHVs) on emergency response (IDSR) e.g. during AWD/ cholera outbreaks7. Conduct hygiene promotion activities using community health volunteers

Mitigation Measures

1 Risk of women, boys and girls exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) when accessing humanitarian assistance• Medair will provide training to staff on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse/ protection mainstreaming and ensure they sign Medair’s code of conduct on protection againstl sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. Medair has designated a safeguarding officer to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow-up on protection of beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse of. Care Group volunteers and health/nutrition workers will also identify passive cases in the community and ensure concerns are reported along with delivering continued behaviour change communication messages on sexual violence and exploitation. Trauma, therapy and first aid will be provided in collaboration with gender-based violence actors in some catchment area for survivors and workers will be trained on specific referral pathways. Medair recognizes that women and children are among the most vulnerable groups in violence and ensures that they are aware of the stigma and freedom to seek treatment2. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms 3. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls do not participate in consultation or decision making process for the project or are excluded in humanitarian assistanceDuring assessment, Medair will engage with existing structures, village committes and community leaders in identification of persons with disabilities in the catchment areas, Also using the community health volunteers each assigned to 15 households information of persons with disabilities will be obtained. During the project design these persons will be inolved in the design of the WASH facitilies to be provided eg latrines at health facilities, water provided at water kiosks. Also their suggestions/ideas will be sort on distributions of hygine kits, soap. The marginalized groups will be encouraged to part of the village commitees to allow participation and inclusion for for humanitarian assistance . They will be made aware of the available feedback mechanism through which concerns can be raised.3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanismMedair will inform the beneficiaries of the beneficiary mechanism in place, information will be shared to patients seeking services at health facilities, through the network of community volunteers who visit the assigned households on bi-weekly basis. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the established dedicated toll-free hotline to receive feedback and complaints. The hotline is complemented by regular face-to-face meetings and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), suggestion boxes, and help desks at supported health facilities. Medair and implementing staff will be trained on the feedback mechanisms, to respond to critical or sensitive protection issues, and to deal with critical and sensitive protection issues. Beneficiaries will be made aware that sensitive complaints will be investigated while maintaining confidentiality4. WASH facilities (water sources and sanitation facilities) will be constructed ensuring that PwD will be able to access them and use them comfortably e.g ramps will be constructed in order to access toilets and PwD will be consulted and their participation ensured throughout the project implementation cycle (planning, implementation, monitoring & evaluation, and phase-out) through dialogue meetings.5. Gender inclusion will be ensured by making sure that the participation of all; women, girls, boys and men in the project activities is done. Medair will ensure that community meetings, FGDs, and committees will comprise of women, girls, boys and men and interventions with gender lens
