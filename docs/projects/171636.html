Project Summary

In 2021 UNHCR will continue to focus its intervention on providing protection and assistance and working towards the attainment of durable solutions for refugees, asylum-seekers and returnees working in close partnership with the Government, international and national actors. In addition to 28,002 refugees and asylum seekers and 109,989 refugee returnees (of these 18,050 are expected to be assisted with return to Somalia in the course of 2021) that are estimated to be in need of variety of protection, assistance and solutions support in 2021, UNHCR will also focus its efforts on working closely with the Government of Somalia for identification and protection of stateless people, as well as prevention and reduction of statelessness. The study on statelessness is yet to be conducted, thus the scope of the statelessness is not yet known for Somalia. UNHCR will continue to provide technical support to the Government of Somalia in that endeavor.

Project Activities

All activities under the Refugee Response Plan will contribute to the HRP Strategic Objectives. The Response activities will promote the full enjoyment of rights, aligning with the third Strategic Objective. Multi-sector assistance and essential services will be provided to refugees and asylum seekers with particular attention to the most vulnerable individuals, and resilience and self-reliance will be strengthened among refugees and host communities. This will help the targeted population to recover from the crisis and support their coping mechanisms and capacities, ensure that they have safe, equitable and dignified access to essential services as outlined in the second Strategic Objective. Provision of access to specialized age, gender and disability sensitive services to the most vulnerable will contribute to the first Strategic Objective. Protection will be put at the center of the response. Pre-existing protection risks were further exacerbated by COVID-19, therefore intervention will be tailored to address and mitigate those risks. UNHCR will seek to strengthen the capacities of national institutions by supporting the government in creating conducive conditions for sustainable solutions in line with applicable international, regional and national frameworks, including Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF), as well as Global Compact on Refugees (GCR). Collaboration with humanitarian and development actors will be a key. The whole of society and area-based support to refugees, asylum seekers and host communities will be provided in the multi-sectoral approach. Based on the projection of number of refugees and asylum seekers, as well as refugee returnees in need of variety of protection, assistance and solutions services, the response will target the entire refugee, asylum seekers and returnee population in 2021. In addition to provision of life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection, the response will cover the areas of livelihoods, reception, registration, education, refugee status determination and documentation, access to justice, health, as well as solutions. UNHCR will provide multipurpose cash grants to returnees to re-establish their livelihoods and accelerate their re-integration.Areas-based projects (covering different sectors, including, but not limited to education, WASH and health as well as livelihood) will be pursued for targeting the high return locations, in addition to the individual and family level support of basic needs facilitated through cash-based interventions for refugee returnees.In 2021 UNHCR will continue to provide technical support to the Government of Somalia in building its national asylum system in line with key international instruments that establish legal framework for protection, assistance and solutions to refugees, namely the 1951 Refugee Convention and the 1967 Protocol, as well as the 1969 Convention Governing the Specific Aspects of Refugee Problems in Africa. With regards to statelessness, as per Agency's mandate, UNHCR intervention will focus on identification, prevention and reduction of statelessness and protection of stateless persons. In 2021 UNHCR will continue to provide technical support to the Government of Somalia in meeting its pledges made during the High Level Segment on Statelessness in October 2019, namely (i) to accede to the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and to the 1961 Convention Relating to the Reduction of Statelessness; (ii) to conduct a study to better understand the situation of stateless groups and those at risk of statelessness, including an analysis of domestic laws. Statelessness study, once finalized, will provide a comprehensive picture on the scope of stateless population in Somalia, as well as their needs.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

UNHCR uses cash-based interventions to provide protection, assistance and services to the most vulnerable. UNHCR has strong experience and existing mechanism, operational standard operating procedures and strategies in planning, designing, implementing and monitoring cash-based interventions. Cash-based interventions as modality of protection delivery provides dignity, flexibility and choice for persons of concern, as they allow people to decide and address their own needs. Cash-based interventions help to increase protection outcomes by enhancing protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safe and dignified delivery and contributing to protection benefits, including in such areas as child protection and SGBV and reducing negative coping mechanisms by meeting basic needs. Moreover, cash-based interventions improve cost efficiency in programming. Particularly in the current situation of COVID-19 pandemic, cash-based interventions enable and enhance the efficiency of program delivery. In addition to this, cash-based interventions have multiplier effects, directly benefiting the local economy as affected populations purchase goods and services in the local market but also promote peaceful coexistence and social cohesion with host communities.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

UNHCR Somalia has framework agreement with Standard Chartered Bank (SCB) since 2014 to implement cash-based interventions (CBIs), which is executed through Al Amal Bank inside Somalia. The project will be implemented through this agreement and UNHCR’s CBI SOPs. As a member the cash working group (CWG) UNHCR uses the cash and market analysis by the CWG to design and implement CBIs. The cash market analysis consists of food pricing and supply by Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and World Food Programme (WFP), which is issued on quarterly to inform CBI programming in Somalia. Through this assessments, minimum expenditure basket (MEB) and Transfer Values are established which will inform this project.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Impact: beneficiaries will be exposed to protection risks and will fail to benefit from protection and assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse is mainstreamed across the whole project cycles and that partners and community leadership structures are also aware of their obligation in this regard. Communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanism to ensure high standards of accountability are maintained and to meet the policy of zero tolerance.Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Impact: those in need might not receive assistance and protection. Mitigation measure: UNHCR will employ vulnerability-targeting criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries. All interventions will be guided by age, gender and diversity mainstreaming approach that ensures that all groups in the community are consulted and their capacities and needs are identified. UNHCR will exercise a zero-tolerance policy to fraud with the framework of the project and across all stakeholders of the project. Existing feedback and complaints mechanisms will be strengthened and communities will be made fully aware of these mechanisms. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. Impact: Lack of existing and accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms may result in beneficiaries not able to report diversion of assistance/fraud and beneficiaries not able to access assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will strengthen and establish multiple complaint and feedback mechanisms including, a hot line or toll-free phone number, suggestion boxes, community structures that allows for community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms, designated emails, UNHCR field and partner staff. In addition to this, regular beneficiary contact monitoring through post distribution monitoring through third partner will further strengthen the complaint and feedback mechanism process. UNHCR will distribute IEC materials with information on existing complaint and feedback mechanisms. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Impact: competition over scarce resources and conflict will negatively affect inter-communal dynamics and may lead to deterioration of protection environment for persons of concern. Mitigation: multi-sectoral projects that promote peaceful co-existence and also benefit vulnerable host communities’ members will be pursued.

Please provide further explanation

In addition to the challenges related to the safety and security and the overall development of the country, there are specific issues which represent relevant and immediate obstacles to ensure the durable solutions for refugees and asylum seekers and reintegration of refugee returnees. These remain a challenge due to several factors, including land, access to basic services and lack of livelihood opportunities. The 2021 priority is to include refugees asylum seekers and refugee returnees in the Somalia National Development Plan (NDP9) and to ensure they have safe, dignified and meaningful access to life-saving assistance in line with the CRRF’s ‘whole-of-society’ approach. The development of the National Strategy on Durable Solutions will be supported, among others through national capacity building, with the aim of mainstreaming protection and solutions as well as galvanizing joined-up solutions programming that enhances the humanitarian-development-peacebuilding nexus. Skills training, entrepreneurship, financial inclusion and other livelihood initiatives will be promoted. Best-practice multi-partner programmes such as the UNHCR-led REINTEG 3 will be up scaled; it incorporates livelihoods, access-to-services, HLP, governance and GBV components. Age, Gender and Diversity considerations will be streamlined in all programmes, from data gathering and analysis, to programme design and implementation. Access to services and systems will be pursued with an inclusivity lens that aims to put the population group at par with the local community. UNHCR will seek to include returnees in programmes and strategies targeting IDPs and host communities.
