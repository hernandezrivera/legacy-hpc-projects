Project Summary

The IRC will provide lifesaving treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) to vulnerable children under 5 years in host communities in Gedaref and Khartoum states, contributing to reduced child morbidity and mortality. The project will support existing Ministry of Health (MoH) capacity to deliver essential nutrition service packages through support to outpatient therapeutic centers (OPT), closely integrating nutrition services with other immunization services, growth monitoring and out-patient department (OPD) services at the primary healthcare centers (PHCs). The IRC will build MoH capacity to improve skills in nutrition screening, growth monitoring, SAM and MAM treatment as well as follow up services. In addition, the IRC will provide infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling to pregnant and lactating women (PLW) to reduce incidence of malnutrition in the communities. This will be closely integrated with water and sanitation (WASH) activities to ensure good hygiene and reduction in WASH related diseases.
The IRC will work closely with protection colleagues and local partners including community health volunteers (CHVs) to identify communities and children at risk of malnutrition and scale up screening services. The IRC will ensure COVID-19 mitigation measures are functional across activities to minimize the risk of infection and support service continuity. This will include strict adherence to infection prevention and control (IPC) measures at facilities and adapting community-based programs to incorporate mitigation measures such as no touch techniques, face masks, hand hygiene, and physical distancing.

1 - Needs

The surge of refugees from neighboring countries into Sudan has worsened the already precarious humanitarian situation in the country, putting additional pressure on the limited resources for social services already strained by a worsening economic situation and the COVID-19 pandemic. Sudan ranks 170 out of 189 countries on the Human Development Index (HDI) and according to the draft OCHA 2022 HNO, there are 9.1 million people in need (PIN) of health services with 15% children, 65% women, and 15% people with disability (PWD). For nutrition, the 2021 HNO indicates 3.9 PIN with the nutrition sector targeting 1.8M in critical need of essential nutrition services. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for Sudan is projected to remain high in 2022 at 13.6%, with 64 localities classified by WHO as very high malnutrition prevalence rate of 15% and above, and 9 localities falling into catastrophic levels of GAM 30% and above. This is against the backdrop of a fragile health system severely strained by economic decline, displacements, flooding, and multiple disease outbreaks including COVID-19 and hepatitis E outbreak. In terms of economic wellbeing, inflation rose to 254% by November 2020 and is expected to remain in triple digits into 2022, eroding household purchasing power and pushing prices for food, fuel, healthcare, and other basic needs. Consequently, communities have experienced increased food insecurity and feeding, and child caring practices have been negatively affected. Deteriorating WASH services further compound the nutrition outlook with increased incidences of WASH related diseases that directly contribute to acute malnutrition. Nutrition sector analysis revealed a very high prevalence with over 36% stunting and micronutrient malnutrition affecting over 48% of under-five suffering from anemia.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under this action, the IRC will support one OTP site at Mayo clinic in Jebel Aulia locality in Khartoum state and a second one in Almafaza locality in Gedaref state. Currently there are 10 OTP sites in Jebel Aulia locality and five OTP sites in Al Mafaza locality, therefore the IRC support to two OTP sites accounts for 13% of total OTPs in the two localities. The IRC intends to expand to additional three OTP sites in Blue Nile state in 2022. In the two newly established OTP sites in Jebel Aulia and Al Mafaza localities, the IRC will treat a total of 310 children under five years with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 2,100 children for moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). SAM cases with complication will be promptly referred to nearby stabilization centres. The key project activities are outlined below :• Outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition without complications for children 6-59 months • Management of moderate acute malnutrition for children 6-59 months • Provide vitamin A supplements • Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) counselling • Training to health facility staff and CHVs• Provide basic IPC supplies and training to ensure continuity of essential PHC services• Basic maintenance of health facility infrastructure• Conduct supportive supervision and mentorship to health facility/community health staff• Referral to stabilization centers

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The IRC will ensure safe disposal of non-medical wastes like plastic package, papers, and medical wastes like soiled swabs and organic materials using the colored bin systems to ensure proper waste segregation before final disposal and sharp containers for disposal of all needles, sutures, and blades. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, the IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. The IRC health team works closely with protection colleagues to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities and referral pathways for services. The action will ensure availability of essential nutrition services through training of health facility staff and preposition of nutrition supplies. Providers will be trained on ensuring privacy and confidentiality including in the health information system (HIS).

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The health team will conduct regular client interviews to collect feedback from caretakers on their experience during the visit to the health service point. This will cover waiting time, quality of services, medications received, their perception on how well they were treated, and what can be improved at the facility, among others. The IRC also generates additional feedback through the protection work in the community with the feedback shared with the health team for action. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC Protection team will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). Overall, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received taking into account, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC will also participate in the UN-led PSEA network, ensuring communities and staff understand PSEA and how to access reporting channels. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
