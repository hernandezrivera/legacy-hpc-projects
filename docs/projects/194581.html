Project Summary

In contribution to the provision of integrated emergency lifesaving health services will target a total of 20,091 vulnerable individuals in Pariang and Duk counties of Unity and Jonglei state respectively. The project will reach 11,964 (7,146 boy, and 4,818 girls) children, 5131 Women, 946 Elderly and 2,050 Men through static sites and mobile strategies during 2023.
 Hold the child will utilize $ 400,000; target to reach at least 30% of the health cluster target in the two counties of Duk and Pariang in 2023 Project cycle. Where it will: (a) Solidifying the local gains for COIVD-19 prevention, the project will deploy integrated approaches for the provision of essential lifesaving health services working through PHCC Primary health care Units, PHCU and Mobile clinics to widen the coverage of essential care, strengthen referral pathways on Mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors, and maternal and child health; (b) Strengthen early detection and timely response to local threats and outbreaks by making use of the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS); and work in collaboration with county health mangers, WASH partners, Protection partners among other to leverage local referrals systems and the best use of the limited resources at hand.

1 - Needs

In the face of a protracted national crisis, the survival and welfare of children continue to bear some of the worst indicators i.e. Under-five, infant mortality, and neonatal mortality rates estimated to be 98, 63 and 40 per 1,000 live births, respectively, reflecting a heavy disease burden for South Sudanese children (UNIGME, 2021). Malaria continues to be the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 63 percent of all morbidities and 69 percent of all mortalities in 2022 (Health cluster); constrained access to care i.e. skilled worker, supplies, equipment and walking distances with weakly developed prevention and first line, risk the vast part of the country to infectious disease outbreaks like COVID-19 and EVD also drive humanitarian needs. Pariang and Duk Counties are some flood planes of the largely underserved Unity and Jonglei states, where majority of the Payams continue to nurse the impacts of the historically unprecedented flood levels since 2020; in the backdrop of under developed infrastructures and insecurity trends, communities are characterized with low routine immunization coverage and “Zero Dose” cohorts and susceptible to outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases (VPDs). Characterized with recurrent outbreaks, and/or upsurge of epidemic prone diseases including acute watery diarrhoea/cholera (e.g. the 2022 cholera outbreath in Rubona next door to Pariang), measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers and children under 5 years of age. The HNO inter-sector severity map ranks Pariang and Duk counties among the extremely vulnerable counties, an opinion which concurs with the documented levels of food insecurity, and limited access to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) infrastructure which are known to feature poor homecare for children, low immunity and ultimately poor health indicators. In the face of reduced health service coverage under the rationalised World Bank targeting of health facilities and BHI roll-outs in the counties; several bomas/payams are left without services and deliveries are at the hands of unskilled attendants; insecurities in Duk, and floods and associated displacements in both Pariang and Duk, the HNO ranking too signal likely trends of further deterioration of health indicators should there be no additional interventions

2 - Project activities

1. Conduct disease surveillance in 2 Counties. Hold the Child will support WeeklyReporting through IDSR for all the supported health facilities and EWARS for IDP Sites. 2. Establish 2 emergency team that will ensure timely Preparedness and Response to any outbreaks within the 2 Counties for Potential diseases of importance like Malaria, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis and Hepatitis E among others. 3. Capacity building for health care workers at health facilities in integrated emergency preparedness and response. 4. Integration of WASH at all health care facilities will be central to the implementation of this project. WASH will be integrated at 6 PHCUs, 4 Mobile Clinics across the 2 counties.(5). Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care in the selected counties and report any vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicines and supplies. (6). Support 8 health care facilities to provided integrated and quality static health care services, 6 PHCUs and 4 Mobile Clinics across the selected Counties. (7). Conduct curative consultations at 10 health facilities to reach estimated 27,551 beneficiaries. Collected data shall target Gender, age and Disability aspects disaggregated by gender where applicable to provide tailored intervention. (8). Training of health care workers in diagnosis and case management of malaria and safe motherhood. Hold the Child will aim to train 52 health care workers in total, 6 clinicians and 20 nurses in malaria and 20 midwives in safe motherhood to ensure skilled deliveries, 6 Data Clerks. (9). Establish and provide SGBV and MHPSS services at 10 health facilities, Provide rehabilitation services to disabled and Psycho-social support services to those with mental illness at 10 supported health care facilities. (10). ProvidedMHPSS services at 6 health facilities to reach 862 beneficiaries of SGBV as well as MHPSS services.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Hold the Child has a developed culture of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own the project. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Community elders, Women, Girls, Youth groups, religious leaders, and people with disability. Hold the Child will engage communities using appropriate language that the community members understand best to express their concerns, views and feedback (eg smiley rating feedback cards). These feedback will form part of the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Hold the Child Organization, will organize Joint Monitoring sessions with stakeholders at all levels, though Focused Group discussions the project will register community complaints, positive and negative feedbacks, and provide corrective measures and way forward/recommendations. Through local events ie the inception meetings and awareness activities, local leaders will be informed about the project scope and limitations, as well as their respective inputs and benefits. Other efforts will include static suggestion boxes and service points to observe privacy, and communities (those who are literate) will be encouraged to make use of them.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Sexual exploitation and abuse are critical issues South Sudan is facing. In-line with Hold the Child's adopted policy on PSEA and Child safeguarding, this project will observe specific measures and activities including (a) Mandatory induction (Child safeguarding, and PSEA) training, coupled with regular and refreshers for all staff, associates and volunteers and a code of conduct be signed as a key commitment to this standard; (c) Key messages will be displayed at service points, and health education sessions will embed given components eg reporting pathways; (c) Reporting of PSEA related incidents will be mandatory through the project cycle, using dedicated channels in collaboration with existing protection partners; (d) All documented cases will be duly investigated and culprits treated in accordance with law.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This project is designed observing inclusion of different beneficiaries with every person has equal rights. Measures including inception sessions, regular interactions with key stakeholders e.g. CHD, chiefs and locals, focus group sessions as hinted elsewhere in this plan, will be opportunities to solicit their respective inputs; periodic (quarterly project evaluations and plans will take close look at inputs and integrate positive changes; where actions have impact on costs, Hold the Child will take deliberate discussions with the grant manager to further guide on the most appropriate ways around these integrations.
