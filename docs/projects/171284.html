Project Summary

The continuation of protracted crises in Somalia, now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, Floods and droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. Approximately, 4.6 million Somalis are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities. Lack of access to water and adequate sanitation in schools is also a factor contributing to poor school attendance. In historically drought-affected districts, lack of access to water for humans and for livestock remain one of the leading causes of displacement, conflict and diseases.
According to JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. The population in need of adequate WASH services in 2021 includes approximately 1.16 million IDPs 3.36 million host communities, 53,906 Returnees and 28,002 Refugees.
According to a Joint Rapid Assessment carried out in Galmudug State in May 2020 by the government and humanitarian partners including CPD, five IDP settlements hosting 2,000 households (12,000 people) have partially or completely lost their shelters following a heavy storm in Adado. The affected IDPs settlements include: Badbaado, Kulmiye, Karama, Daryeel and Xeeb. Moreover, 700 vulnerable households (4,200 people) from the host community also lost their shelters and household items. The multi-agency assessment indicates an estimated 68,000 internally displaced people (IDPs) who are in urban and peri urban areas in Galgaduud region have WASH needs. Most of the IDP’s do not have access to safe drinking water with the only option being unprotected berkads, putting women and children at risk as they are mostly the handlers of water in the household. New IDP’s lack sanitation facilities leading to open defecation, compromising the security and privacy of women and girls.
The proposed interventions will include piping of water from the strategic boreholes to IDPs and vulnerable host communities in the rural areas through cash for work for digging trenches and laying pipes. It will also include rehabilitation of 12 water pans through cash for work across the target areas, provision of water bladders in IDP camps, construction of strategic boreholes, shallow wells, gender-segregated latrines for under-served public primary schools and public health facilities and hygiene promotion activities.
Persons with disabilities face additional challenges and barriers. Barriers include environmental barriers, which inhibit access to WASH facilities, as well as social barriers, leading to discrimination within communities and further aggravating the risk of exclusion to WASH services. Water and sanitation facilities such as latrines will be accessible to people with disability and this will be done through design and construction of disability-friendly facilities. Considering the figures above, there are correlations between districts that have a limited access to water and sanitation and account for a high proportion of total incidence of AWD across Somalia.
CPD is targeting 29,580 people and train management committees in order to avoid over reliance on unsustainable means of accessing clean water by using long term high yielding well managed community boreholes. Committees will also be established to maintain clean sanitation facilities, as well as scale up hygiene promotion initiatives to help minimize AWD/cholera outbreaks during the rainy seasons when there is flooding. Management committees will be inclusive by ensuring representation of women, youth and persons living with disabilities. These marginalized groups will also be consulted in all project phases including design and implementation. Covid-19 regulations and guidelines will be adopted in the project implementation.

Project Activities

Emergency Water Services1) Rehabilitation of 4 strategic existing boreholes and 12 strategic shallow wells where the boreholes will be fitted with solar powered systems, caretaker's room, water tank, animal trough, water kiosks and pipes. The shallow wells will be protected. Up to 6km of water piping from strategic boreholes to IDPs and vulnerable host communities will be done through cash for work. 12 water pans will also be rehabilitated using cash for work. It is expected that women, girls, boys and men from 4,930 Households will be involved in the design and implementation of these projects and will have access to water as a result of the rehabilitation of these water sources.2) Establishment of 28 water committee to ensure proper maintenance of water services. The committees will be inclusive of women, youth and people living with disability.3) Provision and distribution of 15 water bladders (each with a capacity of 10 Cum) to increase water access at strategic water points that lack adequate storage facilities. People with disability, women and girls will have easy access to water within reasonable distance.4) Establish emergency WASH systems like water trucking for a period of at least 2 months and carry out bulk chlorination, point of use water treatment and safe storage.5) Carryout monitoring on water quality at community level.6) Repair of 22 Water Treatment stations (small/medium scale) including desalination systems, sedimentation, disinfections. Sanitation services7) Construction/rehabilitation of 500 lockable gender segregated and disability friendly communal and school latrines with hand washing facilities for IDPs and host communities in Galmudug, South West State, Hirshabelle and Banadir regions. This will include construction of 60 gender segregated twin latrines for 15 schools in the target areas. 8) Carry out 12 cleaning campaigns awareness sessions, focusing on solid waste removal and disposal focusing on behavior change and AWD/cholera prevention targeting 2,500 households for a period of 6 months.9) Carryout spraying and support environmental cleaning to control vectors during emergency in 30 target IDP camps and host communities. 10) Desludge 150 pits/septic tanks in every IDP camp in the targeted 30 camps. 11) Liaise with local community leaders, Camp Management committees, people living with disabilities, women and youth to incorporate protection and accessibility measures in use of WASH facilities. Hygiene Promotion Activities12) Carry out 5 days hygiene promotion training incorporating Covid-19 WHO guidelines for 100 hygiene volunteers (at least 40% being women) selected from the IDPs and host communities in the targeted areas. Thereafter, disseminate health and hygiene promotion messages including Covid-19 messaging to the target populations by the 300 trained hygiene volunteers using IEC messages, radio programes, personal hygiene awareness, latrine use and food handling.13) Carry out 12 Hygiene and sanitation awareness raising sessions targeting 4,930 households for a period of 6 months.14) Distribute 3,500 hygiene kits (Each kits comprising of Washing Soaps, Aqua tabs, water collection, storage jerry cans, Menstrual Hygiene management kits for women and girls) to the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities in the targeted areas. CPD will develop a detailed work plan and implement through close monitoring of project activities on regular basis to ensure that all activities are being undertaken in accordance with agreed outputs. CPD will also ensure that beneficiary feedback is taken into account and feeds into the project learning to improve implementation efficiency. High quality photos with GPS and time stamps depicting various activities planned will be provided as documentary evidence of implementation. Monitoring visits to the sites will be organized for stakeholders. Final evaluations will be carried out after every project completion.

Mitigation Measures

Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities The project will Promote equitable and impartial access for all by:• Ensuring that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritising the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups.• Applying a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries.• Ensuring lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services• Ensuring that the location, distance and access routes to the water points, latrines and distribution sites are accessible to all members of the community, including the disabled and most vulnerable groups.• Separating male and female queuing areas and ensure female staff presence at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls.• Making infrastructure adaptions to WASH structures to make them accessible to persons with reduced mobility (eg. persons with physical disability, older persons).• Establishing mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time.• Considering the gender balance in all project phases and in distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community.• Ensuring that beneficiaries know about WASH services, how to obtain hygiene kits, where and when the distributions will take place, what they will get, how long it should last and how to use the items. • Providing information through various communication means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels eg. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms).• Using this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access, discrimination against particular groups or whether assistance is being diverted. Take this into consideration when determining locations for WASH interventions. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. This will be minimized by;• Being transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them.• Setting up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygiene kits, feedback box at WASH facilities, phone lines• Setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff.• Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place.• Organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works.• Considering a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion.• WASH staff and committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should be given protection mainstreaming training. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area• Taking into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. Advocate with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent.• Obtaining permission before using or building on any land or property, in writing, where possible.

Please provide further explanation

CPD will ensure Management committees are inclusive of elders, men, women, youth and people with disabilities and the committees are registered or recognized by the local administrations.. CPD will use technologies and approaches compatible with the needs, knowledge, and circumstances of the target population, fundamentally addressing critical health hazards. All WASH infrastructure will be climate-resilient. The water and sanitation structures will be protected from contamination, and structural damages resulting from floods. Groundwater sources will be done to standards to provide water during the dry seasons. We will ensure that facilities are designed and located so that they are robust to climate uncertainties alongside other pressures on resources, systems, and services. During project implementation, focus group discussions will prioritize the elderly, the disabled, and other minorities to ensure the needs of all vulnerable groups are being addressed during program implementation. Separate toilets and hand washing stations will be provided for men and women. Doors to latrines will be lockable from the inside. The project will increase the protection of women and girls from abuse by ensuring that sanitation facilities will be located in safe and easily accessible places. The project will also ensure that all facilities such as latrines are designed with adequate private space for women and girls to clean and dispose sanitary materials with dignity. For enhanced protection of women and girls, Community Health Workers will be trained to detect, respond to and refer cases of violence and abuse, and exploitation of boys and girls; also, WASH facilities like toilets and water kiosks will be located in safe places, with good visibility.
