Project Summary

Building on Near East Foundation’s (NEF) experience in Sudan since 1981, NEF proposes an integrated approach to address food insecurity and other basic needs including needs for water, sanitation and hygiene, while building local assets for economic resilience and diversification. NEF’s approach will diversify and improve agriculture production for income and food security and develop collaborative livelihoods that can address immediate food need while enhancing long-term resilience. The project will use the participatory approach which encompasses the “the school farmers” technique, which guarantees adequate agricultural production and income (during rainy ) and the development of collaborative microenterprise and complimentary interventions through income generation intervention to strengthen economic resilience, self-sufficiency, and food production systems, a value chain development approach will be implemented to involving collaborative businesses and access to shared assets and resources.
It is important to deliver targeted food assistance to mitigate the impact of the current economic situation which has led to increased food-insecurity situation. Food assistance for agriculture production, livelihoods strengthening and complementary activities allow the control of the timing of distribution activities, which is critical to ensure that (1) food security and income is possible during rainy season and beyond; (2) diversified livelihoods and value chains that are collaborative are developed that would free up resources for food access and security and minimize conflicts; and (3) food security is achieved in a timely and sustainable way.
NEF planning in 4 years starting from December 2019, a project to improves the sustainable income, leadership and inclusion among 3,028 vulnerable women and 1,010 men (25,876 indirectly) in 18 clusters of villages in North. and South Kordofan, Sudan, engaged in NTFP (None timber Forest Products), such as gum arabic, desert date, christ thorn, tamarind, baobab, honey) and cash crop (hibiscus) value chains, through association building, enhanced human and institutional capacity, and improved market inclusion, collaborative and conflict−sensitive NRM and entrepreneurship, NEF will continue doing so not only in the same localities, but will replicate this in other localities within greater Kordofan and greater Darfur too.

1 - Needs

Agriculture is the main source of livelihoods and food security for most of the population in target communities. However, access to seeds and agricultural tools to restart food production and achieve food security remain the main challenge for smallholder farmers, contributing to limited agricultural productivity, subsistence farming, and high food insecurity. IDPs, returnees and households have a limited capacity to meet minimum food requirements. Prevailing insecurity, displacement, and restricted population movements continue to hamper access to cultivation and seasonal agricultural labor. As a result, “Stressed” (IPC Phase 2) levels of acute food insecurity persist among IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents during the harvest period, with several pockets experiencing “Crisis” (IPC Phase 3) levels; these levels of food insecurity are likely to continue in 2019 (FEWS NET, 04/2019). “Emergency” level (IPC Phase 4) is likely in the IDP-hosting target localities at the August/September peak of the lean season (FEWS NET, 04/2019). Macroeconomic challenges are expected to continue during the outlook period, and staple food prices will likely remain at least 250–300 percent above average. Livestock and labor prices, although increasing, are unlikely to keep pace with staple food price increases and poor households’ purchasing power will remain below average (FEWS NET, 04/2019). In February 2019, NEF conducted needs assessments in CDS and SKS based upon field consultation and desk review of existing assessments and surveys that focused on WASH, agricultural production, and food security, in coordination with the WASH Sector, the FS&L Sector, and relevant line ministries. The assessment identified communities most in need of humanitarian assistance due to the degradation of their hygiene situation, water supply, sanitation, agricultural production, and food security situation. The assessment confirmed that current humanitarian responses are few and fragmented. The assessment team triangulated data with IOM, OCHA, WES, MoA, WFP, FAO, UNICEF, FEWS NET, and other humanitarian assessments. Additionally, lack of basic services and infrastructure, along with the lack of agricultural assets and food production continue to prevent the return of IDPs to their areas of origin and have created an acute emergency for returnees, vulnerable populations, and newly displaced persons (NEF NA, 02/2019).

2 - Activities / Outputs

Working in partnership with community groups and in close cooperation and coordination with aid actors, NEF will (a) increase agricultural production and food security through market-based distribution of seeds and implements, non-formal financing, and targeted training in improved farming techniques and micro-gardening; and (b) institutionalize community-based and inclusive governance management and maintenance of agricultural extension services. The proposed approach does not involve development of new infrastructure , thus enabling the project to achieve rapid results and lay a foundation for decreased dependency on aid. The project will: (1) Support purchase of locally-used seed varieties through agricultural fairs; (2) increase agricultural support for cash crops and off-season production through farmer-to-farmer extension approach; (3) provide essential tools for winter agriculture through vouchers (e.g., digging hoes, spades, axes, rakes, jerry cans); (4) provide farmers access to a range of seeds and agricultural tools based on availability of goods, access to markets, and availability of a safe and affordable transfer mechanism; (5) link primary producers directly to suppliers and support the local private sector to help prevent risks of inflation (e.g., raising prices of scarce items); (6) promote household micro-gardens and farm and off-farm production and sales for food security and income; and (7) ensure quality of inputs, certify and monitor seeds and seed vendors, promote appropriate seed storage, and improve farming techniques through training, coaching, farmer-to-farmer extension approach, and on-farm trial

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

NEF identifies, analyzes, monitors and mitigates risks in a participatory and accountable way across multiple categories (i.e. fiduciary, context, delivery, safeguarding, operational, etc.). For each risk, NEF identifies a risk owner, a description, inherent risks in terms of probability and impact, mitigation approach, and residual risks in terms of probability and impact.NEF facilitates a participatory and collaborative approach that engages local community, local organizational stakeholders as well as line ministries in risk management.NEF’s participatory approach is employed based on the assumption that project components necessitates ownership and leadership by local communities needed in identifying and collaboratively working toward addressing project risks. As per NEF’s experience, the participatory approach supports local communities to develop actions to take to mitigate against identified risks and controls that can be put in place or exercised to manage risks. Moreover, demonstration farms and learning-by-doing opportunities reduce the risk of farmers’ unacceptability of newly introduced agricultural practices. Other mitigation measures include effective extension services through the easy access to local agricultural extension its, including to facilitate an integrated pest management services. NEF and local partner will work with the Ministry of Agriculture to establish community-managed seed nurseries. The nurseries will support extension activities by having demonstrative space where all agricultural trials can be applied and demonstrated to farmers. The nurseries will be managed by local committees, which will be provided with detailed training, ongoing on-the-job support and start up seedlings. The management committee members (made of 15 members) will supply community members, schools and community organizations with a variety of seedlings for a fee. Initially, NEF will procure seedlings for distribution in support of communities hosting IDPs in order to boost the initial startup capital of the nursery site, as well as to rehabilitate the agricultural environment in IDP concentrated areas. Further, seedlings will be provided to female-headed households for use in micro-gardening. NEF has tested this approach locally with a great success.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NEF guiding principles. NEF mainstreams protection through training and enforcement of its Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Through these principles, and its adherence to Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS), NEF resolutely ensures the protection of children and at-risk adults. NEF further ensures the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries through its strict adherence to the principles of Do No Harm (DNH). NEF actively looks for ways to minimize any threats and vulnerabilities of every group of the affected population. Protection is mainstreamed throughout project design and implementation by NEF’s promotion of meaningful, safe, and dignified access. Contractors and project partners need also to adhere to NEF’s guiding principles. NEF applies DNH and Sphere principles to its selection of participants, guaranteeing fair representation, regardless of their ethnicity, religion, or gender. NEF further reflects Sphere’s belief that all individuals have a right to assistance in our commitment to working with marginalized and vulnerable populations, including female head-of-households, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), youth, older people, and persons with disabilities. These considerations guide all of NEF to prevent exposing vulnerable and marginalized people to further harm. Consultation, feedback, and response. Through confidential and secure feedback mechanisms, NEF ensures that all beneficiaries are able to raise concerns and complaints. NEF responds to beneficiary feedback by adopting the DNH principles of constantly learning and improving. In addition to responding to and reducing risk, NEF assists affected populations to claim their rights and access appropriate remedies. As appropriate, NEF will establish community mechanisms (example, WASH committees) that are diverse and representative of the community. Women and girls are at disproportionate risk of GBV and SGBV. NEF strives to mitigate these risks through consultation in project design and implementation. NEF has a formal gender policy as well as a dedicated gender officer and it seeks to mainstream the participation of women in all activities. Throughout the project, NEF will give special attention to gender-based constraints and opportunities to ensure that activities and decision-making processes are accessible to women and girls and that they promote appropriate access to and safe use of NEF services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NEF projects are designed, implemented and monitored with the participation of the beneficiary communities, government line ministries, HAC, donors and local partners. NEF ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle. To help operationalize its commitment to actively seek, listen to, consider, engage, protect and act on feedback from the communities and individuals that are the direct recipients of assistance, NEF maintains an organization-wide Accountability to Affected Populations Framework linked to its organizational policies and practices and is built upon the 5 Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations. NEF expands upon this framework to include principles of protection, do-no-harm, appropriateness, and coordination. Leadership & Governance. NEF works in partnership with local NGOs and CBOs throughout the lifecycle of our projects. Where there is need and demand, NEF devotes resources to capacity building of local entities, aligning projects with relevant policies and priorities. All project staff, regardless of organizational affiliation, will work together as a single team. As the lead, NEF takes full responsibility for project management and implementation, for coordination among partners at an organizational level, for delivering results and for reporting. Transparency. Outreach events include information about the organizational stakeholders, criteria and processes for beneficiary recruitment, participation, and access to project benefits, project approach and objectives, and project sponsors, all in local languages, displayed in public, accessible locations. Community-based project association meetings and trainings will use culturally and linguistically appropriate communications. Feedback and Complaints. Beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be incorporated into program implementation, particularly through monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL) processes. NEF will transparently communicate the purpose of all MEL activities with beneficiaries, and seek their feedback. NEF will consult communities to establish an appropriate feedback and response mechanism. All methods of feedback collection will involve sensitization of staff, partners, volunteers, and beneficiaries on the purpose of methods used and will communicate clear roles, responsibilities, and processes. Feedback will be collected legally, ethically, and with due regard to the welfare of those affected by the results. In addition, the feedback system will uphold confidentiality and non-retaliation principles as per NEF’s Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Participation. To verify needs, gauge acceptance, and ensure the target population understands the project, community consultation and orientation meetings will be held at the beginning of the project.
