Project Summary

The project will target 8,315 IDPs and host community and will focus on the provision of life-saving services to most at risk vulnerable children and their families, as well as community members in the targeted location. UASC, missing and extremely vulnerable children with protection concern will be supported through individual comprehensive and age-specific case management services as well as placement in interim care based on their specific needs. This project will ensure that vulnerable children are provided with direct support which includes NFI, life skills and their foster families will be empowered on IGA so that to provide basic needs of their children, training for foster parents and caregivers will be done prior to placement; and regular case management meetings will be conducted to improve the supervision and coaching of caseworkers.
The project will work toward improvement of psychological wellbeing of the distressed children through regular provision of focused and non-focused PSS activities, the interventions will be through community-based psychosocial support; this includes but not limited to Child-Friendly Spaces, Child rights clubs. Selected children will be engaged in various club activities; help desks will be established in schools to identify and refer children’s at risk to relevant service providers based on the need and consent, children will be informed about the available CP and GBV services through awareness raising. Awareness on child protection risks in the schools and communities will be conducted and regular assessment will be conducted to monitor the protection needs of the affected population and identify trends around protection issues. The community-based mechanism will be established to mitigate and response to GBV risk.
In an effort to ensure effective response with minimal duplication WOCO will coordinate with other Child Protection actors working in Fashoda to ensure Child Protection resources are well utilized. WOCO will primarily focus on returnees from Sudan who have not settled within the IDP population but rather settled amongst the host community. Returnees settled amongst the host community are far more marginalized and lack basic protection services as compared to the IDP’s. WOCO will also focus their activities in locations with limited or no Child Protection services. They will set up CFS and TLS in locations such as Golo and Dethwok where no CP actors ha previously responded.

Needs

The conflict which occurred in February 2017 in the Western bank (Fashoda County) caused numerous population movements and displacement forcing the population from this area to move to safer villages in the host community and further to Sudan to settle as refugees in the camps. The communities while settling in these areas faced several challenges that include lack of inadequate services, family separation, it's estimated that around one million children are believed to be in psychological distress and therefore in need of psychosocial support services. distress among children and women and instances of GBV. Physical and domestic violence among the vulnerable families has increased as well as cases of child neglect, child marriage and child labor has also increased as a result of disruption of their livelihood which was a major source of income. There is an alarming number of children dropped out of schools and currently engaged in different businesses that are exposing them to various form of GBV in the market in Kodok, 44 children (40 girls and 4 boys) were mapped by WOCO in a recent assessment and the driving factors being family failure to provide their basic needs.In addition to the above, the population who are returning back from Sudan and other areas of displacement are exposed to protection risks, which are prevalent in settlement locations. Child protection concerns in Aburoc are mostly driven by the limited livelihood options, food consumption gaps, and lack of well-functioning child protection structure including constrained case-management systems, weak referral mechanisms, and limited capacity of the community-based child protection mechanisms. Parents of school-aged children have been forced to move to distant locations to look for work, which has also resulted in an increased number of neglected children. Child-headed households, where older adolescents are required to take care of smaller children exist. Likewise, some children have lost parents as a result of conflict and have been left to fend for themselves. Consequently, child labor exploitation, neglect, sexual violence, child marriage, and street children in Aweil Center has been increasing at an alarming rate. Exposure to violence, malnutrition and mass displacement can have long-lasting effects on children’s psychosocial well-being and development. There is an unmet need regarding the social and emotional wellbeing of children. There is also a need to address the psychosocial needs of girls who have experienced sexual violence and assault. There is a lack of knowledge among caregivers to support their children.To respond to the emerging protection concerns in Aburoc, WOCO will continue providing CPiE services to affected children and communities through the provision of case management services, including family tracing and reunification services for unaccompanied, separated and missing children, psychosocial support through focus and non-focus intervention and using and strengthening the existing community structures, increase the awareness on Mine risk education through lifesaving messages and ensure that mitigation measures and gender-based services are available.

Activities

To provide crucial services to the most vulnerable and at-risk children in Fashoda, WOCO proposes to improve the social-emotional well-being of children using a center-based, school-based and community-based approaches utilizing UNICEF’s Psychosocial support (PSS) manuals This will be coupled with case management services for vulnerable children including FTR services for UASC. Where needed UASC will benefit from alternative care placements and individualized support. The project shall also build the capacity of community-based child protection structures in the CPiE approach that promotes the participation of different community structures (CP focal points, community gatekeepers, PSS school clubs, religious leaders, teachers, and mother groups) in order to promote longer-term, sustainable change. Regular consultative review meetings between WOCO staff, parents, Child Protection Focal Points, and community leaders will ensure the project responds to the continuously changing needs of the children; analyzing new challenges and designing community-centered solutions. Planned activities will include Training of 35 ( 15 female and 20 male) caseworkers CP officers and volunteers on case management and FTR processes; Provide FTR case management including appropriate alternative care to 100 (60 girls and 40 boys) UASC (Identification ,registration and reunification);20 foster parents and caregivers selected screened and trained on relevant CPiE and alternative care training will be done prior to placement and regular case management meetings will be conducted to improve the supervision and coaching of caseworkers, family tracing and reunification services will be provided to UASC as well as placement in interim care when relevant; regular follow-ups for all active caseload will be done on regular basis to access children’s situation and identify emerging issues.Provide direct support which include NFI, life skills to 500(300 girls and 200 boys) and 60 (40 female and 20 male) foster families will be empowered on IGA so that to provide basic needs of their children, foster parents and caregivers will be selected and grouped in small scale and train on business management, supported and monitored on a regular basis. WOCO will Support 300 (200 girls and 100 boys) extremely vulnerable children with case management services including referral to relevant service providers through individual comprehensive and age-specific case management services based on their specific needs consent.The project will provide structured community based focused and non-focused PSS activities for 3000 children (1450 girls and 1550 boys) ;Establish peer to peer support/club and provide training on CP and child rights for 300 adolescent children (158 girls and 142 boys) this will be through CFS, community-based and school-based PSS both in CFS and in the community, regular provision of focused and non-focused PSS activities to distressed children and children at risk through community-based psycho-social support intervention; this includes but not limited to Child Friendly Spaces, Child clubs, awareness etc. As part of the focused PSS intervention selected children will be engage selected in various club activities. WOCO will conduct awareness on child protection prevention and response to 4000 people through campaigns, sensitization exercises, leaflets and posters which will cover both community and schools including the CFS that will be established in these locations.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

WOCO has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse that ensures our beneficiaries are not taken advantage of in the course of project implementation. Services will not be withheld or provided in exchange for sexual favors. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation and abuse of persons of concern will be reported immediately to the PSEA focal person. Any form of Sexual activity with children is strictly forbidden. WOCO has also set up an effective anonymous and confidential complaint mechanism that is easily accessible to beneficiaries. Our staff are trained and PSEA and we systematically integrate the PSEA topics into information campaigns, training, and meetings with persons of concern. Conduct regular dialogue with the community as part of the monitoring strategy.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

WOCO have well trained staff on the humanitarian principles and at least every Quarter WOCO organizes refresher training for its staff on how to ensure the do no harm principle is upheld, especially people with disabilities within the center and community by respecting them including them in decision making mapping the support structures and giving clear information to the conflict affected communities. WOCO has conducted a protection risk analysis on the elements of child protection interventions included in this HRP such as PSS, Case Management, CBCPM, access, confidentiality, and people with specific needs. Accordingly, the analysis indicated that if the interventions for child protection are not aligned with the guiding humanitarian principles and standards including the code of conduct, the safety and dignity of clients might not be protected. To tackle the anticipated risks the following community based and humanitarian agency mitigation measures are identified through strengthening of the Community Based Child Protection Mechanism and enhancing meaningful participation of the clients in the project cycle management process. Moreover, the project will establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Utilizing a community-based protection approach, WOCO will play a key role in driving the process to examine beneficiaries access to services and program’s impact due to access. Participatory accountability reviews of the project will provide an opportunity for affectedcommunities to examine the program and role of WOCO and provide positive and critical feedback. Beneficiaries play a central role in examining the program’s impact. Programs will be measured against indicators developed from the baseline data on community, attitudes and perceptions of protection problems and strategies collected using participatory methods. These indicators are used to monitor behavioral and attitudinal change throughout the program and evaluate program impact.
