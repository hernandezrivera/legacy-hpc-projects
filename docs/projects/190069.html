Project Summary

The proposed project aims to ensure protection of boys and girls and youth through psychosocial interventions, that include recreational and play activities in Youth and Child Friendly Spaces (Y/CFS, structured parenting without violence consecutive workshops and provision of NFIs and dignity kits for boys, girls and adolescent girls). Save the Children will provide psychosocial services through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) such as Psychological First Aid/PFA and referral for traumatized boys, girls and families who have witnessed or personally experienced physical injuries including death due to conflict and/or to those displaced due to drought. Activities that will raise the awareness of people on the protection of children women and youth will be conducted through workshops, community conversations, and awareness raising campaigns. Functional community-based protection mechanisms will be established and strengthened and the capacity of local government will be enhanced to sustain the program. Through this project, case management support services will be delivered through Woreda Women and Social Affairs offices to benefit individual children affected by violence and abuse as well as COVID19 related/exacerbated child protection risks. Save the Children will also ensure identification and registration of children with different CP concerns (including number, age, and sex, special need/disability) to facilitate referral to other services providers.

1. - Projects activities

Save the Children would like to propose CP project for conflict and/or drought affected communities in Ethiopia. The proposed action is in line with 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan, Ethiopia, Strategic Objective Three. Accordingly, the project will work to ensure that, conflict and drought affected children including adolescent girls and their care givers are protected from violence, exploitation, abuse and harmful practices, through quality and timely response and targeted prevention, and risk mitigation activities. The project will seek to strengthen CP and SGBV Case Management system through capacity building training and regular support for frontline case workers on case management and alternative child care. Technical and in-kind support will be provided for case workers and community volunteers to carry out the protection activities. Also, updating service mapping and existing local multi-sectoral referral pathways for protection services, and creating public awareness on the referral pathways are planned in this project to ensure effective coordination in CP and SGBV service delivery. By doing so, SC will be able to provide timely and appropriate case management services for children who are at risk of or affected by different CP and SGBV due to conflict and/or drought induced displacement. Potential foster care providers who could provide family-based care for children without care providers. With regards to SGBV, we will provide case management services for girls and women who have experienced SGBV through strengthened referral mechanisms. We will map and strengthened CP and GBV service referral mechanisms at local level, including for medical, free legal aid and specialized mental health services (where they exist). We will train frontline staff (including social workers, health professionals, legal professionals) with basic skills to properly identify SGBV risks and cases; to handle disclosures in a compassionate, non-judgmental way; and know to whom they can refer cases for additional care as per standard operating procedures; and on psychological first aid (PFA) services for SGBV survivors. We will work with community based structures and conduct awareness raising campaigns on GBV and PSEA. With regards to MHPSS, Psychosocial Support (PSS) services will be provided for distressed children and their care givers, as well as survivors of SGBV. Dissemination of MHPSS messages for girls and boys, and care givers to support them cope with distress due to displacement and effect of humanitarian crisis are among others considered in this project. Moreover, the project will also strengthen family and care givers environment by conducting sensitization on positive parenting in difficult circumstances particularly in the context conflict and other natural disasters; provision of NFI for highly vulnerable children and their families, including hygiene and dignity kit and coordinate with other non-CP actors to prioritize and support most children and their family- health, nutrition, FSL etc. The project will also have activities to promote prevention measures. Awareness raising on child protection, GBV and PSEA will be conducted for children and community members. In addition to this, SC will strengthen and work with community-based child protection structures (CBCPS) for increased community awareness and action for protection of children from violence; provide technical (coaching and mentoring) and material support for members and leaders of community-based child protection structures to support them carry out their function. Finally, to ensure child protection mainstreaming and in line with its Centrality of Protection Policy SC will deliver training to non-child protection actors (Health, Education, Nutrition, EFSL, and WASH staff etc) on child protection concerns, grave violation, case reporting mechanisms, including child protection service mainstreaming.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

SCI has established a robust Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) system to ensure continual collection, analysis and use of program data and information that aids informed decision making, accountability and inform programming. The MEAL team will be responsible for setting up the accountability system and reporting tools such as the project performance tracking table, which are critical for reporting accurate data and beneficiary satisfaction. This also includes setting up complaint mechanisms for beneficiaries in both IDP & HC, and training staff in how to respond to complaints if any. This will ensure context-based accountability system to the affected population (AAP). A comprehensive MEAL plan will be developed in line with the detailed implementation plan which will be developed by the project team at the start of the project. The plan will be prepared in a participatory method by involving relevant stakeholders. The plan will clarify how indicators are to be measured, detailing data sources, collection processes, as well as who will be responsible and at what frequencies the data will be collected. Appropriate MEAL tools will also be developed or adapted from existing ones to ensure regular collection and analysis of data which will be used to guide the direction of project implementation. The MEAL plan will ensure the collection of Sex and age disaggregated data and the data collection process includes aspects of marital status, ethnicity, rural/urban, IDP/HCs, etc. The data collection process will ensure the equitable and meaningful participation and consultation of women, girls and other marginalized groups with appropriate attention to the availability of a gender-balanced MEAL team. Furthermore, periodic reports will also be written by the operations team, technically reviewed by Programme Development and Quality Technical Specialists and submitted to the donor accordingly to the timeline agreed and indicated in the grant agreement document. The SCI child safeguarding Policy (CSP) and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policy (for adult beneficiaries) and Code of Conduct (CoC) will be applied throughout the project life span through SC’s safeguarding approach of prevention, awareness-raising, and reporting-in age and gender approprate manner.
