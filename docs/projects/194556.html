Project Summary

QC will conduct an assessment of existing water sources, including the functionality status, major issues (if not functional) and estimated number of populations benefitting from the water source in the targeted areas. Based on the results of the assessment, the project will provide rehabilitation for non functioning water stations including installing solar systems to provide sustainable power source for it.
In order to monitor the effectiveness of this result, reports will be maintained by assistant engineers, tracking the number of IDPs/host communities who have access to safe drinking water through rehabilitated water stations. In addition to this, water quality tests will be conducted and the number of main stations and sub stations which are functional will also be monitored.
Water will be treated with chlorine, to ensure the safe consumption by beneficiaries. Additionally, dosing chlorine pumps provide the correct amount of chlorine treatment, just outside of the water well, in order to ensure that water is appropriate for human consumption. Water testing will be completed on a daily, weekly and monthly basis, in order to ensure water quality. Water samples will be taken at random locations throughout the entire water network and tested for Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) and microbiological presence, ensuring that the water is pathogen free.
QC will meet with the local council and local water authority to explain how the repair and rehabilitation of the systems will be conducted, and organize trainings for them in order to continue the water pumping after the project is completed through the collection system, which secures the sustainability of the service.
The sewage systems in the most urban areas/district in northern Syria, discharge to a network of sewage ponds that provide secondary treatment for sewage. In assessments carried out by QC’s team, it was found that some of the sewage systems are provided with pumps and power supply for pumping the wastewater, through the piped network to the ponds. Some of these ponds are situated alongside an existing natural water system. As long as households have access to a sufficient and reliable water supply as well as support to the pumping system, wastewater will move through the network consistently. However, there are blockages and leakages in the sewage system, as with any network. Thus, QC will support the target communities with resources to repair blockages and leaks, so the system is in good working order.
Also Qatar Charity will target schools and health facilities in the area with rehabilitation activities to help improving the services provided by these facilities.
The project team will conduct hygiene promotion sessions through a hygiene promotion team to promote diseases prevention mechanisms, focusing on the most vulnerable groups, such as children < 5 years, lactating mothers and pregnant women, the elderly and households with critically ill family members, and then hygiene kits will be distributed to the targeted groups.

3 - Needs

The need in WASH sector remains huge where there is 4.2 people in need in northwest Syria (northwest Syria fact sheet as of 09 August 2022), most of these population have limited access to safe water and sanitation services which was clearly seen during last Cholera pandemic. For Qatar Charity the field staff prepared need assessment for schools in need for wash facilities in Azaz area which needs to be targeted with wash services. Also as we already did rehabilitation for 5 water stations in Idleb area (Dana & Maaret Masrin). During implementation we noticed that there are more water stations that are out of service because of lack of maintenance.All populations without public services are in a worse situation, as they rely heavily on informal water sources, face shortage and insufficient water supply, suffer from poor water quality, and suffer from serious problems with sewage disposal or lack of garbage removal services especially in camps.Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites , as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services Especially with the spread of cholera in NWS.

4 - Implementation of activities

QC will directly implement project through qualiﬁed staff as the following activities:- Rehabilitation of major water networks, such as water stations, and the construction of new water systems for areas without access to established water facilities in communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools.- Installation of solar systems for water stations and provision of Lubricants and spare parts stocks such as filters to the local water authorities.- Construction/Rehabilitation of the sewage networks for communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools.- Capacity building to local council and water authority for operations and maintenance of water system and cost recovery system.- Hygiene promotion campaigns, with more focus on pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, including the distribution of Hygiene kits.- Distribution of water tanks to the IDPs in camps and communities to increase the HH water storage capacity for adequate and safe water.- Distribution of hygiene kits and water tanks to the IDPs in camps and communities- Organize single sex focus group discussions for women, girls, boys and men to choose the location and design latrines and bathrooms.- Rehabilitation of wash facilities in schools and health centers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will carry continuous Protection Risk Analysis as part of the implementation plan. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence and exposure to explosive hazard. The project will mitigate the risk of inequality in accessing services by selecting of the target beneficiaries based on need assessment and that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context for each activity of the project and the project will coordinate with the stakeholders to ensure equitable access to the project activities. The project will address risks related to work safety by adopting the standard safety measures during the operation and maintenance of the WASH facilities. Workers will use the Personal Protective Equipment to minimize risk of injuries or exposure to diseases. The project will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, etc.) to GBV. The project team will ensure women’s voices is heard during design of WASH interventions and identify any GBV risk(s). The locations of the WASH facilities will be central, accessible and in well-lit areas with good visibility of the surrounding area. staff will be trained on GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

QC has a clear strategy to mitigate the risks of PSEA, the strategy includes direct and indirect beneficiaries of our programming, adults in wider communities in which we work in. It also includes QC’s staff, partners, suppliers sub-contractors and any other individual engaged by QC's programs. Particular attention is needed for vulnerable groups, including women and children, and those most at risk of discrimination based on disability, gender identity and sexual orientation, age, and religion. Our approach to PSEA is through the following:1- Ensure that all staff, representatives and third parties are aware of the high standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect individuals of SEA.2- Having an organizational culture based on accountability where there is zero tolerance for PSEA.3- Have an endorsed reporting mechanism for staff, beneficiaries and partners to feel safe to report violations.4- Ensure that reports are handled in a manner that respects due process, and swift and credible investigation for any violations. And actively promote fair investigation and sanctions.5- mitigating risks in programming by identifying the risks and design the program accordingly. Starting from needs assessments through project design to the implementation to closing phases.6- Comprehensive operational, HR and mgmt. measures are also required to PSEA. By conducting relative vetting and checks of staff, suppliers .
