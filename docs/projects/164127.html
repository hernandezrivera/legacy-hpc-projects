Project Summary

Through the proposed project, IR Syria aims to support those who are in need for wash assistance. 88,000 people will beneﬁt from improved access to water and sanitation facilities. The project is primarily designed based on Humanitarian Need overview 2019. The project will support the formal and informal settlements as well as well host communities with core WASH services. The activities included in this proposal are in line with the WASH cluster objective which is support to water, sanitation and sewage systems to ensure continuous services for affected people in Syria and deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to most vulnerable people. IRW prioritizes WASH support interventions for the conﬂict affected population in the target location to enhance beneﬁciaries’ resilience in coping with ongoing crisis through the provision of access to clean water as well as supporting them in cleaning the wash facilities in a clean and healthy environment. Below will be the intended activities to achieve the project objectives: 1. Adequate quality and quantity of water: Water supply will ensure through water trucking. IR will sign an agreement with a contractor to supply water to the targeted camps on daily basis (23 L per person will be provided on daily basis). This water will be suﬃcient to cover the drinking, bathing and household need of the individuals. If required by the targeted families, IR will also distribute Jerry cans. This will be contributed by IR. 2. Water testing and treatment (chlorination): IR team will conduct water testing and chlorination before it is available for use by the beneﬁciaries. This will help to eliminate the risk of using contaminated water. 3. Waste removal service (solid waste management): Through a service provider (contractor) IR will ensure to collect the garbage from the camps and deport it to the agreed damp sites on daily basis. This will improve the overall environment and decrease the risk of diseases spread. IR will also place garbage bins 4. Operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities: Sanitation facilities (latrines and bathing areas) will be gender segregated and will have protection measures this will be considered during the regular maintenance of the facilities (lockable doors, lighting of paths to facilities). The WASH facilities in the schools will also be maintained/rehabilitated.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO, including a country-wide individual household-level survey at sub-district level (25,644 household level interviews from 268 sub-districts across Syria) and a household-level survey focused on IDP sites in north-west and north-east Syria (2,910 household level interviews in 280 IDP sites in 34 sub-districts). Both assessments included drinking water sampling for free residual chlorine (FRC) testing at the end-user level to determine water safety. The assessments revealed that, despite overall slight improvement of WASH conditions in Syria, vast WASH related needs continuous to exist in many parts of Syria, affecting various population groups. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites , as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on comprehensive preparedness. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have direct negative impact on people physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. After nine years of conflict, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant investments, repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destructions, displacement of technical staff and effects of climate change and poor water resources management. 42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs .
