Project Summary

GOALPrime Organization Nigeria (GPON) target to reach a total number of 50000 people; out of which 500 (80% girls & 20% boys) who are survivors of sexual assault will be reached with MHPSS/Counselling service, 2000 vulnerable household heads will be reached with door to door risk communication while 47,500 people (60% women/girls and 40% men/boys) will be reached with motorized risk communication with messages on prevention and control of cholera across camps and host communities in kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 20000 Information Education and Communication-IEC Materials will be printed and distributed to beneficiaries who will be reached with risk communication. The IEC materials will be developed in Hausa, Kanuri and English languages in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar of the Health sector and the Borno State Ministry of Health. 10% of the total number of beneficiaries to be reached will be people living with disabilities.
GOALPrime in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar will develop prevention and control jingles/messages on cholera which will be aired consistently on in the Radio Station with coverage reaching Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. GOALPrime will also procure and distribute small transistor radios and batteries to the aforementioned 2000 vulnerable household heads during the door to door risk communication to ensure that they listen to the prevention and control messages aired through the radio stations.
In Kala-balge, GOALPrime will implement in Bullamari and Ajari Host communities; Boarding Primary school camp, Kilagaru camp, and General Hospital camp; in Damboa, GOALPrime will implement in Unity Camp, General Hospital Camp, Old Secretariat Camp, Lowcost Camp, and Hausari Host Community while in Yola South, and Damaturu, GOALPrime will carry out motorized risk communication in 20 camps and host communities.
GOALPrime through its ongoing Community Based Psychosocial support (CBPSS) and Lifeskills project in KalBalge and Damboa LGAs has identified the critical need for mental health psychosocial support especially for adolescent girls and survivors of sexual assault as well as the need to deploy more health staff to both LGAs. GOALPrime will be fully operational in both LGAs all through the implementation of this project.

1 - Needs

Evidence shows that majority of people in need in BAY states are in Borno state which is the epicentre of the armed conflict. One in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age. Women and children are mostly affected by the crisis as they constitute 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements (2021, Humanitarian Needs Overview). Sexual and gender-based violence, including rape, is rampant due to the ongoing conflict, insecurity and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. Sexual exploitation is also a major concern with widespread allegations.Women and girls are facing a more substantial burden as some have taken on additional roles within their families and communities at the risk of greater exposure to violations and negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, forced and child marriages, and transactional sex in exchange for food or other assistance hence the need for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support services as well as Sexual Reproductive Health Life skills services. The COVID-19 pandemic which also is affecting Borno state has presented its own challenges to humanitarian programme intervention. Excerpts from the Health Sector’s Needs Analysis in the 2020 HRP Addendum for the COVID -19 Response Plan advises that the COVID-19 pandemic will affect the protection and well-being of girls, boys, and their caregivers. As of 22 June 2020, 20,919 COVID-19 cases have been confirmed in Nigeria, with 525 deaths and 6,109 recoveries. The BAY states recorded 577 confirmed cases; 476 in Borno and 45 in Adamawa, and 56 in Yobe in the same period.Factors that may contribute to the spread of COVID-19 are a weakened health system, multiple disease outbreaks, high population concentration in specific IDPs camps and Host Communities, lack of access to safe potable water and sanitation infrastructure, inadequate awareness on preventive measures, and traditional practices. This is particularly true in the conflict affected Borno State, that have been affected by a decade of violent conflict. According to the Borno State COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Plan, IDPs are a high-risk category for the spread of COVID-19. The most vulnerable population groups are children, women, the elderly, and people with chronic medical conditions and decreased immunity associated with diseases and risks typical of humanitarian contexts such as comorbidities of malnutrition, measles, malaria, HIV. Kala- bage and Damboa appears to be among the LGAs with high-risk identification for COVID- 19 because of crowded IDP camps, border town to an affected country, weak health system and lack of access to potable water, therefore effective risk communication, preparedness and responds are needed. There is a serious shortage of skilled health care workers, particularly doctors, nurses and midwives as the unpredictable security situation hampers the movements of health workers, drugs and other medical supplies. In Kala- Balge LGA, 112,405 (30825 women, 21752 men, 35,094 girls, and 29,734 boys) andDamboa LGA, 164,621 (37917 women, 32768 men, 51,121 girls and 42,816 boys) are people in need of multi-layered humanitarian assistance, (Source: 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview - https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/nigeria/document/nigeria-2020- humanitarian-needs-overview). The critical health needs have also been confirmed by GOALPrime Organization Nigeria from the relevant community and camp stakeholders in both LGAs during GOALPrime’s rapid needs assessment (June, 2020) through which GOALPrime gathered evidence of MHPSS/counseling for survivors of sexual assault, reproductive health life skills services, deployment of health staffs to the health facilities in Kala-Balge and Damboa; and Risk communication in both LGAs with an inclusion of camps and host communities in MMC.

2 - Activities

1. Consult with and agree with the State Ministry of Health and the Borno State Primary Health Care Development Agency on the use of 4 health facilities in Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs for MHPSS/Counseling service. 2. Consult with community stakeholders, parents, caregivers and notify them of availability of service in Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 3. Sensitize community stakeholders, parents, caregivers, heads of households, and adolescents on reporting of sexual or suspected sexual assault to the MHPSS/Counselling Facilitators in the Health Facilities. 4. Conduct training for MHPSS/Counseling Facilitators deployed to Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 5. Carry out MHPSS/Counseling for boys and girls reported or identified to have been sexually assaulted in the Health facility. 6. Consult with community leaders on the availability of vulnerable households in their camps and host communities in Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 7. Identify and train community-based Community Cholera Health Educators in Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 8. Identify, through the support of community leaders, vulnerable households in Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 9. Deploy trained community- based Cholera Health Educators to engage identified and profiled vulnerable households in Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. 10. Follow-up to monitor behavioral change amongst reached household heads and members of their household. 11. Consult with the Risk Communication Pillar, the Health Sector, and the Borno State Ministry of Health and Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) on Cholera prevention and control messages to be developed. 12. Consult with community, religious and traditional leaders on contextualization as well as key Cholera key messages to be developed. 13. Develop Cholera prevention and control messages in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar, Borno State Ministry of Health, NCDC, and the community leaders/people. 14. Develop illustrations and animations on prevention and control of Cholera in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar, Borno State Ministry of Health, NCDC, and the community leaders/people. 15. Engage GOALPrime's Risk Communication team to camps and host communities in Kala-Balge, Damboa, and MMC for weekly motorized and megaphone awareness-raising on prevention and control of Cholera. 16. Engage Peace FM and Danda Kura on the continuous airing of developed jingles in Hausa, Kanuri, and English. 17. Distribute printed IEC materials (English, Hausa & Kanuri) to target individuals in camps and host communities reached with awareness-raising outreaches in Kala-Balge, Damboa, and MMC LGAs. 18. Follow-up to monitor changes in behaviour post-awareness-raising outreaches.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime will establish or strengthen new or existing feedback and complaints mechanisms in target LGAs. GOALPrime will hold bi-monthly feedback focus group discussions with girls and boys including adolescents’ beneficiaries and their caregivers to understand the project's impact on them and also receive suggestions from them on areas they want project improvement. The Focus Group discussionswill however be in compliance with the COVID-19 regulations of maintaining social distancing and the use of face masks. GOALPrime will also carry out at least two child-friendly surveys –one at the beginning of the project and another one at the end of the project to collect perceptions and feedback from children that should guide project activities and strategy improvement. In addition, GOALPrime will still have a complaint and suggestion box kept at strategic positions within the project environment for beneficiaries and their parents to drop their complaints if they feel more comfortable with making anonymous complaints and also their suggestions on how they want the project to serve them better. GOALPrime will provide feedback of complaints to the beneficiaries, parents, and other relevant community stakeholders as soon as complaints are received and reviewed. There are suggestions also will be taken into consideration in the review of Programme approach. Effective and timely actions shall be taken to address concerns received through our established feedback mechanism. However,lessons learned from past projects are. that complaints received via the complaint box mechanism are mostly not related to the project being implemented. To address this, GOALPrime will intensify sensitization of the project to both the target beneficiaries and the community people so that they understand the expected project outcomes and make relevant complaints related to the project.GOALPrime will also provide complaints and feedback hotlines in the LGAs.
