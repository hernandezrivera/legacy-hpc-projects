Project Summary

The multi-sectoral response is targeting two geographic areas: (1) one vulnerable South Sudanese refugees in West and South Kordofan, and (2) two refugees fleeing from Ethiopia in Gedaref and Kassala States. This intervention links with and responds to the 2021 Refugee Response Strategy under the HRP to strengthen the protection environment for refugees, to enable access to timely protection and lifesaving assistance as well as to provide equitable access to basic services for refugees and strengthening opportunities for resilience and self-reliance.

The programme targets a total population 50,000 vulnerable refugees across the two regions (West and South Kordofan states, Kassala and Gadarif states). 35,000 South Sudanese refugees in both South/West Kordofan are targeted in over 9 localities. As part of the Tigray response we will target 25,000 vulnerable refugees fleeing from Ethiopia to Kassala and Gedaref state.
Concern’s intervention will provide integrated health, nutrition, WASH, FSL and shelter/NFI services for vulnerable refugees in 1) host community setting in Kordofan through health/nutrition facilities and their catchment areas, and 2) reception centers/refugee camp setting and nearby catchment population in Kassala and Gadarif states.
Under the health/nutrition sector, the project will deliver primary health care services through medical consultations and treatment, reproductive heath services for pregnant women, outpatient treatment and severe acute malnourished treatment for children under 5 years of age, treatment for moderately malnourished children/PLW. For the most impact and benefit, the project is integrating WASH services in to the health and nutrition facilities and their catchments. The project proposes to renovate water facilities (and construct water points in health/ nutrition centres and catchments). The project will also provide soap and sanitary kits for women to improve personal hygiene for vulnerable refugees and emphases on C-19 sensitive WASH programming.
A small component of the response, particularly in West and South Kordofan state will provide FSL services (cash programming and cross cutting environment protection component) for the SS refugees.
Under NFI and shelter intervention, the program will distribute NFIs for both regions, and shelter solution will be the priority for the Ethiopian refugee response in Gadareef locality.
The total budget proposed is: $ 2,174,100 = $ 1,089,100 for the response targeting the South Sudanese Refugees in Kordofan, and $ 1,085,000 for the East Sudanese refugee response for the Tigray refugees.

1 - Needs

According to the latest September 2020 update, there are 98,820 SS refugees in West and South Kordofan states. They are in protracted status although their situation remains vulnerable. In both states, the life-saving health, nutrition, WASH services are limited, and with the current economic hardship of Sudan (200% devaluation of currency), shortage of fuel, bread and water/electricity, the presence of SS refugees create an additional burden on the already challenged local host community. During the field work of Concern in both West and South Kordofan states, our field teams observed an increased demand for basic services, not only amongst refugees, but their host community HHs as well, triggers with insecurity in some areas, particularly near to the SPLA/N areas. Basic services such as access to a Primary Healthcare package, WASH services and nutrient rich food for children under five years of age. Climate driven disasters e.g. flooding is a frequent phenomenon in the region that requires addressing basic needs e.g. shelter and NFI support. Sudan is facing multiple challenges, including over 200% annual inflation, currency depreciation and fuel shortages that is compounded by lockdown restrictions due to COVID-19. The 2021 HNO estimates over 12.7 million people in need, an increase of a third compared to 2020. One million of the people in need are refugees. The growing tensions in the Tigray region could lead to large scale displacement with large movements across the border into East Sudan. The planning scenario is for 200,000 arrivals within six months. According to the UNHCR daily update on new Ethiopian refugee arrivals are 49,593 people have entered in Sudan through main entry points of Hamdayet, Lugdi, Abu Rafi and Village 8, as a result of intensive fights in Ethiopia Tifray region (UNJCR 10 December 2020). The majority of the refugees chose to be transported to Um Rakoba refugee camp although significant number of people still waiting at the reception points to be supported. Therefore an urgent humanitarian intervention is needed at the border sides and in Um Rakoba refugee center (health, nutrition, WASH and shelter/NFI). Therefore UNHCR has led a joint Appeal for funding, and Concern has been part of this Appeal amongst many other humanitarian partners and plan to intervene in health, nutrition, WASH and NFI/Shelter response. Concern WW has conducted a needs assessment between 23 of November and 3 of December and indicated huge gap in the life-saving minimum services e.g. primarily health care (medicine, referral, outpatient care, PLWs care and save delivery), nutrition (food provision for children u5, IYCF support, lack of vitamins and minerals due to poor diet), WASH services (lack of potable water, sanitation services, dignified shower place, personal and community hygiene), and shelter NFI provision. Provision of an integrated services in health care, nutrition support, WASH and NFI/shelter in the refugee camp would encourage refugees to move and trust in the humanitarian system. Therefore, Concern Sudan team has been closely coordinating with with UNHCR and its technical team on the ground and started community engagement in health, nutrition in Hamdayet, and plan to intervene in similar services alone with shelter and NFI distribution in Um Rakoba. The implementation plan was coordinated with UNHCR RRF Team and contributed in the Refugee Response Appeal.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. However, it isacknowledge that shelter provision and WASH improvement practices that the project seeks to support sometimes have negative impacts on the environment. For instance, in some of the localities where theproject will be implemented, environmental degradation is a major concern. This iscaused by cutting down of trees, poor agricultural practices and over-grazing, amongother factors. The project will receive 2000 ER tents from overseas that will contribute to a sustainable shelter management. WASH improvement (e.g. emergency latrines and showers will use locally available materials and will also introduce fiber-based slab techniques for sustainable management of resources. The assessment, planning and implementation of shelter and NFI activities willconsider the impact on the environment and will aim to mitigate any negative impactensuring synergy with afforestation activities and selecting durable shelter/NFImaterials. The shelter materials are locally available, are easy to use for self constructionand will absorb twelves through time and will not have an environmentalimpact.During the project design and implementation climate change adaptation approacheswill be considered particularly for the running of health/nutrition facilities, and thewater source renovation and management. During the technical assessment andtechnical design of the renovation work, the reduction of water waste will be one of theimportant elements to consider. During the renovation of health/nutrition centres in Kordofan, solarsystem and related innovative solutions will be considered. Nutrition and healthactivities will also consider the environment by using environmentally manageablematerials for building renovation, and waste management (debris) will be organizedduring and after the renovation. However the project is not directly impacted by theenvironment, during the health and nutrition capacity building training environmentprotection particularly related to medical waste management (storage and incineratingmedical waste) will be considered, and a daily waste disposal will be organized aroundthe health and nutrition facilities. Other aspects related to designing and constructingHH latrines will use locally available, natural resources to reduce the negative impacton ecological footprint.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As part of rapid context analysis, the project will identify the most common protectionrisks, the high risk group and then design mitigation measures. However, the projectwill take measures to ensure that women and children are protected and are not put ina situation that may increase their protection risks. For instance, the distribution ofsupplies will take place within the communities, awareness raising will be done, MPCAwill involve both men and women. Lack of food in the household can sometimes leadto protection risks such as GBV and child labour and by increasing availability andaccess to food and livelihood opportunities, the project will indirectly be reducing theserisks.In East Sudan in particular, where the project will work in camp setting, protection issues will be coordinated and referred to the organizations most adequate to manage. Concern will continue coordinating with the protection focal point in UNHCR on-day-today protection issues and also will do a protection mapping with the support of other actors to ensure that safeguarding, child protection issue will be addressed. Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies.Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protectprogramme beneficairies– children, women and men. Concern’s approach toprotection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) therisks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerablegroups including refugees within these communities, iii) the risks our programme maycreate; andreducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its ChildSafeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. -The affected population, who live very far from the health centers will be in focus.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In late 2017, Concern was the Frst Irish agency to be accredited with CHS certifcationbased on a rigorous external process.(https://www.chsalliance.org/verify/certi􀂦cation/) As part of accountability to affectedpeople, Concern has put in place a robust Complaints and Response Mechanisms(CRM). The CRM spells out how the project beneficiaries are supposed to theircomplaints, analysis of the complaints, reporting, feedback and actions to be taken ifrequired. The implementation of the CRM system starts at the project design stagewhere the beneficiaries are informed about the platforms available to report any casesof misconduct by project staff, including partner staff and contractors. At theimplementation stage, Concern usually rolls out the CRM at the lowest level of projectimplementation, with the establishment CRM committees with membership from thecommunity, including women to ensure that the system is implemented at thegrassroots. The CRM system is monitored and overseen by the senior managementstaff of the organisation. The feedback from the CRM system are analysed, reporteddownwards to the beneficiaries, including the actions taken and upwards to the seniormanagement in country and the headquarters in Dublin. Concern’s projects is alignedwith minimum standards and accountability mechanism to beneficiaries includingCRM, lessons learned and PDM.In the camp setting in East Sudan, Concern will work together with various partners to refer to within the CRM system and also will work with the camp management system to ensure each complain will responded, feedback provided.
