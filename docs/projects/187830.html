Project Summary

In 2022, Shafak Organization proposes to mitigate and respond to the protection risks faced by the conflict-affected people in Jabal Samaan, Jarablus, Al-Bab and Azaz districts in Aleppo governorate, as well as in Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Ariha and Idleb districts in Idleb governorate through a community centers and protection mobile teams.
The purpose of this project is to empower local communities and conflict-affected individuals in North West Syria, and support their role in building society and in determining their future by provision of protection services and emergency protection services to 107,570 IDPs and host community members, through five community centers, two protection tents and 25 mobile outreach teams. The provided protection services will comprise both mitigation and response services as well as specialized and non-specialized protection services.
The proposed project will offer gender and age appropriate general protection service including, structured PSS, recreational and MHPSS activities, case management involving life-saving assistance to vulnerable people in ensuring their access to existing services, referral to specialized and non-specialized services, awareness raising on protection related topics, life skills and vocational training to enhance the community resilience and livelihoods opportunities, legal assistance and legal awareness raising session of HLP and CSD through qualified and well-trained legal teams, protection monitoring, as well as supporting community committees.
Shafak will maintain gender-balanced team in all its projects in order to facilitate the access of all gender groups to the services in line with the humanitarian principles. Moreover, training on HLP and CSD will be delivered to partners’ legal staff and community actors in Syria and Turkey by specialized legal staff in accordance with the protection cluster and HLP TWG guidance and SOPs.
These services will be offered to vulnerable IDPs and host community members with particular emphasis on quality services to the following groups:
• Women headed households
• Children headed households
• UASC, CAAFAG and Child labor
• PWDs
• Elderly women and men
• Family has children at risk of child labor
• UASC and orphans who are being cared for by another family.
• New IDPs who fled their home and have no financial means
In case of emergencies, the project activities will be shifted to respond to the emergency protection needs of new displaced people delivering the protection emergency package like: PFA, referrals and case fund support to respond to the urgent needs of IDPs.
Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on protection topics such as; CPMS, Safe identification and referral and communication skills to enable them identify and refer people in need to the case management team.
The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specificities of targeted populations. All static centers will be rehabilitated and well equipped to be disabled-friendly spaces.
COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken in line with WHO, Global Protection Cluster and the Protection Cluster’s Guidance, including strict enforcement of physical distancing, minimizing of participants per activity to maximum eight people and maintaining the social distancing between BNFs and staff, the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) by staff/beneficiaries.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.

4 - Needs

Since March,2011, protection issues in Syrian protracted crisis are worsening in NWS. The deteriorating economy and widespread poverty, lack and loss of livelihoods, destruction and loss of housing and property, displacement and continuing of COVID-19 pandemic, exacerbate protection needs and increase reliance on negative coping mechanisms. Many groups have heightened vulnerability towards protection risks such as PwDs, older people and women who have experienced divorce or widowhood. Lack/loss of civil documentation was reported in 76% of assessed Communities, Restrictions of movement were reported in 51% of assessed communities and 61% of assessed communities reported HLP concerns.Children and adolescents face violence, abuse and exploitation in many life situations including exposure to new forms of violence due to harmful coping mechanisms within the family. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, separated from family, detained or to be involved in child labor, while adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage, online harassment and other forms of GBV. Men and boys are at risk of sexual violence. while women and girls continue to be affected by GBV, for example by the loss of the principal breadwinner, but become at-risk when they carry out their usual livelihood and household activities. (Draft HNO 2022).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1:Different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services and assistance Likelihood: High, Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1.Ensure consultation with communities that may be at particular risk of exposure and risk of exclusion. 2.Work with national actors and community based groups to ensure sustainability of the intervention.Risk2:Tension in community including risk for project staff. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1.Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities. 2.Consult with actors to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible throughout protection service providers.Risk3:Exploitation or abuse by staff members when accessing assistance and servicesLikelihood: High. Impact:High. Mitigation measures:1.Communicate clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended. 2.Design specific complaint mechanism and follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality. 3.train staff and service providers on CoC and PSEA.Risk4: Risk that by providing information about documentation which may not be recognized is perceived as endorsement of said documents/legal systems. LH: High. Impact: High. Mitigation:1. Advocacy for inclusion of civil documentation and HLP redress as confidence building measures2. Ensure humanitarian actors in northern Syria are informed of the potential risks in CSD/HLP programming in opposition area


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security-related SOPs in place. 1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to preempt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover, or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. Risk 2: Disputes between host communities and IDPs; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 2.1:Targeting host community, 2.2: ensure Complaint mechanism is accessible by all people. 2.3 integrity and clear verification process conducted according to announced criteria. 2.4 Prior coordination with local actors and stakeholders in the community to ensure the process.Risk: Outbreak of COVID 19; Likelihood: High; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 3.1:Daily check of staff to ensure their safety is a major symptom of the Coronavirus.3.2:Emphasis during work on the need to leave a distance of at least 1 between workers.3.3:The necessity not to exchange equipment and tools before sterilization. 3.4:Ensure the presence of awareness brochures within the work area and in a clear place.3.4:In case of imposed curfew. Suspend the activities and change the modality to be through service providers and distribute the dues of the staff
