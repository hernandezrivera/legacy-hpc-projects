Project Summary

The project supports the coordination and COVID-19 preparedness and response to front-line United Nations personnel and their eligible dependents in Cox's Bazar as well as RRRC and NGO frontline workers in light of the COVID-19 pandemic. Support mechanisms ensure inclusion of distance consultations and detection and treatment referral pathways and improvement of health facilities. The facilities allow for testing of the virus and are equipped to address the needs of the humanitarian frontline aid workers should they test positive for COVID-19 and need urgent assistance.

1 - Identified Needs

The more than 896,879 Rohingya Refugees currently residing in 34 camps in Cox’s Bazar are highly vulnerable to COVID-19 due to overcrowded living conditions and poor WASH infrastructure. Rohingya refugee camps are among the most densely populated place on earth with more than 42,000 people/ km2 and physical distancing measures are expected to have a limited impact. Poor food consumption scores and underlying malnutrition contribute to weakened immune status of individuals. Elderly persons above the age of 60 and those with underlying health conditions are at higher risk of developing serious disease and will be affected disproportionally. As of 28 November 2021, Bangladesh has 1,574,636 confirmed cases, including 23,514 in Cox’s Bazar, 3,276 which are in the refugee camps. Front-line workers providing services and activities in the camps face increased exposure, and there have been a number of confirmed cases requiring specialized medical assistance. As the capacity in the operational area to provide the necessary health support continues to be limited, and in order to be able to duly exercise the Duty of Care for humanitarian personnel, it is essential to maintain a response capacity that would include quick and quality health services. In response, a facility dedicated to providing COVID-19 care for UN staff, their dependents, and other front-line staff of partner organizations was established in the duty station in 2021, and continuous quality care must be provided in 2022.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Support coordination, collaboration and information management to humanitarian staff responding to the COVID-19 pandemic in the camps. Through the COVID-19 Medical Treatment Facility (MTF), continue to provide medical care that is compliant with WHO guidelines on infection prevention and control and manage severe acute respiratory infections and other complications resulting from COVID-19 infections. Procurement of PPE and medical supplies for the detection and treatment facility.
