Project Name

01/01/2023

Project Summary

ADA -HRP 2023 aims at addressing emergency shelter and non-food items needs of new and highly vulnerable populations, protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities through single and multi-sector responses to holistically restore community dignity. The project directly targets 27,309 individuals: 7,487 in Ayod, 1,868 in Fangak, 6,182 in Nyirol, 4,901 in Pigi/Canal Counties of Jonglei State; and 6,871 in Nasir County of Upper Nile State. Ayod, Fangak, Pigi/Canal, and Nyirol Counties are north of Jonglei State; while Nasir County is in South East of Upper Nile State. Based on South Sudan IPC projections for October 2022 to June 2023, all five counties are expected to be in IPC 4 level (severe food insecurity). The same source predicts that Fangak and Pigi/Canal Counties might slide into IPC 5 level (acute food insecurity). Source: IPC. https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1155997/?iso3=SSD
As ADA will be static in the mentioned counties, it will be collaborating with other mobile partners in the same counties. The envisaged synergies with the partners (in all the five targeted counties) will include IOM on CCM responses, NRC on protection needs, PAH for WASH concerns, and MedAir for multi-sector interventions. Overall, ADA will selectively use single-sector or multi-sector interventions, based on the emerging needs of respective county populations within 2023. In all cases, ADA will endeavor to provide interventions that would contribute to holistic programming for the most vulnerable communities. Where a single-sector approach will be used, ADA will ensure integrating it with protection mainstreaming; land, housing, and property rights; Covid-19 guidelines; Ebola risk mitigation measures, besides an inclusive engagement with all key stakeholders in the target areas.
All integrated responses will, at a minimum, aim at protection mainstreaming including the establishment of community social networks and safety nets; conducting protection training to project area committees to implement, and conducting mine risk awareness sessions through the committees to ensure the safety of the communities.
In all project areas, ADA, and the mobile partners named above (and others, on a needs basis) aim to address CCCM, protection & SNFI needs of new and highly vulnerable conflict and disaster-affected communities through in-kind S/NFI assistance, for both repairs and maintenance. ADA, through a mobile approach, will coordinate responses with other partners and SFPs and monitor both the single and multi-sector services through its static and roving teams to deep field locations in all its project areas.
The project will be implemented over a period of 12 months in all counties through single and multi-sector interventions, which will be boosted by agencies’ existing funding streams.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to experience extensive flooding, high levels of food insecurity, violence. and Disease outbreak continue to impact people across the country. Findings from rapid needs assessments indicate that over 616,000 people were affected by floods and torrential rains in 22 counties and Abyei area as of 30 September. The number of people affected is expected to increase as the rains continue, and assessments continue to verify people’s situations. (Source: https://reliefweb.int/report/south-sudan/south-sudan-humanitarian-snapshot-september-2022). As of 28 October, over 1 million people were verified as affected by torrential rains and flooding in 36 counties across South Sudan and over 20,000 people in the southern part of the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA). The ongoing flood response is hampered by renewed violence and insecurity, inaccessibility due to impassible roads, broken bridges, flooded airstrips, the lack of air assets, the lack of critical core pipeline supplies and funding constraints. An alert has been raised amidst media reports that the Government of Uganda may open dams on the White Nile River to relieve congestion. Priority humanitarian needs include food assistance, shelter and non-food items (NFIs), health, nutrition and WASH supplies, as well as fishing kits for livelihood support. (Source: https://reliefweb.int/report/south-sudan/south-sudan-flooding-situation-report-no-1-31-october-2022)As noted above, multiple shocks will likely be creating vast humanitarian needs for South Sudanese populations. It is against this background that ADA, in line with guidance from UNOCHA and South Sudan humanitarian sector Clusters is preparing this 2023 HRP proposal. In the 2023 HRP, ADA is seeking to provide humanitarian support to five Counties: Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, Pigi/Canal in Jonglei State; and Nasir County in Upper Nile State. It is these counties that the humanitarian needs are highlighted in this section. Based on South Sudan IPC projections for October 2022 to June 2023, all five counties are expected to be in IPC 4 level (severe food insecurity). The same source predicts that Fangak and Pigi/Canal Counties might slide into IPC 5 level - acute food insecurity. (Source: IPC. https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1155997/?iso3=SSDThe net effect of the cumulative shocks and lack of access to resources is that affected people’s coping mechanisms are weakened leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour, sexual exploitation and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards, pushing people to the brink, especially in urban areas. The foregoing continues to impact people’s physical and mental well-being, and living standards. This is expected to further deteriorate in 2023. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan were estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 including in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, Pigi/Canal, and Nasir Counties. The figures for 2023 are likely to rise.The increase in needs is largely driven by the rising food insecurity across the country. Food insecurity compounded by socks like flooding, internal conflicts (e.g. the Atar skirmishes of August /September 2022), Covid-19, vandalized shelters were identified as the primary challenges faced by the majority of affected people across sex and age groups (HNO Jan 2021). Similarly, poor living conditions, especially for the displaced, returnees, protracted IDPs and vulnerable host community, weaken their health and security and affect their dignity. Among the most vulnerable people are newly displaced families, communities hosting large numbers of displaced and/or recently returned people, and households that are headed by a single parent or looking after older people or people with disabilities. The 2023 situation in the target counties is likely going to be affected by an impending Ebola in neighboring Uganda and DR Congo.

2 - Project activities

Community mobilization, assessment, registration, distribution of items and PDM will be conducted in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, Pigi/Canal and Nasir Counties. ADA will mobilize and sensitize the affected community through appropriate channels that are in line with Covid-19 preventive measures (e.g., install hand washing facility, sanitizers, social distancing). Local authorities including women, church leaders, chiefs and persons with disability will be engaged & consulted on the needs survey, and be used as focal and entry points for the project activities. 2) Needs Assessment of 27,500 beneficiaries: ADA with support of partners & authorities will conduct a rapid needs analysis to determine the vulnerability of the target populations and select project beneficiaries. Data will be collected by trained enumerators, analyzed and recommendations presented for intervention, to ensure ADA clearly understands the community needs. The project will employ various data collection techniques like household questionnaire, FGD, KII and observation. ADA will use the cluster standard tools to collect, analyze and present the data. 3) Registration of most vulnerable 27,500 beneficiaries (4,584HHs), upon approved reports will be done, through consultation and coordination with local authorities, community committees, enumerators and static partners. The project will target 50% of newly IDPs; 35% of returnees and 15% of vulnerable host community, this targeting aims at promoting strong community cohesion for peaceful coexistence. Of these, 60% (16,500) will be female and 40% (11,000) male. The project will target 5% (1,375) with awareness sessions on disaster/flood risk reduction. Also, 5% (1,375) disaster affected elderly will receive SNFI replacement to de-congest from communal shelters to reduce covid-19 spread. ADA will use the S/NFI Cluster’s selection criteria of household-to-household verification of needs and registration of the most vulnerable affected beneficiaries to receive the assistance. Similarly, cluster tools will be used during the registration, and priority will be accorded to the most vulnerable communities especially the elderly, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), female and child-headed households, and persons with disability. Tokens will be issued to the registered beneficiaries to facilitate quick and easy identification in the distribution sites. 4) Distribution of SNFI supplies to 27,500 beneficiaries (4,584HHs): ADA will identify distribution points at all project areas and consult its protection team and partners to conduct safety audit, to ensure affected persons dignity and rights are promoted and protected. Trained community mobilizers will be tasked to move from community to community to sensitize the beneficiaries about the distribution dates, venue and time. Prior to the distribution, subsequent consultation will be conducted with local community to ensure participation; distribution will take place at an appropriate agreed points where the entire beneficiary will come to receive the supplies. The distribution points will be selected not more than 30 minutes’ walk to mitigate protection incidences during return home. Due to Covid-19 pandemic, and a possible Ebola threat, distribution will be staggered. 5) ADA will administer a PDM tool after three weeks of distribution to evaluate the project impact and feedback on level of project satisfaction. Local enumerators, will be identified with support of authorities, trained and deployed to the field for data collection. Different data collection techniques will be employed including FGD, KII, household questionnaire and observation. A random sampling method will be used to pick respondents to ensure each beneficiary has an equal chance of being selected to provide their opinions. Raise awareness on flooding, HLP and Covid-19 and Ebola at all project phases.

If the forecasts go up too much it is a sign that the spending will be out of control by the end of the project. So, a decision can be made at this point. Performance to project case -finally, ADA shall regularly re-visit its log-frame and see what it has originally agreed upon, and determine how is the project shaping up. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA shall adopt inclusive methods for carrying out consultations with various community members, considering sexes and age groups. This shall involve conducting separate community meetings such as focus group discussion with women and girls alone, and men also separately ensuring persons with disabilities are engaged too, which allows for easy speak up of sensitive issues of disadvantaged groups for project attention. The project is design to recruit local enumerators and community mobilizers -those who can speak the local languages will be given high priority to be selected into the project, to ensure adequate translation of project activities to those who are illiterate. The project shall hold meetings with authorities and disadvantaged groups to share assessment findings, verify needs and triangulate data to ensure comprehensive mapping of PSNs is done, and that physically impaired persons are reached, consulted and involved into the project. ADA project designs typically consider ultimate beneficiaries whether and how to consult with at least a sample of some of them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ADA will consult the beneficiary and other stakeholders like chiefs, religious leaders on where they feel distribution locations shall be established. This consultation shall be done during the beneficiary registration timing. Also, beneficiaries will be consulted during the needs assessment to provide at least their top three (3) priorities items (needs) for consideration during the recommendation of supplies for distribution. The different opinions of beneficiary will be harmonized during FGD to ensure the most top three (3) items meets the needs of all affected communities. ADA will engage community leaders including chiefs, RRCs, RoSS, church leaders and women groups to agree on HLP issues. This engagement will ensure, that the IDPs or returnees have access to land to construct their temporary shelters for settlement and cultivation of crops. ADA will ensure that authorities provide written MOUs between the host community and the IDPs/Returnees on the land that the beneficiary will settle on. This aims at minimizing communal conflicts. During these engagements. ADA will have a chance to provide information on HLP guidelines in accordance to the South Sudan land rights. ADA will employ various processes and mechanisms to solicit opinions and feedback from the key stakeholders like beneficiaries about the SNFI assistance on their lives, the feedback loop will include: participatory methods and processes for needs assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation; accountability frameworks and mechanisms; complaints and response mechanisms; listening exercises between project team and beneficiary; conduct social audits to ensure protection mainstreaming in its activities and story-telling sessions, among others. The feedback loop mechanism; ADA will receive and provide information to beneficiaries through focus groups discussions, personal interviews with selected respondents especially government authorities and static partners, open community meetings, trained community mobilizers going door-to-door, establishing listening exercises and appointing reporting lines, community monitoring field visits, existing community liaisons contact persons appointed by beneficiaries, weekly coordination meetings, establish story telling by affected communities and community project committees. Additionally, ADA will train key stakeholders including community project committees on grievances procedures, especially linked to complaints mechanisms, also the community mobilizers will translate and pass key messages with aid of megaphones to read information printed on fliers or reports. The feedback and complaint mechanism shall be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. All feedback will be recorded, and responses given to the community members or groups, besides a monthly report of the feedback received and responses given is sent to ADA head office in Juba for documentation, filling and refining into implementation matrix. The project team shall include staff with specific responsibility for supporting the complaint/feedback mechanism.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This project aims at promoting initiatives that encourage a speak-up culture amongst the affected communities; and support field-level mainstreaming of PSEA activities. ADA Juba headquarter will assist its field offices to mainstream inter-agency complaints mechanisms at community level, strengthen communication with communities, support survivors to access services through referral pathways, and related activities. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) through ensuring its employees are trained on and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support are enrolled to access the SNFI assistance. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through identifying reporting lines and awareness raising. Also, this project is designed to provide information on who to report to. Preparators will be reported to local authorities, police and partners like Save the children and UNICEF. Reporting will be done case by case and as they arise. Map out PSEAs partners and establish safe referral pathways for survivors to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure zero tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including taking legal steps against such preparator. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee should be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. Not only that, it will provide ADA with feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, will strengthen the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Essentially, when positive feedback is received, it motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also feedback help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct, thus places ADA in position to know who to keep in the organization and who not to keep. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project has been designed to include capacity building approaches, like enhancing knowledge and networks of primary stakeholders (e.g., individuals, households, communities) through awareness creation and trainings sessions to strengthen the ability of affected persons to withstand adverse impact from external stressors including armed conflict, sub-national violence and disaster like flood. The project design has process of incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity of affected persons while receiving the SNFI assistance in the project locations. It is the responsibility of ADA and partners to ensure safety audits is done at all its distribution points; and that beneficiaries walks for less than 30 minutes to receive their items; distributions are conducted at morning hours (from 10am-3pm) as late or early hours are always prone for armed attacks. Distributions points should be located where there is shade and at least minimum WASH facilities to reduce exposure of beneficiaries to dangers of looting, rape and diseases. The project design prioritizes safety and dignity, and with strategy to avoid causing harm. It will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of SNFI intervention which could increase affected people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. Also, the project aims at promoting equity and meaningful access; the approaches take pro-active steps to ensure beneficiaries’ meaningful access to impartial assistance and facilities -in proportion to need and without any barriers (i.e., discrimination). Besides that, the project approach pays special attention to lobby for inclusion through ensuring community infrastructures like schools, health centers, training halls, NGO offices, water and supplies distribution points have facilities that enables disable individuals and groups to participate like any other persons. These groups may include the disable persons, lactating and pregnant mothers, female and child headed house, elderly 60 years+, unaccompanied minor and person with ill health condition. Accountability to affected populations: ADA shall set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need to make informed decisions, provide feedback on the SNFI assistance provided, share concerns and submit complaints. This will be done through community project committees constituted from the onset of the project. Accountability is articulated internally through the project’s own mechanism, and independently through an independent feedback and complaints mechanism. The Communication with Communities (CwC) approaches shall include sharing of lessons learned and coordination of activities implemented by ADA. Participation and empowerment: The project design support the development of self-protection capacities and assist affected people in claiming their rights, including but not exclusively the rights to shelter and NFIs, and Ebola. Participation and empowerment approaches are integrated throughout the project phases while static protection actors in the proposed multi-sector project locations propose to assist people in claiming their rights by promoting both the duties and responsibilities of the authorities, recognized community leaders, and the beneficiaries towards the services provided.
