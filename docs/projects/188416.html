Project Summary

The organization seeks to reduce, alleviate and mitigate the accidents related to the presence of explosive ordnance (EOs) in contaminated areas and increase the protection of at-risk persons through clearance and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE).
The Clearance component includes the bellow key activities:
- Technical and non-technical surveys
- Application of the Land Release Process
- Marking & Demarcation
- Demolition work
Non-Technical Survey Teams (NTS) collect data in order to identify and mark potential EO and clearance teams (TS) render safe and dispose of the EO contamination. Prioritization is in a normal context decided upon by the National Mine Action (Controlling) Authority (NMAA). Currently, in the absence of the NMAA, the organization Clearance capacity is self prioritizing, based on communal needs and then self- tasking. Currently, the organization implements clearance activities in the Ar-Raqqa governate and will shortly expand to Deir Ezzor if the context allows.
The EORE component includes three key activities as follows:
1. EORE Sessions’ aim that the at-risks persons, living in contaminated areas, have the necessary information to be able to adopt safe behaviors and reduce the threats posed by the presence of explosive hazards. Based on context, access and needs analysis, this is conducted either through direct EORE sessions at households’ level, public information activities (indirect EORE activities) or through Training of Trainers to strengthen the skills of key local actors and ensure the proper dissemination of the messages through a sustainable approach.
2. Contamination Impact Surveys (CIS) which aim to collect information to identify the level and type of EO contamination, the areas identified as hazardous and the need in the community.
3. Accident and Victim surveys which aim to identify the areas in which the accident occurred, and the type of explosive device that is suspected to be the cause of the accident. An accident could result in one or more victims or even no victims at all.
During the provision of EORE activities, people who need multi-disciplinary rehabilitation services are internally referred for further assessment. In addition, safety briefings are provided to humanitarian actors (local and international NGOs) and for public and private sectors to raise their awareness within the humanitarian community about risks and the consequences of explosive hazards and to minimize the risk of accidents among staff and aid workers.
Finally, the organization's advocacy team continues to lobby for the ban of the use of Explosive Weapons in Populated Areas and produce then disseminate evidence-based advocacy factsheets to mobilize and influence local, national, regional and international stakeholders by raising key humanitarian and protection issues for populations and humanitarian actors in Syria.

4 - Needs

10.3 million men, women, and children are at risk from explosive contamination in Syria (HNO 2021). One in three communities is potentially contaminated by explosive ordnance (EO). Agricultural lands and roads are most commonly reported contaminated land and farming/herding is the most common activity. The location and nature of activities related to incident explains why men and boy are reported as being more exposed to explosive threats due to existing gender roles. The threat posed by EO continues to be major protection concern, as demonstrated by key informants indicating presence of EO and/or knowing someone who was injured or killed by explosive incidents. Contamination by EO poses threats to safety of civilians, exacerbates vulnerabilities and hampers safe access to humanitarian support, and prevent safe returns. The latest available indicates that 35% of direct victims have been killed and 65% are survivors with a high number of survivors facing loss of body part or function (UNMAS 2020). Low level of knowledge on risk is also confirmed: 76% of interviewed reported to have not participated to EORE awareness in the previous 3 months (HNO 2021). Victims face many barriers to access assistance and services, knowing most needed services are related to economic support, medical care, prosthetics services and provision of assistive products. The main barriers identified are the unavailability of qualified services and medical staff, distance and costs of services.

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention will count on teams of 4 EORE agents in North West (3 teams in Aleppo and 4 teams in Idlib) and teams of 2 EORE agents in NES (8 teams in Raqqa, 8 teams in Deir ez Zoor and 4 teams in Hasakeh). The EORE team’s intervention starts at community level by gathering relevant information on EOs related accidents, victims, and presence of contamination trough Contamination Impact Survey. The EORE field teams will be able to identify record and refer EO victims to appropriate services. They will then conducted EORE awareness sessions, either through direct individual or group sessions, public information activities or training of community volunteers. All activities are inclusive to Disability, Gender and Age (DGA). Persons with disabilities will participate through conducting activities, or identification of activities linked to barriers accessing information. All IEC materials will be easy-to-understand and culturally appropriate. Also, conduct analysis on its internal and secondary data, share relevant data with actors, and produce evidence-based advocacy factsheets highlighting protection concerns on use of explosive weapons. In Raqqa, EORE teams will also conduct Non-Technical Surveys to gather and analyze data on suspected and confirmed hazardous areas and EOs to support prioritization for clearance. 3 Mine Action teams of 8 will then ensure spot tasks and mine field clearance and 1 team of 5 will ensure EO destruction.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Protection of Field staff and partners: keeping low visibility to avoid risk to partners, staff and beneficiaries, the INGO does not conduct MA activities in GoS areas. •Clearance representing a highly at-risk activity for the staff, close monitoring on SOPs compliance trough QAQC is determinant.•Conflict sensitivity approach. The INGO will conduct prioritization assessment of land/property or areas to be cleared based on: local priority, land/property ownership, contamination evidence and density. •Facilities safe closure and protecting data. Field staff trained on safe withdrawal/evacuation including ways to protect and/or destroy data. •Applying PSEA policy for staff, partners and suppliers. •Presence of explosive hazards limiting access and posing a threat to beneficiaries, mitigated by delivery of risk education training to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat; include first aid component in RE sessions and TOT level. •Creation of beneficiaries wrong expectations, who could wrongly attribute to risk education teams ability of removing EO, this is mitigated by clear explanation of intervention and objectives.•Not reaching all vulnerable groups: mitigated by involvement of community focal points and inclusion team in planning, adaptation and delivery of inclusive RE sessions •Lack of information on accessibility to services or condition of service facilities mitigated by collection of information from health sector and stakeholders.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The INGO expresses its determination to combat the sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment of children and vulnerable adults. Also implement measures to reduce risks on programs. •Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: PSEAH and Child Protection policies and Code of Conduct is effective; Mandatory and refresher PSEAH training and other safeguarding policies for staff and partners; reporting mechanism is effective •New employee is not aware of policy: all candidates seeking employment shall undergo recruitment process and submit background checks. Staff signing permanent or fixed-term employment contract, accompanying family members, trustees, consultants, interns, volunteers shall be informed of PSEAH policy. •Beneficiaries not aware of rights of services: information session will be provided for beneficiaries, as well as complaint number and process. Country Manager is responsible for information on policy and practice, and for monitoring matters related to abuse within the INGO network.•Sensitive cultural and beneficiaries refuse services of opposite genders: To address cultural sensitive and risks with GBV and CP, INGO will ensure gender balance of staff, with the aim of increasing number of female staff. •Partners are involved in actions of abuse or harassment against beneficiaries: Any proven allegations of such practices shall result in follow-up of the case and termination of agreement unless the partner commits to and effectively ensures a radical change in behavior.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In NES and NWS: •Loss of access and border closure mitigated as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted. •Lack of acceptance mitigated through positive relations with the community. The INGO and its partners make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation. •Loss of funding is mitigated by close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding. •The available budget reduction due to exchange rate loss, managed by continuous use of USD value for money transfer as well as, close monitoring the local market flow. •Spread-out of COVD-19. To mitigate this risk: i)use alternatives remote modalities ii) ensuring PPE and hygiene items are available;iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging in activities; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation. Specific to NES :•Inability or delay to get HMA supply: anticipate needs, coordination inter-HMA NGO and with Coalition forces. Enhancing networking in KRI and NES for supply through Semalka or Al Waleed BCP. •Border closure impact the HMA operation as, in the absence of medical evacuation, capacity clearance operation cannot be carried on. •Authority interferes in HMA activities: No agreement on the taxation policy and request of suspension of activities. Advocacy effort supported by NES Forum, HMA INGO, donors and international actors.
