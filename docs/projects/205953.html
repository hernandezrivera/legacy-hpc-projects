Project Summary

CIDO proposes a 12-month integrated emergency health response in Akobo & Nyirol Counties of Jonglei and Twic of Warrap States. The overall objective is to accelerate comprehensive integrated emergency health response to meet the increasing needs of the most vulnerable populations including Returnees, IDP, PWD, women, men, and children while improving equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV issues to crisis affected populations through 4 health mobile clinics and 2 static health facilities (i.e. 1 PHCC & 1 PHCU). The Response targets a total of 60,442 people (17,980 men; 14,050 women; 13,780 boys & 13,782 girls) including PWD costing 1,200,000 USD (CPB=19.9$). The key considerations underpinning the design of this response are the choice of locations/payams where there are existing gaps in current programming, mindful of a targeting criterion that is responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable groups as recommended by the cluster with consideration of integration with other clusters such as WASH, Protection, Nutrition and Food Security & Livelihood while ensuring effective coordination with health cluster and Ministry of Health at all levels i.e. national, State and County to maximize efficiency and impact. The focus will be on provision of basic essential health services as per the national standards centered on the context epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. This will ensure prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation of activities responding to people in need, with attention to access for vulnerable population such as women, children as well as people with special needs.
The response aligns with Health Cluster Strategic objectives as follows; CO1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations CO2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases CO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Specifically, the response will deploy, 4 mobile health clinics as follows: -2 Mobile Clinics i.e. 1 in Akobo East and 1 in Akobo West, 1 in Nyirol while 1 in Twic counties and 2 Static health facilities (1 PHCC and 1 PHCU both in Akobo East, West). The communities and existing development health program along with the CHD will be engaged in the planning and organization of emergency health services to be entrenched in the cluster coordination mechanism both at national, State and sub national level to enable other partners and authorities support in the implementation, monitoring and review the response activities.
The response will deploy qualified health workers to improve quality health care services through capacity building of CHDs and health workers, Procurement of medical supplies, use of EWARS and weekly IDSR system, use of the HMIS & other internally improvised tools to ensure the quality & consistency of data. The Complaints Feedback/Response Mechanism desks will be set up to enable project beneficiaries provide feedback by installing CRFM box in strategic places such as CIDO offices, 2 static health facility’s and 4 mobile clinics within the response catchment to facilitate Communication and Community engagement. Therefore, consideration of this response will optimize CIDO’s 9 years’ experience to provide humanitarian services and technical expertise in providing humanitarian services and exploit its capacity to ensure ultimate objective of coordinated response that saves life

1. Needs

South Sudan faces challenging humanitarian context in which peace and security remain fragile. The Sudan crisis, Influx of returnee’s, Intercommunal violence, food insecurity, and the severe impact of climate change present enormous challenges. The 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) estimates that 9.4 million people in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian assistance. Evidently, low overall general service availability score of 30.4%, with health infrastructure and service utilization indices at 43.2% and 15.05%, respectively per the WHO report 2021 is on record. Shortage of human resources and skills, referral constraints, financial constraints, challenges with availability and last-mile distribution of supplies, floods, and insecurity characterizes the weak health system at both facility and community levels translating to child mortality rate of 78 deaths per 1,000 live births. 75 per cent of all child deaths within are due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhea, malaria and pneumonia. Akobo, Nyirol & Twic counties apparently aren’t left out in the current statics indicating that the country has a maternal mortality ratio of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births – one of the world's highest maternal mortality ratios (MMR) (MMEIG, 2022.In 2021, an estimated 31,000 children died before turning five years old, out of which 20,000 died before turning 1(UNIGME, 2022). These absolute figures translate into under-five, infant, and neonatal mortality rates of 99, 64, and 40 deaths per 1000 live births in 2021.The estimated 40% of the under-five deaths occurring during the first month of life reflects the high risks associated with delivery and the immediate post-delivery period for both the mothers and the new-borns. Low birthweight, pre-term or small for gestational age, birth asphyxia, perinatal and neonatal infections. Estimates from WHO indicate that low birth weight contributes 5% of total deaths in South Sudan, reflecting an age-adjusted death rate of 23 per 100,000 populations and putting the country at number 18 globally. Malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia are responsible for over 75% of deaths among under-fives, the burden for malaria is over 50% and is a major contributor to the high burden of malnutrition among children in the country. The prevalence of wasting among young children in South Sudan (16.2 per cent) simultaneously, a recent smart survey in Akobo by SCI-June 2023 cites a GAM prevalence of 27.9% above the World Health Organization (WHO) emergency thresholds (15 per cent) attributed to poor dietary intake, household food insecurity, inadequate feeding and caring practices, high prevalence of common childhood illnesses, poor access to and inadequate utilization of both preventive and curative basic health, nutrition, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), and protection services watering down efforts provided by health and Nutrition partners within the response catchment location. Further, ISNA (2922 Report) affirms that the country has the worst food insecurity emergencies in the world, with 75 per cent of the returnee households reported food as a priority need while mental health and psychosocial needs amongst displaced communities, including returnees remain elevated and largely unaddressed. Notably, in the ongoing health response by CIDO (November 2022- April 2024) in Akobo - Malaria, AWD and RTI are the leading morbidities hence need for accelerated efforts to address the trend. As we speak, influx of returnees coupled with deadly measles cases in Akobo are on the rise as already 4 deaths have been reported with 58 suspected cases recorded by the end of October 2023(Akobo – CHD). CIDO expects to use its static presence in Akobo, Nyirol and Twic Counties and 9 years’ experience to provide humanitarian services and technical expertise in providing humanitarian services and exploit its capacity to ensure ultimate objective of coordinated response that saves life

2. Project activities

The following activities will be undertaken through the response period: - Conduct OPD consultation, Conduct Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities, Clinical Management of Rape, Ensure community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities, Carry out Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response, Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population. Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, Coordinate with WASH clusters for support on rehabilitation of broken down boreholes & toilets alongside WASH related supplies to CIDO health facilities, Refer SAM complicated and MAM cases to OTP and TSFP sites for nutrition services, while encouraging both Nutrition and FSL to support Kitchen garden farms within health facilities as well as work with protection clusters on protection related matters by ensuring psychosocial support desks are established within the health facilities to address GBV issues
