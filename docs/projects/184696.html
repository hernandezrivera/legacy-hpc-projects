Project Summary

Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH), and national NGO (IPs) in Somalia seek to contribute to improving accessibility to emergency life-saving WASH assistance to the Somali population affected by environmental disasters and protracted crisis, exacerbated by COVID-19 outbreak. This project targets 18,000 most vulnerable IDPs and host communities facing acute water shortage, at risk of AWD/cholera and enhances preparedness and mitigation of risks that have deep impact on the lives of people and their livelihoods. The planned interventions include preparedness, response and recovery activities focusing on strengthening age, gender and diversity, considering the needs, priorities, and capacities of girls, boys, women, and men and people living with disabilities (PLWDs) within COVID-19 constraints. It will also include distributing menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits to vulnerable primary school girls. The planned interventions are rehabilitation and improvement of available water sources; and provision of water to the vulnerable populations during an emergency through water trucking, rehabilitation, or construction of sanitation targeting communities without any access and promotion of safe hygiene practices, including awareness on COVID-19. DKH strategies will include use of Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) whenever feasible to target the affected populations. Retailers will be identified, interviewed and pooled among retailers that accept payment through vouchers and will be contracted based on their financial capacity, for the redemption of restricted vouchers and ensure an unlimited supply of water before getting reimbursed and adequate quantity within 24 hours of notice. DKH and IPs will use existing vulnerability criteria for unconditional multipurpose cash through IPC, and cash-for-work/assets to identify the project beneficiaries for the vouchers at household (HH) level and using a community approach. The vouchers will be handed to the beneficiaries personally and cash transferred through mobile money. A PDM will be done and recorded per distribution for compliance and utilization, and feedback and complaint mechanisms installed for accountability. Unconditional and restricted water vouchers will be provided to the affected HHs during draughts, ensuring water access for survival by the HHs from the private suppliers and their economic structures, while enhancing their purchasing powers. DKH will facilitate establishment and training of WASH committees (WMCs) on operations and maintenance of the water points after rehabilitation for sustainability. DKH approach integrates hygiene promotion into all water and sanitation activities. The design and delivery of the WASH facilities and services will consider the needs of PLWDs to ensure their safe access. The project targets cleaning of congested IDP camps using voucher for work, while desludging of latrines will benefit from small-scale enterprises having equipment able to access the latrines. Solid waste in IDP camps shall be collected and disposed or recycled at designated places while liquid wastes will also be disposed in designated channels. In case of floods of the Shabelle River, DKH’s IP, SYPD, will respond through distribution of treated and tested water from the river to displaced people in Beletweyn and Jowhar where two EmWatKits are prepositioned, and at the same time provide emergency latrines to the affected populations. DKH will also support construction and rehabilitation of water storage facilities and collection structures like water pans, sand dams, shallow wells to assist communities to prepare and mitigating climate-induced shocks. Sensitization on safety of water at HH level, including storage containers and random water quality testing with DKH's own water testing kit, will also be conducted. The project incorporates protection issues for women and girls in informal camps through installation of solar lights on the way to toilets and inside the toilets

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project prefers the use of cash and voucher assistance as it provides vulnerable households with a source of income through CVA to enable them access safe WASH services during emergencies, in addition to enabling them to make choices while at the same time building the local economies given the constrained livelihood situations in the context. CVA are conditional to building community assets that enhance recovery and reduce the effects of drought and other natural disasters.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. DKH consider the use of MISMA and EMMA tools to enable a detailed market analysis to support the market systems and to contribute to improving response analysis and programme implementation; and to better understand, accommodate, and make use of market systems. The use of these tools will therefore support consideration of market functions in the cash decision. DKH will also conduct PDM and market systems monitoring system which will also be used to monitor the effects of the cash distributed into the local market. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect on the market functionality
