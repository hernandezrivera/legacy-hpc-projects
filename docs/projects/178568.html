Project Summary

This project aims to provide integrated nutrition, health, child protection, and hygiene promotion services through the activation of 14 rapid response teams (RRTs). It will cover 144,901 people, disaggregated as follows:
14,453 PLW, 36,042 (CU 5) Children under 5.
The services shall be delivered at 19 communities (3 in Dana sub-district, 2 in Qourqeena sub-district, 1 in Jandairis sub-district, 1 in Afrin sub-district, 6 in Sharan sub-district, 2 in Atareb sub-district, 1 in Ariha sub-district, and 4 in Mhambal sub-district).
Activities are:
1- CMAM & IYCF programs:
- Screening, treatment, referral and follow up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished
- Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women
- Disseminate messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices,
- Monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes
- Support health/nutrition education and promotion through IEC materials in line with cluster recommendations during COVID 19 pandemic,
- Nutrition Supplements distribution
- Integrating nutrition supplementation with other routine child and antenatal services.
The proposed intervention will be integrated with other services provided by other sectors as follows:
Integration with Health, Wash, and Child Protection Services through:
- Screen, referral CU5 & PLWs with dangers signs and Medical problems
- Referral of medical cases to the nearby health facilities.
- Support hygiene promotion.
- Rising Wash awareness by providing Key messages (Hand Washing, Food Processing,).
- Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against child U5 and PLWs during Nutrition screening.
This project will be managed and run with full restriction to COVID-19 protection measures, for exam the beneficiaries using the checklist agreed by the health cluster task force and measurement the fever identify the suspected cases of COVID-19 and to inform the EWARN team about the suspected cases.
These integrations between services will help to create and provide safe and more secure environments for children and their families.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 13.4 M people in need. 4.9 M pregnant and lactating women and children in need of life-saving nutrition intervention, of which 3.7 million are in acute need. If urgent needs are not addressed, malnutrition will continue increasing across Syria and will have devastating, long-term and intergenerational impacts. Chronic malnutrition remains one of the major public health and development concerns in Syria, where annually, more than 0.6 Million Syrian children lose their future development and learning potentials because of stunting. This is strongly linked with poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and maternal malnutrition, high levels of anaemia. In North-west Syria, 11 per cent of mothers are malnourished, and it is worse among displaced. poor nutrition situation is largely attributed to the continued exposure of children and mothers to unsafe living conditions, diseases (including COVID-19), suboptimal IYCF practices, high maternal malnutrition, economic downturn, low purchasing power, poverty, food insecurity, family separations and poor care practices, early marriages, and destruction of infrastructure causing limited access to health services and depletion of skilled human resources, and inadequate sanitation and water supply high risks during pregnancy, and childbirth complications. Malnutrition in all its forms, requires immediate, comprehensive, and multi-sectoral services to fully address the nutritional needs of PLW and CU5 in Syria. According to population figures, in August 2020, the project population is 144901 (IDPs 57%) and they are a high priority according to SCHF 2021 First Standard Allocation Paper.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection risk 1: Child labor. Mitigating measures: IHR will be sure that the staff working in this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding, Child protection, and GBV principles. Protection risk 2: Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs in the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: 1- Availability of wheelchair for PWD at the reception point, trained staff to deal with PWDs, Priority for the PWDs, included in the criteria, House to house distributions that will ensure that those with special needs that can’t reach the distribution points can reach the assistance. Protection Risk 3: Sometimes, crowding during the distribution of bread, may not provide the services at the desired quality, which might affect the BNFs dignity, especially women. Mitigating measures: Set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations Raise the awareness of community leaders on GBV and child protection principles and make sure that all BNFs can voice their concerns through the designated channels. Protection Risk 4: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation for women during the implementation of the project. Mitigating measures: Choosing appropriate time for distributions of bread and receiving of the wheat crop, voucher exchanging in Hawala agent will taking place during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting, conducting awareness sessions for women about issues surrounding with GBV to minimiz damag
