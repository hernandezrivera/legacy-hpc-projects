Project Summary

Since 2007, JAAGO has been conducting online education with an aim to provide free of cost education to the underprivileged children with the synchronous and blended teaching-learning approach using Audio-Visual aids. Children living in the Rohingya camps and adjacent host communities have limited access to education, social messages, and the importance of schooling. The JAAGO Foundation has the necessary resources and expertise to introduce online schooling education in Rohingya camps and host community schools.
A total of 660 Rohingya individuals including 396 females and 264 males from 4 and 24 years of age will receive quality online education through Learning Centres (LC) and Tablet devices. JAAGO aims at establishing 5 LCs educating 330 individuals from strategically selected camp 22, where the organization has already been working to ensure psychosocial support through ‘Safe Haven’. Five online teachers will be trained on The Learning Competency Framework Approach (LCFA) and the Sector Partners will provide technical support for the delivery of effective training. Additionally, 10 Rohingya volunteers will be guided on classroom management.
JAAGO will digitize 3 classrooms from 3 government primary schools in WhyKong Teknaf Upazila where 6 teachers will be given capacity development training on the necessary skills required. A total of 600 Host community individuals comprising 360 females and 240 males aged between 4 and 24 years old will benefit from receiving inclusive education.
The local actors from the community will support to identify the children who do not have access to education. The community members will be the primary channels for feedback on hygiene practices. Furthermore, JAAGO will conduct Rohingya and Host community awareness on the importance of schooling, COVID-19 prevention, and social harmony.
After project completion, a survey will be conducted among project beneficiaries to evaluate their satisfaction level on quality education, hygienic aids, and social awareness messages.

1 - Identified Needs

GAM (Gender with Age Marker) Report (2020) pinpointed lack of access to education for individuals aged between 3 and 24 is a huge concern for both the Rohingya and the host communities. According to JRP 2021 Education Sector, a total of 9967 Rohingya individuals where 4900 boys and 5067 girls need access to education aged between 3 and 24 in camp 22. The Education Needs Assessment (ENA) Report 2019 states boys and girls between 15 years and beyond in the Rohingya community have not attended LCs where the proportion of girls has been at a minimal rate. 15-18 years of students were reported taking private lessons at home. Of the 80% of households with children who attended any form of learning, of whom at least one child continued studying remotely, 43% stated they do not attend LCs due to lack of learning materials as per the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) Factsheet, 2020. The report further points out despite the learning centers remaining closed, 86% of children continued studying remotely. However, 14% of households decided not to send their children again back to learning centers and 12% responded that the families are more dependent on their children for carrying out daily activities. According to Rapid Gender Analysis (RGA, 2020), 34 percent of girls aged between 12 and 18 years attended LCs last year, compared to 67 percent of boys that age. Only 17 percent of adolescent girls reported attending home-based learning on a typical day, compared to 36 percent of adolescent boys, and only 4 percent reported going to an adolescent-friendly space compared to 28 percent of adolescent boys.The RGA report also stated that women are excluded from decision-making systems in the community, and their lack of access to reliable information, consultations, and feedback mechanisms limits their ability to influence both prevention and response plans. In the refugee camps, women and girls exposed disproportionately to gender-based violence like child marriage, women and girls trafficking, sexual exploitation, assault and violence, and all sorts of harassment (GAM, 2020). RGA (2020) report demonstrated that only 41 percent of adolescent girls (aged 15 to 20) reported being married, compared to 20 percent of adolescent boys of the same age. Moreover, 94 percent of 19 caregivers and 85 percent of adolescents reported that parents could discipline children by resorting to violence.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: A qualified Bengali teacher will take two-shift classes per LC with four hours of class duration, a total of 80 students attending per LC. The teacher will conduct classes via live video conferencing technology and 1 Rohingya volunteer will facilitate online classes and teach the Burmese language. For Camp 22, JAAGO will build 5 LCs for 330 individuals,198 females and 132 males aged between 4 and 24. For the 5 LCs, 3 online teachers and 5 Rohingya volunteers will be assigned. 5 Rohingya night guards will ensure the security of JAAGO’s 5 established LCs. In the case of natural disasters, JAAGO will rebuild the affected LCs. JAAGO will prepare health protocols for emergency situations on 5 LC’s for 330 individuals. To educate 330 individuals( adolescent girls, differently-abled, and special needs learners) in the camps, 165 Tablet devices will be distributed and 2 online teachers and 5 Rohingya volunteers will educate them. Each Tablet will support 2 learners. To reach 600 HC learners,360 females & 240 males aged between 4 and 24 years, 3 classrooms from 3 different Community Government Primary Schools (GPS) will be digitized from WhyKong Teknaf Upazila. A Project Coordinator, Monitoring and Evaluation Officer, Academic Coordinator, IT and field Officer, two Support Staff, and 5-night guards will be recruited to oversee the project activities. Activity 2: JAAGO will train project staff on inclusive and gender-sensitive education, Rohingya Safeguarding policy, Case Management, Human Rights, and Education in Emergencies guidelines. JAAGO’s R&D team will train 5 online teachers on LCFA curriculum and the Sector Partners will provide technical support for effective training. 10 volunteers to be trained on online teaching techniques, classroom management & covid safety measures. 6 HC GPS teachers to be trained on online teaching techniques, computer literacy, Pedagogy, Content digitization, Blended teaching-learning approach, interpersonal skill, MS Office. The cleaning staff from the LCs and HC school will be trained in ensuring disinfection and be equipped with personal protection equipment. Activity 3: JAAGO will apply synchronous and blended teaching-learning approach through interactive live video conferencing software. Screenshot of the class participants will monitor the student's attendance. Student grade-level competency will be evaluated based on test scores. The teachers will follow up with students and their parents over phone twice a month to update the child’s monthly progress. Activity 4: A baseline survey will be conducted to identify the out of school children, the needed LCs in Camp 22. To strengthen the community's resilience, JAAGO will engage local actors to clean and disinfect the LCs. Also, JAAGO will involve these actors in decision making & on accessible, gender & age-friendly feedback mechanisms Activity 5: With the help of Rohingya Volunteers JAAGO will prepare and disseminate age-friendly, inclusive awareness messages to parents, caretakers, religious leaders & Majlis on the importance of education, ending early child marriage, protecting women and girls trafficking, eliminating sexual exploitation, frequent hand washing, social distancing and advising elderly people to stay indoors. Activity 6: The 1260 beneficiaries comprising the Rohingya and HC individuals along with the community members will interchangeably disseminate information to the alternate community regarding social harmony, cleanliness and safety measures. Activity 7: JAAGO will closely work and coordinate with 5 sector partners to capacitate on inclusive and gender-sensitive education practices. Additionally, the existing online teaching capacity of JAAGO can create a scope for other partner organizations' teacher’s training. Activity 8: JAAGO will conduct an online or face to face survey after the project among parents, students, and community members to measure their level of satisfaction and changed perception.
