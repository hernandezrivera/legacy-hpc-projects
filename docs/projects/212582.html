Project Summary

Based on ISIMM Jan/24, CCCM database includes 1,556 IDP sites (83.4% are self-settled meaning they often lack camp management (12%), site planning, infrastructure and services) with inadequate access to shelter, safe water, food, health and psycho-social support and hosts 2 million IDPs or 377,000 HHs, 79.5% are women & children, and 91,187 PwDs. According to ISIMM plus data, 88% of IDPs in NWS are living in IDP sites that need camp management. There’s a need to improve living conditions to uphold humanitarian standards through regular care and maintenance. In NWS, 70% of IDPs are living in sites with a critical level of population density. IDP sites are particularly vulnerable to incidents, like floods, fires and high-speed winds
From Jan- Dec 2023, CCCM in NWS reported 821 incidents that led to 200 injuries, 25 deaths, and the damage/ destruction of 9,508 tents. Winter creates additional risks of incidents in IDP sites. Fire risks are intersected with the severe economic situation of IDPs. These risks are coupled with lack of space and services, and hazardous electrical wiring. Only 20% of IDPs are living in sites where there is no gap in the availability of fire points. CCCM found that Only 20% of IDPs sites in NWS equipped with fire mitigation measures, and almost one million IDPs under the fire incidents risk.
Likewise, critical gaps in infrastructure in IDP sites exacerbate the likelihood and impact of floods.
Furthermore, 35% of IDPs lack access to livelihood opportunities, deepening their hardship and resilience. Urgent interventions are imperative to enhance living conditions, mitigate risks, and address the multisectoral needs of IDPs in NWS.
The project seeks to align with sectors objectives represented by the coordination of lifesaving assistance in IDP sites and strengthening basic infrastructure through the related activities including the need assessments by conducting FGD sessions with camp leaders and representatives to draw the service map, this also is supported by establishment of services related committees (WASH, Health, Education…) to allow better assessment and coordination for these services in IDP sites to support camp management. Community centers may also established for mobilization and information sharing and to conduct different sessions.
Furthermore, the project includes fire mitigation activities (monthly awareness sessions, training fire committees) and facilities (fire points, fire equipment) in IDP sites, this also includes the site improvements and maintenance to the physical infrastructures.
Another key aspect of the project is enhancing the quality and accountability of camp management through providing the necessary training for the community leaders and representatives to build their capacity and to enhance their ability for management and related tools, along with awareness sessions/campaigns on cross-cutting issues (PSEA, AAP, Protection mainstreaming.) for camp residents through periodic sessions by qualified staff providing definitions, importance and procedures for dealing with each issue.
Moreover, the project aims to strengthen household and communal coping mechanisms in camp and camp-like settings through two main activities, the first one is community-led integration and skills-building activities for different groups by providing training then start-up grants to initiate skilled base projects contributing in beneficiaries strengthening and resilience, the second activity is represented by the Implementation of tailored livelihood activities designed to enable HHs to restore their assets and build resilience for better solutions.
Lastly, the project conducts site profiling to identify and update needs, facilitating better advocacy by pinpointing gaps, available services, facilities, and up-to-date statistics on IDPs in each site.
In summary, the project will target a total of 50 IDP site (85400 beneficiaries) with the mentioned activities aligning with cluster objectives.
