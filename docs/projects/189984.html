Project Summary

CARE will restore livelihoods for IDPs and host communities in Nampula and Cabo Delgado through the provision of agricultural inputs and training on enhanced climate-resilient agricultural practices. Households without access to land will receive vocational skill training and receive non-farm livelihood inputs.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project proposes to improve the self-reliance and self-sufficiency of Internally Displaced People (IDPs) and vulnerable host community households in Metuge (9213 people), Montepuez (9213 people), Meconta (18,425 people) and Malema (15,000 people) districts to recover their livelihoods. CARE will begin the project with a Rapid Participatory Livelihood Assessment to identify the most appropriate livelihood restoration intervention for target IDP households. All planned livelihood IGA support that takes into consideration gendered household roles and promotes women’s economic empowerment. Considering the context, CARE anticipates livelihood support likely focusing on assisting IDPs to increase agricultural production. Based on assessment results, CARE will reach a total of 51,851 beneficiaries to restore and/or develop gender-sensitive, climate-smart agriculture-based and non-agriculture-based livelihoods. CARE will target IDPs with access to land for agricultural-based livelihood support and provide non-agriculture-based livelihood support to those without access to land. Agricultural-based livelihood support includes the provision of agricultural tools and inputs (such as climate-resistant seeds) for one agricultural campaign and of training on climate-resilient agricultural practices. Non-agricultural livelihood support includes vocational training on IGAs relevant to the local market and the beneficiaries past experience, based on the findings of the assessment. CARE will prioritize livelihood IGA support that takes into consideration gendered household roles and promotes women’s economic empowerment.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Activities: 1) Conduct Rapid Participatory Livelihood Assessment to identify most appropriate livelihood restoration activities for target group; 2) Distribute agricultural tools (hoe, rakes, spade) and seeds (seed package depend on outcome of assessment however sample package could include maize, cowpea and sesame) to beneficiaries with access to land to begin cultivating relevant agricultural products for both self-consumption and sale; 3) Provide training on enhanced climate-resilient agricultural practices that can increase yield and improve climate resilience, providing general guidance on improved farming techniques including the importance of using organic manure and how to prepare it; the advantages and disadvantages of row planting; weeding and weed control; and harvest and post-harvest storage techniques, etc.; 4) Distribute non-farm livelihood inputs (“start-up kits”) to livelihood groups (with 10 members each who do not have access to land) to support income-generating activities; Selection of livelihood dependent on assessment outcomes but examples include baking good, hairdressing, re-sell of salt, etc.; 5) Provide vocational/livelihood trainings livelihood groups who received ‘start-up kits’ (activity 4) and individuals with no access to land; trainings will be facilitated by trainers with relevant experience, in partnership with CARE; 6) Incorporate awareness raising on gender equality and inclusive household economic decision-making into livelihood trainings (addressing topics such as traditional roles and responsibilities of women and men, girls and boys in the household, advantages of more equitable decision making and economic power, equitable division of domestic labour, etc).Target locations: Metuge, Montepuez, Meconta and Malema districts.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle. This begins at the beginning of the project cycle, with project design being informed by various analyses conducted by CARE including a recent Conflict-Sensitive Gender Analysis and the livelihood assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure a relevant and appropriate response and identify potential risks. CARE will regularly collect data through our Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), as well as complement them with secondary data, to best examine how our intervention may interact with the conflict and social context. CAREs 4 FAM entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project focal point during activities. The results will inform any adaptation of activities to prevent negative impacts. Finally, CARE has robust safeguarding policy in place, with accountability holders throughout the organizations, ensuring safety and dignity are prioritized. In the proposed project, CARE is prioritizing livelihood support for women and girls (65% of beneficiaries) but is reaching both sexes with awareness raising on on gender equality and inclusive household economic decision during livelihood activities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

To ensure accountability to affected people and communities, CARE will utilize its established Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAM). CARE will encourage the use of its established FAMs through verbal messaging and IEC material during project activities and especially at WGFSs. The FAMs allows all groups including men, women, boys and girls of different ages, illiteracy abilities and other vulnerabilities to provide feedback at different points during implementation. The 4 FAMs entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project staff at distribution exit points. CARE will be using all 4 channels noted above but will consider any communicated preference by the target population. The availability of these CFMs ensures at least one accessible option for all. For those who cannot access the hotline or are illiterate, PDM and direct communication with CARE staff provide an appropriate option. CARE has conducted need assessments in the target districts, which includes participants of different ages, sex, and ability; this informs CARE’s planned interventions in 2022.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the assessment planned at the beginning of the project, CARE will be able to identify and assess the needs of People with Disabilities (PwDs) as well. This will allow CARE to consult PwDs to determine how assistance can be delivered in an appropriate and accessible manner. During distributions, CARE will accommodate PwDs to remove barriers to assistance. This includes a bypass system for the queue, registering an “alternative collector” to pick-up items, adapting the distribution site where possible to ensure they’re safe (e.g., removing tripping hazards, etc.) and setting up a ‘buddy’ system for PWDs to access and have support in the livelihood activities. Staff conducting the livelihood trainings will apply an inclusive approach which motivates and build confidence of PwDs. Training content can also be adapted to disability type (e.g., use of raised soil beds). When incorporating gender equality and protection messaging into livelihood activities, CARE will highlight the specific protection risks PwDs face and how they can be responded to and prevented. Finally, CARE having Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), information dissemination and awareness raising will come in a variety of formats, to account for different disabilities (radio, use of images, speakerphones, etc.).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Overall, the project will have a minimal impact on the environment. As this project has a material support element through dignity kits, CARE will make smart procurement decision to reduce the environmental impact of humanitarian operations and keep packaging at a minimum (or have it been recyclable/reusable). In CAREs programming, aid packaging waste has had minimal environmental impact as nearly all materials/packaging will be re-used by beneficiaries in some way. For example, items such as sacs distributed alongside with seeds/tools are used by community members to sell charcoal and other goods. For aid packaging, plastics straps can be used to secure tarpaulin in shelter and plastic bags have been used to transport and plant seedlings and wrap food in, such as cereals and beans. Resourcefulness of the recipients of aid and CAREs own reuse of material/packaging in its own warehouse ensures resource efficiency. Finally, a climate-smart approach will be applied in trainings, including different techniques such as vegetal covering, crop association, direct planting and use of pest and drought resistant varieties and botanical pesticides such as repellant plants (removes need for synthetic pesticide and reduces GHGs).
