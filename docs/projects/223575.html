Project Summary

The Concern on Climate Change for the Community Initiative (FOURCi) will implement this project to enhance food security and agricultural resilience in Damboa L.G.A of Borno State and Madagali L.G.A of Adamawa State. The initiative will focus on providing food assistance through Agricultural Resilience Support by delivering livelihood inputs, assets, or community-based productive asset creation/enhancement activities using appropriate modalities such as in-kind support, vouchers, cash, or multi-modal approaches.
FOURCi will integrate innovative and locally acceptable approaches to food production and resilience-building, contributing significantly to the physical and mental well-being of internally displaced persons (IDPs). The project aims to address the root causes of food insecurity and promote sustainable agricultural practices by building resilience and supporting agro-based income-generating activities.
Leveraging its extensive experience in food security and previous projects, including wet feeding programs, FOURCi will design a tailored response to meet the specific needs of affected communities. This response will integrate agricultural resilience with innovative food production techniques that align with the local context and priorities.
Recognizing the complexities of northeastern Nigeria, the project will adopt a participatory planning approach. FOURCi, in collaboration with the food security sector, will engage affected communities from the outset through planning sessions. This will ensure community involvement in project design and implementation, fostering ownership and integrating community-shared ideas and best practices for effective and sustainable outcomes.
By focusing on resilience-building activities, this project will empower communities to recover and thrive in the face of ongoing challenges, laying the foundation for a more secure and sustainable future.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2024 presents a synopsis of humanitarian needs across Nigeria. The analysis primarily focuses on the so-called BAY states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) in north-east Nigeria, given the magnitude and severity of needs in this region caused by a domestic armed conflict. The HNO is based on a comprehensive needs assessment and analysis carried out in BAY states, in line with Inter-agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) guidelines. Further data collection and analysis was conducted in north-west Nigeria (Sokoto, Zamfara, Katsina, Kaduna, Jiwaga, Kebbi and Kano) with an emphasis on food insecurity and malnutrition and informing the interagency Operational Response Plan (OPR) for the north-west. Likewise, an overview of needs is provided for the rest of the country based on secondary data review. Five themes were identified as the key drivers of humanitarian needs in 2024: conflict and displacement, food insecurity and malnutrition, disease outbreaks (including cholera), floods, and camp closures and involuntary relocations and resettlement. Fourteen years into the conflict, the humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria remains profound and widespread. Across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, over 7.9 million people face severe protection concerns, extreme deprivation beyond their existing poverty levels, and daily threats to their well-being. The severity and complexity of affected people’s needs have not diminished. Vulnerabilities differ based on location, age, and displacement status, and the interplay of the main drivers of the crisis (conflict and insecurity, the impact of climate change, disease outbreaks, food insecurity and malnutrition, population movement and camp closures, economic factors, and historical underdevelopment). Women and children, particularly girls, remain the most affected, comprising 83 per cent of those in need. Borno is the most affected state, where more than half its population – 3.9 million – are classified as people in need (PiN). Adamawa and Yobe states follow with a PiN at 2.1 million and 1.9 million, respectively. Food insecurity and malnutrition continue to be major concerns in the BAY states, where an estimated 4.4 million people are expected to have either crisis or emergency food needs in the lean season. Likewise, some 1.53 million children under five years old are expected to face acute malnutrition over the same period and about 511,800 children are expected to face severe acute malnutrition, a life-threatening condition. The number of displaced people, 2.1 million, remains high reflecting that people are reluctant to return to their places of origin where availability of basic services, livelihoods opportunities, and security are considered paramount. Please note that in the case of nutrition status, in 2023 for this HNO, the assessment was undertaken at an earlier time than in 2022 for the 2023 HNO. This may have impacted on the results with regards to a year-on-year comparison. The severity and magnitude of needs in Borno State have remained the same and have even worsened particularly in areas with a high concentration of IDPs, such as Bama, Damboa, Dikwa, Gwoza, Maiduguri,Monguno, and Ngala. In Adamawa and Yobe the number of people in need decreased by 10% and 6% respectively, contributing to a reduction in the overall number of people in need in 2024. This decline is found in host communities of both states. In Adamawa, this decrease is partially linked to the aftermath of the 2022 floods, which impacted over 120,000 individuals leading to an increase in the 2023 PiN estimates.In 2023, the floods were limited, coupled with some improvements in health and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) sectors, , resulting in a reduced number of people in need. In Yobe, the reduction in people in need is primarily due to improvements in the WASH and health sectors, including more effective cholera prevention measures and enhanced water supply systems.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) Nexus package aims to enhance the coherence and synergy between humanitarian, development, and peace efforts to address complex crises sustainably. Its goal is to simultaneously address immediate humanitarian needs, support long-term development, and promote peaceful coexistence to reduce vulnerabilities and prevent future conflicts. Components of the HDP Nexus PackageHumanitarian Assistance: Provides immediate relief to save lives, including food assistance, healthcare, and shelter for affected populations.Development Initiatives: Focuses on strengthening livelihoods, education, and infrastructure to build resilience and reduce dependency on aid.Peacebuilding Efforts: Promotes social cohesion, conflict resolution, and governance structures to foster sustainable peace.Key ActorsKey actors include: Humanitarian agencies (e.g., UNHCR, Save the Children)Development organizations (e.g., USAID, World Bank)Peacebuilding institutions (e.g., NRC, local governments)Community leaders and civil society organizations (e.g., FOURCi)Interaction with HDP NexusFOURCi’s project complements the HDP Nexus by integrating these components: Humanitarian: Delivering agricultural livelihood inputs to meet immediate needs.Development: Promoting innovative food production methods to ensure long-term resilience.Peacebuilding: Engaging communities in participatory planning to foster collaboration and reduce tensions.FOURCi collaborates with actors like Save the Children and NRC to align efforts and achieve shared goals under the HDP Nexus framework.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming, the project will implement the following modalities: Community Participation: Engage all groups, including women, youth, and the elderly, in planning and decision-making to ensure their needs and perspectives are prioritized. Inclusive Access: Design interventions that are accessible to vulnerable populations, such as providing livelihood inputs tailored to physical abilities and cultural considerations. Safe Spaces: Establish safe and confidential mechanisms for reporting protection concerns, ensuring that beneficiaries feel secure and supported. Capacity Building: Train staff and community members on protection principles, gender sensitivity, and age inclusivity to promote equitable implementation. Monitoring and Feedback: Integrate regular assessments and feedback channels to identify and address protection risks throughout the project lifecycle. These modalities will safeguard the dignity, rights, and safety of all beneficiaries, while reducing harm and ensuring equitable participation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, the project will implement the following strategies: Accessible Feedback Mechanisms: Establish multiple channels, such as suggestion boxes, toll-free phone lines, and community feedback desks, to accommodate various preferences and abilities. Community Engagement Sessions: Conduct targeted focus group discussions (FGDs) and one-on-one interviews with women, youth, the elderly, and persons with disabilities to gather their perspectives in a safe and inclusive environment. Anonymous Reporting: Provide opportunities for anonymous feedback to encourage openness, especially for those who may feel hesitant to speak publicly. Local Facilitators: Employ community-based facilitators who understand local languages and cultural contexts to bridge communication gaps and build trust with vulnerable groups. Regular Monitoring Visits: Include direct interactions with marginalized community members during monitoring visits to ensure their voices are heard and recorded. Feedback Integration: Ensure feedback is systematically reviewed and incorporated into project planning and implementation, with updates provided to communities on actions taken. These approaches will ensure that even the most vulnerable individuals have an active role in shaping project activities.
