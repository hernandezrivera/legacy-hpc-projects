Project Summary

The Livelihood project aims to empower female headed households, households with a disabled / elderly family member, households with infant and young children and large household and members of the community who have lost their livelihood as a result of the conflict. The project will focus on providing vocational skills training, support existing small businesses, livestock owners and small farmers, as well as supporting the rehabilitation of affected businesses.

Needs

As conflict continues into its eighth year, 13.1 million people are in need in Syria, of which 2.9 million are people with disabilities and 5.3 million are children. 6.3 million are internally displaced persons (IDPs), some 2.98 million people are in hard to reach locations (HNO). Millions of Syrians have escaped across borders, seeking safety as refugees in neighboring countries. Those displaced have been forced to abandon their homes, jobs, businesses and productive assets, limiting their ability to provide for themselves and their families, having a resulted impact on all basic needs, including food security, education, health, WASH, and shelter. Depletion and in many cases the loss of productive assists and increased debt as well as limited economic opportunities have all resulted in extreme hard living conditions and disruption of livelihoods. This has resulted in an estimated 69 per cent to be living in extreme poverty. This has led to the exhaustion of coping capacities of many Syrians, affecting the most vulnerable of the population, especially children, youth and adolescents. As a result of increased house hold debt, children and youth have dropped out of schools and there was an increased number in child labour and early marriage. In addition, men and women have faced different livelihood needs, where many women have lost their husbands, brothers and fathers and have become responsible to provide for their families. The need to empower those women and enable them with livelihood opportunities, especially vocational training, is fundamental to support their families and children. ADRA Syria will conduct vocational training courses for women, especially those in female headed households, in fields such as food processing, knitting, cooking and handicrafts…etc. With this training, they may have the option to work at home to generate income and at the same time be able to take care of their children. On the other hand, many men have suffered loss of work, job opportunities, assets, tools and work supplies, especially those who work in crafts and professions such as bakers, barbers, carpenters, electrical workers, etc. ADRA Syria will support those targeted beneficiaries with the needed supplies, kits and tools to reestablish their professions and rebuild their lives.ADRA Syria will work with local partners, NGOs and government, by offering opportunities to those in need by supporting new businesses and rebuilding lost ones. The targeted areas identified by ADRA Syria Homs, Aleppo, and Rural Damascus – are areas with a severity scale average between 4 6, with a population percentage not less than 10% IDP to host community, where freedom of movement and access to services remain limited and challenging and needs are considered most severe.

Implementation of activities

ADRA Syria will implement capacity building activities through vocational and life skills trainings, and will provide youth aged (18 24) years old particularly with entrepreneurship and startups trainings, all these trainings will be followed with a provision of a productive assets (equipment, tools and seeds; etc..) principally to the persons with a project idea, willingness and enthusiasm. Awarding the priority to the project that can benefit the entire community, generate income and create a safe and sustainable living that meets their basic needs and contributes to their empowerment.Priority will be given to female headed households, households with a disabled / elderly family member, households with infant and young children and large households of IDPs, returnees and host communities • Activity1.3: Provide vocational and skills training for men women and youth: ADRA will provide training to men women and youth in: Added value food production, knitting, and handicrafts, baking, carpentry plumbing etc. ADRA will coordinate with local stakeholders to assist in advertising for this activity. ADRA staff will be equipped in conducting these trainings, with close attention to cultural sensitive topics and humanitarian principals • Activity 1.4: Entrepreneurship supportedADRA plans to Support Entrepreneurship and startups through small grant and business specific trainings prioritizing female headed households, households with a disabled / elderly family member, households with infant and young children and large households. The grants will be provided in kind based on the requirements of each individual household taking into consideration the capacities of these selected households. ADRA Syria has the tools and techniques to monitor and evaluate the activities mid project, at the end of the project, as well as six month post implementation, to ensure that the targeted beneficiaries received the necessary knowledge and skills and to assess the impact of the training, activities, and provision of productive assets. Statistical updates and progress reports will be prepared on a regular basis, according to the project cycle, starting from needs assessment, to present ‘highlights’ of the activities together with successes and difficulties (and their proposed solutions), as well as the financial situation detailing amounts received and expenditures to date.The project and will be implemented and monitored by ADRA staff and stakeholders, taking into consideration the needs and capacities of targeted households including men and women and undertaking modifications as necessary based on the feedback received. Feedback could be received directly to male and female staff and volunteers who then pass the information to project management, or through the established anonymous complaints mechanism and hotline.

Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA Syria will ensure that the Livelihood project works closely with all stakeholders, following a ‘do no harm’ approach however, due to the nature of conflict, it is possible that some risks may occur. There is a risk that beneficiaries face hostility and discrimination due to their participation in this activity, jealousy might occur from other members of the community. Due to the later, there might be a risk that members of the community boycott these businesses. Another risk is that the newly established businesses might face low returns due to the high household debt and low cash availability, which might result in a tardy takeoff for these businesses. There is also a possibility that by supporting women they might face GBV, as well as, the kids of working mother might face discrimination in school and in the community as a result of that. In all cases outlined above, ADRA will maintain strong community connections with multiple stakeholders, clearly outlining the aim, purpose and selection criteria of the project to all concerned. Tools will be developed to ensure the impartial selection of beneficiaries and awareness raising will take place among the stakeholders, including beneficiaries themselves about their rights and ways in which to report potential abuse or discrimination. ADRA staff will be trained in protection mitigation measures and humanitarian principals and extensive monitoring will take place to identify and mitigate any potential risks.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

* Ability to reach the most vulnerable individuals as they move frequently from place to place, and sometimes back to their home areas. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: Working closely with the concerned government parties and local partners to ensure that needs assessments in the suggested areas has correct information and data regarding venerable populations, including IDPs, returnees and host community members, their numbers and social and community situation. * Ability to reach female beneficiaries with regards to the community traditions and constrains. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: LowMitigating measures: Ensure that there is a willingness from the community members and leaders regarding the necessity of women’s education and ability to work, and providing awareness sessions for communities and parents to help support the activities.* Ability to find appropriate places to provide vocational and life skills trainings. Likelihood: Low; Impact: MediumMitigation measures: Ensure to prepare a stakeholder map to select the partners who can support the activities by centers and sites, etc. ADRA Syria will work closely with the local authorities, government, another NGOs, community leaders, relevant stakeholders, etc., to overcome obstacles and make the selection criteria of the project target and places clear from the beginning to confirm that the activities will be implemented in a safe and secure environment for all beneficiaries. * Tension could be created among the target groups, between host community members and IDPs when distributing the productive assets (equipment, tools and materials; etc.). Likelihood: Low; Impact: HighMitigation measures: Develop clear and defined criteria for the targeted beneficiaries and communicate with local authorities and leaders. Criteria will ensure that IDPs, returnees and host community members can access assistance.All project activities will be implemented using the principle of Do No Harm, taking into consideration and mitigating against any potential unintended consequences
