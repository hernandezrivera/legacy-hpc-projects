Project Summary

HNO reports that estimated 9.4 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023/24, a staggering 76 percent of South Sudan population, this is an increase by 500,000 people in 2022.
The projected 9.4 million people in need for 2023/24 include 1.9 million IDPs, 1.4 million returnees, 5.8 million returnees and 337,000 refugees. Among them are 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million girls. Nearly 15 percent of the population in need are people living with disability.
In Melut the IDPs started to arrive in August and September 2022 due to escalating conflict and fighting in Fashoda and Manyo Counties to the north, specifically to the areas of Oriny, Nyigir, Yony, Kalagani, Patuoj, Pajak and Abrouc. More than 90 per cent reported arriving to the site between November 2022 and January 2023, with the month of December 2022 alone accounting for 72.9 per cent of reported arrivals. The majority of IDPs reported fleeing from Fashoda County (50%) and Manyo County (46.9%), whereas over 500 IDPs reported being displaced from Melut, Panyikang, Maban, Malakal, Baliet and Longochuk counties. More than 97% of the IDPs reported they were in Melut to stay. The situation become worse due to the Sudan crisis whereby people were fleeing the war-torn Sudan into South Sudan through Renk, a significant number of these populations ended up in Paloch, Melut county.
In this project, SALI will seek to improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving non-food items to 16724 individuals through general in-kind assistance in Melut county, UpperNile State. To enhance the coping mechanism of the affected populations, SLI will support most vulnerable Residents, Returnees and IDPs with durable solutions such as training on Fuel Efficient stoves and how to make shelter using locally available materials such as grass, soil and bamboo. In locations experiencing Land, Housing and Property issues, SLI will seek to build the capacity of local authorities and affected populations with skills and knowledge on resilience building and access to land, housing, and property.
Semi-permanent and transitional shelters are the predominant types of shelters used by refugees and Internally Displaced People in most locations of South Sudan. Newly arriving returnees need new constructions and other internally displaced persons need regular repairs of their shelters, replenishment of Non-Food Items like blankets, cooking pots, sleeping’s mats, Kangas, plastic sheets and jerrycans to fetch water. However, because of financial constraints the needs outweigh the pace and capacity of the response. The gaps in shelter and Non-Food Items provision compromise the returnees and IDPs safety, privacy, security and dignity.

1. Needs

In the prevailing acute food insecurity situation in Melut County and the possibility of more IDPs arriving from Sudan due to developing conflict in the country, vulnerable internally displaced persons and residents who have lost household assets as a result of conflict require in-kind NFIs support. Such support will enable them dedicate their remaining recourses to buy food and medicine instead of using the money to replenish lost household items.Due to worsening humanitarian situation amongst the IDPs, some of the negative coping mechanisms which include going to bed earlier due to mosquito infestation, reducing meals per day due to lack of sufficient food and adequate cooking items, staying under trees during day and seeking shelter at night from neighbors due to lack of shelter materials, and using animal hides to cover children and elderly persons at night due to lack of blankets is not only a threat to their health but also denies them the dignity of living normal lives. Insufficient supply of mosquito nets, blankets and Kangas threaten the lives of children (under 59 weeks), pregnant and lactating women, elderly persons and those who are already sick, Malaria is a major killer disease. The proposed support is not only necessary but lifesaving. Providing the affected population with non-food items will also enable them to reduce protection risks associated with food insecurity. These include, according to the most recent IRNA findings (JULY 2023), living in close quarters with strangers, cooking outside at late hours due to inadequate cooking pots and lack of adequate lighting at community settlements. These risks that are associated with lack of shelters and other Non-Food Items are likely to expose women and girls to GBV and sexual exploitation if not addressed. During the assessment, elderly women were observed wearing torn or old clothes exposing them to elements of weather and indignity. Providing Kangas will better enable their mobility and ability to access markets and other food sources with dignityWhereas flooding is the main driver of S/NFIs needs in Uppernile, localized conflict, disruption of supply routes and markets, macroeconomic instability (hyperinflation), food insecurity that is leading families to spend the little they have on food than buying shelter or nonfood items, time poverty and Impact of Sudan crisis have contributed immensely to the needs. As a result, IDPs & Residents/Returnees are forced to sleep under trees, occupy abandoned structures and institutions, or share living and sleeping spaces with residents or relatives. In such circumstances, the affected individuals live in inhumane conditions and are exposed to elements of weather and protection risks. Women and girls sharing sleeping spaces with male counterparts or sleeping outside lack privacy and at risk of exposure to sexual gender-based violence. OCHA projects that the impact of the floods will last into 2022 necessitating provision of S/NFIs items to affected populations. Beyond flooding, food insecure households are most likely to utilize the little resources or money they have to buy food instead of shelter materials or other household items such as mosquito nets, cooking sets or clothes. Others are likely to sell the little items they have or exchange them for food. As a result of fighting, markets, supply routes and livelihoods got disrupted. Vendors vacated insecure areas while transporters removed their vehicles from the road for fear of being attacked. Job opportunities shrunk as private, public and nongovernment organizations stopped or scaled down their operations. The cumulative impact on such disruption has resulted in reduced production, collapse of markets, unemployed population that rely on aid and reducing purchasing power among populations.

2. Project activities

OUTPUT 1.1: 16724 beneficiaries comprised of Returnees, Internally Displaced and residents provided with shelter and non-food items through General In-kind Distribution (GIK). ACTIVITY 1.1.1: Conduct community communication, consultation and sensitization meetings on the S/NFIs assistance with local authorities, community leaders and members to provide project information and the role of the community in the implementation process. ACTIVITY 1.1.2: Community Mobilization, Registration, verification and selection of 16724 beneficiaries with the participation of community members, leaders, and local authorities, and beneficiary’s representatives. ACTIVITY 1.1.3: Prepositioning of Shelter and Non-food items from core pipeline ACTIVITY 1.1.4: Conduct distribution of Shelter and Non-food Items to 16724 using S/NFIs cluster Criteria in target counties. ACTIVITY 1.1.5: Conduct PDMs on Shelter and Non-food items with 17% beneficiaries randomly selected to ascertain use, appropriateness and benefits of items distributed. OUTPUT 2.1: ACTIVITY 2.1.1: 16724 most vulnerable returnees and host communities provided with awareness and training on durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions and disaster risk reduction. ACTIVITY 2.1.2: Conduct awareness on disaster risk reduction through local authorities, project staff, chiefs and community leaders targeting 8000 affected persons. ACTIVITY 2.1.3: Establish and train three (3) Disaster Management Committees and assist the committees develop and implement their own Climate Change Adaptation Plans.
