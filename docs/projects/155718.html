Project Summary

The Province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is located in the north-west of the country and borders Afghanistan. It comprises 25 districts and five Provincially Administered Tribal Areas (PATA). KP and FATA continue to experience crises and humanitarian emergencies. Numerous operations by Pakistan’s security forces since 2004 as well as violence by terrorist splinter groups (often religiously motivated) have resulted in a complex crisis, leading to the mass displacement of hundreds of thousands of people, mainly from FATA into neighbouring KP. Although the authorities attempted to initiate repatriations as early as 2015, these efforts are far from completed and have been largely unsuccessful. According to official figures, around 90% of IDPs have returned to FATA, but recording methods are, in reality, inadequate and can easily be circumvented. In both KP and FATA there is immense need for healthcare services for the IDPs and the host communities, since owing to the ongoing conflict, there is virtually no public healthcare provision. Statistics indicate that the maternal mortality rate in FATA is 395 per 100,000 live births (FATA household development indicators, 2012/13).
In May 2018, the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) region were merged with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) Province, therefore integrating FATA, now called Tribal Districts of KP, into Pakistan’s mainland governance structures. Although the government proposition finally found consensus after decades of efforts, with the army support, there are still two distinct authorities’ bodies (ex-FATA and KP), and a granted access to KP still doesn’t imply having access to the Tribal Districts. No real change has thus been acknowledged on the ground until now.
MdM is focusing on primary healthcare and reproductive health issues of communities of catchment area. MdM is also providing therapeutic supplements to under 5 years Malnourished children.
MdM started 2 BEmONC services each at RHC Naryab-Hangu and RHC Gomal-Tank district and planning to start one at RHC Band Korai-D.I.Khan and one at Thall-Hangu as well.
MdM is also providing psycho-social support and support to GBV victims by counselling and first aid treatment.
MdM divided overall project goal in three results.
• Result 1: IDPs, host communities and returnees have access at the supported facilities to high-quality, comprehensive primary healthcare, enhanced by staff capacity-building.
• Result 2: Sexual and reproductive health services (treatment and advice) for women and girls, including obstetrics and neonatal care (in selected healthcare facilities) are expanded and accessible
• Result 3: The capacity of beneficiary communities to provide a humanitarian response to disasters, emergencies or disease outbreaks is strengthened through educational initiatives on disaster preparedness, health issues and preventive measures.
