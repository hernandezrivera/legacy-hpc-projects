Project Summary

In 2019, about 5,725,000 South Sudanese women, men and children face protection risks and violations due to widespread conflict (Protection Cluster, October 2019). While the situation is no longer escalating at a rapid speed, the country remains in the grip of a serious humanitarian crisis (SS HNO, 2019). Target population for GBV assistance is 0, 8 mil individuals. Yei County in CES is among protection severity 5 classification, with 162,500 PIN (SS HNO, 2019) and Wau county in WBeG state is protection severity classification 4 with 280,500 PIN for protection assistance (SS HNO, 2019). With high probability of rising the number of returnees, current situation in Yei has likelihood to deteriorate, with expectations that number of PIN will be higher that currently estimated (950,000 South Sudanese refugees are in Uganda and DRC, UNHCR, October 2019).
The proposed intervention aims to provide assistance for vulnerable population and GBV survivors with structured PSS in WGFS static and mobile centers; to improve overall security and influence reduction of GBV incidents in the selected locations by establishing Community Based Protection Committees (CBPCs); and, to improve safe access to WASH facilities, hygiene promotion activities, provide women and girls with dignity kits and increase protection from Ebola virus (EV prevention will be provided in Yei).
The project will seek to enhance protection and to provide protection, GBV prevention and response assistance to 17,135 vulnerable individuals, host communities, IDPs and returnees women, men, boys and girls in two locations: Yei County in Central Equatoria state and Wau County in Western Bahr el Ghazal state in South Sudan.
In both locations IRW is present since 2016 and has good cooperation with all stakeholders. In Yei, IRW will provide assistance with implementing partner NNGO Voice for Change and will establish 1 static WGFS in Yei, while 1 mobile center will operate in Gimunu, Mugwo, Lasu and Otogo payams. 4 CBPCs will be established in each payam, with total of 15 members in which male and female will be equally represented and engaged, and will be trained to communicate with the communities, collect information, and discuss critical protection areas, and locations where GBV incidents happen, to improve overall security for all affected population, with an emphasis on women and girls, and reduction of GBV incidents. Additionally, in cooperation with IRW WASH team in Yei, community members will be provided with hygiene promotion sessions, dignity kits for women and girls’, and Ebola virus prevention measures.
In Wau County, IRW will independently establish 1 WGFS in Wau town, and 1 mobile center will operate in Baggari, Bazzia and Bessilia payams in Wau County. IRW Wau field office provides FSL, health, nutrition and WASH assistance which will enhance quality of the support and allow beneficiaries to be referred to additional services, especially in FSL activities that will provide survivors with tools and income generating skills. 4 female and male Community Based Protection Committees (CBPC) will be established in Wau and listed Payams and 60 members male and female will be trained to identify critical locations for overall security and activities that put women in girls in increased threats of GBV incidents.
Project will have mass awareness rising component to increase rise of knowledge and understanding of all GBV related components, including identification of GBV cases, prevailing GBV types in the locations, triggers and consequences of GBV on individual, family, community and social levels. IRW will conduct gender analysis with an emphasis on identification of the GBV and protection issues within the targeted communities and among community members, IDPs and returnees women, men, girls and boys.

1 - Needs

Widespread GBV in South Sudan constitutes a significant impediment to women’s participation in recovery and development. Physical violence, commonly by an intimate partner or someone known by the survivor, continues to be the most common form of GBV, accounting for 42 % of all reported cases, while SGBV constitutes 20 % of reported cases. GBV continues to be severely under-reported due to stigma, the survivors are often abandoned, with most receiving no legal assistance to help them seek justice, and with children born out of rape facing multiple protection risks (HNO, 2019). Since the restricted access from February 2019 when 20,000 of population were left without assistance, the situation in Yei remained critical up till today (PC, 2019). Number of PIN in the targeted locations in Yei County – Yei town 21,200; Gimunu 5,221; Mugwo 8,930; Lasu 13,151; Otogo 11,700 (VfC data, September 2019). In the Protection Situation Report (February 2019), there was an urgent request for humanitarian actors to increase protection services immediately, including assistance for GBV survivors, since sexual violence is prevalent outside of Yei town due to the presence of armed elements. Despite requested assistance, based on South Sudan Operational Presence 3Ws (as of June 2019) list of partners per cluster shows that there are no humanitarian actors providing GBV prevention and response in Yei County. Yei/Lasu protection assessment (UN, June 2019) finds that despite of number of rapes and abduction of women and girls, there are hardly no support services for GBV survivors, PSS services and very limited access to health, justice and livelihoods. Women survivors of GBV report negative impacts on physical, mental and reproductive health. Often negative health and social consequences are rarely addressed because of unfamiliarity with the existing services. In Wau County, WBeG state, the situation is better in the sense of access, but overall situation, especially related to security and GBV for women and girls requires strengthening of the support and assistance. Number of PIN in the targeted locations is: Wau County – Wau town 151,320; Baggari 29,789; Bazzia 8,775; Bessilia 11,777. Based on the GA in Wau County conducted by IRW-SS in September 2019, situation for women and girls related to safety and security is deteriorating, with few humanitarian actors with comprehensive support to vulnerable individuals and GBV survivors. 80% of assessment participants stated that they were, or personally know women/girls who are GBV survivors. Most of the perpetrators were husbands/fathers (60%) and 40% of perpetrators were soldiers (in 2018). Participants stated that they do not have any community based protection structures, and poor access to GBV prevention and response, and were interested in learning about improving protection and reduction of GBV related incidents. (IRW-SS GA, 2019). Additionally, GBV SC identified water fetching and use of other WASH facilities where women and girls are the most vulnerable to GBV incidents, and project will ensure to mitigate those risks with cooperation with IRW WASH department in both locations and to provide safe use of facilities, hygiene promotion for all women, men, girls and boys with special emphasis on PWD, elderly, PLW and other persons with specific needs, an distribution of dignity kits for women and girls in both locations.Affected population need mass awareness rising component to increase rise of knowledge and understanding of all protection and GBV related components, including identification of GBV cases, prevalent GBV types in the selected location, triggers and consequences of GBV on individual, family, community and social levels. IRW will conduct comprehensive gender analysis with an emphasis on identification of the protection and GBV issues and existing gaps in GBV prevention and support services within the targeted communities and among community members, IDPs and returnees’ – w/g/m/b.

2 - Activities

IRW activities in Yei and Wau counties are designed to respond on GBV Sub-cluster needs and findings. Total of 17,135 beneficiaries (W-6,069; M-3,066; G-5,000; B-3,000) will receive services under the project in line with PC Objectives 2020 HRP for SS and will ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of people among vulnerable w/m/g/b in hard-to-reach and priority geographical locations (contributes to SO1 and SO2). IRW and VfC will provide following activities harmonized with GBV SC SS HRP 2020 objectives in Yei and Wau counties: reduce the suffering of w/m/g/b who have experienced various forms of GBV and reported to service delivery points in priority geographical locations and hard-to-reach areas (contributes to SO1) through providing CM/individual and group PSS; mapping services and establish/maintain GBV referral pathways; referral services to specialized service delivery points; establishing static WGFSs and mobile PSS unites and providing women and girls with access to life skills trainings, recreational activities and dignity kits. Project will ensure the provision of GBV prevention and risk mitigation measures to address the priority needs of people among vulnerable w/m/g/b in priority geographical locations and hard-to-reach areas (contributes to SO2) by providing awareness raising sessions on GBV related issues, engaging men and boys in GBV prevention initiatives through EMAP or SASA! (tbd in cooperation with GBV SC), community outreach, and capacity building for service providers. By establishing CBPCs project will strengthen risk analysis and vulnerability assessments among the affected populations to identify risks and levels of exposure to various forms of GBV in priority geographical locations (contributes to SO1 and SO2) and activities under this objective will be risk analysis, identification of the most problematic areas in each location and mitigation measures to improve security and reduce number of GBV incidents (mainstreaming/safety audits/protective accompaniment) and cooperation with other clusters. In Wau County (independently) and with implementing partner VfC in Yei County IRW will establish 9 community based protection committees with 15 f/m members in each location. 135 CBPCs members, will be trained on identification of the problematic locations where community members, especially women and girls are facing protection risks including risks to GBV incidents. CBPCs members will receive support in communication skills for discussions on protection and sensitive topics such as GBV. IRW and VfC will deliver workshops for community and religious leaders and other community members on GBV issues, and how they can influence on the improvements in overall safety, and reduction of GBV cases, and CBPCs will be information channel for GBV survivors, or in risk of GBV about WGFS and IRW mobile teams’ activities. GBV CM will be provided in line with IASC principles for working with GBV survivors: survivor centered approach, confidentiality, non-discrimination and respect for the GBV survivors and their needs. GBV CMs will be trained in GBV CM approach, use and safe storage of GBV CM forms, referrals and service mapping. Mobile teams will provide PSS for w/m/b/g and inform w/g about WGFSs activities where they can receive additional support and trainings. Awareness sessions on GBV will be selected based on GA and in line with AAP suggestions provided by the community members. Activity will raise knowledge and understanding of protection and GBV, and will influence reduction of GBV cases in the communities. With IRW WASH team in Yei and Wau w/m/g/b will receive hygiene promotion sessions to improve hygiene, prevent hygiene/water related diseases, and learn about EV prevention modalities (Yei). 2000 w/g in Yei and Wau counties, will participate in MHM sessions and will receive dignity kits.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

IRW is member of Core Humanitarian Standards Alliance and has capacity to respect and provide assistance in line with CHS. Project will ensure that all humanitarian assistance will be provided in a manner that will enhance and promote the human dignity of host community, IDPs and returnees’ women, girls, men and boys. Beneficiaries have the right to utilize all humanitarian assistance as they determine is in their best interests. Also, project will fully respect beneficiaries’ right to privacy through the protection of personal data as key element of individual autonomy and dignity. IRW and VfC will use IRWs GBV Case Management SoPs, where all elements of data protection are precisely defined.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will be fully inclusive for all community members - host community, IDPs and returnees women, men, girls and boys. IDPs will have equal access and benefits from the project as all other affected and vulnerable population from host communities and returnees. All affected women, men, girls and boys from all groups will participate in the project activities equally, including their participation in Community Based Protection Committees, participation in awareness session, assistance in GBV prevention and response services and as recipients of dignity kits, live-skills and vocational training and recreational activities. Special emphasis will be given for additionally vulnerable groups such are minorities and individuals - PWDs, PLW, elderly population, youth population, especially IDP girls vulnerable to GBV and boys vulnerable to force recruitment.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked with government systems and will need support from government officials in establishing WGFSs centers and will cooperate with the department of Gender, Children and Social Care at counties level. Linkages with community systems will understand cooperation with community and religious leaders, women and youth groups, local NNGOs and protection actors. These links will ensure that project is supported and well accepted by main stakeholders. Community and religious leaders, women and youth groups, local NGOs will be active partners in providing assistance in line with project document, and some of them will be part of Community Based Protection Committees.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, project is aligned with following objectives: Social protection; "Creation of sustainable protective environment"; and national objective to "End all forms of violence in the country". Additionally, South Sudan is determined to reduce GBV cases, and project is in line with this national strategy, as well as with strategy of "Reduction of Child Marriages in South Sudan" supported by SS First Lady initiative.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable in Wau County, however, Yei County has protection and safety issues. IRW in partnership with Voice for Change will cooperate with Protection Cluster and other actors in the areas to receive regular updates on all protection related issues. IRW and VfC will follow up eventual changes of the situation and adapt activities to minimize eventual risks and prevent negative any consequences. Additionally, by establishing CBPCs, project will improve understanding of protection related issues, influence improvement in overall security in Yei and Wau counties, and mitigate/reduce number protection related issues and number of GBV cases. Community members - host community, IDPs and returnees w/m/g/b will have skills to continue with these structures independently. In cooperation with IRW program departments - especially FSL, GBV survivors will be supported in life-skills training and tools that will allow them sustainable income.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, after project closure, Women and Girls' Friendly Spaces and Community Based Protection Committees will be handed over to the communities. After one year of work of this structures, it is expected that communities will be familiar with working principles and approaches in improving overall safety and security through CBPCs and providing prevention and response to GBV survivors, and working on rising knowledge about GBV, mitigation and reduction measures, under WGFSs among other activities. Project will work in sensitization of the community member on all GBV related topic and providing them with sufficient knowledge and skills to be able to continue to independently run CBPCs and WGFSs.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IRW is member of CHS Alliance and has all AAP related policies, including PSEA and CP Safeguarding. IRW considers affected population as equal and active partners and they will be included in all project phases and initiate full participation of the beneficiaries throughout all project activities. By established a functional Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) and reporting procedures at the field offices, all beneficiaries and community members will be informed about purpose of CFMs and different options to submit their grievances/complaints. A safeguarding focal point (SFP) and complaint/feedback mechanism (CFM) focal person will available in each of the field offices, and community members will have different forms for submitting their complaints and feedbacks – direct in person to SFP and CFM focal persons, via CBPCs that IRW and VfC will establish in all locations of operations, by phone, mail and in written form in CFM boxes. IRW in line with AAP initiate full participation of the beneficiaries throughout all project activities. All IRW staff, volunteers and partners are trained on PSEA and child protection safeguarding issues at the beginning of their assignments, and communities are continuously informed about PSEA and CP policies and how to report any breaches and access to services and support with full respect of their identity, privacy and confidentiality. IRW applies Zero Tolerance on any breach of its safeguarding policies, and IRW safeguarding focal point will provide comprehensive PSEA and CP training to all Voice for Change employees, to ensure they are fully familiar and informed with them and able to work in respect of IRW safeguarding high standards. Additionally, IRW puts special emphasis on PWDs and illiterate population, and Protection & Inclusion policy with "Leave no one behind" approach which is guarantee that IRW and by training VfC will provide appropriate assistance based on the different needs of the affected population.
