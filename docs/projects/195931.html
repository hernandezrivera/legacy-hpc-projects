Project Summary

Bhasan Char was established by the Government of Bangladesh to host up to 75,000 members of the Rohingya community from Myanmar. Aimed at addressing the overcrowding in Cox’s Bazar and improving living conditions, the project is led by the Government with relevant Ministries and agencies actively involved, as well as national NGOs. With the MoU between Government of Bangladesh and UNHCR (on behalf of the UN) in place, UN operations have been developed on the island and as of 31 October 2022 some 30,000 refugees have been relocated there. Humanitarian community will continue to make efforts to supplement and complement the humanitarian activities of the national NGOs in support of this national led effort. The project will support refugees on the remote island of Bhasan Char, who are highly vulnerable and entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Women, children, and person with specific needs reported the most significant unmet needs, leaving them vulnerable to negative coping strategies. Bhasan Char is vulnerable to climate risks such as cyclones, storms, frequent lightning and flooding due to monsoon and tidal surges. The Inter-Agency efforts will provide support and assistance to refugees including protection, shelter, food and non-food items, water, sanitation and hygiene, health and nutrition, education, and skill development and livelihoods. To mitigate disaster risks, emergency, preparedness, and response (EPR) will be part of the inter-agency response.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The socio-economic vulnerability of Rohingya refugees on Bhasan Char has impacted their physical and mental well-being, quality of life, and protection environment. Refugees need ongoing humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs and access essential services in health and nutrition, food security, protection, education, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), electricity, and non-food items. Community resilience to disasters and climate change impacts requires multi-sectoral inter-agency cooperation on emergency preparedness and response. Domestic violence and child marriage practices are the prevailing forms of GBV amongst the Rohingya refugees and affects most of the population, to one or other extent, in their daily lives. The absence of appropriate alternative accommodation for unaccompanied and separated children and survivors of gender-based violence, poses major bottlenecks in finding adequate solutions to complex protection issues such as unaccompanied and separated children, child marriage, and domestic violence. Persons with disabilities living on their own or heading a household, face even greater challenges as they need to earn a living in order to support themselves and their families but lack adequate means of self-reliance. Tertiary and secondary healthcare is extremely expensive, depend on availability of specialised boats, key to medical transportation, and cannot be done on urgent basis. Refugees find it difficult to cover the costs. Options of affordable secondary-tertiary level mental health care are very limited. Post treatment follow-up and rehabilitation are not provided, due to other more urgent and lifesaving priorities. A small portion of the Rohingya refugees finds volunteers opportunities. However, a general lack of livelihood opportunities for Rohingya refugees is making it difficult for them to make a living and sustain themselves and their families. Although there has been substantial investment in infrastructure, continued maintenance remains critical. Due to the lack of maintenance, a large portion of the shelters as well as other facilities like roads, drains, etc present infrastructure issues affecting access and safety. Without adequate and sustained access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), preventable disease outbreaks will continue and worsen. Acute watery diarrheic (AWD) is a high risk for Bashan Char and can aggravate deteriorating malnutrition rates of Rohingya children. Hygiene practices and the barriers to achieving them remains a critical challenge. The current situation poses a real threat for AWD and other WASH related disease outbreaks. Effective WASH interventions are critical for saving human lives.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Bhasan Char Inter Agency Response is based on a shared understanding of needs, Sector objectives, and priorities among partners. This prevents duplication of effort, improves coverage—especially for assisting the most vulnerable—increases response continuity, and provides coordinated, coherent, collaborative, sustainable, and cost-effective responses. Some of the efforts to promote rationalisation of response include: (a) geographic gaps or overlaps in planned activities are avoided and a one-partner-per-settlement approach has been considered and optimised where appropriate, (b) partners are members of the PSEA Network and have PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points, (c) partners have, or can, obtain appropriate permissions from the Government in a timely manner, (d) maintain continuity of presence - working in the same Sector in the same cluster/settlement in 2022, (e) appealing partners comply with the minimum technical standards, experience, and meet the minimum service package requirements, (f) partners have realistic resource mobilisation plans to support their planned activities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Inter-agency activities will target to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of joint intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks; ensure access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without any barriers; pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services; set-up appropriate mechanisms through which refugees can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints and support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to access their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.

3.2 - Gender

Inter-agency efforts will be targeted to increase the meaningful and active participation of women and other gender groups with the aim of ensuring greater influence over decision making that impacts them, their families, and their communities. The strategies include organizing women-only discussions, training women in skills, raising community awareness of the potential positive dividends of promoting gender equality and enrolling women as refugee outreach volunteers.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Inter-agency response has factored in the different capacities, needs, and exposure to protection risks of the refugee women, men, girls, and boys targeted in the programme, and have incorporated them into inter-agency assessments, planning, implementation, monitoring, reporting, and evaluation. Activities will continue to enable access to services and assistance services to diverse persons we serve, in particular women, persons with disabilities, older persons and youth.

3.4 - Diversity

Inter-agency response will engage with diverse groups of refugees in identifying needs, designing, implementing, monitoring, and evaluating programmes. Activities will continue to empower communities and increase the participation of marginalized groups including the persons with disabilities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Inter-agency actions taken in relation to AAP include protection hotline, participatory assessments, feedback boxes, public information boards, community meetings, interviews, counselling sessions, and home visits.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Bhasan Char hazard profile is characterized by exposure to multiple hazards including tropical storms and cyclones, inland flooding from heavy rains, destructive winds, lightning, and fire. Disaster Risk Reduction measures are integrated into sectoral response. The SMS Sector is assisting the Government to work with key stakeholders to strengthen emergency preparedness measures and to facilitate a coordinated response to natural disasters and hazards through continuous monitoring, drills, and simulations, as well as prepositioning essential relief items. Cyclone preparedness (CPP) and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) volunteers are trained and mobilized in each cluster to support communication with communities during anticipatory, response and recovery phases of disasters.
