Project Summary

Somalia has been without a government for almost three decades, this has caused a decade of conflict climate shock, diseases outbreaks and poverty which has lead a huge WASH gap that is life threatening. An estimated 6.4 million need emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 3.5 million will need safe drinking water, 5.1 million sanitation services, and 6.4 million hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64 per cent children and the elderly for 4 per cent. This presents an increase of 39 per cent from 2021, in which 4.6 million were estimated to be in need of WASH services across Somalia. This increase is due to adjusted population figures of Somalia, in combination with continuous failed rainy seasons which are expected to further strain existing limited WASH services. The population in need of WASH services in 2022 includes approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. An estimated 6,058,603 people (38 per cent) lack access to safe water and proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to AWD and cholera. It is also estimated that 965,754 (15 per cent) of persons with disabilities and 254,465 (4 per cent) of elderly persons face the risk of exclusion from assistance due to the barriers they face when accessing WASH services. A recent assessment confirmed that 40 per cent of females and 75 per cent of males with disability never collected water for their households due to distance and in-accessibility of water points and relies on neighbors and children to collect water for them.
The high humanitarian WASH needs in Somalia are driven by seasonal floods, severe drought conditions, acute water diarrhea/ cholera outbreaks and protracted crises, exacerbated by COVID-19 outbreak. From February to May 2021, 50 of the 74 districts in Somalia faced severe water shortages and water prices increased by 66 per cent. In addition, flash floods contribute to the severity of needs among riverine communities, in middle, lower Shebelle, and Hiraan regions seasonal floods damaged 82 per cent of water and sanitation infrastructure triggering spikes in AWD/ cholera cases.
Access to basic water supply in Somalia is only 56 per cent. Over one third (39 per cent) of vulnerable people in Somalia do not have access to sustained, improved and safe water source for drinking and other domestic uses, while 25 per cent still walk for more than 15 minutes to collect water from a nearest water source. Due to these constraints, 1 in 5 Somalis do not have enough water per day to cover their basic needs for both for drinking and domestic use. Access to improved sanitation facilities in Somalia is very low. JMCNA 2021 noted that 57 percent of households do not have access to improved sanitation facilities. About 25 per cent of latrine in Somalia are un-improved and poses a risk to public health. Additionally, 23 per cent of people across Somalia still defecate in the open.
According to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative 2020 (JMMI), replenishment of WASH items is slow, compounded by the quality of the poor roads. Critical WASH goods at risk of stock depletion in the open markets are chlorine tablets, soap bars, drinking water, and menstrual hygiene management.
Aid Vision proposed project is aimed at providing emergency life-saving WASH interventions to 11,000 affected populations in Banadir and middle Shebelle regions. Aid Vision has a presence in the two regions for over two decades and established networks of community structures and the local authorities. The project will provide rehabilitations of shallow wells and boreholes, construction and rehabilitation of communal latrines, rehabilitation of institutional latrines, collection and management of solid waste, conducting community hygiene promotion and institutional based hygiene promotion.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1: Construction desludgeable and disaster resistance latrines70 percent of the Benadir and Middle Shebelle region defecate in the open field away from home; a practice that will lead to outbreak of diseases. To address the gap identified Aid Vision plans to construct 200 new desludgeable, gender friendly latrines, with hand wash facilities and provision of soap for hand washing. Activity 1.2: Institutional latrine constructionAid Vision will support six schools with sanitation gaps. Aid Vision proposes to build a total of 12 blocks of each two stances. Each of the 6 schools will get two blocks, one for girls and another for boys with hand wash facilities and sited away from each other.Activity 2.1: Hygiene kits distributionIn areas where AWDs and other water related diseases are prevalent, Aid Vision will distribute hygiene kits to 3000 vulnerable households families comprising of two 20 liters jerry cans for fetching and storage water, 2400 grams of soap, 3 sachets of sanitary pads for menstrual management for school girls and women, 100 tablets of aqua tabs per family and intensify hygiene promotion and awarding of super mothers to promote community behavioral change that uses emotional drivers as one the Risks, Attitudes, Norms, Abilities, and Self‐regulation (RANAS). Activity 2.2: Community hygiene promotionIntensive hygiene promotion campaign that involves behavioral change that uses emotional drivers like Risks, Attitudes, Norms, Abilities, and Self‐regulation (RANAS), behavioral factors approach tools, house to house visits, focused group discussions in specific strategic places like water points, IDP camps, health centers, nutrition centers, hygiene promotion sessions, simple household water treatment sessions, and use of IEC materials to raise the level of awareness on critical hygiene practices. Aid Vision intends to hire hygiene promoters, community mobilizers, water quality monitors and train them on modern hygiene promotion approaches to ensure that at least 85% the targeted communities adapted good hygiene practices at end of the project and will continue adapting it after project end. Activity 2.3: Institutional based hygiene promotionSchool Hygiene promotion: Through formation of working school hygiene clubs, CHAST training, hygiene promotion sessions, drama, debating, training children on good use of sanitation facilities in schools and keeping them clean, Aid Vision will reach out to schools to ensure an accelerated uptake of good hygiene practices. Children are normally quick to learn and are referred to as Agents of Change. They will take home and practice what they will learn in the school. Aid Vision will form hygiene clubs in six targeted schools. Aid Vision will also use IEC materials to undertake hygiene promotion in the targeted schools. Activity 3: Capacity building (water committee and WASH training)Formation and training of CHWs, Hygiene, hygiene volunteers, community elders and Imams, sanitation committees,PHASTand hand washing training,CHAST and hand washing training.Through the project, Aid Vision proposes to train hygiene volunteers from targeted communities such as community health workers, community influencers, youth group and women group to mobilize, train and engage their communities. Activity 3.1: solid waste managementTo facilitate the work of the committees and ensure that latrines are maintained, Aid Vision will purchase and distribute a set of sanitation tools to each committee. The tools will comprise wheel barrows, shovels, rakes among others and will be used for both maintenances of latrines and management of the household solid waste. Activity 4: Rehabilitation of shallow wellsAid Vision intends to rehabilitate 4 shallow wells and one strategic borehole to ensure safe and reliable water to the affected community.Activity 4.1: Chlorination of waterAid Vision will undertake chlorination of water points in all the areas it will operate to prevent AWDS.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Aid Vision will ensure all minorities and the majority as well as people living with disabilities are involved in the implementation of the project and be able to be transparent in terms of employment. Qualified engineers, WASH experts, and Community health WASH workers selection and training will also consider gender sensitivity including men and women. All this will ensure gender and equity issues are addressed among the community members. Aid Vision will collect gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to health activities to ensure they have equal access to quality health care services. Aid Vision will address WASH inequities by improving access by all segments of the affected population including women and those with disabilities who have equal access to WASH assistance. Aid Vision will do social mobilization mechanisms seeking behavior change on minority and marginalized communities who are at risk of being neglected/ excluded are also given the quality and equal health care services to prevent common water-borne illnesses. Aid Vision will also advocate community participation in the implementation of the program. This will help in getting complaints and feedback responses that enhance effective decision-making and promote accountability to the affected population. Aid Vision proposed project is designed in a way that prioritizes the rights, safety, and dignity of the beneficiaries. The target communities are fully involved and aware of the roles of each stakeholder where each sees the other as partners and equals. The proposed project will not discriminate against colour, age, sex, and origin against any beneficiary and will enable equal and impartial assistance and services for all. Targeting vulnerable community members will be done on basis of their specific needs. The staff will be inducted on their duties and responsibilities and particularly the rights of the beneficiaries to ensure there is no form of patient exploitation at the facilities and Aid Vision offices. Watertight mechanisms will be put in place to ensure served populations' voices are heard and can file their complaints without being victimized.
