Project Summary

The project intended to increase access for primary health care services through 9 mobile clinics, 3 in Afrin district, 2 in Idlib and 4 in Euphrates shield area, in addition to 6 Primary health care centers, 3 in Afrin 2 in Euphrates shield and 1 in Idlib, Ariha, Three of the mentioned PHCs should be BmONC centers.
The project will increase also access to physical rehabilitation, by supporting three physical rehabilitation centers, 1 in each of Afrin, Idlib and Euphrates shield area. in addition to the participation in providing assistive devices and needed physiotherapy equipment to more than 30 centers.
The project will also be intended to have at least 1 CmONC center in Afrin district, in addition, to support of Ariha dialysis center.
All the facilities will provide services for both IDPs and host communities.

Needs

Throughout 2018, Al-Ameen for Humanitarian Support continued to provide lifesaving support and services in north-west Syria to conflict-affected populations in Idlib and Aleppo governorates. Since late 2017, north-western Syria received several influxes of internally displaced people (IDPs) from different parts of the country, The continuous waves of displacement within and into the north-west of Syria have created additional pressure on health systems and further stretched the limited capacity to provide health and nutrition services "OCHA,2018". According to the health cluster, the people in need "PIN" in Gaziantep accessible areas have become 2.96M, and Acute PiN (severity score 4 & 5) is 1.8M people. Through the services designed with both mobile and fixed primary health services, the project services will mainly focus on Al-bab and Afrin city with a severity score of 4 and 5 respectively, in addition to specialized care in Ariha with severity score 3. The project will include also MISP services in all primary health care activities to deal with women reproductive health and GBV cases, according to the health cluster input in HNO 2019, Pregnant adolescent girls and their babies are at high risk of medical complications and death. They constitute a substantial high-risk group in Syria, with early marriage reported to take place in 57 percent of assessed communities, in addition to that Both health providers and community representatives highlighted the limited availability of clinical management of rape (CMR) as a services gap across Syria. Through the primary health care centers, at least four centers will have EPI services for children and will be part of mass vaccination campaigns, according to UNICEF/WHO they estimate that in 2019, about 577,219 newborns will require routine immunizations across Syria.The project through the variety of clinic with the PHCs like pediatrician, vaccination, general medicine, gynecology and family planning will address all the differences in ages and gender. In Afrin health facilities and mobile clinics were established and provide services to overcome the shortage in the services in that area, according to RNA conducted by OCHA in April 2018 as shared by health cluster, the number of health facilities accessible by the population is extremely low, and that when those facilities exist, they do not have the staff and resources to function properly. Women answers highlight that for them it is much more difficult to access services, probably due to restriction/danger of movement and lack services corresponding to their specific needs. Through the project 3 physical rehabilitation centers will be supported to increase access to the services, according to the Physical Rehabilitation & Disability Working Group, in their report ‘Disability and Access to Healthcare: Western Aleppo, Idleb & Ar-Raqqa’ 2018, they stated that An average of 45 percent of persons injured during the Syria crisis are expected to sustain a permanent impairment (e.g., amputation, spinal cord injury, brain injury), in addition After seven years of hostilities in Syria, disability is increasingly prevalent, with a recent assessment on disability among adults in Western Aleppo and Idlib governorates indicating an average prevalence rate of 30 percent. Over 60 percent of persons with disabilities state that their disability has been exacerbated by the crisis, with a lack of access to healthcare and difficulties in meeting their basic needs listed as the most common reasons. In addition, through supporting dialysis center in Ariha city the project be one of 20 centers providing life-saving services to around 837 patients in NW Syria according to Turkey health cluster mapping dialysis services, which considered as essential to being continued according to the health cluster input in HNO, 2019.

Implementation of activities

Within its primary health care fixed center, each center will have multiple clinics to address all the needs for different age categories and gender, the services will be provided through general medicine clinic, dental clinic, a pediatric clinic with IMCI program, vaccination, midwifery, family planning and gynecology clinic with MISP program, in addition to diagnostic procedures. Through the mobile clinic and outreach activities the IMCI, MISP and family planning will be complemented. The physical rehabilitation clinics will be 1 in each of Afrin, Euphrates shield and Ariha city, they will provide services to people with disabilities through different approaches like providing sessions, distribute assistive device and basic psychosocial support. While the dialysis center in Ariha will continue providing life-saving sessions, which will include consultation, providing session and medication.

Protection Risk Analysis

Different risks are available during through this project it can be summierized with as the following: 1). Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers. Mitigating measures:- Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities.- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare.2). Risk of Gender Based Violence GBV:Mitigating measures:- having access to the reproductive health services of the (MISP) as part of services provided by health centers.- Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases.- Presence of female staff in health facilities increases access of women and girls to health services. 3). Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:Mitigating measures:-Effective community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.-Awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. -Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave. 4). Community Engagement and Participation: Mitigating measures:-Establishment of a common feedback mechanism utilizing available communication means, social media, in addition to the face-to-face approach.- Production and dissemination of key messages to ensure affected communities have access to information needed to receive humanitarian assistance and provide feedback.-Strengthen community based health services to facilitate dialogue with communities. -Regular follow-up and reporting on the results of accountability mechanisms in place.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risk could be summarized as: 1- Attack and maybe changing in power control in some areas: this could be solved by preparing an evacuation strategy, according to the situation the whole activity could move from that area to where people go, and try to provide services there. 2- The current staff left and no adequate staff available: this can be solved by either having some staff to be traveled from Turkey to Syria or training staff.3- Changing in security situation: this will be alerted by the security officer who will report the issue so a plan of suspending activities or moving or continue to be discussed.
