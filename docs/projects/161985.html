Project Summary

The project intends to provide support to refugees in settlements for self-reliance and Livelihoods grant support for host community targeting most vulnerable population including special needs (Female headed HH, household with disabled members or elderly people, HH with irregular income, HH with no or limited productive assets, HH impacted negatively by natural or human-made disaster etc.)
The ultimate goal of the project is to contribute in ensure food security and livelihoods resilient and to reduce disaster vulnerability for Refugees and host community.
The project will be linked food security, livelihood, and DRR interventions in an integrated manner. Food assistance (Cooked meals distribution), Livelihoods assistance (Homebased food production, IGAs or Cash base programming-CFW/DRR), Self-reliance and skill development are key strategies to reach the goal of the project. Progress and process monitoring will be conducted during implementation.
Action Against Hunger has own gender policy which incorporates key gender equality measures of IASC Gender with Age Marker. The project will be ensured safe environment by upholding gender equality and protection-mainstreaming principles are some of the measure taken to ensure gender responsive programming. The project prioritizes service to special group’s especially female-headed households, Pregnant and Lactating Women, under 5 children (vulnerable to malnutrition), People with Disability and Elderly during implementation in the proposed projects interventions.
Community led approach and Complain Response Mechanism (CRM) will be followed to ensure the engagement and participation of the community in different activities including beneficiary targeting, CFW programming; demonstration of mock/fire drill for host and refugees; awareness raising on DRR and Household contingency plan development for both refugee and host communities. With vast knowledge and experience in emergency and disaster management, the project will have contingency plan to respond to man-made and natural disasters (monsoon flood, land slide and cyclone) with close collaboration with all stakeholders.

Needs

Recurrent disaster hit in the Cox's Bazar district have severe negative impact on income opportunities and livelihoods of the people. This situation have been further aggravated by the influx of refugees, which has led to both the reduction of agricultural land and a labor surplus, driving wages down, and an increase in the price of basic food and non-food items. According to the NPM by IOM in June 2019, majority of the crisis affected population still do not have any source of income and the food assistance remains the main source of food for the refugees. According to REVA 2 by WFP in May 2019, over one year into the largest influx of Rohingya nationals in Cox’s Bazar, their levels of vulnerability remain high where 88% of the overall Rohingya population remains highly to entirely relying on life-saving assistance. Economic vulnerability remains the main driver of food insecurity, with 78% of refugees unable to meet the monetary value needed to cover their essential needs. The majority of the refugees see in their food assistance the main resource to access all their essential needs. Inadequate food consumption affects around 4/10 refugee HHs, with highest rates among the old unregistered (50%) and new arrivals (44%). Female-headed HHs or HHs with People with disabilities (PWDs), chronically ill people, and with numerous children are the most vulnerable. Among the host communities, small-sized households (especially those with one or two members) or households with three or more children under five years of age are more prone to be food insecure. Lack of dietary diversity and poor family support increase the risk of malnutrition among PLWs. ACF Nutrition survey shows that women and girls are most affected by malnutrition especially under five children (61.1%), adolescent girls and PLWs. Recent findings indicate high prevalence of elderly malnutrition (GAM 18%).According to Self-reliance Assessment of Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar conducted by Save the Children, BRAC, World Vision, WFP and UNHCR, during Dec 2017-Jan 2018, Poor dietary diversity due to limited options in food security interventions and livelihood opportunities further exacerbates the malnutrition. Among the host community, approximately 34% of households have poor food diversity score, 56% of HHs are in borderline and only 10% of HHs have an acceptable score. The Host Community have acute needs for income generation activities. Livelihood grant support, rebuilding agriculture sector, and creating opportunity to have work through previously known skills remains their major demand. Resilience building in terms of resources (capacities, livelihoods) and self-reliance (skills development) remains a key need across host communities and refugee camps respectively.
