Project Summary

Nigeria is among the top 10 largest food crises in the World and the largest food crisis in West Africa, south of the Sahara, despite its remarkable potential for food production. According to the preliminary result of October 2023 Cadre Harmonise (CH), approximately 4.4 million people in BAY states are projected to experience high acute food insecurity (CH Phase 3 or worse) during the peak of the lean season (June to August 2024), a period that coincides with the rainy season. The situation could have been worse due to the lack of dedicated funding for livelihood interventions. The main drivers of the prevailing food insecurity in Nigeria are largely conflict and insecurity, i.e., the protracted armed insurgency in the northeast led by Boko Haram, banditry, kidnappings, and natural resource-based conflicts in the northwest and north-central. The situation has been exacerbated by the continued increase in food prices as a consequence of the ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict and new government economic measures, including the removal of fuel subsidies and the continued devaluation of the country's currency (Naira). This has worsened rural households' capacity to access food and agricultural inputs. The most affected are vulnerable groups such as internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, and within those groups, more especially, children, women, and persons with disabilities. Without sustained and scaled-up food availability, facilitation of food access, and livelihood support, it is estimated that a significant proportion of the population in BAY states will continue to endure food insecurity and economic hardship. In addition, negative impacts of climate change and variabilities such as floods and frequent dry spells, pests, and diseases potentially cause severe crop losses.
This year, the northeast states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) have experienced a prolonged dry spell (June- July 2023), affecting food production and food insecurity. Climate variability negatively impacts people living in conflict areas, further compounding the vulnerability of the food production system, which results in poor crop yields and livestock productivity loss with a direct negative impact on households’ livelihood, income, and food security and nutrition.
The greatest burden of food insecurity is in the northeast, in BAY states which are being affected by protracted conflict. The armed insurgency in the northeast BAY states has been ongoing for over a decade and is responsible for severe hardship in most communities. Insecurity led to massive displacement across the BAY states with over 2.2 million people IDPs, of whom 82 percent in Borno, 10 percent in Adamawa, and 7 percent in Yobe (IOM DTM Nigeria Displacement Report 43, February 2023) . A significant number of IDPs (58 percent) are hosted in communities, while approximately 42 percent live in camps or camp-like settlements.
Also, the continued risk of child mortality due to acute malnutrition is alarming, as the number of children suffering from acute malnutrition has increased from 1.74 million in 2022 to over 2 million in 2023. According to the latest IPC Acute Malnutrition Report for May 2023-April 2024, over 1.53 million children under the age of 5 years are acutely malnourished (GAM), with over half a million severely malnourished (SAM). In addition, more than 200,000 pregnant and lactating mothers across BAY are acutely malnourished .
The attacks on civilians have gradually decreased, and in line with the Government’s policy of closing IDP camps and facilitating resettlement in their place of origin, approximately 2 million people have returned to communities of origin, according to IOM.

1 - Needs

Nigeria's food crisis looms large, positioning itself as a significant challenge within the global landscape and emerging as the foremost concern in West Africa, despite the nation's substantial agricultural potential. Since 2022, inflationary pressures have intensified, driven by soaring food prices and energy costs and the depreciation of the exchange rate. High levels of insecurity as a result of the protracted conflict and climate variabilities such as prolonged dry spells and flash floods continue to hinder the gains that has been achieved in restoring the livelihoods and productive capacities of vulnerable communities in these states, in September 2024 during the agricultural season the BAY states experienced a record flood which severely impacted livelihood assets, current food stocks, and the yield prospects for the upcoming main harvest. This situation is expected to exacerbate the vulnerability of household's dependent on agriculture to food insecurity throughout much of 2024, couple with the ongoing food inflation in country, it is anticipated that food security will worsen. As of 13 September, the floods have impacted over 2.5 million people and submerged 1.5 million hectares of land, including approximately 707,000 hectares of farmland. Due to the extent and persistence of the floods, the potential annual production losses for maize, sorghum and rice, combined, in the flooded areas could be about 2 million tonnes. This could meet the kilocalorie requirements of about 23 million people for a year. In financial terms, the potential cereal crop losses amount to upwards of USD 1.2 billion in economic losses nationwide. key outlooks for 20251. Projections: An estimated 33.1 million Nigerians are projected to face high levels of food insecurity during the peak of the lean season in 2025. This figure represents15.9% percent of the analyzed population in 26 states and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT) and depicts a 4 percent increase from the same period this year, and 33.5 percent compared to same period last year (2023). 2. Heightened severe food insecurity: An estimated 1.7 million people are projected to experience Emergency (Phase 4) food insecurity in 2025, characterized by severe levels of acute malnutrition and excess mortality, and just one phase before catastrophe (Phase 5). This figure represents a 70 percent increase compared to the same period this year (2024). 3. Children and Vulnerable Groups: Approximately 5.4 million children and nearly 800 000 pregnant and breastfeeding women are at risk of acute malnutrition, with an alarming 1.8 million children expected to need urgent treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). This has worsened rural households' capacity to access food and agricultural inputs. The most affected are vulnerable groups such as internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, and within those groups, more especially, children, women, and persons with disabilities. Without sustained and scaled-up food availability, facilitation of food access, and livelihood support, it is estimated that a significant proportion of the population in BAY states will continue to endure food insecurity and economic hardship. In addition, negative impacts of climate change and variabilities such as floods and frequent dry spells, pests, and diseases potentially cause severe crop losses. Understocking and poor veterinary and animal husbandry practices, especially in conflict-prone areas, contribute to declining productivity among pastoralists.

2 - Activities

In 2025 FAO will continue to target households that are vulnerable and affected by the ongoing conflicts including IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in selected local government areas in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States. Under the Food Security Sector Strategic Objective 2, FAO will lead the support on Agriculture-based livelihoods assistance to enable communities to produce their own food, priority interventions will include. a) Distribution of improved quality seeds and fertilizer for rainy and dry season food production. b) Distribution of small ruminants, goat and poultry through livestock restocking c) urban and peri-urban homestead gardening. d) Distribution of aquaculture kits for fish productione) Distribution of Local Supplementary Feeding (Tom Brown)f) Distribution of Fuel-Efficient Stovesg) Distribution of briquette h) provision of water pumps and small irrigation facilities I) Establishment of small agro-processing centersj) Establishment of Group Saving and Loans Associations k) Establishment of Water harvesting Scheme l) Establishment of Farmer field Schoolsm) Establishment of Early Warning Systems and Anticipatory Action

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FAO will undertake a broad scale protection risk analysis to understand the risks of specific groups across agricultural value chains in the northeast and is well placed to design projects which consider the specific risks facing at-risk groups. FAO has field experts including female field assistants and staff who speak the common local languages in the BAY states and are frequently deployed during trainings, distributions, monitoring and planning missions. To greater understand the protection risks faced by HHs, FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training to staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. By adopting a CBA, ensuring beneficiary participation in decision-making, and involving local and traditional authorities, the project will promote accountability, capacity building & empowerment. Five percent of the budget will be allocated for the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholder's communication and advocacy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
