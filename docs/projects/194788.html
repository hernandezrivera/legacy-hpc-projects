Project Summary

The malnutrition situation in Sudan remains dire with an estimated increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition due to the deteriorating nutrition situation following the catastrophic humanitarian crisis. The nutrition sector estimates that 4.6 million children under five, pregnant women, mothers, and caregivers of children under-two will need humanitarian assistance to meet their nutritional needs in 2023.
UNICEF is the sector lead for nutrition, with a mandate to uphold children’s right to nutrition and end child malnutrition in all its forms. UNICEF has been working for children in Sudan over the past 70 years, building and maintaining strong relationships with the Ministry of Health, civil society organisations (CSOs), the private sector, community health and nutrition workers, and other nutrition stakeholders at national and subnational levels, utilising its technical expertise and partnerships to implement effective nutrition programming in the country.
Through this project, UNICEF aims to (1) increase equitable access to an integrated lifesaving nutrition preventive and protection minimum package services targeting the “1000- day window”- as a critical opportunity to prevent child wasting and stunting among children under two years, (2) improve early detection of acute malnutrition (3) improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services to treat severe acute malnutrition among children under-five years. UNICEF will prioritize new hotspots and IDP areas while scaling up integrated lifesaving nutrition preventive and curative services for those already in need.
Over 1.6 million pregnant women, mothers, and caregivers of children under-two, and 5.9 million girls and boys under-five will be reached with a package of essential nutrition interventions for the prevention, early detection, and treatment of child wasting with an estimated cost of USD 346,245,840. However, the total budget for this project is USD 234,700,848 as the estimated target and funding requirement is for seven months from June to December 2023, and not an annual target and funding requirements.
The package of preventive nutrition interventions will be focused on improving diets, services, and practices that protect, promote, and support good nutrition among the most vulnerable children and women, with a specific focus on children under-two. This essential package will ensure that pregnant women have access to good antenatal nutrition, including iron-folic acid; infants benefit from timely and exclusive breastfeeding from birth to six months; and young children receive adequate age-appropriate complementary foods and micronutrients while breastfeeding continues. To facilitate access to nutritious diets and nutrition services, UNICEF will also support the provision of cash transfers targeting 380,000 of the poorest pregnant women/caregivers of children under-two.
UNICEF will strengthen and scale-up early detection and referral of children suffering from wasting using different strategies. To improve access to lifesaving treatment services, UNICEF will scale-up the provision of nutrition curative interventions through the outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) and strengthen the quality of services. UNICEF will target around 621,600 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition and will procure 808,080 cartons of ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) (including 30 per cent buffer stock).
To reach the estimated targets and planned response scale-up, UNICEF will build on existing nutrition services at the health facility and community level to deliver maternal and child centered integrated nutrition services. As a provider of last resort, UNICEF will scale up the adoption of a ‘simplified approach’ to treat SAM cases until fully cured in all OTPs.

1 - Needs

Prior to the current conflict, malnutrition in Sudan has been a persistent issue over the past three decades, with child stunting and wasting rates amongst the highest in the world. Chronic malnutrition is a major public health concern with a prevalence of 37 per cent which is above the WHO critical level, meaning that more than one-third of children in Sudan are unable to develop to their full physical and cognitive potential. The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) stands at 14 per cent with an expected incidence of over 3.4 million children under-five suffering from wasting and 621,600 of these children suffering from severe wasting (SAM); the most life-threatening form of child malnutrition. Around 40 per cent of the localities in Sudan, have GAM rates that exceed the 15 per cent WHO emergency threshold. The nutrition sector and partners are estimating an increase in the burden of acute malnutrition as result of the deterioration of the underlying determinants of nutrition status. These estimates were based on extensive discussion within the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG) of the nutrition sector who estimate 30 per cent increase in the caseload of acute malnutrition in hotspot states (Khartoum state and some localities of Darfur state), a 15 per cent increase in IDP hosting localities, and a 10 per cent increase in remaining localities. This resulted in increasing the total annual caseload for SAM among U5 children to 690,000. Micronutrient malnutrition is also widespread with 48per cent of children under-five being anemic and 58 per cent of children under-five being vitamin A deficient.Acute malnutrition among mothers is also high at 8.6 per cent, with 16 per cent of women of reproductive age estimated to be too thin, and almost 30 per cent of non-pregnant women and 40 per cent of pregnant women being anaemic. This contributes to low birth weight, which affects 32 per cent of newborns in Sudan, contributing to the intergenerational cycle of malnutrition. The contributing factors to malnutrition in Sudan are wide-ranging and complex – a combination of poor diets, high prevalence of co-morbidities (malaria, diarrhoea, respiratory infections among children under-five years), limited access to basic health, nutrition, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and social protections services and inadequate nutrition practices. These factors are underpinned by high levels of food insecurity and poverty, exacerbated by the ongoing catastrophic humanitarian and economic crisis, inflation and high food prices, protracted conflict, internal displacements, climatic shocks (drought and floods), harmful social norms and cultural practices. Approximately half of the population is at or below the poverty line, and food insecurity remains alarmingly high. The number of acutely food insecure people continued to increase for the third year in a row, reaching a record 16.2 million people in high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), which is 24 per cent of Sudan’s total population. Food insecurity forecasts projected to increase an increase in the number of food insecure people in Sudan as a result of the ongoing conflict; meaning that children under-five, pregnant women and mothers/caregivers of children under-two will continue to face limited access to nutritious, safe and affordable food. Infant and young child feeding practices among children under-two years are suboptimal in Sudan. The rate of exclusive breastfeeding at six months is 62 per cent, reaching its lowest (20 per cent) in Central Darfur (Um Dukhun locality). The timely introduction of adequate complementary feeding is limited with an estimated 75 per cent of children 6-23 months not consuming the minimum dietary diversity recommended by the WHO and UNICEF.

2 - Activities/Outputs

UNICEF’S nutrition response will be in line with the nutrition sector’s main objectives as outlined below. To reach these objectives, UNICEF will work with the State Ministries of Health and humanitarian partners on the ground on accelerating the implementation of its maternal and child-centred integrated nutrition services. Objective 1: Scale-up access to quality integrated lifesaving preventive and protection minimum package nutrition services. To significantly reduce the number of children who suffer from wasting, UNICEF will deliver a package of preventive interventions, ensuring that the youngest, poorest and most vulnerable benefit. The package will take into account the main drivers of malnutrition and the strengths and limitations of the health system and community platform. The package is focused mainly around the First 1,000 Days and includes iron-folic acid for pregnant women, maternal, infant and young child feeding nutrition (MIYCN) counseling for pregnant women and caregivers of children under-two, vitamin A supplements and deworming. Overall, 1.6 million pregnant women and mothers/caregivers of children under-two, and 5.9 million girls and boys under-five will be reached through this package at the facility and community-levels, including 2.2 million of the youngest and most vulnerable children under-two years of age. In addition, UNICEF will also support the provision of cash transfers to 380,000 of the poorest pregnant women and caregivers of children under-two in order to help eliminate the financial barriers that women and children may experience in accessing nutritious diets and essential nutrition services. UNICEF will strengthen and scale-up early detection and referral of children suffering from wasting. A combination of strategies will be used including bi-annual mass MUAC screening, targeted locality screening, family MUAC and routine household-level screening. Objective 2: Improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services to treat severe acute malnutrition among under-fives. UNICEF will increase the treatment coverage of severe acute malnutrition among the under-fives and ensure timely procurement of 808,080 cartons of RUTF (one carton per child, including 30 per cent buffer stock) to reach 621,600 children with SAM. This aligns with UNICEF’s nutrition acceleration plan that aims at increasing treatment coverage, improving the quality of services and scaling-up the package of preventive and curative interventions. As part of this, UNICEF will support multiple and context appropriate approaches across the states of Sudan to increase the treatment coverage for children with SAM while ensuring complementarity with targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP). Objective 4: Improve understanding of the nutrition situation in prioritized localities. UNICEF will support evidence generation through high-quality MICS and SMART surveys to provide up-to-date information for understanding the evolving nutrition situation. To support the implementation of integrated preventive and curative nutrition services UNICEF will:• Continue to secure the pipeline of all essential preventive and curative nutrition supplies to prevent, treat and assess children with acute malnutrition. UNICEF will ensure the timely procurement and distribution of therapeutic milk (F-75, F-100), ReSomal, routine OTP drugs, deworming tablets, vitamin A, iron-folic acid and anthropometric measurements. • Strengthen the health system through efforts to strengthen supply chain management of nutrition commodities, nutrition information systems and building the capacity of front-line health workers on the nutrition package and monitoring to ensure the delivery of high-quality nutrition services.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Beneficiary's safety and protection are one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. UNICEF has zero-tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation andabuse (SEA) and harassment. UNICEF has trained and sensitized its staff and implementing partners to report any incident of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence: both incidentsrelated to staff and incidents related to beneficiaries. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners on their sensitivity to the matter, including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy on protection (prevention and response):  The children, women andmen UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse. UNICEF has zero-tolerance for (sexual) exploitation, abuse nor harassment. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors. All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment. All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone, or tolerate behaviour that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, and harassment:  An organizational culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organizational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero-tolerance for sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented.  Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights.  Swift and credible investigation and sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and sanctions for violations by UNICEF personnel, and actively promote swift and fair investigations and sanctions by (as appropriate) Government partners, implementing partners, commercial vendors, and other UN System organizations.  Survivor-centred response: Provide survivor-centred assistance and support that is timely, predictable, sustainable and adequately resourced.  Engaging and equip partners in the fight against SEA and harassment

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is one of UNICEF core commitments in humanitarian actions. UNICEF is consistently encouraging its partners at the national and sub-national level to design and implement beneficiary-centred interventions. Efforts will be made to advocate for establishing efficient feedback and complaint mechanisms at implementing partners project sites to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have suitable means and methods to raise their voice and reflect their ownership of the services provided. Different modalities will be used, such as establishing hotlines, dissemination of a mobile number of focal points for feedback and complaints, provision of complaint boxes at health facilities, community feedback logbook and patient exit interviews. UNICEF is also an active member of the interagency platforms and is committed to making significant-quality contributions to it. At the global level, the UNICEF’s Executive Director has assumed the role of Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Champion for the prevention of SEA and SH for one year. As part of this role, UNICEF has led the development and adoption of an IASC Champions Strategy on the protection from and response to SEA and Sexual Harassment.
