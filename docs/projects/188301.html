Project Summary

NRC project will contribute to Education sector HRP objectives 1, 2 and 3. NRC will deliver this project through a combination of direct, partner-led and hybrid modalities.
The Education intervention forms part of NRC’s safe, integrated and inclusive response in Syria. NRC’s Education team will continue working in close collaboration with NRC’s ICLA/Capacity Building, Shelter, WASH, and Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS) programmes to enable conflict and displacement affected populations to meet their basic needs, enjoy their rights, and benefit from pathways to durable solutions. NRC will deliver pathways back to learning, integration into formal education, acknowledgement of previous learning through supportive policies (accreditation, certification, examinations, and documentation) and expansion of skills for employment. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable children and youth across Syria through the delivery of its Child Education Pathways (CEP) and Youth Education Pathways (YEP) programmes. Each response modality (Emergency, Transition, and Recovery) is designed to ensure that conflict and displacement-affected children and youth are provided with age and context-specific educational pathways. All three modalities have been designed to provide equal access to and to welcome all out of school children and youth, prioritizing those that have been displaced by the conflict and are returning home from both inside and outside Syria. All three modalities include capacity development opportunities for teachers, trainers, facilitators and coaches and will be implemented in cooperation with NRC’s ICLA/Capacity Building, Shelter, WASH and LFS programmes and partners. The teacher training package has been adapted from the INEE Teachers in Crisis Context and includes NRC’s Better Learning Programme (an evidence-based Psychosocial Support intervention developed by NRC and the University of Tromso). A particular focus is given to a safe return to school after periods of COVID-related lockdowns: teachers circles will integrate the guidance included in UNICEF “Ready to go back: Teacher preparedness Training Package”.
The target group of NRC’s Education Programme in Syria is defined as conflict and displacement-affected children and youth , including Syrian IDPs, IDP and refugee returnees, host community members, Third Country Nationals and Iraqi refugees. Exact target group definitions will vary for the different response options:
• Emergency Response activities will target newly displaced and returnee children and youth;
• Transitional Response will target out of school children and youth in contexts of long­ term displacement and prolonged absence from school with activities tailored on their specific context (see more below);
• Recovery activities will target vulnerable populations living in post-conflict and/or stabilized camp and non-camp settings (see more below).
Teachers, facilitators and trainers working in NRC programmes in formal and non-formal education settings, as well as vocational trainers will also be targeted across the 3 response options. Whilst equal access will be provided to both male and female educators, considering the current scarcity of male educators, those present will be actively encouraged to participate, in an effort to provide positive male role models in all activities.
For all NRC Education activities, civil documentation will not be a pre-requisite to receive assistance. Parents engagement is fostered through awareness raising campaigns and the set-up of Parents and Teachers Associations.

4 - Needs

11 years into the Syrian crisis, over 7 million children (3-17 years) are in need of education support (Education Sector Findings). In 2020, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020). Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions and increased population displacement, the number of children out of school and of children and youth who are at risk of dropping out is almost certain to increase. 84% of all school aged children live with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditions. The conflict has forced young people to assume adult responsibilities due to the inability of households to meet basic needs. Instability continues to underpin crippling levels of poverty across the country, with 90% of the population living below the poverty line and unemployment at 55%. Compared to the overall Syrian unemployment rate, youth unemployment is significantly higher. Female youth reflect the highest rate of unemployment (84%). 213,219 teachers and other education personnel are in extreme or catastrophic need and over a third of schools operate in shifts" and remove from "leaving to understaffed. Improving teachers' capacity is reported as the top priority for improving education services.

5 - Implementation of activities

CEP & YEP Emergency: designed to provide safe/welcoming spaces for children/youth when newly displaced. Includes life skills, psychosocial & recreational skills. CEP-Transition: non-formal learning opportunity for out-of-school children, preparing them to access education in formal settings. Unconditional Cash for Education used to respond to financial barriers to enrollment and attendance in formal education and to limit drop-outs and maintain attendance in CEP remedial/YEP transition programs.CEP-SLP: A non-formal programme for children who have been out of school for over a year to catch up on learning. CEP in Camps/Communities: A non-formal programme targeting children without access to formal schools. YEP-Mobile Vocational Skills: 5-10 day competency-based training modules providing opportunity for unemployed male & female youth to develop new skills. Creating Welcoming Schools: Rehabilitation of learning spaces, teacher professional development, in kind materials, Student Councils & Parent Teacher Associations, NFI kits. YEP 3+3: 3-month competency-based training, followed by a 3-month coaching, targeting unemployed male & female youth for safe & sustainable livelihood. CEP-Remedial: a 3- 6-month programme for children recently returned to school at risk of dropout. YEP Transition a 3-4 months programme for youth who are preparing for grade 9 &12 exams. PSS activities integrated in all programs. NRC pathways available for distance learning.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Potential SEA risks are identified during beneficiary selection process, the distribution of assistance and while accessing services. NRC communicates clearly with communities on the expected assistance modalities and confidential complaint and feedback channels available to them. During post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs), NRC inquires if people were asked to exchange anything for aid or service. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks in PDMs who, according to the recipients of aid, provided the assistance they received, enabling identification of possible areas of concern. All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC's Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and child safeguarding (CSG) policies. Several sessions take place with staff to raise awareness on PSEA and CSG, in addition to sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete mandatory online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and CSG reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams who regularly updates guidance to emerging needs and trends to tailor management and support to cases. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM have started and are expected to conclude by mid-2022. Child Friendly PSEA materials are also made available to the project teams.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. NRC staff, are injured, killed, or suffer psychological trauma due conflict: Mitigation: Security briefings, HEAT and HEIST trainings, briefings, more support from UNMAS.2. Fraud/corruption/bribery. Mitigation: enforcing NRC's policy of zero tolerance through strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the "whistle-blowing" policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures. 3. NRC is unable to timely operate due to the impact of internationals sanctions. Mitigation: request for derogation, looking for different banking channels, advocacy.4. NRC is unable to timely implement due to delays in approvals from Government/local authorities. Mitigation: Advocacy plan in place, set the lead time for approval submission to be 2 weeks with a close follow.5. NRC is unable to timely deliver the planned activities due to rapid inflation and change of prices. Mitigation: review budget and targets on a quarterly basis, communication with the donors, find more convenient payment terms to suppliers.6. Donor policies, including overly restrictive redlines and perceptions, affect the ability to implement and scale-up programs. Mitigation: Engaging with donor, Joint advocacy efforts with DINGOs, Intensify the negotiations with local partner and MOUs, Long terms programs and reconstructions, media coverage in private and public advocacy.7. C-19 Restrictions: SOPs for safe implementation during C-19; adapted implementation modalities.
