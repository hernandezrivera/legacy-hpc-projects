Project Summary

Since the start of the conflict in December 2013, IOM has been providing lifesaving humanitarian assistance to Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in conflict affected locations in South Sudan. The proposed project will build on IOMs significant experience in the provision of health and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services and will continue to support rapid response mechanisms, which provide lifesaving health services to conflict-affected populations in hard-to-access areas.
In 2019, IOM will aim to achieve the below four health cluster specific objectives:
1 - Improve access and scale-up responsiveness to integrated essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality;
2 - Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks;
3 - Increase access to essential clinical health services among vulnerable populations targeting SGBV, NCDs, SAM with Medical Complication;
4 - Improve resilience among vulnerable populations by increasing access to mental health and psychosocial support services.
Through static and rapid response teams, IOM will continue to provide essential preventative and curative primary health care (PHC) services in Bentiu, Malakal and Wau protection of civilian (PoC) sites, and other locations across South Sudan, targeting a total of 150,000 beneficiaries, of which 78,786 will be reached through mobile rapid response teams, and 71,214 will be reached through IOM's static PHCU facilities.
Health services will be provided in line with the Health Cluster’s essential health care package for PHCUs and Mobile Teams.

Needs

Five years after the crisis, access to primary health care (PHC) services continues to be unavailable for a large majority of the population in South Sudan, with an estimated 3,869,574 South Sudanese women, men and children requiring humanitarian healthcare services in 2019 according to the Health Cluster HNO for 2019. An unstable political environment, protracted conflict and resulting displacement, and continued economic deterioration and inflation of the pound has created a disabling environment for a well-functioning health system and impact health seeking behaviour. The number of health workers in the country is below global critical levels, standing at 4.4 per 1,000 population, alongside a doctor to population ratio of 0.015 per 1,000 people, and a midwives/nurses population ratio of 0.02 per 1000.Women and girls are disproportionately impacted by the protracted crisis due to an increase in sexual and gender based violence (SGBV). South Sudan repeatedly experiences multiple and simultaneous infectious disease outbreaks year after year, most recently the 2016 - 2018 cholera outbreak, the longest in the country's history. Challenges in procurement and transport of medicines is another challenge that leads to stock outs of medicines and medical supplies. The prevailing health system challenges are exacerbated by lack of water quality control, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management, expansive malnutrition and a looming threat of viral hemorrhagic diseases, including Ebola, necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health securities to mitigate disease spread and enhance beneficiary experience. As a result of these aforementioned factors, it is imperative that lifesaving health care is provided through both static and mobile responses in areas highly populated with vulnerable individuals. There are now approximately 1.95 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) living in prolonged displacement, many in host communities and sharing already scarce resources. This includes 198,446 people inside PoCs (2019 HRP PIN). IOM will maintain its static clinic strategy in the PoCs in Bentiu (3 clinics), Malakal (1 clinic) and Wau (1 clinic), as well as Wau collective centres of Nazareth and Cathedral (2 clinics). All seven static clinics provide lifesaving PHCU and referral services to IDPs as per the Health Cluster’s Essential Package of Health Services. IOM will also continue to provide comprehensive HIV and TB testing and treatment services in the PoC locations. To address vulnerable populations outside of the PoC, IOM will continue to mobilise its Rapid Response Health Teams (RRT), which deliver lifesaving primary health care and outbreak response (including immunization campaigns) in hard-to-reach populations. The conflict has created conditions that have left men and women and girls and boys vulnerable to stress and trauma and sexual and gender based violence. The protracted nature of the conflict has caused the traditional coping mechanisms to fragment. IOM will continue to mainstream MHPSS into ongoing PHC services through strengthening the multi-layered approach to community-based mental health and psychosocial support activities. This includes the provision of focused psychosocial support targeting primary health care facility patients and caregivers, as well as integration of key messages promoting mental health and psychosocial well-being in IDP sites and surrounding host communities. The project also intends to contribute to capacity building activities for existing community institutions and social service providers, such as HIV counsellors and mental health workers, and the integration of MHPSS into case management and protection mechanisms. All proposed actions are in line with the IASC guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, which outlines an integrated, inter-sectoral, and multi-layered approach in providing MHPSS services.

Activities

Health services will be provided in line with the Health Cluster’s essential health care package for PHCUs and Mobile Teams as outlined below; Static PHCU;• Outpatient consultations for common diseases;• Maternal and Child health (MCH), including pre- and post-natal care, normal deliveries, family planning, and MISP;• Routine EPI and mass vaccinations;• Outpatient for TB and HIV;• Nutrition screening for children under 5 with MUAC;• Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support; • Basic management of SGBV survivors, including PEP, STI treatment and wound management;• Management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity;• Referral of complicated medical cases;• Health promotion/risk communication on various health topics, including malaria (use of bed nets, avoiding stagnant water), use of clean water and sanitary facilities, health seeking behaviours, promotion of breastfeeding, mobilization for vaccination campaigns;• Water quality control at health facilities; • Environmental health (vector control and medical waste management);• Sentinel site of early warning system of epidemic prone diseases, outbreak response (EWARS).Mobile (RRT) response• Outpatient consultations for common diseases;• Mass vaccination and case management for epidemic prone diseases (meningitis, measles, OCV);• Basic management of SGBV survivors, including PEP, STI treatment and wound management;• Provision of psychosocial first aid;• Vaccination against measles and polio for all children under 5;• Vaccination against tetanus for pregnant women (if not received);• Management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with sever food insecurity;• Referral of complicated medical cases;• Health promotion/risk communication on various health topics including malaria (use of bed nets, avoiding stagnant water), use of clean water and sanitary facilities, health seeking behaviours, promotion of breastfeeding, mobilization for vaccination campaigns, mental health messaging;• Activity based reporting.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

IOM Health Unit undertook a client satisfaction survey in October 2018 targeting clients seeking primary health care services in the IOM static clinics in the PoC sites. The survey asked open-ended questions on what the clients thought were the strengths of the clinics (services) and what ought to be improved. Recommendations/suggestions for improvement from actual clients who have received our health services was sought, which has been utilized to inform the design of the program. Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IOM works with health and hygiene promoters, who provide a link between the health facilities and the community. They pass on messages at community level through various modalities such as house-to-house and through community engagement sessions, such as at the market place, schools, and other areas of congregation. These health and hygiene promoters also provide support in identification and referral of patients to health facilities, as well as defaulter tracing for HIV, TB and other services. IOM also takes opportunity to commemorate global health-events such as World Health Day, World TB day, World AIDS Day, World Mental Health Day, etc at which key health messages and information is relayed to communities on services available and means to access. Regular meetings are held with community leadership (CHC), at which health information is passed, to be relayed to communities, and feedback loops allow community leadership to pass on issues and/or complaints.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context-specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity, for example reproductive health messages specific for women of reproductive age. IOM develops messages based on the information collected during assessments to understand community needs. Message are translated into local language keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. IOM communication team are experienced in developing context specific messages on issues such as malaria, Ebola and HIV/AIDs, and coordinate with other cluster for specific sectoral messages such as messaging on protection and GBV. IOM also coordinates with relevant partners, such as UNFPA and UNESCO, in the design of outreach materials and messages for youth.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The IOM Health teams are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse in line with the IOM Code of Conduct. Health teams also work closely with IOM’s Protection Officer and IOM’s GVB Specialist to ensure that protection and PSEA is mainstreamed in all activities and that safety and dignity of targeted population is safeguarded. At the field level, Heads of Sub-Offices have rolled out PSEA training for casual workers and volunteer staff.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Project consultations are conducted prior to mobile responses, with community leaders such as chiefs, and representatives of varied groups within the community, such as people with special needs (PSN) and women to ensure that beneficiaries are identified without bias. When possible and appropriate, IOM holds separate discussions with different groups, ensuring a wide range of ages are covered. The IOM Client Satisfaction Survey was designed as an assessment tool to enable feedback from a broad and representative sample of beneficiaries; it is provided to a random sample of those exiting the health facility, with questions relating to satisfaction with services.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IOM highlights the availability of feedback and complaint mechanisms and encourages beneficiaries to raise complaints. In all health facilities, IOM maintains a suggestion box to enable beneficiaries to confidentially raise any concerns. Furthermore, in IOM-managed PoC sites, IOM maintains a regular complaint and feedback mechanism where individuals can reach out to camp management who will then channel the complaint to the responsible sector. The Health unit meets with CHC on a weekly basis, as well as with community groups on a regular basis, at which feedback is obtained, which is utilized to inform the delivery of subsequent actions, as appropriate.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring and Evaluation processes prioritize feedback from communities to measure the impact of the intervention. IOM ensures that all groups in the targeted community have opportunities to voice their opinions on assessing the results the intervention. The information are analysed and shared with the response team to inform intervention.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM is committed to ensuring the specific needs of women, girls, boys and men of all ages, abilities and ethnicities are identified, considered and addressed. Gender considerations are integrated in planning, assessments, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of all projects. Gender and age disaggregated data is collected to assist service providers and protection partners to provide targeted and responsive programming. All Health interventions are based on a needs-assessment, baseline, context and conflict-analysis, stakeholder analysis, and risk analysis considering specific needs of the targeted community. Further, all activities are coordinated with Cluster and State Focal Points that provide crucial background information and analysis on protection risks, as well as specific needs of different population groups. The Health team also works closely with IOM’s Protection Officer and GBV Specialist for all interventions. This enables the team to have a comprehensive understanding of protection risks considering specific needs of different groups, and upon which ways forward are identified and carried out. Assessment missions are undertaken jointly with other sectors, using internationally recognized tools that include protection elements, such as the IRNA tool.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The Health team will conduct regular risk assessments and develop risk mitigation and reduction measures to ensure that access to health facilities is safe and dignified. The team will regularly consult women and girls on their use of facilities and the feedback received shall be used to enhance program implementation so as to reduce risks that women and girls will face assessing health facilities. Mobile responses are monitored to analyse the access of communities in order to inform the design and delivery accordingly. IOM ensures that there are female staff on all teams to tackle cultural barriers whereby women prefer to be consulted by women, particularly as regards reproductive health.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Prior to any intervention, needs-assessment, baseline, context and conflict-analysis, stakeholder analysis, and desk reviews enable the health team to identify any protection threats or inequalities and design projects accordingly. IOM ensures the involvement of women in all health teams.
