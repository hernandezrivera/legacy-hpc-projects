Project Summary

This project will cover a period of 12 months. The project will start in January 2025 and will be concluded in December, 2025.
This project will be working closely with youth groups at various strata of the northeast society to explore their potentials in managing conflict, reactions to VEOs misinformation and peace building. The project is also designed to provide gender awareness, leadership induction, advocacies and trainings directly benefitting youths and advocates media campaigns through Radios, Printed Media, and Bill Boards. This media campaign will reach to almost all youth generation in the target area and raise awareness about their active role in civil society and policy initiatives. The project will adapt a cultural sensitive strategy, where all the activities will be planned and conducted in a way that the negative impacts will be avoided. Youths, which are both males and females, will be the target and they will be reached during the course of this project. Based on our team observations, our society is now ready to accept that youths are integral part and change agents of the society. The project will take advantage of this new emerging feeling of the society and build upon the concept that this is the time where Nigerian youths must play a great contribution to bring prosperity to the war-affected society (Boko Haram Insurgency) by influencing the policy initiatives. In this generation, media has become an important part and plays a vital role in our society. Additionally, the stronger role of media is now obvious in Nigeria; it has broken the shackles of the centuries-long self-imposed superstitions in almost every walk of life. People feel empowered with the rising level of awareness on various issues. The media can eliminate the existing deprivation of youths and the steps could be taken towards their empowerment and their role in the Nigerian civil society and policy initiatives. So through broadcasting of radio programs (Phone-in) and messages about youth empowerment and their role in the society, they will be aware and sensitized in the society. However, this project will make the targeted youth groups fully empowered through various capacity building trainings and empowerment programs on peace building and Violent Extremism towards building community resilience to emerging conflicts. YOYICOD will use it technical skills and expertise to encourage them to take part in community development activities, managing conflicts and better understanding of their role in peace building, conflict transformation agenda, networking, learning and communication.

1 - Needs

A decade of conflict and insecurity, hunger and massive displacement in Northeast Nigeria has left the populace in extremely vulnerable and at high risk of violent extremism situation. While the humanitarian response in the region has stabilized the condition of small number of the previously affected populations, perpetual external shocks limit the population’s access to basic services causing fluctuating levels of vulnerability. As a result women, youth and children are forced to adopt risky and sometimes harmful coping mechanisms, including listening to campaign by JAS/ISWAP group to recruit vulnerable community members through religious sermons, cash inducements, loans, gifts and provision of food items. Pressure for survival intensifies the potential continuous violence. Communities in NE Nigeria have a long history of shared culture, values and customs that are unique in fostering community bonds and solidarity which is reflected in the peaceful nature of communities especially in the pre-insurgency era in the region. However, while infiltrating communities, VEOs seek to divide the communities and drive them towards conflict by providing negative messages that undermines government policies, misinterpretation of religious provisions and teachings. This reinforces ethnic tensions, stereotype, diversity as well as creating loopholes for VEOs to utilize. Under this project, YOYICOD will engage traditional and religious leaders, youth and women groups, professionals that directly engage with community members like the department of social services, public health, teachers etc to support in popularizing alternative narratives to VEOs negative messages through different campaign approaches and strengthen community cohesion to increase citizens consciousness for peaceful coexistence and tolerance in Bade through the following approaches (1) Build and sustain resilience at the community and individual level: For many of those who have experienced terror events, psychological trauma is the immediate result. This invisible wound has adverse, pernicious and long-term effects on the victims and the communities. (2) Strengthen victims’ roles in preventing violent extremism: Victims of terrorism, whether direct, secondary or indirect, have credible voices to add to the counter-narrative debate, from policy formulation to prevention and intervention. By sharing their own stories, victims give a human face to the effects of terrorism, thus providing a damning critique to the terrorist discourse. (3) Contribute to inter-faith, intra-faith and intercultural dialogues: One of the aim of this project is to bring young victims together, from different communities and different religions, to promote peace-building and foster resilience. Encouraging dialogue between victims of terrorism, who have different backgrounds, experiences, stories, faith; helps contribute to their resilience and foster tolerance. They feel less isolated as they share a common experience. That way, they understand that they are not alone and they develop empathy by interacting with others

2 - Activities

Community entrance and Joint advocacy to the State and Local Governments of the targeted LGAsHigh-level engagement with the Governments authorities will be made in the places where this intervention will be carried out. To gain buy-in from the relevant stakeholders to achieve full community ownership and sustainability of the initiative in the future, advocacy visits would be organized for the identified key influencers such as traditional and community leaders, religious leaders, ward development committee heads, wealthy individuals, as well as associations in communities. (1) Government Authorities (National Emergency Management Agency – NEMA, Sate Emergency Management Agency – SEMA, Nigerian Army): YOYICOD has developed good relationships with all the Government authorities at STate and LGA levels. Although the humanitarian capacity of these official entities is extremely limited, YOYICOD will maintain the same level of cooperation with them, whose support is essential to ensure a proper project implementation.(2) Village leaders/ Lawan & Bulamas (key informants, religious leaders, elderly people): YOYICOD as a CBO regularly works with the village leaders, who use to play a key role in identifying targeted beneficiaries within their communities, as well as in sharing information, monitoring activity progress and providing feedback. Following an owner-driven approach, YOYICOD will keep on involving the village leaders, who will be mobilized to ensure the participation and cooperation of their communities throughout the project implementation. Joint visit to the communitiesYOYICOD in consultation with local authorities will plan joint field visits to the communities in Bade. These joint visits will mainly focus on raising awareness, identification of project beneficiaries for interventions, and planning of field activities on monthly and quarterly basis Mobilisation and sensitisation of communities on peaceful coexistence, tolerance, violent extremism and survivors of Boko haram: this will create awareness in the community of their right and role in identifying and setting priorities on preventing violent extremism, counter radicalization and survivors’ re-integration towards peace building through their participation in the community Multi-stakeholder Dialogue Sessions: There will be dialogue sessions to enable the stakeholder understand the Disbandment deradicalization and VEOs messaging and misinformation process and contribute their quota in preparing communities participation. Capacity Building workshop: Capacity-building sessions will be held on monthly basis for 8 months curriculum to support selected youth to be peace champions and mentors to other youth. Topics may include, among others: SDG 16 and the role of youth, VEOs misinformation, leadership, good governance, conflict management and peace building, civic responsibilities, behavior change communication, advocacy, human rights, gender and women’s empowerment. This will build the capacity of traditional and religious leaders, social welfare officers to facilitate sensitization process such as youth platforms and radio programs. Radio programmes would focus on the positive role young people can and do play in conflict prevention, the prevention of violence, and the promotion and consolidation of peace. This session will create opportunities for youths to share goals and aspirations with adults as well as among each other, to capitalize on their experiences and assets, and to engage in multiple areas, including social, emotional, moral, spiritual, civic, vocational, physical, cognitive, personal and cultural development. Increase, understanding and opportunities for cooperation among Youths, parents and elders, in order to act jointly to prevent and resolve violence and transform conflicts. Youths’ leadership and roles in preventing and resolving conflict, violence and extremism are rich resources essential to building sustainable peace.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming, the project will implement several key modalities. First, all activities will be designed to uphold the safety, dignity, and privacy of beneficiaries while avoiding harm. Risk assessments will identify potential threats, especially for vulnerable groups like women, children, and persons with disabilities, allowing for effective mitigation measures. The project will establish accessible and confidential feedback and complaint mechanisms to ensure accountability to affected populations. Communities will be actively engaged in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of activities, ensuring that interventions align with their needs and priorities. The project will also promote inclusive participation by addressing barriers that may prevent marginalized groups from being involved, such as cultural norms, language, or mobility challenges. Disaggregated data will be used to monitor participation and tailor interventions to address inequalities. To ensure access to services, adaptations such as transportation support, accessible infrastructure, and multilingual materials will be provided, with a focus on reaching the most marginalized communities. A conflict-sensitive approach will be adopted to minimize unintended consequences that could exacerbate tensions or vulnerabilities, with an emphasis on fostering social cohesion through collaboration and dialogue among diverse groups. Additionally, all project staff and partners will receive training on protection principles, gender sensitivity, and safeguarding practices to strengthen their capacity to identify and respond to protection risks. By embedding these modalities into all project activities, the project will create a safe, inclusive, and empowering environment for all beneficiaries.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure that feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members through a combination of inclusive, accessible, and culturally sensitive mechanisms. Dedicated outreach efforts will be made to engage marginalized groups, including women, children, persons with disabilities, and the elderly, to understand their unique needs and challenges. Feedback channels will be designed to accommodate varying literacy levels and language preferences, including the use of verbal, written, and pictorial formats, as well as multilingual communication materials. Confidentiality and anonymity will be prioritized to encourage open and honest feedback, with options for submitting feedback in private settings or through trusted community representatives. Mobile feedback teams will visit remote or hard-to-reach areas to ensure that even those without access to transportation or technology can participate. Community feedback committees, composed of representatives from diverse vulnerable groups, will be established to facilitate ongoing dialogue and input. Regular community engagement sessions, such as focus group discussions and town hall meetings, will provide opportunities for direct interaction and feedback. These sessions will be scheduled at times and locations that are convenient and safe for vulnerable individuals. Additionally, dedicated feedback and complaints mechanisms, such as toll-free helplines, suggestion boxes, and digital platforms, will be available for continuous input. All feedback will be documented, analyzed, and incorporated into project design and implementation to ensure that the voices of the most vulnerable are central to the project’s success.
