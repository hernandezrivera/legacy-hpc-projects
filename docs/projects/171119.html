Project Summary

Project Objective: The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected in Gaza, drawing from the following Country Strategic Objectives and FSS activity:
• Country Strategic Objective #3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to
violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
• FSS Objective #1: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their
resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced and Objective #2:
Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.
The project focuses on the most vulnerable, including small-scale farming households who live in the most vulnerable communities and have been affected by the ongoing deterioration of the situation and/or COVID-19 crisis in Gaza Strip
The project aims to support affected communities in Gaza by rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for small scale women and men farmers, breeders, water wells and carrier lines including promotion of technical capacities and improving their production. The activities are designed based on standards adopted by technical working groups of the FSS, and in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected communities. The activities are closely coordinated with FSS, MoA, stakeholders and local CBOs in the targeted locations.
Moreover, and as part of cross cutting activities, women’s role and participation at a community level will be promoted through active engagement with local project committees and local council committees.

1 - Needs

In the oPt food security continues to be affected by the protracted nature of the crisis,repeated shocks, continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and a lack of economic opportunities, following the restrictions imposed to contain the COVID-19 pandemic. These restrictions have had a severe disruptive impact on the entire food system, including producers, processors, marketers, transporters and consumers. Small producers have been hit by the reduced demand, the unprecedented challenges in securing basic inputs, and the impediments in accessing credit and markets. 62% of the HHs, or 1.3 million people in Gaza, equivalent to 68% of population considered severally or moderately food insecure (FSS, HNO 2020), where the COVID-19 pandemic and the PA’s financial crisis have compounded the socio-economic impact of the longstanding Israeli blockade, the internal Palestinian divide and the frequent escalations of hostilities. Following the outbreak of COVID-19, the unemployment rate in Gaza Strip reached 49.1% (HNO 2021). CARE and MAAN conducted comprehensive needs assessments during Sept 2020, where farmer communities in Gaza face challenges in their protection, livelihoods and resilience due to ongoing crisis, blockades, and climate change shocks. According to RAPID GENDER ANALYSIS TO INFORM THE 2021 HRP in oPt conducted by CARE/OCHA food and nutrition security is also connected to challenges associated with the Israeli occupation. Women &children in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector, reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility, and limited technical preparation. As a result, food insecurity has also disproportionately impacted women/children and marginalized groups. The overall guiding principle for CARE Palestine (WBG) theory of change is adopting an impact driven approach, acknowledging that our impact groups (women, girls, boys and youth) require emergency and early recovery supports, however CARE will utilize interventions that address both immediate needs, as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. CARE focuses on restoring the capacity of vulnerable farming communities and livestock holders, and enhancing the ability of women and men farmers to recover from the protracted crises and cope with the environmental shocks. Based on CARE various reflections and learning, the gender transformative programming, female Gazans negative experiences of the crisis are compounded by social norms which inhibit their access to food, inputs, finance and livelihoods’ services, as well as lower control over income and household decision making. In Sept 2020, CARE Palestine (WBG) and OCHA generated gender analysis reports with the aim of helping HPC actors in the oPt. Women in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector. This is reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility, and limited technical preparation. According to the need’s assessment conducted by CARE/MA’AN in Sept 2020 and interviewed CBOs, women and male farmers farmers and herders are particularly vulnerable considering their direct exposure to the effects of the conflict, current COVID-19 pandemic and their dependence on small-scale productive activities to meet their basic food and income needs. The intervention builds on CARE and its partner MAAN various and comprehensive assessment carried out during Sept 2020, the findings indicate that, the destruction due the conflict and the effect of COVID-19 pandemic heavily impacted the agricultural sector. There are still significant needs in poultry farms which recently remain unmet (MoA 2020). Most of the interviewed framers indicated constrains accessing to irrigation water due to damaged wells. The lack of possibilities to rebuild these agricultural infrastructures leaves households unable to provide neither for their own consumption, nor for income generation.

2 - Activities

This intervention will aim to restore and enhance the resilience, food security, productive capacity of vulnerable households in Gaza to protract their livelihoods taking into account the local impact of climate change and current COVID-19 pandemic. The report generated by CARE Palestine (WBG) and OCHA in September 2020, showed that the COVID-19 pandemic has resulted in additional significant declines in livelihoods, workforce participation, and household incomes for both women and men. As such, the project aims to support affected communities in Gaza by rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for small scale women and men farmers, breeders, water wells and carrier lines including promotion of technical capacities and improving their production and using the BBB (Build Back Better) approach for increased resilience. The activities are designed based on standards adopted by technical working groups of the FSS, and in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected communities. The activities are closely coordinated with FSS, MoA, stakeholders, local CBOs, while being aware of what is offered in the targeted locations, as per the FSS coordination mechanisms 2020. The activities will be implemented in partnership with MAAN who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the field level. CARE will be responsible of the overall quality management and will act as the main contact for donors and stakeholders. The project activities will be as follows: Activities for O #1: Lands Rehabilitation1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end beneficiaries.2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, and vulnerabilities.3: Cleaning & ploughing, and installing the local irrigation system.4: Distributing farming manual tools, accessories, local fertilizers, seeds and seedling.5. Temporary employment will be created (CfW) for 70 unemployed skilled and unskilled workers to generate income. Activities for O #2: Poultry Farms Rehabilitation including provision of renewable power supply1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end beneficiaries.2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, and vulnerabilities.3: Rehabilitating the poultry farm at targeted HHs.4: Installing solar system (1 kw) for the targeted poultry farms as per their outlet capacity needs with close and strong coordination with the MoA and FAO. Activities for O #3: Maintenance/Rehabilitations of Water Wells.1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end localities and beneficiaries.2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying the localities and damages.3: Rehabilitating the wells as this will improve access to water for many farmers who don’t have access to water and will be able to get the water after the wells’ rehabilitation. Activities for O #4: Installation of renewable power supply for Irrigation water wells and collective ponds.1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end localities and beneficiaries.2: Conduct need assessments to identifying the BoQ.3: Installing the solar system for the water wells (20 - 35 kw) and ponds (2-8 kw) as per their outlet capacity needs with close and strong coordination with MoA and FAO.

9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection will be mainstreamed in the project delivery (including respect for principles of non-discrimination, do no harm, etc.); and cross-cutting issues, including gender, age, environment and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Furthermore, the project will be incorporated into CARE’s broader Impact, Learning and Accountability System (ILAS), which has overarching quality control standards and process for monitoring, promoting reflection and capturing learning, evidence, outcomes, and impact. Finally, there is a potential adoption of indicators under the guidance of the FSS, upon the availability of resources and consolidation of good practices. CARE is committed to assuring accountability to the communities we serve and stakeholders in all aspects of the project. the design of the project took into consideration the different needs of the different groups in the communities (sex and age), where the communities informed the design of the project activities, the composition of the teams, frequency of the services, etc. Monitoring will have carried out through on sight monitoring of distributions and activities; monitoring system will be designed to include segregation of data by sex and age. Partner will follow the same monitoring guidelines and through reporting provide information. A confidential complaint and feedback mechanism will be designed, allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE. CARE's complaints mechanism: in order to ensure that the interests of the beneficiaries are continuously addressed and in line with CARE's 'Do No Harm' approach and Humanitarian Accountability Framework. In order to protect CARE's beneficiaries from any unintended harm caused by the project implementation CARE is committed to assuring accountability to the communities we serve and stakeholders in all aspects of the project. In addition, a monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and communities leaders about project development and challenges, and beneficiary’s satisfaction survey will be completed which will enable beneficiaries to provide recommendations to maximize the benefits and highlight lessons learned. A comprehensive risk analysis and security incidents will be discussed and updated in a participatory approach with the target population. Consequently, mitigation measures (such as low profile methodology of activities by beneficiaries) will be carried out to reduce potential risks adhering to the principle of “Do No Harm”. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

During the needs assessment phase, several meetings with local committees and different stakeholder will be conducted. The content of these discussions formed the basis on the projects scope, approach and trend of damages, which help us to develop our vision of the project’s design. As well, the project will have local committees, which will be formed to represent and facilitate the access to farmers in the field. The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of targeted communities, based on a number of key sources, including comprehensive assessments conducted for similar project implemented by CARE/MAAN (AYADI Project), various and comprehensive need assessments conducted by CARE & MAAN in Gaza Strip, HNO 2021/FSS, coordination with FSS and with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Gender Transformative Insights (CARE WBG 2019), a series of consultation workshops/ FG were held by with representatives of farmers/ herders, women's CBOs & local councils in targeted locations to revalidate their needs; with taking into consideration the gender and age groups’ needs. All of the designed activities reflect the needs realized at the field level, and which also are in line with what has been identified from HNO 2021. Most of the interviewed framers/herders indicated high constraints in accessing to irrigation water due to damaged/ deteriorated water wells, as well as high needs to rehabilitate of agriculture lands and maintenance of poultry farms through the need assessment phase. people with specific needs were also addressed in our need assessment and are included within CARE projects. Beneficiaries have contributed to the design of this action through local committees. In addition, beneficiaries will be involved in the project monitoring and evaluation process during and after the implementation in order to provide recommendations to maximize the benefits and highlight lessons learned. Beneficiary knowledge of this system has been promoted through a number of field assessments and monitoring visits. In addition, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report any complaint through the beneficiary feedback mechanism. CARE/MA'AN will publish the project’s interventions. Numbers of Orientation workshops for the targeted communities will be conducted to announce the project and give an idea about the project’s plans, budgets, selections criteria and other details of the project intervention. In addition, advertisement tools will be distributed in public places such as: mosques, municipalities, and CBOs, to ensure that the information about the project would reach most of the potential beneficiaries. CARE/MAAN have successfully created a coordination and cross-checking mechanism with other actors such as (MoA), food security sector (FSS), local and international NGOs, along with the local committees in order to verify and identify the neediest and affected areas and beneficiaries.
