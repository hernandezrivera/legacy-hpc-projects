Project Summary

Giving continuity to its previous experiences, COOPI will work in the Governorates of R.Damascus, Dar’a, Aleppo, Hama, Homs,Idleb and Raqqa. Moreover, COOPI will achieve fruitful synergies with the currently implemented projects on protection and food security and livelihood, as the ongoing interventions grant access to the targeted underserved areas and to the most vulnerable beneficiaries. Project aims to enhance resilience-building supporting targeted vulnerable households (HHs) to reach self-reliance.
Thanks to a special attention to youth and women as preferred targets of empowerment activities, project will also contribute to the wellbeing of their families and communities, supporting the reinforcement of income generating activities (IGAs). Vocational trainings will be organized to enable the beneficiaries to gain needed skills and knowledge to start their own production and to maximize the resources provided through FSL activities (e.g. dairy products, vegetables). Project will subsidize the costs of training for all beneficiaries in order to guarantee their participation.
In vocational trainings on transformation and conservation of vegetables, the topics will also focus on basic economic elements functional to the marketing of the products and to the good management of the resulting revenues, while vocational training for the production of dairy products (eg: cheese, yogurt, etc.) will focus on the physical and chemical characteristics of milk, heat treatments, production methods of dairy products (yogurt, cream, cheese, butter, etc.).
This activity aims furthermore to purchase the equipment (eg: jars, gas stoves, kettles, etc.) necessary for the production and storage of vegetables in order to have a lasting supply of food even after the harvest period and basic equipment for the production of dairy products for the start of a small income-generating business linked to the sale of products on the local market
COOPI will target the vulnerable families with the characteristics to be engaged in the proposed initiatives in urban and rural areas, providing them the needed support to reduce their social vulnerability and to find sustainable coping strategies. Furthermore, this approach will allow to move from dependence on humanitarian aid to more sustainable food security for the HHs. On the model of the previous experiences in Aleppo, for 2022 and 2023 Community Led Initiatives (CLI) will be carried out also in other targeted areas, in order to sustain self-managed initiatives coming from affected communities, to respond to their needs and to strengthen internal community cohesion and resilience. Additionally, COOPI has already established very good relationship with community leaders, resulting in greater acceptance by communities and easier provision of assistance in a timely manner.
The project will contribute to SO3 “Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities”, especially among the most vulnerable HHs and communities, reaching in total 6,600 people in 2 years through vocational trainings, IGAs and rehabilitation of productive assets. A focus will be kept on woman headed HHs, vulnerable youth, HHs with PwD and high number of children, IDPs and Returnees in order to facilitate the cohesion and the relationships with the host communities. Though information sharing and consultation with the communities, the main community’s needs will be identified and COOPI will give the support to implement the actions proposed directly by the participants to the CLIs.
COOPI will adopt an integrated approach among its projects taking place in the same governorates, in order to avoid overlapping and promoting synergies. COOPI will coordinate with all the relevant Sectors, but also with national and international actors active in the same areas in order to implement a harmonized response.

4 - Needs

During the past year, the protracted crisis continued to sharply affect the socio-economic situation, pushing in need 14 M people, representing 64,5% of Syrian population (according to the Global Humanitarian Overview 2022). The lack of early recovery activities leads to an increased reliance on harmful coping mechanisms, as high risk jobs, child labour and early marriages, as well as to livelihood long-term instability for the less resilient people. Furthermore these challenges are pushing people to spend their savings, go into debts or reduce the number and quality of meals. According to HNO 2022, 79% indebted HHs increased its debt and 70% of population is unable to meet their basic needs. The pandemic worsened dramatically the already critical situation, exacerbating pre-existing and underlying socio-economic vulnerabilities, by reducing availability and access to basic services, livelihoods and employment opportunities. In line with HNO 2021, an estimated 11,6M people need early recovery and livelihood support all across the country. Among 66% HHs suffered loss of income sources, and about 50% lost 75% to 100% of their previous income sources. Additionally, most vulnerable groups, such as PwDs and female-headed HHs, increasingly depend on assistance rather than being able to rely on different recovery-oriented, livelihoods opportunities. Furthermore, the shortage of livelihoods resources led to competition on the job market, creating tensions within the communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in the Governorates of R. Damascus, Aleppo, Dar’a, Homs Hama, Idleb and Raqqa. The intervention aims to improve the quality and variety of food products for the rural families through the increase and diversification of crops and vocational training courses for the food and domestic horticulture production and preservation. Furthermore, vocational trainings about diary products production and preservation will take place, including marketing and business management, in order to enable the participants to start their own activity. IGAs will be addressed to selected vulnerable beneficiaries, focusing on woman headed households and youngs. This activity aims furthermore to purchase the equipment necessary for the production and storage of vegetables and dairy products in order to have for the start of a small income-generating business linked to the sale of products on the local market and preservation of crops.In all project’s locations CLIs will be realized, enhancing community cohesion and resilience. FGDs and consultations with the communities will be carried on in order to promote the direct participations of community members. Once the communities’ initiatives based on the detected needs are proposed, COOPI will identify the initiatives to be implemented and will provide the selected group with the materials, support and required tools within an agreed timeframe of implementation.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R: stigmatization and discrimination of the individual/groups receiving the services at community level or in the family if the interventions are not properly explained or not conducted in the proper environment. L: Medium. I: High. MM: ensure consultation (before and after the activities) with potential beneficiaries and targeted individuals, as well as local authorities when appropriate, for proper design of the intervention (location/space, staffing and gender consideration, modalities of service provision, communication with the communities on the nature of the services); based on the feedback received, ensure consultation with communities on the type of services, the aim and scope of the intervention, the nature of the services.R: tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. L: High. I: High. MM: establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities; consult with actors on the ground to communicate in transparent manner clear, transparent and targeting criteria and project activities. R: backlash from family or community on women participating in vocational or empowerment activities. L: M. I: H. MM: community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle; thorough gender analysis prior to start of programming; engagement with the community and local authorities and clear communication on project objectives.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers during delivering assistance. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation measure: establish formal and informal feedback mechanisms in each area of intervention; provide staff with training on Code Conduct and PSEA; ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; establish mechanisms and procedures for ensuring compliance with PSEA zero-tolerance policy. Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA focal point have been established in COOPI country offices and headquarter. All staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitations and abuse. When COOPI will intervene in new areas, PSEA FPs will be nominated and trained, being in charge of supervising the use and the dissemination of PSEA reporting tools among communities, staff and third parties.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R: tensions between IDPs and host communities. A: the host communities insist on different initiatives and approaches in implementation of CLIs. MM: transparent process of the selection of CLIs. R: the consultation processes with the communities are hijacked by community leaders. A: community leaders are not involving the community, especially vulnerable people. MM: COOPI will facilitate communication with the community spreading information about the initiative throughout different levels, organizing also preparatory meetings. R: high drop-off of the participants to the vocational trainings. A: women are not allowed to participate to the trainings, lack of interest by the beneficiaries. MM: proper assessment and targeting of the beneficiaries in order to determine real interest in participating to the trainings; selection of trainings suitable for beneficiaries preferences; engage with the community and local authorities and clear communication on project objectives. R: staff/community members are infected with COVID-19. A: sanitary measures are not respected or PPE are insufficient to protect the staff and the community members during the meetings. MM: organize the meetings and the activities with the communities adopting the needed measures to safeguard people's health, avoiding to create concentrations of people; provide enough PPE for the activities; train staff and the participants to the CLIs about the measures to prevent COVID-19 infections.
