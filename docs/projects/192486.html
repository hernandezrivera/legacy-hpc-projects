Project Summary

Women migrant domestic workers (WMDWs) – an already marginalized group, continue to be disproportionately impacted by Lebanon’s manifold humanitarian crisis. Due to the Kafala system, WMDWs live with almost no legal and social protection, facing high levels of exploitation and different types of abuse while facing huge obstacles to access justice. They rarely resort to the authorities to seek redress, often due to isolation in their place of work, fear from the authorities, lack of awareness about legal procedures and their rights, the absence of financial means, and language barriers.
In light of this situation, UN Women in partnership with Kafa (enough) Violence & Exploitation and GIZ are partnering to address the emergency protection needs of WMDWs in Lebanon. This project works towards the achievement of the ERP Objective 3: to enhance timely, unhindered, and equitable access to protection assistance for migrants. Under this project, migrant women will have access to a 24/7 helpline, increased access to legal and protection services, better access to quality MHPSS services, and basic and humanitarian return assistance. Socioeconomically marginalized migrant women will be targeted across all regions in Lebanon. UN Women will oversee and monitor the overall project implementation. Services will be provided directly by KAFA, with support on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) technical assistance provided by GIZ.
Under 1.1, UN Women and Kafa will conduct mass awareness raising of legal information as a first step to increase their access to protection and obtainment of their legal rights. The campaign will utilize both social media platforms and traditional communication materials, including fliers and leaflets in high traffic areas. Given the fuel and electricity crisis, expanding outreach interventions to migrant women who are not online or have access to internet and mobile phones, is essential. All materials will be translated in the languages of migrant workers. Legal aid will also be provided via individual and group legal counseling, and legal representation for specific cases.
Under 2.1, Kafa will continue the operation of an established 24/7 helpline dedicated to MDWs. The helpline provides information counseling and provide referrals to specialized legal actors and service providers. Victims of physical and sexual abuse, they will be provided with immediate socio-legal protection information and/or measures. MHPSS will be implemented through different modalities. Social workers operating the helpline will provide immediate psycho-social counseling for WMDWs in psychological distress and make specialized refers. KAFA will also provide direct PSS interventions, including targeting the mental health of migrant community leaders in order to help them manage stress and prevent burnout. GIZ has been a key actor in the MHPSS response for migrants in Lebanon, and will provide quality assurance, and advise on technical referrals to specialized services, as well as coordination with the MHPSS task-force for migrant domestic workers.
Under 3.1, While protection data will be collected and monitored across all project activity implementation, UN Women will specifically collect and analyze data on female migrant population in North Lebanon (Tripoli and Akkar), with a focus on gender and protection in order to respond to data gaps in that region and improve it service provision outreach.
Under 6.1, UN Women and Kafa will provide multi-purpose cash assistance to address urgent needs of WMDWs. The cash assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable WMDWs to cover rent, and other basic needs.
Under 7.1, UN Women and Kafa will facilitate voluntary humanitarian return assistance for WMDWs facing high-risk protection issues. A specific focus will be given to scaling up voluntary return support to migrant women who are in detention, and arbitrary detention – a violation of human rights.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The specific project activities include: provision of legal assistance (1.1), quality protection and mental health and psychosocial support services (2.1), gender and protection assessment of North Lebanon (3.1), multi-purpose cash assistance (6.1), and voluntary return assistance (7.1)

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

This project will use gender sensitive approaches to ensure women’s protection, such as prioritizing the recruitment of female frontline staff, monitoring women’s utilization of protection cash assistance, and adopting new outreach strategies to identify migrant women left behind and not accessing services and assistance available. In addition, UN Women has strong policy and prevention mechanisms in place to prevent SEA in humanitarian response context. Under this project, all partners must meet UN Women’s minimum standard requirements for PSEA including having a PSEA focal point, staff mandated orientation/trainings and ensure beneficiaries have access to a safe and confidential referral mechanisms.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Ensuring a feminist approach and women’s empowerment is at the heart of this project, whereby the needs and perspectives of female affected populations will be prioritized. This is an incredibly important issue of all recipients of aid, but particularly migrant populations who have not been accustomed to receiving aid on a wide scale in Lebanon. Accountability for affected populations will be integrated into every aspect of UN Women’s project monitoring and evaluation framework. The project will ensure that there is a complaint and feedback mechanism so that all migrant women can voice their concerns and resolve disputes with regards to the delivery of aid. PSEA will be integrated into these mechanisms. In addition, a steering committee made-up of migrant women beneficiaries from the targeted communities will be the main forum for regular community-led feedback. Their needs and suggestions will help guide project implementation to keep agencies accountable to migrant women.
