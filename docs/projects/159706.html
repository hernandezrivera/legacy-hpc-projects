Project Summary

Following cumulative below-average rains due to consecutive seasonal poor performance Deyr (October-December 2018) and Gu’ (April-June 2019) rains coupled with dry harsh condition of the first quarter of 2019 and limited recovery from the effects of 2016/2017 drought, Somalia continues to experience troubled and protracted humanitarian crisis illustrated by deteriorating food security crisis, diminishing livelihood opportunities and basic services access challenges. As a result of climate shocks, conflict, widespread poverty, and increased vulnerability, the lives of 6.3 million Somalis is again at stake comparable to the 2017 Gu’ figures. Up to 2.1 million people are projected to be in crisis and emergency as of December 2019 and more than 2.6 million people remain internally displaced due to drought, conflict and lack of livelihood opportunities of which the recent analysis by FSNAU indicates that most of the 14 major IDP camps are in crisis or stressed in the presence of humanitarian assistance
Access to education in Somalia is among the lowest in the world, especially for girls and women. Much of the population, including many youths, do not have sufficient skills to take advantage of income generation and employment opportunities. This project aims to address the need for skills training among unemployed and underemployed persons, to enable them to find jobs, move up the skills value chain or earn income through self-employment. Training subjects have been selected based on an assessment of growing sectors and skills gaps in the economy
This project mainly targets to address socio-economic problems that faced by youth from both men and women. Skills shortages affect both men and women although women are especially disadvantaged by having less access to education. The increased household income that will be made possible through skills training will benefit all household members. An estimated 80 percent of beneficiaries of monthly household transfers are female and 20 percent male. Through this, SWA will help empower women and young girls to access income generation and improve their HH livelihoods. In addition, SWA advocated for the inclusion of women in the project management committee at community level. One of them will actively participate in decision-making discussions.
SWA proposes to implement the vocational skills trainings as outlined above for 300 participants (180 beneficiaries) from the IDP communities (60%) and Urban Poor families (40%) for the period January to Dec 2020. The first month will be for selection of beneficiaries and biometric registrations and training will be undertaken for remaining 9 months. In addition to this, literacy and numeracy skills will also be offered for the selected students as required. In most cases, SWA selects participants from IDP and poor host community, and the selection criteria is food insecurity and basic literacy - which means the selected students must at least be able to read and write some basic Somali. Those who cannot read and write are enrolled in a different program of literacy and numeracy, which runs between 3 and 12 months depending on the level of the participants. The literacy / numeracy participants however will not benefit from this program- as this proposal is mainly focused at the 300 students under vocational skills training. The organization has good relations with MOE as the ministry monitors the program, and even accredits the courses that are offered.

Project Activities

SWA will submit all distribution reports as agreed with Food Security Cluster, and to confidently implement the C&V programme as per agreed guidelines from the Food Security Cluster. SWA will also seek support from FSC team on ground from time to time, to ensure that the programme is implemented successfully. SWA has the capacity and commitment to successfully implement the work plan. At last SWA will be responsible for the implementation of this program, and will also ensure good coordination between the funding partners, and also with the line ministries. The programme will be implemented as per FSC set standards in terms of interventions, monitoring, result reporting, feasibility and all other mechanism that ensure the success of the project and assures that these most disadvantaged persons are reached promptly. and the organization will also submit the needed reports accordingly. Whenever possible, the organization will coordinate the different partners for joint monitoring at the center. Weekly attendance sheets will be used to record the number of hours each trainee attendees. The minimum accumulative attendance of the trainee must be greater than 80% to qualify to sit for the final exam. The ministry team will check attendance registers and will allow only these trainees who meet the eligibility.The proposed project envisions to build on implementing the needs assessment and market assessment on skills gap and vocational programs that are tailored to suit the needs on ground. The beneficiaries will avail from a wide range of easy to master marketable training skills that has proven to successful and enhanced reduction of poverty, job creation and dependency. Women and girls will be the majority of the participants and this is designed as a mainstreaming strategy for gender economic empowerment that leads to economic independence of women, accessing skills and market and delivering women and girls in decision making process. The staff will perform the day to day activities of the centre in delivering training sessions, mentoring and administrative issues. One of the staff is assigned for the security of the centre as to safeguard the students during the sessions. SWA has also implemented human rights projects - mainly advocay for women rights, education and good governance. the organisation has partnered with NRC,CARE,ADRA,Worldvision on most of these projects. SWA has a good working relationship with the Puntland authorities and with the communities that it has served, and is confident that this proposed project will be successful. SWA has hired two experts trainers who will be training for the beneficiaries during the nine months of the project. The trainers had over ten years experience in working skills training programesThe target area has experienced repeated food insecurity crises over the last several years. Human capital has deteriorated through lack of investment in education and there are major skills gaps in the economy. There is a need to develop skills that will enable community members to earn an income that is sufficient for their household's food security. A recovery type response is recommended to address this situation. Since it began this project lots of change has created inside of livelihood improvement since those who benefited this project have learnt skilled that developed their income.
