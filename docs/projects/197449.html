Project Summary

SEMA through this project will provide CP and EDU intervention as integration approach,
the action will increase access to non-formal education and strengthen the protection of
vulnerable girls and boys who are out of school or at risk of dropping out, to build their
resilience and lead them to easy transfer to formal education in Al Bab, Afrin, Azaz, Idleb and Jisr Ash-Shugour sub districts. The non-formal education
interventions for 4500 (B:2250, G"2250" including 1) Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for out of school children of the ages 5-17 who have missed more than two years of school, when the children complete the planned program and assessed through ASER to prove that they can transfer to their suitable classes in formal education) For schooled children
with learning gaps, remedial using curriculum B and homework support will be offered to limit the risk of
dropping out. SEMA will provide children with (3-6) aged group with ECCD program to enhance their skills to start the formal education smoothly with good emotional and social skills. SEMA will enhance the capacity for 100 educational actors (M:50, W:50) through providing trainings and coaching to ensure teachers’ and children’s wellbeing and to help teachers obtain skills and methodologies for the creation of a safe and quality learning environment for children. For guided self-learning modality, teachers will be trained to support caregivers with guidance and tips to support their children’s learning and provide them with a safe
learning environment at home. In addition to developing the capacity of teachers on child
protection and enhancing the accessibility and safety of the school environment. Teachers
will be trained on safe identification and referral for CP concerns.
SEMA will provide social emotional learning as an integrated approach. Teachers will be
trained to work with children to develop their life skill, resilience and learning
competencies in the safe learning environment of the classroom by using SEL " IRC "curriculum.
To enhance the access of CwDs SEMA will provide transportation for 30 children (B:15, G:15) to the CWD and other
vulnerable children such as girls at risk of early marriage in addition to rehabilitation for
learning spaces to be accessible for CwDs, provide cash for education to 200 (B:100, G:100) children who are
out of school for economic difficulties based on vulnerability selection criteria, establish
temporary learning spaces where none exists to provide a safe travel distance for
children. In addition, SEMA will ensure a safe& quality learning environment, through
providing school rehabilitation with WASH facilities, light repairs, and school kits and
stationery.
Evaluation and Monitoring for all activities will be in place. Cholera prevention procedures will be taken into considerations as well.

3 - Needs

In reference to HNO 2023, There has been a 4 per cent increase in the sector PIN, with6.9M children and education personnel needing emergency education services,Attendance rates are similar for boys and girls up to 11 years, after which there is genderdisparity with more boys not attending. Transitioning to secondary school remains achallenge, only six governorates had <10% of children attending secondary school. This isfurther exacerbated by the lack of vocational schools. Only 11 per cent of four-year-oldand 36 per cent of five-year-old attended any type of learning, limiting school readiness.Rates of non-attendance was highest in Aleppo (16%) and Idleb (15%). Data shows that ifa child is displaced more than three times, there is a higher chance of not attendingeducation (20%), with 42% less likely to attend in the first three years of displacement.Dropout due to marriage is more of barrier for IDPs in camps (5%) than other populationgroups. Additional data sources estimate that there are over 2 million children (6-17) thatare out of school. The action will address needs that will support the elimination ofbarriers to access to education. Those barriers include: poverty not allowing families toafford education costs, displacement, security due to distance to safe learningspaces/schools, child labour, unavailability of number of learning spaces, and disabilitieshindering access to school and at school if buildings are not equipped to facilitate access.

4 - Implementation of activities

SEMA will provide 5,000 children with activities to ease access to education byrehabilitating classes, establishing temporary learning spaces, WASH facilities and schoolkits, and with appropriate learning programs as per children’s assessed needs. ALP willensure out of school children (OOSC) receive the cycles necessary to support asustainable reintegration using curriculum B. Remedial education programs, based on theformal curriculum in NWS and NES, will ensure retention as children recover gaps in theirlearning. Guided self-learning programs using the SLP curriculum will reconnect childrento learning to ensure that no child is left behind and limit the OOSC numbers. Teachersvisit children weekly to provide support, follow-up and assign learning materials. Cash foreducation can also be provided where schools are available, but the economic conditionsdo not allow families to send their children to school. Children will take a placement testto assess their academic level and provide them with the program, grade level andsupport appropriate to build on and further their learning experience. The children with 3-6 aged groups will be provided with ECCE program using AS IRC curriculum. Class observation on a regular basis will support M&E and adaptation, regarding CPintervention it will be through providing: Structured PSS sessions, enhance the capacity ofteacher through conducting trainings on CPMS, Identification and referral and SEL.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usuallyattached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. SEMAwill ensure the field teams attend PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessionsprovided by SEMA internally or externally. SEMA’s PSEA focal points responsibility is toensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either softcopies in case of online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed toboth staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including thecomplaints pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor ifany danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings inorder to take the appropriate measures. SEMA team will also inform the community,particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order tomitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.
