Project Summary

The project intends to provide a holistic, comprehensive and intersectoral response to the problems of the Education System in EES and Lakes State, starting from the assumption that schools represent platforms through which also Protection, Nutrition, Hygiene, Health and Psychosocial needs of vulnerable children can be addressed. Moreover, the intervention wants to support the entire education system, starting from nursery and primary school, up to secondary and Vocational training, including the alternative programs offered by the Accelerating Learning Program (ALP). Therefore, learners and teachers from all the different steps of education system will be supported in an integrated way, by providing scholastic materials, training and sensitization sessions on subjects such as education, child protection, SGBV, health, psychological well-being. Structural interventions will be done in schools to improve the quality and safety of the learning environments, i.e. Temporarily Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines and school kitchens, erection or rehabilitation of fences. Schools will be used as an entry point to promote good hygiene and health practices through hygiene sessions, HIV/AIDS prevention and reproductive health sessions. The students and teachers will also be supported in improving their food security status, by receiving training and agricultural inputs for school gardens for their daily consumption. Moreover, the project wants to introduce an innovative intervention to improve the nutrition status in the schools, providing schools with improved stoves with the benefit of limiting the costs for schools and families spent on fire wood for the preparation of school meals and mitigating the use of fuel wood, the most essential local source of energy. Upon successful utilization by the school matrons, similar stoves can be built in the house of these women and presented as a model to neighboring households.
On the basis of a holistic concept of education, the project aims to involve also the other relevant actors in this process, first of all the parents and the communities at large. For these reason, parents, community members, PTAs & SMCs will be involved in Campaigns, Open Days and meeting to promote the importance of education and discuss about sensitive topics such as education for girls, violence and SGBV, child protection, among other.
The objectives of the project are:
• To improve the quality of education and to ensure access to safe learning spaces for vulnerable children, adolescents and youth
• To provide access for children, adolescents and youth to integrated social protection services
• To promote resilience and capacity building of teachers, parents and community at large
The beneficiaries of the projects will be students, teachers, PTAs and SMCs from 1 VTC in EES and 61 schools; 50 schools in different Counties in EES, i.e. Magwi County (including Nimule), Ikwoto County, Magwi County (including Nimule), Narus/Kapoeta East County, Kapoeta South County, and 11 schools in Cueibet County, Lakes State.

Needs

Five year after the beginning of the conflict, the state of education in South Sudan continues to be worrisome. As reported by the 2017 SS Education Cluster Assessment, 41% of schools were not functional at the end of 2017. As shown by the Annex E (School Functionality) of the mentioned assessment, this problem has affected especially the communities of Eastern Equatoria State, where AVSI conducts most of its educational projects. The lack of adequate teaching and learning materials and functioning school infrastructures, both in term of quantity and quality is a major challenge. Many schools supported by AVSI miss classrooms to host all the pupils, especially the youngest ones, having nursery sessions and P1-P2 often occurring at open-air. Latrines often need renovation and are not sufficient or gender segregated. Moreover, schools are missing fence, paramount to ensure a CFS, where pupils can study and play safely. Another major problem is related to the scarcity of food. Although AVSI and WFP have increased the supply of food deliveries to schools, inadequate funds for the acquisition the firewood needed for meal preparation is limiting the impact of this effort. Based on AVSI’s experience in more than 60 schools supported in 2018 across SS, (AVSI’s studies and surveys are accessible in the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response website) those who are penalized the most by the combination of food insecurity, poverty, violence, and the underfunding of the education sector are the youngest. AVSI’s Final Impact Evaluation of the UNICEF-funded 2017 PEACE project revealed that children between 3 and 9 years of age and pupils enrolled in nurseries and P1/P2 classes face major challenges to enter and remain in school, thereby losing the opportunity for a healthy cognitive, physical, linguistic, and socio-emotional development, at a critical development phase. In most schools where AVSI works, in fact, the average enrolment for P1 and P2 classes is lower (approximately 25% smaller) compared to P3, to P5 classes. Likewise, dropout rates are greater for lower classes, especially for P1 pupils. Anecdotal evidence confirms that, and indicate that pupils in P1 and P2 are less likely to be attended by teachers, to have a class and proper desks, and to be supported by family members, who also put more effort in the education of their older children. Girls are another vulnerable group of students, both in terms of enrolment (high masculinity rate) and dropout rates. By analysing 33 schools across EES, AVSI found that there is a gender gap in terms of enrolment (not much noticeable in Torit, Magwi and Ikwoto, where the masculinity rate ranges from 53% in P1 to 59% in P8, remarkable in Kapoeta, and very severe in Cueibet --Lakes State --, around 70%) and in terms of dropout (very widespread across EES and Lakes, especially among students enrolled in higher classes (P6, P7 and P8). The mentioned weaknesses are strongly exacerbated by the lack and turnover of teachers and pupils. The replacement of trained teacher by volunteers is leading to a de-professionalization of teachers, both in terms of basic pedagogy and classroom management. As revealed by AVSI’s 2018 Greater Ikwoto Multisector Household survey, this problem is very much felt by local families, who reports the lack of teachers as being the second most important reason for not enrolling children in schools. Teachers also lack the capacity to give appropriate psychosocial support to conflict affected children. According to the 2018 AVSI Greater Ikwoto Multisector Household Survey, in Ikwoto 7% of HHs reported sexual abuses on 7 to 17 years old children in the previous 12 months, around 15% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physically abuses and harsh punishments, 8% was discriminated against. AVSI’s Kapoeta region Multi-Sector Household Survey shows that 24.1% of children reported of having either been forced into sex, defiled or raped.

Activities

in 2019 AVSI will continue implementing a 2-year UNICEF-funded EiE project (PENCILS) along with additional activities of PSS, child protection and GBV & peace building, nutrition and food security, scholarship and training for primary and secondary pupils and teachers , TVET and ALP all funded with own funds, the EU-funded BRICE project (Torit; Ikwoto) and the EU (DEVCO) SORUDEV SSR project (Cueibet; Rumbek Central), that AVSI realizes by acting as EU's implementing partnerBelow are the activities proposed; • Structural interventions in schools, i.e. Temporarily Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines, kitchens/stores. In 3 schools it will be tried a pilot project of living fence, for example using Moringa oleifera, with the add value that Moringa leaves can be eaten • Provision of teaching and learning materials to primary and secondary schools, and desks and chairs to nursery• Support to school gardens to ensure children are benefitting from school meals, through training and delivery of inputs• Support to sport and peace clubs, Provision of recreational materials and organization of recreational activities in primary and secondary schools• Formation/support to hygiene clubs and Provision of hygiene supplies (soap, detergent, handwashing facilities, etc) to primary and secondary schools• Hygiene promotion and sessions on Cholera prevention, HIV/AIDS prevention, Reproductive Health, Menstrual Hygiene Management and distribution of sanitary kits to grown girls • Sessions to sensitize female adolescents in primary and secondary school on girls’ education, early marriage & pregnancies, SGBV• Training for nursery teachers and PTA on Early Childhood Development and Provision of ECD kits to nursery schools• Training for teachers and PTA and SMC on school and classroom management, life skills, Mental health, PSS and counselling; child protection; referral pathway; parenting education; SGBV awareness• “He for she”: creation and sensitization of men’s groups on SGBV • Involvement of parents, community members, teachers, PTAs & SMCs in Campaigns and Open Days in the schools, to promote the importance of education, with a focus on girls and young children’s education• Follow-up of absentees and irregular pupils at school through visits at home to address the issue• Filling of Feedback & complaint forms on activities implemented in schools to submit to lists of randomly selected pupils’ parents (number of parents proportional to the pupils enrolled in each class of the school) • Periodic school-based meetings with PTAs, SMCs and Community leaders on Education and Child Protection status in their area (obstacles preventing the access to education, challenges faced, opportunities, way forward, cases of abuse, violence and SGBV, family separation, use of drugs, discrimination) and introduction of a decentralized mechanism for monitoring and reporting• Improved stoves delivered to schools to decrease costs in firewood and mitigate the consumption of firewood. Upon successful utilization by the school matrons, stoves would be built in the house of these women and presented as a model to neighbouring households as a means to sensitize and spread the adoption of those stoves.• Cash assistance to a pre-selected list of most vulnerable protracted IDPs willing to send their children at school• Cash-for-work for school infrastructures rehabilitation and construction for those schools not supported with PENCILS• Support to ALP through delivery of learning and teaching material, teachers trainings and incentives, to promote access to education in accelerated timeframe for disadvantaged and over-aged children and youth who are out of schools• Support to 1 Vocational Training Center, through provision of materials and items for 3 vocational courses on Building and construction, Carpentry and joinery, Tailoring, dye and tie courses, start-up kits for learners and incentives for tutors and other staff

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yearly AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and subcounty (payam) officials and traditional leaders (boma and sub-boma chiefs). During these events AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. Last September, for instance, in Ikwoto, along with the results of the 2018 Greater Ikwoto Multi Sector Household Survey, the need to move toward more development-oriented interventions and thus to apply more demanding criteria for the selection of beneficiaries was discussed with the communities. In that occasion AVSI received very positive feedbacks from the community as well as suggestions regarding operative mechanisms to employ for the implementation of projects.AVSI current interventions in Education sector target more than 60 schools, all located in Eastern Equatoria State (Torit, Magwi, Ikwoto, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East counties) and Lakes State (Cueibet county). The affected population benefits from the daily presence of a widespread network of 13 Community mobilizers covering the supported schools and providing beneficiaries with information on the already implemented and the forthcoming projects’ activities. In addition, a team of 10 Social workers plus 1 Teachers mentor is conducting weekly visits and activities in the supported schools, maintaining a regular contact with beneficiaries and reporting the challenges faced and the level of satisfaction of students and school administrations.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As AVSI works a lot with vulnerable communities, the messages, training and guidance provided by AVSI personnel are tailored to the needs and capacities of people with almost no schooling experience and provided in the local language. Most of the field work realized by AVSI is facilitated by the employment of local personnel who speak the local language and have the notions and the experience needed to contextualize guidance and messages. Beside the staff presence in the field, also the organization of Open days and meetings with parents taking place in several schools, and during which SMCs, PTAs and teachers are likewise involved, allows to tailor interventions to the specificity of the operational context, representing a model for a deeper local community engagement as well as a mean to guarantee the relevance and sustainability of teaching and learning. Furthermore, AVSI’s long-standing experience in the field, and in the Education sector as a whole, contributes to increase the effectiveness of the Action.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The numerous permanent bases that AVSI has throughout the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes for unanimous reporting of abuses or complains. Moreover, prevention of GBV (sexual exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at services access points (schools, health facilities and community). Concerning the projects, focusing on gender and age-groups of the affected communities, AVSI designs its projects in order to reinforce the protection of the most vulnerable beneficiaries. Here is the ratio of including tailored activities targeting female adolescents and younger pupils as well as sessions to sensitize selected groups of people on SGBV (men included), early marriage and pregnancies, hygiene practices and physical and mental health.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Conducted two two-stage sampling household survey that gathered information from around 800 families. The main findings of the surveys, and their implications in term of activities recommended, were discussed and validated through community meetings attended by RRC representatives, subcounty authorities (payam administrators) traditional leaders (boma and sub-boma chiefs) and open to the population at large. Besides that, the affected population benefits from the daily presence on the ground of 13 Community mobilizers based in the supported schools and 1 Teachers mentor based in Ikotos, ensuring a regular dialogue with the headmasters and a constant sharing of information on the implemented and planned projects’ activities. Moreover, weekly visits to the schools are conducted by a team of 10 social workers, creating an important link between the field activities and the office, reporting challenges and the level of satisfaction of the beneficiaries.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Besides the annual surveys, AVSI has a tested system of Detailed Post-Distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community at large. During each field visit AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes for unanimous reporting of abuses or complains. Furthermore, and in addition to Open Days and meetings with parents in the schools, the above-mentioned activities concerning the introduction of follow-ups forms to register absenteeism and irregularity cases among the pupils, and the collection of feedback and complaints from the pupils’ parents, are intended as complementary tools to strengthen the existing mechanism of gathering beneficiaries’ opinions on the services provided and to possibly reshape the interventions intercepting evidence-based needs. Pupils’ enrollment and daily attendance monitoring is fundamental to safeguard their educational path.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As conceived, this project proposal incorporates mechanisms for keeping the relevant stakeholders fully and periodically involved in the decision-making process, and fully informed of the implementation and results, thus reaching transparency and accountability toward the affected population.Every other month AVSI conducts community campaigns that allow for open discussion over the quality and progress of the activity conducted. Several actions, for instance Field Farming Schools, have built-in systems of community monitoring. Monthly AVSI reports to the Education State cluster the result of its activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, discapacities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 5 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Besides the above-mentioned survey, for its largest projects AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind a follow up interview of Key informants, field personnel is conducted to review precisely the barrier that impede a more equitable targeting of the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers. This has led, to cite some examples, at timing the interventions of FSL more in line with the seasonality followed by farmers, and focusing educational support to nurseries and lower primary classes.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AVSI’s Foundation shall ensure that the assistance during this project does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the women, men, girls and boys receiving it, and is provided in ways that respect their rights. Equal participation will be ensured through the involvement of women and men equally in decision-making right up from induction meetings, and routine focus group discussions. Likewise, it will be made sure that trainings will target at least an equal number of female and male, that the monitoring will includes sex- and age-disaggregated data, that the project personnel is trained on gender/ protection topics including GBV identification, handing and referral. Moreover, AVSI’s projects include activities of livelihood and capacity building especially tailored to women, individually and as women groups, and is matched by educational projects that always include GBV and peace building sensitization for pupils and training on GBV and referral path for teachers and PTA members. The proposed intervention includes two modes of protection-related activities, preventive and remedial ones. The first category is referred to activities aimed at preventing abuses to happen or reducing exposure to protection threats for vulnerable people, favouring an environment where the rights of all different groups are respected. These specific activities regard session on disease prevention (Cholera, HIV), trainings on SGBV and periodic school-based meetings on cases of violence and abuses among children, with the proposal to introduce a mechanism of monitoring. The remedial activities of the proposed intervention have the purpose of rehabilitation and reparation after the abuse occurred, including the support to access to healthcare services and psycho-social support, for example after episodes of violence or sexual abuse. Both in cases of prevention and remedial approach the intervention will stress on the promotion and support of community self-help activities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

For its largest projects AVSI conducts impact analysis and in-depth post- distribution monitoring reviews, which are analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind a follow up interview of key informants and field personnel are conducted to review precisely the barrier that impede a more equitable targeting of the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers. AVSI plans to extend the monitoring tools already used in its education projects also to this one. A school assessment form will be introduced in order to collect information on the current status of each school in terms of infrastructures and draft plans of intervention aimed at improving the access of beneficiaries (especially younger pupils and girls) and addressing the needs highlighted above. The enrolment and daily attendance at school will be registered by AVSI staff deployed in the field, monitoring the access of vulnerable groups to education; beside that, robust data on P1 pupils will be gathered to properly address the higher dropout rate in lower classes). Access to education will be also monitored through follow-up visits in cases of irregularity or absence from school. Support will be given to disadvantaged and over-aged children to facilitate their access to school. The presence at school of IDPs children selected for cash assistance will be monitored as well.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews for its projects that include questions about the safety of beneficiaries. For instance, post-distribution monitoring asks beneficiaries about any hazard met on the way to reach the distribution sites. Moreover, annually AVSI conducts multi-sector surveys which include numerous questions aimed at assessing the safety, perceived safety, incidence of hazards, and conflicts and disputes over natural resources.The project includes a pilot intervention for the creation of living fences for safer school areas. The implementation will be under monitoring. The above-mentioned community self-help activities represent a way of monitoring cases of abuses and discrimination, with the aim to increase the sense of safety inside the targeted communities. Finally, the periodic school-based meetings involving SMCs, PTAs and Community Leaders on Child Protection status in each area include the introduction of a monitoring and reporting system.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

AVSI has among its core objectives to defend and enhance the dignity of the people that it serves. AVSI surveys include questions such as “Do you think you can bring a special and unique help to your family and community”, which are aimed at monitoring the level of self-esteem and capacity to control one owns life of the beneficiaries of AVSI support, for that is the ultimate objective pursued by AVSI. The intervention will monitor the affected population access to those basic services listed in the project’s activities. AVSI’s action is in line with the religious and cultural identity of the beneficiaries, promotes community-based self-help activities and encourage the creation of networks able to increase the level of security of the affected population and to offer adequate psycho-social support even after the end of the intervention, with the aim to ensure and reinforce each person’s dignity.
