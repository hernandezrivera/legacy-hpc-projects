Project Summary

To respond to the identified needs of the affected populations in camps, camp-like settings and outside of camps in Unity and Upper Nile states, DRC will implement CCCM activities adopting a static and roving/area based-approach. Through this project DRC aims to contribute to the following CCCM HRP strategic objectives:
- Ensure a protective environment in camps and camp-like settings for the displaced population and host community
- Ensure predictable and coordinated delivery of service, provision of information aimed at improving the quality of integrated services for the displaced populations in camps and camp like settings
- Enhance self-governance through participation and engagement with displaced persons
- Strengthen community coping mechanisms geared towards lasting solutions
In line with these objectives, DRC will continue providing CCCM activities including; establishing and strengthening community governance structures and enhancing the representation of different groups, enhancing accountability to affected populations through community engagement initiatives and functionable static and mobile complaints and feedback mechanisms, conducting multi-sectoral assessments, site profiles and ensuring up to date information through its information management products, strengthening service coordination and improving the site environment through site care and maintenance activities.
Overall, DRC aims to reach approximately 128,000 affected persons through CCCM activities.

1 - Needs

Two years after the signing of the revitalized agreement, the level of displacement in South Sudan has not decreased. An estimated 1.6 million people remain displaced, either due to displacement triggered by subnational violence or flooding. According to 2021 HNO, almost 80% of these persons live displaced with host communities, mostly in rural areas while the remaining are in camp-like settings, including IDP camps, PoC sites, collective sites and informal settlements. Amongst this number, there are existing IDPs who got displaced to a new location. In addition, Covid-19 had an impact to the lives of IDPs as health services put an additional effort on taking preventive and response measures to mitigate the virus, education was disrupted and women and girls were more exposed to various forms of GBV. According to DTM,IDP communities in Unity state have seen an increase to their needs in 2020 while people who got displaced in Unity and Upper Nile due to conflict expect to remain in this situation in 2021. Currently nearly 33,137 IDPs live in Malakal PoC and receive humanitarian assistance while almost 17,000 persons remain displaced in out of camp settings in Malakal county.Although the transition process of the PoC site is ongoing, it is expected that a significant number of IDPs will remain in the site after its re-designation due to various factors, including safety and security constraints, lack of services and livelihood opportunities as well as housing, land and property issues since the majority come from Malakal town or other locations in West Bank where their land has been occupied.These IDPs will be in need of CCCM services as continued coordination and management of the site will be required to ensure continuation of humanitarian services and minimum standards as it has been mentioned in the 2021 HNO CCCM section. IDPs who reside in collective sites and informal settlements in urban and hard to reach rural areas are severely underserved and have limited access to CCCM services. In Aburoc IDP informal settlement there are 8,452 IDPs who rely on humanitarian assistance to cover their basic needs. Residents of Aburoc mentioned during the beneficiary satisfaction survey conducted in October 2020 that the security and safety situation in their areas of origin and habitual residence contributes to their staying in the Aburoc site. In the projection period from December 2020 to March 2021 of the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, it is expected that Fashoda county will be projected to be classified in the IPC (Emergency Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Farming activities have been affected by flooding expecting to have an effect on cereal stocks earlier than expected. Currently Aburoc falls under the lean season distribution and as the level of water continues to rise this could have an impact on food availability of households creating new higher needs for the population there. In Unity state, approximately 3,000 IDPs are in collective sites in Bentiu and Rubkona towns while around 64,000 IDPs remain displaced with host community in urban and hard to reach areas across Unity state with minimum access to service provision as well as information about their rights and available services. Their access to multi-sectoral assistance cannot be guaranteed due to their inaccessibility all year long due to lack of basic infrastructure, weather hazards and insecurity. Unless there is adequate coordination of services based on needs, the protection concerns and vulnerabilities of these IDPs will continue to increase.Due to the protracted nature of displacement in South Sudan, IDPs in Unity and Upper Nile state will continue to require camp management services in 2021, including coordination,monitoring,capacity building,community engagement and site care and maintenance activities wherever feasible, to ensure their dignity, improve their well-being and minimum living standards and enhance their self-reliance.

2 - Project activities

To ensure that displaced persons have equitable access to basic services and assistance, DRC will implement its CCCM activities by adopting a static and a roving/area-based approach in line with the strategic objectives of the CCCM cluster.To ensure a protective environment for the displaced persons in Malakal PoC and Aburoc IDP informal settlement as well as the host community, DRC will conduct site care and maintenance activities with a protection mainstreaming approach to make sure that vulnerabilities of the population are taking into consideration in these interventions. As camp management agency, DRC will conduct consultations with the community either through FGDs or KIIs on perceived safety across all areas of the site including also areas outside of the site. In addition, through regular site monitoring and safety audits in collaboration where possible with other protection actors, DRC will inform its site care and maintenance interventions ensuring that the residents of the sites live in a safe, secure and dignified environment with minimum exposure to harm or violence.To ensure predictable and coordinated delivery of service, provision of information aimed at improving the quality of integrated services for the displaced populations in Malakal PoC, Aburoc IDP settlement both in and outside of camp settings in Unity and Upper Nile, DRC will continue strengthening or establish coordination mechanisms. This will enable DRC in coordination with other actors to identify gaps and needs by conducting multi-sectoral needs assessments/profiles, avoid duplication of assistance and ensure equitable access to services by coordinating the response of the humanitarian actors and continuously mapping services the humanitarian actors provide, and set up effective referral pathways including, where feasible, the host community. DRC will enhance the community participation and engagement by strengthening existing community governance structures to be able to advocate for the needs of their communities and for need based responses, to participate in decision making and be accountable to the persons they represent by developing terms of reference to abide them with their role. DRC will also provide capacity building trainings and coaching to enhance their skills amongst others on coordination, community engagement, needs identification as well as enhance the participation of vulnerable groups in the structures by working closely with women, youth and older persons committees. Lastly, DRC will work to strengthen community coping mechanisms geared towards finding solutions. To achieve this, DRC as camp management will work with the Protection cluster partners and DTM where feasible to conduct intention surveys with IDPs to inform and guide the programming of actors in camps, collective sites and informal settlements. DRC will also introduce cash-based initiatives, mostly focusing on cash for work activities, to support the social safety nets of IDPs. DRC will continue operating complaints and feedback mechanisms in all its locations to ensure that beneficiaries can express and receive feedback, have access to information needed to make decisions as well as providing a two-way communication enhancing the accountability to affected populations.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project takes into consideration the existing market assessments so the design of the cash activity will be driven by understanding the context, needs and incentive structures. In addition, during the project design wage rates are critical to determine the cash amounts of the cash-based intervention. So DRC follows the CCCM cluster casual labour wage rates for the implementation of cash based activities to ensure no disruption of the markets or avoid any social tensions. These rates have been determined based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket that has been calculated by the South Sudan Cash Working Group.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash will be provided for Cash for Work activities as a compensation or incentive for work that is done on communal/public projects to improve, preserve or rehabilitate community services, resources or infrastructure (such as repairing roads, cleaning camps through solid waste management, garbage collection, or rehabilitating public infrastructure, like schools or health centers, etc.) as they have identified by the community. They are being used instead of in-kind support to provide income support to vulnerable beneficiaries through short term, unskilled labour to meet their essential needs. Beneficiaries have been selected following specific criteria and the assistance targets only one adult per household.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Community governance structures are trained on protection mainstreaming and PSEA with the support of the protection team. In the locations we have static complaints and feedback desks there are posters providing information on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has established complaints and feedback mechanisms in all the locations it works, both static and mobile ones. The desks operate by two CCCM staff that register the feedback they receive from individuals who visit the desks. Furthermore, DRC through community leaders receives feedback from community members who cannot access the complaints and feedback desks due to vulnerability or not feeling comfortable to visit them. Last but not least, DRC also receives feedback from partners about vulnerable groups through the coordination meetings.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will provide continuous information to the community governance structures through the coordination meetings about the camp management activities DRC implements in the different locations and how they can express their complaints to the existing complaint mechanisms. As these structures represent their communities, DRC provides them with this information so they can pass them to their communities. In addition, DC organizes community engagement sessions by informing communities through focus group discussions or megaphones about the project and how they can provide their feedback through the complaint and feedback mechanism as well as receive their feedback of the beneficiaries about the project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback will be taken into consideration on the approach and modality the beneficiaries suggest and will be used to revise the way we implement the activities. Furthermore, the project collects beneficiaries’ feedback about the services they receive or don’t receive by other service providers. Camp management shares this feedback with the respective service providers so they can adapt their assistance and service provision based on the feedback shared by the beneficiaries.
