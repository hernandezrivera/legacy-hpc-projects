Project Summary

The humanitarian hubs are providing humanitarian partners in Maiduguri and seven deepfield
locations with safe and secure accommodation, office spaces, and support services
enabling access of partners and delivery of services to the affected population.IOM
operates a humanitarian base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and an additional eight field
hubs in seven locations, namely Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki and
Damasak in deep-field locations. The humanitarian base camp in Maiduguri
accommodates up to 150 people at a time, and each of the hubs in the deep-field (except
the extension hub in Banki,) accommodates up to 15 people at a time. There are two
hubs in Banki – a standard 15-bed main hub and an 84-bed hub extension.Under the
proposed project, IOM aims to support the maintenance and operation of eight
humanitarian field hubs and one (1) humanitarian base camp. IOM will also complete the
establishment of and operate the humanitarian hub in Rann. The tented structures in the
Banki humanitarian hub extension will be replaced with concrete structures. The
perimeter walls of all the field hubs will be reinforced with concrete interior walls and the
height will be increased by an average of 2 meters using cyclone wire fencing to improve
the security of the field hubs.

1 - Needs

Due to the security situation, which continues to be extremely volatile and unpredictable,humanitarian access continues to be a challenge to all humanitarian actors in trying toreach vulnerable populations in north-east Nigeria. The increase in security incidentsreduces the already limited access to the affected population, while close to one millionpeople living in hard to reach areas are already unable to access basic services and lifesavingassistance. To improve access, and facilitate better coordination amonghumanitarian responders, the Nigerian Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) decided toestablish humanitarian hubs in various crisis-affected local government areas (LGAs)across Borno State. This decision was based on a risk assessment and analysis primarilyconducted by the Operational Humanitarian Country Team (OHCT) and the Borno Inter-Sector Working Group (ISWG). IOM has been mandated by the HCT to assume the role asthe Humanitarian Hub Management Agency in north-east Nigeria. The Organization istherefore responsible for the construction, management and operation of the hubs onbehalf of partners responding to the crisis. The hubs provide secure and safe operatingenvironments for aid workers in deep field locations across the north-east, includingaccommodation, office space, meeting/training facilities and connectivity services. Asenablers of access to field locations, the hubs are considered critical for a sustained andeffective humanitarian response in Nigeria. The hubs are strategically located in areasbenefitting from larger multi-sectoral humanitarian operations. Due to this, the hubs actas a unified platform for coordinating assistance across and within critical sectors of theresponse in the deep field. This is particularly relevant for the sectors of camp coordinationand camp management, water sanitation and hygiene, health, shelter and non-food items,and nutrition. To date, IOM has constructed and operationalized a total of ninehumanitarian hubs. The hubs are located in Maiduguri (base camp), Gwoza, Bama, Ngala,Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (2) and Damasak. Each of the field hubs (except for the hub inBanki with an additional 84 beds in its extension hub) can accommodate up to 15humanitarian workers. The hub in Maiduguri has a capacity of 150 beds. The totalcapacity of the nine hubs is 339 beds. The current funding is not sufficient to sustain theoperation of the humanitarian hubs. IOM has put in place a cost-recovery mechanism andreduces staffing and operating costs to a minimum to extend the current funds availablefor operations and maintenance. However, the funds that are collected from the cost recoverymechanism are projected to cover approximately 40% of the needed operatingcosts of the hubs. While the base camp in Maiduguri is expected to generate funds tocover most of its operational costs due to its much larger capacity and high occupancyrate, the field hubs, with their limited capacity, cannot generate sufficient funds tomaintain a sustained base for continuing efforts by the humanitarian community.

2 - Activities

1 - Complete the construction of and operate/maintain the humanitarian hub in Rann; 2 -Convert the tented structures in Banki Hub extension to concrete structures; 3 -Improve/reinforce the perimeter walls of the field hubs; 4 - Manage and operate 10humanitarian hubs including the provision of accommodation, food, internet, incollaboration with ETS, and office services
