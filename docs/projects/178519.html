Project Summary

MF aims through this project to provide adequate shelter and full spectrum of humanitarian lifesaving multisectoral assistance to 17,400 IDPs; 2900 families/ HHs, in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates, where the project's areas of intervention include three location;
- Ehteimlat Planned camp 1367 RHUs- Aleppo Suran- Ehteimlat (1367 HHs).
- Sheikh Bahir planned camp 400 RHUs - Idleb- Maaret Tamsrin- Sheikh Bahir (400 HHs).
- Kafr Jalis Planned camp 1133 RHUs - Idleb- Maaret Tamsrin- Kafr Jalis (1133 HHs).
Through this project, MF seeks to continue what started for and follow-up on the second phase related to management of these three planned camps, and aims to alleviate suffering and improve living conditions of vulnerable IDP households in different areas in Idleb (Kafr Jales and Shiekh Bahr PC), and Aleppo governorates, in the Euphrates Shield area - (Ihtemlat PC) in northwest Syria (NW Syria).
MF will support CCCM cluster responses to immediate needs of the most vulnerable IDPs who were forced to flee their homes due to hostilities or those who affected by floods or fires and were left with no safe shelter.
For this project, MF will use Refugee Housing Units (RHUs) as shelter units which expected to alleviate many of the challenges being faced with the ordinary tents.
It also would provide with more dignified and private accommodating, and has improved fire safety& ventilation advantages over the normal tents.
Recently, MF has started receiving the IDPs in the planned camps and providing them with CCCM activities from its private fund. Therefore, MF through this project aims to provide 17,400 IDPs, 2900 (HHS) living in three planned camps with core CCCM services.
The project activities aligns with the structure of the strategy paper for the allocation and CCCM cluster priorities. where;
Camp Management:
-Providing streamlined humanitarian lifesaving assistance, multi-sectoral assistance to the affected IDPs and strengthen the basic infrastructure support in identified IDP sites.
- Registration process: organized reception process upon arrival at the site (including registration and issuance of registration card to each household, protection profiling, vulnerabilities screening, shelter allocation, basic orientation, information about available services), with data safely recorded in a confidential database system.
-Regular maintenance of site infrastructures including replacement of broken/damaged fixtures, welding, painting, reinforcement.
Enhanced accountability to IDPs through establishment and implementation of CCCM mechanisms, Community leaders are elected and community committees are formed for equitable representation of all whole camp population .
-Set up accountable complaint and feedback mechanism (AAP).
-Hold fair elections for IDPs to elect community leaders representative of camp population.
-Establish community committees
Capacity Building:
-Provide capacity building trainings for the Camp management field staffs- strengthen the capacity of staff.
-Provide capacity building trainings for community leaders and committees members, (train members to maintain camp infrastructure (WASH, lighting, fence, replacement of broken parts, welding, painting..).
Fire Mitigation Prevention &Fire Awareness Campaigns:
-Developing fire warden systems and creating fire committees
-Raise community awareness through training and awareness campaigns.
-Conduct fire trainings with ensure that women are included in
such trainings.
-Provision of fire response resources (fire extinguishers, fire blankets, &first aid kits)
Maram will mitigate the effects of the COVID- 19 in NWS by accommodating more IDPs in these three planned camps and alleviate congestion in existing camps operating over their capacity.
Maram will ensure taking COVID-19 prevention measures and following precautionary procedures. MF’s extensive CCCM experience is demonstrated by establishment and the ongoing successful management of Planned Camps in NW Syria.

4 - Needs

Ten years into the conflict in Syria, the life-threatening needs among crisis-affected populations and communities continue to increase in scale, severity, and complexity. Referring to HNO 2020, in NWS Syria, the number of people living in last-resort IDPs sites reached 1.5 million individuals by November 2020, up from 868,000 individuals in Nov 2019. IDP sites continued to provide a safe space equipped with immediate humanitarian life-saving assistance to the most vulnerable IDPs.The majority of IDP sites listed in the CCCM database are self-settled, meaning that they often lack proper site planning, infrastructure and camp management systems. According to CCCM Cluster reports and Monthly Displacement Updates NW Syria - Dec 2020. (Total IDP Population: 2,754,020 IDPs, Camps Population: 1,624,286 IDPs, Recent Displacement: 32, 539 IDPs, Returnees: 3,479 IDPs).As of Dec 2020, the CCCM database includes 1,302 IDP sites that host 1,624,286 IDPs or 301,852 families (29% G, 29% B, 22% W& 20% M). 80% are women and children, 21,448 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. Between 13 Dec 2020 and 18 Jan 2021, 29 incidents (8 Floods &21 Fire incidents) occurred in IDP sites in NWS, which caused one death and three injuries, and destruction of 895 shelters.As part of MF CCCM response, MF will accommodate families most affected to live in the newly established planned camps to cover their CCCM needs, improve the shelter condition, and provide a dignified life; MF will provide adequate and safe shelter for the most vulnerable displaced, affected by floods, fires, and conflicts. And MF will contribute to the mitigation of the effects of the COVID 19 in NW Syria by taking COVID19 prevention and precautionary measures.

5 - Implementation of activities

Maram will provide adequate shelter and full spectrum of humanitarian lifesaving multisectoral assistance to ensure safe and dignified living conditions for the most vulnerable people, and will establish effective coordination mechanism with relevant clusters and NGO service providers to allow timely provision of multisectoral life-saving humanitarian assistance to 17,400 IDPs; 2900 families/ HHs of the most vulnerable displaced who have fled their areas due to conflict, floods or fires, and accommodated in Kafr Jales, Shiekh Bahr and Ihtemlat Planned Camps. Maram will serve as the camp management agency, and set in place proper staffing/management personnel to conduct CCCM activities, including care and maintenance to basic infrastructure, registration/data collection, vulnerability mapping, verification, establishment of gender-balanced camp governance/ participation management structures, and accountability mechanisms, routine site renovations, and strengthening fire mitigation and response in the identified Planned Camps.In addition to enhance community participation and form committees, strengthen the capacity of staff and committees by Providing capacity building trainings for the staff and the committees’ members, and build accountability mechanism towards IDPs by established mechanism for coordinated multisectoral services provision, established mechanism for community engagement and accountability to IDPs. It will consider the different priorities and needs of IDPs from different ages including men, women, boys, girls, elderly people and people with special needs.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions, risks, and mitigations: Risk 1: Organizations Acceptance by beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders: CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: Maram Foundation will prepare a community outreach plan, will attending and conduct coordination meetings with target community members and the local authorities to explain project activities, targets, and purposes. Risk 2: Deterioration of safety and security context: CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: In general, the security situation will be acceptable because of the project located in the demilitarized zone, but any way Maram Foundation, in addition to the procedures that are always taken into consideration in all projects: - MF maintains a security risk management system for its areas of operation in order to adapt and respond to changes in context - MF maintains a full stakeholder and power analysis for its areas of operation in order to better understand the context -MF on a daily basis reviews security information related to its areas of operation and monitors staff movement as per the current security conditions. Risk 3: Exchange rates change significantly and therefore the budget is insufficient to reach the targeted number of beneficiaries CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: -MF will maintain a regular dialogue with its donor in the event of a significant change -MF conducts regular price monitoring, utilizing existing information where available - MF will contract with the contractor in the dollar to avoid the risk. Risk 4: in addition to what mentioned, MF will contribute to the mitigation of the effects of the COVID 19 in NW Syria by taking COVID19 prevention measures with regards to awareness, sterilization, social distancing, health screening of IDPs.
