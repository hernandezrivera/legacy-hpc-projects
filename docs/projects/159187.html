Project Summary

The overall health situation in Somalia u continues to deteriorate, especially due to conflict-affected and seasonally hard-to-reach areas due to persistent drought, continued insecurity and other underlying factors. This project will strive to address the emergency health needs of marginalized and underserved communities in Deynille, Daykenle, Karan, Hawwadag districts in the Benadir region with an aim of targeting 50,420 people (20% of-252,101 of the population). The project aims at improving access and utilization of integrated primary healthcare services, including reproductive health and immunization coverage through the support of static facilities with a mobile response. Mobile service delivery will concentrate in areas not currently served by static facilities, thus increasing access to health services for marginalized communities.
IRC is currently supporting integrated healthcare services in Deynille, Dharkenley, and Karan andHawlwadag. Under the proposed program, four static facilities (4 Primary Health Care Centre (PHCC) and) and two mobile teams will be supported. Both facilities currently have the necessary equipment and infrastructure to provide basic medical, surgical, laboratory, nutrition screening, and pharmacy services. Critical patients attending mobile clinics and pregnant women with obstetric complications will be referred and managed at the health facilities. Pregnant women in need of Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care (CEmONC) will be referred from the PHCC to Benadir hospital. Support for PHCC under this program may include, but not be limited to recruitment, payment, and training of healthcare workers, provision of essential equipment and supplies, and regular supportive supervision, provision of comprehensive reproductive healthcare services: through Female skilled birth attendants.. Pregnant mothers will be mapped and targeted information will be provided by CHWs at the community to refer them to ANC and prepare for safer births. The CHW will be involved in identifying vulnerable and needy community members with other non-communicable diseases including persons with mental health needs who will be attended to at the health facilities since all clinicians within IRC supported facilities have been trained on the WHO Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP). This program will provide services to GBV survivors through a survivor-centered approach where the survivor’s rights, needs, and wishes are prioritized and respected at all times.

Project Activities

•Procurement & supply of supplementary essential drugs & supplies for the health facility Looking at drug management systems in health facilities and ensuring they adhere to national /international standards• Identify and establish mobile outreach services for PHC services which will address the needs of women, men, and children of the targeted population• Provide 24 hours of health facility delivery services by skilled health workers• Provision of micronutrients (Vitamin A /Folic acid) to lactating mothers/Infants • Provide EPI routine services at the facility level & outreach services with an emphasis on DPT3 coverage• Train frontline health workers (CHWs, nurses, clinicians) on IMCI service protocols and distribution of guidelines• Provide integrated Basic Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (BEmoNC) services, by skilled health workers• Provision of outpatient consultations for men, women, boys, and girls within the targeted districts. • Ensure adequate staffing levels at the supported health facilities to manage patient caseloads.• Conduct community dialogues before, during and after outreach to identify key health issues in communities and to address barriers to access. These community dialogues will target different age groups within the communities and at different times: married youth, unmarried youth, religious leaders including opinion leaders, women, and men, boys and girls. To maximize mortality reduction and improvements in health outcomes, supply-side intervention needs to be complemented with demand-side intervention. Hence community strategies will improve knowledge and address social and cultural barriers.  Training of CHWs (30) will be conducted on defaulter tracing for immunization, antenatal care clients and clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS). Community health workers (CHW) will be trained to track and support pregnancies and to refer for complications and delivery and they will also be involved in the promotion of health education at the community level to contribute towards increasing demand for sexual and reproductive health services (SRH). The importance of CCSAS will also be emphasized.  Quarterly joint supportive supervision: The IRC and MoH will strengthen supervision, mentoring and follow-up to ensure that new knowledge and skills are put into practice, together with training and support for the implementation of wider quality improvement interventions. Strengthening the use of data to monitor and improve quality of care: Routine quality of care assessments will be conducted at the facility level. This data will be channeled through the MoH. Key staff will meet monthly to review data, follow up on progress towards implementation and to highlight any issues that need to be discussed. Exit interviews will be conducted to identify whether services are meeting the needs of clients who attend the facility and to identify areas to improve clients’ satisfaction. Furthermore, this information is required for monitoring the scale of services, and to assess the client profile by collecting more socio-demographic information about clients such as the level of education, marital and employment status, so as to better understand the type of client seeking different services and who is accessing the services to be able to see if there are client types (e.g. poorer people) that the IRC could try to target as well as assessing the extent health services are reaching underserved populations. This is one of the forms of getting feedback from the beneficiaries in order to improve the quality of health services.

Mitigation Measures

• The IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance. Room will be given to clients to report any IRC related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness or responsiveness. A communication structure is in place in collaboration with Complaints Response Mechanism and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The community health committees established will serve as forums where feedback on program implementation can be shared and ideas on further improving activities generated. An exit interview will be conducted within the project period to get the feedback from the community• The main barriers to accessing healthcare in these areas include lack of quality health services including lack of emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. In addition to the above, the recurrent conflicts have disrupted routine health programs leaving a large sector of the population without access to health services. Hence IRC will recruit and engage qualified service providers to provide services and outreach responses that create more access to health services.• The proposed project supports the gender theme in that it promotes health service provision to all sections of the population including men, women, girls and boys, without discrimination
