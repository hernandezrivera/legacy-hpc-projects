Project Summary

Chabash Development and Health Initiative – CDHI proposed a 1-year project focused on community-based approaches to strengthen sexual reproductive health right SRHR and GBV mechanisms that support effective delivery of urgent lifesaving protection services in 3 targeted accessible LGAs (Jere, Konduga, and Kaga LGAs) in Borno state, with a focus to address the needs of most vulnerable population affected by the Boko Haram protracted crisis that is in its tenth year. The community-based approach means that CDHI – a women welfare centered agency will not only provide relevant and individualized services to vulnerable women in communities but will also ensure that members of the community contribute greatly in the building of a system of care that supports women to thrive and attain their full potential in live. This approach will address both the short-term (humanitarian) and long-term (development) protection needs of girls, boys, women and men, eventually. CDHI will specifically strengthen gender and age sensitive community-based protection mechanisms. This will entails working with communities and government structures to develop a context specific evidence-based capacity building strategy that will build upon existing formal and informal community-based protection mechanisms and link them to formal government structures. This approach will reinforce the participation of affected population in timely uptake and utilization of SRHR, GBV prevention services and strengthen accountability throughout the project cycle. CDHI will focus on the following activities;
• Build psycho-social resilience of GBV survivors and individuals at risk of GBV through the provision of survivor-centered, age and gender appropriate case management and psycho-socia support services.
• The project will strengthen the capacity of service providers including the training of psycho-socia workers, community-based structures and committees to provide safe and confidential services in line with the minimum standards and guidelines with emphasis on managing child survivors.
• Further, the project will engage community-based case workers to provide follow up support to survivors. Sexual Reproductive Health Rights information and referrals will be integrated into the GBV case management services provided at the women and girls safe spaces by the project.
• Survivors including women and adolescent girls will receive culturally appropriate safety and dignity kits including integrated livelihood and or income generation support. This will be augmented by a community-based saving initiative that provides opportunities for women to boost and sustain their business while increasing access to financial loans that supports business initiatives and builds resilience.
•
Strengthen coordination and capacity of actors through service providers mapping, developing and updating of referral pathway at the primary implementing unit for optimal uptake of services.
• project will facilitate regular meetings among protection actors and advocate for services for survivors of GBV including other women or girls at risk.
• Other humanitarian actors providing services including legal, psychosocial, education, WASH, Livelihoods, Nutrition, and Security etc. will be trained on SRHR and GBV principles and guidelines. Focal persons will be identified to support effective referral of women and children with protection concerns.
• Strengthen protection monitoring, response and accountability to project participants: The project will implement risk mapping that will enable women, men, adolescents’ boys and girls to identify and respond to protection risk factors at the community level. Regular protection monitoring will be conducted by trained protection monitors and referrals will be made for protection concerns beyond the project scope
CDHI intends to reach 20,000 direct beneficiaries through different project implementation approach.

1 - Needs

Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV): Demographic dynamics pose a challenge, considering that a large segment of the Nigerian population is young. There is a high dependency burden due to youth unemployment and lack of opportunity, exposing girls to greater risks of sexual violence and abuse, early and/or forced marriage, teenage pregnancies, trafficking and abduction as sex slaves. Various forms of gender-based violence (GBV), including forced and early marriages, physical, psychological, sexual, and economic violence affect women and girls. Nearly 3 in 10 Nigerian women have experienced physical violence by age 15 . Sexual violence, including rape, is a defining characteristic of the ongoing conflict, with 6 out of 10 women in the north-east having experienced one or more forms of GBV. Women, boys and girls are at particular risk within the current environment, with many reports of survival sex in exchange for food, money and freedom of movement (into and out of IDP sites). Women and adolescents are abducted by armed opposition groups and are forcibly married or raped during their captivity; their children born out of rape face stigma and rejection upon their return to their communities. Similarly, despite the consequences of forced marriage, families continue to subject their daughters, especially adolescents, to forced marriage with poverty being the main reason for such acts. About 48% of IDP women, many of whom are heads of households, continue to live in overcrowded and culturally inappropriate conditions, especially in camps, resulting to lack of privacy as well as protection of their dignity. Adolescent girls are at greater risk of SGBV as they are often targeted while performing their basic tasks such as traveling to water points, collecting firewood to cook or going to communal latrines . Regardless of the social context in which GBV occurs, incidences of GBV are usually covered in silence and under-reported due to the fear of stigma faced by the survivor . Further, the disruption in the socioeconomic lives of the women in north-east Nigeria by non-state armed groups NSAG insurgency has placed women and girls at a high risk of all forms of abuse from the insurgents, security agents deployed to protect the communities, and other members of the community. Lack of humanitarian access compounded with the absence of service providers and services in newly accessible LGAs, including transit centers, remains a major challenge for girls, women, boys and men to access GBV and Sexual & Reproductive Health (SRH) services. The GBV sub-sector working group has identified an estimated 3.00 million people (86% women and girls, and 14% men and boys) in need of GBV prevention and response and has targeted 1.3 million people for GBV prevention and response services in 2019. To address the needs of women, girls, boys and men in need of GBV response services, the capacity of health facilities, psychosocial workers, security and legal actors to respond to those needs must be strengthened through training and provision of relevant materials. Health workers and facilities need to be supported to provide adolescent friendly service provision, as well as development and dissemination of treatment protocol and the availability of relevant rape treatment supplies including post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) kits. Further, survivors of GBV and women, girls, boys and men at risk of GBV need to be supported to build their resilience through case management interventions that address their psychosocial needs. Further, community-based mechanisms need to be strengthened to increase awareness on the consequences of GBV and identify and respond to risks of GBV through community-based initiatives including dialogues, focus group discussions and advocacy events.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1. GBV survivors have access to age and gender appropriate services. To respond to the needs of women, girls, boys and men survivors of GBV, a survivor-centered approach to providing quality services of case management will be adopted. 300 GBV survivors, especially those released from captivity and formerly associated with armed forces or groups, will be reintegrated into their communities and will be incorporated into community based psychosocial support initiatives with follow-up support and referrals for relevant services will remain a primary priority to their reintegration. The capacity of Case Workers will be enhanced through relevant training and follow-up supervision support while 200 professional service providers and community-based mechanisms will be strengthened to identify and facilitate referrals of survivors. Community knowledge of GBV and its consequences will be improved, and community members will be engaged through transformative behavior change initiatives including dialogues, peer-support groups and community-led advocacy meetings and forums. Output 1.1. Provide age appropriate and gender sensitive GBV case management and multi-sectorial psychosocial support and health services to 300 GBV survivors. In order to ensure age appropriate, gender sensitive case management, and psychosocial services for GBV survivors, CDHI will recruit and train Case Workers on Case Management guidelines and principles and further support Case Workers through monitoring, supervision and coaching by trained GBV Project Officers. After their training and deployment, the Case Workers will support individual survivors through identification, documentation, assessment, care plan development and implementation as well as referral and follow-up. Output 1.2. Train 200 GBV service providers, security personnel and local leaders, and community-based mechanisms to identify and respond to GBV cases. CDHI will mobilize 30 health workers, 30 LGA level authority, 20 security personnel and 20 Social Workers and 100 Community Based mechanisms members and will train them with focused training gender responsive identification and responding to unique needs of women, girls and children. CDHI will begin with the mapping of existing service professional service providers and community mechanisms and their capacity; this will lead to the development of a tailored training package that will address the gap in knowledge, attitude and skills. Output 1.3 Women and adolescent girls reached with information on psychosocial support, GBV and sexual reproductive health right. A minimum of 20,000 women and adolescent girls will be reached through community awareness activities. Trained community-based structures will be used to facilitate the delivery of tailored key messages on sexual reproductive health right services and GBV prevention. Women and girl friendly safe spaces (WGFS)will be established to provide opportunities for women and girls to discuss and share experiences on SRHR and GBV related issues. One on one counselling will also be provided to women where necessary through the WGFS. Output 1.4 Conduct regular safety audits and assess the safety concerns of women and girls in the communities: CDHI will conduct safety audits at community level to identify key safety challenges faced by women, girls, boys and men as it relates to Gender Based Violence. CDHI will engage the communities in using a participatory approach in identifying these challenges. Additionally, the same participatory process will be used to engage the communities (women, girls, men and boys) in developing community safety action points. Safety/dignity materials will be provided to 500 women and girls to reduce the risk of GBV especially sexual violence
