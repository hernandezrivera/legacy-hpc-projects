Project Summary

WHO’s response strategy is aligned with the overall Health Sector Strategy as articulated in the Joint Response Plan 2021. The activities prioritized under this project have been selected based on the Health Sector needs highlighted by the mid-term review of the Joint Response Plan 2020, Joint Multisectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA)- September 2020, and Population In Need figures (ISCG, November 2020). WHO remains the only actor in support of the MoHFW with the mandate and capacity to set priorities, minimum standards, and support quality health service delivery.
In 2021 WHO, as the lead of the Health Sector, will continue support government and partners respond to COVID-19, to work towards equity and quality of services, advocate on behalf of the Health Sector on inadequately addressed issues, strongly engage government in localization efforts and stimulate documentation and publication of lessons learned on COVID-19 pandemic and the Rohingya humanitarian crisis in general
WHO will focus on bringing the COVID-19 outbreak under control through innovations like vaccines, therapeutics( as they become available), and other measures already deployed and support revitalization of the utilization of essential services with quality service delivery as part of efforts to sustain what is achieved in the two and half year before the COVID-19 while maintaining the capacity to continue support response to emerging needs that result from emergencies due to natural disasters and other disease outbreaks in this protracted humanitarian crisis over and above ensuring continuity of emergency services during the monsoon and cyclone seasons.
WHO intends to continue providing regular and timely information on public health risks and requirements and filling gaps through effective coordination of partners and effective response ensuring access to life-saving interventions mediated by a rationalized health service delivery system.
The underlying principles of WHO’s work remain and will be reinforced to liaise with national government on the governance and leadership through Civil Surgeon’s office and MOHFW Coordination Cell, support in developing and adopting standards, guidelines, and protocols, extending of national programs to the camp settings and; avail of the health workforce, vaccines, and essential technology, health information, service delivery based on minimum essential services package, for neonates, children, boys and girls and women and men in both the Rohingyas and host community. Support will also be extended to institutions that offer access to specialized services including the Institute of Epidemiology Disease Control and Research, IEDCR Field Laboratory at the Cox's Bazar medical college, Cox's Bazar Sadar hospital, and others.
The planned interventions are guided by initial and periodical service availability assessments during implementation, beneficiaries will participate as volunteers in pre-implementation planning and delivery of the project activities as the technical capacity of the different age group and gender will allow for example as guides, volunteers, mobilizers vaccinators during routine activities and campaigns, among others. External assessments and reviews will be utilized as opportunities for the Rohingya and host community populations to directly review the work of WHO. Indirectly through the various organizations, the yearly health sector performance review will also contribute to influencing the coordination of the health service delivery for the affected populations

1 - Identified Needs

• The need for reversing the disruption in essential health service delivery resulting from the COVID-19 while maintaining the capacity to swiftly respond to any upsurge in severe cases of COVID-19 and with more resilient health systems within the camps and nearby host community• The need for coordinated health response with the ministry of health for the over 130 health partners providing health services to the affected population in Rohingya camps and host communities remains a priority. This has ensured minimal duplication of efforts, fill critical gaps on availability, safe access, and quality of health services for men, women, boys, girls, and at-risk groups. • The refugee population remains vulnerable to weather-related emergencies which also impact disease trends disproportionately for men, women, boys, and girls. There is a need for continued coordination of emergency response and preparedness in relation to monsoon and cyclone to minimize health impact on the affected Rohingya population and host community• Need to improve immunization coverage and quality of services to achieve coverage of >95% and a dropout rate of less than 5%. • Need to ensure institutionalize infection prevention and control and adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities that address the needs• Need to deliver comprehensive emergency obstetric care with services much closer to the Rohingya refugees, newborn care, family planning provision (particularly increasing uptake of long-acting methods) and be responsive to adolescent health needs•Need to operationalize a function pre-hospital and critical patient care management systems for girls and boys, women and men• Need for continued strengthening of health facilities to deliver GBV care based following several capacity building initiates that have been undertaken to bridge identified gaps. Dedicated supportive supervision, in addition• Need for continued advocacy effort of extending national health programs (such as EPI, TB/HIV/AIDS) to Rohingya men, women, boys, and girls • Address shortages in Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity and build on community-level behavioral adjustments for health promotion and lifestyles• Address mental health and psychosocial support training gaps, advocacy of child mental health services and address access to information on substance abuse to inform response interventions through collaborative arrangements • Need for health care waste management solution including safe disposal of expired medicines and enhanced water quality surveillance in collaboration with DPHE, UNICEF, and WASH actors.• Rational use of medicines to reduce the risk of antimicrobial resistance.• Need to address risks for communicable diseases, strengthen and sustain the system for prevention, early detection, and response to outbreak prone diseases

2 - Activities or outputs

through capacity building, supportive supervision, gap-filling of supplies, referral, assessments and planning for substance abuse, child/adolescent mental health and other key interventions6.Support facilities deliver GBV services with technical coordination, dissemination of tools to improve quality of interventions to the unique needs of GBV survivors with SRH WG 7.Undertake supplementary capacity building initiatives on SRHR through the SRH WG to support comprehensive sexual, reproductive, maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (SRMNCAH) interventions8.Support health logistics by promoting proper stockpiling and storage practices, including risk-based testing of medicines in liaison with DGDA 9.Support field laboratories, including UHCs, on good lab practices, quality management, reporting, biosafety and security with capacity building on maintenance10.Develop and disseminate adapted strategies, plans, guidelines, protocols for quality delivery of essential services package, provide technical support on communicable diseases and other niche technical areas11.Support safe blood transfusion through training, establishment of blood storage units in UHCs and blood donation systems strengthening 12.Explore innovations in digital health to address information gaps and support rational access and utilization of services 13.Ensure a robust IPC system is established through training, support with logistics, monitoring and operationalization of IPC committees 14.Ensure robust system for prevention, early detection and response to outbreak prone diseases, through EWARS, CBS; undertake innovation, train and deploy strategies for surveillance 15.Deploy epidemiologists, public health officers and consultants to monitor emergency and pillar-based outbreak response16.Communicate data on epidemiology and risk factors for gender and age groups, provide health intelligence through risk assessments and public health situation analysis. 17.Conduct surveys to understand previous and current disease burden among girls and boys, women and men in camps and host community, and tailor interventions 18.Coordinate with WASH sector, undertake WQS for evidence-based decisions, in collaboration with DPHE 19.Continue WHO WASHFIT, enhance IPC practices and strengthen healthcare waste management 20.Support the functionality of the CS HEOC through training, exercises, equipment and intermittent deployment of expertise21.Conduct training on investigations and rapid response to public health events22.Support prevention and management of communicable diseases (e.g. HIV, hepatitis, TB, Malaria, and others including NTDs) through training, monitoring, and provision of emergency supplies23.Update risk communication strategy to address priority health issues and manage infodemics 24.Develop and disseminate risk communication materials, engage communities in risk mitigation and health promotion, including GBV and harness CHWs25.Conduct studies to understand needs and tailor public health messaging to community specificities 26.Lead and coordinate health sector and health response to emergencies in collaboration with government, other sectors and partners 27.Monitor reporting in EWARS, DHIS-2 and 4Ws on adherence to SADD; disseminate information products and conduct training28.Undertake, and support partners and government to conduct, assessments and research
