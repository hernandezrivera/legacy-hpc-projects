Project Summary

HRO aims to continue providing GP & MA services for IDPs and HC in our already existed community centres in Idleb (Jisr Ash Shugur, Darkosh) and in Aleppo (Afrin), besides planning to establish new CCs in Idleb (Harim, Salqin, and Kafr Jales) & through outreach visits to the widows’ camps (2 in Janudiyeh and 2 in Darkosh) to target the female widows & children with different GP, MA activities. HRO will also provide integrated protection services through community centers and mobile teams, to ensure access to all age groups, including vulnerable groups such as widows, divorced women, elderly, and people with disabilities, including the following topics: Risk education awareness sessions, victim assistance services, protection monitoring, legal awareness sessions and legal counselling, awareness-raising sessions (integrated services) other than legal awareness, initiatives and community-based protection structures, recreational activities, training the humanitarian workers and local authorities on protection principles and RE.
HRO will build the capacity of employees on (GP, MA, Inclusion of PwD), and will provide services to IDPs, HC and the most vulnerable groups who are at risk of protection. Awareness-raising activities for community and caregivers will include Community-based protection services, Legal services (HLP & CSD), Protection monitoring service through conducting service mapping and establishing referral pathways and reporting on protection concerns and issues, in addition to monitoring the application of protection principles during mass IDPs influxes and IDPs relocation, Risk education awareness sessions, Victim assistance services: including Psychosocial support, orientation & Referral to external services & case management, PM awareness sessions on core protection principles and protection mainstreaming.
M&E team will be responsible for ensuring confidentiality of beneficiaries’ data, through ensuring files are stored in a safe place within the facilities, communication about cases is being made through coded names, and monthly reports include key findings attached with MoVs and monitoring findings will be shared with Donor and with cluster as requested.
To ensure that IDPs and host community are effectively involved in the project lifecycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns. 62,050 beneficiaries including (40,330 IDPs and 21,720 HC) will be targeted with the project’s activities including 39,712 Females and 22,338 Males (450 Young Children, 19,930 Children, 36,000 Adults, 5,670 Elderly) among them are 3100 PwDs, through 7 community centers including 26 protection staff and 8 MA staff (13 outreach teams), M&E teams will ensure the quality of the activities implemented and the application of the accountability mechanisms, considering the gender equality. HRO will ensure smooth coordination with the protection NGO partners civil local bodies, stakeholders, and camp mangers throughout the project period, in addition to the provision of MHPSS services through our ongoing projects to ensure the integration between our protection and MHPSS services considering the WHO standard COVID-19 and Cholera preventive measures throughout the implementation period.
It is worth mentioning that HRO is a member of the protection cluster and is committed to updating the protection 4WS monthly.

3 - Needs

According to humanitarian programme cycle issued in February 2022, 14.2 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance. According to HNO 2023, displacements throughout 2022 ranged between 7,000-11,000 new displacements monthly in NWS. As of 2022, almost 20% of IDP HHs report going through 5 or more displacement cycles.Over 1.8 million IDPs living in 1,421 last-resort IDP sites in IDP sites in NWS, an increase from 1.7 million in 2021, 87% are self-settled IDP sites, 23% are women and 56% are children. Overall, 79% of IDP sites are critically overcrowded. 64 % do not have shelters set-up to accommodate PwDs and 93% do not have services easily accessible for PwDs.According to MSNA 2022, community-based assessment shows that the presence of explosive ordnance is a safety concern for 23% of IDP households. 76% HHs surveyed in the 2022 MSNA report not having received explosive ordnance risk education. CSD issues remain particularly prominent, especially in NWS, where only 87% of HHs report not having all the official documentation they need. Administration fees and transport costs, as well as safety reasons, remain the main obstacles to accessing civil documentation. The prolonged inability to secure civil documentation and officially register key life events is particularly detrimental to HLP and civil rights and increases the risk of a generation of IDP children becoming stateless.

4 - Implementation of activities

After ensuring the preparedness of the project logistics and recruiting procedures, staff will be trained on GP, MA basics, inclusion of PwDs, Risk education. Afterwards, teams will proceed with legal awareness raising on CSD & HLP, Risk Education awareness sessions: HRO Outreach team will raise the beneficiaries’ awareness on risk education using MA Sub-working group IEC materials, communities reached with protection monitoring, Referrals made for protection & non-protection. Referrals will be done according to the most updated service mapping, victim assistance services including Psychosocial support, orientation & Referral to external services and case management, training the humanitarian workers and local authorities on protection principles, Risk education.M&E plan will include a target-based performance, ITT, regular site visits by the M&E Field assistants to verify & document project implementation progress including routine meetings with key informants at the field level to generate qualitative data on the effectiveness of project interventions, besides beneficiaries’ satisfaction surveys. Depending on beneficiary consent forms, photos & videos & MoVs of project activities will be documented. Complaints’ mechanisms are in place to ensure quality, transparency, and access of vulnerable groups to all available services. Key messages and IEC materials on Cholera and COVID-19 will be disseminated at the end of each session.
