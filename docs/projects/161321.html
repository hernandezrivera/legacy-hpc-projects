Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen a rights-based protection response for IDPs, IDP returnees and to a lesser other affected populations, in particular those most vulnerable; through stronger advocacy, timely life-saving activities, streamlining prevention-oriented initiatives and evidence-based planning, with a focus in expanding outreach and developing the capacity of affected communities to cope, recover, contribute to and achieve Solutions.
The targeted population for UNHCR’s 2020 Programme consists of 680,000 internally displaced persons and returnees directly benefiting from assistance and delivery of protection services. The project also foresees benefiting the entire displaced population and returnees through continuous advocacy and support in the area of Law and Policy, fostering the successful engagement with the Transitional National Legislative Assembly (TNLA), the Ministries of Justice; Home Affairs, Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management and other relevant executive bodies (such as RRC, ROSS, etc.) with a focus on the approval and implementation of the new IDP Act; as well as related policy and regulatory frameworks; and building upon previous efforts of the Operation on capacity building and technical assistance to Parliamentarians and members of the Executive that culminated in the passing of the Kampala Convention and the final draft of the IDP Act in 2019.
The 2020 programme is designed to ensure that the Operation is fit-for-purpose, aiming for increased operational presence (static or mobile); efficiency in performance; as well as improved response and delivery, both in quality and speed. UNHCR has expanded its field protection unit to ensure systematic mobile protection monitoring and a strong presence in integrated missions and rapid assessments as well as mobile interventions. While response and monitoring will continue to cover POC and collective sites; coverage will also continue to expand to the 190 identified priority locations, including remote or hard-to-reach areas, in full support to the Protection Cluster Strategy for mobile assessments and response. Resources and advocacy will have a primary focus on Solutions, either durable or transitional (where the former is not yet possible), including activities planned to rapidly assess needs, risks, intentions, feasibility of returns, relocations or integration; family reunification, absorption capacity of host communities; while also strengthening community-based structures, HLP initiatives, promotion of livelihoods and peaceful coexistence. Improving coordination and quality of assessments and monitoring will be a key component; using the mobile tools for population profiling for more accurate and quicker data collection and triangulated analysis, which will improve the relevance of information products, leading to a more fitting risk-mitigation approach, reduced response time; while underpinning protection mainstreaming across other operational interventions, including of other Clusters. UNHCR will continue to make the best of its key membership of the protection cluster and ensure regular reporting to the clusters’ 5W and strong participation and contribution in the different clusters’ working groups (i.e. SAG, HLPWG, MPCF) to ensure coordination and complementarity of interventions, avoiding duplication with other members and ensuring regular sharing of reports with the cluster, as per the common practice pursued so far. UNHCR has been leading the coordination efforts in protection response in several locations in 8 of the 10 States across the country and it will continue fostering this important role to ensure that a more coordinated planning and response enhances efficiency and efficacy of programmes; successfully addressing gaps and targeting those in need in a timely manner.

1 - Needs

By the end of 2020, an estimated 1.5 million persons will be internally displaced in South Sudan. New displacement continues to be reported though in a much lesser extent, mainly in Central Equatoria, Upper Nile and some areas in WBG, Warrap, Jonglei and Lakes. Most IDPs continue to be settled in Unity, followed by Jonglei, Upper Nile and Central Equatoria. Around 68% of IDPs are women and children; the most vulnerable among the displaced. Displacement has also been influenced by severe food insecurity, resulting not only from climate shocks but especially aggravated by the effects of the prolonged conflict, which has compromised access to land, water, material inputs, markets as well as due to the overall deterioration of local economies. In areas such as Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity, the unprecedented floods in 2019 will also leave affected communities increasingly vulnerable due to loss of crops, cattle and property and many of such areas are also the places of origin of the vast majority of the current IDP population. Over the past years, the progressing collapse of the economy and public services has added extra pressure on the population, exacerbating intra-communal tensions, overall criminality and negative coping mechanisms, expanding the scale of needs to another level, where multiple displacement and complex dynamics just confirm the adverse effects of the growing protractiveness of the crisis. In such context, needs remain dire as IDPs continue to face serious protection risks, including threat to physical safety, serious human rights abuses such as torture, summary execution, disappearances, arbitrary arrests, limited freedom of movement, forced recruitment (including of children), family separation, GBV as well as destruction or illegal occupation of properties and minimal access to livelihoods. The number of IDPs living in hard-to-reach areas continues to make the majority of the displaced population, requiring a shift in humanitarian response as the current overall security stability now allows greater humanitarian access. As a result, previously inaccessible areas will require further attention; making integrated missions, rapid assessments and mobile response more of a priority; requiring robust expertise of Agencies with proven capacity to attend multiple areas and deliver both first-line response while maintaining regular protection monitoring in the deep field. Synergies with other clusters is required to ensure a protection-focused response to the changing needs of the populations; which now start transitioning to early-recovery and transitional solutions. With the positive prospects for the formation of a Unity Government, spontaneous returns are expected to continue increasing. Based on the data from UNCHR Profiling exercises conducted in 2019 (which covered 125,000+ IDPs, both from POC sites and spontaneous settlements), the main challenges for IDPs to return remain the lack of access to information about intended destinations; as well as lack of means for transportation and to rebuild their houses. On average, 40% of the POC populations consider leaving the site at one point, but security at intended destinations remain a concern; hence the increasing need for IDPs to receive updated information on areas of return. While the overall conditions across the country do not seem conducive yet for mass returns; pockets of opportunities remain for IDPs to act upon their intentions and seek transitional solutions while the future political scenario gradually unveils. As spontaneous returns are already a reality, originating both from neighboring countries and IDP sites, there is a critical need to support IDPs to make well-informed decisions and also invest in receiving communities applying an area-based approach and linking with other clusters and development partners, with a view to expand absorption capacity and peaceful coexistence; while boosting resilience and reintegration prospects of those returning.

2 - Activities

Main activities: - Participate in IRNA and other joint missions and support rapid assessments and mobile response;- Strengthen mobile protection monitoring through innovative technology and standardization of tools;- Ensure updated data is collected from the IDP populations and other affected populations (such as host communities and returnees) to ensure comprehensive understanding of population profiles, their needs, aspirations and challenges so as to improve programming and advocacy (within the protection cluster and other clusters); - Strengthen identification and support to persons with specific needs (PSNs), particularly women and children at risk; - Develop and strengthen referral pathways for protection response, including but not limited to assistance to PSNs;- Build the capacity of community structures in the identification and support to PSNs; - Provide support to PSNs, including core protection case management, material assistance or livelihoods/IGA activities where feasible;- Provide specialized assistance to persons with disabilities, including support services and devices; - Promote participation of IDPs in programming and monitoring; including through participatory assessments or surveys (both qualitative or quantitative data collection);- Promote peaceful co-existence with local communities and extend support to host communities targeting the most vulnerable individuals; - Support initiatives geared at resolving tensions or conflicts; - Strengthen/establish community self-management structures strategically engaging with youth, older persons, women and persons with disabilities through targeted programs;- Conduct participatory assessments through meetings with community representatives, focus group discussions, project monitoring and evaluations; - Prevent and respond to GBV; particularly in key locations;- Support the government to promote the development, strengthening and implementation of comprehensive solutions strategy in conformity with applicable international standards; - Support the humanitarian-development nexus in ensure the implementation of strategies related to Solutions, Early Recovery and Resilience in the main intended areas of return or (re)integration;- Conduct comprehensive solutions and intentions surveys of IDP population in selected priority locations; - Support feasibility assessments for voluntary returns and relocations;- Support return and relocations movements when feasible, either through the provision of information on intended destinations, material or transportation support, either through in-kind or cash assistance; - Support advocacy for local integration and linkages with development actors for early recovery projects;- Support the implementation of POC decongestion strategies; - Support IDPs access to legal remedies in relation to their rights, including reparations of violations; - Continue to support the government for the implementation of the Kampala Convention as well as the passing of and future implementation the new IDP Act;- Support development and strengthening of national policies affecting the rights of IDPs; - Assess HLP concerns of IDPs in main IDP sites to identify potential remedies;- Advocate for restitution of HLP rights for IDPs, including legal representation, mediation and information awareness campaigns.- Apply standard monitoring methodology across the locations, including to enhance understanding of the population profiled and to increase advocacy and referrals to other services providers; - Ensure systematic and quality data recording and reporting with a view to develop the IDP database for improved case management, advocacy and solutions programming;- Coordinate protection response in IDP locations where UNHCR is the focal agency for the protection cluster;- Lead the Solutions Working Groups in 07 locations, ensuring the HCT and IASC guidelines on Solutions are duly implemented and a comprehensive strategy is pursued;

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Throughout the project, UNHCR applies a rights-based approach. It will seek to ensure that the rights of IDPs and affected populations are not only known but respected sustainably. UNHCR has worked hand in hand with the government to ensure a legal framework on the rights of IDPs is developed and approved so that IDPs benefit from a more sustainable protective environment across the country. UNHCR will continue to promote the participation of IDPs in the National Dialogue, in the national consultations for the IDP Act as well as on discussions of any policies or frameworks that may affect their current status. UNHCR has done and will continue to promote discussions and awareness campaigns for IDPs and host communities to know more about IDPs' rights and how to enforce them. UNHCR has a legal assistance component on several projects with its partners which seek to increase rights education as well as individual legal counselling attached to the possibility for free legal aid in the form of mediation or legal representation. Simultaneously, UNHCR will continue to work closely with central and local authorities to ensure awareness on IDP's rights, particularly vis a vis the Guiding Principles, the Kampala Convention as well as the provisions in the other relevant regional and international instruments.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Perspectives of the Displacement-affected population are collected through regular participatory assessments conducted by UNHCR and its partners using the Age-Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) Methodology in all main IDP sites locations where it delivers protection assistance and/or implements protection interventions. In addition, UNHCR also collects information on opinions, aspirations and perceptions of IDPs, IDP returnees, Refugee Returnees as well as host communities through household-level surveys deployed on regular basis in the main IDP sites where it has static presence as well as through mobile assessments in hard-to-reach areas. The combination of both qualitative and quantitative data on the perspectives of the beneficiaries informs UNHCR and its partners in the annual planning as well as during the regular monitoring of the progress obtained through the implementation of the activities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

While some of the activities of the project relate to specific humanitarian/life-saving interventions; many of the outputs are aimed to strengthen resilience and empower communities. UNHCR has also strived to support government structures where feasible, and most in particular, the capacity of the Government to develop and implement the necessary legal and political framework to respond to Internal Displacement. As such, a significant component of the Project refers to the continuous support to the Government (including the Ministry of Justice, MAHDM and the Parliament) to implement the provisions of the Kampala Convention as well as to pass the recently-developed IDP Act (the latter drafted with substantial support from UNHCR). In 2020, further support to decentralized structures will also be sought, particularly with a view to increase investment in areas of return, strengthening communities capacity to absorb returnees and securing peaceful coexistence. With the increase stability across the country, should conditions allow, UNHCR will seek to increase capacity building to local government structures with a view to ensure (i) enhanced participation of IDPs in the development of policies and local plans; (ii) increased awareness on protection mainstreaming and on IDPs' rights; (iii) improved understanding of international standards for implementing solutions strategies. Last but not least, in the area of HLP and Legal Assistance, UNHCR will continue to seek close cooperation with communities' structures to ensure alternative resolution mechanisms are implemented in the best interest of IDPs and to ensure a human-rights approach in the resolution of potential disputes, including but not only on property restitution, custody and family matters.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives. As protection lead, UNHCR's project abides by the HCT strategy as well as by the relevant government policies. It is noteworthy that UNHCR has contributed significantly to the development of national policies on IDP response and Solutions to Internal Displacement and it works in close collaboration with the relevant governmental bodies to ensure international standards are pursue and the rights of IDPs taken into account during the establishment of national plans, strategies, policies and/or drafting of relevant legal instruments.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although the current context in South Sudan is not yet conducive for durable solutions; pockets of opportunities for transition solutions exist across the country. With the progressive implementation of the Revitalized Peace Agreement and the overall cessation of hostilities, there have been areas where stability and progressive growth have been noticed. IDPs have been increasingly expressing interest to return or relocate; particularly to recover their properties and avail themselves of livelihood opportunities. Based on UNHCR Profiling data, many still require further information about their intended destinations to act upon their intentions; a challenge that UNHCR will treat as a priority. In 2020, the Operation will prioritize feasibility assessments in intended destination as well as post return monitoring missions in order to ensure IDPs receive as much as possible information about their areas of return or relocation and can therefore make informed decisions. UNHCR will also continue monitoring returns in order to ensure eventual risks are mitigated or prevented and that information is also disseminated to those wishing to return to the same areas. Information on potential areas of return will also support the discussions among refugees wishing to return to their places of origin. Through the Solutions Working Group, UNHCR will seek further engagement with development actors, including the government structures, to ensure absorption capacity is enhance, peaceful coexistence is secured with the host communities and that reintegration projects through quick-impact interventions result in better conditions to allow returns in safety and dignity.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes a sustainability/handover component where feasible, particularly in the areas of Law&Policy, Community Mobilization and HLP, though the latter highly dependent on factors beyond the Organization's control. UNHCR has sought to increase its support to national NGOs and to ensure capacity is built throughout the project in benefit of the latter. In the area of Law& Policy, UNHCR has ensured a strong component of capacity building was included so as to empower government structures to take the lead and be better prepared to respond to any challenges. In the area of HLP, a component of HLP will explore the need to further support local land committees to resolve claims through mediation based on a rights-based approach. While in many of the interventions, handover is yet not possible due to the limited presence or capacity of other actors to take over the activities, UNHCR strives to ensure that community empowerment is at the core of the interventions and that participation of IDPs and affected communities is secured throughout, from the planning to the evaluation phases of the project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNHCR will ensure through its AGDM methodology that the views and interests of minority groups are heard and respected. Through both qualitative and quantitative methodologies, it will ensure the project and its partners are informed about the specific needs of such groups and any particular risks they might face, especially taking into account the "do no harm" approach, seeking not to expose the latter to further risks. UNHCR will also continue to strive preventing gender-based violence throughout the implementation of its project, be it through a very strong approach on PSEA as well as through mainstreaming gender-sensitive interventions in the delivery of assistance and protection services, as well as through the conduct of protection assessments. It will also complement its services with GBV prevention activities engaging women, girls as well as men and boys and ensure awareness campaigns are developed in an effective way to foster behavioral change. Profiling exercises will continue to be implemented to map out particular risks and locations affecting the well-being and safety of women and girls; which findings will inform UNHCR planning and advocacy with other services providers. On potential disputes related to land and tenure, where UNHCR will support shelter reconstruction or reclaiming of land and property, it will do so with full engagement of IDPs and local land committees and relevant leadership. UNHCR will conduct its HLP interventions through especialized partners, with full experience on land/property restitution, which over the years have already proved successful. Awareness campaigns and community mobilization will also be a strong component of such interventions in order to ensure participation and understanding of the affected communities. Last but not least, UNHCR will continue to support host communities and include the most vulnerable in distributions where funding allows. Quick-impact projects will be implemented for that purpose as well.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR conducts at least twice a year participatory assessments using the Age-Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming Methodology in all main locations where it delivers protection assistance and/or implements protection interventions. The Participatory assessments are a condition sine qua non for the planning of the annual programming and feedback sessions to the communities are also planned so that persons of concern, including but not only Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs), remain informed about the findings of the assessments and their own recommendations can be also validated. Apart from that, continuous FGDs and KIIs are organized throughout the year through the protection desks as well as mobile missions to collect qualitative data on the performance of partners and progress of the implemented activities. In addition, based on the roll-out of the Protection Monitoring Tool (PMT), UNHCR also collects the perceptions of IDPs through quantitative data collection initiatives which informs UNHCR advocacy, implementation as well as any need for review of its project whenever necessary. Particular attention is drawn to the most vulnerable groups, whose opinions' are particularly assessed by specific interviews made through household visits planned for the PMT; in case their voices are otherwise missed in group sessions.
