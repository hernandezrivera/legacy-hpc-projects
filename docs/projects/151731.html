Project Summary

The project will provide immediate access to unconditional and conditional cash assistance to the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in IDPs and host communities, it will also provide capacity enhancement to these target farmers in Baladweyne and Baidoa as well as agricultural seed inputs and tools to increase their farming production thus improving the nutrition of the target population in Bakol, Bay and Hiran region. The project will provide through cash for work will enable to provide immediate access to household income thus improving the overall wellbeing of the target communities. HIDIG will ensure to coordinates the foods security and livelihood activities closely with other humanitarian actors and concern ministries at regional and federal level to ensure better information sharing.

Needs

The acute food insecurity is showing gradual improvement as it can be seen from the total number of people in various IPC phases. However, trend analysis since 2011 (time series data of FSNAU by season) suggests about 43% of Somalia’s population (5.3 million) faced with acute food and livelihoods insecurity at given time. About 17% of them (2 million) faced with severe form of acute food insecurity (phase 3 and worse). The acute food insecure population is projected to be about 34% (4.6 million) up to end of the year and about 12% (1.5 million) will face severe food insecurity as compared to the long-term average (LTA). The FSC intended to prevent further deterioration of projected acute food insecurity by addressing the existing emerging situation in 2019.In the central regions, 548,000 people are in IPC Phase 3 and 269,000 people are in IPC Phase 4, while in southern regions 850,000 people are in IPC Phase 3 and 169,000 people are in IPC Phase 4.Exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized groups are contributing to high levels of acute humanitarian needs and lack of protection among some of the most vulnerable. At the same time, disease outbreaks such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD)/ cholera, other waterborne diseases and measles continue to lead to preventable deaths across the country”. The prolonged and complex natural and man-made disaster has caused heavy crop and livestock losses, displacement of the communities and disease outbreaks. It is critical to invest in lifesaving initiative which will not only help to save lives and mitigate a further deterioration of food security but will also help the communities to protect their livelihoods assets and minimize negative coping strategies. Livelihood protection and support is needed for nearly 3.1 million additional people who are stressed (IPC Phase 2).The deterioration of the overall nutrition situation in Somalia is largely attributed to the acute and widespread food insecurity.

Activities

•Provision of emergency unconditional cash transfer and safety nets to 800 (300 IDPs and 500 HC) vulnerable IDP and host community households considering 60% of the beneficiaries registered as vulnerable women household in Bakol and Hiran region south central Somalia. Each household will received 81 USD per months for 3 months period.•Provide cash for work activities to 300 men and women household heads and enable create short term job opportunities and create income to the vulnerable drought displaced and AWD affected IDPs and host communities to enable improve the community livelihood structure hence provide alternative livelihood •Sensitize vulnerable household benefiting from unconditional cash transfer on nutrition, health and hygiene promotion for food preparation to enable reduce malnutrition and disease hence improve community living standard.•Conduct training to capacity building farmers (48 men and 62 women household headed) on proper preparing, harvesting as well as management of farm inputs storage hence enhancing their knowledge and thus inculcate skills for their livelihood in futures

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Baladweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing MCH/food security and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing mobile OTP teams in consultation with the host communities and local authorities. Where not possible provided mobile outreach. Based on the food security and livelihood Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the IDPs and host communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneficiaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and food security and livelihood infrastructure, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, HIDIG will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence.
