Project Summary

Over one year into the conflict, the overall security situation in Northern Ethiopia remains complex and fluid, hindering effective delivery of lifesaving assistance to the most affected populations, including refugees and internally displaced people. The conflict in Northern Ethiopia (Tigray, Afar and Amhara) has led to the internal displacement of over 3 million people, with displacement continuing to occur on a daily basis across the region, and 9.4 million people are in urgent need of food and other aid.
Specifically, in Tigray region, according to regional authorities, the conflict has displaced over 1.8 million people in Tigray region and out of this, partners supported the return of more than 8,000 IDPs to their villages and towns in Central and Eastern zones. About 5,000 people from Dedebit IDP site in North-Western Zone were relocated to Selekleka on 15 January due to security concerns following an airstrike on the site. In addition to this 4,149 IDPs (2,350 households) who have voluntarily elected to return from Mekelle to their places of origin in Central and Eastern zones have received assistance. To date, 5,294 individuals (3,198 households) have returned in five convoys since 8 December from Mekelle to South-Eastern, Central and Eastern zones and ongoing mobilization to assist 219 people (43 households) who arrived in Mekelle from Zelambasa in Eastern Zone have been placed in Adishmdhun site.
When IDPs are forced to flee and leave behind their homes and belongings, IDPs settle in various set ups, including communal settings. Accordingly IDPs demands different humanitarian supports, in tigray region there are 4.2 million people need assistance with ES/NFIs for ensuring adequate shelters and NFIs for IDPs living in highly congested and substandard living conditions and relocation of IDPs from schools for their re-opening to adequate alternative shelters and for those willing to return to areas of origin, and to IDPs currently living within the host community across tigray region and considering the upcoming rainy season which will be the difficult condition for war affected communities.
As a result the emergency assistances requires intervention of international and national humanitarian organizations. The main purpose of the emergency assistance is to help to get relief, to enhance protection and safety for IDPs. Accordingly, SRO which mainly works on emergency humanitarian assistances wants to intervene on ES/NFI and Protection assistance. Hence, the main purpose of this project proposal is to provide in kind or cash emergency shelter, shelter repair kit and NFI’s and protection( which is going to be implemented through providing dignity kits, Conduct trainings on GBV for frontline GBV service providers and of GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls provided with livelihood skills and support for IGAs which is expected to enhance capacity of GBV frontline service providers and GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls) assistance to IDPs and host communities in tigray region and projects will be designed to address cross cutting issues as mainstreaming protection, accountability to affected people, ensuring PSEA and gender, Disability and Environmental protection considerations. The vulnerable and disable people who will receive assistance will be identified based on the data from BOLSA or UN agencies. Using the data’s obtained from BOLSA or UN Agencies, SRO will conduct data verification before the assistance for all beneficiaries to ensure that they have access to aid. The planned intervention is for one year (01/01/2022-31/12/2022) focusing on addressing gaps in accessing Shelter and Non-food items distribution and protection assistances (GBV). The Project starting from its design phase will be planned to integrate with other projects of different clusters through closely working with proposed to be relevant cluster. During the projects implementation relevant stockholders will be involved.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In humanitarian settings, aid agencies often assist populations by transferring resources, usually using cash, vouchers or in-kind aid, such as food rations, shelter materials, seeds, tools and kits of household items. The use of cash or vouchers to replace in-kind aid is most pronounced in food assistance programming, but it is increasingly being considered for emergency shelter, education and many other kinds of programming.Giving recipients cash that they can use to meet their needs as they think best, or giving them some form of voucher, which makes the assistance available through a local market system but restricts to some degree what recipients can buy or where they can make their purchases. All are important based on their condition of usage because the in-kind assistance is important but the in-kind assistance can’t fulfill beneficiaries other demands. Hence, in order to fulfill the other demands of the beneficiaries’ cash or vouchers are important.
