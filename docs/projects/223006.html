Project Summary

The OBAT Learning Center project aims to provide equitable access to education for 760 students including 345 girls and 295 boys in Rohingya Community (RC) & 120 (30X4) students including 75 girls and 50 boys in Host Community (HC). Students are in the 3-14 age group will follow the Myanmar curriculum approved by the Education Sector for Rohingyas and digital learning for the host communities. Among the total number of students 3% CWD students (27) will be included.
The implementation of this project will take place through continuing operation of 12 Learning Centers (LCs). 8 LCs in Camp- 4 and in the Host Community 4 LCs
By implementing this initiative, OBAT helpers aims to foster an environment that promotes equitable learning, empowering Rohingya children and Host community children with knowledge and skills that will have a profound impact on their overall well-being, both in the present and in the future. Parents and the guardians of the children will be properly engaged in supporting the initiatives as such quality of the services could be ensured. Relevant staff and volunteers will be trained in line with the guidelines of the Education Sector as well as engaging all stakeholders where it is necessary.
OBAT Helpers, with partner organization, has been actively involved in providing support to the Rohingya response program since early 2018. OBAT Helpers has been operating a Health Project, OBAT Learning Center Project in Camp # 4, and emergency response.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Rohingya and Host community children need an equitable learning environment to empower them with knowledge and skills that will have a profound impact on their overall well-being, both in the present and in the future. Due to poverty and pressing family needs, school-aged children are being forced into income-generating activities to support their families. Furthermore, young girls are subjected to early marriages, depriving them of their right to a childhood and a promising future.The lack of access to education for Rohingya children will have profound and long-lasting repercussions on the future generation of Rohingya. Their overall well-being, opportunities, and potential for a brighter future will be significantly compromised if their educational needs are not adequately addressed.In response to these pressing challenges, the OBAT Helpers Learning Center Project has been meticulously designed to guarantee quality education for as many Rohingya and host community children as possible while maintaining cost-effectiveness. The guardians and the community engagement in the project is also identified as crucial to ensure quality education services.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

OBAT helpers design and implement programs considering potential protection risk and potential violation factors and ensure that the maximum protective measures are undertaken. The OBAT Helpers Learning Center Project will ensure that its interventions have no adverse effect on people’s dignity and safety. It will reduce vulnerability as well as physical and psychological risks. Efforts will be made to recruit female staff members both within the project and the organization. This step aims to enhance female participation and ensure diverse perspectives. The Project will ensure access to the services without discrimination, gender, disability and aged related barriers. Vulnerable individuals and communities will be given proper attention in its service delivery. Students of the vulnerable families and communities are selected. To establish effective feedback mechanisms and address concerns, OBAT will introduce a complaint/advice box. This box will serve as a confidential channel for beneficiaries to voice their complaints, provide advice, and make suggestions. The implementation of this approach aligns with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding policies ensuring a safe and respectful environment for all.To maintain community engagement and receive feedback on project activities, OBAT will continue involving parents and local leaders such as imams, Head Majhis, and sub-Majhis in the evaluation of learners. Their participation provides valuable insights and strengthens community ownership of the project. Moreover, OBAT will collect information from the complaint/advice box allowing beneficiaries to share their thoughts, concerns, and recommendations. In brief, OBAT Helpers Education Program demonstrates a comprehensive approach to protection mainstreaming by integrating protection measures across various components of the project.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The OBAT Learning Center project aims to provide access to education for 960 (80X12) students including 528 girls in Rohingya Community (RC) & 240 (60X4) students including 132 girls in Host Community (HC). Students in the 5-14 age group will follow the Myanmar curriculum approved by the Education Sector.OBAT Helpers is dedicated to promoting inclusivity and ensuring the participation of people with disabilities (PWD) and children with disabilities (CWD) during the implementation of the project. As part of this commitment, OBAT Helpers will strive to include a minimum of 5% of PWD and CWD within the project, based on their availability within the targeted population.By actively including PWD and CWD in the project, OBAT Heleprs aims to provide them with equal opportunities and access to the project's activities, services, and benefits. This inclusive approach recognizes the rights and unique needs of individuals with disabilities and seeks to create a supportive and empowering environment that promotes their well-being and development.OBAT Helpers will ensure that appropriate measures and accommodations are in place to facilitate the participation and engagement of PWD and CWD. This may include providing accessible infrastructure, adaptive learning materials, specialized support, and targeted interventions to meet their specific requirements.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

OBAT Helpers strongly feels that the impacted people should have the chance to raise concerns, offer input, and receive a response, so it made sure that this initiative has the appropriate mechanisms in place. The comments gathered from the impacted populations will be examined during project implementation, and necessary measures will be implemented on a regular basis. In this project, possible PSEA hazards in program delivery contexts will be evaluated and mitigating actions will be determined. OBAT helpers also provides copies of the PSEA Code of Conduct to all employees, as well as other safety precautions, and reminds them of their responsibilities. Through the project outcomes framework, the project focus is tasked with keeping an eye on AAP-related outputs and indicators. To ensure active engagement and a successful community feedback system by the facility, OBAT Helpers also includes affected populations in the monitoring process to ensure community feedback through facility based CESG & Parents’ meeting. OBAT Helpers will also collect feedback in ‘Complaint & Suggestion box’ in each of learning center.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The OBAT Learning Center project is in full alignment with the objectives of the Cox's Bazar Education Sector and also takes into account the priorities of other sectors, including Health, Livelihood, Gender, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Disaster, and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The project adheres to the standards set by the Inter-agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE), ensuring that the education provided meets internationally recognized guidelines.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. To reduce the dangers of future disasters, OBAT Helpers will plant a suitable number of trees on the boundary of the Learning Centers and took into account the usual flooding and landslides when building the facilities. OBAT Helpers took all kinds of initiatives for any kind of incidents and climate disaster. All Learning centers will be equipped with kits and have DRR plans to protect & survive from disasters. The LCs are used as Safe Havens for the disaster affected victims. Before monsoon appears, OBAT Helpers teams tie down all the facilities taking preparation to cover up the disaster.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

OBAT Helpers have a strong commitment to build capacities of local communities as well as staff and volunteers in line with the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR). OBAT will also keep allocating funds for the immediate response in any emergency. The following measures will be taken - Improve community-based disaster preparedness by leveraging trained local volunteers. For example, expand the Cyclone Preparedness Program to support and train Rohingya and more host communities.- Identify local capacities and engage with local actors for disaster preparedness, including non-traditional actors like faith-based groups, the private sector, and other civil society organizations.- Increase disaster preparedness among local and national organizations by building organizational policies and processes that would facilitate disaster response coordination responsibilities- Work with local government to increase knowledge of the Standing Orders on Disaster and other disaster management policies and guidelines, and to prepare their own contingency plans through a formalized training program

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project has a plan to engage youth through different activities with a view to keeping them engaged in activities of community participation as well community development. Activities based youth groups will be formed and further plan will be developed with them. Coordination with other development actors in the camps will be developed to adopt a coordinated approach
