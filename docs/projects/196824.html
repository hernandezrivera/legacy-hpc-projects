Project Summary

This project aims to support the immediate needs of the most vulnerable displaced populations in northwest Syria through the distribution of emergency multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA).
IOM will target households with an emergency one-off MPC distribution ($150 USD per household, as recommended by the Cash Working Group (CWG) for 2023, and informed by the monthly Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB); however, the emergency MPCA budget line ($1,228,500) includes all the associated cost to distribute the cash, including IP cost and transfer fees) to address their immediate needs after experiencing a shock. This amount is tailored to cover a family’s needs in food and consumables for a month and will focus on beneficiaries in emergency conditions. IOM will target vulnerable households in informal settings or in host communities. These payments are therefore designed as a response to crises such as recent conflict, displacement, or natural crises. The proposed approach is in line with the S/NFI cluster recommendations in NWS.
IOM’s Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) department through TPM will conduct a beneficiaries’ verification of at least 20 per cent of the caseload to ensure adequate targeting. A rapid market capacity check shall be undertaken by the IP prior to each distribution to attest those targeted markets can absorb the demand for goods and replenish supplies. In the event of significant changes to the markets, IOM would reassess the project locations. Three to four weeks after the distribution, PDM activities will be coordinated between the partner’s M&E unit and IOM’s TPM to assess the impact of the distribution on the local economy and its quality, relevance, utilization, satisfaction, and effectiveness among beneficiaries.

3 - Needs

Displacements due to extreme poverty and hyperinflation have become major humanitarian challenges in NWS. Nearly two-thirds (61%) of all displacements occurring in August 2022 were a result of the deteriorating economic situation in the location of origin, and a further 37 per cent of displacements occurred due to a perceived increase in livelihood opportunities in their new location (HNAP Monthly Mobility and Needs Monitoring reports - August 2022). Currently, 1.7 million people are residing in over 1,400 unplanned, self-established IDP settlements (Northwest Syria: Unmet Shelter Needs). These camps are severely overcrowded, with most families living in old tents or makeshift shelters, often with also no access to basic services.

4 - Implementation of activities

Beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability criteria from the S/NFI Cluster and the CWG. Based on the CWG’s recommendations, IOM will ensure through TPM that the IPs observe protection mainstreaming and COVID-19 mitigation measures in the distributions. A rapid market capacity check shall be undertaken by the IP prior to each distribution to attest those targeted markets can absorb the demand for goods and replenish supplies. Three to four weeks after the distribution, PDM activities will be coordinated between the partner’s M&E unit and IOM’s TPM. IOM will use PTT to first transfer resources from Turkey to PTT offices in NWS, and then utilize a Financial Service Provider (FSP) to transfer the money to the chosen areas. Following CWG recommendations, cash assistance is provided in USD as standard. When USD notes are insufficient in quantity or quality, beneficiaries have the option to select the currency in which the remaining amount can be received. The distribution of cash assistance shall be organized in full by IPs with the announcement to beneficiaries, organization of the premises, registration, data control, distribution, accounting, and security staff. The role of the FSP’s transfer agent will be only to hand the money to the selected beneficiaries under supervision of the IP upon verification of identity by the IP.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

In cash distributions, tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees is a risk. IOM will mitigate this by involving all stakeholders, communicating the selection criteria, distributing according to verified needs, and establishing a complaint mechanism. Given the unstable security situation in NWS, there is the risk that conflict may impede humanitarian access to certain locations. IOM continuously monitors the security situation and has contingency plans in place, and IOM and IPs maintain communication with local authorities. Distribution sites will be assessed and selected close to beneficiary households to reduce security risks on transit.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All IP staff are required to have undertaken training on PSEA and signed a PSEA Code of Conduct. Details of IOM’s AAP mechanism are communicated to participants, as well as the Interagency PSEA hotline where appropriate. Gender segregated lines and gender-mixed distribution teams are always used by IPs to further mitigate SEA risks. As IOM is the leading agency for PSEA, all programmes are required to follow the network’s recommendations and incorporate them into each project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks include access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines, aid diversion by armed groups, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, inflation of market prices and COVID-19. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will be in regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents and conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations. IOM also has a dedicated access unit that monitors and facilitates principled access for partners within Syria, and IOM will continue to participate in OCHA’s access working group. For a potential closure of Bab Al-Hawa, IOM will work with the broader humanitarian community to further mitigate the risk of non-renewal. To mitigate the impact of inflation on cash recipients, IOM will conduct strong and constant market monitoring and will distribute USD to prevent currency devaluation. The risk of COVID-19 infection to beneficiaries and staff will be mitigated through IPC measures. Additionally, there is the risk that beneficiaries will use cash for unintended purposes which IOM will mitigate by tailoring cash to SMEB needs and by raising awareness of the intended use of cash to beneficiaries during distributions.
