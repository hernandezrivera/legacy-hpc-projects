Project Summary

This health project will provide emergency life- saving health care services to persons affected by humanitarian situations with focus on children, women and vulnerable populations including internally displaced, elderly, those with disabilities, refuges and returnees for a period of 12 months. The direct beneficiaries to be reached by UNICEF across all health interventions will be 1,231,055 (31%)of the 3.9 million people in need targeted by Health cluster. This includes persons with disabilities (estimated at 15%) Outpatient consultations will be provided to 1,096,305 of which 515,263 will be under 5 years of age (263,113 girls and 252,150 boys) 350,818 women and 230, 224 men; 49,334 pregnant women will be targeted for skilled birth attendance; 83,320 (45,826 girls and 37,494 boys.) under 5 to be vaccinated against measles and 2,096 (1,174 females and 922 males) frontline health workers will benefit from capacity development to improve service delivery. Twenty -nine districts are proposed in nine priority regions (Bakool, Banadir, Bay, Galgadud, Gedo, Hiraan, Lower Jubba, Lower Shabelle, Middle Shabelle)
based on trends of cyclic humanitarian shocks and crises such as floods, droughts, conflicts and outbreaks. UNICEF will support services through Primary health care facilities, outreach and mobile teams to treat those in underserved areas including the elderly and persons with disabilities who have limited ability to reach the fixed health facilities. Health care interventions will be based on the Essential Package of health services (EPHS) with the delivery of antenatal and post- natal services, emergency obstetrics and neonatal care, immunizations both routine and campaign such as OPV, measles and cholera as required ,outpatient consultative services for under 5 children with focus on common childhood illnesses and care for adults including the elderly. Services will be tailored to address the impacts of above-mentioned outbreaks like AWD/Cholera (through CTCS, CTUS and ORPs), COVID-19 ,malnutrition and other co-morbidities while strengthen referral systems for management of complications and for access to comprehensive care and psychosocial support for those affected by Gender based violence. In partnership with the nutrition cluster preventive and curative nutritional services for malnourished children (MAM and SAM) and women, deworming, breastfeeding services for PLW and micronutrient supplementation will be provided in the health facilities and outreaches. Skills of frontline health care workers will be built on case management of common illnesses, GBV survivors and clinical management of rape, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA),disease surveillance. Additionally, capacity development will be provided for Infection Prevention and control with focus on both medical and non-medical cadre and in the context of COVID-19 improve triage and handwashing activities in supported HFs. Community health workers will be capacitated to provide key messages on outbreaks and services available and support disease surveillance activities in the communities. Health interventions to be provided will be done in a consultative manner through use of NGOS with presence in the area and Civil societies and community development committees which cuts across women , men and youth groups. This will be done in partnership with the UNICEF communication for development team and the Risk Communication and Community Engagement pillar. Whilst different groups will be involved in the design and implementation UNICEF will provide supported health facilities with essential medicines, kits, PPE, IPC supplies and equipment for emergency response. UNICEF will work with FGS/MoH, SMoHS and NGOS/CSO, the national health cluster and sister UN agencies in public health assessments, planning, coordination and implementation of preparedness and response (P&R) measures with focus on district level team (DHMT) strengthening.

Project Activities

UNICEF's 2021 proposed activities will align to the Somalia Humanitarian strategic objectives, Health Cluster Objectives and UNICEF organizational 2021 Humanitarian Action for Children ( HAC) appeal. The activities will include ;1. Provision of access to integrated emergency Life - saving health interventions and essential primary healthy care services for conflict -affected persons, IDPS, host communities in health facilities; 2. Use of mobile medical services to deliver health care to underserved and hard to reach populace; 3. Provision of emergency reproductive health services for crises-affected and underserved populations including referral for comprehensive emergency obstetric and neonatal care;4. Provide basic care for gender-based violence survivors and link them to designated health facilities and organizations with expertise for comprehensive and psychosocial support ;5. Provision of immunization services; 6. prepositioning and provision of essential medicines and supplies and 7. Capacity building for health facility staff and Community health workers on basic case management, disease surveillance, community engagement and health education including COVID-19 messages to community members.COVID response support will include 1. Refresher training of frontline health workers and Community health on COVID case management, IPC, continuation of essential services and Community health workers on IPC and home- based care and delivering of Key messages.2. Operational cost to support surge staff such as retired nurses and CHWs to strengthen to the strain on the already weak health system.3. Establishment and strengthening of triage stations through provisions of tents, staff dedicated to ensuring functionality of the triage station the stations and temperature reading devices and PPE4. Collaboration with WASH sector provision and utilization of handwashing stations in health facilities through provision of soaps, hand sanitizers and water5. Procurement of PPE for health services delivery for frontline health workers 6. Provision of COVID- specific therapeutics- e.g Oxygen cylinders, pulse oximeters Twenty percent (USD $ 4,742,174) of the total cost is proposed for the COVID-19 Activities in 2021. With the increased global demand of PPEs and shortage from manufacturers, fluctuation costs of supplies especially pneumonia diagnostics and therapeutic equipment and supplies, challenges with restrictions in flights and increased cost of transportation UNICEF supports NNGOS and INGOs to implement health care services through signed humanitarian and contingency program documents following a default competitive open selection process or direct selection in sudden onset emergencies. Partners are required to state in the legal documents all other funding sources and where co funding is agreed the percentage that UNICEF funding support will be utilized to support synergy and avoid duplication. Supplies are procured by UNICEF procurement system and provided directly to IPS on quarterly basis. In 2020 UNICEF’s humanitarian response in the proposed regions supported 16 IPS providing health care services in 61 Primary health care centres and health units and 111 outreach and mobile locations and this will be continued in 2021 based on funding availability. The modalities that will be used for services delivery include out- patient consultations, inpatient care and referrals services to next level of care through the primary health care and health units, while outpatient consultations and referrals will be done through outreach teams and mobile teams.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1 Mitigation measure -1: Provision of respectful and inclusive basic primary health care services within IDP camp settings and host communities; 2. Use of mobile health services to reach the unreached and reduce geographical barriers; 3. Supported HF environment will be well lit outside and on the access route to the facility and health services will take into consideration cultural and religious practices and will consider separate waiting areas for males and females. Facilities will be encouraged to have a significant proportion of female health staff to address cultural preferences and sensitivities; 4. Installation of hand railings and handle bars for persons with disability and 5. Engagement with affected populations through dialogue during designing and implementation of services to support co-ordination, problem resolutions and prioritization of the most vulnerable for limited resources and also with host communities who may feel deprived by the additional pressure on health services and other resources Risk 2: -1. Information dissemination and awareness to women and populace that humanitarian services are free and expected code of conduct for humanitarian personnel ;2. Capacity building of frontline health workers on 'Do No Harm' principle, SEA and identification, basic management ,investigation and reporting of cases of GBV 3.In cooperation with protection actors, reporting mechanisms will be established to report security incidents concerning GBV survivors Risk 3-4 mitigation measures 1. Engage health workers with skills and experience working with children; 2. Clear and context specific policies and code of conducts emphasizing expected and prohibited behaviors and consequences to be available in organizations supporting humanitarian service delivery and should apply to all staff, contractors and vendors; 3. Available complaint reporting mechanism aware to affected populations that is confidential; 4. Communications materials in English & Somali available in offices, project sites ,health facilities and communities. Risk 5 mitigation measures for land disputes and tenure insecurity -1. order to avoid community tensions, host communities will be provided with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities; 2. Both displaced and host communities will be consulted about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.; 3.Promotion of rights-based approach to service provision, providing access and services to all affected people without discrimination; . Prepositioning essential supplies in strategic hubs for immediate response, providing outreach and mobile health services to displaced communities, providing referral services for critical casesRisk of explosive hazard contaminations- 1. Please provide further explanation

This project will provide gap filling emergency response with certain elements that could serve as potential for longer durable solutions. The provision of essential integrated health services will support continuous functionality of the health facilities and access of the populace to health care. The integration of GBV and PSEA activities will also protect the rights of the populace. Community engagement through dialogue and participation during designs and implementation will ensure ownership and utilization of the available health resources and involvement of community members including community health workers and social mobilizers. Increasing proportion females health workers in services delivery will boost morale of women, address cultural sensitivities and enhanced sense of safety to utilize services.
