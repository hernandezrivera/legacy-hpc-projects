Needs

From the 921,000 refugees in Cox's Bazar, roughly 52% of the population are women and girls. Beyond the targeted gender-based violence (GBV) women and girls experienced in Myanmar, there are also harmful social norms that expose them to more risk. Furthermore, following displacement, women and girls are exposed to further risks due to poor camp conditions and seasonal natural disasters causing additional stress. Other stressors such as limited basic resources, restricted freedom of movement, and the lack of economic self-reliance contribute to poor psychosocial wellbeing, which may lead to negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, survival sex, trafficking, and other GBV. To address the critical needs and key priorities noted in the 2018 JRP, IOM established ten Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) where women and girls can feel safe, access information, services, and social networks. IOM is now fully operational in nine camps managing ten Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) for women and girls and GBV survivors. In the WGSS, women and girls can access individual GBV case management services, age-appropriate psychosocial support (PSS) activities, life-skills trainings, information and awareness sessions, support networks, and referrals to other services. Through sessions on GBV mitigation and prevention, awareness and informational sessions, women and girls are empowered to manage their own safety and protection. Community mobilisers are simultaneously doing outreach with communities, by conducting awareness raising sessions, conducting safety mapping and planning, sharing information on IOM’s available services, and conducting referrals. Beyond safe spaces, IOM also established the only 24/7 emergency safe shelter in for GBV survivors and victims of trafficking (VoTs) at imminent risk of serious harm or trafficking who have no alternative safe housing option. Shelter residents are provided with board and lodging, counselling, PSS and life skills training, access to educational and recreational activities, other social resources, and support in return to their permanent/makeshift housing. Looking forward to 2019, and reflecting upon the past year’s GBV response, there is still a gap in access and information on life saving services for survivors, particularly around health and safety. At the time the 2018 JRP was written, actors regularly received hundreds of GBV incident reports in a week and based on the mid-term review of the 2018 JRP, only 43% of minimum service coverage was achieved for urgently required GBV case management and PSS support for children and adults. There is also an increasing need to provide a static integrated space for conducting case management with male survivors - across all sites, there are limited entry points to provide care for male survivors. Furthermore, there is an overall gap in services and engagement of men and boys pertaining to GBV.

Activities or outputs

As the emergency response approaches early recovery, IOM will prepare for durable solutions by expanding its GBV prevention activities, particularly life skills and resilience building opportunities for women from both refugee and host communities through a strengthened and integrated community-based protection response while improving the quality of existing services through strengthening capacity and ensuring sector standards. IOM will continue to support the capacity of stake holders on GBV prevention and mitigation and bolster coordination and communication by participating in joint lessons learned sessions and exchanging best practices. The team will also aim to adapt a specialized curriculum for the Rohingya and host communities’ men and boys for male engagement in GBV response and prevention. This will also serve as GBV sensitization in engaging women and girls in skills building and livelihoods activities and in engaging women in community decision-making and leadership. IOM will improve coordination among the sub-sectors by responding to the growing need for an integrated emergency safe shelter for EVIs, GBV survivors, children, and VoTs.Currently, IOM works with male survivors, however, case management is conducted in IOM’s site management offices and dedicated counselling spaces for male survivors remain limited and ad hoc. In 2019, IOM will aim to establish a community center where men and boys can engage in a specialized curriculum focused on gender roles, GBV, and substance abuse, and begin male engagement in GBV prevention while also serving as an entry point for case management, PSS, and other services for male survivors. In parallel and to ensure a gender-synchronized approach, women and girls will continue to engage in a complementary curriculum on GBV guiding principles, gender roles, and sexual and reproductive health. While the GBV team currently makes referrals to Transition, Recovery, and Development’s CfW scheme focusing on women, IOM will look for other life-skills and resilience building opportunities to strengthen the women’s and girls’ self-reliance and sense of empowerment.IOM will continue to provide essential GBV services through survivor-centered case management and PSS and focus on improving the quality and awareness of these services. IOM will continue mainstreaming GBV into emergency preparedness and response interventions, training women and girls in DRR, and working closely with other sectors. IOM has dignity kits and solar lanterns prepositioned for emergencies.Lastly, with the existing emergency shelter having reached maximum capacity, and the different needs of reported cases that call for an integrated shelter, the GBV team will be closing its existing emergency shelter and reopening at a new location to support the growing numbers of vulnerable individuals including separated facilities for children, GBV survivors, VoTs, and other extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs). Individuals will be able to receive interim specialized support and care until safely relocated to their residence or new site. In summary, IOM plans to carry out following GBV activities in 2019:Activity 1: Maintain ten safe spaces and one emergency shelter as entry points for GBV case management, PSS, and other life-saving servicesActivity 2: Quality case management services will be accessible to GBV survivorsActivity 3: Quality and focused PSS activities will be accessible for women and girls at WGSSActivity 4: Legal assistance for women and girls at risk of GBV and survivors of GBV Activity 5: Life-skills and resilience programming will be conducted for women and girls at WGSSActivity 6: Community outreach will be conducted to raise awareness on GBV and its guiding principles as well as to share information on available GBV servicesActivity 7: Capacity building of GBV service providers, and other key stakeholders on GBV standards, principles, core concepts, and referral pathways
