Project Summary

This project is designed to complement HAA ongoing Health projects in the counties supported by SSHF/UNDP, WHO, UNFPA and the National Malaria Control Program (NMCP)-MoH to reduce excess morbidity, increase access to Integrated quality life-saving primary health care services among vulnerable Populations in hard to reach Counties; Canal Pigi, Jur River, Morobo Terekeka and Renk.
The response is aligned to the Health cluster objectives; CO1: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, CO2: Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations and CO3: Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
Context analysis:
Dire humanitarian needs among displaced, host and returnee populations remains an issue of major concern in the counties under high severity; IPC 3, 4 &5. Canal Pigi, Jur River, Morobo, Terekeka and Renk are hard to reach Counties classified under these severities (IPC Aug 2019).
In Canal Pigi County; health coverage is low with returnee Populations in Alam and Kaldak walks (2-3) days to access health service. Limited Vitamin A supplementation (6-59) Months and low immunization coverage. Populations rely on river Sobaat and inland swamps. High morbidity and mortality is attributed to diarrheal diseases both acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and Acute bloody Diarrhea (ABD). During the flood flash, 5984 IDPs and 3,809 returnees were affected in the County (PHSA; HAA Sept 2019). Oral fecal infections, dysentery etc. SGBV is a major concern in dry spelt as women walk for (2-3)hours to reach water points between Mar-May when the nearby swamps dry up. The (IMC May 20 19 Smart survey) recorded a GAM rate of 24.3% and SAM rate of 6.7% in children (6-59) Months. Equally, Jur River County experience cross border movement and communal conflicts. Most of the health units are dysfunctional. Measles outbreak persisted throughout despite reactive campaigns in 2019. Psycho social distress, mental health is majorly attributed to SGBV-Rape, STIs, lost of capital assets and fire outbreaks. There are high inflows of returnees from Sudan (IoM DTM Oct 2019)
Renk in Upper Nile experience inflows of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia. (REACH Jan-Mar 2019) a vicious circle of diarrheal diseases both acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and acute bloody Diarrhea (ABD), nematodes with malnutrition, typhoid and HEV. There is inadequate health services (Situation over view Upper Nile; REACH Jan-Mar 2019).
Morobo lie along the Ebola susceptible zone close to Congo. Armed conflicts devastated the health infrastructure. The population access health care from either Congo or Uganda. In Terekeka County, much as severe food insecurity across the areas is unprecedented, Prevalence of common diseases is high especially (AWD as well as Respiratory Tract Infections (RTI
Intervention Mode: Intervention Mode: scale up 2 mobile teams in canal pigi and 1 in Morobo, support 1 PHCU in Morobo and 1 in Terekeka, support 1PHCC in Renk. The selected packages will compliment health systems to ensure delivery of a well-coordinated access and utilization of health services. First, through effective coordination of services delivery at Boma level, Payam level and County level through working with the Health Committees, Community Leaders, CHDs staff, ensuring access and utilization through reliable opening and closing hours as per government regulations that will be managed through reporting and departure register; and Fourth, promptly attend to cases at respective levels and use appropriate referral forms to transfer cases for further management at appropriate health facility level.

2 - Activities

Activities:The Project activities will enhanced delivery of integrated health services through a network of health facilities(Mobile response, PHCUs and PHCCs) through effective coordination of services delivery at Boma level, Payam level and County level. HAA will work with the Health Committees, Community Leaders, CHDs staff; ensure access and utilization through reliable opening and closing hours as per government regulations that will be managed through reporting and departure register; promptly attend to cases at respective levels and use appropriate referral forms to transfer cases for further management at appropriate health facility level. HAA will conduct a Public Health Situation analysis (PHSA) in the selected CountiesA) Activities to be implemented at the PHCUs for Primary Health Care Services: Provide input and deliver Health Education, Promotion and Counselling. Manage uncomplicated cases and prompt referral of cases with complications or danger signs Management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT, SFP, Outpatient consultation for common diseases , MCH (ante natal care, uncomplicated/normal delivery), Routine EPI and Mass vaccination, Outpatient for TB , Application of the Minimum Initial response for HIV, Provision of psycho-social support, Basic Management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment STI and wound), ORS Corner and Environmental health (vector control and medical waste management) B) Activities to be implemented at PHCCs for Primary Health Care Services:Health Education, Promotion and Counselling.Curative services, including use of Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood illnesses (IMNCI) guidelines. Routine EPI services, EPI outreaches and defaulter tracing. Managememnt of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT, SFP, - Management of SAM with medical complications, minor surgery and observation ward, Outpatient consultation for common diseases & outreach capacities, Clean and safe normal delivery, Essential newborn care: basic newborn resuscitation + warmth + eye, prophylaxis + clean cord care + early and exclusive breast feeding, Obstetric Care: Basic Emergerncy Obstetric and Neanatal Care (BEMONC), manual vacuum aspiration (MVA), post abortion care (PAC) , PMTCT, Expanded Management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care, emergency contraceptive pills, prevention care for tetanos, Hepatitis B and mental health care ), Management of TB, Application of the Expanded response for HIV/AIDS patients: STI & HIV/AIDS: Syndromic management of sexually transmitted infections, prophylaxis and treatment of opportunistic infections, prevention of mother-to-child HIV transmission Surveillance/EWARNreporting/: - We will conduct Weekly Surveillance IDSR & EWARN data activity based reporting.C) Activities to be implemented in the mobile response services:HAA will deliver expanded community health services for the prevention and treatment of common conditionsscreening all under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC ,Rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women, Screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy, Screening for vaccination needs and Screening for presumptive TB. Essential Health care Package: Management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT, SFP, Outpatient consultation for common diseases, Vaccination against Measles & Polio for all under 5, Vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women (if not received), Mass vaccination and case management for epidemic-prone diseases (Menengitis, Measles and OCV), Provision of psycho-social first aid,D) Activities to be implemented for Community level engagement:• Community participation in Boma Health Committees and Health Facility Management Committees.• Community and household-based health education and counseling with a focus on Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights, Safe Motherhood and Gender Equity & Social Inclusion (GESI).
