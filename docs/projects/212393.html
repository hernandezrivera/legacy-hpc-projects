Project Summary

Needs:
The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024 Summary states that 15.4M people are in need of Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) assistance. Furthermore, 43 subdistricts are categorized as severity score 4 or 5 for ERL needs. Massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment & poverty, coupled with high food prices, sanctions & inflation have all contributed to increased humanitarian needs across Syria. Per the sectors’ advice, ZOA is taking into consideration the most vulnerable groups (IDPs& families with PWDs) & is working in areas that have interventions under other sectors for sustainable results.
Intervention, implementation of activities:
ZOA will work closely with relevant local authorities, the sector and local communities to ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable. ZOA is planning to implement the following activities, building on its previous experience: 1) rehabilitation of schools, 2) rehabilitation of irrigation systems, including repair of cladded and hanged canals and cleaning of drainage canals, 3) removal of debris, 4) rehabilitation and solarization of water infrastructure, including pumping stations and water filtration stations, and water pipelines, 5) rehabilitation of sanitations systems, such as sewage lines, 6) provision of short-and long term work opportunities, including through cash for work and community mobilization activities, 7) provision of technical, soft skills and vocational trainings. Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) is the responsibility of the country-based Manager of Programme Quality. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logframe, indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and to demonstrate impact. Detailed projects including infrastructure rehabilitation activities have also been submitted to the relevant sectors for FSA, WASH and Shelter/NFI and Education.
Targets and budgets included in this intervention are covering only 2024. Additionally, for 2025, ZOA aims to reach another 100,000 persons (including persons benefitting from infrastructure rehabilitation works), and have an estimated budget of 2.5M USD.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures:
ZOA identified the following project related risks and mitigation measures:
1. Changes in exchange rate and increasing inflation: ZOA closely monitors market prices, and contractors are committed to their offers for a specific period to avoid big changes in pricing due to exchange rate changes or inflation.
2. Lack of fuel availability: ZOA has contracts with several transportation service providers, allowing us to conduct the necessary trips without major constraints.
3. Lack of skilled labor: ZOA already has contracts with experienced local skilled laborers.
4. Delayed approvals: ZOA has an MOU with MOWR to facilitate faster approvals.
5. Security risks affecting implementation in the field: ZOA continually assesses the situation in the field and coordinates wit INSO, UNDSS, local authorities and community leaders.
6. Fraud and corruption: ZOA has clear policies preventing and mitigating risks of fraud and corruption including segregation of duties, and regularly trains its staff on these topics. All financial transactions are documented, filed and monitored and regular spot-checks and audits are conducted. Communities and stakeholders are well informed about their entitlements using multiple channels and good reporting channels are in place.
7. Protection risks: Please refer to protection risk analysis and PSEA strategy under Project summary extension in the response plan section.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis:The project will adopt an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach so that all groups can safely and equitably access assistance. Target populations are at the center of decision-making processes throughout the project, for example consulting on the eligibility for assistance, which are based on context specific needs, preferences and vulnerabilities. This project considers the safety and dignity of beneficiaries to avoid creating or exacerbating protection risks for individuals, households or communities. (i.e. safety of beneficiaries, social tensions, intra-household dynamics). Project related protection risks include potential abuse, exploitation or other unacceptable behaviors by contractors, suppliers or staff within the community during the implementation of the works or during distributions. Therefore, all contractors, suppliers and ZOA staff/ volunteers will sign ZOA’s Code of Conduct, including a child protection policy. Also, contractors are required to provide a safe and secure work environment for their workers. Accessibility for persons with disabilities is taken into consideration during the design of any facilities/ buildings. Multi-channel feedback mechanisms are in place and ZOA staff knows how to deal with different types of feedback, including referrals for psychological and protection services and support. All staff including volunteers are trained on protection, PSEAH, ZOA’s CoC and child protection. PSEA strategy:All ZOA staff, volunteers and contractors sign a Code of Conduct, including a child protection policy, informing them of their obligations as well as consequences of involvement in sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff is trained to make safe referrals to relevant services for people identified to be in need of specialized protection services in coordination with the Protection Sector. The project will put in place several accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries, staff and partners, that include a variety of confidential feedback channels, eg. hotlines, social media, complaints box. Beneficiaries and other stakeholders will be informed about these feedback and reporting channels from the start and throughout the intervention and are also encouraged to share their preferred way of providing feedback or reporting issues and complaints. All the feedback and complaints from beneficiaries will be systematically collected, assessed and responded to in a timely and confidential manner.
