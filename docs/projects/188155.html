Project Summary

Throughout 2022 and 2023, VO is planning to implement comprehensive food security interventions applying a variety of modalities (In-kind, Cash, and vouchers) to address the needs of the different groups with proper modality.
The proposed project will effectively contribute to improving food security status to the assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance through improving food consumption, dietary diversity, and avoiding negative coping strategies. the planned activities to be implemented during 2022 and 2023 are the following activities:
1. Emergency Food assistance distribution for HHs of crisis-affected IDPs through proper modality (Cash - In Kind)
2. Cooked meals provision for COVID-19 positive confirmed cases admitted in 2 CCTCs in NWS
3. Regular Food assistance per modality (in-kind, Cash, Voucher) for HHs
4. Provision of free and subsidized bread for IDPs individuals and host communities.
More over; in terms of the implementation activities, Violet is planning for cash and vouchers modalities whenever possible for regular food assistance to ensure markets revival in the targeted location with taking into consideration, market functionality/proximity/capacity, also functionality of financial service providers in the targeted location. the value of the voucher will be according to FSL and CWG recommendation for the food assistance which is currently 60 USD per HH based on their last update on this regard, and in the meantime, VO will consider in-kind modality for regular food assistance where there are low-functionality of the markets in the targeted areas to increase food availability in the targeted locations, the weight of the in-kind food basket (when applied) will be based on the WFP recommended food basket composition and FSL cluster response packages .
Regarding beneficiaries targeting, VO will still consider blanket distribution to the camps residents based on the current cluster recommendations, and applying faire eligibility and vulnerability criteria when it comes to food distribution off-camps.
Based on the situation/context analysis, VO will ensure considering the best suitable modality all of the food assistance , then if voucher, then Violet will ensure proper and enough vouchers distribution and redemption period, and if in-kind, then the distribution will be through setting distribution points in the targeted location based on recent updated VO distribution SOPs during COVID-19 context.
Out of the total project total amount (4,185,000 USD), the below amounts are already secured:
- 120,600 USD is secured for emergency food assistance during 2022 covering RTEs and cooked meals
- 590,940 USD is secured for the regular food assistance.
- 86,250 USD is secured for the Provision of free and subsidized bread for IDPs
In total, 677,910 USD is already secured. Moreover, 1200,000 USD is in the pipeline.

4 - Needs

According to the drafted 2022 Syria HNO , Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties, the protracted crisis, now heading into its 11th year, is exacerbating food security and livelihood needs for many families across the country, further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The overall estimated sector PIN for 2022 - of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 14.2 million to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity. Furthermore, 1.8 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, with an increase of 470,000 people facing acute food insecurity in 2022 compared to 2021. This alarming and worsening food insecurity situation is concerning, especially in the backdrop of significantly low wheat production in 2021. FAO preliminary projections indicate that wheat yield dropped to 50 per cent of last year’s output and is only a quarter of pre-crises levels .From the general analysis on (FCS), (rCSI) and HDD, it is clear that food security in Syria has consistently worsened over the past two years. The FCS levels have seen the number of people consuming an inadequate diet increase from 21% in 2019 to reaching around 44% by the summer of 2021.More over, according to the same report, food needs remains the first priority for 71% of the population followed by livelihoods assistance for 51% of population.

5 - Implementation of activities

VO aims to respond to emergency needs by the implementation of different activities with an appropriate modality based on the needs, BNFs preference availability, and functionality of the markers, however; Prior to the implementation, VO will engage the BNFs, and project stakeholders in the project design and implementation to ensure the local ownership of the project. Thus, VO will sign MOUs with the LCs demonstrating their roles and responsibilities. Moreover, VO will ensure close coordination with the FSL cluster and humanitarian actors in the field to avoid potential overlappingDuring the implementation, VO will ensure the BNFs protection and do No Harm principle by consulting the BNFs on the distribution location, Timing, and arranging the priority ques for elderlies, women, and PWD, but also through training the staff on the Organization code of conduct and PSEA policy. To avoid the tension that may occur between the communities and different groups, VO will include a % of the most vulnerable HC members in the project, but also will announce the project criteria and make sure it's clear to the BNFs and activate the CRM mechanismFor the CVA modality, VO will ensure the market is functioning prior to the distribution in order to avoid the harm that may occur to the non-BNFs

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excludedLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighBefore implementation, VO conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from assistance or distribution.Likelihood: Low; Impact: HighInformation sharing and distribution points are suitable for PWDs, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance (transportation/delivery) for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to and from or in overcrowded distributionsLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighSelection of suitable distribution (time, location, accessibility), referral of special cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, operate with multiple distribution sites.4) At-risk group do not receive needed servicesLikelihood: Medium; Impact: MediumCoordination with relevant sectors to avoid duplications, collaborate with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematically use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection, consider intersectionality in targeting


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

VO targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements.Potential risks:1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision.2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staffMitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded distribution sites and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation or delivery, participation of protection team in the distribution, if neededViolet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitoring


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: activities implementation is stopped by violence or interference by the opposition groupsMitigation 1: monitoring the security situation and reporting criminal violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation.Risks 2: fees might be collected by the LCs or camp management in return for assistance leading to Aid diversionMitigation 2: ensuring the humanitarian nature of the project by communicating that red flags with the BNFs and community leaders. In case of aid diversion, the activity will be suspended and the incident will be reported to OCHA. stakeholders will be involved to facilitate the activities and getting their supportRisk 3: markets are not functioning properlyMitigation 3: VO confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allows the implementation. In case the situation would change, VO would consider relocating the areas of intervention where the required conditions are metRisk 4: Diversion of cash when using (MTA)Mitigation 4: VO disposes of 3 FWA with MTAs for all CBR activities and its currently in the process of contracting with new MTAs. the MTAs will be vetted by an ATC check (in both UK and USA ATC platforms). Risk 5: A tension might occur between the HC and IDPs if targeted BNFs of cash assistance (in USD) are only IDPs, Particularly with SYP devaluation and Covid-19 outbreak, when HC HHs/non-BNFs are economically vulnerable and they lose their purchasing power.
