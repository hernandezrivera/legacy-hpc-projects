Project Summary

Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRC) will improve access to emergency food assistance to the most vulnerable displaced and non-displaced, rural and urban populations, while strengthening access to emergency livelihood inputs support to non-displaced rural populations in Qardo, Baidoa and Balad districts. The target population for livelihood support are mainly pastoral and agropastoral communities.
The food security interventions will have conditional, and unconditional cash and vouchers transfers as modalities of interventions with each modality guided by market dynamics- availability of goods in the food baskets within the local markets, ability to source and prices, and availability of service providers for respective modality.
Unconditional transfers will target vulnerable beneficiaries HHs with MAM pregnant and lactating women, SAM and MAM under-five children, elderly and sickly.
Conditional transfers will target labor-rich but vulnerable households. The conditional transfer will also target the rehabilitation of community productive assets. The beneficiaries will rehabilitate jointly identified productive community assets- feeder roads, market infrastructures, livelihood infrastructures including secondary irrigation canals, dykes, river embankments. Social Mobilization and community sensitization will precede any screening and registration.
The intervention will target 24,580 (displaced- 14,748, non-displaced-9,832) vulnerable beneficiaries for the provision of conditional and unconditional transfers, and livelihood support. At least 15% of the population beneficiaries will be PWD (3687).
On access to food assistance, QRC will undertake the provision of conditional transfer support through cash or voucher to 799 vulnerable households ( 2402 female, 2397 males) in Qardho, Baidoa, and Balcad districts. Unconditional transfer targets 1863 vulnerable households (6,706 females,4472 males). Primary targeted households are those with screened SAM and MAM cases and those with under five children. The transfer modality will be either cash or voucher, based on market assessment. Of the target population, the unconditional transfer will target 1,122 pregnant and lactating mothers and under five households.
Protecting livelihoods targets 1434 HH with emergency agriculture and livestock support (788 agricultural and 646 livestock support) for 6 months. (Female =5162 Male=3441). It will include the provision of agricultural input support; seeds/seedlings, fertilizer to agro-pastroralist in Baidoa and Balcad. Livestock support will entail; range cubes, emergency vaccinations, multi-nutrient blocks, destocking, and restocking, and emergency water trucking for northern inland pastoral.
Post distribution monitoring will be undertaken to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention as well as its effect on the market. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured to reduce unintended adverse effect by and on the program.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project justification for adopting cash and vouchers: The ability of the markets to supply goods and services at an appropriate price and quality. The project locations under consideration have an adequate number of vendors who can adequately handle cash transfers or voucher redemption. Access of beneficiaries to service and good providers: The beneficiaries will be required to travel short distances to redeem the transfers. Predicted effectiveness: The different transfer modalities have been considered to meet the appeal desired outcomes in the areas under consideration. Additionally, they offer timeliness in their delivery. Different kinds of transfers will result in different results since people will rarely buy exactly the same items that are chosen for them by aid agencies (e.g. they may prefer to buy cheaper or different kinds of food). Beneficiaries' actual choices are hard to predict since different households will make different decisions, based on their circumstances and priorities. The method chosen will be based on an assessment of how sensible any assumptions (e.g. about likely expenditures or consumption outcomes) and how well-reasoned the link is between the choice of transfer and desired outcomes at the decision-making stage. Timeliness is an important aspect of effectiveness, particularly in emergencies. The time required to set up and deliver an intervention is considered in decision-making when urgency is a priority. Impact: Potential impact beyond immediate project objective: Potential desired outcomes could include supporting the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs (i.e. not those covered by the objective), providing recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets, and promoting financial inclusion. Efficiency and cost: Cash and voucher transfers are considerably lower and require less effort to deliver in comparison to in-kind. Protection and gender: The transfer and delivery mechanisms aids are accessible to all genders and those who face constraints related to gender, age, disability, and other factors influencing access. The effect of transfers on the safety, dignity, and integrity of recipients is a consideration.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project considers that the feasibility of cash and vouchers rests on the expected future ability to appropriate price and quality and markets to respond to increased demand if cash or vouchers were provided as the modality. Thus, access of beneficiaries to markets and not simply on the state of the market before any intervention. As part of planning, consideration of possible measures that could be taken to address any market constraints (e.g. addressing constraints facing traders, such as capital, credit, storage, and transport) are factored. Notably, seasonal, local, regional, and global trends can all influence prices. Possible price rises caused by external factors do not rule out the use of cash and vouchers if they can be managed through the program design. If in-kind assistance/voucher is purchased locally or regionally, the ability of traders to ensure timely supply at an appropriate price and quality may be critical. The potential impacts on markets of different types of transfers should also be considered. Capacity: Some market players have more experience managing different types of transfers. There is a responsibility to provide appropriate assistance, and so choices should not be justified based only on pre-existing skills and experience: there is an obligation to ensure that necessary market skills and experience are recruited. However, it is unreasonable to expect the capacity to deliver ‘new’ transfer types to be built up immediately. Thus, evaluation should consider whether consideration is given to the vendor's ability to build its capacity to the scale of the project being undertaken – a massive, urgent emergency response may not be the time to depend on newly acquired skills. Delivery Mechanisms: The project's reliable delivery mechanism will be determined by the vendor supply chain., financial sector, and Communications Technology (ICT) capacity or in-kind systems. Qatar Red Crescent will consider the different delivery mechanisms that could be used with different transfers. The choice of transfer will consider the reliability of delivery mechanisms that exist or can be established.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. -Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence. -Install lights outside and on the access route to the distribution centers. -Use crowd control mechanisms including ensuring different lines for genders. -Staggering registration/distribution times/days to reduce overcrowding that could trigger violence. The risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. -Ensure FSL staff and relief committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability). -All staff and members receive protection mainstreaming training. -Sensitize staff in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented. -Minority groups' representatives, including PwD will be sought, engaged, and consulted in planning projects and implementation. -Ensuring registration/distribution is accessible to Persons with Disabilities. -Collect accurate information about marginalized groups' specific needs and preferences for registration/distribution center location, design, and services. The risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms -Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that does not require the beneficiary to expose themselves to project staff. -Respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. -Organize awareness-raising sessions so that people know how it the mechanism works. -Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion. Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. -Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). - In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of workers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. -Siting of project centers in areas that do not pose risks -Ensuring activities are completed on time to reduce exposure during beneficiaries accessing and returning.
