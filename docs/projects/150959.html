Project Summary

The proposed project will be implemented in 4 districts in Bakool (xudur,wajid,rabdhuure and elbarde), 3 in banadir (Kahda,hodan,abdiaziz) ,1 in Lower shebelle region (Afgooye) , 2 districts in Nugaal region(Garowe and eyl) and 1 in to cover health needs of internally displaced people and host community women, men, boys and girls.
The project will use an integrated approach to offer free curative and preventive primary health care services through facility and community based approaches with the aim of contributing to reduction of maternal and child morbidity and mortality. This will include,screening and registration at the point of entry, medical consultation for under fives, antenatal and postnatal care for PLW, Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases for both U5 and PLW ,inpatient management of complicated severe acute malnutrition.The approach used will be through established strategically located mother and child health clinics and mobile teams.Integrated community case management (ICCM) approach will be used to treat u5 boys and girls at household level.
A total of 115,092 persons including 27,622 women of child bearing age, 39,476 U5 boys and 41,088 girls, 120 key community leaders (72 men and 48 women) and 60 staff (36 men and 24 women) will be targeted in 11 districts for a period of 12 months.

Needs

There are 4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in Somalia due to adverse effects of drought and insecurity that still persist in Somalia. FSNAU 2018 post Gu assessment shows that crude death rates of (1.06%) and under five death rates of (2.56%)among Mogadishu IDPs are above the emergency threshold (i.e. CDR > 1/10 000/day and U5DR > 2/10 000/day) . A situation that deserves urgent attention. In addition to that, morbidity data from the same survey shows that 16 out of 33 surveyed areas had a rate of >20 % With the IDPs being the most affected. WHO Epidemiological country report shows that disease burden is still high in Somalia. 7,119 cases of suspected measles have been reported across Somalia since the beginning of 2018. The high number of cases reported requires sustained efforts through routine immunizations and campaigns to avert major outbreaks. In addition, 6464 AWD/Cholera cases were report from December 2017 to October 2018.This includes 43 deaths with a case fatality rate(CFR) of 0.7%. The Gu 2018 rains were above average in most parts of Somalia resulting in flooding across the Shabelle river .In bakool, Elbarde district where Action against Hunger is implementing health services, 451 AWD cases were reported and managed between April and June 2018 as a result of GU rains . FSNAU forecasts that there will be average to above average Deyr rains between October to December 2018.This will likely lead to flooding and displacement as well as increased risk of communicable diseases such as AWD/cholera and acute respiratory illnesses.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention is aligned to the Health,WASH ,Food security and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to poor waste disposal, deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity and water scarcity . By increasing access to health services through use of mobile teams and community focused approaches,the project reduces abnormal migration of human to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages by exerting pressure on exisiting services . In the absence of latrines, in the new areas of displacement, open defecation would increase posing serious risks to the environmental wellbeing. Portions of land are usually left open to be used by the displaced population for human waste disposal. This poses serious health risks due to the spread of faecal matter, especially during the rainy season when there is seepage into water wells used for drinking water. This has been partly responsible for the periodic outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) in IDPs and villages in Somalia which increases the risk of high morbidity and mortality rates among vulnerable groups.This proposed project is foreseen to impose minimal risks to the surrounding environment.The project will use an integrated approach with WASH to Provide latrines with hand washing facilities that will ease the level of public health risk posed by providing safe places and consequently check the spread of human waste. The location of latrines will also be far from the water wells to check the risks of contamination. Public health will be ensured through proper disposal and management of medical equipment, supplies and waste, including the use of incinerators. A component of the hygiene training will also include lessons on environmental awareness with the objective of providing skills to the beneficiaries on better waste management, which doesn’t endanger their immediate environment. The project will jointly with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of community groups ( Community Health Committees, camp management committees, Mother support groups) to ensure resilient community systems are in place. The immediate/emergency components of the project will be linked to ACF’s resilience program in the country and handovers to the capacity built community resource committees will ensure that the resources established are maintained and contribute to building resilient communities.The services and commodities planned for transfer to beneficiaries under this project adhere to government policies and have been designed jointly with community user groups. The transfer will be witnessed by local authorities to ensure it fits within the existing legal framework at district level.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main target population for the project are IDPs and the surrounding host communities. Displaced populations, youth, women and the elderly, most notable those in camps lack health services. In addition, poor access and long distance to these services expose them, especially women and young girls to sex and gender based violence. Moreover, access to basic services and opportunities is further compounded by the pervasive marginalization and exclusion of IDPs and certain sections of the host communities mostly attributed to clan dynamics. IDP and host community families from weaker clans or minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, trafficking and discrimination. To mitigate against the risks identified, ACF will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis to identify the specific protection challenges of the target population at project implementation stage. Targeting of project locations and beneficiaries will be guided by need with specific attention to households that may be marginalized due to family and clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. ACF staff are already trained and sensitized on PSEA and have signed a code of conduct that commits them to strictly adhere to humanitarian principles when providing services. This is expected to curtail any cases of humanitarian staff exploiting the beneficiaries. Health programs will employ approaches that reduce distance to treatment/consultation sites. This includes use of mobile teams that visits villages where the most vulnerable are located. This will minimize exposure to potential distance related risks. Proper consultations will be done at the inception of the project with all the key stakeholders in the camp to ensure that those who benefit from these interventions are not put at a risk.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The Health projects mainly target children under 5 years of age and women actively in the reproductive stage regardless of their age. Communities in hard to reach areas due to insecurity and poor infrastructure suffer from lack access to Health services. Young, mostly first time mothers are likely to lack pre and post-natal services which puts them at risk of birth related complications.. Immunization services are also likely to be difficult to access for these communities, which predisposes the PLW and under 5 years children to preventable illnesses. The roll out of mobile outreach services will ensure that remote but accessible villages are reached with essential health services. This will include engagement of community health workers who represent the various clan profiles, who can increase reach to all groups in the target communities. Moreover, the project will engage with all major groups to ensure that they are aware of the availability of services and their entitlements. The Project will also engage Traditional Birth attendants and improve their capacity so that they can provide services closer to the population. The project will also pro-actively engage male caregivers so that they don’t miss on services. Data collection and analysis will be designed in a way that allows ACF to better understand age and gender needs to ensure inclusivity.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

ACF works in areas where there is active conflict between state and non-state armed actors or between clans. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way to service delivery points and attacks on humanitarian staff ACF applies conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at the household and community level. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. ACF local teams are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Additionally, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential with consent being sort if any information such as pictures are to be shared. ACF will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm’s way, for example by ensuring that staff are back from the field stations early enough. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics.
