Project Summary

Water Supply:
DRC will construct/rehabilitate and optimization of water supply infrastructures in selected LGAs and will coordinate with stakeholders, including the WASH Cluster and local authorities, to select the wells to be rehabilitated. DRC will then undertake a technical assessment to identify the scope of work needed.
Sanitation:
DRC will construct/ rehabilitations of sanitation facilities and hand-washing facilities in the selected LGAs including schools and health clinics, along with the provision of environmental cleaning kits to improve access to dignified sanitation facilities and prevent the spread of diseases and further deterioration. DRC will provide shared community latrines in IDP sites based on need. In each of the targeted locations, DRC will conduct a mapping of the latrine coverage to determine the required scope of the response.
Solid Waste Management/Faecal Sludge Management (FSM):
Solid waste clean-up campaigns will be conducted to promote a clean and healthy living environment and address the risks associated with accumulated solid waste. DRC will promote eco-friendly solutions for disposal of sludge treatment and disposal.
Hygiene Promotion
DRC will conduct hygiene promotion activities in selected LGAs through the mobilization of community-based-based Hygiene promoters (CBHP), who will be trained and equipped to conduct hygiene promotion sessions in the communities. The CBHP will be from the targeted communities to facilitate community acceptance and positive outcomes. This activity will complement the WASH infrastructure component by promoting awareness of personal, household, and environmental hygiene and positive change in community behaviors and practices
WASH NFIs
DRC will support the conflict-affected population through the distribution of various WASH cluster standard kits – hygiene kits, replenishment kits, and MHM kits with special consideration for women and girls
DRC will target conflict-affected individuals including members of host communities, IDPs, refugees, and returnees. Vulnerable households will be selected through several criteria such as families with unprotected water sources, female-headed, child-headed, or elderly households, households with physically challenged persons, and households with malnutrition children. Total WASH beneficiaries will be about 50,000 individuals (7,000 households). DRC will consider age, gender, and diversity (AGD) in WASH interventions with special consideration to enable women and children to benefit.

1 - Needs

The conflict-displaced population faces multi-faceted basic needs based on the abrupt and unexpected leaving of their homes to other areas including food, shelter, and WASH services in the new IDP sites. The limited access to safe sources of water and sanitation facilities, poor hygiene practices, and inefficient solid waste management increase the most vulnerable people’s exposure to diseases and malnutrition, especially children: poor access to sanitation and poor hygiene practices were recorded as the main risks of being exposed to disease and malnutrition. DRC’s MSNA 2023 reported that 59.9 % of households had access to safe water, with 22.1% using a combination of safe and unsafe sources, and 12.3% relying solely on unsafe sources. Hygiene practices varied, with 59.9% demonstrating good knowledge of critical handwashing times. However, 45.3% used unimproved sanitary facilities, and over 75% shared sanitary facilities making it difficult to maintain good hygiene standards.Inadequate latrines and few existing water points are the main reasons advanced by the households to find alternatives outside of the camps and the communities, which increase their exposure to protection risks – especially GBV. Ongoing disease outbreaks seriously affect the PiN, with the World Health Organization reporting 461 cholera-related deaths in 2023, primarily concentrated in Borno (85%), out of the BAY states. As of October 30, 2023, 653 suspected cases of diphtheria were reported (39% being children aged 0-15) across 15 out of the 21 LGAs assessed in Borno, resulting in 65 deaths. The outbreaks are attributed to a lack of access to clean water, improved sanitation facilities, and hygiene practices.

2 - Activities

WASH activities will focus on both immediate and sustainable intervention in the IDP sites and host communities that includes - Improving access to safe water through rehabilitations and/or extension of existing water infrastructures, construction of new water infrastructures equipped with appropriate pumping and power systems, storage water tanks and distribution networks, the establishment of gender-balanced water management committees for ensuring continuity of services, appropriate operation and maintenance and as well strengthen women leadership and participation in WASH projects, installation of new sanitation facilities (culture sensitive and addressing specific needs of different sex and disabled groups) in IDP sites hosting newly displaced population and hygiene promotion campaigns focusing on hand washing with soap and hygiene kits distribution, including MHM kits. Solid waste management(SWM) and fecal sludge management(FSM) will also be part of WASH priorities including protection mainstreaming initiatives to ensure ‘do no harm’ principles are integrated.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection considerations: 1 - Prioritize safety & dignity: DRC ensures to avoid causing harm, prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of interventions that can increase people's vulnerability to GBV and other forms of physical and psychosocial risks. 2 - Meaningful Access: DRC ensures the targeted population has access to assistance and services based on their vulnerability and needs without any barriers such as discrimination or partiality. DRC pays attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. These individuals or groups are captured in the DRC vulnerability criteria which aligns with the humanitarian response plan and humanitarian needs overview capturing the vulnerable groups within the context. 3 – Accountability: DRC conducts feedback sessions with beneficiaries to receive concerns, complaints, and relevant information. DRC ensures that the community response mechanisms are strengthened, and beneficiaries are aware through which affected populations can give feedback on the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4 – Participation and empowerment: DRC supports the development of self-protection, and capacities and assists people in claiming their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education through advocacy, sensitizations, community training and coordination with other partners.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Risk and Compliance (RC) department, an independent unit at DRC, manages the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) with an interface with the protection department for complaints related to DRC programs. During field activities, the RC team creates awareness and sensitization about CRM, beneficiaries are informed how to access, what type of complaints to be raised, and the various platforms to use which include toll-free phone numbers, complaint desk, direct contact with trained DRC staff and regular feedback-collection meetings. The objective of the complaint and response mechanism is to ensure that complaints from beneficiaries are heard, recorded, and resolved effectively and professionally within a determined period, and any lessons learned are incorporated to improve programming, ensuring effective and efficient delivery of assistance.
