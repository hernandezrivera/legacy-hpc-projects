Project Summary

MaCDA will integrate GBV and Nutrition in its proposed one year, WASH intervention in Lafon and Kapoeta South of Eastern Equatoria. This project will target the specific needs of vulnerable IDPs, Host Communities and returnees. The project targets a total of 15,600 beneficiaries (Kapaoeta South 8,000 beneficiaries and Lafon 7,600 beneficiaries). 60% of the total beneficiaries will comprise of women and girls .The project will also target 1300 malnourished children (under 5 years) through integrated WASH minimum packages in Nutrition.
In Kapoeta South MaCDA will integrate Nutrition and GBV in WASH activities, while in Lafon MaCDA will focus on integrating nutrition in WASH. The WASH and Nutrition integrated activities will target malnourished children including their care givers/mothers from the nutrition centres (OTP/SC) till the household level. Rehabilitation of water point and provision of WASH NFI at the nutrition centres will ensure availability of sufficient safe water for SAM/MAM admitted children.
To mitigate WASH specific related gender based violence (GBV) the project will involve female staffs in conducting protection assessments to determine the protection needs of women and girls. Establishment, design and rehabilitation of WASH facilities will be informed by findings from consultation of women & girls. Culturally gender appropriate toilets will be established/rehabilitated and fitted with appropriate lockable doors to enhance protection. To manage menstrual hygiene the project will facilitate the acquisition and distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls.
This project will leverage on MaCDA’s nutrition and protection(GBV ) experience in these locations which has been attributed to organization presence in the areas since 2016, and ongoing nutrition interventions in kapoeta East (Kapoeta town payam, Longeleya payam and Nachilagur payam) funded by WFP and UNICEF(PCA) and GBV prevention and response activities funded by UNFPA in the same locations.
The total budget for the integrated project is 312,000 USD, with WASH activities covering 60% (187,200USD),GBV 20% (62,400 USD) and Nutrition 20% (62,400 USD).
This project aims at achieving the following objectives
1. Integrating WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package.
2. Provide timely /sustainable, equitable access to safer water, Sanitation and hygiene for IDPs host communities and returnees.
3. Mitigate WASH-related gender based violence.

1 - Needs

Localized inter-communal violence and cattle raiding in the pastoral areas of Greater Kapoeta, continue to have a negative impact on humanitarian needs and access to services. Access to safe water and sanitation facilities coupled by poor WASH behavioral tendencies (Poor hygiene practices and chronic open defecation) amongst the populations, has been identified as the major cause of WASH related illnesses, AWDs including cholera (cholera hot spots classification in South Sudan 2014-2017). In Lafon and Kapoeta East boreholes are the main source of safe water for drinking and other domestic use. Due to long queuing times, communities sometimes prefer to collect water from unsafe sources like swamps and open well. Boreholes usually break more frequently due to over-use. Food security and malnutrition have also increased rapidly in these counties. In Kapoeta East, the majority of the pastoralist communities have lost livestock to inter-communal raiding and disease outbreaks increasing their vulnerability to food insecurity. Malnutrition in Lafon and Kapoeta South is rated to be in IPC AMN Phase 4(IPC report August 2019). A recent assessment by save the children has also indicated that Lafon has severe acute malnutrition rate of 2.9% well above the humanitarian threshold. High malnutrition rates are strongly linked to poor WASH conditions demonstrated by diarrhea-malnutrition cycle which is prevalent in vulnerable populations. Displaced women and girls continue to be disproportionally affected by protection related WASH issues, with gender based violence occurring due to insufficient and unsafe access to water and sanitation facilities. MaCDA has experience implementing nutrition project in kapoeta South since 2016.Currently MaCDA has active PCA and FLA with UNICEF and WFP respectively for MIYCN, OTP and TSFP activities targeting the malnourished population (Children < 5 and PLW). These activities were also link with available WASH programs to ensure that all OTP/TSFP sites in kapoeta south have access to clean drinking water for appetite test, hand washing facilities, and latrines. MaCDA also has an active GBV project supported by UNFPA in Kapoeta south which focuses on GBV prevention and mitigation activities, including menstrual hygiene management (MHM) for vulnerable women and girls. The GBV project has been instrumental in supporting WASH actors through conducting GBV assessments and analysis of the WASH infrastructure and facilities. These activities have provided sufficient knowledge and experience in integrating GBV and Nutrition in WASH, including the understanding of project context positioning MaCDA to provide integrated WASH, Nutrition and GBV services to vulnerable population in Lafon and Kapoeta South.

2 - Activities

MaCDA will target the most vulnerable communities in Kapoeta South (Nakwasingira, Napetait and Kapoeta town payams) with integrated WASH, GBV and nutrition activities, while in Lafon(Lafon centre payam) focus on WASH-Nut. WASH and Nutrition integrated activities will target malnourished children including their care givers/mothers from the nutrition centres (OTP/SC) till the household level. Rehabilitation of water point and provision of WASH NFI at the nutrition centres will ensure availability of sufficient safe water for SAM/MAM admitted children.To mitigate WASH specific related gender based violence (GBV) the project will involve female staffs in conducting protection assessments to determine the protection needs of women and girls. Establishment, design and rehabilitation of WASH facilities will be informed by findings from consultation of women & girls. Culturally gender appropriate toilets will be established/rehabilitated and fitted with appropriate lockable doors to enhance protection. To manage menstrual hygiene the project will facilitate the acquisition and distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls. Activities Summary: •Rehabilitation of non-functional hand pumps/water points•Rehabilitation of water points and distribution of WASH NFIs at nutrition centres (OTP/SC) targeting SAM/MAM admitted children.•Hygiene promoters training on proper hygiene practices to prevent AWD and other water borne diseases.•Hygiene promotion campaigns, at house hold level, in public places/gatherings, food stalls and markets coupled with distribution of WASH NFIs•Focus Group Discussions with women and girls on safety, design and appropriate location of WASH facilities•Establishment and rehabilitation of gender appropriate latrines with hand washing facilities •Training of Nutrition workers/volunteers on the WASH minimum package of activities•Distribution of Hygiene kit to SAM (especially children under 5 years)•Distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The beneficiaries right to life have been considered in this project through provision of necessary life saving WASH-Nut services to prevent mortality especially for children under 5 years and PLW. The right to life will also be recognized through provision of sufficient safe water for drinking and domestic use hence reducing the rate of deaths related contaminated water.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

yes, In kapoeta South and Lafon, IDPs are living within the host community and sharing the same resources including WASH facilities, hence the project interventions will cut across all the social groups (IDPs, Returnees and host community). Special considerations will be given to people with disabilities and the elderly to ensure equal access to project benefits.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project has a link with the local government and local community. Assessments and consultations have been carried out to inform community needs and community led solutions. WASH-Nut facilities will be established/rehabilitated on the available government owned nutrition facilities. Water Management Committees (WMC) will be established to ensure management of water points beyond the project duration.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is in line with the Kampala convention (African Union convention for the protection and assistance of internally displaced person in Africa). This project addresses the convention call to prevent arbitrary displacement, to ensure IDPs are protected and assisted, and to support durable solutions. IDPs will be protected and assisted through addressing WASH related protection needs (with focus on GBV), and provision of WASH-Nut and GBV services. Provision of these services and facilities will prevent arbitrary displacement in search of services.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The targeted locations are stable/safe.This can be attributed by reduction in armed conflict as a result of R-ARCSS signed on august 2018.The population movement to Lafon and Kapoeta East is due to availability of relative services these locations being town.The protection environment(with focus on GBV) will be improved with the GBV component of this project which despite focusing on WASH related GBV,it will also conduct GBV advocacy targeting the general population.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the established WASH facilities will be handed over to the community through the state ministry of Water and infrastructure. Community and government based pump mechanics will also be provided with maintenance tools. Trained water management committees (WMCs) will be empowered to have modalities of acquiring spare parts to help in maintenance beyond the project cycle. Efforts on alternative methods of water purification such as boiling will be encouraged during hygiene promotion to ensure access to safe water even in absence of purifying tablets eg Aqua tabs
