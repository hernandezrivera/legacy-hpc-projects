Project Summary

The crisis in South Sudan has deepened affecting more than 4.3million people. AFOD is local partner implementing food security, livelihood and nutrition intervention in Raga, Jur river in Western Barh el Ghazel and Buid, Magwi in Eastern Equotoria. The food security in these counties due to the current displacement or migration of people and their livestock will deteriorate from alert phase to serious. Livestock movement on the otherhand poses challenges to the health of livestock in host communities as well as for the migrating livestock. And this remains a key challenge for the livestock farmers affecting production of shoats and cattle. For instance, spread of various prevalent diseases, including foot-and -mouth disease (FMD), East Coast fever, peste des petits ruminants (PPR), contagious bovine pleuropneumonia (CBPP) and trypanosomosis. Significant food security deterioration is observed in Western Bahr el Ghazel as a result of the current economic crisis and conflict that has disrupted and displaced thousands in Raga, Wau and Jur River. The disruptions of the western trade corridor through Western Equatoria State and the persistent violence in Wau County have further resulted in reduced trade flows into the region in addition to displacing thousands of rural residents. While in Greater Equatoria region, the food security situation has significantly deteriorated in parts of Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria due to inadequate food and seed stocks from last harvest, insecurity and population displacements, and the prevailing economic downturn, resulting in an unprecedented 24 percent of its population experiencing Crisis and Emergency food security situations. The nutrition situation according to the IPC analysis is likely to deteriorate in several counties in Greater Bahr el Ghazal and parts of Eastern Equatoria. Significant deterioration is expected in Western Bahr el Ghazal’s Wau County where people that have been displaced (about 38,000individulas) due to recent on-going conflict are displaced with no access to food. The updated IPC report of April 2016 indicates an overall deterioration in food and nutrition security situation in South Sudan with estimated 4.3 million people in Phases 3, 4 & 5 which is a significant increase from the 2.8 million people projected between January–March 2016 by the IPC. AFOD proposes in this project in addition to meeting the food gaps, to increase agricultural production, strengthen community mobilization & sensitization of community on early warnings, disaster risk reduction strategies for the local hazards and promote resiliency to food security and nutrition. It plan to reached 34,000 beneficiaries of whom 10,000 are IDP individuals. The project beneficiaries will comprise of 60% women, 30-40% men. 21% will be children < 5yrs. Main intervention activities shall focus on life saving and local capacity for increase agricultural. production including animal health.

1 - Needs

The political conflict that has lasted for over 7 years since it started in December 2013 has significantly affected the security and the livelihood of people in Western Bahr el Ghazal particularly those in rural areas of Jur river, Raga and Wau counties.According to August IPC report Greater Western Bahr el Ghazal region, an estimated 2.37 million people experienced Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity in August 2019 – approximately 60% of the population in the region. An estimated 10,000 people were likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) The proportion of people experiencing acute food insecurity reported in August 2019 is approximately the same as compared to the 2018 lean season. While increased access, both physical and among humanitarians, has supported a decline in the number of people experiencing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Western Bahr el Ghazal compared to the same time last year, continued incidents of inter communal conflict, market disruptions and prolonged depletion of household assets, inaccessibility due to poor road network in some places continued to limit household food availability and coping capacity. In Western Bahr el Ghazal State, an estimated 440,000 people (68% of the State population) are expected to be in Crisis or worse, up from the 280,000 people (54% of the State population) in August 2018. In Wau county, the continued delivery of humanitarian food assistance due to improved security and access has led to the county being classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), with fewer populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) than the same time last year. The peace conference between Tong and Wau authorities including the signed South Sudan revitalized peace agreement is holding water at the grass root in Jur River County. Especially, respect to the agreement between Tong and Wau authorities has great impact on the civilians, hence prompting returns. Returnees are believed to be coming from Wau POC, Tombora, Uganda, the Sudan, Central African and other places, this has increase burden on already scarce resources couple with limited agricultural activity, poor harvest, depletion of livelihood activities. Therefore, an intervention is required inform of food aid to prevent extreme levels of food insecurity and promote livelihood activities. Therefore, the implementation of this project will increase production and productivity of food for improved household income, address issues of food security and reduces incidences of malnutrition in the communities above all contributing to protecting venerable households affected by crisis against hunger and malnutrition. The appeal for the support is guided by the existing conditions of households in Wau and Raga. The plan is to have immediate and long-term integrated interventions with key clusters like Nutrition, WASH, health and protection that will support continuity and sustainability of food security situation in the state. AFOD being a strong NGO is well versed and placed to implement owing to the presence of experienced and qualified local staff that are familiar with the counties targeted for these interventions.
