Project Summary

Key demographic indicators illustrate the poor health situation in South Sudan, with 96.4/1,000 under-five mortality rate, 62/1,000 infant mortality rate, 40/1,000 neonatal mortality rate, and only 35% of under-five children’s births registered (UNICEF). Access to health care remains particularly challenging in Jonglei State. Many health facilities shut down, looted, or are not fully functional due to lack of funds, increasing the risk of outbreaks due to lack of healthcare services and health surveillance (Social Assessment Report for Essential Health Services Project, November 2018). Ayod County has only 3 functional health facilities, while the other 13 healthcare centers have been vandalized and remain nonfunctional. Of the 8 facilities that were active in Pibor (Boma) County, only 1 remains functional; the other 7 have been destroyed, roads are impassible during the rainy season, cutting off large areas from the rest of the country.
These counties, with an estimated population of 382,455 (approximately 80,315 children under five years of age and 15,299 pregnant and lactating women) are in great need of increased access to healthcare services. For instance, many people living in Boma must walk for 2–3 days to access medical services at the only hospital in the county. For this reason, the population is more likely to try and solve health problems through traditional medicine, which usually causes further complications.
Against this backdrop, the proposed project is intended to deliver a mix of static primary health care and outreach services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations and is expected to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in both Ayod and Pibor Counties, and generally improve the health of the population in the area. This, combined with emergency preparedness and response, will ensure service continuity and will play a crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of “increasing access, utilization and quality of health and nutrition services, with emphasis on maternal and child health and with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity” (Ministry of Health National Health Policy, 2016-2026)

1 - Needs

Floods caused by torrential rains have worsened the living conditions in many parts of the country including Ayod and Pibor among the many counties affected (OCHA, 11 Oct 2019). These have affected more than 364,600 people in different locations including more than 31,600 people in Akobo, Pibor and Uror counties in Jonglei and have destroyed shelters, crops, water sources, public infrastructure like schools and health facilities, increased the risk of water-borne diseases among communities, and rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas. (OCHA, 27 Sep 2019). South Sudan health cluster August 2019 bulletin indicates a rise in malaria cases with fatality rate of 3.80% this is attributed to the flooding and destruction of health facilities.Also key demographic indicators illustrate the poor health situation in South Sudan, with a 96.4/1,000 under-five mortality rate, 62/1,000 infant mortality rate, 40/1,000 neonatal mortality rate, and only 35% of under-five children’s births registered (UNICEF). Many health facilities have been shut down, looted, or are not fully functional due to lack of funds, increasing the risk of outbreaks due to lack of healthcare services and health surveillance (Social Assessment Report for Essential Health Services Project, November 2018). Ayod County has only 3 functional health facilities, while the other 13 healthcare centers that were active before the conflict have been vandalized and remain nonfunctional. Of the 8 facilities that were active in Pibor County, only 1 remains functional; the other 7 have been destroyed. In addition, roads are impassible during the rainy season, cutting off large areas from the rest of the country. These counties, with an estimated population of 382,455 (approximately 80,315 children under five years of age and 15,299 pregnant and lactating women) are in great need of increased access to healthcare services. For instance, many people living in Boma must walk for 2–3 days to access medical services at the only hospital in the county. For this reason, the population is more likely to try and solve health problems through traditional medicine, which usually causes further complications.Against this backdrop, the proposed project will play a crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of “increasing access, utilization and quality of health and nutrition services, with emphasis on maternal and child health and with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity” (Ministry of Health, 2016).Developing permanent facilities to provide nutrition and health care services in these counties could take several years. However, with the establishment and operation of a well-stocked, fully staffed mobile health clinic, will be possible to quickly implement lifesaving nutrition and healthcare services for the population of Ayod and Pibor. The versatile mobile health clinic will move from one community to the next, accessing vulnerable groups in even the most remote, unreached areas. By improving access and utilization of healthcare services among the population—especially mothers and young children—the mobile health clinic will contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality rates.

2 - Activities

Real Medicine Foundation will provide life saving health interventions which are inline with the South Sudan Ministry of health national health policy 2016-2026 which conforms with the cluster objectives. The mobile clinic will improve the quality of life by providing access to health services in remote and conflict affected hard to reach areas particularly access to maternal child health and general consultation and treatment of diseases.The main activities will include;• Providing life saving interventions through medical screening, consultations and referral of critical cases both at outreach and fixed health facilities.• Providing adequate environment for urgent medical consultations and delivery of regular primary health care services for displaced as well as host communities.• Providing early detection, diagnosis, and treatment of emergency and/or life threatening injuries and other health conditions such as diarrhea, fever, respiratory infections, malnutrition among others.• Facilitate access and utilization of immunization services.• Focus on the special needs of especially vulnerable groups (children, pregnant or post partum women, newborns, the elderly, and the critically injured).• Facilitate medical referrals and transfer of patients through ambulance to secondary or tertiary health facility as needed.• Procurement of essential drugs and other pharmaceutical equipment to support the project• Procure mobile truck to provide a versatile, flexible platform for primary health care services to reach the undeserved remote communities. • Establish 3 primary health care centers and 4 primary health care units. •Conduct 24 mobile clinics services in targeted locations in Ayod and Pibor counties to increase access and utilization of health services by community members. • Provide focused antenatal care and postnatal checkups to attending mothers during outreach and in supported health facilities. • Conduct normal deliveries in all the 3 PHCCs and refer complications for comprehensive maternal health services in the nearest hospital. • Provide family planning services and awareness raising in all the established facilities. • Establish 3 cold chain system for the vaccines in the PHCCs • Provide Basic management of survivors of gender based violence (PEP, CMR, treatment for STI, wound care etc) • Provide training to 30 health workers on Psychological Support and Psychological First Aid for people affected by the conflict (Children, women, and people of special needs). • Establish 12 Mobile outreach sites in each county Ayod and Pibor counties. • Conduct RRT Mobilized Training for 50 health workers on integrated Health, WASH, Protection and nutrition response. • Train 30 health workers including county health department on emergency preparedness and response.

The Project will address community structures and increase the population awareness of their rights, specifically their right to health and how to exercise this right. Action will be conducted where change is most needed, through existing mother to mother support groups, local community health workers and Boma health teams. RMF will work in partnership with care international in Pibor and CMD in Ayod, a national NGO present in Ayod with extensive experience in community mobilisation and primary health care (PHC) services and support. Support to existing women’s groups will be provided in order to promote local women’s initiatives and access to information. Healthcare workers will receive financial and technical support. The team will assess training needs with the aim of providing higher quality healthcare services for rights-holders. Advocacy actions will be directed to the United Nations system, international donors and the government of South Sudan, to raise awareness of the humanitarian emergency and health situation, provide information on the specific needs and gaps in health services and the emergency response; and to obtain funds and supplies of health products to provide sexual and reproductive and child health services.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes:This project is closely linked to government/community systems, the intention is to deliver a mix of static primary health care and outreach services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations. These will be complemented with the rolling out of community-based health services, such as the Boma Health Initiative, to bolster community resilience and basic service provision, even while communities are exposed to shocks and cannot be accessed. It will also be essential to make additional efforts to address the plight of women and child survivors of violence and extend lifesaving services to improve accessibility.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment in South Sudan generally is very unpredictable, previous reports indicates several aid workers being attacked coupled with revenge killings from within communities hence limiting access to where aid is needed most. This project is designed with a DO NO HARM approach ensuring that both the communities and the environment are protected. The operation of a healthcare system will inevitably result in the generation of by-products such as medical waste. This includes not only curative activities, but also vaccination campaigns, all creating different waste streams which affect the environment.RMF has envisioned the potential negative environmental and social impacts of the project to be localized, temporary, and fully mitigated through sensible planning and management techniques, as well as diligent management practices. A comprehensive medical waste management plan will cover all types of medical waste originating from healthcare activities. The following steps will be included in the strategy:1) Segregation using color coded waste containers for safety measures during the collection, transportation and disposal.2) treatment process for hazardous waste that achieves at least the minimum required disinfection level followed by safe disposal of residue; Medical waste produced during outreach clinics will be transported to facilities with functional incinerators for safe disposal. 3) Infection prevention committee will be set up at each county and health facility level to provide training on waste management and develop Sops for waste management. Real Medicine Foundation will ensure that technical support is provided to the CHD.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes:RMF works closely with the Ministry of Health, both at national and state levels, in the implementation of its projects. The static clinics will be set up in existing government facilities, which will ensure that RMF’s project complements, rather than works parallel to, government nutrition and health services. The County Health Departments will be closely involved in the implementation of this project, and monthly HMIS and NIS reports will be shared with the CHD, state Ministry of Health, and national Ministry of Health to ensure full participation of government structures in the project. RMF will also work closely with communities benefitting from the project, listening to their concerns and addressing requests as much as possible. Community members may also contribute work, or in-kind local materials, including the identification and construction of shelters for those accessing the mobile clinics in their communities. This will enforce full ownership of the project by the community and continuation after the project period

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Implementing feedback mechanism is a standard procedure in RMF projects as one of our systems to monitor quality, promote participation and improve accountability. We use different tools adapted to the language, context and culture to avoid barriers preventing the usage and facilitate the access. We also follow strict confidentiality protocols in order to assure the privacy of the informant and the information is processed by the correct staff.
