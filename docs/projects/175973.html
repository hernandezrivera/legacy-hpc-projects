Project Summary

DRC/DDG will deploy 2 clearance teams capable of mine action survey, and both rapid response emergency mine action and resilience-focused static clearance capacities across the country, removing hazards to promote recovery and access to humanitarian services as needed. Additionally DRC/DDG will deploy 5 teams focused on delivering Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) to conflict-affected communities to further mitigate risks of explosive hazards.
It should be noted that the locations included in targeting below outline possible areas of intervention, rather than a list of every location to be targeted under the project. Exact target locations will be determined based on needs at the time of prioritization within the project period, with a focus on enabling the delivery of other sector activities supported through the HRP wherever possible.

1 - Needs

The Revitalised Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) presents the strongest opportunity for peace and post-conflict recovery in several years. However, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian needs. An estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis published in December 2020. In this context, Humanitarian Mine Action agencies act to remove barriers to accessing humanitarian services, sustainable population returns and economic recovery. According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, more than 654,800 people are impacted by anti-personnel, anti-vehicle mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW). The UN Mine Action Service (UNMAS) currently records that all known contamination currently amounts to an area of 18.5 km2. According to Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) data from January 2021, there are 443 hazardous areas (HAs) identified across the country including 8.5 km2 of land covered by minefields, 3.4 km2 of mined roads, and 6.6 km2 of land contaminated with cluster munitions and other ERW. Much of this contamination straddles the primary return routes for refugees in neighbouring countries. In addition to risks of injury or death, landmines and ERW impact far higher numbers by restricting freedom of movement, including access to key socio-economic assets providing healthcare, education, and food production and livelihoods opportunities. Based on a recent analysis of historical data covering 2000 to 2018, in March 2020 the National Mine Action Authority (NMAA) reported that trends indicate minefield HAs are most likely to be located on, or very close to agricultural land. Of the minefields for which data was available, 52% of HAs were located less than 50m from agricultural land and 14% were located less than 50 metres from land used for natural resources. Due to contamination, women and girls may also be forced to travel further to gather resources for the household, thereby increasing their vulnerability to Sexual- and Gender- Based Violence (SGBV). During January – December 2020, 42 people were killed or injured by landmines or ERW, including 51 boys, 5 girls, and 1 woman. By definition, landmines and ERW do not discriminate in terms of posing physical safety threats and restricting movements. However, due to distinctions in traditional household roles and movement patterns, they impact women, men, boys and girls in different ways. This likely explains the prevalence of boys as casualties during landmine / ERW accidents, given the curious nature of children when playing outdoors, and the fact that girls may be engaged more in household chores at times. These casualty rates also highlight the importance of awareness tailored to the needs of children, reflected in the fact that DRC/DDG will target 65% of its Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) activities towards children under this project. In terms of landmine and ERW contamination levels, the 5 counties that are classified as catastrophic (severity 5) include are Magwi, Juba, Terekeka, Canal / Pigi and Torit. However, looking beyond contamination levels only, DRC/DDG may also deliver mine action survey, clearance and Explosive ordnance Risk Education (EORE) activities in lower severity areas where the mitigation of explosive hazard risks would support sustainable returns and recovery, particularly given possibilities for returns following Protection of Civilians (PoC) site transitions in Unity, Upper Nile and Western Bahr El Ghazal States, as well as to support safe behaviors amongst those displaced populations across the country that may be less familiar with hazards in areas that they have recently moved to.

2 - Project activities

The overall goal of this project is the prevent and mitigate against the physical risks posed by landmines and ERW through clearance, and enhancing preparedness and resilience through EORE messaging. The proposed intervention directly supports Protection Cluster Objective 1 by reducing vulnerabilities of women, men, girls and boys at risk of mortality through clearance. The intervention also supports Protection Cluster Objective 2 by increasing awareness of safe behaviours through EORE, thereby enhancing community level resilience against explosive hazards. Targeting considerations will also be adopted with the aim of supporting durable solutions aims under Cluster Objective 3, targeting clearance activities towards areas where the removal of landmines and ERW can support local integration, returns and relocation by easing strains on land access and use. To support the above objectives, DRC/DDG proposes to undertake the following activities: 1. Ensure the provision of critical assistance to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard to reach and / or priority geographical areas. 2. 1.1: Carry out desk research and engage with other stakeholders to identify population affected by conflict / are observing returns or displacement and that are likely to have been heavily affected by landmines or ERW, including restrictions on access to hujmanitarian assets or service delivery. 3. 1.2: Conduct Non-Technical Survey (NTS) in severity areas 4 and 5 to inform a clearance, EORE and overall humanitarian response. 4. 1.3: Survey infrastructure and transit routes through NTS to facilitate the timely, effective and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance by other stakeholders. 5. 1.4: Conduct meetings with humanitarian agencies to prioritise locations in terms of humanitarian access / service delivery, including warehouses, compounds, distribution sites and/or schools. 6. 1.5: Conduct refresher training for DRC/DDG staff on NTS, technical survey / TS (using detectors) and clearance methodologies. NTS training includes strategies designed to ensure participation and active engagement from women and children, recognising the obstacles presented by societal norms, and gender / age-specific daily routines and movement patterns. 7. 1.6: Conduct (mobile) Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) / (static) Battle Area Clearance (BAC) / Manual Mine Clearance (MMC) to remove and destroy dangerous items. To Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience; 8. 2.1. Conduct EORE targeting at-risk women, men, boys and girls within displaced and host communities to promote greater awareness and safer practices in relation to landmine 9. 2.2: Conduct refresher training for DRC/DDG staff on EORE delivery, including key messaging, participatory learning methods and engagement strategies aimed at securing participation across different age and gender groups. DRC/DDG will monitor gender and age specific attitudes towards explosive hazards, with the ability to develop tailored risk reduction strategies where required. To Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other populations.DDG will retain the capacity to work in both static and highly mobile team formats. 3.1: Conduct desk research and survey in areas of return or displacement in severity areas 2, 3, 4 and 5 through NTS to determine the extent and impact of landmines and ERW on local integration, returns and relocation. 3.2: conduct EOD, BAC and MMC in areas of returns or displacement to reduce strains on access to socio-economic assets and ease access to humanitarian service delivery. 3.3: Submit to the Protection Cluster / NMAA / UNMAS reconnaissance / needs assessment reports indicating the need for a mine action response in potential target areas.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC/DDG conducts regular awareness raising with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms, as well as provides information on PSEA to beneficiaries in multiple formats.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC/DDG adopts a community-based approach, where men, women, boys, and girls are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities, which is achieved through sharing information, engagement with different segments of the community, and ensuring effective complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM). DRC/DDG operates several types of CFM to ensure that there are no barriers for different community members to access them. Examples of CRMs include information desks, telephone hotline, and household surveys. In the event that any challenges are identified by the community in the implementation of the project, DRC/DDG seeks to adapt the intervention where possible. Very young children, such as those five years and under, excluded from influencing the project due to communication challenges and to ensure adherence to protection and safeguarding principles.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRC/DDG has IEC materials available in multiple formats that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on the feedback and complaints mechanisms. DRC/DDG conducts regular awareness raising with communities on the presence of the complaints and feedback mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DRC/DDG adopts an age and gender diversity (AGD) mainstreaming approach across all its programmes, meaning that it gives full consideration to AGD in the design, implementation, and monitoring of its activities. For example, under the project DRC/DDG’s Community Liaison capacity engages community leaders, religious leaders, women, men, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities on targeting considerations. To achieve this, existing representative structures are drawn on wherever possible (if initial survey deems that the community considers them to be legitimate). Further to this, DRC/DDG’s Community Liaison Coordinator visits each task site during prioritisation to determine additional vulnerable groups that may not be represented by traditional community structures. In addition, DRC/DDG engages with members from all segments of communities to solicit their feedback on the project to ensure that activities are tailored and meet the needs of different groups. For instance, when carrying out a training or awareness raising session, DRC/DDG obtains feedback from men, women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities to select an appropriate time. DRC/DDG also considers physical access issues for those with disabilities when selecting an activity site.
