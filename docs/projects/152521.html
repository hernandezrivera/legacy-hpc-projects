Project Summary

The project aims to provide basic health services to the IDP population of the “informal” camps of Juba through a mobile clinic and a network of PHCCs/Us, also conducting screening on STD, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis, visual apparatus diseases. The project will support PHCCs/Us in collaboration with the MoH Jubek State through supply of drugs, consumables and micronutrients with a particular focus on Malaria prevention and treatment and on pharmacological management of cases of epilepsy.
In the framework of the project there is the direct management of 1 PHCC and 2 PHCUs in town (which are the main health referral structures for the Urban IDP Camps), the support to other 2 governmental PHCCs through capacity building of the staff and drugs and supplies delivery, the management of ante natal and post natal care units in two dispensaries. Particular attention is put on prevention of diseases in new born children leading to disabilities.
A support to the Health County Department in the large scale vaccination campaign is also included, as well as hygiene promotion through events and distribution of soap and chlorine.
A specific activity regards nutritional support, in the direct managed PHCC, for children with MAM and referral of SAM cases, as well as support to PLW through the distribution of vitaminized milk.
All the activities are carried on with a particular attention on gender, focusing on the role of women at different levels. Furthermore an important component of the action is the attention on disability, particularly to children with disabilities, ensuring access to health services and rehabilitation, directly dealing with particular cases such as epilepsy, cleft lip and palate, spina bifida, hydrocele, osteomyelitis, cerebral palsy.

Needs

The needs analysis in the HEALTH sector results from the following considerations:• Within the Protection of Civilian Camp (PoC: i.e. UNIMISS and UNHOUSE) there are numerous actors who offer services for IDPs, even in the field of health, while the “informal” Urban IDP Camps are almost completely neglected. From the requests of support by the Ministry of Health in this regard, and from the needs exposed during the Clusters regarding the IDP Camps in the Urban Center of Juba (Mahad and Don Bosco IDP Cluster in particular) there is a lack of health services in the Urban IDP Camps which, since 2013, have spontaneously grown in different areas of the City of Juba and its surroundings. Since 2014 OVCI guarantees health services, through Mobile Clinic, present in those informal Camps in the urban area of Juba. Initially active only once a month, the service was then expanded and strengthened by OVCI, thanks to the co-funding of the Italian Cooperation Agency. In this way, basic health services were guaranteed, through the Mobile Clinic, to more than 30.000 people. The activity of the Mobile Clinic is daily recorded in the health reports of OVCI. • At the Health Cluster it has been repeatedly highlighted the difficulty of the South Sudanese Government in the management of its Primary Health Care Units and Centers, especially those based in the most remote areas. There is a constant lack of medicines and poor equipment of the facilities. Furthermore, the poor level of basic knowledge of the health staff is highlighted. OVCI collaborates with the dispensaries of the urban area of Juba, supporting them with the supply of medicines and ongoing training courses for health staff. In addition, OVCI supports the strengthening of two PHCUs (namely Way Station and Mangateen2, located in remote areas of Juba, which are very important structures of reference for their respective IDP Camps), ensuring the procurement of medicines, support of personnel, structure management, and training. OVCI has also been supporting the Jubek State County Health Department for three years in State Vaccination Campaigns, with more than 200.000 children annually vaccinated. • In the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2018 an alarming presence of cholera is described throughout the Country, especially in the urban areas. OVCI provides chlorine and soap for prevention, and sensitizes the population of the camps with hygiene promotion events. Moreover, it guarantees rapid tests for malaria to the dispensaries that need it, and distributes mosquito nets: with the rainy season, in fact, there is a rapid increase in the spread of malaria, anyway present in every period of the year. In addition, Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs) are spreading very fast, especially in the IDP Camps (mainly due to the isolation of the structures), with particular reference to HIV/AIDS and syphilis. OVCI has been carrying out a series of screening and awareness on how to recognize and prevent them, reaching about 7.000 beneficiaries so far. Finally, special attention is given to the improvement of prenatal and postnatal services, since only a small part of the State Health Facilities offer monitoring and care services to pregnant women. Lack of food and depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound, have led to an increase in the price of cereals and imported goods, worsening the food security of the population and increasing its vulnerability.The most affected ones are obviously the children: it is estimated that, in 2018, about 1.100.000 children under the age of 5 have suffered from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). OVCI supports children with Moderate Malnutrition (MAM) at the nutritional center of Usratuna PHCC and refer cases of SAM to Al Sabah Hospital. Moreover, there is an important attention to the feeding of PLW with the distribution through 14 peripheral centers of vitaminized milk.

Activities

1.1 Continuous supply of drugs and rapid tests for disease screening (malaria, syphilis, etc.), essential nutrients for life (vitamins, folic acid, iron) to 5 PHCCs/Us in partnership with the Ministry of Health of Jubek State and the leading Agency of HPF for Juba County. 1.2 Provision of expert health personnel in 1 PHCC (Usratuna) and 2 PHCUs (Way Station and Mangateen2), and support to the health staff already present in the other 2 governmental PHCCs/Us. 1.3 Management of a unit for prenatal and postnatal care and prevention in Usratuna PHCC and Way Station PHCU. 1.4 Strengthening the therapies for malaria cases in the governmental PHCCs/Us of Juba indicated by the State MoH 1.5 Supply to governmental PHCCs/Us of essential micronutrients, in particular Folic and iron, as well as vitamins, mosquito nets, and Sulfadoxine + Pyrimethamine (Fansidar) to pregnant women, for the prevention of diseases in new born children. 1.6 Support to the County Health Department and the Ministry of Health of the Jubek State, for large-scale vaccination. 1.7 Training workshops for local health workers of PHCCs/Us on:- Early diagnosis and disability referral, including epilepsy;- Management of pregnancy problems and obstetric emergencies- Correct and proper management of a Health Facility and proper rationalized use of resources 1.8 Mobile Clinic activities in urban IDP Camps in Juba and in areas with poor access to health, offering basic medical services and tests for STDs, vaccinations and visits for pregnant or post-gestational women. 1.9 Screening of the diseases of the visual apparatus, STD, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis in the IDP camps, and referral of the most serious cases to specialized structures. 1.10 Continuous screening, in the IDP camps and in the host communities, of cases of disability of the developmental age, and reference of the cases to Usratuna Rehabilitation Centre 1.11 Pharmacological management of cases of epilepsy in the Urban area of Juba and support for treatment in the PHCC of Usratuna. 1.12 Sensitization meetings in the Urban IDP Camps and in the host communities, on the issues of disability, nutrition, STDs and pregnancy. 1.13 Informative meetings with IDP Camps leader and with the IDP population over the health facilities run by the MoH or other NGO/Agencies present in town. 1.14 Hygiene Promotion events in Urban IDP Camps for the promotion of good sanitary practices, and distribution of chlorine soap and tablets for water purification. 1.15 Nutritional support, through WFP in kind contribution, at the PHCC of Usratuna, for children with MAM and referral of SAM cases. 1.16 Support for PLW through the distribution of vitaminized milk

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Social Mobilization and sensitization will be carried out weekly in the camps by the social mobilizers designated among the IDPs themselves, after proper consultation with the community leaders.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are tailored for:- families with children with disabilities, through the sensitization on particular diseases leading to disabilities, on specific disabilities cases and on the possibility of rehabilitation held in Usratuna. Sensitization is done also about the rights of PWD in general.- Women: informative training for PLW are continuously held in our PHCC by our midwives and our nutritionist during pregnancy and for the first 6 months of the child life. Sensitization on STD is also done during the screening days.

Yes

The staff of the project is receiving at the beginning of the year, in the framework of another OVCI's financed project, a dedicated training on management of GBV cases and the correct protocol to be implemented, including the correct referral pathway to follow.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Many international validated documents prove that people with disability, in the general context of humanitarian crisis, risk to be systematically excluded from access to basic services. The parallel project of “Community Based Rehabilitation / Inclusive Development” that OVCI is being implementing in the Urban IDP camps of Juba, will assure, through Community Workers, that people with disability could overcome barriers to access to health.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

No

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities address specific cases of neglect, with particular regards to people, especially children, with disability, which are particularly “forgotten” in complex crisis like the one South Sudan is facing. OVCI has always focused on people with disability by mandate, and will do so even in the context of this emergency project, assuring that they are able to access to basic services, on an equal basis with the others.
