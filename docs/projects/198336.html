Project Summary

This project aims to integrate 55,000 IDPs children from NWSW regions.
In order to enable them to easily integrate into host school classrooms and have an equal level of education as their peers, the IDP children, as well as other children from the host community who have temporarily dropped out of school, will participate in remedial classes for a period of two months during the vacations and will then receive the necessary materials prior to their integration.
To this end, members of the PTAs and school management committees will be sensitized, and their support solicited to facilitate the integration and retention of displaced children in schools.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The massive influx of school-age children and youth into the host regions of the West and Littoral has resulted in overcrowded classrooms, with an average ratio of 200 students per class, exposing students to multiple protection risks.In 2022, approximately 79,954 internally displaced persons were reported in the Littoral Region, of whom 40% were school-age children. Some families have been able to send their children to school, however, from their areas of origin to their host areas, there has been a lag time with certainly a loss of schooling compared to other children who have studied continuously without any interruption of school. Most of the children who have dropped out of school and want to return to school are having difficulty catching up with their classmates because of the gap in schooling caused by the crisis. The remedial classes will allow some out-of-school children to catch up and easily reintegrate into the school environment and feel more confident and on par with their peers. Generally, displaced children are easily admitted to host schools. However, with displacement and parental poverty, children do not receive the support they need to facilitate their learning. The lack of learning materials is one of the needs expressed by the communities, which hinders learning and limits children's ability to continue their education. Learning materials will be provided to students in the targeted schools, in addition to materials for recreational activities which will also be distributed to the targeted schools for children to enable them to organize recreational and sports activities that will also build the children's confidence and contribute to their moral, physical, and psychological well-being.The local municipalities will accompany the implementation of educational activities in support of formal education. The Ministries of Basic and Secondary education will equally be actively involved in the whole process through their reginal representatives. Primary education is free throughout the country, but in most schools, there are still parent-teachers in elementary school and temporary teachers in secondary school. In public schools, the fees are not high, but given the vulnerability of communities in general and IDPs, some parents are unable to meet this obligation, which makes the children concerned more likely to drop out of school. Also, due to the lack of legal or school documents, the integration of some displaced children in schools is not always easy. The participatory management structures of the schools, in particular the parent-teacher associations, the school management committees, and the children's governments, will be identified, sensitized, and revitalized. They will thus ensure the monitoring and protection of assets provided to schools and will also help facilitate the integration of displaced children into the host schools. The participatory management structures of 100 targeted schools will be revitalized.Partners from local organizations will be at the forefront of facilitating this activity.
