Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to reduce protection risk through the mitigation of threat, reduction of vulnerability, and response to rights violations/protection incidents. The project also interventions will ensure continued monitoring through community networks and official bodies (governmental and non-governmental) and the ongoing monitoring will ensure the design of the programming is appropriate and adaptive, and will inform wider protection strategies at local and national level. DRC’s protection team will focus their efforts on the four key outcomes;
• Improved evidence-based protection response and advocacy through Protection Monitoring and analysis
• Ensuring improved access to preventive and responsive protection services through identification, referral and provision of Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) and Psycho-socio Support Services (PSS)
• Reducing risk exposure and responding to Gender Based Violence (GBV) through community awareness and GBV Case Management services
• Enhanced community protection capacities through community-based protection and community awareness raising
Vulnerable people are supported through full MPCA to minimize negative coping mechanisms
also, The project aims to enhance the improved access to safe water and basic sanitation services; Increase and sustain access to safe and adequate drinking water, dignified and acceptable sanitation facilities and improved sanitary conditions and hygiene practices among refugees and host communities. The success of the intervention will be based on a tailored approach addressing the evolving needs of the refugee population as well as the needs of the host communities affected by the conflict. Though, with a specific attention paid to the sustainability and the integration with other DRC interventions to increase the impact.
Outputs: -
• Supply of potable water increased or maintained
• Population lives in satisfactory conditions of sanitation and hygiene
• Solid waste disposal and management
• Hygiene promotion
• Capacity Building on Operation and Maintenance of WASH facilities

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

DRC upholds the centrality of protection and protection mainstreaming principles, including meaningful access, participation, and empowerment, throughout the entire project cycle. As such, DRC ensures consideration of age, gender, and other relevant diversity characteristics in its context and needs assessments, protection risk analyses, selection criteria, and interventions. All project data is disaggregated by sex and age to ensure sufficient reach to all population groups. Frequent engagement with communities and community leaders ensures understanding of local dynamics, including the specific needs, barriers to access services and assistance, and protection risks affecting different age and/or gender categories. Project activities are available at the individual, household, and community levels to guarantee diverse access to information, assistance, and services. In addition, all feedback mechanisms, particularly post-distribution monitoring, will be designed with a protection and gender lens to obtain knowledge on any potential challenges or negative effects experienced by certain age or gender categories before, during or after distribution or other support provided by DRC.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will prioritize a participatory, transparent, and responsive approach to community feedback. By actively involving the community in the feedback loop, the Protection, WASH, and Shelter activities aim to be dynamic, adaptable, and most importantly, reflective of the needs and aspirations of the communities they serve.Several key strategies will be employed such as; establishment of feedback mechanism through development and clear implementation of feedback mechanisms that are sensitive to the cultural context like suggestion boxes, community meetings, hotlines, and digital platforms, ensuring that various channels are available for expressing opinions and concerns. Other strategies are; regular inclusive community consultations, survey and assessments, engagement of community leaders and influencers, develop a transparent and responsive reporting system to share project updates, outcomes and any other changes, capacity building for effective feedback, involve the community in co-designing solutions and adaptations based on their feedback, establish a robust complaints and grievance mechanism that provides a confidential and accessible avenue for community members to express concerns and learning and knowledge sharing among others.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Accountability is a DRC priority and forms the backbone of our work around the world. DRC’s participatory approaches rely on community engagement and the use of access and acceptance in affected communities to identify diverse groups that can represent the spectrum of stakeholders in need of support in all intervention areas. DRC’s feedback, complaints and reporting mechanism (FCRM) will also enable communication from communities and households to DRC’s MEAL team on issues facing target population as assistance is provided. The FCRM is also the pathway that beneficiaries are encouraged to use to raise concerns, and report suspected SEA which is then immediately channelled into DRC’s Code of Conduct Reporting Mechanism. Based on feedback, DRC will adapt its approaches to ensure that activities are reaching the most vulnerable, and a commitment to ‘Do No Harm’ is maintained at all times. DRC prioritizes participation of affected people in the action whenever possible, which is critical to securing buy-in and access to the most vulnerable.
