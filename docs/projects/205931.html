Project Summary

UNH a dual mandated organization that provides development & emergency response. Based on our experience in emergency response, developed this project following consultative meetings with the respective state MOH, CHDs, local authority & affected population including elderly & PWD. UNH has operations presence in Central & Eastern Equatoria, Warrap, Jonglei, Upper Nile States & Greater Pibor/Abyei Administrative Area providing essential health services.
UNH’s HRP for 2024 targets Abyei, Pibor, Tonj North, Twic & Renk targeting Host communities, IDPs & returnees. These areas are mainly inhabited by pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, land disputes, interclan/revenge attack resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility & humanitarian crisis. Moreso areas bordering Sudan, Renk, Twic are receiving a lot of people from Sudan due to Sudan crisis since April 2023, Greater Pibor also receiving returnees from Ethiopia. More cattle keeper’s area also moving to Abyei from Sudan increasing the risks of violence with the farmers.
Health needs of Abyei, Pibor, Tonj North, Twic & Renk enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, IPC measures, food insecurity, flood, threat of disease outbreaks, limited health facility functionality, stockouts of essential medicines. Cases of measles reported in all the counties (IDSR Week 44). 2022 DHIS analysis for AAA, GPAA, Twic, Tonj North & Renk shows malaria is the top cause of morbidity followed by ARI & AWD. Health needs of the beneficiaries include BeMONC, EPI, treatments for malaria, ARIs, AWD, MHPSS, GBV, services for PwD, positive SBC messages.
Severe food insecurity coupled with Sudan crisis, subnational violences, contracted economy, effect of COVID-19, weak healthcare system will drive malnutrition rate & health indicators in these counties will deteriorate if no emergency health services supported.
In 2024, UNH will support PHCCs/PHCUs, mobile clinics & BHI to provide emergency & essential primary healthcare services to the vulnerable population. Will implement following activities which are in line with health cluster specific objectives & will contribute to the HRP objectives.
• Implement emergency preparedness & contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases including COVID-19.
• Provide OPD consultations for major cause of morbidity/mortality
• Provide EPI services.
• Provide BeMONC services
• Provide life-saving essential quality health care services
• Provide MHPSS/CMR services.
• Provide services for PWD
• Provide emergency kits to the health facilities.
• Conduct community consultative meetings
• Support regular meetings with CHD
• Conduct meetings with humanitarian, development & peace partners.
• Conduct quarterly community health events at each health facility for SBC
• Conduct quarterly suportive supervisions.
• Provide community health services using BHI
• Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) that will be conducted in UNH operational areas in 2024.
• Strengthen IPC in our health facilities
• Provide management for SAM children with medical complication.
• Support Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care.
Direct project beneficiaries 85,000 (7,688 male U5, 8,071 female U5, 14,715 male 5-17 years; 14,842 female 5-17 years; 17,106 male 18-60 years, 18,499 female 18-60 years; 2,175 male >60 years, 1,904 female > 60 years). PWD (2,240 male under 17, 2,291 female under 17, 1,711 male 18-60 years, 1,850 female 18-60 years; 218 males >60 years, 190 females > 60 years).
To implement those activities, UNH needs 1,600,000 USD in 2024, aligned with health cluster’s strategy on cost per beneficiaries. Will work with other health, Nutrition/WASH/FSL/Protection partners to enhance synergies, will engage CHD & development health partners in each county to take over at the end of the project to ensure sustainability.

1. Needs

November 2023 Health Cluster (PIN), estimated 6,324,453 people in South Sudan & Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) will need humanitarian health services in 2024, population targeted for response 51.15%. UNH will response in AAA, Pibor, Tonj North, Twic, Renk targeting 14,750, 19,010, 22,690, 21,540 & 7,010 respectively mainly IDPs, returnees & vulnerable host communities. Inhabitants of AAA, Pibor, Tonj North, Twic & Renk mainly pastoralists, prone to conflicts due cattle raiding, land disputes, revenge attack leading to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility, humanitarian crisis. Pibor has 36 health facilities, 14 supported by UNICEF. In Tonj North, 13 HFs supported by HPF3 out of 18 (Tonj North CHD October2023), no support for Marial-lou hospital. Most HFs non-functional, limited BEmONC, low EPI coverage, vulnerable people in remote areas have no access to lifesaving healthcare. Areas devastated by flood & subnational violence for the past 3 consecutive years. OCHA 26 October 2023 report indicates returnees voluntarily coming to Pibor from Ethiopia & malnutrition is 33% from the screening conducted by the partners. County of Twic, Renk & AAA are receiving a lot of people coming from Sudan due conflict in Sudan. As of 24 October, UNHCR/IOM have recorded 332,979 people crossing the border from Sudan to South Sudan since 15 April. Of these, 86.7% South Sudanese, 11.9% Sudanese, remaining are refugees/asylum seekers. This exerts pressure on the limited available health services. In Twic 18 HFs supported by HPF, 17 not supported/non-functional, Renk 11 HFs supported by UNICEF out of 25, area with bordering Sudan with a lot of people coming in. AAA has only emergency health partners, no developmental partners. 2022 DHIS2 analysis, AAA, OPD consultation 93,402, malaria accounted for 48.1%, ARI 23.7%, AWD 8.2%; in GPAA OPD 159,676, malaria 34.9%, ARI 16.8%, AWD 9.8%; Twic OPD 304,453, malaria 53.1%, ARI 21.3%, AWD 6.3%, Tonj North OPD 120,295, malaria 53.8%, ARI 23.2%, AWD 9.5%; Renk OPD 84,358, malaria 51.7%, ARI 29.6%, AWD 7.5%. IPC published on November 11, 2023 puts GPAA, Twic & Renk in IPC4 for period October 2023-June 2024, Tonj North at IPC3, returnees in IPC5. Main drivers for this: subnational violence, displacement, contracted economy, flooding, low agricultural production. HMIS exist in all counties, reporting progressively improving, malaria, AWD & ARI remain the major cause of morbidity & mortality. (IDSRweek 44, 2023). Low reporting for AAA, Upper Nile at 35%, 48% respectively. 56 cases of measles in Tonj North, suspected measles cases in AAA & GPAA, 4 cases of measles in Renk at Point of Entry (PoE) which has measles vaccines. Cholera out outbreaks reported in 4 states in Sudan increasing the risks in bordering areas. Health needs of AAA, Pibor, Tonj North, Twic & Renk enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, IPC measures, food insecurity, flood, threat of disease outbreaks, limited health facility functionality, stockouts of essential medicines. Health needs of the beneficiaries include BeMONC, EPI, treatments for malaria, ARIs, AWD, MHPSS, GBV, services for PwD, positive SBC messages.Severe food insecurity will have more impact on children, PLW. Poor maternal nutrition is associated with adverse pregnancy/birth outcomes, food insecurity, violence, erratic population displacement, inadequate public health services increase the risk of disease outbreaks among undernourished population, are major cause of excess morbidity/mortality.UNH will provide emergency Healthcare targeting 85,000 beneficiaries (7,688 male U5, 8,071 female U5, 14,715 male 5-17 years; 14,842 female 5-17 years; 17,106 male 18-60 years, 18,499 female 18-60 years; 2,175 male>60 years, 1,904 female> 60 years). Out this, 8,500 are PWD (2,240 male under 17, 2,291 female under 17, 1,711 male 18-60 years, 1,850 female 18-60 years; 218 males>60 years, 190 females> 60 years).

2. Project activities

UNH has aligned its activities to MOH Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services and proposed to implement the following activities in 2023;• Implement emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases, disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, alerts reporting and verification within 24 hours, outbreak investigations and case management for selected diseases including COVID-19.• Provide OPD consultations focusing on major cause of morbidity, mortality and epidemic prone diseases, malaria, ARIs, AWD, measles, ABO.• Provide routine Immunization at mobile/static facilities against vaccine preventable diseases including COVID-19 vaccination, strengthen the capacity of health workers on EPI through training, support reactive measles vaccination of boys/girls between 6 months – 15 years.• Build the capacity of health workers on BeMONC, provide BeMONC services in our primary health centres to enhance deliveries assisted by skilled birth attendants. • Provide life-saving essential quality health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static health facilities & mobile clinics & build the capacity of health workers on provision of lifesaving essential health care.• Integrate & provide MHPSS services. • Integrate & provide services for SGBV including CMR & enhance referral to safe houses where necessary. • Provide services for people with disability, rehabilitation services disaggregated by age and sex.• Coordinate with core pipeline managers to provide emergency kits (WHO Interagency emergency kits, UNFPA Reproductive Health Commodities including Rape kits and cold chain equipment including vaccines from UNICEF) to support our health facilities.• Conduct community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected populations & strengthen feedback mechanism at the health facilities to ensure beneficiaries provide feedback on the project.• Scaleup human-centred design for demand generation for health services. Will conduct focus group discussions with health workers, community/youth/women/religious leaders, chiefs, care givers, influencers & patients to derive context specific human centred design solutions to increase uptake of services such as immunization including C. 19, MNCH, nutrition, WASH, GBV. The derived messages will be integrated into routine positive Social and Behavioural Change (SBC) messages. • Support and strengthen regular meetings with the County Health Department.• Conduct meetings with humanitarian, development & peace partners.• Conduct quarterly community health events at each health facility for behavioural change communication.• Conduct community sensitization and disseminate IEC materials include those for COVID-19.• Provide technical & operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervisions.• Provide community health services using BHI & strengthen the linkage with facility-based services. • Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) that will be conducted in UNH operational areas in 2024. • Strengthen IPC in our health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities.• Participate in the training on Rationale use of essential health care commodities.• Conduct MUAC screening for children under 5 & PLW to identify MAM and SAM cases and refer to TSFP and OTP centres respectively. • Provide management for SAM children with medical complication in our PHCC.• Support the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA) on Health Care and provide regular report on the attacks. • Support and submit timely, accurate and complete reports through EWARS, IDSR and 5Ws. • Coordinate the health response with Health Clusters, MoH and all levels, CHDs, partners including UN agencies, national and international NGOs to enhance synergies.
