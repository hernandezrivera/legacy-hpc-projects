Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) seeks to empower Rohingya women through this project by involving them in shelter construction activities in Camp 17. This initiative aims to improve women's livelihoods and promote gender equality through gender mainstreaming, addressing immediate shelter needs identified in the 2023 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) while strengthening community resilience through the active involvement of women in construction and shelter maintenance.
SC will establish additional women’s groups in Camp 17, offering comprehensive training on safe shelter construction, maintenance, disaster risk reduction (DRR), and climate change adaptation. These efforts equip women with practical skills that enhance financial independence and community participation. To ensure robust Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) through transparency, inclusivity, and responsiveness, SC will utilize Feedback and Response Mechanisms (FRM), and conduct community needs assessments and focus group discussions (FGDs) to align project activities with the priorities of the community. To foster wider community acceptance of women's engagement in the shelter activities, the project will also engage men, sensitizing them on the importance of women's roles in construction and breaking gender stereotypes and barriers in the community.
Integrating Disaster Risk Management (DRM) principles, the project promotes sustainable practices by using environmentally friendly materials and durable construction techniques, which reduce repair needs and environmental impact. Training sessions on climate awareness and DRR prepare beneficiaries to respond to disasters such as fire incidents, floods, landslides, cyclones, etc. Accessibility and safety are prioritized in all components, ensuring shelters meet the needs of vulnerable groups, including women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities.
The project aims to increase women’s participation in income-generating activities, improve family livelihoods, enhance community preparedness, and promote gender equality. These goals align with critical community needs for secure shelters, sustainable livelihoods, and inclusive planning. Women’s groups will be encouraged to assume leadership roles, ensuring their continued involvement in construction activities. Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms will measure the project’s impact on women’s empowerment and community resilience.
Shelters in Camp 17, primarily constructed from bamboo and tarpaulins, are highly vulnerable to fires and extreme weather, risks exacerbated by climate change. SC’s localization strategy builds the capacity of refugees to anticipate and respond to environmental threats. Training sessions on safe shelter construction and climate adaptation engage both male and female construction workers, promoting collaboration and fostering a resilient community. Under SC’s supervision, trained women will construct 85 new shelters and repair 30 existing ones, adhering to Sphere Standards and ISCG Shelter/NFI Working Group guidelines.
SC will collaborate with stakeholders to explore alternative funding mechanisms for addressing gaps in future programming. Vulnerable groups, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities, will be actively consulted to ensure shelters address their specific needs. Community awareness sessions will guide shelter design and repairs, emphasizing hazard resilience and accessibility. SC will coordinate with the Shelter/NFI sector, Camp-in-Charge (CiC), and other stakeholders to ensure collaborative implementation of repair efforts.
This project not only empowers women but also strengthens community resilience and promotes gender equality, contributing to a safer and more inclusive environment in Camp 17. Through skill-building and shared responsibilities, SC aims to foster lasting improvements in shelter safety, enhance social cohesion, and build the resilience of displaced communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

“Promoting community-led improvement of the living environment in camps for displaced Rohingya refugees” project addresses critical needs identified by the MSNA 2023, focusing on shelter safety, livelihood opportunities, and gender-inclusive practices. With 79% of households lacking adequate shelter and NFIs, and 63% living in structurally vulnerable spaces, immediate action is required to enhance living conditions, particularly as climate change exacerbates risks from fires, floods, and cyclones. The reliance on bamboo and tarpaulin shelters compounds these hazards, creating unsafe conditions for all, especially women, children, and the elderly. Based on the J-MSNA 2023, one of the priority needs in Camp 17 was shelter materials upgrade. Besides, 63% HHs reported not living in a functional domestic space. 42% reported their LPG refill did not last 3 months. Moreover, leaks during rain, damage to roof, and walls were most common issues reported by the Camp 17 community. The project empowers Rohingya women by equipping them with practical skills in shelter construction, maintenance, and disaster risk reduction (DRR). These activities address MSNA findings that female-headed households face significant economic challenges and limited income opportunities. Financial management training for 40 women fosters economic empowerment and promotes household resilience. By encouraging women’s participation in shelter activities and challenging traditional gender roles, the project fosters social inclusion and allows women to contribute directly to community resilience.The project incorporates a robust Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) framework, ensuring transparency and participation through focus groups, feedback mechanisms, and open communication with beneficiaries. Findings from these engagements are disseminated to sensitize men about the importance of women’s involvement in shelter work, promoting community cohesion and acceptance of gender-inclusive practices. To ensure safety and accessibility, female construction workers are provided with appropriate safety gear and visibility equipment, while their specific needs are assessed and addressed to create a secure working environment.Aligned with Disaster Risk Management (DRM) principles, the project integrates sustainable practices to enhance resilience. Durable materials are prioritized to reduce the frequency of shelter repairs, minimizing environmental impact. Training sessions equip women with knowledge of climate change adaptation, safe shelter construction, and hazard preparedness, addressing vulnerabilities caused by fires, flooding, and other climate-related risks.Deliverables include the construction of 85 new MTS shelters using durable, hazard-resilient materials and repairs to 30 existing shelters. These efforts prioritize the most vulnerable groups and adhere to Sphere Standards and ISCG Shelter/NFI Working Group guidelines. Additionally, the project budget includes wages for female construction workers, a shelter engineer, warehousing costs, and vehicle expenses to support staff operations. Monitoring and accountability mechanisms will document lessons learned to inform future shelter interventions.By building technical capacity, promoting inclusive community engagement, and prioritizing safety and accessibility, this project not only addresses immediate shelter needs but also supports long-term resilience and gender equality. These efforts align with MSNA recommendations to provide stable shelter, enhance livelihoods, and ensure inclusive community planning while incorporating climate resilience and safety measures.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Save the Children enhances shelter resilience and community capacity to protect vulnerable populations, especially children. SC will impose its strict zero-tolerance policy on abuse underpins all its efforts, with the Centrality of Protection embedded in this shelter project and its capacity-building activities.Save the Children trains its project staff and workers to uphold protection standards, fostering local skills for safer shelter construction and maintenance. For example, the Shelter Engineer and Shelter Officer ensure all construction activities adhere to protection-sensitive guidelines, incorporating safe programming measures such as safety signs, first aid kits, and site safety protocols. Female construction workers will be provided with safety gear, including vests and gloves, to ensure their security and visibility during their work.Although the project will not involve volunteers or community participants, staff-led community engagement efforts are designed to gather insights through feedback and response mechanisms and focus group discussions with the project participants to improve protection-sensitive shelter planning and design. These activities contribute to fostering trust, transparency, and inclusivity in the shelter programming process.By collaborating with local and international stakeholders, SC advocates for policies that reinforce protection in shelter initiatives. Through capacity building, such as financial management training for women construction workers to promote economic empowerment, and strong accountability mechanisms, SC ensures shelter solutions safeguard the Rohingya refugees. Monitoring, accountability, and lesson-learned workshops further strengthen the project's alignment with protection principles, ensuring that all shelter activities prioritize safety, inclusion, and resilience in compliance with humanitarian standards.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project prioritizes age and disability inclusion by ensuring that all activities and facilities are designed to be accessible, meaningful, and participatory for individuals of diverse ages and abilities. During the planning and implementation phases, targeted assessments will identify specific needs related to age and disability, ensuring these are addressed in shelter repair, construction efforts, and capacity-building activities.Key activities include accessibility audits of project sites to ensure shelters and related facilities are user-friendly for elderly individuals and people with disabilities. Input from community members, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities, will be actively sought through structured consultations and focus group discussions, allowing their perspectives to shape the design and implementation of interventions.Accessibility features such as ramps, wider doorways, and other assistive adaptations will be integrated into new shelter constructions where feasible. In training sessions for female construction workers, content will emphasize inclusive construction practices and provide examples of adjustments that improve access for individuals with mobility challenges.The project will also work closely with stakeholders to raise awareness about the importance of inclusivity, ensuring a safe and enabling environment for all community members. This approach embeds age and disability inclusion throughout the initiative, creating more resilient and equitable outcomes. SCI utilizes the Washington Group Questionnaire to identify and assess disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Save the Children (SC) ensures full Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) by transparently sharing project details, budgets, activities, and timelines with all participants. Open communication is prioritized to foster trust and engage the community in decision-making processes. In addition to the Feedback and Response Mechanism (FRM), SC utilizes Community Needs Assessments and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) to better understand and respond to local needs. Quality control is upheld by encouraging community participation through these tools, empowering beneficiaries to voice concerns or provide input. This approach ensures that community needs and perspectives are integral to shaping project outcomes.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

This project upholds Safe Programming principles and adheres to a "Do No Harm" policy, with a strong commitment to Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). All project activities are designed to safeguard the dignity and rights of the project participants. To ensure this commitment, the project includes PSEA training for staff, implementing partners, vendors, and third parties, equipping them to uphold PSEA standards throughout all implementation phases.Additionally, PSEA awareness sessions will be conducted for female construction workers and other identified groups involved in or impacted by shelter activities, ensuring their understanding of rights and reporting mechanisms. Tailored Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials will be developed and distributed to raise awareness of PSEA principles.Community consultations will also be conducted to better understand risks related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), the preferred communication channels for reporting, and to strengthen the Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM). These activities are integrated to create a safer environment for all involved, particularly female construction workers, ensuring their full participation in a respectful and protected setting.This comprehensive approach aligns with Save the Children's PSEA policy while addressing specific needs within the scope of this project.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project incorporates Climate Action by addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and environmental issues through climate change adaptation and mitigation strategies. Save the Children promotes sustainable practices by using environmentally friendly materials and constructing durable shelters that reduce the need for frequent repairs, thereby minimizing environmental impact. Tree planting activities contribute to reforestation efforts and help mitigate climate risks, while training sessions on DRR and DRM equip beneficiaries with the knowledge to prepare for and respond to climate-related disasters. These initiatives foster resilience within the community and encourage eco-friendly construction practices that align with climate adaptation and environmental preservation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project has embedded Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures. Save the Children (SC) has a comprehensive business continuity plan and maintains an emergency stockpile, including shelter materials and other essential Non-Food Items (NFIs), to respond to emergencies like cyclones and fires. Additionally, SCI provides training to beneficiaries on emergency response protocols, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to handle crisis situations effectively. The project also includes training on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and safe shelter construction practices, which enhances community resilience to disasters and strengthens local preparedness. These measures ensure the project's continuity and adaptability in the face of potential emergencies, aligning with SC's commitment to protecting vulnerable populations in crisis.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.
