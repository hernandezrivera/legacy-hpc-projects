Project Summary

In referring to needs, ATAA seeks to provide emergency and sustainable interventions to help the people in need in different locations in NS with coordination and collaboration with WASH, Health, Nutrition, Education and Protection clusters and acting organizations.
This project will target 174,507 BNFs (34900 HHs ) living in 14 camps and living in 20 communities in Idleb, Aleppo, Hasekeh and Raqqa governorates with WASH services, and Hygiene promotion activities in camps and communities as well as in schools and health care units.
As part of ATAA strategy to reduce the negative impacts of water scarcity on most affected population in NWS and to contribute to decreasing the AWD/Cholera epidemic, ATAA will work, design, and implement different activities on using both approaches
1 - Lifesaving approaches
2 - Sustainable approaches
As for the lifesaving interventions, ATAA will continue support the camps and IDP informal settlements with WASH lifesaving activities including Water trucking - SWM - Desludging and HYG Promotion.
In terms of sustainable solutions, and in coordination with ERL Cluster, ATAA targets communities, camps, and residential communities to create a dignified living environment, including the extension/construction of sewer networks - extension/construction of drinking water networks, including the rehabilitation of water stations suspended and the provision of operating systems or necessary operating costs of Fuel, chlorine and periodic maintenance. Furthermore, ATAA will make sure to merge the solarization in its intervention in order to contribute to decrease the climate change effects reduction and create sustainable access to the provided service.
This includes as well WASH intervention in Schools and health facilities to overcome the epidemic of cholera and other diseases and create better and healthy environment for children and underserved groups of BNFs.
ATAA, as part of its strategy to reduce the negative impacts of open and unsafe wastewater on the environment and society as well as the scattered and random solid waste land fills and to help combat water scarcity, it will work, design, and implement wastewater treatment interventions for re-using in agricultural purposes or safe disposal in public networks in collaboration with FSL and ERL Clusters. Additionally, the design and implementation of adequate landfills will be at the core of the planned interventions of ATAA WASH team.
ATAA plans to implement these interventions in collaboration with other sectors to ensure integrated intervention and reduce the spread of epidemics and cholera and raise awareness of the community and the health of beneficiaries.
During the design and implementation of those activities in the mentioned sites, ATAA will conduct community engagement session to the residents targeting all groups of people and establishments WASH community representative committees in order to have better design and implementation of activities.

3 - Needs

According to WoS HSN issued by UNICEF 2022, water scarcity and electricity shortages remain a key constraint to water supply systems across the country, along with a reduced flow of the Euphrates River, which has also diminished the hydroelectric potential of the dams which make the water supply challenging for hundreds of thousands of people (especially thus living in camps). Climate change, droughts and aquifer overexploitation only exacerbate the situation.Impact of the Water Crisis continue into 2023 impacting on hundreds of water supply stations creating outbreak of water-borne diseases.WASH infrastructures require significant support and substantial investments to keep providing a minimum level of services.Based on HRP 2022/23, Up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water to meet or complement their water needs, at least 70% of sewage being discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems not functional, over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency. In both rural and urban communities.With ongoing cholera outbreak, COVID-19 and higher malnutrition rates affecting especially children and PLW, appropriate WASH and IPC in (HCF), and nutrition clinics and general hygiene promotion are critical. WASH in School needs are growing – 56% of students complained about WASH-related issues - with poor hygiene conditions affecting overall enrolment and especially girls’ attendance.

4 - Implementation of activities

The activities are divided into:1- life-saving services in camps and camp-like sites and will include: Water trucking with monitoring and chlorination in Camps, mobile clinics and schools - tanks distribution - Construction of communal latrines - Providing access for PWDs in to WASH' facilities through installation of special arrangements - Solid waste management and transfer the waste to safe landfill - Desludging septic tanks In camps – Its worth mentioning that the linked septic tank to the communal latrines will be designed and implemented in a way that assures primary treatment for the wastewater is achieved. Hence the treated wastewater can be discharged to public sewage systems or seep to ground safely.The intervention will include as well Hygiene promotion sessions with increasing health awareness especially in schools with children as well as Distribution of Hygiene/cholera kits taking consideration of instructions from the WASH Cluster in terms of the type of basket and the number of rounds of distribution.2- sustainable approach which includes the following:-Rehabilitation/expansion of old water supply drinking water systems & sewage networks -maintenance and operation of pumping stations -Emerging Solarization in power supply for pumping stations-establishment of waste water treatment plants/mechanisms to be integrated with food security sector- SWM (landfills) In both directions, the community engagement is essential for exit strategy & accountability

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will got orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.
