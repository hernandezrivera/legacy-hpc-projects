Project Summary

The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs.
The impact of Syria’s crisis continues to hinder people’s ability to meet their immediate food needs and livelihoods, in a context marked by very complex drivers and contributing factors. This includes economic worsening and weakened local currency, soaring food and non-food prices, as well as drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity, and limited energy supply. In addition, food and agriculture commodities prices increase, inflation, lack of purchasing power due to limited livelihood and lack of income sources, population movements that show no sign of abating, and the Ukraine crisis have had an important economic impact on markets and commodities cost. Oxfam assessments also confirm the above-mentioned information and the findings show the high need for rehabilitation of basic infrastructure ( bakeries, Mill, Market-related agriculture activities), and support local community in agriculture inputs, and appropriate training Oxfam has very experienced and competent staff to implement the rehabilitation of basic infrastructure, increase their skills, and provide needed capacity building for the target group to enable an increase in their food security in additional distribution of agricultural inputs, and linking between target beneficiaries and the Market, based on market assessment and value chain assessment for agriculture goods. risk assessment will be done before the start of the project to understand the beneficiary's preferences, modality, delivery mechanism, and potential risk and mitigation measures.
The planned activities will include: Farmer field schools and pastural field schools, distribution of agricultural inputs and climate smart inputs, in-kind modality or cash, and the rehabilitation of irrigation infrastructure with traditional equipment and renewable energy based on technical assessments, and the rehabilitation of irrigation canals in addition to providing communal mobile solar systems. Moreover, Oxfam will rehabilitate market infrastructure related to agricultural value chain and will provide trainings will all these interventions to the relevant stakeholders.
It will further adopt a participatory community engagement approach to ensure the active participation of the target people enhance inclusion and reduce exclusion errors.
A total of 49,552 HH households will be benefited from this intervention and 25% of them are female-headed households.

The action will conduct price monitoring of the basic items in the critical market , and climate change impact to understand the changes of price trends and adjust the aid , and type of intervention accordingly. DDP, Post distribution monitoring , and evaluate the applicaants practice will be conducted to measure the impact and beneficiaries' satisfaction. (continued in extended project summary)

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Oxfam through this action has planned to enable the vulnerable and affected families in the targeted subdistricts to have access to essential basic needs, and production inputs with the required skills especially in the rural areas where the livelihood diversification is less than in urban, and agricultural activities consider the main activities. Oxfam will ensure coordination among various partners operating in the same geographical setting to reduce duplication but rather enhance integration and complementarities. •Oxfam will adopt a community engagement approach to enhance the active participation of the target communities in the entire cycle of the action and also to develop the vulnerability and eligibility criteria for the selection process (Beneficiary-centered approach). •SEA triggered by unequal power dynamics between e.g. staff responsible for applyingbeneficiary selection and communities in need of assistance: Oxfam’s PSEA and ChildSafeguarding policy; safeguarding focal points in all hubs; training and refreshers onthe topic delivered to staff and partners; CFM; reporting lines in place; Manager’sresponsibility to secure a working environment conducive to reporting SEA-relatedconcerns; multi-layer verification of the list of selected beneficiaries against Oxfam’svulnerability criteria and MoVs, in-house capacity to operationalize Oxfam’s reportingand investigation routine. Risks: - Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions and transactions delays prevent the implementation of project activities. - The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds, or not enough funds to fulfil commitments and project objectives. - Potential of fraud and aid diversion, amongst Oxfam staff, suppliers and partners - COVID19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities and of humanitarian workers delivering assistance. - Assumptions: - No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. the local authorities will allocate enough production inputs for the rehabilitated bakeries. the community will have the capacity to manage the work in the rehabilitated bakeries sufficiently. The social norms and traditions will enable women to participate in the communities’ decision-making process. Mitigation: Oxfam will perform rapid market assessment to check the availability of the required items and their prices. All Oxfam and partner’s staff will be inducted on Oxfam’s standards on safeguarding and accountability. Very clear accountability mechanism will be put in place prior to the implementation. Oxfam will go for NCEs to absorb the surpluses that it might have as a result to an increase in the exchange rate.
