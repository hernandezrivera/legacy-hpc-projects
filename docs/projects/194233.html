Project Summary

Organization for Peace, Relief and Development (OPRD) is proposing a project to provide equitable, accessible, inclusive and essential support to the Education Sector in Greater Kapoeta, Eastern Equatoria state in South Sudan, with particular emphasis on girls and boys of school going age. It aims to contribute to removing barriers that inhibit the education of girls and boys in primary school level. The purpose of the project is to improve enrolment, retention, and completion rates as well as levels of learning achievements of girls and boys in primary schools in the Greater Kapoeta.
The project aims to achieve these three key objectives: 1) to increase access to equitable, inclusive and safe learning environment for girls and boys in Greater Kapoeta; 2) to Strengthen the capacity of state ministries of education, head-teachers and school communities on school governance; 3) to Enhance household and community awareness on the value and importance of education. These objectives align with the education cluster and MoGEI strategic objectives.
The project will deliver the following outputs: 1) mobilize communities for girls’ and boys’ education through the behavior change strategy; 2) facilitate and support the delivery of learning materials ECD to primary schools; 3) improve the quality of education and resilience in the primary schools through: provision of teacher training and school based support to SMCs, PTAs, Payam education supervisors and county education departments on all aspects of school governance and school supervision; provision of school based training for head teachers and teachers according to the teachers professional development strategy; provision of training to peer mentors, school mothers and selected teachers to organize annual career-fairs and set-up network of female role-models; setting up Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS); establish and support girl mentoring and learning groups in primary schools; 4) provision of sanitation and hygiene support (gender segregated) support to girls education. The project will also build the capacity of state ministries of education on school/education governance.

1 - Needs

Education is not only a fundamental right, it is essential for development and productive living. Research and evidence shows that investing in education is an effective way to not only improve and hasten development as well as breaking the intergenerational cycle of malnutrition, hunger and poverty.Following the impact of conflict and COVID-19 that resulted to destruction and closure of schools, disruption of learning, displacement of teachers, insufficient learning materials which contributed to increased drop-out rates, psychosocial trauma and poor learning outcomes for children and youth, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship has exacerbated the existing education challenges and inequities. According to UN-OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), in 2022, 3.6 million people, 97% of whom are children, are estimated to need education services. In addition, about 139,000 refugee children also need education support. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. In May 2021, after 14 months of school closure due to COVID-19, schools in South Sudan re-opened. The current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million. Additionally, gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7% between 2020.As recurrent insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality, the active conflict and recurring communal violence in Eastern Equatoria state throughout 2021 led to school closures. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities, as well as destruction or occupation of school premises and are also barriers to access education facilities. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as impediment for learner's access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. NBeG state has been the worst impacted by the floods, and officials in this state reported that some schools were closed due to flooding. In light of the aforementioned, it is estimated that 3.6 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict- and crisis-affected areas including Greater Kapoeta state will have inadequate or no access to education in 2022 (HNO), including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. In addition, some 139,000 refugee children will need education support in 2022. Increasing climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel, and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. Needs are particularly dire and may worsen in Eastern Equatoria state.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

OPRD has feedback mechanisms in place, with messaging to be conducted in the local dialects, using megaphones and community radios. Several channels will be in place to collect maximum feedback from the stakeholders including interviews with KIs, focus group discussions, suggestion boxes, the establishment of hotlines and PDM exercises. PWDs will be attended to specially, as well as the elderly. The project has created a feedback and complaints mechanism that will cater for the interest and challenges faced by the illiterate segments of people in the target locations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

OPRD has AAP protocols clearly stated and understood by all stakeholders; clear feedback mechanisms in place, AAP focal person present within the organization. Feedback desks will be installed at the schools targeted. The location of the complaints and feedback desks in the communities will be discussed at the inception meeting, as well as the point of contact for complaints should community leaders wish to report them directly. Complaints and feedback signage will be prominently displayed throughout the communities. Copies of the project agreement will be disseminated at the inception meeting and available upon request throughout the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Staff training and consent of the PSEA and Do No harm protocols in the organization; clear referral mechanisms, clear feedback pathways; profiling of key stakeholders to eliminate previous offenders. All current and recruited staff of OPRD who will be involve in this HRP Project will be briefed on the OPRD, Code of Conduct, Child Safe Guarding Code of conduct, and SEA Code of conducts and to sign said codes.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be used throughout the programming cycle to make adjustments and adaptations to activities and services. Feedback will also be used as part of ‘upwards’ accountability (between OPRD and donors), where information from beneficiaries will add to the evidence base. This can be a one-way, extractive process, or will involve some form of two-way interaction through, e.g. validation and dissemination of results. Beneficiary feedback will be used for ‘downwards’ accountability, i.e. between OPRD and target the groups or populations in the target locations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

OPRD field staff will provide awareness sessions on humanitarian principles and OPRD’s code of conduct especially focusing on the prohibition of sexual exploitation and abuse amongst the beneficiaries and community at large. Beneficiaries will be made fully aware on where to submit gender related concerns and issues through the complaints and feedback mechanisms such as suggestion box and complaints desk for support and referral. To mitigate against the risk of beneficiaries not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, OPRD will employ a range of communication modalities which will be made available -as relevant and appropriate- to targeted communities, ensuring communication channels are easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as project resources and beneficiary vulnerabilities and preferences. While the most commonly used modalities are complaints desks (including dedicated accountability staff), phone lines and direct engagement with office and M&E staff in the field as possible channels. Multiple communication means ensure that vulnerable people – especially those with limited mobility such as the elderly and people with disabilities – are able to provide feedback, while guaranteeing total confidentiality of information shared. To avoid risks associated with unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. OPRD already have data protection and safeguarding procedures that will be observed together with the following ethical standards or safeguards in data collection and information storage, pertaining to beneficiaries and other stakeholders: 1. Ensuring the information gathering activity is necessary and justified. 2. Adoption of informed consent and voluntary participation procedures, including written and oral consent. 3. Adopting appropriate confidentiality procedures sensitive to the needs of the target groups and more specifically children, in case they are also participating • Being sensible and prepared in terms of understanding and being mindful of cultural, religious, gender and other significant considerations within the communities in planning, conducting and reporting findings. 4. Ensuring that all staff and external parties contracted to undertake specific M&E tasks are adequately briefed of the above ethical issues and they sign to indicate willingness to adhere and be held accountable in case of a breach. 5. Ensuring that all enumerators are carefully trained in data protection and all internal protocols are closely followed to mitigate against risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries
