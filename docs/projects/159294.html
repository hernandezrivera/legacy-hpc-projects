Project Summary

This proposal is in-line with the HRP 2020 emerging needs, objectives and framework under the strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Further, this project is also aligned with the WASH cluster HRP objectives as follows: (1) Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, (2) Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks.
The overall objective: Contribute to strengthening WASH emergency preparedness and crisis response for the most vulnerable population in Gaza, while the project include the specific objectives: (1) To provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for vulnerable communities and HHs affected by the Gaza March of Return including women, children, girls and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities. (2) Strengthen capacity of WASH actors to ensure well coordination and effective emergency response.

1 - Needs

The aftermath of the 2014 war on Gaza has been characterized by a deteriorating humanitarian situation exacerbated by the ongoing Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations resulting in high numbers of casualties. Excessive use of force by Israel during the GMR deepened vulnerabilities at the household level. The large number of casualties of unarmed Palestinians including women, the use of live-ammunition and the impact of tear gas canisters all raise concerns of excessive use of force. Based on OCHA Humanitarian snapshot produced in July 2019, the total Number of casualties in Gaza and Gaza North is 15,581 individuals. According to the National Society for Rehabilitation (NSR), there are 51 persons with disabilities (PwDs) in Gaza Governorate and 45 PwDs in North Gaza Governorate (20% are females) who suffer from different types of disabilities such as amputation, longitudinal paralysis, lower paralysis, quadriplegia, muscle weakness, etc. The Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) has witnessed a deterioration of the WASH infrastructures, mainly in public health centers (HC). According to the 2019 Global Baseline report on WASH in HC facilities, the baseline showed that 26% of HC facilities lacked basic water services, while 21% have no sanitation service. In June 2019, through Oxfam, the WASH cluster updated its contingency plan and Standard Operational Procedures (soPs). The process of updating the WASH cluster contingency plan was initiated by assessing the capacity of WASH actors to identify the needs and gaps of emergency preparedness and response. As a result, the updated contingency plan identified the minimum preparedness actions that need to be addressed by WASH cluster to fill the gaps in the contingency plan. Based on this context, Oxfam and the (PHG), in coordination with the disability WG, carried out rapid need assessment (40 HHs visits, 4 FGDs & two KII) in Sep 2019 to identify people affected by the GMR in Gaza and North Gaza governorates & their needs. According to the assessment, at least 70 out of the 96 have a long-term physical disability (20% are females). The main need of those affected people was the adaptation of their HH’s WASH facilities to meet the needs of people with disabilities including women. This activity will be conducted based on the experience of Oxfam working on designated emergency shelters in similar interventions at HH’s level in 2017 and by incorporating its local and global experience related to WASH, hygiene promotion, gender and disability inclusion.Moreover, In August 2019, Oxfam and MA’AN agreed with the Ministry of Health in coordination with the Islamic relief, WASH cluster’s members and GVC to conduct in-depth assessments of three HC centres located in vulnerable neighbourhoods close to the borders (Sabha Al Aharzeen and AL Sourani located in Al Shujaia and Horria HC Center in Al Tofah locality), who suffer from shortages of domestic and drinking water and who provide health services to the casualties of GMR. The assessment showed a need to upgrade the water networks, replace water and sanitation items, construct a water well to ensure sustainable water flow for the unit and install a PV Solar system to operate the desalination plant and the well in addition to carry out of community engagement activities to strengthen the preparedness capacity at the community level. Furthermore, based on the findings of the updated WASH cluster contingency plan, it was recommended in June 2019 by the WASH cluster members to have clear and specific communication mechanism to connect and identify the list of local representatives (male and female) of the 178 localities with the WASH area focal points throughout the Gaza Strip, in addition to build the capacity of the identified representatives and service providers for using the developed rapid need assessment tools and mobile application for effective information management, monitoring and reporting.

2 - Activities

The action will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The do-no-harm principle and the main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration. Explicit plans to reduce risks, and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted and refreshed. Oxfam will be responsible for the implementation of the action in coordination with PHG and MA’AN with relevant stakeholders to ensure access to systems, procedures and standards are in place. Oxfam’s particular added value will be in its ability to bring together multiple stakeholders, local authorities, end users through its active participation in the WASH cluster and engagement with related stakeholders. In addition, Oxfam will provide advice and support on gender and protection sensitive project implementation and gender mainstreaming, will support the PHG and MA’AN in designing and implementing the gender and hygiene promotion plan. The PHG and MA'AN will lead the field implementation, the community mobilization actions, the beneficiary's selection process, and the engineering technical assessment. 3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive collaborative and effective, results-focused and accountable MEL and social accountability practices throughout project implementation. Learning and feedback will also be key practices that will be an integral part of the MEAL framework and will be documented by Oxfam in close coordination with implementing partners. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies are applied. Gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues will be monitored during the project implementation ensuring gender sensitive project reviews and evaluations. Monitoring tools, data gathering analysis, and reporting will encompass qualitative and quantitative gender and age disaggregation and analysis. This will help in tracking performance against indicators, implementation plan. Monitoring tools such as baselines and endlines. key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, Focus group discussions (FGDs) and others will be included as relevant. In OPT, Oxfam developed a comprehensive Feedback, Complaint, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) under its Saving Lives Program (including under this project) and will be piloting a mobile case management tool to capture real-time data from the field through use of Information Communication Technologies (ICT). The FCRM ensures access of women and men to submit their feedback with safety and privacy. Feedback may be received either online, verbally or through hotlines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The system has an effective referral system by which the submitted feedback referred to relevant program manager based on the selected category by the reporter. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH need which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. technical assessment will be conducted in a way that takes into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on and enhances the roles each member of the household fulfills and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

On a community level, local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks at community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting orientation sessions to inform targeted communities about services that will be provided as well as on safety measures and access modalities. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing on the participation of women in the project’s committee as well as further exploring women and disabled people needs and will ensure safe exit for the project through providing more sustainable solutions for the identified needs and the proposed intervention. Following inclusive approach which ensure the targeted women, men, PWDs and other vulnerable groups is the main strategic WoW that Oxfam use to ensure sustainability.
