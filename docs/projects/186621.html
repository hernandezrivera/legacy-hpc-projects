Project Summary

Street Child will enhance the protection of 40,000 GBV survivors, particularly children, through strengthening the delivery of and access to quality GBV preventive and response services. This holistic protection package will cover community awareness, MHPSS, GBV case management, and livelihood support. The project will focus on child and adolescent survivors (70% girls) and aims to provide a package of support activities to beneficiaries that facilitates psychosocial recovery and reintegration.
In addition to direct service provision, the support package will be localised with capacity building provided for local actors to replicate GBV prevention and response services. It will also implement awareness activities by engaging community stakeholder groups, building knowledge of GBV risks and prevention-response strategies at the local level.
Within the scope of this project, the following LGAs have been identified for intervention:
Adamawa LGAs: Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South
Borno LGAs: MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Hawul, Mobbar, Dikwa, Konduga
Yobe LGAs: Gujba, Gulani

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in northeast Nigeria is primarily a protection crisis, which has exposed civilians to violence, exploitation and abuse, including gender-based violence. Gender-based violence is widespread, and has been aggravated by displacement, limited access to services, disruption of livelihoods as well as harmful socio-cultural practices. The 2021 Nigeria HRP identifies 1.5 million people in need of GBV assistance in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States with the 81% being women (24%) and girls (57%). In the northeast, female-headed households and adolescent girls are at heightened vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse – for instance survival or transactional sex, early marriage and childbirth, and forced prostitution. Women and girls have also been abducted and forcibly recruited by armed groups and subjected to forced marriages and rape. Women and children formerly associated with armed groups are furthermore exposed to social stigmatisation upon leaving armed groups, which further heightens vulnerability to GBV concerns as these individuals struggle to rebuild livelihoods and reintegrate into the community. Unaccompanied and street-connected children (UASC), who often are without social protection mechanisms, are also at heightened risk. Despite the particular vulnerability of women and girls, it is important to note that all population groups - girls, boys, women and men from host community, IDP and returnee communities are at-risk and in need of support. All forms of GBV, experienced by all types of survivors, are chronically under-reported due to stigmatisation and marginalisation as well as the lack of effective reporting and response mechanisms. Hence the importance of awareness creation and community engagement that encourages prevention and access for support for victims of GBV. There are significant gaps in the humanitarian response to GBV. There is a general lack of GBV services available, and where services are available the quality (including making of timely referrals and follow-up of referrals made) is often low. Coordination between different partners tends to be weak, and there is also a need to strengthen coordination at the LGA level by building the capacity of community actors and stakeholders as well as local partners. Borno State is the most affected; the GBV needs are categorised as catastrophic in the LGAs of Monguno, Mobbar, Damboa, Biu, Gwoza, Dikwa, Konduga, Jere and MMC. In Yobe, the most severe needs are in the LGAs of Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the most severe needs are in the LGAs of Michika, Madagali, Mubi North and Mubi South.

2 - Activities

Street Child will provide immediate psychological first aid and material assistance, including dignity kits, to 15,000 children affected by GBV, including children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). 15,000 survivors of GBV will also be provided with case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up. This will include mental health and psychosocial support by trained psychologists, and referral for other specialised services (such as medical and justice services) where appropriate. Survivors will also be supported with livelihoods support through a tailored package of vocational and business skills training, mentoring and cash grants to enable the survivors to access sustainable income-generating activities and promote their socio-economic reintegration. At the community level, Street Child will also build community-based protection and response mechanisms. This will include awareness raising activities and training of community-based committees on GBV prevention and response principles. Community-based GBV monitoring and support groups will be established as forums for peer support and mentoring, empowering community members to facilitate awareness-raising, prevention and response activities. These will also include establishing men's groups against GBV in order to engage men in combatting GBV and violence against women and challenging harmful gender attitudes. Through all activities, Street Child will seek to address the limited institutional capacity of national GBV partners and to improve the quality of service provision. Street Child is committed to localisation and works exclusively through local partners to implement project activities in addition to providing tailored mentoring and capacity building to strengthen technical and organisational capacity. The focus will be on building the capacity of local partners and stakeholders to respond to GBV in line with the inter-agency minimum standards for GBV in emergencies programming. Capacity building will also seek to improve coordination at the local level, including through the mapping of referral pathways, with the aim of improving the facilitation of timely care, intersectoral referrals and timely follow-up.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The prioritisation of protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising unintended negative impacts ensure that the communities where Street Child works can access meaningful, dignified support. Street Child is committed to centring and mainstreaming protection throughout its activities and projects, and has pre-emptively identified potential protection risks within the project as well as taking active steps to integrate mitigation and management strategies. Protection reporting, response and prevention mechanisms are integrated into the design, and protection mainstreaming indicators are included within the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we work and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff, partner staff, and project-affiliated community members such as volunteers and committee members receive comprehensive training of the Street Child safeguarding policy. This comprehensive approach, which is also intended to be rolled out to vendors, ensures that all actors within SC projects are operating under the same rules and procedures and creates a harmonised safeguarding environment. In addition to training key community members on the Street Child safeguarding policy, wider community outreach and sensitisation is carried out via hosting of community forums and meetings, or use of visual aids including posters and murals which offer accessible and culturally relevant and appropriate key messages to empower community members on understanding and reporting concerns. Street Child is also a member of the PSEA Working Group and the focal person representing the organisation is also the safeguarding focal person and is involved in this project. Any incident report will be also shared with the WG who request regular reports.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Proven feedback mechanisms, which are already widely used and understood by stakeholders and beneficiaries in northeast Nigeria, will be utilised at all stages of the project cycle. At the planning stage, a needs assessment will be conducted in targeted communities and utilise focus group discussions (FGDs) to collect direct feedback from a diverse range of stakeholder constituencies, including gender, age, disability, and other group markers. FGDs will take place at both baseline and endline assessment, for community members to give feedback on the project life cycle and to allow SC to measure community satisfaction. In all target communities, a dedicated complaints mechanism officer (CRM) will be identified and made responsible for collection of feedback and coordinating the sharing and actioning of feedback between Street Child, partners, and community members. The CRM officer will hold weekly helpdesk sessions in each community for any beneficiaries who are unable or unwilling to use other feedback mechanisms. A complaints register, featuring details of complaint and actions taken, will be maintained. Outside of individual feedback, Street Child or project partner staff will attend weekly coordination meetings with community coalitions and structures, as far as possible with an equal representation of women and men, to collect feedback and share progress relating to specific child protection concerns. For community members who want to give feedback anonymously, anonymous complaint boxes will be placed in each community and checked weekly. A toll-free line will also be advertised where community members can call at any time to provide feedback or share urgent concerns, for instance acute safeguarding concerns. All feedback received will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.
