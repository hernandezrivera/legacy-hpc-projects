Project Summary

Supporting the health system in Al-Hasakeh governorate, Al-Hasakeh district, Areesheh sub-district, Abdan community, by activating a comprehensive primary health center (Abdan health facility) and linking it to a mobile clinic, this intervention will improve the disease surveillance in the area by reporting in a timely manner to the Early Warning Alert and Response Network and to the Health WG.
The CPHC center will be located in a sub-district with reduced access to a secondary care facility and cover up to 150.000 beneficiaries as a catchment area.
The proposed facility is located in a vital place because all emergency cases, infectious diseases, chronic diseases, and surgical cases in the area have to travel for more than 70 KM due to the absence of a nearby high-level care center, also, the proposed facility is close to several informal settlements and collective centers in the area, where a mobile clinic could be operating.
In addition to the daily outpatient services of the standard EPHC packages, 24/7 basic obstetric, neonatal, and abortion care will be provided. Normal deliveries will be performed, minor surgery and first aid, along with the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) as it will be also part of the static and mobile clinic.
Extended diagnostic, laboratory, and clinical services will be provided through specialist clinics, such as gynecology, pediatrics, and internal medicine.
Patients with perinatal complications requiring Caesarean Sections, blood transfusions, neonatal intensive care will be referred to the district hospitals (135 km south or to 70 km north).
Within the CPHC and the linked mobile clinic, trained Community Health Workers (CHWs) will support mothers and caregivers in appropriate feeding their infants including education and support for re-lactation (a focus on infant care, including skin-to-skin, encourage early and optimal breastfeeding) and will do MUAC screening in the house visits for children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW).

4 - Needs

12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years – including an estimated 503,000 live births expected, and 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years). Al-Hasakeh governorate is one of the most 4 governorates that showed greater barriers, costs, and service non-availability as compared to other areas of Syria, while dense urban settings, crowded and overburdened areas, as well as last resort sites including camps and camp-like settings, continue to be at particular risk of poor health outcomes due to shortages in water and hygiene supplies, risk of communicable disease and the likelihood of overcrowding and long waiting times at health facilities. The health impacts related to access to water supply networks are well studied and should be seen as direct contributors to the fact that Increases in waterborne diseases are a reality in 2021, have more than doubled since last year.The water crisis in northern Syria has triggered significant increases in waterborne diseases and disease vectors, particularly in parts of northeast Syria. The increasing rates of acute malnutrition in NES are not being addressed due to a lack of coverage in nutrition services available by the current humanitarian response. Sources:HNO 2022

5 - Implementation of activities

After coordination with the local health authority and the Health WG for NES, medical and admin staff will be recruited, and a plan will be drawn up to train the cadres and raise their capabilities. The necessary equipment to carry out the activities will be purchased, including non-medical equipment such as a power generator to provide 24-hour power, a medical warehouse will also be equipped with the necessary medicines and consumables, in addition to activating a laboratory, an incinerator will be built after training the medical staff on health waste management.The center will provide primary healthcare services within 3 clinics (internal medicine - gynecology - pediatric) with a 24/7 emergency room, a room will be prepared for labor and natural birth, and an effective referral system will be equipped with an ambulance to refer cases that need emergency surgical work. Health education services through CHWs, where there will be a mobile clinic linked to the center that provides diagnostic, curative, and preventive services for IDPs and hosts in hard-to-reach areas.Mental health services will also be a part, as a general practitioner with a psychological counselor will be trained on the mhGAP to provide diagnostic and therapeutic services.
