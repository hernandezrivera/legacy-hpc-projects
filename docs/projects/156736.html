Project Summary

Now entering its eighth year, the humanitarian crisis in Syria continues to deteriorate with a staggering 13.5 million people, including 5.8 million children, in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. The infrastructure in the country is destroyed and the economy is in ruins after seven years of conflict.. According to the FAO’s nationwide assessment , agriculture was the most important economic sector in Syria before the crisis and is still the second largest contributor to the GDP (26%) despite the war. The agricultural sector represents a critical safety net for roughly 6.7 million Syrians . However, as a consequence of the war, there has been a loss in food production of US$16 billion as well as a dramatic reduction in quantity and quality of food intake. As a corollary, negative coping mechanisms, such as selling livestock and productive assets and resorting to child labor, are being used to compensate for the loss of income and to meet basic needs.
In Afrin, income from livestock used to contribute significantly to livelihood of farmers and farm breeders. Farmers and breeders account for nearly 40% of Afrin's workforce, where large number of families and rural households have experience in livestock and poultry, contributing significantly to their livelihood.
Accordingly, The project aims at minimizing the most pressing problems limiting livestock development in Afrin through distribute livestock, sheep in particular, for breeders, plus providing veterinary services through a roaming clinic in the villages where the people work in livestock breeding. The project also aims to implement a training program that allows to develop skills related to the production.
In an attempt to contribute towards the response to the contextual situation in Afrin, by improving income generation and food security of targeted households while also benefiting the surrounding community; Olive Branch proposes providing 80 vulnerable-conflict-affected households with a comprehensive package of materials that will enable them to restart their livelihoods, along with establishing a vet clinic to provide one-on-one consultations to direct and indirect project beneficiaries as well as building the awareness across the general population of the district.
The project builds on the proven track record and experience of Olive Branch. Since 2012 Olive Branch accumulated considerable experience through the implementation of many livestock projects in different locations in Syria.

Needs

After eight years of conflict, livestock sector suffered from severe deterioration. As number of livestock decreased significantly in whole Syria. That was directly reflected not only on breeders who used to get income from their livestock but also on the domestic market.Livestock is one of the fastest growing sectors. It is part of the food and economic safety net for farmers and livestock breeders in Syria, It also represents a job opportunity for many rural residents. It provides the community with meat, milk products, eggs, leather and natural fertilizers. Livestock is facing harsh conditions as a result of the ongoing conflict. Air strikes, military operations and high prices, in addition to many diseases without proper medication, and many other reasons have led to significant losses in the sector. According to FAO report in 2016, "some breeders were forced to abandon part of their flocks and move to neighboring countries after livestock were exported. The number of cattle decreased by 30 percent, sheep and goats by 40 percent”.Furthermore, FAO issued a report warned of the "seriousness of the continuation of the agricultural crisis in Syria, stressing that the losses of agriculture reached 16 billion dollars in six years." The international organization confirmed that Syrian food production reached the lowest level, while half the population of Syria can not meet their daily food needs.Afrin district is located in Aleppo governorate to the north east area of Syria. The total population is over (270,000). The area has witnessed many changes and challenges such as military actions and large waves of displacement. Afrin is considered one of the richest agricultural areas in Syria. Its fertile soil and its moderate climate made it an agricultural area with distinction. Due to the availability of all its agricultural components and its various types and varieties of trees (olives, citrus, apples, forest trees) Horseradish, etc.), and grain (wheat and barley), in addition to the diversity of livestock, especially sheep, cows and bees. This diversity in agricultural and animal products have earned the region the signs of self-reliance on providing food and sufficiency Self before the war. Since statistics show that animal production reaches about 40% of the agricultural sector in Afrin in the prewar period. But as mentioned previously, the war has broken this equation, and now these figures have declined more than 40% due to the war and its reflection on all aspects of life. The military operations along with insecurity situations that took place last year in Afrin, prevented breeders from reaching grazing areas to feed their animals. That was combined with high prices due to loss of materials such as hay and medicines. The chaos also increased theft, including cattle herds. As a result of that Afrin lost huge number of livestock in 2018. Breeders suffer from lack of resources and poor incomes.Olive Branch suggests a logic intervention in order to provide the needed assistance to the vulnerable breeders to help them get back their works, and promote the livestock sector in Afrin from another hand. The proposed activities will significantly improve the livestock health in the area through Vet Clinic and will support 80 household to get jobs and income through providing sheep and fodder.

Implementation of activities

Livestock Distribution:The project would provide 80 families with 6 sheep for each. With the sheep ownership belonging to the beneficiary after the end of program (given that the beneficiary proved his/her competency in retaining ownership by abiding to basic minimum requirements set out in the signed agreement between him/her and OB at the beginning of the project).Fodder Distribution:The project would provide 80 families with fodder support. Each beneficiary family would receive 600 Kg of fodder for the sheep, on a monthly basis, for a duration of 3 months. This is sufficient to sustain the nutritional need of the livestock throughout the project’s life. Later the beneficiary should have the required financial stability and purchasing power, as a result of the project, to self-sustain that need post-project life.Barn Rehabilitation:The project would offer a cash grant, worth approximately 300 USD, for each beneficiary to help him/her build or rent a barn. This grant should be able to cover the affiliated costs of any rehabilitation work to set up the barn to host the cow in a suitable environment, as well as possibly rent a barn whenever necessary (based on the beneficiary’s need).Dairy Products’ Production Kit Distribution:The project will distribute a production kit to each household to help beneficiaries making dairy products such as yogurt, cheese and butter to maximise their profit from the produced cow Noting that, as per selection criteria, all the beneficiaries are all farmers whom have experience in raising farm animals and in producing dairy products, so they are familiar with the process of producing Laban & Labneh. If anyone of the 80 beneficiary family has any difficulty in using any of the tools within the kit, then they could refer to the veterinaries that are regularly doing round checks to the beneficiaries.The project, through the veterinary doctors and field staff, would encourage the beneficiaries to maximize their production cycle by investing in the production of other dairy products than Milk (Yogurt, Laban, Labneh etc…), as this would ensure that the beneficiary would sustain a reasonable level of income to be food secure throughout the year.Veterinary Services:The Project would establish a Mobile Vet Clinic to provide awareness and technical support to the beneficiaries and supervising the health situation through providing required vaccines. The veterinary centre would be staffed by three trained veterinarian per the following specializations:1. Animal production specialist,2. Diagnosis and treatment specialist,3. Reproductive Care specialist.These vets Doctors will carry out essential surgeries on the livestock in Afrin The Vet clinic would also help the indirect beneficiaries in the community by offering medical consultations and surgeries to other animal breeders in the area, including for other types of livestock. Awareness Sessions:The veterinary staff would be conducting various awareness workshops and training sessions to the selected beneficiaries.
