Project Summary

On 25 August 2017, violence erupted in Rakhine State, Myanmar, triggering a massive refugee influx into Bangladesh, 728,000 refugees have since arrived, bringing the total refugee population to 921,000. Over the past year, humanitarian actors have sought to meet the basic and emergent needs of the refugee community, yet given the critical needs and lack of funding response activities have focused on short term solutions, leaving households still highly vulnerable as they dwell in weak shelters, lack sufficient clean water, relevant health services, and the prevalence of gender based violence (GBV) increases. The monsoon season lasting from May through September led to a deterioration of conditions, over 50,00 people were affected by landslides, windstorms, fires, waterlogging and flooding. There were 6,000 refugees that were displaced. There were substantial number of latrines that were damaged or required decommissioning, and also number of tube wells were damaged or found contaminated and GBV and protection risks increased. Weakened infrastructure further hampered mobility and access to services which disproportionately impacted the 75% of the refugee population that are women and children decreasing their ability to access required health and gender-based violence services.
Following the monsoon season, a year into this response, only 39% of the Join Response Plan for 2018 has been funded. Funding is particularly low for health (28%), WASH (27%) and protection and GBV (37%) programming. The lack of funding for these sectors have direct implications on the safety, security, wellbeing and health of women and children. The current Joint Reponses Plan for 2018 has identified the remaining gaps in maternal health, family planning, GBV response and prevention support, safe spaces for women and girls, clean water, sufficient latrines and effective hygiene promotion in order to minimize contamination and disease outbreaks. It also recognized that weak gender mainstreaming and referral mechanisms between health, GBV and WASH are undermining the efficacy and efficiency of available services. Based on the inter sector coordination groups strategies this project seeks to increase access for women and girls to life-saving reproductive health services, provide trauma and GBV response and prevention support, and enhance WASH infrastructure and hygiene awareness. By offering a integrated multi-sectoral project we seek to enhance links between services to leverage available resources for greater impact.CARE has operated in Bangladesh since 1949 and has established its expertise in GBV, WASH, health, and SRH. CARE’s thematic, technical and local experience enabled CARE to be a first responder within this crisis, beginning programming in September 2017. Over the past year, CARE has implemented over 30 programs. Based on assessed gaps, CARE’s programming has been centralized in camps 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 1 W. This project will seek to build on our efforts, the relationships established with the community and the momentum achieved to respond to the persistent needs in camps 12, 14, 15, and 16.By continuing our work on WASH, we can compound our efforts and achieve more substantial and substantive gains.
CARE Bangladesh places women and girls at the centre of its work. CARE aims to create an enabling environment that supports women and girls’ empowerment and leadership in humanitarian decision-making CARE`s response will aim to address specific needs of women and girls, and also men and boys throughout all stages of the WASH project activities by applying principles of the five gender minimum commitments in our programming. This assertion is based on recent 'Rapid Gender Assessment' that CARE conducted.

Needs

CARE has conducted a WASH survey in 1W, 1E, 11, 12, 13, 14,15 and 16. The results showed the continues need of water, sanitation and hygiene promotion. The findings were: • Drinking water and water for domestic use: 99% of households have access to hand pumps, 45% state they lack sufficient water for their household needs. The given reasons were distance (36%), limited volume (22%) and lack of sufficient containers (19%). • Household water treatment: 52% of the households reported to always treat water before drinking, 29% sometimes and 12% do not treat water collected. This has been a major concern; UNICEF's previous findings indicated that 75% of household samples are contaminated with faecal coliforms and nearly 35% with E. coli. • Hand washing: 48%+ people have knowledge of proper hand washing practices (after defecation, before cooking and eating). Only 27% mentioned before breast-feeding, and 12% before feeding babies.• Hygiene Promotion: 55% of the respondents participated in the community meeting on health and hygiene issues, 61% of them women and 49% men. 68% would prefer a home visit by the health workers.• Sanitation 72% of households use communal latrines for defecation; the remaining openly defecate. 92% of latrines are being used. 98% of respondents note insufficient lighting in latrines. While latrine usage is high, open defecation by children under 5 remains a concern requiring promotion campaigns to mothers.• Menstrual Hygiene: FGDs with women reflected a good understanding of menstrual hygiene management and practice. A follow-up FDG was conducted by CARE to speak with women who expressed difficulties in washing their reusable clothes in public or at home. CARE has started to work with host communities and will still continue in 2019. On various visits of CARE and in facilitation of focus group discussions, CARE has indentified their needs such as lack of water supply due to lowering and drying of shallow tube wells, open defecation is prevalent and they need proper sanitation, and most households behavior on appropriate hygiene practices needs to be improved.

Activities or outputs

The activities or outputs are the following : A. Water: Construction and repair and maintenance of WASH facilities. Sanitation: 1. Construction and repair and maintenance of latrine facilities. 2. Installation of hand washing facilities. 3. De-sludging of existing latrines 4. Construction and maintenance desludging pit. B. Hygiene promotion: Community meetings, household visits, training and capacity building of hygiene promoters, menstrual hygiene promotion management campaign, distribution of hygiene kits. C. Contingency Planning and preparation for moonson season: Prepare contingency stocks, training on prevention and response for AWD outbreak. D. Formation of WASH Groups: 1. Training on operation and maintenance of WASH faclities 2. Provison of tools, materials and spare parts for repair of WASH facilities. : E. Integration with Gender: Women and girls are enabled to participate actively in all aspects of WASH. All CARE's projects are desgined in close consideration of 'do no harm' principles. In all stages of the project cycle re: assessment through M&E CARE will ensure that beneficiary's confidentiality, privacy and dignity are upheld. Beneficiaries will always be consulted and their views incorporated into the project designs. When siting locations for WASH facilities considerations will be given to specific protection related issues regarding distance, lighting, privacy and dignity of beneficiaries. No personal information will be collected from the beneficiaries without consent. In view of the wider application of 'do no harm' principles and to avoid any potential conflicts, CARE wil ensure all projects being planned cater for both refugees and nearby host communities.
