1 - Needs

Across different assessments, food is reported as one of the biggest unmet needs among conflict-affected HHs. The effects of COVID-19 prevention protocol –restrictions in movement, inflation, increases in prices of food staples, and low agricultural inputs– further strained HH capacity to meet immediate food needs. The October 2021 CH estimates that from June to August 2022 (peak lean season), 1,477,620 people will be in critical need of emergency assistance in Borno State with severe negative coping strategies. This Activity will help curb the trend by supporting HHs to obtain food assistance. Additionally, SC PDM and recent evaluations shows a decline in food security indicators among supported HHs.Furthermore, the 2021 HRP estimates that more than 2.1 million people need skills training and start-up packages. Livelihoods activities across the BAY states have significantly deteriorated, most livelihoods have not fully picked up from the effect of the pandemic (COVID-19), insecurity, and increase in the cost of production inputs. In the projected period, HHs in some states (Borno inclusive) are likely to resort to emergency coping strategies that may result in further collapse of livelihoods in the absence of conscious livelihood intervention stimulus packages. Findings from the MSNA conducted by SC (Sept.’21) in Borno State show that community members’ most pressing challenges are the limited availability of stable livelihoods and income-earning activities, and lack of financial support and start-up capital. The assessment recommends livelihood skills and vocational training programs with cash transfers for startup kits to vulnerable HHs to address challenges relating to low incomes and limited livelihoods.

2 - Activities

Restricted unconditional food distribution (e-voucher food redemption): To meet theimmediate food needs of conflict-affected HHs, the SFEG consortium will provideunconditional food assistance using a restricted electronic voucher to redeem at pre-+− Leaflet | © OpenStreetMapselected local vendors. The SFEG’s e-voucher system is cashless and offline, enabling HHsin locations without network coverage to access food items from nearby vendors. Thesystem allows the beneficiaries to shop at times and locations of their choosing, and usetheir voucher at once or across multiple occasions. - Multipurpose Cash: All new arrivalsthat meet the criteria for assistance will be provided MPCA. SC will provide HHs with cardsloaded with their MPCA value to cash out at designated agents, or make transfers to bankaccounts for beneficiaries with established accounts. HHs will be trained on the intendedpurpose of the cash transfer. - Economic Recovery & Market Systems (ERMS): SC provideslivelihoods options for HHs receiving food assistance. Provision of IGAs alongside foodassistance allows HHs to meet their food and non-food needs without relapsing into IPC 3or worse, or resorting to negative coping strategies, and reduces the need for humanitarianassistance over time.SC, with support from relevant government MDAs, will linkbeneficiaries in the new livelihoods restoration component to master trainers/artisans forapprenticeship programs with certification at the end of the program. SC will distributeIGA inputs through cash, using SC’s e-transfer service provider. - Financial Services: SC willsupport HH resilience and asset development by expanding access to financial servicesby creating a savings and credit base for HHs while building a safety net to fostereconomic growth and resiliency. n VSLAs, members of a self-selected group voluntarilyform a group and save money, through purchasing shares.SC will target HHs receivingIGA support, IYCF support group members and some non-program beneficiaries includingprevious IGA HHs, local residents (including returning IDPs and Host communitymembers), and the wider community. - Nutrition: The program will provide services tomothers, fathers, caregivers, and their children under five years of age, pregnant women,and adolescent girls to prevent and treat acute malnutrition in children 0-59 months.Children with acute malnutrition will be treated through community- and facility-basedpreventive and curative interventions for acute malnutrition. SC will scale up the MAMICare Pathway to leverage and build on existing support services to support small andnutritionally at-risk infants under 6 months and their mothers. The program will integrateits interventions both within the existing public primary health care (PHC) system andcommunity-based systems. SC will work closely with existing PHCs to identify at riskinfants and mothers through existing ANC/PNC/MCH services. On the other hand, SC willalso refer mothers and infants to ANC/PNC/MCH clinics to receive essential services likeimmunization. The program will be implemented jointly by SC and Greencode. Greencodewill lead the implementation of community-based MIYCN-E activities and communityfollow-up of beneficiaries and Tom Brown approach. SC will provide technical support andlead in Mass MUAC screening and referrals and the implementation of facility-basedMIYCN-E, inpatient (stabilization centers) and OTP services. - Agriculture: majority oftargeted HHs continue to live in IDP settlements with limited access to land to engage inmeaningful agriculture. To complement food assistance and improve dietary diversityespecially among children 6-59 months and PLWs, SC will support vegetable productionthrough micro gardening. Using an e-voucher system, SC will provide micro gardening kitsto caregivers of children in IYCF support groups to encourage HH vegetable productionand improve availability of nutritious food.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will promote transparency and accountability in all its operations and ensure thatbeneficiaries, community leaders and other community members have access to thefeedback and response mechanisms (FRM) and SC will ensure confidentiality toprotect beneficiaries. The feedback mechanisms will be established together with andfor beneficiaries – including children, adults, persons living with disabilities and othervulnerable groups, and take timely corrective actions to address safety, dignity andaccess issues raised by users and/or to redefine their assistance. During sensitizationmeetings, SC will reiterate messages to beneficiaries, partners, local leaders and othercommunity stakeholders emphasizing that SC assistance is free and impartial, and theright to aid as being unconditional and that no one should ask for any kind of favor inreturn for aid, particularly sexual and/or monetary. These messages will be reinforcedin radio jingles on local radio stations in local languages to provide information to thewider population. SC will use the preferred feedback mechanisms selected by thecommunity, these may include a toll-free mobile phone number, help desk, andcommunity meetings. Within the shopping areas, a FRM desk will be set up andmanaged by two SC staff (male and female) to ensure beneficiaries can providefeedback on any challenges they face relating to this sector and other components ofthe program and that they are addressed efficiently. Community feedback meetings toengage with community structures and beneficiaries on the impact of programactivities to the community and ways of improving on the support provided. Inaddition, two weeks after distributions, SC will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring toobtain feedback from beneficiaries about their shopping experience. See additionaldetails in Annex Fiii Accountability to Affected Populations Plan.
