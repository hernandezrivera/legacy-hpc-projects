Project Summary

This project aims to ensure continued and improved access to primary health care and emergency services to refugees and IDPs residing Al Hol camp, with particular attention to the most vulnerable cases.
The project supports the Primary Health Care clinic located in phase 1 of the camp in providing the following services:
- PHC with nurses, midwifes and doctors providing outpatient consultations across the three specialities (gynecologist, pediatrician, internal);
- Emergency (24/7) and nursing care room;
- Laboratory for clinical tests;
- Maternity department for reproductive health programs and deliveries;
- Pharmacy to provide medications upon medical prescription;
- Outreach volunteers recruited in the camp and embedded in the Health Promotion team, to support in organizing awareness raising campaigns.
Thanks to its strategical position, next to the main entrance, the clinic plays a very crucial role for the whole camp. The proximity to the reception area also makes it the best positioned health post in case of mass arrivals. Due to the above mention reasons, this facility is hosting the 24/7 Operational Desk, from which all the internal and external referrals are organized and coordinated.
This project targets a variety of direct and indirect beneficiaries: refugees and IDPs will be mainly targeted in Al Hol camp with a specific focus to women and girls, through the reproductive health program,
and children, as pediatricians will be available in the clinic for consultations.

4 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview, the scale, severity and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive. This is the result of continued hostilities in localized areas, new and protracted displacement and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during close to nine years of crisis. Across Syria, an estimated 11.7 million people are in need of various forms of humanitarian assistance, with certain population groups facing particularly high levels of vulnerability.Essential basic services are widely lacking, including health, shelter, food, education, water and sanitation. IDPs living in last resort sites and newly displaced population face severe deprivation and have survival needs that cut across all sectors of intervention. Across Syria health-related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to health care due to a combination of damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources, and imposed access constraints. Al Hol camp, between December 2018 and March 2019, witnessed a large, continuous flow of internally displaced people emerging from southeast Deir-ez-Zor; more than 63,000 people arrived in the camp, putting existing infrastructure under significant strain and reaching a pick of 73,000 people. The most pressing priority in the camp remains meeting basic humanitarian needs and preventing morbidity and mortality in a camp designed to accommodate a population half the current size and in a geographical area in which the context is constantly changing. Data collected by REACH in October 2019 show how the 48% of the households who required treatment reported barriers in accessing medical care. The most commonly reported barriers were lack of medicine and long waiting times. The health needs identified by the project are in line with the Whole of Syria plan and with the three health sector strategic objectives of HRP.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities implementation supports the PHCC located in Phase 1 by providing the following services: o PHC providing outpatient consultations across the specialties (gynecologist, pediatrician, internal); o Emergency and nursing care room (24/7);o Laboratory for clinical tests;o Maternity department for reproductive health programs and deliveries (24/7);o Pharmacy to provide medications upon medical prescription;o Outreach Volunteers recruited in the camp to support in organizing awareness raising campaigns.To shape the strategy with a long term sustainable approach and to boost quality of care 56 health staff among which gynecologists, midwives, nurses, general surgeons and pediatricians will be trained by UPP internal medical advisors (not charged on this project) on: Identification of PWDs; Psychological First Aid (PFA) assistance; Identification and referral of SGBV survivor cases; Contact tracing in case of epidemics such as tuberculosis or poliomyelitis; Community mobilization; Breastfeeding counselling; identification of malnutrition cases, TRIAGE, EWARS, morbidity, data collection and other topics.UPP will also provide through other projects and internal resources the additional services:o Ambulances for emergency referrals to secondary health facilities in Hassakeh (24/7); o Case Manager to follow up on referred cases;o Health Counsellor;o Community Health Workers for Health and Hygiene Promotion activities;o Additional Health Outreach Volunteers recruited in the camp;o Promotion team, to support in organizing awareness raising campaigns;o Cross border shipments.UPP attends on weekly bases the camp cooordination and the regular health sector coordination meetings and is also member of the HWG and NES forum. This allows UPP to avoid duplication and to improve the coordination with other partners. The reinforcement and expansion of the PHCC will be carried out to protect the clinic against attacks and to accommodate more patients.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:- Further escalation between SDF and GoT, extended to territories beyond the agreement reached, and increased waves of population displacement (medium/high risk);- Attacks to health facilities in case of escalation between GoT and GoS (high risk)- ISIS resurgence and regained control over areas (low risk) or infiltrates carrying out attacks (medium risk);- GoS extends militarily and administratively further into existing SDF territory (high risk);- Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies for IP; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (high risk);- Cross-border INGOs evacuate expat staff and response capacity is limited while active hostilities are ongoing (high risk);Mitigation1. Flexible response: Implementing partner can rapidly shift its support to facilities most in need or use mobile response to follow population needs. Preparedness plan, including contingency stock of pharma, is in place. 2. Contingency stock: emergency pharma storage will be aranged. To ensure the safety of a contingency stock, a second warehouse was opened in Nov. ’19 in Hassakeh. 3. Office/Facilities Sanitation: zero-paper policy in supported facilities. Every one or two weeks UPP staff brings to KRI official and sensitive documentation. 4. Acceptance: most of the staff recruited comes from the communities where they work. This ensures a high degree of ownership.The implementing partner through its own Charter of Principles, adheres to ICRC CoC, despite not officially being member. A joint commission of UPP together with KRC board is established to ensure a common commitment to humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence. KRC fully abides to ICRC CoC, addressing the needs of the vulnerable groups without distinction of ethnicity, religion, gender. Moreover, a comprehensive CFM has been established by: complaint box available in each health facility; WhatsApp/Telegram number contact.
