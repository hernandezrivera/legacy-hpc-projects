Project Summary

The world’s largest congested Rohingya camps and surrounding host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar have become food unsecured due to loss of economic opportunities in pandemic COVID-19 and global economic inflation. Approximately half of the assessed households in camps not having an acceptable food consumption score, and reporting challenges related to their food assistance; The most worried issues are that still 10% HH are poor in FCS (Ref: J-MSNA, 17 Oct’2021).
The same study shows that in camps, 70% of households reported having adopted livelihoods-based coping strategies due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. Though, Host are in better position, yet 89% of households reported engaging in coping mechanisms due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. However, 20% of households, reporting this strategy not to be available to them or to already have exhausted it.
Due to Rising prices of oil, agriculture inputs, vegetables, eggs, fish, and broiler meat have had a devastating effect on poor households who struggle to afford the basic food basket. Prices have risen to 55% due to a shortage of supply. To manage rising food prices, poor households are eating cheaper and less nutritious foods. Poor households seek the cheapest vegetables and sacrifice on quality. Project focusing on economic crisis recovery due to inflation by adopting on-farm, off-farm and restoration.
Under the objective 2; 1560 (Female 1560) and including 156 differently abled persons from the refugee households will receive self-reliance support. 1650 Women headed HH will receive cash for task support for Skill development on tailoring, handicrafts, kitchen gardening and homemade marketable ready-made food for targeting local markets, for diversified food catering business at market.
Under objective 3; 100 women and 20 differently abled persons from the host community HH will be supported for livestock rearing, poultry rearing, paddy production, pond and shrimp production, tailoring, kitchen garden, vertical garden for subsistence by adapt climate smart technology and will be trained for self-reliance’s & additional income for economic recovery.
The Project planned to boost up food consumption, increase household income that reduced selling of productive assets by creating option of additional homemade income for women, boost up agricultural productivity and enhance food storage for hard times. These will be done by improving contributing Factors of production and income by supporting on-farm and off-farm, facilitating terms of trade for market actors and increase Food Expenditure.
Moreover, the project aims to build social cohesion by establishing market linkage among FDMN and host community. Proper sensitization and coordination meeting will be organized with relevant government and other stakeholders to incorporate the findings and learnings and to adopt those learnings accordingly. The project is focused on accountability mainstreaming to ensure that the needs of the affected population are at the center of every intervention. Additionally, projects will be implemented through local partners, with adequate capacity building and mentoring that promote localization.
In addition, CAID has an in-house accountability tool called COMPASS to ensure community’s feedback is systematically recorded and resolved. Moreover, CAID is an active member of the PSEA network and regularly receives training and learning materials from the network to further disseminate in the community. Besides, CAID adheres to its own accountability and safeguarding policies at all levels.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar specially Ukhiya and Teknaf are food deficient zone, almost 70% vegetables and other necessities comes from other districts (FAO Study,2019). Moreover, due to COVID-19, Rising prices of vegetables, eggs, fish, and broiler meat have had a devastating effect on poor households who struggle to afford the basic food basket. Prices have risen to 55% (Scoping Study Potential Livelihood and Enterprise Options for Host and Refugee Communities in Cox’s Bazar) due to a shortage of supply. To manage rising food prices, poor households are choosing cheaper and less nutritious foods. Poor households seek the cheapest vegetables and sacrifice on quality. Bangladeshi people of Ukhiya and Teknaf believe that their livelihood opportunities-historically dependent on agriculture, livestock and fisheries have been significantly hampered since the 2017 influx. And historically, Ukhiya and Teknaf are climate change impacted areas. Year-round production is not possible due to lack of climate adaptive technologies and inputs. COVID 19 and preventive measures has resulted in an increase in Multi-Dimensional Deprivation or multi-dimensional poverty: driven by deprivation in education due to school closures, deprivation on health, income, and food access. The disruption on livelihoods and self-reliance activities, combined with increased food prices, have reduced household purchasing power and capacity to cover their basic needs (REVA-4)Rising prices and falling wages – recognized as the most common challenges for both host and Rohingya communities often hurt the poorest and most vulnerable groups the most. Prices likely to vary with distance to camps. Hosts living closer to camps are more likely to compete with the displaced population in markets for natural resources like land and fuelwood, labour and goods, compared to those living further away from camps.Due to instable international as well as local market conditions, especially the rise of oil price and other prices of agricultural inputs, has had significant negative impact on agricultural production. As a result, the farmers have to adopt negative coping mechanism such as low-quality seeds or inputs for production which eventually causing gross loss of production. Cash for Work at host and camp through Portable skill development training and distribution of inputs can help tackle the situation.As there are shortage of cultivable land due to the accommodation of Rohingya community, loss of Livestock, distortion of Terms of Trade, loss of Household Income and decreased Percentage of Household Food Expenditure. During CAID’s need assessment FGD with handicraft entrepreneurs who were mostly female, pointed out their limitation and lack of support while working from home for some earning. These includes lack of electricity, able to bear the cost of solar panel, failing to open a saving account in the bank and so on. The need of support to cater based on these priorities is crucial.With the reduction of livelihood activities in the camps, some cash-for-work and self-reliance programming have halted increasing the added vulnerability of the community. For refugees, opportunities for generating an income in the camps tend to be limited, ad hoc and small-scale which often are distributed disproportionately within the community which often leaves out women headed households, person with disabilities and transgenders. While male members of this community have consistently voiced their preference for reliance on work over aid, voices of the women and left behind groups are not raised. As a result, women, person with disability, transgender finds less opportunity for building capacity as a step towards self-reliance.CAID's need assessment link is:https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/12SyPGeJtoUIG3fZGw9Wrm-m_dzRIukPf?usp=sharing

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Currently CAID with the partnership with DSK is implementing FSS activities in camp 14, 15 and in Palongkhali host community (Hakimpara and Jamtoli). The camp activities are well coordinated with the other stakeholders and the blocks for DSK are selected from by the CIC. So there are no overlapping of service provisions. The host community area, particularly the Unions are selected by DC and UNO. All the host community area are recommended by Government authorities including UNO, Upozila Social welfare Officer, Upozila Agriculture Extension Officer, Chairman and member. The same area is not by other organization in the host community too. In the camp DSK is following the integrated approach with other co-operating partners. To ensure uninterrupted service delivery, DSK and CAID regularly attends different sector meeting organized in camps where activities are duly shared with others. The number of project participants is also well defined and well-coordinated. CAID is continuously working to develop the partners capacity by including them in different sector provided trainings. Localization is one of the central approaches of CAID’s intervention. For the continuation of services, CAID is planning to adopt activities that can be self-sustained. Training program are designing in a way that could be disseminated by community themselves. CAID is working to continue further relations with several donor agencies for continuing it’s intervention and even encouraging partner organization to look for such opportunities so that the interventions can be continued without the presence of CAID. In terms of coordination with sector, CAID regularly submits 5W report and volunteer engagement report to FSS. CAID regularly attends sector arranged trainings including GAM, Inclusion, Protection mainstreaming, EETWG and other trainings organized by several sectors. CAID FSS focal and sub-focal regularly attends FSS monthly meeting and contributes to improve humanitarian program design.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection is the cross cutting theme followed in the project. To strenghten the livelihood opprotunities, selected participants receive 2 types of training including a life-skill and a soft-skill training. During the sof-skill training, the participants receive GBV awareness messages, prevention messages, referral mechanism, menstrual hygiene management and safe access of women and adolescent girls.

3.2 - Gender

Prioritizing gender equality, CAID is implementing the interventions focusing on the needs of beneficiaries especially the females in the camp and host community. DSK with the technical support from CAID, continuously works at the field level to assess the needs of women and girls. On a similar note, in this reporting period, CAID conducted training with partner organizations focusing to ensure a safe workplace for female. Prioritizing gender equality, DSK recruited 8 Female Master Trainers for facilitating the self-reliance activities and all of them are from local community which promotes localization. ToT was provided for building the capacity of the master trainer along with the existing community mobilizers. After receiving the training, these master trainers cascaded this information down to the community mobilizers.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project ensures age including child safeguarding by providing the safe space for women and child. The livelihood participants can bring their child while they receive the trainings. DSK master trainer and field facilitators ensure no harm during the sessions. Apart from these, the child are taught about hygiene and protection safety. The WGSS is partly works as child safe space too where their mother receive portable trainings without fearing of their childs safety.

3.4 - Diversity

CAID conducted a qualitative survey to understand the current challenges people at-risk group (pregnant women, older persons, and persons with disabilities). Livelihood/self-reliance beneficiary selection and disability disaggregated data collection were done by using Washington set group of questions. After the primary selection, verification, and consultation, finally the list is shared with CIC for approval so that there is no overlap with other organization from the camps and in host communities the list is shared with UNO, after their final approval, the people with disability are included in the livelihood program. 2 Self-Help Group (SHG) are formed with Person with Disabilities in host community. The purpose of the group formation is to prepare, empower and explore representation of People with Disabilities in community development. Also, SHG will help to improve equal opportunities and adequate participation in decision making for accessible and inclusive service.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CAID introduced its proven and tested digital CFRM mechanism within CAN DO/EKOTA consortium. A rigorous Complaint, Feedback, Response Management (CFRM) mechanism developed in consultation with EKOTA, Caritas, RDRS, DSK and communities in collaboration with the MEAL JWG/CMU. CAID oriented all programmer’s staff on COMPASS (a digital feedback management system) followed by accountability assessment conduction. Information on how to use the CFRM will be provided to the communities and particular awareness activities also have been undertaken for children, youth and other people at-risk groups. Local co-partners and affected populations are engaged in designing and implementing the program through arranging co-creation workshops, rigorous needs assessment with the affected population, sharing the program plan and budget with the affected populations, and installing functional CFRM in place.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The proposed project will focus on boosting agriculture production by adopting nature friendly cultivation process. Mass forestation and optimal use of lands will be prioritized. During the training, department of agriculture extension officer will be involved to train the participants. The training module will focus on mitigating climate change effect. Forestation and household gardening will be prioritized along with the livestock rearing, fishing and chicken farming. Chriatian Aid is currently implementing a 2 DRR project under WFP funding including Agricultural Infrastructure Development through food for assest and DRR camp project. In both of the project, one of a core activities are tree plantation and CAID has active presense with EETWG. The participants who will be utilizing the grants for chicken/livestrock rearing will be encouraged to adopt the homestead gardening and CAID will distribute seeds from other linked project.
