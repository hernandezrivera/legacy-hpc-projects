Project Summary

The project aims at effectively contributing to building the resilience of the community and enhancing equitable access of most vulnerable to basic health services through providing community based health services and reaching out to PoCs by community health workers, building the capacity and mobilizing the community in addressing health risks, and through assisting in the revitalization of the health system by contributing to light rehabilitation of health infrastructure while building the capacity of NGOs in health. Contribution to the sector efforts to respond to COVID-19 pandemic will be integrated through supporting risk communication and community engagement related to COVID-19 prevention and vaccine rolling out, coordinated support to case management, surveillance and continuity of care through the provision of medical equipment and supplies, and assisting in enhancing preparedness and response capacity through supporting the establishment of 8 points of entry jointly with WHO. In 2022-2023 the project will target nearly 750,000 vulnerable returnees, IDPs and affected communities in geographic areas of moderate to high needs and priority, in urban, rural, camps and camps like settlements, with special focus on health vulnerabilities in children, women at reproductive age, older persons and persons with disability including mental disability. Community and right based approaches will be adopted in addition to networking and collaboration with health actors and interlink with WASH, nutrition, protection and shelter sectors influencing the determinants of health.

4 - Needs

Based on the HNO, health needs continue to be one of the priorities of an estimated 12.4 million people in the country with 11% under 5 children, over 25% women at reproductive age, and 4% older persons with multiple health care needs including non-communicable diseases which account for 45% of the morbidity countrywide. The impact of the crisis on health infrastructure is clearly observed by UNHCR field offices and the RAMS reports. Stating that only 50% of the health facilities are functional, coupled with an evident shortage of human resources in the public health sector. The overall severely deteriorated economic situation, limited livelihood opportunities combined with poor housing conditions, all increased morbidity and affected the access of PoCs to health and medicine services. COVID-19 added to the unfavorable conditions increasing morbidity and impacting access to health. This includes low vaccine coverage, which is estimated at nearly 10%. Out of the 3,735,039 individuals recorded In the database of 110 community centers supported by UNHCR NGO partners, 39% were children (below 18 years), 9% were older persons and 51% were female. 9,3% of the recorded beneficiaries reported health needs (mainly chronic conditions), 6.7% reported MHPSS needs, while 4,1% reported disabilities in congruence with MSNA report in 2021 estimating 4% disability prevalence (1.3 million persons).

5 - Implementation of activities

The planned disengagement of health services was not possible due to COVID-19. Thus, the health strategy is to responsibly disengage by end of 2022. UNHCR will invest in strengthening coordination mechanisms, capacitate partners to maintain the quality of service and reinforce referral pathways. Partners will provide community health services in 55 static health points embedded in the community centers, which are operated by 135 community health workers and 440-825 trained community health outreach volunteers. Health promotion and disease prevention activities including COVID-19 will be implemented, in line with the national health priorities. Adequate engagement and participation of the community throughout the program cycle are warranted by the selection of outreach representative volunteers, FGDs, and 4 community health initiatives in each of the 55 locations. GBV prevention and response will be mainstreamed in the design, implementation, and monitoring. Linkages with levels of care will be ensured through local mapping, coordination, and cooperation with formal and non-formal health actors to address health needs and optimize community resources. In coordination and collaboration with MoH, 16 health facilities will be rehabilitated. Furthermore, 7 medical posts at points of entry will be established jointly with WHO. Based on MoH standards and specifications, UNHCR will provide the required furniture and equipment support.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Gender-based violence: Mainstreaming and reporting on GBV prevention and response, using sector adopted templates and customized checklists to examine the physical environment, processes, policies, and SOPs in place covering prevention methods and effective response including safety and confidentiality of survivors, and coordination with GBV actors to ensure referral pathways are in place are some of the mitigating measures in place. Risk 2: Lack of community engagement and equitable access to health: The community-based approach is adopted and integrated into the project design and implementation. Outreach volunteers, community outreach, and the establishment of community feedback mechanisms are mitigating measures in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Health services are heavily dependent on human resources and interaction, hence, PSEA risk should always be considered. Mitigation measures include: (a) a zero-tolerance policy adopted by partners. (b) Assigning a PSEA focal point to ensure mainstreaming and development/update of dedicated SOPs. (c) Regular training of staff of partners, volunteers, and contractors. (d) Code of conduct with PSEA commitments clearly explained must be signed by each worker/contractor. (e) Free services and information regarding PSEA and safe reporting mechanism are posted in health points and PHCs.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational risks include high staff turnover especially in rural/remote areas, fuel and electricity shortages that can hinder the provision of services, access, and outreach services. COVID-19 spikes that can impact contact with PoCs, depreciation of the currency and increase costs of services, scarcity of medicines in the local market, and access to service delivery in remote areas for monitoring and capacity building. The high turnover is mitigated by having backup staff and recruitment policies that give priority to residents in remote areas who meet qualifications requirements, in addition to having a component of resident outreach volunteers and allocating additional budget for transportation. Adequate coordination with health actors is essential to ensure maximum utilization of resources. Fraud and PSEA are major reputational risks, especially when activities are taking place in remote-rural areas difficult to reach and monitor by UNHCR and its partners. Regular monitoring, directly, through partners, and third-party monitoring contractors are measures that can be adopted alongside measures described under the PSEA section.
