Project Summary

Almost two months since the conflict between the RSF and SAF broke out, 1,428,551 people (286,207 households) have been displaced (Sudan DTM SitRep 7). The humanitarian needs in Sudan have been consistently increasing in East, North and South Darfur and Gedaref States. Particularly, the Darfur the insecurity and attacks on civilians reported has increased. Whereas Gedaref hosts an increasing number of IDPs, more than 80% living with their relatives with limited access to humanitarian assistance. Consequent to the food production and supply chain disruptions, market dysfunctionality, fuel shortages, disrupted transportation network, scarcity of imported food items, and stark inflation rates many people are unable to afford food items. The revised Sudan HRP 2023 revealed that in the next 3 to 6 months, 19.1 million people are projected to be food insecure. The food security cluster reported that the local food basket price increased to 23,206SDG per person per month in May and is expected to increase by 25% in the next 6 months. Thus, ZOA and partners aim to provide an emergency food security and livelihood support to IDPs, returnees and host communities East, North and South Darfur and Gedaref States. We aim to target 4500 total beneficiaries i.e., 2,250 IDPs, 675 returnees and 1,575 host community members.

1 - Needs

The conflict between the SAF and RSF flared up almost two months ago. Displacements of people from one state to the other and within the same state have been observed in all 18 States. The number of internally displaced people in the country have reached 1.4 million. The increase in insecurity, attacks on civilians, rampant lootings of residents and humanitarian actor’s premises coupled with sharp inflation, poor transportation network of food and non-food items, market disruption increased the overall humanitarian needs. According to the Sudan DTM SitRep 7, the top priority needs of the displaced people are food, health care and emergency shelter. Currently, South Darfur hosts 89,820 IDPs, Gedaref 42,240, North Darfur 29,300, and East Darfur 7000. More than 78% of the IDPs are hosted by the host community, and the remaining in rented accommodation, IDP camps, schools, abandoned building and informal settlements (DTM SitRep 7). Majority of them are children, women, elderly, and others with special needs. The Darfur States are mainly dealing with frequent insecurity, inter-communal violence and civilian attacks including looting, disruption of basic services, limited access to food and non-food items as the main routes to transport goods are often under attack. Prior to the conflict, these states were also hosting a tremendous amount of food insecure people living under IPC 3 and 4 classifications. The looting of WFP warehouse also reduces the food assistance which can be provided by WFP and partners to IDP. In May, the price of local food basket price increased to 526 SDG per person per day. It’s expected to further increase by 25% in the next 6 months. Additionally, the ongoing insecurity and fighting diminished the productive livelihood assets and capacities of the people in the targeted States. The agriculture season is from end of May until mid-July, ensuring planting takes place within the season requires a concerted effort from all humanitarian actors and the target community. Otherwise, food insecurity, malnutrition and unemployment will increase while food availability drops down. The deteriorating food security situation in East, South and North, and Gedaref States demands attention. Moreover, the looming planting season could be missed if agricultural inputs assistance is not provided to farmers to encourage planting. Thus, in this intervention, we propose to address the needs of 4500 total beneficiaries in East, South and North, and Gedaref States. This consists of 2,250 IDPs, 675 returnees and 1,575 host community members.

2 - Activities/Outputs

There are two main types of assistance considered urgent under the FSL component. We plan to support 2840 households of IDPs, returnees and host community members with a onetime food assistance either in-kind or with commodity vouchers depending on the market functionality. The target locations include all four states. Localities and villages clusters will be determined based on needs. The onetime food assistance includes wheat, sugar, oil, milk, etc. The main targets will be women headed households, households with disabled family members and unaccompanied children as they are the most vulnerable.In addition, to boost the production capacity of local farmers, ZOA will benefit 1660 household farmers in all target states with agricultural tools, improved seeds and fertilizers. This component of our intervention is pivotal to increase food availability by the harvest season, reduce malnutrition and the number of people who could slip into food insecurity. This will be complemented by farmer field schools where target farmers will be provided with regular practical training.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is designed based on the pronounced and observed needs of the project target groups. We will ensure that the needs of IDPs, returnees, vulnerable residents will be addressed while protecting their dignity, and safety while creating access. The project is designed to put community participation as the main pillar. Thus, the project will not be implemented and monitored by ZOA staff only, but rather with the participation of beneficiaries and community members to ensure inclusion, relevancy to the needs of the people and success of the project. In this manner, ZOA will ensure that people from different groups are part of the community consultations when it comes to determining priorities. Additionally, they would be included in setting criteria for the beneficiary’s selection and reviewing the selected lists. In line with protection principles the project beneficiaries will be targeted by an inclusive committee representative of all the community groups men, women, youth, elders, disabled persons, etc. We will also work through and closely with protection actors in the targeted areas and make referrals where necessary. During registration and distribution of assistance, we will ensure that people with special needs have very short queue time and separate lines for women, men and people with special needs.
