Project Summary

This project aims to improve the shelter conditions of 22597 vulnerable Rohingya refugees living in the camps in Ukhiya, with additional targeting of 1430 individuals (women and youth) in construction skills development in Ukhiya. DRC aims to assist in total 87082 individuals through the provision of quality shelter assistance, which includes: the distribution of shelter materials for care and maintenance of shelters through the vouchers modality, a comprehensive mid term technical solution for shelters, lattice project for women, skills development training for youth and pilot catchment focal point system in case of emergency. It is worth noting that DRC successfully achieved a pilot project of 25 mid term shelter in camp 2 Extension in 2019.

Needs

Despite the positive impact of shelter interventions on living conditions, shelter condition is a concern because the main materials used, untreated bamboo and tarpaulin, are not long-lasting. 71% of Rohingya households (MSNA 2019) report roofs leaking. 46% report rotten or damaged bamboo. Solutions are needed that provide enhanced structural strength, protection from the elements and resistance to insect attack. The Rohingya refugee’s main safety concerns are a) unstable structure, b) shelter deterioration, and c) no adequate internal lighting (NPM R15). Insufficient supply of all the required NFIs has also been observed. In 2019 there has been a continual requirement from the emergency shelter response for repairs, rehabilitations and relocations due to rapid and low-cost housing constructions. These were often due to self-build by Rohingya constructed in ill-advised locations and with bamboo as the main construction material getting damaged. DRC has been providing shelter reparation materials through the voucher assistance and shelter upgrade materials through emergency vouchers and reached 7800 HHs in camps number: 2w,2e,5,6. Through the care and maintenance vouchers, DRC assisted around 14200 HH in 2019 in 4 camps. The needs are still significant due to limited lifespan of the materials. However, camp 6 started accepted care and maintenance vouchers later than other camps and received less material support. TSA activities were also minimal in the camp 6 comparing to camps 8W and 8W, therefore, through this project, DRC will assist camp 6 with vouchers assistance (steel footings, materials and training) so the HHs could meet all Minimum Performance Standards. As per the recent UNHCR demographic population fact sheet (September 2019) 52% of the Rohingya population is female, therefore through the provision of shelter tailored interventions, DRC will ensure that women and men will benefit equally from the project and will provide additional assistance for women and youth in skills development and livelihood opportunities. Based on DRC’s experience, for female-headed households, women-at-risk and other marginalised groups who are particularly susceptible to exploitation, direct cash assistance does not reliably ensure access to adequate living conditions. These groups are more likely to benefit from interventions involving specific needs of women, girls. In Rohingya culture, women are not welcome to work outside their houses. Those needs will be accounted on two levels: 1) provision of training in terms of understanding construction methodology to youth and 2) providing training which will lead to small livelihood opportunities for women linked to the construction materials (lattice project). This project aims to build on existing Site Management in the targeted camps and DRC’s operations will focus on achieving the sector’s objectives in terms of strengthening community engagement, women and youth representation to support in decision making processes with a focus on disaster risk reduction and improving living conditions through the shelter assistance. DRC SMS team is providing support to CiC through camp coordination meetings, inter-sector focal point meetings, and sectoral meetings to ensure follow up on issues raised through community groups, CFRM, listener groups, and service monitoring results. When relevant Shelter team will coordinate with SMS team to respond to particular beneficiary’s needs or refer to other agencies if the activity is not in the DRC scope.
