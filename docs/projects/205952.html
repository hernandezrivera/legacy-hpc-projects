Project Summary

Our HRP proposed response is aligned to the Health Cluster’s aim to improve access to and utilization of essential health care services to reduce excess morbidity and mortality amongst 44,445 IDPs including host populations and Returnees.
LiveWell will provide health services that are adapted to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention while continuing to provide essential lifesaving health care. Community and County Health System reliance will be promoted through bringing synergies and optimizing use of limited resources in the World Bank Projects LiveWell is currently implementing in Bor South, Twic East, Duk, Pibor, Pochalla in Jonglei State and the current Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) and previously been providing lifesaving health services in Lakes States more especially the eastern part in Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial Counties.
We will also collaborate with CUAMM in Lakes states during the implementation of SSHF emergency projects in Awerial, and Yirol East, CARE International & MDM in Bor South, Twic East and Duk in Jonglei State, and Pibor & Pochalla in Greater Pibor Addministrative Area (GPAA) respectively. Our proposed budget for 8 counties to implements 3 Health Cluster’s clinical package is at US$ 1,600,000 for 44,445 targeted beneficiaries. This was calculated using HC’s cost per beneficiary of $36 as this proposal humanitarian response is aligned to Health Cluster’s strategies and allocation for HRP 2024.
The benefit will aim at supporting the following 8 health facilities based on the following level services per HRP 2024's Health Cluster Clinical Packages
1. Six (6) Mobile Clinics (1 in Pibor, 1 in Pochalla, Duk, 1 in Twic East, 1 in Yirol East, 1 in Awerial, and 1 in Bor South counties to cover over 30,444 IDPs, Returnees & Host communities.
2. Four (2) PHCUs in Yirol East and Pochalla counties to reach 14,000 IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities in the targeted proposed areas of implementations.

1. Needs

South Sudan has the worsening health indicators in the world: Under-five mortality rates with 98/1,000 live births (over 40% of deaths in Under-five years’ children occurred during the first month of life due to high risks associated with delivery and post-delivery period for both mothers and their new-borns), infant mortality rates (IMR) in South Sudan is at 63/1,000 live births, neonatal mortality rate(NMR) with 40/1,000 live births, an Age-adjusted death rate of 23/100,000 population (UNIGME, 2021) According to WHO, 75% of deaths in under-five are caused by Malaria, diarrhoea, and pneumonia. Maternal mortality ratio(MMR) with 1,150 per 100,000 live births, the highest ratio in the world (MMEIG, 2017) - The leading direct causes of maternal mortality were PPH with 16%, obstructed/prolonged labour with 13.3% and Antepartum Haemorrhage with 12.9% (MOH, 2013). ANC at 39% women during pregnancy, and institutionalized delivery at 12.3% as well as skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3%. Access to BEmONC is very low, Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6% with modern methods at 5%, with the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7% for 2020 (FP 2022). In Jonglei State, Lakes State and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) have the lowest EPI coverage due to unstable security. Attack on health workers make it difficult to conduct the outreach vaccination activities in hard-to-reach villages. In September 2022, LiveWell reported an attack on COVID-19 Vaccinator who was killed in Pochalla county while on his normal outreach duty to Otalla area. Recurrent flooding has caused massive destruction of health facilities (HFs) in Pochalla, which reduced the effort to conduct EPI outreaches in hard-to-reach areas (IRNA Pochalla report, Sep 2022). COVID-19 outbreak responses continue to put more pressure on the existing scarce HR capacity for EPI implementation, and its compromised the routine immunization service delivery capacity (EPI, 2023). GPAA is classified under IPC4 (FSNMS, 2019). Reports of cattle raiding, child abduction, and inaccessible road have resulted into displacement of vulnerable groups and increased number of returnees from Ethiopia (IRNA, 2022/2023)Our healthcare system heavily relies on humanitarian support and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services to the vulnerable people. According to National Health Cluster data (2023): there are huge inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the country: 59% of the country’s population can access health facilities in one hour’s time while 80% reported barriers in accessing health services. There is low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage), making children more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. The humanitarian situation across Lakes, Pibor and Jonglei States have continued to deteriorate as a result of floods, cattle raiding, child abduction and food insecurity (IPC 4 & 5). This is evidenced by SMART surveys conducted in July 2022 by Action Against Hunger in Duk County. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of children in Duk, GAM was estimated at 22%.(REACH_SSD_ Situation Overview _ Jonglei_ Sept 2020). With limited humanitarian access in Lakes State, information gaps exist on the severity of these needs and the locations of vulnerable populations. Yirol East is at IPC 4 whereas Awerial and Yirol West Counties are in IPC 3. High levels of malaria and low access to medication across Lakes reportedly drove health needs in September. (REACH_SSD_ Situation Overview _ Lakes_Sept 2020). LiveWell is therefore seeking your approval to recommend our concept note “Improve equitable access, scale-up response and strengthen the capacities of health facilities to provide lifesaving essential health care services to the targeted IDPs

2. Project activities

• Provide Rehabilitative and disability services • Conduct epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including investigation and response. • Conduct immunization for routine, mobile and outreach services • Provide IPC WASH materials, protocol and refreshers training to prevent disease outbreak COVID-19 • Conduct mass and routine COVID-19 Vaccination campaign to protect health workers and the population at risks. • Increase access to Boma Health initiative by providing ICCM and IMNCI guidelines • Provide lifesaving essential health care services such as out-patient, in-patients, mobiles services. • Procure essential drugs and supplies to run the lifesaving services • Train health workers on IEHK kits, rational use of antibiotics, IPC & WASH in health facilities etc • Provide reproductive health services such as ANC, skilled deliveries, adolescence health, family planning, abortion care and PNC • Conduct BEMONC & CEMONC services in PHCCs and county hospital • Provide vitamin A supplementation and deworming tablets for children under 5 years • Conduct mass measles and cholera vaccination • Give PMTCT & advance HIV/AIDs management and care • Distribute LLIN/ mosquitoes’ nets in hard to reach locations. • Conduct community consultation meetings to provide feedback and also ensure compliance• Treatment and management of mental health conditions • Provide psychosocial support and psychological first aids for survivors of rape • Provide M & E tools for mobile and static health facilities. • Clinical management of a rape and associated sexual infections• Treatment of malaria, pneumonia and AWDs using IMNCI guidelines • Conduct health education, promotion and preventions at community level. • Conduct personal and environmental hygiene for schools and households. • Implement mobile and outreach clinical activities in hard to reach locations.
