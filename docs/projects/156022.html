Project Summary

The project aims at addressing the health needs of the displaced people residing in northern Syria, providing various health services at Orient Health facilities, linking them to each other through a single management network with plans to provide high-quality services and referral of patients within and out of this network

Needs

After seven years since the beginning of the Syrian crisis, many Syrians have been displaced to Idlib governate, which according to UN estimates hosts 2,363,035 people, 1,164,580 of them are IDPS. Being the hub of IDPs in northern Syria, it is necessary for Idlib city to have the capacity to provide the most significant specialized health services, especially the support of many health facilities has interrupted for various reasons, which increased the need for support and expansion of other facilities. In addition, Idleb receives forcibly displacements from other areas which ın turn increases the need and burden on health facilities.According to the assessment conducted by Orient, the areas of northern Syria, which includes the governorate of Idlib, rural Aleppo, and western villages of the northern Hama, there is only a burn hospital that provides primary services, and Idlib Health Directorate ensured that establishment of a burn hospital is considered one of its priorities. This request has also been supported by the World Health Organization (WHO) to provide the necessary specialized service for a large geographical area with high population density.Ibni sina dialysis Center provides its services for the city of Idlib and its surrounding areas. The population covered by the center is estimated to one million, more than 350 patients periodically visit the center since there is no other center within this area. According to statistics conducted by the Idleb Health Department of dialysis centers in the region, Ibni Sina Medical Center receives 28% of patients in the whole area. It also receives serious cases referred from neighboring areas, and after several dialysis sessions, they return to their areas. Besides, the center contains 22% of the equipment in the area, making it the most important one in the region.

Implementation of activities

Programming proposed by Orient has extensively coordinated with Health sector partners through the Health Cluster and Health Directorate to avoid duplication of services.Key activities of Implementation are highlighted below :In February 2019 (preparation for burn hospital):o Rehabilitation of the facility o Purchase of facility equipment o Equipped the facilityo Recruitment of qualified staffo Conducting special training related to burn treatment to ensure the overall readiness of the staff to worko Providing the needed supplieso Recruitment of the project manager to follow up the project and make a field visits to oversee the progress of the projecton February 15th, the hospital begins to provide health services with other facilities in Orient network.Other facilities are currently operational. Key activities of Implementation are highlighted below and are dependent on the modality of care:* Orient will provide operational costs, staff salaries, medication & consumables for all facilities in the network for 12 months for the provision of primary health care, specialized health care, ambulance system for men, women, and children.Burns Hospital provides specialized services for all beneficiaries (men, women, girl, boys), including surgery, intensive care, skin restoration, ambulance, patient care, and follow-upOrient Medical Complex also provides specialized health services including 8 specialized clinics, lab, and pharmacy.Armnaz Center and ORIENT Mobile Clinic provide primary health care services and appropriate medication to patients in their coverage areasORIENT ambulance system transfers patients and injured persons urgently in their areas of coverageDue to the high pressure on the center and a large number of patients, the work will be on two shiftsat morning and eveningThe Center receives patients according to their previous registration at the center in which they are asked about any medical developments they have received and undergoes the necessary testsThe new patients are registered, the necessary tests are performed and the complete medical data is collected to become permanent patients.Periodic reports are given on the number of patients and the number of sessions they receive according to sex and ageThe necessary kits are brought to the center from different sourcesMedication, consumables and all materials will be purchased based on Orient procurement procedures in Syria. The staff of the project will be recruited as needed and according to Orient's employment procedures.A Project Manager will be recruited by Orient and will be responsible for managing the activities. The project manager will conduct supervisory visits for the project’s facilities, and report on the progress of the activities and gather documentation.Orient has a strong working relationship with Health Directorate (HD) in areas of operation in Syria (i.e. Idleb). HD is currently considered as one of the best sources for information on health needs in each region along with mapping of what other national (local) and international NGOs are operating by area in terms of location and type of facility (i.e. primary, secondary, etc.). HD continuously liaises with Orient and Health Cluster to ensure coordination amongst other health actors and ultimately facilitate effective and efficient aid.As for medical training, Orient will coordinate with the World Health Organization (WHO) and with technical groups in the health sector in order to send the network staff to attend its training sessions throughout the project period. Besides, Orient will coordinate with partners in the field who are undertaking technical exercises during the project period.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Ignorance of safety measures and response to risks by staffProbability: moderate. Effect: highMitigation measures: Staff will be trained on safety measures and joint operational principles (JOP) during emergency response. Patients will be educated on safety guideline through posters and brochures distributed throughout community outreach, and in waiting rooms inside the facilities. Risk: no access or high risk to access for beneficiaries, especially for women.Probability: weak. Effect: highMitigation measures:- Advocacy for safe access for beneficiaries, and for a safe environment for staff members, will be expanded and shared with coordination bodies at all levels.- Orient health facilities’ locations are fully accessed to all beneficiaries and multiple routes exist.Risk: gender groups discrimination when providing servicesProbability: weak. Effect: highMitigation measures:- In this project, all services are provided to all gender groups (women, men, boys, girls) without any kind of discrimination- Tailored services are provided to vulnerable gender groups (children and women)- Dedicated female staff is recruited to provide sensitive health services to women e.g. reproductive health care.project manager, security and safety officer, M&E team will follow up the measures of mitigation.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Heavy air strikes and military operations do not affect program progress in the operational area.- Orient can scale up response rapidly. - Security risks are forcing qualified personnel to leave conflict areas. Orient will try to provide an encouraging work environment for health and administrative staff.- Cross border operations are limited by border status. Orient will coordinate with authorities to obtain permissions for border-crossing for its staff, and will be focusing on local market when applicable.
