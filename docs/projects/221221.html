Project Summary

Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) is a critical aspect of ensuring gender equality and improving education outcomes for adolescent girls. It is high time that this aspect is given the attention it deserves. In many parts of Cameroon, menstruation is still a taboo topic. This, coupled with inadequate access to menstrual products and a lack of education, is hindering girls' development. These challenges inevitably result in school absenteeism, poor academic performance, and even dropout, further exacerbating gender disparities in education and limiting future opportunities for girls.
The Pad Bank Initiative is going to solve these problems in schools across Buea and Limbe. We're going to provide affordable, sustainable menstrual products and menstrual health education. This initiative will empower girls to manage their periods with dignity, reduce absenteeism, and create a supportive environment for menstrual hygiene awareness. This will unlock girls' potential and enable them to thrive academically and socially.
The Pad Bank Initiative is designed to:
We will provide access to affordable and sustainable menstrual products. It is unacceptable that many girls in low-income communities and internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Buea and Limbe cannot afford commercial sanitary pads, forcing them to rely on unsafe alternatives. The initiative will establish pad banks in schools, where girls can access reusable sanitary pads at no cost or at subsidized rates.
We will reduce absenteeism and improve academic outcomes. Menstrual-related absenteeism is a significant barrier to education for adolescent girls. We will address this challenge to ensure that girls attend school regularly and perform to their full potential.
We will also foster menstrual health education. Many girls lack knowledge about menstruation due to insufficient education at home and in schools. We will integrate menstrual health education into the school curriculum and provide training for teachers to create a supportive learning environment.
We must promote social inclusion and reduce stigma. The stigmatization of menstruation isolates girls during their periods. The initiative will conduct community sensitization sessions, including for boys and parents, to reduce stigma and promote inclusivity.
We will empower women through economic opportunities. The initiative will create sustainable livelihoods and foster community ownership of the project by training local women's groups to produce reusable sanitary pads.
Target Beneficiaries
Direct Beneficiaries:
- 10,000 school girls aged 10-18 years in 50 primary and secondary schools in buea and Limbe
- Local Women's group trained to produce reusable sanitary pads
These girls are enrolled in 50 primary and secondary schools in Buea and Limbe.
Indirect Beneficiaries
- Teachers will gain the knowledge and skills to support menstrual health education in schools.
- Parents and community members will be informed about menstrual health issues and the impact of MHM on girls' education, fostering a supportive environment at home and in the community.
- Boys in schools will be sensitized to reduce stigma around menstruation, promoting inclusivity and respect for their female peers.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Below is a comprehensive overview of the core needs that this project will address and how its activities are specifically designed to meet these needs. 1. Access to Affordable and Sustainable Menstrual ProductsMany girls are unable to afford commercial sanitary pads, forcing them to use unsafe, unhygienic alternatives. Activity-Establish pad banks in schools to distribute free reusable sanitary pads to girls. - Train local women's groups to produce affordable, high-quality reusable pads to ensure a steady supply and sustainability. 2. Reduce menstrual-related absenteeism.Girls frequently miss school during their periods due to a lack of menstrual products and supportive environments. This leads to poor academic performance and an increased risk of dropping out. Activity: - To educate girls on proper menstrual hygiene management (MHM) to build confidence and reduce the shame and fear that contribute to absenteeism. 3. Menstrual Health EducationMany girls lack adequate knowledge about menstruation due to insufficient education at home and in schools. This leaves them unprepared to manage their periods safely and confidently. Activity:- Organize menstrual health workshops in schools and communities to teach girls and boys about the menstruation, hygiene practices, and how to use and care for reusable pads.- Train teachers to incorporate MHM education into their curriculum for sustained knowledge dissemination.4. Combat the stigma and social exclusion surrounding menstruation. Menstrual stigma and cultural taboos often lead to bullying, isolation, and a lack of inclusivity for girls, which negatively impacts their self-esteem and participation in school activities. Activity:- Sensitize Boys in schools, to reduce stigma and encourage supportive peer environments. - Organize community awareness campaigns to address misconceptions and foster a culture of inclusivity and understanding about menstruation. 5. Girls should have access to private toilets, clean water, and disposal mechanisms. Activity:- Improve school sanitation infrastructure, including the construction of handwashing stations, and waste disposal systems. - Train school staff in the maintenance and management of these facilities to guarantee long-term usability.6. Economic Empowerment of WomenLocal women need economic opportunities to support themselves and contribute to community development. Activity:- Train women's groups in the production of reusable sanitary pads, providing them with skills and a source of income. Support these groups in establishing sustainable businesses that serve the community and contribute to the initiative's objectives.7. Support for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)Need: IDP girls face heightened financial and logistical barriers to accessing menstrual products and education, increasing their vulnerability. - We will host targeted menstrual health workshops for schools and communities with high IDP populations. These interventions are empowering girls to stay in school, thrive academically, and live with dignity. They are also fostering inclusivity and breaking the cycle of menstrual stigma.
