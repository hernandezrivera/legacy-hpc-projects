Project Summary

Malnutrition is one of the most persistent humanitarian challenges in Somalia despite concerted efforts to address it. It is estimated that 1 in 10 children under the age of five in Somalia is acutely malnourished with the median National GAM of 13.8 percent at Serious level, translating to 1.08M boys and girls of which 178,000 are affected by life threatening severely malnutrition while 830,000 are moderately malnourished. A further 832,000 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of nutrition assistance. According to FSNAU/FEWSNET Post-Gu Assessment, (2019), the overall median GAM prevalence rate of 13.8% across Somalia. Pockets of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15% were reported in 10 out of the 33 areas surveyed in the 2019 assessment, with the majority (51.7%) of malnourished children cases concentrated in the six regions of Banadir (14.8%) Shabelle (11%), Bay (8.8 % Hiran (6.1%), Woqooyi Galbeed (5.6 %), Gedo (5.4 %). These regions also realized below average rainfall and the expected harvest is 68% lower than the long-term average since 1995. Further, they are also been affected by lack of basic WASH services, increased morbidities and host several IDP sites, indicating increased vulnerability to a declining nutrition situation overall. There is also high level of micronutrient deficiencies among women (41.5% iron deficiency) and children below five years (59.3% anaemia). Complementary feeding also appears compromised, with only 15% of children 6-23 months receiving the minimum dietary diversity, and only 9% consuming the minimum acceptable diet. Bottle-feeding is prevalent, with 50% of infants 0-23 months bottle-fed. This project will focus on the 11,550 agro-pastorals in Buurhakaba and Baydhaba districts, Bay region where PASOS will increase the nutrition services coverage among the refugee returnees, host communities and other vulnerable populations through lifesaving curative, preventive and promotive nutrition services among among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases, provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions.

Project Activities

The following activities will take place in order to achieve the project goals.Kick of meeting in Buurhakaba and Baydhaba districts in Bay region in January 2020 Quarterly review meeting that will take place in April, July and October 2020. Conduct community sessions in targeted communities and select beneficiaries ensuring participation of women and relevant community groups Form community based networks and select community mobilizers of both gender. Training of 40 health staff on Vitamin and MMN protocol administration malnutrition. PASOS strive to ensure that there is gender balance in all trainings of their staff. Training IYCF and nutrition promoters in 16 target location with equal gender balance. Set a community based committees to coordinate key interventions. Conduct CHW workshops on maternal nutrition. A balance gender involvement will be ensured with women, girls, boys and men targeted. Such training will involve fathers and husbands amongst targeting mother. Train on use of locally available traditional foods with high nutritional value to supplement available food sources.Training of Community health workers and community mobilizers on health, Nutrition and Hygiene Promotion. Form 4 mobile OTPs in rural areas to reach the rural Bay agro-pastorals. Screen, admit, treat and refer malnourished and vulnerable community members with special interest in children under 5 years, pregnant and lactating women and elderly. Children under five years old (6-59 months) provided with measles, deworming tablets and penta 3 vaccines Pregnant women receiving TT immunization at the static and outreach facilities Provision of the multi-micro nutrient and deworming tablets. Mothers/caregivers receiving Individual counseling and/or breastfeeding support during the project period. Provision of BSFP among the malnourished populations.Conduct monitoring and evaluation of the project activities.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement The returnees targeted population risk of exclusion is medium since PASOS will ensure that data is collected among the beneficiaries with the help of already community structures and effective approaches such as outreach services so that all the vulnerable populations will be reached. Lack of documentation among the returnees does not mean that they are excluded but rather their vulnerability analysis. PASOS will also ensure that all the affected population understand the process of obtaining food assistance and livelihoods opportunities at the appropriate time, date and safe location.Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age The risks of exclusion based on gender and age is medium. The project’s gender focus creates new challenges for women and/or HH and community conflict by promoting women’s and age equality. PASOS will mitigate this risk by ensuring that activities targeting women include men in appropriate ways; male leaders engaged to support women’s involvement in decision making; gender impact of project (positive & negative) closely monitored; implementing agencies trained to refer GBV cases. Issues related to age exclusion will also be mitigated. There is a risk of excluding child headed household in the project. Minors will not be considered in restricted cash interventions but rather PASOS will involve the relevant local agencies to ensure that the child-headed households benefit from the unrestricted cash interventions. The risk of beneficiaries experience intimidation, extortion or theft of resources following distributions is medium and PASOS will Inform beneficiaries of potential risks, ensure distributions are conducted in secure areas and allow beneficiaries ample time to travel during daylight hours, ensure robust complaints mechanism and post distribution monitoring is in placeMitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence The main risks include: a) Inaccessibility of project locations due to security concerns. The risk is medium and PASOS will ensure that all the project locations are in both secure and potentially insecure areas to ensure continuous implementation of activities; and will also regularly review security status with local authorities and institutions. 2) There is also the risk of taxation by local authorities/power holders on distributed resources that the beneficiaries will face. The risk is medium and PASOS will mitigate the above mentioned risk by work with local authorities and other stakeholders to create awareness that these resources and assets are not subject to taxation, monitor and take immediate action on reports of taxation through accountability and feedback mechanisms.

Please provide further explanation

PASOS focuses on the marginalised populations, including women and girls, minorities and IDPs is expected to increase community and government inclusiveness and capacity in planning and implementation of the project. The quality of participation will ensure that respect and attention is given to opinions, ideas and perspectives of the community in planning and implementation. The result of broad-based consultation and engagement is expected to lead to greater ownership of the programme by communities, government and other stakeholders. The programme will be built upon relationships the PASOS already have with local communities, leaders, and government representatives, and complemented by long-term programming and other sectoral projects each agency is implementing in the area. This will considerably mitigate negative impacts of the programme’s closure further helping to enhance the target beneficiaries’ knowledge, awareness, and perceptions towards nutrition and it's determinant factors. Sustainability is exceptionally important to build on increasing awareness and level of knowledge on maternal nutrition, IYCF, importance of handwashing, vaccination and other nutrition-related factors among the community members. This will depend on the two key conditions: 1) communities’ willingness and ability to embrace the project beyond the intervention period; 2) the appropriateness and adaptability of the project itself. Community-led vulnerability assessment and analysis provide understand on local conditions, resources, needs, skills and knowledge. Ongoing engagement and communication with local and regional authorities, cluster partners as well as government officials will be critical for the success of the programme to ensure buy-in, ownership and sustainability.
