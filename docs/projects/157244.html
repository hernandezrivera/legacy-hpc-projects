Needs

A joint UN Mission assessment in September 2017 revealed that Um Dukhun town is served by a single basic health facility operated by IMC, and Garaaya village is served by a biweekly mobile clinic. There is no health facility or mobile clinic serving Beltebei residents, who must travel to Um Dukhun town for treatment. Overall, 26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care is offered in only 30% of facilities, with 40% providing Integrated Management of Childhood illness (IMCI) services. Only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated, with 55.9% of children vaccinated for measles. More than 2 million children U5 are undernourished, half of those suffering from severe acute malnutrition. Only 48% of children are exclusively breast fed (EBF) and 0% of non-breastfeeding U5 children receive the minimal acceptable diet. Only 8.2% of breastfeeding children under the age of five in Central Darfur receive the MAD. Given the high levels of acute malnutrition, poor socio-economic status and food insecurity, key micronutrient deficiencies are expected to contribute and further increase the undernutrition among children U5 and PLWs. Infections, malnutrition and lack of sanitation are all closely linked as they negatively affect morbidity and mortality rates. Malnutrition increases children’s susceptibility to infections because of its negative impact on normal immune system of the body, which can increase opportunistic infections. Diarrheal disease, respiratory infections, malaria, skin disease and eye infections are the top causes of morbidity in the State. Interventions to build capacity of MoH staff are desperately needed to provide sustainable, reliable health and nutrition services in these communities, accompanied by short-term relief efforts such as screening and treating malnutrition and community education and sensitization around health and nutrition.

Activities

Improved food security and livelihoods can reinforce the durability of short-term gains in health and nutrition indicators by giving families the capacity to weather future shocks. CRS will collaborate closely with the Ministry of Health, UN agencies, and INGO actors at the state, regional, and capital level. Activities: - Animal health services (including vaccination and de-worming of 30,000 animals) and training of 30 CAHWs• Support returnees and vulnerable host communities by establishing 30 Farmer Associations with agri-business training and 180 SILC (savings and internal lending) groups- Distribute 600 Goats/sheep, to 300 vulnerable HHs (2 goats/sheep per HH). • Establish, reactivate and strengthen community collective institutions such as; youth associations, producer associations, village savings and lending associations, Community Development Committees with the participation of all social groups to enhance collaborative livelihood activities as well as social cohesion• Support return communities with agro-enterprise training to raise their marketing skills and diversify livelihood opportunities• Vocational training targeting women and youth will open market opportunity for them and generate new employment opportunities• Agriculture inputs and provision of animals and veterinary services• Health services for the vulnerable and affected communities through medical consultations - train and equip 50 health workers• Screening of the children under 5 and PLWs acute malnutrition including SAM, MAM and MAM PLWs• Referrals of the identified malnourished children under 5 and PLWs to the respective OTP and TSFP sites for treatment

Impact on environment/natural resources

Healthier and more numerous animals will graze land if animal mortality is reduced, so impact on natural resources must be considered. Distributed animals will also require more land for grazing and thus have an impact on resources. In conjunction with DCPSF-funded Shaping Our World, this project will also consider competition over natural resources and the resulting impacts that improved livestock management may have. Youth agricultural associations and animal migration information systems help notify community about natural resource management issues. For animal vaccination campaigns and CAHW trainings, CRS will include waste and drug disposal training to enhance the benefits of training. CRS will promote safe disposal/recycling of vaccination materials. For Health, working with WES and referring to geophysical surveys will help the project avoid depleting the water table. Measures will be taken to reduce erosion around water sources provided through the health project.
