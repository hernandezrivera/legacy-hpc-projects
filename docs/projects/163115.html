Project Summary

Zimbabwe is suffering from the effects of economic crisis combined with climate shocks. Over the years, prices of basic commodities such as food and fuel have been rising steeply. Basic service delivery such as health, water and sanitation and education in government institutions have been a nightmare. Furthermore, the nation was hit by the EliNino induced drought of 2018/19 season and according to the Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) 2019 rural and urban surveys an estimated 7.7 million people are in need of food assistance during the 2019/2020 lean season assistance period.
The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe was worsened by Tropical Cyclone Idai which made a landfall in Zimbabwe on the 15th of March 2019. The cyclone smashed the districts of Chimanimani, Chipinge, Makoni, Buhera, Nyanga and Mutare in Manicaland Province as well as Bikita, Gutu and Zaka in Masvingo Province affecting about 270,000 people and causing significant loss of life, damages to homes, infrastructure and destroying livelihoods. The government of Zimbabwe and humanitarian actors intervened with emergency shelter and non-food item (NFIs) support. However, the assistance has managed to reach about 18% of the affected people due to limited resources. A situation assessment report by the International Organisation for Migration (IOM), compiled in November 2019 shown that a huge gap still exists to address the shelter needs of about 90,000 individuals in need of emergency shelter and 50,905 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) across all the affected districts. Most of the affected households have their homes either completely or partially destroyed hence they need assistance to rebuild their homes. Households who were assisted with emergency tarpaulins are in urgent need of permanent shelter as the temporary structures are not resilient to withstand the prevalent violent wind storms being experienced during the 2019/2020 rain season.
The onset of the 2019/2020 rain season has started badly. The rains have been characterised by strong hailstorms and whirlwinds in October and November 2019. According to IOM Situation Report of November 2019, the rains have affected the lives of more than 341 and has destroyed homes, schools, clinics and bridges in Buhera, Mutare, Chikomba, Chimanimani and Zaka districts.
The targeted districts of Chipinge and Buhera districts are prone to climatic related disasters such as cyclones, hailstorms and whirlwinds which have been recurring over the years.
It is against this background that Christian Care Zimbabwe Council of Churches is appealing for funding to implement a transitional shelter recovery program in Chipinge and Buhera districts targeting 10,000 individuals (49% female and 51% males) to meet their shelter needs in line with SPHERE minimum standards in a safe and dignified manner by December 2020. Beneficiaries of the project will be selected considering the findings of the shelter needs assessment combined with the community based participatory processes.
The appealing organisation have been active in Cyclone Idai Shelter Response activities in Chipinge, Chimanimani, Zaka, Gutu and Bikita districts since the onset of the cyclone in March 2019. It has assisted over 160 families to repair and reconstruct new homes in these districts. The organisation have been cleared by Manicaland Provincial Coordinator to conduct emergency shelter and recovery activities in any district of Manicaland.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

The planned activities of the program are shelter needs assessment, provision of shelter construction materials for repairs as well as facilitating the repairing of homes. Shelter needs assessments will be conducted at initial stage to determine the requirements of affected families in terms of materials and any support that may be required. Shelter repairs will be conducted to meet the needs that will be determined by the assessments. The project will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, shelter cluster humanitarian organisations and local leadership. The organisation will attend all coordination meetings that will be called by government departments, other partners and the Shelter Cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through recording of:1).Number of households assessed 2.) Number of affected households supported with transitional shelter
