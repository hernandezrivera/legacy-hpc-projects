Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to provide support in line with the spectrum of the Somalia food security cluster strategic objectives of:
1. Improved Access to Food and Safety Nets (IASN): SC will provide unconditional unrestricted cash assistance and food vouchers where appropriate to improve access to immediate food needs for the most vulnerable and marginalized households with emphasis on pregnant and lactating women (PLW), HHs with U2 and U5 children and households with disability. The cash transfer will be delivered through mobile money platform to minimize protection risks.
2. Protecting and restoring livelihoods related to food security and income sources. SC will provide livelihood inputs to the two main sources of livelihoods in Somalia i.e. crop and livestock agriculture. In the crop agriculture sector; seed inputs, tools, agriculture trainings, tillage hours, rehabilitating main agriculture related infrastructure e.g. canals will be done while in livestock agriculture, livestock treatment, provision of water and restocking where and when is possible.

Save the Children adopted mobile money transfer back in 2014 and in Somalia context Mobile money is a feasible transfer mechanism for a number of reasons, including wide community acceptance and strong private entities (eg Hormuud, Golis and Somtel), efficient and timely and fast set up, and safe and secure means for HHs to receive and manage cash transfers. Across Somalia in general, and in proposed program areas specifically, mobile money is a mechanism widely accepted for payments with a broad array of vendors operating as mobile money agents. According to findings from SC post distribution monitoring, shops and cash-out agents are generally available within a 30 minute to 1-hour walk for most HHs, with the exception of pastoralists who might take longer pending their location. Beneficiaries are able to both cash-out their accounts, or but make direct purchases through mobile money without cashing out, depending on their preferences.
Mobile money enables a timely and efficient transfer for cash to target as it restricts the shop/vendor and food commodities to be transferred to beneficiaries. It also offers improved safety and security compared to in-kind or cash transfers, which can expose HHs and women, as primary beneficiaries, to greater risk. It also minimizes unnecessary beneficiary movement and profiling at the point of payment.
SC will mobilize and sensitize mothers through IYCF sessions on how to utilize cash received to purchase quality and variety of foods for better nutrition outcomes for children, particularly in the first 1,000 days, as the primary cash recipients. SC will also carry out other bulk messaging on Fraud awareness creation, cash diversion and COVID-19 awareness messages to take advantage of the larger reach of cash transfer beneficiaries.
Cash programing will enable HHs to purchase preferred foods (rice, sorghum and wheat) from a wide number of shops and markets while also strengthening local drought-affected shops and markets, with a potential broader impact on markets than voucher programming, which typically works with only a few commodities and or vendors. In addition, cash is most appropriate to address reduced purchasing power through improved food access, while also increasing demand in local markets with resultant stimulation of market growth and increase of trading opportunities.
SC has been monitoring the market situation at key markets (both intervention and supply markets) in all target regions since June 2018.

Project Activities

1. Unconditional and unrestricted cash transfers targeting 38,604 HHs (231,624 vulnerable and marginalized people). 1.1 Community mobilization and beneficiary identification. SC will carry out awareness creation to target communities about the project, their allocation and responsibilities in collaboration with the Village Relief Committees (VRC). SC’s new cash transfer SOPs sets milestones for women representation in VRCs (40% of the VRCs should be women). A targeting criterion to ensure inclusive targeting of most vulnerable and marginalized groups will be agreed upon including targeting PLWs and U2& U5 children to ensure better nutrition outcomes. SC will engage a third party to monitor and evaluate the process by conducting interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to verify fairness of the selection process. Baseline assessment and protection measures (safety and dignity) will be undertaken.1.2 Monthly cash transfers. Based on Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for communities in IPC 3, 4 and worse, the value will be 100% whilst communities in IPC 3 will be 80%. Payment will be made on a monthly basis through mobile phone. 2/3 of beneficiaries will be women.1.3 Post distribution monitoring (PDM). Proactive calls will be done after every transfer to solicit beneficiary feedback. Beneficiaries will have access to the project complaint and response toll free telephone number throughout the project period. For transfers more than 3 rounds, SC will also conduct PDM after 2 weeks of the transfers to assess usage of cash purchasing power and spot check operational efficient of the transfer. SC will commission an independent third-party monitoring. 1.4 End line evaluation. Will be done by SC Monitoring, Evaluation Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team to evaluate the extent the project has achieved its intended outcomes, impact on beneficiaries, lessons learnt and good practices. 2. Provision of agriculture and livestock seasonal inputs. The project will support poor pastoral and agro pastoral communities to protect and recover their livelihoods and increase income through providing livestock and agriculture inputs. 2.1. Beneficiary selection: Community based targeting will be pegged on cash supported HHs, those in IPC 3and 4, high Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates and villages not targeted by other organizations.Beneficiaries will be registered using KOBO, a mobile phone data collection platform.2.2. Livestock treatment. This will be done though a voucher system by the Ministry of Livestock veterinary teams. This will be conducted during in Xagaa season (July to September). 2.3. Post-Distribution Monitoring. SC will conduct post-treatment monitoring at least one month after the treatment to assess the exercise. 3. Provision of agriculture inputs. 3.1. Beneficiary Selection: Vulnerability criteria will guide in selecting beneficiaries prioritizing HHs with low purchasing power or those who have no access to humanitarian support and are likely to cultivate less land or fail to plant at all due to lack of production inputs priority given to female-headed HHs, HHs headed by elderly people, poor returnees, and HHs headed by children or disabled people. 3.2. Provision of seeds and tools: Crop production kit will be aligned to the recommended packages outlined in the guidance note on the provision of agricultural inputs in Somalia provided by the Food Security Cluster , and Household Economy Analysis (HEA) minimum package in some livelihood zones. SC will identify competitive suppliers who will deliver seeds/tools to the sites and distribute to the community. Commodity vouchers will be used to redeem items to increase accountability. Where possible SC will explore cash for seeds and seed fair. 3.3. Agriculture training. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

SC will use cash as modality of delivering assistance to the most deprived communities, due to its feasibility in Somalia supported by relatively market stability and prevalence of mobile money. Cash supports dignity and provides choice to the household. Should food items not be available in markets, SC will propose other modalities.

Mitigation Measures

SC will ensure that beneficiaries will not be exposed to danger during registration and when receiving assistance. SC will not ask beneficiaries to travel to far places in order to be registered or receive assistance. SC will support communities where they are living or located. Cash will be delivered via mobile phones where beneficiaries will not need to travel to far places to receive assistance. SC will employ geo targeting where support will be channeled to places where the need is greatest e.g. areas of IPC 3 or worse and to IDPs particularly. SC is aiming to cover as many beneficiaries as possible so that those left will not have to migrate. SC has strong safeguarding policies including sexual harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse policy, SC has mainstreamed this policy with implementation of gender equality policies into the programs, this include making 40% of cash village relief committees women with a role into committees.The project will deliberately prevent risk of exclusion of most vulnerable and marginalized groups (based on gender, age, minority clans, IDPs, returnees, persons with disabilities, etc) by taking the following: • Awareness raising and sensitization: SC will start sensitizing stakeholders including community leaders and local authorities on the objective, processes and targets of the project. The organization will than meet people in target communities in a public place where we will ensure all those who can attend come to the meeting. In these meetings, information about the project including objective, entitlements, duration, targeting criteria, targeting approach will be shared. • Project relief committee: after sensitization, communities will be asked to establish project committees. To do this, communities will be asked to establish them in public and endorse the committee in public. The committee will represent all different groups and age brackets (excluding children but youth), and any other groups including women and minority. SC will sign an MoU with the committee which will detail the role of committee and beneficiary’s selection criteria. • Setting selection criteria: SC will, in consultation with the local authorities where possible, and by endorsement of the community, establish a selection criterion which includes but not limited to: Very poor HHs with limited income, few assets, and no external support, IDPs and newly displaced or evicted HHs, high number of malnourished children under 5, child headed HHs, female headed HHs, elderly-headed HHs, disabled HH members, pregnant and/or lactating women among others. This will help us ensure minimize age-based inclusion and exclusion. • House to house and public verification: Community committees will do the selection. Once the list is submitted by the committee, SC, committee and local leaders will take a sample (10% -12%) from the list and verify them physically, after which a public verification will be conducted. • Setting community response mechanism: The action will establish community response and feedback mechanism. A hotline will be provided and time will be given before registration. During verification and mobilization, SC MEAL team will provide a help desk. SC will sensitize communities on how best they can provide their feedback about the program and therefore establish safe and accessible CRM. SC will avoid community tensions between displaced and host communities by providing the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets and inputs. SC will consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between communities. Please provide further explanation

The project will enable IDPs/returnees and vulnerable host communities to meet their immediate consumption needs and then thus reduce distressful coping strategies exposing them and their children to protection risks. Save the Children will collaborate and link IDPs/returnees to long-term food security and livelihoods projects (implemented by SC or partners) in order to enable them settle/stabilize and build their resilience. The project will be linked to SC and other partner’s long terms resilience building project i.e. BMZ in Awdal, BRCiS-DFID-2, WB and BMZ in Southern States and RESTORE in Bari and Nugaal.
