Project Summary

Following the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) approach that IOM strongly supports and actively promotes in Cameroon, and building on the programmes and activities implemented in the recent years, IOM plans interventions for 2022 along two main axis; humanitarian emergency response and promotion of peace and development. Humanitarian emergency response will focus on coordination, protection, shelter and health interventions, while HDPN focus will be strengthened through early recovery interventions. IOM Cameroon will mainstream protection in all its proposed programmes.
IOM’s multisectoral response in the Far-North will be composed of the following activities:
Coordination Sector
Contributing to the overall highest level of humanitarian coordination in the Far-North by sharing timely and accurate information on displacement figures and trends, IOM plans to:
- Conduct regular Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNAs) in coordination with humanitarian sectors and OCHA.
- Conduct mobility tracking rounds to track the trends of displaced populations within crisis areas.
- Produce and distribute regular IOM emergency tracking tool (ETT) dashboards, that present reliable and up-to-date information on the sudden displacements events, triggers, trends, needs with the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) and other humanitarian actors.
- Collect information on multi-sectoral indicators of stability within crisis areas in order to help facilitate geographic targeting and strategic planning for transition and recovery programming.
- Collect information on the return intentions for displaced populations in crisis areas.
- Support local humanitarian actors’ coordination platform (CHOI) for the conduct and supervision of an MSNA in the Center region.
Shelter/Non-Food Item (NFI) Sector
IOM plans to work closely with the Shelter/NFI Cluster in the Far-North to provide a range of different shelter solutions to displaced populations, with specific interventions including:
- Distribution of 2,000 emergency shelter kits to populations who have been recently displaced and are in need of quick assistance to meet their basic needs, with technical support to a proportion of beneficiaries for their construction.
- Distribution of 500 transitional shelters to protractedly displaced populations that are expected to be in need of a shelter solution for more than six months, with technical support to a proportion of beneficiaries for their construction including through the development of information, education and communication (IEC) materials.
- Usage of cash-based modalities to respond to the needs of displaced populations (200 households) who are based in urban and peri-urban areas that are renting shelters.
- Distribution of 2,500 NFI kits including basic household and cooking items, but also emergency Water, Sanitation and Hygiene items.
Health sector
IOM aims to bolster the well-being of crisis-affected populations through the implementation of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) activities. Actions will be tailored to the age and gender of beneficiaries and will include:
- Further needs assessment to understand the key challenges, psychosocial needs of different groups and opportunities to MHPSS services in crisis affected areas.
- Capacity building for existing MHPSS structures on topics such as Psychological First Aid (PFA), basic MHPSS, knowledge of referral pathways.
- The strengthening or establishing of safe spaces for women, children and youth.
- Provide direct MHPSS interventions such as socio-relational activities (e.g., sport and play activities, creative and art-based activities, non-formal education) through the deployment of a multidisciplinary psychosocial mobile team (PMT.
- Continue co-leading the MHPSS working group in Far-North with the holding of monthly meetings.
Early recovery sector
- Ensure targeted displaced communities have increased opportunities for inclusive and participatory dialogue.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian needs in Cameroon remain high. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2021, approximately 4.0 million people (or one in six) are in need of emergency assistance as the country has three humanitarian fronts (NWSW, Far-North, East refugee crisis) to cope with, in addition to a global health crisis. In the Far-North region, ongoing hostilities and violence have heavily impacted local populations during the past five years, where displacements are still triggered due to small scale sporadic attacks by Non-State armed groups as well as seasonal floods, other disasters and intercommunal tensions. The region has witnessed continuous hostilities that have uprooted more than 357,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 51,000 out-of-camp refugees and 135,000 returnees (IOM Mobility tracking Round 23, Displacement Tracking Matrix, July 2021) . Insecurity in this region is exacerbating the already limited access of displaced populations to essential social services such as education and health as looting and destruction of goods and public infrastructure are becoming more recurrent, further harming community cohesion and leading to higher inter-community tensions. Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility. Low funding has meant that there is a greater need in the Far-North for the reinforcement of a Rapid Response Mechanism. Recently, additional coordination has been made between Premiere Urgence International (Far-North RRM coordinator) and IOM in order to better synergize efforts through the RRM. The last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in the Far-North region (July 2021) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors. For the Shelter/NFI sector, 44 % of respondents noted that the principal problem with shelters was that they were too populated, and 33 % noted that they were of a poor standard. This was coupled with the findings that more households lacked a series of basic NFIs such as hygienic towels (72%), toothbrushes (59%) and bed mats (57%). For the health sector, 64 % of respondents noted that they had difficulty accessing health services, with the principal reasons being that they were too costly (42%), the service they needed was too specialized (20%) or the infrastructure present was not sufficient (10%). Women and girls were the most impacted by lack of access to health services (38%) followed by pregnant and lactating women (33%). In addition, IOM data collection has revealed that return movements are representing 25 % of total displacements in the Far North region (Mobility Tracking Round 23). However, this does not indicate that displaced populations are returning back to their communities due to an enhanced security situation, as it has been found a high number of these returns are due to negative reasons. In response to the needs identified, IOM plans to provide a multi-sectoral response to alleviate the suffering of displaced populations, host communities and other persons in need within the Far-North through two axis of response, with humanitarian activities for the provision of timely and reliable information on displacement figures, needs and trends (coordination sector), the distribution of emergency shelters, and NFIs partly using cash-modality solutions (Shelter/NFI Cluster) and through the provision of direct MHPSS assistance to populations impacted by crisis using PMTs and capacity building of MHPSS actors including local authorities (Health sector), while also using additional analysis on return intentions and community dialogue for more durable solutions (early recovery sector).
