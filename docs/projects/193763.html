Project Summary

The Logistics Sector 2023 is an internal coordination platform with UN Agencies and NGOs, in order to collect, compile, analyze and disseminate information on supply chain procedures to be followed for shipping humanitarian cargo to Gaza / West Bank.
In the light of previous crisis (Gaza escalations, Covid19, ... etc.) and what is happening these days in West Bank has emerged that the efficiency of a logistics common system depends on the level of preparedness. Thus, the proposal aims to prospect possible crisis by creating the basis for a logistics coordination mechanism and establishing service platform and procedures that can be swiftly activated at the moment of need to improve the efficiency of the logistics response.

1 - Needs

The Palestine Logistics Sector was established just after the May 2021 escalation, because the delivery of humanitarian relief and humanitarian cargo in the Gaza strip were facing several challenges due to restricted access. Kerem Shalom crossing point has been the main entry for cargo movements in Gaza. Movement of cargo have been limited to only certain type of items like food, NFIs and drugs & pharmaceutical products; and from August 2021 there was also a significant increase of construction material allowed to cross in Gaza. Many other items remain restricted and require sometimes lengthy coordination processes that are not adequate to respond to the emergency needs in Gaza. The Logistics Sector was locally established to scale up and augment the existing logistics capacity, to provide coordination and information management to the partners in Gaza and West Bank, and to overcome potential logistics bottlenecks related to the cargo importation mechanisms. The Logistics Sector approach was established to provide a single coordination structure to the different humanitarian actors (UN Agencies, INGOs, NGOs) that have humanitarian, recovery, and development projects; and to enhance predictability and efficiency of the humanitarian response under the logistics coordinate approach.

2 - Activities

This project will support the HRP coordination strategic objectives through the following:SO 1: Access support provided to humanitarian staff and programmes:1) Guidance and Procedures - Provide overall supply chain guidance on procedures to be followed for shipping humanitarian cargo in Gaza / West Bank.2) NGO support – Support I/NNGOs who are the most affected by the border closure for a harmonized and informed process through support engagement and dealing with relevant authorities.3) Best Practices, Coordination Meetings and Workshops.4) Capacity Building - Invest into partners’ logistics capacity in Gaza / West Bank on a regular basis, building on Warehouse Management training and new trainings on preparedness, emergency response and health supply chain to be held for Gaza only in 2023 (as in West Bank it was done in 2022). SO 2: Coordination meetings are held at country and operational levels to ensure a common situation awareness and timely, appropriate and effective humanitarian response.5) Information Sharing, Analysis and Coordination Meetings. Engage the partners in sharing projects logistics data on planning, priorities, and bottlenecks faced during the importation of cargo in Gaza / West Bank. Assess gaps to avoid duplications, and to promote a harmonized logistics response. 6) Sector Information Dissemination - Provide information management services with a dedicated Logistics Sector webpage, through situation updates, meeting minutes, assessments, and logistics snapshots with the aim of supporting operational decisions making to improve the efficiency of the logistics response with:• logistics gaps and bottlenecks,• logistics infrastructures geographic information systems/mapping tools and products ,• operational information on roads conditions, borders closure & entry points, and Israeli authorities’ official communications.7) Reporting and Information Management- On quarterly basis, produce an operation infographic overview of the different organizations involved, the sectors of intervention, the type of materials imported, the importation mechanism used, and the common challenges faced by the logistics partners. SO 3: Strategic humanitarian response is updated at all levels and sectors, as needed, before end 2023 and Preparedness measures for humanitarian emergencies are in place.8) Preparedness Measures - Trainings on emergency response, procurement, transportation medical logistics & health supply chain.9) HRP update – Based on partner’s lessons learned, data collected and analyzed during the year, and logistics best practices; update the HRP plan with relevant activities aligned with the correct strategy in order to support partners and beneficiaries in the most effective way. 10) Cross-sectoral Inputs - Contribute to inter sectorial coordination at the central level with the Identification of needs and response modalities that may require inter-cluster adhoc harmonized response.11) Support Fundraising - Develop and support resource mobilization and partnership strategies to strengthen relations with other clusters, UN agencies, governments, donors, I/NNGOs and other key partners.

The Logistics Sector approach is to engage the entire logistics humanitarian community in order to respond in the most effective manner. To do that, the Logistics Sector collects needs and requirements from partners on the ground through different mechanisms and approaches such as: coordination meetings, bilateral discussions, workshop, survey and field assessments. All relevant information is then collected, compiled, analyzed, and the key results disseminated to the entire humanitarian community through the ICCG and HCT, in order to find the best way to support beneficiaries’ needs in the field.The sector includes representatives of UN Agencies, I/NNGOs and the Red Cross societies to ensure provide one of the right supports, at the right time, through the right approaches, to different humanitarian partners. Individual I/NNGOs and UN Agencies that are partners of the Logistics Sector build their own programs based on the input from the beneficiaries they serve.

The Logistics Sector is coordinated through the World Food Programme, and accordingly adheres and implements WFP’s Accountability to Affected Populations Strategy and Guidance. The sector operationalizes this guidance through ensuring that affected populations’ input and feedback are taken into account to inform the sector plans and implementation. These AAP control and risk management mechanisms are effective tools to mitigate the risk of abuse, protect the rights of beneficiaries and WFP’s accountability towards them. The Logistics Sector uses WFP Palestine’s several channels for any feedback or complaint: complaint boxes that are available in shops participating in general food assistance activities, feedback and complaints hotline telephone system which is available to all Palestinians (beneficiaries and non- Beneficiaries).

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The Logistics Sector will emphasize with the different partners the needs and obligation to respect Do No Harm principle while implement their projects in the field. We would prepare a survey to be sure that all humanitarian actors that have logistics operation in Palestine adopt a specific policy on this subject. Additionally, in adherence with WFP policies the logistics sector facilitates the activities of the entire humanitarian community based on need alone, without discrimination, and contributes to the safety, dignity, and integrity of beneficiaries by giving them a voice in decisions that affect their lives.
