Project Summary

Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are only a few of the major shocks that have uprooted at least 2.9 million Somalis. In urban, semi-urban, and rural locations around the country, the bulk of displaced persons have self-settled in over 2,400 substandard and unplanned IDP settlements. Persons who have been displaced to these locations are living in dangerous situations, with the majority of them not being able to meet their basic needs due to inconsistencies in service delivery or being denied access to humanitarian aid. More than 80% of sites are informal settlements situated on private land, with the bulk of sites located in metropolitan areas, according to the CCCM cluster's comprehensive site assessment findings (DSA) and site verification assessments.
In Somalia, internally displaced persons (IDPs) are the most vulnerable population group: Somalia's IDP population continues to exhibit traits that make them more vulnerable than people living in host communities, whether they are urban or rural. Factors like as deplorable living conditions in settlements, inclusion challenges, and overall disenfranchisement in obtaining key services continue to be common among IDPs, making it even more challenging for these populations to achieve substantive change. Persons with disabilities (PWDs), members of minority clans, women, and youth are among the populations in IDP camps with the most pressing needs, and they face significant challenges to receiving humanitarian aid, preventing them from living in dignified conditions.
Therefore, the overall goal of the proposed initiative is to improve the living conditions and general security of IDP population in the camps, as well as provide fair access to services and assistance for all those in need. Through this, the initiative is anticipated to strengthen safe access to multi sectorial services at site level through improved site management and coordination. Secondly, the project will improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance and decongestion initiatives. Lastly, the project is anticipated to strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations.
The outcome of the initiative is to have site- level information available and shared with stakeholders. The activities that will generate this outcome include; conducting site verifications on bi-annual basis, establishing or updating service mapping of partners operating in various sites at the target districts. Carry out service monitoring activities on monthly basis aimed at highlighting service gaps at the site-level and ensuring that minimum standards are being adhered to.
The second outcome is to have activities aimed at improving site-level living conditions administered across the regions targeted by the project. This will include; providing trainings on-site maintenance and site planning to camp committees; supporting community-led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees, distribution of tools); implementing emergency sites improvement activities to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, fire prevention). Administer joint stakeholder site decongestion activities allowing for short-term improvements in settlement standards, minimizing protection threats and ameliorating COVID-19 transmission. Conduct safety audits annually to inform site improvement activities and to illustrate targeted initiatives that sector agencies should administered
Lastly, the project aims at ensuring that all sites in the target districts have a governance structure in place that serves to fulfil the needs of the displaced population. This will be achieved by undertaking mapping of existing community structures; providing core trainings (CMC Training Package) to community governance structures; identifying and supporting governance structures, encouraging participation.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

IRDO will set up a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in which a center will be established be managed by IRDO. This will ensure prevention of GBV/PSEA is enhanced in all IDP site where the project will be implemented. It will also facilitate easy and quick reporting of GBV issues in the IDP settlements. IRDO will also conduct consultative meetings that will ensure that the IDP population embrace efforts to carry out site decongestion activities so as to ensure safety and life saving strategies are laid across all the IDP settlements. Site decongestion activities will be carried out according to the stipulated guidelines and agreed upon by the community and other partners drawn from other clusters like shelter and HLP. Similarly, IRDO will ensure that service mapping in all the sites and site monitoring will be conducted to facilitate the identification and location of referrals.
