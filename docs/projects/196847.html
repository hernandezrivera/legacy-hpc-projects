Project Summary

The project aims at reducing vulnerability and improving physical and mental well-being of crisis-affected population with particular attention to specific vulnerabilities linked to Disability, Gender, and Age (DGA). The INGO action focuses on providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation services tailored to the needs of people with physical impairment and their caregivers through direct intervention or through the support of local partners in North-West, North-East and GoS-controlled parts of Syria. The intervention includes provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services and psychosocial support services to physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and their caregivers. Furthermore, to ensure continuity of the tailored treatment plans, remote follow up services are provided through tele rehabilitation/tele-PSS sessions in case of movement restrictions, due to COVID-19 or existing gender norms. These services help in recovery of physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and support the caregivers through a holistic approach in order to alleviate stress, fatigue and other psychological impacts related to their responsibilities. The project improve access to services through referral, accessibility work in health structures, training of (female) rehabilitation staff and integration of the community through Self-Help Groups, which will increase resilience of beneficiaries and services provided, awareness raising sessions and mobile outreach to increase access for IDPs, underserved communities and persons who are incapable of reaching the service centers. The INGO also mainstreams COVID-19 key messaging in the activities package according to WHO Guidelines. In GoS- controlled areas and NWS the intervention includes the provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to people suffering from psychological distress without having physical rehabilitation needs. MHPSS services include psychological first aid, psycho education, and individual and/or group session therapy. Across whole of Syria including GoS controlled areas, the INGO focuses on developing rehabilitation services in areas where services are not available. Concerning DGA mainstreaming, all activities and delivered services are prepared based on a rapid assessment to study the sensitivity of the context and ensure inclusion according to DGA. This includes the promotion of equal opportunities for people with different needs and abilities. In North-West conditional cash for transportation is also provided to patients and caregivers according to individual assessed needs based on pre-defined criteria. The INGO closely coordinate with the Cash working group in the targeted hub to align the common standards and guidance. Conditional cash will be provided to patients and/or caregivers based on individual assistance and the INGO criteria taking into account socioeconomic, travel, and clinical factors. The health staff assess the needs case by case based on predefined criteria. The cash is conditioned to the attendance of patient to the treatment sessions aiming to ensure the rationalization of the treatment plan. Acknowledging the dire economic situation in WoS, where possible close linkages with other actors are envisaged to support beneficiaries accessing unconditional cash and support actors to enhance inclusion within CVA programs. The INGO provides support to its local health partners through technical, financial and managerial support, to improve the quality of services and ensure the sustainability of impact of the project. The capacity building support is tailored to the needs of partners. The Inclusion specialist support the health clusters, by guiding through several session on how to implement DGA inclusion principles, how to identify vulnerability risks of persons with disabilities, setting indicators on data for identification of persons with disabilities. In addition to trainings related to understanding needs of PwD including Women.

3 - Needs

An estimated 15.3M people need health assistance (HNO/HRP2023). Syria is one of worst affected countries in the Middle East and North Africa region from COVID-19. In 2022, OHCHR documented 450 civilian casualties, ground-based shelling, armed clashes in NWS, and explosive ordnance incidents, including improvised explosive devices, and landmines. An overall lessening in hostilities reduced population movements since March 2020, there is some evidence that the increasingly dire economic situation overall These factors prompted pre-emptive movement towards camps and sites where there is more likely to be humanitarian assistance. Access to health was reported among top priorities by 60% of HH with at least one family member with disability. Main barriers included lack of existing services, or available but too expensive, lack of finances to cover transportation. For mental health, crisis continues to affect psychosocial well-being, exposure to violence, loss of income and reduced access to basic services, touching the youngest in particular. The psychological distress in boys and girls under the age of 18 are reported by more than 27% of households and are the highest for female-headed households or when the head of household is living with a disability. Overall, 26 per cent of households reported that the reason their children do not want to go to school is because the child feels depressed, unhappy or lacks motivation (HNO/HRP2023).

4 - Implementation of activities

This project is a continuation of the services provided to Syrian population, INGO works with partners and relies on mobile teams in NWS, NES and GoS. Rehab services will be provided by gender-balanced teams, and will include physical and functional rehab sessions, provision of ADs, P&O devices, education sessions and/or PSS sessions. MHPSS will be provided to persons affected by psychosocial and psychological issues caused by conflict, displacement and multiple losses. Persons in needs are identified through outreach teams and other services. Identification and needs assessment of persons with injuries and/or disabilities will be done in hospitals, rehab centers, IDP camps, communities. Health services, specifically multi-disciplinary rehabilitation services (Psychosocial support, education and PSS to caregivers, P&O) will be provided with support of 3 rehab centers in GoS, 7 Hospitals/rehab centers in NES, 6 hospitals and 1 rehab center in NWS. Facility-based services will be complemented by mobilization of outreach teams in the hard-to-reach areas to improve access to services. Health services aims to prevent of longer-term impairment and promotion of independence for PwDs, INGO will strengthen national technical capacity by providing recommendations and action plans, training, coaching and follow ups. These needs-based activities were designed to help vulnerable people to be able to overcome their functional limitations and able to do their daily tasks.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection of field staff and partners, INGO keep low visibility to protect identities of staff. In case of an attack on health care, activates its DoC policy and provide elderly staff with psychological support to prevent burn out. •INGO applies PSEA policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. Also raises awareness on its policy on PSEA and complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. Part of inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to staff and its partners to highlight vulnerabilities faced by persons with disabilities, person of older age and girls and women (GBV, sexual exploitation, etc.) is implemented. The INGO has protection task force, to ensure protection mainstreaming is optimized. Referral task force works towards improving protection referrals and processes. •Healthcare Provision for vulnerable groups and persons with disabilities and equitable access to health services; staff regularly trained on how to interact, communicate and engage with persons with disabilities. •Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field workers are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data. •Working through implementing partners: before becoming an implementing partner, each actor goes through a thorough assessment and vetting process. Reporting is required, monitoring visits, feedback and response mechanism are in place.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Also implement measures to reduce risks on programs. •Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: PSEAH and Child Protection policies and Code of Conduct is effective; Mandatory and refresher PSEAH training and other safeguarding policies for staff and partners; reporting mechanism is effective •New employee is not aware of policy: all candidates seeking employment shall undergo recruitment process and submit background checks. Partners are involved in actions of abuse or harassment against beneficiaries: Any proven allegations of such practices shall result in follow-up of the case and termination of agreement unless the partner commits to and effectively ensures a radical change in behavior.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Security context deteriorates substantially in intervention areas - Military control shifts in the area of operations- A security incident involves directly staff and/or partner staff -Local authorities refuse the necessary authorisations and deny access -Authorities of a cross-border country refuse the necessary authorisations and deny access as well as UNSC cross border resolution uncertainty -Intervention is affected by aid diversion and/or corruption/fraud -Shortages in local markets and difficulties in crossborder supplies -The available budget is reduced due to exchange rate loss (Syrian Pound and Turkish Lira depreciation) -Lack of qualified staff in the area of operation Mitigation measures: -Weekly conflict analysis and daily follow up of security situation; contingency planning for remote management, shift of operations to partners -Daily follow up security situation; Clear security guidelines and SOPs in place for staff; Support to partners to analyze and manage risks -Adapting activities according to the security context -Advocacy effort at international level to maintain, at least, one border crossing point under UNSC resolution and scenarios ready to be implemented in case of non-renewal -Secure stock of key project goods and items / Maintain regular communication with donor and discuss the revision of targets/budget if required / Purchase in "strong currency" when possible -Building capacities to local staff and partner on long term
