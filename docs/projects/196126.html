Project Summary

First intervention
The project aims to increase the resilience of the flood-affected communities in Harim, thus reducing their dependence on aid, and securing them increased access to livelihood opportunities, which are among the most vulnerable groups of society. The project will focus on the displaced and the host community as well.
The project aims to implement major infrastructure rehabilitation projects to improve access to basic services and basic infrastructure for the most vulnerable IDPs and the host community and those living in Harem with the aim of accessing livelihoods through the creation of Quick Impact Work Opportunities (CfW) for 300 displaced families from the most vulnerable population In Harem camp (200 men, 100 women; 40 of them are people with special needs).
The focus of this project will be on implementing the following projects
Rehabilitation of public markets
Rehabilitation of public service facilities: such as the fire station
Rehabilitation of rainwater drainage canals
Rehabilitation of important roads
Rehabilitation of public transport facilities: such as bus stations
The rehabilitation total activities 620,050.
Second intervention
Karim Microfinance Fund.
A developmental and socio-economic project that adopts the principle of microfinance developed by the Sham Humanitarian Foundation, provides lending services Youth to men and women on easy and recoverable terms without interest, establishes a work culture and the principle of self-reliance, provides free lending and secures job opportunities AHF has been operating in the region since 2018, and is disbursing funds through PTT according to the name of the beneficiary So that the level of young people is raised and qualified to secure opportunities to open new projects
Project design system reduces the risk of non-payment, periodic monitoring and provides guidance to beneficiaries.
The project aims to support livelihoods by providing the opportunity for those with the skill, ability and desire to work to obtain the necessary liquidity to accelerate and develop their own activities, which helps to create new job opportunities and increase income for the beneficiaries
The project will provide financial inclusion and lending services to300 new clients, and the loan amount will reach $600as a first stage, and from the monthly redemption amounts loans will be provided to other clients, and it is expected that the number of clients will increase by 90additional clients/borrowers in the following year.
The project consists of3phases that together form an integrated plan to get out of poverty that gradually moves customers from extreme poverty to the poverty line and then to the middle income group
The project will contribute to attracting Syrians to enter the labor market, reduce unemployment, integrate the displaced into the host community, and empower women targeted to contribute to strengthening the local economy, combating poverty, and supporting livelihoods.
Third intervention
160 IDPs will be selected from the FSL beneficiaries and will be provided with vocational training to move from aid dependency to the production stage, the selection of beneficiaries will be according to pre-defined selection criteria and from the most vulnerable IDPs in Dana sub-district camps.
the topics will be identified according to a market assessment.
The trainings will be evaluated according to the needs of the market and the desire of stakeholders in cooperation with the community committees inside the camps from the long list, as the training period is four months to give ourselves enough space to divide the trainees into 8 groups and train 4 groups (80 trainees) only per day, and in kind grants will be distributed according to the type of training
80 in-kind grants will be provided to those who have committed to training and passed the exams, and the cost of each grant will be $1,200, which will make them start their own businesses.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Low level of obligations related to sexual exploitation and abuse, women disproportionately exposure to misconduct by humanitarian workers at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, and low level of safe reporting of complaints of SEA.Mitigation:1 - Impose mandatory training on protection from PSEAon all employees, including management, field staff, day laborers, volunteers, contractors and vendors, in each area where there are active processes to ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and to equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to both. 2 - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from SEA at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.3 - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.4 - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and societyAll project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA
