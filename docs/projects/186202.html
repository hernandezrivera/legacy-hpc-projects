Project Summary

WFP will be implementing food assistance to crisis-affected populations under Strategic Outcome 1 of the WFP Cameroon's Country Strategic Plan 2022 - 2026. Apart from implementing activities in the other regions, for the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, WFP specifically aims to address the essential food needs of the internally displaced persons (IDPs) and vulnerable local communities affected by crises in rural and peri-urban areas. Activities under this outcome will be implemented as an enhanced and integrated support package. WFP will provide life-saving, conflict-sensitive assistance in crisis-affected areas, responding effectively to augmented needs, focusing on unconditional assistance with food and cash transfers coupled with enhanced malnutrition prevention and treatment in targeted areas, supplemented by emergency school feeding, and social and behaviour change communication (SBCC) across all activities. Expansion of CBT, providing sustainable pathways to self-reliance, recovery and resilience activities, will be a critical component in tailoring WFP’s emergency response.
In the North-West Region, WFP will target 262,990 beneficiaries of which 212,000 will be targeted under the general food assistance (GFA) from the seven divisions of Boyo, Bui, Donga Mantung, Menchum, Mezam, Momo and Ngoketungia. 89% of GFA beneficiaries will be assisted through in-kind support and 11% through cash-based transfers (CBT). WFP will also provide nutritious foods (presented as a separate HRP project) to 19,590 children under the age of 2 years and 8,400 pregnant and lactating women and adolescent girls (PLW/G). For school going children, WFP plans to reach 13,000 school-doing children through emergency school feeding (also presented as a separate project) using in-kind and value voucher system, homegrown school feeding and take-home rations.
In the South-West Region, WFP plans to reach 132,330 beneficiaries from the six divisions of Fako, Lebialem, Kupe Muanenguba, Meme, Manyu and Ndian. Of the total, 91,000 will be supported through GFA of which 67% and 23% will be under in-kind and CBT, respectively. Through integration, WFP will implement emergency on-site feeding, home-grown school feeding and take-home rations in the targeted schools/locations, reaching 12,000 school-going children (presented in a separate project). 16,930 under-2 children and 2,400 PLW/G from households identified to be food-insecure will also receive nutritious foods (presented as a separate HRP project) as treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition alongside gender- and nutrition- informed SBCC.
For each region, WFP further plans to introduce early recovery activities, reaching out to 20,000 beneficiaries (10,000 per region). Targeted beneficiaries will receive cash for assets creation while enhancing food security.
WFP will cooperate with the government and other partners in ensuring that the planned activities are smoothly implemented in the targeted regions.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since 2017, the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon have been caught up in a socio-political crisis between State Security Forces and Non-State Armed Groups. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan, there are nearly 770,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. The deteriorating security situation caused by escalating hostilities between the two sides has continued to affect the food security of the households, especially the displaced populations and smallholder farmers who are already extremely vulnerable due to the disruption of their livelihood activities since the conflict began four years ago. Nearly one million people are estimated to be food insecure in the two regions between June and August 2021 (Cadre Harmonise, 2021). Preliminary results from the October 2021 food security assessment update (Cadre Harmonise) indicates that the NWSW regions are the most food insecure regions in Cameroon, with about 1.2 million people (26% of the population) in IPC phase 3 and 4. The impacts of the crisis have been worsened by the COVID-19 pandemic, which is disrupting livelihoods and food systems thus leading to fluctuations in food prices. The humanitarian space is also shrinking due to security risks.
