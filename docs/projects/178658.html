4 - Needs

Despite the reduced level of hostilities and violence in many key densely populated areas of the country by the end of 2019, millions of Syrians continue to face huge challenges in accessing basic services, due to widespread infrastructure destruction, erosion of the productive base, scarce resources, and massive internal displacement. The social fabric of the country has been severely damaged. According to HNO 2021 report, there are 11.6 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support given the cumulative impact of the crisis on the population and broader macro-economic developments. While areas exposed to active hostilities have shrunk over time, populations continue to experience poor living standards 25 percent of basic and social infrastructure is destroyed. approximately 60 percent of males and females who are part of the workforce are without sustained access to employment due to lack of livelihood and income-earning opportunities as well as vocational skills. Such high rates of lack of sustained access to employment is a result of years of crisis with women, female-headed households, people with disability and elderly being particularly vulnerable. The local context analyses indicate that poor economic conditions, physical destruction of infrastructure, and large-scale displacement have led to competition over scarce livelihood opportunities and overburdening of available social services. A combination of these factors is also responsible for posing a significant hindrance to some of the displaced people wanting to voluntarily return to the places of their origin, forcing them to live in a state of constant uncertainty and to remain dependent upon humanitarian assistance for survival

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) considerations are overriding components of all activities. This project will take several steps (training staff, use of the feedback mechanism, recruitment of male and female staff, and clauses in contracts) to mitigate any susceptibility to such risks. QRC is an active member of the PSEA Network in Gaziantep and has supported the establishment of the inter-agency reporting mechanism to ensure procedures and materials are in line with community understanding and preferences. QRC staff sign a code of conduct, including protocols to ensure that sensitive beneficiary information is kept confidential. We have a feedback mechanism (FM) that includes a mobile phone and WhatsApp hotline number for beneficiaries, other stakeholders, and the affected population in general, to provide feedback (positive or negative).Information about the mechanism is distributed broadly through activities (i.e., posters in public areas and during outreach). One dedicated staff is responsible for monitoring the feedback number, logging all messages, and alerting the appropriate team member about relevant feedback in a way that is sensitive towards the complainant, and taking necessary action to respond. Where necessary, QRC will modify program implementation to address concerns raised by affected people. QRC FM receives all reports, including SEA. If a SEA case is received, the receiving staff will immediately refer it to the QRC PSE
