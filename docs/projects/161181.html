Project Summary

South Sudan experiences a malnutrition burden among its under-five population. Among children under 5 years of age, the prevalence of malnutrition is very high. Based on WHO epidemiological criteria, the prevalence of stunting and wasting are classified as very high. Incidence of malnutrition rises sharply among children 6-23 months as exclusively breastfed infant transition to complementary foods. According to the FSNMS Round 24 (2019), the proportion of children who meet their minimum acceptable diet remains extremely low at a meager 7%. Whether moderate or severe, acute malnutrition weakens a child’s immune system and places them in far greater risk of dying from common childhood ailments such as diarrhea, respiratory infections, and malaria. These diseases can lead to further malnutrition, creating a vicious cycle of malnutrition and disease. SAM is a life-threatening condition and young children with SAM often have no appetite, and little energy to play
Access to specific and necessary nutrition remains one of the major challenges in reducing child mortality, especially in conflict-affected countries, where they are most vulnerable.
Through the project, NSDO will focus on provision of nutritious food to 489 (235 Boys and 255 girls) under 5 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), 7,189 (3,451 Boys and 3,739 girls ) under 5 children suffering from Moderate Acute Malnutrition and 180 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW)suffering from acute malnutrition in Kajo keji county in Central Equatoria State. Amongst the population of children 6 – 59 months suffering from severe and moderate acute malnutrition a total of 1,047 (503 boys and 545 girls) will be targeted for Vitamin A supplementation.
In addition, the organization will undertake recovery and resilience initiatives to cushion the vulnerable population from further deterioration of their nutrition status through establishment of kitchen gardens in nutrition sites.
Given that malnutrition is the major cause of death among under the under five children there is need for sustained investment for life-saving nutrition service delivery in order to curb the situation.
During the project cycle, NSDO field team will coordinate with key partners on ground who are providing nutrition and protection services at other sites in same Counties. Regular joint activity monitoring sessions will be conducted. In addition, NSDO will also prioritize daily and weekly reporting and information sharing.
The nutrition services will be provided through static sites, mostly in secure and accessible areas. Outreach services will be provided in sites with large catchment areas. Rapid Response Mechanisms (RRM) will be implemented in insecure and inaccessible locations. Emergency Response Teams (ERT/MET) will be deployed at emergency onset and to areas with limited coverage.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, malnutrition is the major cause of death among under the under five children and as such requires sustained investment for life-saving nutrition service delivery in order to curb the situation. Among children under 5 years of age, the prevalence of malnutrition is very high. While anyone can experience malnutrition, people who are particularly vulnerable include young children, adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women, older people, people who are ill or immuno-compromised. According to the report published UNICEF, “Childhood under Attack”, more than one million people in South Sudan are acutely malnourished. A large part of the underlying causes of malnutrition are related to inadequate childcare practices: breastfeeding, weaning, feeding, hygiene, and health seeking. The high prevalence of disease cases is due to hostile environment, lack of safe drinking water, poor personal and environmental hygiene, poor health seeking and limited health services. While these factors affect similarly all children, including children aged more than 5 years, children aged 6-29 months appear as the most vulnerable since they particularly suffer from inadequate breastfeeding and weaning. In Kajo Keji, the problem is compounded by neglect of children by their mothers additionally, households and communities that host IDP returnees especially in the county were found to be more exposed to malnutrition aggravating factors. According to the FSNMS round 24 results, South Sudan has a GAM prevalence rate of 16.2%, implying the country has crossed the emergency threshold of 15%. Incidence of malnutrition tend to rise sharply among children 6-23 months as exclusively breastfed infant transition to supplementary foods. According to the FSNMS Round 24 (2019), the proportion of children who meet their minimum acceptable diet remains extremely low at a meagre 7%. Malnutrition can lead to a weakened immune system meaning children are more vulnerable to disease. These diseases can lead to further malnutrition, creating a vicious cycle of malnutrition and disease. The protracted conflict further exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition due to the reduced ability of children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons to meet their nutritional needs because of displacement and increased morbidity caused by disease outbreaks, lack of access to clean water, sanitation facilities and basic health services, the situation is similar in the county health facility, Kajo Keji PHCC. Kajo Keji County is one of the counties on high alert in terms of malnutrition, hence in order to reduce morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children in the County, where MAM is estimate to be 50% and SAM estimated at 20% according to county health officials. NSDO through the project will target 489 (235 Boys and 255 girls) under 5 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), 7,189 (3,451 Boys and 3,739 girls ) under 5 children suffering from Moderate Acute Malnutrition and 180 PLW suffering from acute malnutrition in Kajo keji county. Amongst the population of children 6 – 59 months suffering from severe and moderate acute malnutrition a total of 1,047 (503 boys and 545 girls) will be targeted for Vitamin A supplementation. The nutrition services will be provided through static sites, mostly in secure and accessible areas . Outreach services will be provided in sites with large catchment areas. Rapid Response Mechanisms (RRM) will be implemented in insecure and inaccessible locations. Emergency Response Teams (ERT/MET) will be deployed at emergency onset and to areas with limited coverage. Failure to respond to the needs will lead to increase in number of deaths especially of children given that malnutrition is one of the top nutrition-related causes of death in children under five. A child with SAM and MAM is twelve and three times more at risk of dying compared with a well-nourished child.
