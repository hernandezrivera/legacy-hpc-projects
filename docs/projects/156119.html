Project Summary

Basic WASH needs of people at risk of water borne diseases in Afrin and Manbij districts are provided through multi WASH activities.
1- : Conflict affected population in Afrin and Manbij districts receive critical and lifesaving WASH assistance
Activities:
1.1.1 Reception/ procurement, storage and distribution of family hygiene kits in Afrin and Manbij districts.
1.1.2 Collection and safe disposal of solid waste in Afrin district and Manbij district.
1.1.3 Access to safe and adequate water through repair and operation of 15 water systems.
1.1.4 Improvement of hygienic environment in children centred facilities by repairing WASH facilities in 40 schools and 4 child friendly spaces with hygiene promotion sessions.
1.1.5 Repair of WASH facilities in 2 health facilities.
1.1.6 Repair of water supply and sewer network in Afrin and Manbij
2- IDPs in last resort shelter and informal settlements in Afrin, Jarablus and Albab districts receive life-saving WASH assistance in response to recent displacements and flooding emergencies targeting 3,665 HHs, 14,509 individuals.
Activities:
1.2.1 Repair/ preparation of drainage systems including graveling and levelling in camps affected by flooding in Jarablus stadium and Zoughra informal settlements in Jarablus, Duyuf Al Sharqia informal settlement in Al Bab and Deir Ballout, Mohammadiye and Tranda camps in Afrin.
1.2.2 Rehabilitation and installation of latrines and bathing facilities in camps affected by flooding in Jarablus stadium and Zoughra informal settlements in Jarablus, Duyuf Al Sharqia informal settlement in Al Bab and Trande camp in Afrin.
1.2.3 Procurement and distribution of hygiene kits and replacement of damaged water tanks in camps affected by flooding in Jarablus stadium and Zoughra informal settlements in Jarablus, Duyuf Al Sharqia informal settlement in Al Bab and Trande camp in Afrin.
1.2.4 Provision of safe water to IDPs in Jarablus stadium and Zoughra informal settlements in Jarablus and Duyuf Al Sharqia informal settlement in Al Bab via water trucking
1.2.5 Solid waste management to improve hygienic situation in Jarablus stadium and Zoughra informal settlements in Jarablus, Duyuf Al Sharqia informal settlement in Al Bab and Trande camp in Afrin.
3- Conflict affected population in Afrin and Azaz districts have adequate access to safe and adequate water through providing operational support for Midinki dam and Sharan water treatment plant.
1.3.1 Provision of operational support for Midanki dam and Sharan water treatment plant for two month to ensure sustainability of water system treatment plant.

Needs

Despite the tremendos efforts carried out by Bahar and other WASH actors in Afrin district, water borne diseases remain as a serious risk across its 325 communities. Reports and weekly upadates of EWARN on leishmaniasis and water borne disease in Afrin flags the need to strengthen water supply systems, scale up solid waste management work and the preparedness to properly respond to water borne diseases outbreaks with family hygiene kits during 2019. Furthermore, the prevelance of acute juandice in Bulbul, Sharan, Raju and Sharan subdistricts in the 4th quarter of 2018; urges UNICEF and Bahar to support the rehabilitation of the key water systems in these areas in addition to rehabilitate more WASH facilities in schools and health centers with provision of hygiene promotion sessions, mini hygiene kits to studenets and IEC materials to students. Based on Bahar’s lessons learnt from 2018 program, the aforementioned approach needs to also be replicated at the child friendly spaces supported by this program to ensure integration of WASH with child protection by the approperiation of WASH facilities at CFSs to children, weekly hygiene promotion and in-kind distributions of mini hygiene kits to children in order to encourage them adopt sound and adequate self-hygiene practices. While the absence of a medical solid waste management jeopredises the public health in Afrin city where medical waste is not being properly disposed. On the other hand, the success story achieved by the comprehensive rehabilitation and restoration of operationality of Midanki dam and Sharan water treatment plant is at risk to be disruppted due to low revenues collected by tariff system in its first 3 months where users’ trust in the sustainability of system was still questionned given the mass rehabilitation work, which took time to restore the maximum capacity of these two critical facilities, which provide adequate and treated water to 220,000 beneficiaries in both Afrin and Azaz cities.. Although Bahar has spared a considerable resources to carry out repairs and improvements of water network in both Afrin and A’zaz cities, Bahar’s needs assessment highlights the need for expansion of the water networl to increase its efficiency and thus improve the revenues of tariff system. sources: EWARN, HNO, Bahar Assessments Total targeted beneficiaries: 300,000. Protection Risk Analysis

Provision of NFI packages – To ensure protection of women and girls during distribution and avoid any GBV arising to them, Bahar will prepare the distributions in cooperation with the local authorities/camp management and obtain the approval of community leaders, it will organize the distributions in safe places during day hours, secure different queues for men and women, involved women in their team to handle women and girls. In addition, the staff will be trained in basic Protection to be able to refer any GBV case that they may discover. Bahar will provide complaint mechanism to register any feedback from beneficiaries and it will try to embed the advices received in its programming dimension. Sensitive supplies will be stored according to SPHERE standards and rigorous monitoring will be conducted, only authorized persons being allowed to handle these supplies. The inequality in distribution will be mitigated through clear lists with criteria, supporting the most vulnerable people from the IDPs and host community side; special focus will be on women, child and elderly headed households. The criteria will be discussed and shared with the community. The content of the hygiene kits will be gender sensitive serving the needs of women, men, girls and boys. The distribution of items will be doubled by awareness raising campaigns on the importance and use of the kits. Verification of items will be made periodically in the warehouse and before distribution to ensure that any expired product is replaced. Emergency water supply – Bahar will ensure the supply of water based on a specific schedule avoiding dark and unsafe places. In designing the intervention for each location, women, men, girls, boys, elderly and people with disability will be consulted. The distributions points will be set as close as possible to tents to avoid long distance and ensure the safe travel of people. The water will be checked to comply with Syria water quality standards, while the team will conduct regular monitoring of the quality of water at the level of water sources, tracking system, distribution points and end users. The minimum quantity of water per person will be distributed. Gender based considerations will be applied, prioritizing women and girls during the distribution and securing different queues.Emergency sanitation – Gender considerations will be prioritized when rehabilitating and installing new latrines and bath facilities. The WASH cluster standards will be considered to ensure the safety and separation of women from men. Cultural traditions will be also envisaged, preparing the places to fit to the local norms. Women and girls will be involved in the designing of the intervention and consulted on regular basis within the complaint mechanism on their satisfaction. Desludging trucks will have established routes and will avoid crowdedness, especially the hours when children go or return from school.Solid Waste Management – The community will be consulted on the routes and frequency of waste disposal, along with disposal points. The staff/contractor involved in removing the waste will receive protection equipment. Training on UXO and ERW will be provided to the staff and contractor to ensure their safety. Support to water supply systems – Clear selection criteria will be prepared with the community to benefit equally all people in need. To protect the infrastructure from damage and theft, MoUs are to be signed with local authorities. A cost recovery system for longer term maintenance and ownership will be promoted to enhance community ownership. Supplies will be stored and handle according to the minimum standards. Training on UXO and ERW will be provided to the staff and contractor to ensure their safety. Minimum safety measures at the working place will be set.Child labour - Bahar applies its Code of Conduct on the field and obliged contractors to use it. The Code forbids the use of child in labour.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks associated to conflict (shelling, bombing, fights on the ground between different armed groups): Livelihoods: Afrin – low; Manbij – medium/high; Mitigation: continuous monitoring of the security situation from the field; stop the operations for limited time to ensure safety of beneficiaries and team; provide safety and security training for the team. Diversion of aid – armed groups might seize up supplies; Livelihood: low; Mitigation: Bahar will be in touch with OCHA and report immediately any disruption of aid. in addition, Bahar has experience in conducting negotiation and it will use its trained and experienced staff for the first line of negotiation. Risk associated with storage of supplies – supplies might be damaged due to conflict related risks or due to storage conditions. Livelihood: low. Mitigation: the warehouses of Bahar respect the SPHERE standards; proper conditions for storage of specific supplies as chlorine are respected; the team is checking the supplies while stored or before distribution; checklists to ensure quality of storage exist and are used by the team.Risks associated to safety at the working place; Livelihood: low; Mitigation: Provide training on safety at the working place; prepare and use specific SOP; distribution of Personal Protective Equipment for all staff and contractors; conduct a rigorous monitoring of the way the safety regulations and protocols are respected.People selling their kits – Livelihood: medium; Mitigation: Considering the harsh situation from Syria, families might be forced to sell their goods to obtain cash. However, the team has prepared kits containing the basic supplies to cover people’s hygiene needs. Distribution of kits combined with awareness raising on the importance of hygiene decreases this risk. In addition, post monitoring survey will be conducted at the level of beneficiaries to register their interest and use of supplies in order to programme better future interventions. Risks associated to the tax collection system; Livelihood: Medium; Mitigation: The quality of water will increase the confidence of people in the system; Bahar will raise the awareness of the importance of the tariff system with the Local Authorities and local leaders offering advisory on how to collect the tax from people.Risks associated to influx of IDPs – Due to ongoing fights, more IDPs will be heading to formal and informal camps needing WASH facilities, water tracking and waste management. Livelihood: Medium; Mitigation: close monitor of IDPs movement based on CCCM bulletin information; ability to provide supply based on an emergency SOP, rehabilitate the toilettes according to the WASH cluster recommendations.Assumptions: the border will be opened and all needed materials will be transported in Syria; the procurement will be organized in due time and all the needed supplies will be in place at the time of activity beginning, Bahar will work closely with the local authorities and camp management
