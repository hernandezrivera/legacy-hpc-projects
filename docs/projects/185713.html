Project Summary

"During 2022, AGRAJATTRA will provide overall shelter provisions in 03 nos. of Rohingya camps (Camp 14, 26 & 20 Ext) and 02 host community unions (Palongkhali & Hnila). Providing TSA 1 assistance for Rohingya refugees living in camp 26 for 715 HHs (Block B). TSA 3 Assistance for 605 HHs in camp 14 (Block C), AGRAJATTRA have extensive regular experience since influx 2017 in distributing TSA materials in different camps as per Shelter/NFI guild line.
New Mid Term Shelters will construction for 250 HHs in Camp 20 Extension.
All the HHs receiving TSA-1, TSA-3, MTS would receive training on DRR, shelter protection and shelter reinforcement mechanism, fire safety, health and cleanliness. A priority would be given to female beneficiaries in this training. Provision of NFI through in kind distribution including support to EVI with porter assistance be kept for all possible 1570 HHs.
For the host community in Palongkhali & Hnila 255 HHs would be given support for maintenance, 600 HHs be given support for Solar or connection to grid line, cash or in-kind material would also be given to this 255 HHs. This would have a positive impact on the host community that the NGOs support the host community not only Rohingya. This will help building social cohesion and reduce inequality.
For Gender and inclusion activities with light work 150 female/EVI individuals would be hired for light jobs (Small scale works at the distribution points, crowd & queue management, providing drinking water etc in camps/host. This will ensure that no one is left behind.
AGRAJATTRA M&E unit will conduct PDM of shelter materials, other NFI items and the shelter quality. This with the community feedback mechanism will guide project to cater appropriately to both communities. A structured and rigorous M&E plan will guide assessment of project achievement as per target, on-going assessment, data collection and analysis.
AGRAJATTRA will maintain coordination with different sectors to ensure context specific shelter management in camp and host communities and evidenced-based application of non-food items. As per AGRAJATTRA’s strict safe-guarding policy, all staffs will be oriented on PSEA/safeguarding and gender mainstreaming, thus ensuring appropriate environment for both beneficiaries and staff, and interventions tailored as per needs of the vulnerable groups."

1 - Identified Needs

"After massive influx 2017, A million of Rohingyas fled to Bangladesh. Currently, over 860,000 Rohingya reside in makeshift and emergency shelters across 34 camps, most of which meet less than the basic humanitarian standards, overcrowded, and will not withstand seasonal and adverse weather conditions. The quality of the current shelters and poor terrain as well as the lack of privacy within these shelters has a serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees, especially for women, children, older persons and person with specific needs (PwSN). Also, according to the government decision to support the host community who has been impacted by the Rohingya influx, AGRAJATTRA will ensure that the host community receives at least 25% of the support. Shelter improvements and upgrading in terms of durable and long-lasting materials (Treated Bamboos) provision. But it is well known to all that space is limited in the very crowded camps. Shelters on hills are at risk of landslides and in flood prone areas. AGRAJATTRA’s assessment along with that of the Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment (Oct 2021) has found that during the COVID-19 lockdown that in the camps there was lack of sufficient shelter assistance to make repairs was a common concern; some shelters have been completely destroyed (collapsed/flooded) – in particular, shelter located on hill-side and in low-lying areas reported to be at risk; Rohingya HHs lack money to repair shelter themselves and additionally were not able to maintain social distancing because of lack of space.It was not possible to venture to collect shelter materials and the quality of materials currently in the shelters is deteriorating. 28% households have not been able to make improvements to their shelter despite reporting issues and another 28% was able to purchase materials to make improvements. In the host community, the main concern was about not being able to make repairs due to loss of income and an increase in price of shelter materials. 39% HHs reported that they had no money to pay for materials, 9% reported they could not pay for labor, 8% could not access materials and 5% did not receive any support. 10% Households reporting paying money or goods as rent in the 6 months prior to data collection. 58% Households reporting not having enough light. {Ref : https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/bgd_2020_jmsna_preliminary_findings.pdf} 12% of individuals were identified as persons with disabilities. Moreover, disability prevalence was found to increase with age, ranging from 2% among 2 to 4 year-olds, to 51% among older persons (aged 60 and above). Estimates of disability prevalence also varied geographically, ranging from 6% to 19% depending on the camp. (Ref : https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/REACH_BGD_Report_Age-and-Disability-Inclusion-Needs-Assessment_May-2021.pdf) Despite the efforts of the humanitarian response to increase women’s participation in community based discussions, so that their specific needs are heard and addressed, there is a community resistance to this, especially in the camps. Women and girls still lack access to information about provisions and services, complaints and feedback mechanisms, making them less confident to voice their needs, complain and seek redressed. AGRAJATTRA has found that the needs of the HHs in the target areas vary considerably according to shelter condition and geographical location of HHs. Rohingya households require essential needs and replenishment of items, such as solar lamps which have been distributed in the previous year and are amongst the commonly requested NFI items. A lack of money to pay for materials or labor was the most commonly reported reason for not having made shelter repair hence we need to make provision for labor support along with EVI support after providing TSA-2 support."
