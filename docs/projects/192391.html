Project Summary

This project builds on the ongoing work Tabitha is engaged in responding to the Beirut blast of 2020 and the prevailing political and economic crisis that has left almost half of the Lebanese population under the poverty line. For the most vulnerable, the risk of food insecurity is growing more severe and resorting to negative coping mechanisms, such as reduced food consumption, remains a worrying trend. In order to provide immediate assistance to support the critically vulnerable, Tabitha will expand its ongoing work to provide food parcels to the elderly, female headed households, households with persons with disabilities, and households with chronically ill members in the areas of Aley, Batroun, Achrafieh, and Chouf. The project will distribute 1,500 food parcels on a monthly basis for 6 months, reaching an approximate 7,500 individuals. Tabitha will engage in this activity utilizing its three community centers in Mt Lebanon, North, and Beirut. These community centers are trusted entities that have been serving communities for several years, running a multitude of programs, including protection services (case management, legal assistance), homework help, and psychosocial support. Tabitha has also used these community centers in the past to conduct emergency food distributions following the initial COVID-19 lockdown and immediately following the Beirut blast, and provide nutritious snacks to children.
In line with the FSS guidance on food parcel composition, food parcels will contain cereals (rice, pasta, bulgur), legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans), canned fish, vegetable oil, sugar, salt, vegetables, and tomato paste. Parcels will be delivered once per month. Tabitha has already secured $ 165,000 (project ended in march 2022) for this project under the Lebanese Humanitarian Fund and will engage in resource mobilization for the other funding needed. Tabitha is still working with a wide array of donors- this includes foreign governments, UN agencies, foundations, and international NGOs- and has already submitted similar proposals to continue this critical programming.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

This project falls under objective 1 of the food security sector by providing emergency in-kind food assistance to the most critically vulnerable within the Lebanese population. The project targets under-served communities across 2 governorates and Beirut, and within those communities, will reach female headed households, and those with elderly, chronically ill, and persons with disabilities who are struggling to meet their food needs on a daily basis. Beyond provision of food, this project also links food parcels distribution with distribution of hygiene kits, specifically designed for babies and the elderly. This project is already being implemented in Aley with OCHA funding. Food parcels contents and delivery are in line with food security sector standards.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Tabitha has immense experience working on inclusion and gender mainstreaming, particularly under its protection program. Tabitha utilizes social work methodology to ensure that beneficiaries receive individual vulnerability/needs assessments through a network of outreach workers (selected among vulnerable host communities) to facilitate trust building and access to isolated groups. Tabitha mainstreams protection in all its activities which allows maximization of the protective impact of the project through ensuring that the activities target the most vulnerable and enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of beneficiaries without contributing/ perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. Tabitha also engages in social cohesion activities that are designed to bring together individuals of different backgrounds. Tabitha staff have been sensitized to gender & diversity and should recognize that vulnerabilities differ from one to another.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Community engagement is at the core of Tabitha's humanitarian work across Lebanon. For this reason, Tabitha has created a robust two-way feedback mechanism, operational through various channels including calls, emails, written boxes, and verbal feedback. Tabitha builds many of its projects based on the feedback received at its community centers which are trusted, longstanding entities. Tabitha also maintains high monitoring and evaluation standards, and ensures that staff are well-trained on the code of conduct and humanitarian standards. Tabitha has successfully participated in a series of coaching/mentoring sessions on PSEA & safeguarding integration done by ABAAD in partnership with OCHA, LHF and the Lebanese National PSEA Network. A training on integrity is followed by all staff of Tabitha and it is mandatory.
