Project Summary

The Somalia 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) states a spike in the number of people in need in 2021, driven by climate shocks, conflict, and increased vulnerability, resulting in 5.9 million people requiring humanitarian assistance. This includes 4.3 million non-displaced people, 2.9 million people displaced by conflict, insecurity, droughts, and floods, as well as 109,989 refugee returnees, and 28,002 refugees and asylum seekers. It is estimated that 20 per cent of Somalis will suffer from the direct or indirect impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021. These conditions are more moderate in Bay region and severe in rural areas of Baidoa district, which is a nomad community and is prone to droughts due to the shortage of expected rains and spread of communicable diseases such as water borne diseases, measles and malaria. The surrounding villages of the district have a low presence of humanitarian organizations especially those offering primary health care (PHC) and BEMoC services. Lack of water and insufficient food rations to families is impacting on the long term health of families particularly children and women of child bearing age.
Due to the shocks, its estimated that 182,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will be acutely malnourished thus affecting growth and development of the foetus. one in 1,000 women aged 15 - 49 years will die due to pregnancy related complications, availability of BEmOC and CEmOC services remains low and where these services are available there are challenges either distance, preference of traditional birth attendants over SBA etc. Uptake of prenatal services remains low in both districts and with the current situation the numbers are even lower. According to a health report, one in eight children will die before they turn five years old; and 15 per cent of the population will experience some form of disability.
The federal government of Somalia in conjunction with UN national priorities is to see all PLW access ANC and PNC services to ensure healthy pregnancies and deliveries. The policy also advocates for immunization of children to promote healthy life and avoid disabilities and loss of life. As a result of the above women and children remain the most vulnerable, pregnant women are not getting the right Pre-natal care (PNC) that they need this complicates the birth process and outs both the mother and child at risk. Also as a result of the gaps mentioned above immunization for 0-12 months’ age group remains low. This project aims at providing health services that are so dire in Baidoa district.

1. - Projects activities

To ensure continuation of delivering health activities like primary health care services, and integrating related hygiene promotion practices on AWD/Cholera and COVID-19 prevention SAMA will implement this health project in Baidoa district. The health prevention component will focus on delivering routine immunization services to 0-12 month’s old infants including undertaking Measles and Polio vaccination campaigns to U5 children, ANC services for pregnant women, health education of expected women on danger signs during pregnancy, health education sessions on prevention of other communicable diseases including COVID-19. SAMA will offer these services in health centers as well as one outreach post district. For immunizations SAMA will use monthly outreach programs where they advocate for immunizations door to door for children who are not brought to the health facility. SAMA will also embark in mass awareness creation to ensure that people are aware of the services offered under this project hence improving uptake of health services. SAMA will collaborate with other partners in to ensure that uptake of health services is improved. To prevent cholera and AWD outbreaks that are common in Baidoa, SAMA will conduct hygiene promotion and water chlorination. At the health facility SAMA will treat moderate cases of cholera and conduct contact tracing for disinfection, hygiene promotion and water treatment at Household level.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Gender: This project aims to promote health of both female and male genders. Inasmuch as the project aims at promoting maternal health, men with existing conditions or diagnosed with other diseases will have access to life saving health interventions. In addition to this SAMA will ensure to recruit both genders to implement the project.Equity: SAMA will reach out to crisis affected populations especially the marginalized, vulnerable, host and IDP communities. There will be no favourism in any of the beneficiary groups. To ensure equity, SAMA will work with leaders who will offer an oversight in the process and implementation of the project throughout the 12 months. In addition to this SAMA will establish a discrete feedback mechanism where beneficiaries can report irregularities or give feedback on the process or services offered under this project.
