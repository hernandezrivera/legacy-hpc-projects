Project Summary

The project aims to continue strengthening the provision of humanitarian assistance through supporting the coordination of service providers, enabling community participation and feedback to humanitarian programming, reducing risks from safety and natural hazards, and increasing the degree of access to key services in refugee camps and host community of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas. In collaboration with implementing partners and other stakeholders, IOM intends to target Rohingya refugees and host communities that will benefit from improved service provision, site improvements, site planning interventions and disaster risk reduction which contribute to ensuring equitable access to assistance, protection, and services that improve quality of life and dignity during displacement.
Site Management Support (SMS) activities in 18 camps of IOM’s Area of Responsibility will continue to support the Camp-in-Charge officials (CiC), ensuring services are monitored, coordinated, and appropriately targeted, including through community engagement activities that enable effective avenues for two-way communication with the affected population, while also ensuring that risk mitigation, inclusion and GBV considerations are mainstreamed and implemented across all activities within the camps. Providing humanitarian and development actors with data and information on needs that support informed decision making, the project ensures targeted and appropriate humanitarian service provision within the camps as well as the host community.
Site Development (SD) works implemented through IOM and partners, and involvement in the Site Maintenance & Engineering Project (SMEP), will contribute to a safer, more dignified, and more accessible living environment for the camp population, working to ensure further community involvement in the identification of priority works. IOM will work towards inclusion of age-gender-disability groups in various community-based structures wherever possible. Mobilization of cash-for-work labour will be key to deliver the site development/improvement results as most of the works are implemented using this modality, which in turn becomes a main income-generating source for refugee households.
Needs and Population Monitoring NPM will continue supporting the SMSD sector through the establishment of a facility monitoring tool. The tool can be used by a variety of stakeholders, both for site planning and development purposes, as well site management operational activities on the ground. It will grant humanitarian partners a better understanding of service provision in camps, thus promoting adequate coverage.
Through Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) interventions, both refugee and host communities will benefit from improved disaster preparedness and response activities and strengthened community resilience. DRR activities will also be streamlined into SMS and SD programming, with a focus on community-based preparedness and response and referral mechanisms, camp and host community contingency planning and mitigation measures. Learning centre and school-based disaster management will improve the access and safety measures of vulnerable people using emergency shelters during seasonal floods, cyclones or other hazards.
Finally, IOM will continue to lead the Site Management and Site Development (SMSD) sector, with coordination and information management capacity and supporting resources, to enable inter-sectoral coordination, including SMEP and other site development and improvement actors. The SMSD sector will continue to work closely with the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) secretariat, NPM and other sector and working group coordinators to enable tools that will support centralized data collection of service monitoring and incident tracking to better inform them about the quality of services and gaps across camps, incidence of events due to natural hazards, results of safety audits and other assessments.

1 - Identified Needs

Entering the fifth year after the 2017 influx of Rohingya refugees into Cox’s Bazar district, the space for increasing the degree and quality of services is steadily shrinking. Uninformed - and suspected involuntary - relocations from Camp 23 in Teknaf upazila to Ukhiya upazila camps and from all camps to the island of Bhasan Char are expected to continue in 2022. The Capacity Sharing Initiative, led by the SMSD sector and implemented by IOM and UNHCR with the support of other sectors and actors, has been working closely with the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) to enhance the cooperation between humanitarian sectors and actors and the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) in the management of camps. Though much effort has been put in Cox’s Bazar into creating the space of open exchange and discussion about the aligning the UN/Humanitarian objectives with that of the Government, another reality prevails in the camp which negatively affects already vulnerable refugees. Further needs have also been identified regarding community participation. According to the report “Our Thoughts” (2021, IOM & ACAPs), 72% of female and 67% of male focus group discussion participants did not feel engaged in decision-making processes. They expressed that Mahjis, NGO volunteers, literate people, religious leaders and older men are more likely to be included in decision making consultations, while illiterate people, younger people, and women and girls are not. One major challenge is that formal representation forums continue to be restricted and alternative ways have had to be found to ensure that the most vulnerable can be heard and can influence the overall humanitarian programming. While this goal is pursued, many continue to feel uncomfortable sharing opinions with humanitarian actors during regular engagement. This is especially the case for women, older persons, and members of the community that have less access to the public space as they are required to leave their community-blocks. The study also found that about half the participant reported negative experience when trying to report problems and issues in the camps. Only 33% of male and 19% of female participants were able to recall a positive experience when providing feedback and requesting help from humanitarians. Access to services and mitigation of risks from natural hazards and safety issues continue to drive the needs identified during the current year. In March, a fire outbreak expanded throughout 3 camps and consumed over 10,000 shelters and a large amount of service facilities, leaving almost 50,000 individuals affected (IOM, JNA 2021). In July, 268 households where displaced by sudden floods that peaked on 26-27 July. Reconstructions for these events which have been undertaken during monsoon season have required long efforts and investments. The impact of monsoon, a seasonal event that requires site development and site improvement mitigation works every year, could not be fully addressed due to restrictions to camps for humanitarian actors, as part of nation-wide movement restrictions during the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, access, land and watershed maintenance, management and repairs continue to require large investments which are often doomed to have short term impact, due to the decision of the GoB to permit the use of more durable materials that could reduce costs in the long term. Together with needed works for reforestation, land rehabilitation, slope stabilization, watershed management and risk mitigation, site development and improvement activities demand constant assessment and design of solutions to be able to address immediate needs, which are mostly identified by the refugees themselves.

2 - Activities or outputs

1- SMS: Contribute to the Capacity Sharing Initiative to train government staff deployed to support the RRRC office, and other camp stakeholders. Lead and staff the SMSD Sector and continue to strengthen camp coordination of different service providers, and monitoring to highlight gaps, prioritize facilities and services, avoid duplication and advocate for solutions. 2- NPM: Emergency preparedness and response - SMSD daily incident assessment: Throughout 2022, NPM will continue to provide ongoing support to the Site Management sector through the SMSD Sector’s Daily Incident Assessment. The purpose of the system is to capture key information on the impact and damages caused by weather related incidents. NPM provides the technical support to this framework and produces daily datasets and factsheets which are shared widely to all humanitarian actors3- NPM: Facility rationalization/mapping: Facility monitoring set up and operational in cooperation with the SMSD sector. 4- NPM: Thematic research: Research topics for thematic/sector-specific assessments will be determined through direct consultation with key stakeholders and relevant working groups. The research will inform programmatic and operational decisions. 5- SMS: Strengthen participation and inclusion by forming additional and strengthening existing Age-Gender-Disability groups; and support them in designing/implementing projects. Ensure the Women’s Participation Project is expanded based on the UN Women’s Empowerment, Participation & Leadership curriculum. Strengthen committees and pursue legitimate representation structures wherever possible. 6- SMS: Strengthen community engagement, community awareness, and feedback in camps. Ensure refugees better lead programming via the Common Feedback Platform. 7- SD: Disaster preparedness and risk reduction interventions for erosion and landslides through slope stabilization, for flood mitigation through drainage construction and cleaning and for water quality preservation through promoting nature-based solutions and training and equipment to disaster management units. 8- SD: Site planning for shelters and facilities and infrastructure prioritisation; construction of access roads, pathways, staircases and installation and maintenance of streetlights to enable safe and meaningful access to key services and protection assistance. 9- SMEP: Maintaining vehicular access through the maintenance, reconstruction and emergency repair of access roads. 10- SMEP: Disaster preparedness and risk reduction including slope stabilization, flood prevention including drainage cleaning, maintenance and reconstruction, and provision of machineries and mechanical supports to improve safety and acceleration of preparedness and emergencies response. 11- SCU: Reinforcement of camp community shelters, learning center-based disaster management exercises, upgrading of contingency plans and training and equipment support to CPP volunteers to mitigate the suffering of refugees. 12- SCU: Renovation of cyclone shelters, development of access roads, GBV and protection training and equipment support to CPP volunteers and cyclone shelter management committees, water safety training and equipment support to boatmen, school-based disaster management planning exercises, preparation & simulation of Ward contingency plan and slop stabilization of landslide vulnerable areas.
