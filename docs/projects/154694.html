Project Summary

ACTED’s strategy for CCCM activities in 2019 intends to continue to provide both in and out of camp CCCM services to conflict affected populations throughout Ninewa, Dohuk, Sulaymaniyah and Diayla governorates. Planned interventions are designed based on the CCCM Cluster objectives for 2019.
As such, ACTED as member of the Ninewa Governate Returns Committee as well as the CCCM Cluster Strategic Advisory Group and the co-lead of the Dohuk CCCM Cluster will continue to promote all facets of the Principled Returns Framework.
ACTED intends to continue to support government and national camp management via its tested mobile CCCM team modality, which can support both formal and informal sites across a large geographical area, and can flexibly be deployed to support existing or any new needs as they arise. This will include 17 camps in the Dohuk governorate, 1 camp in the Sulaymaniyah governorate and 1 in the Diyala governorate. ACTED will tailor its interventions to the needs of the residents in each camp and the capacities of the camp management. ACTED will also continue to support informal settlements including conducting RASPs in Dohuk and Northern Ninewa in collaboration with the REACH initiatives and the CCCM Cluster, on a regular basis as required, or should new displacement take place in these areas. ACTED will also provide an information management and humanitarian coordination via opening a Community Resource Centre in Mosul throughout 2019.
Under its 2019 CCCM programming ACTED will reach approximately 202.609 conflict-affected individuals (women, men, boys and girls) across four governorates (Dohuk, Ninewa, Sulaimaniyah and Diayala) including internally displaced persons residing in formal camps (190,317 persons), namely Salamyiah 1 and 2 as in Ninewa governorate, Tazade camp in Sulaimanyiah, Qaratu camp in Diyala as well as all Dohuk camps (Berseve 1 , Berseve 2, Derkar, Chamishku, Qadiya, Bajidkandala 1&2, Khanke, Sharya, Kabarto 1 and 2, Essian, Shekhan, Mamrashan, Garmawa , Mamilian, Dawodiya). In informal sites ACTED will reach 12.292 displaced persons in following locations: Sumel, Zakho (Dohuk governorate) and Shikhan, Akre, Tilkaif, and Mosul (Ninewa governorate). The project will also target 10,000 returnees who will benefit from establishment of Community Resource Center in Mosul City in collaboration with OFDA. As at the stage of writing the ACTED was yet finalising the selection process of the location for establishing the CRC, the number of the members of host communities targeted with this element of CCCM programming cannot be estimated.

Needs

Despite the end of the conflict against ISIS, around 500,000 IDPs remain living in IDP camps across Iraq. This includes around 160,000 across 17 camps in the Dohuk governorate, around 20,000 in the Sulaymaniyah governorate and nearly 300,000 in the Ninewa governorate, including 28,000 in the Salamiyah 1 and 2 Camps where ACTED is the current camp manager in collaboration with UNHCR. In addition, around 1,500,000 IDPs are living in informal sites. IDPs living in and out of camp in protracted displacement face a variety of barriers to return, which result in low percentages of IDPs intending to return in the next 3 or 12 months as evidenced by the REACH initiative and CCCM intentions surveys. From the 1,188 households (HHs) interviewed across 17 informal sites, the vast majority of IDPs living outside of camps demonstrated no intention of returning home. Among the most the significant findings at governorate level, 98% of the IDPs HHs surveyed in Sulaymaniyah voiced the intention to remain in their current location in the upcoming 12 months. Similar results were featured in the key findings from Dohuk and Ninewa, with 84% and 66% of surveyed HHs respectively demonstrated the intention to remain in their current locations. Although intentions’ surveys highlight regional variances, several themes can be highlighted across IDP populations in and out of camp as barriers to return persist and these include (in different weighing depending on areas of origin and in/out of camp displacement): 1) security related concerns (lack of security forces, fear of discrimination), 2) damaged or destroyed homes, 3) lack of livelihood opportunities and in particular in the Mosul Response camps 4) lack of finance. Other factors include not sufficient basic services in areas of origin (AoOs), the presence of explosive hazards and challenges related to the social cohesion. This is also echoed in the Principled Returns Frameworks which sets out minimum standards for principled returns.Given these significant barriers to return, formal and informal sites are likely to remain throughout 2019. IDPs in and out of camps face diminishing service provision across several sectors. According to the sector specific findings of the Informal Site Assessments by REACH initiative and CCCM Cluster, the most significant gaps across informal sites Ninewa remain with access to the healthcare, food (due to mainly limited economic resources) and education. In Dohuk findings underscore significant gaps in the access to the permanent primary source of drinking water as well as challenges in accessing healthcare and food (around 90% of the KIIs report encountering problems with accessing healthcare and food). Significantly, the assessments highlight high level of negative coping strategies applied by the IDP HHs in both governorates via borrowing money/credit, child labour and reducing non-food expenses.For the in-camp locations, governorate level findings of the intention surveys highlight that 74% of respondents in Dohuk governorate have no intention to return to their AoOs in next 12 months, which will translate in enduring strains within camps in 2019. Considering the above, ACTED is well positioned to continue addressing existing gaps with operational and programmatic expertise in CCCM assistance in formal and informal settlements in Dohuk and Ninewa governorates. Working closely with Board of Relief and Humanitarian Affairs, Shelter, CCCM and Protection Clusters as well as all other stakeholders, ACTED will continue improving information management by providing mapping, technical and monitoring assessments, capacity building support to the camp management (in Dohuk camps only, as in Ninewa governorate ACTED with support of UNHCR remains a direct camp manager).Additionally, with regards to the coordination and information management in Mosul district particularly, the substantial gaps also remain in light of the high number of returnees, IDPs and host community members living there and supported by a wide network of services providers. According to the IOM and REACH Initiative joint localised assessment it is estimated that up to 200,000 individuals with various displacement status (i.e. returnees, displaced, host community). The different vulnerabilities and needs of each population group and individuals within these groups require enhanced coordination and information provision mechanisms, especially in light of the number of service providers currently active in the municipality (at least 13 programmatic stakeholders). Although the humanitarian response in Iraq is well-coordinated through the Cluster system, at the national and governorate levels, multi-sectoral localized structures are critically lacking; often resulting in missed opportunities for comprehensive and coordinated interventions. This is particularly the case as the recovery and stabilization process becomes engaged and additional stakeholders come into play.

Activities

ACTED’s 2019 CCCM strategy will contribute to all 3 objectives of the CCCM cluster, namely: 1. Facilitate and coordinate the provision of multi-sectorial interventions and conduct site risk reduction activities to ensure safe and dignified environment for internally displaced people in formal and informal sites including newly and secondary displaced.Under this objective, ACTED will target: a) Formal Camps (with a focus on the Dohuk IDP Camps and the Salamiyah 1&2 Mosul camps) to: - Continue to monitor service delivery in formal camps in Dohuk and Salamiyah 1 and 2;- Participate in the relevant CCCM coordination mechanisms including co-leading the Dohuk CCCM cluster and attending the CCCM SAG;- Support camp managers in the Dohuk IDP camps to streamline referral pathways;- Provide capacity building training to Camp Management in the Dohuk camps on risk management, protection and sessions on GBV, communication skills, and community participation, with a focus on women and people with disabilities; - Support existing governance structures in the Dohuk Camps and promote improved participation of vulnerable groups such as women and people with disabilities; - Continue to the roll out and training for Camp Managers on the new Formal Site Monitoring Tool in 2019 in the Dohuk camps.In addition to the above activities, ACTED will support improvement of key infrastructure in formal camps. This includes:- Electricity connection to the power grid for Tazade camp;- Switching from communal to individual water tanks in Qaratu camp.Within the 17 Dohuk camps, BRHA, together with ACTED have developed a set of priority infrastructure projects which include:- Repair and maintenance of the critical infrastructure in camps (this will include but will be not limited to public buildings, warehouses, rub halls and cabinets);- Improvement and maintenance of electrical network; - Maintenance and improvement of access roads;ACTED will also continue to improve infrastructure in Salamiyah 1&2 camps, including:- Rehabilitation and maintenance of communal spaces (including kitchen and sport fields)- Construction and maintenance of spaces for the community meetings;- Repair of fences (including building cement basements);- Improvement of fire safety installations; b) ACTED will also target Informal Sites in order to: - Establish mobile CCCM teams able to monitor sites and engagement in community mobilisation;- Support information gathering on IDPs in informal sites as required (e.g. master lists, 3Ws, intention surveys);- Train or/and support governance structures and support referral pathways from informal sites. 2. Empower the affected-population and local actors to prepare, maintain, and coordinate CCCM actors.Under this objective, ACTED will: - Work with BRHA in the 17 Dohuk IDP camps to facilitate and provide trainings to the camps staff with focus on assessments, informational management, risk management, protection and GBV concerns and community participation;- Support existing community mobilisation committees (women’s committees, youth committees, site maintenance committees) with developing/reviewing their TORs followed by provision of the trainings as needed, these may include but will be not limited to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, communication skills, risk identification, integration with communities etc.;- Provide materials and equipment for CCCM actors in target locations where necessary;- Promote the self-reliance of camp residents through cash-for-work schemes (for infrastructure support activities) in the 17 Dohuk camps, and in Salamiyah 1&2, in collaboration with UNHCR. - Develop pilot vocational training scheme and technical support in several locations (based on the needs and preferences identified) with the engagement of community mobilisation committees in order to ensure that residents are able to diversify their livelihoods. - Develop where relevant small-scale schemes with the camp management to promote household resilience depending on the needs identified. 3. Facilitate sustainability of return and support to affected populations. (Community Resource Centres)ACTED as member of the CRC Steering committee (SC) in 2019 will manage a CRC (selected based on the coordination with the CRC SC) in Mosul city with the support from OFDA. Activities implemented under the CRC include: - Support communities to identify, and access services and assistance delivered at CRCs or in community including awareness raising, outreach campaigns etc.- Support multi-sectoral coordination and referral mechanisms among humanitarian & recovery actors and liaison with government; community forums, FGDs;- Promote an area-based approach to displacement management; map assistance and services provided, contribute to the assessment of needs, monitor assistance provision and living conditions;- Develop collaborative response plan for humanitarian and recovery intervention.

Protection Risk Analysis

Among the people of concern in the context of Iraq, ACTED identified IDPs in formal and informal sites as well as returnees as the main target group. The prolonged crisis has exacerbated the vulnerabilities of IDPs, in particular among marginalized groups. Many IDP households in Iraq are facing economic hardship due to limited socio-economic prospects. There is wide recognition that sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) increases during conflict and the interconnectivity of conflict, economic hardship and difficult living conditions places women and girls as well as men and boys at increased protection risks. A decrease in humanitarian funding is placing an increasing risk for lack of service provision while service gaps are still present across the local structures. Similarly, conflict affected population groups that have decided to return are often faced with the challenge of rebuilding their lives in areas of large scale destruction and interrupted basic service provision and access to livelihoods. Limited financial resources and technical capacities at local government level in addressing the needs of the still approximately 1.5 million internally displaced people both in formal and informal settlements is putting beneficiaries at risk for interruption of access to essential and life-saving services. In addition to the outlined protection risks of beneficiaries, both ACTED and its targeted population are facing the following potential risks in humanitarian operations in Iraq: Risk 1. Activity of ISIL sleeper cells in project areas.Risk 2. Access to the project area restricted by KRG and/or GoI.Risk 3. Contamination with Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and/or Unexploded Ordnance (UXO).Risk 4. Target population hostile towards INGOs.Risk 5. Breakdown or blocking of communication networks.Risk 6: IS resorts to use of chemical weapons.

Mitigation Measures

ACTED’s mitigation measure with regards to increasing protection risks of beneficiaries due to the extended nature of their displacement, is to ensure targeted populations are widely informed about the services available to them, while ensuring that local stakeholders, incl. camp managers, are supported in their capacities to address the protection needs of their population, incl. those of marginalized groups, under consideration of community participation and looking at long-term decreasing assistance from the humanitarian community. ACTED aims at supporting the process of returns by facilitating information dissemination on services in targeted areas. While ACTED will still ensure service provision in certain areas, such as rehabilitation of critical infrastructure, the empowering of both beneficiaries and local stakeholders aims at addressing the risk of service interruption in a two-sided approach. In addition, ACTED will: Mitigation 1: ACTED will maintain close coordination and communication with relevant security forces and community leaders (Mukhtars) in the project areas and will continuously monitor security situation.Mitigation 2: ACTED already has access permissions for most of the project areas and maintain coordination with relevant governmental offices (BRHA, Asayish) ensuring that necessary permissions are obtained. Shall the access permissions be withdrawn or refused, ACTED might consider distribution of assistance through relevant traditional community structures, taking into account feasibility.Mitigation 3: ACTED will ensure that all staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of ERW/UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe.Mitigation 4: ACTED will ensure that their operations are culturally sensitive and will involve traditional leaders to ensure that role of INGO is well understood and accepted by the community.Mitigation 5: ACTED has a stock of satellite phones, which can be deployed to field teams should the traditional communication network be incapacitated or blocked by government forces. ACTED will leverage its existing relationships with the local government to ensure that as much information as possible about planned communication limitations is received before networks are disabled, in order to effectively plan for implementation in case of limited communication abilities.Mitigation 6: Staff will be trained on Chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRNE) defence, provision of protective equipment to field staff will be considered.Prior to any humanitarian assistance delivery, ACTED will assess all risks mentioned above and adopt risk mitigation strategies for the beneficiaries and ACTED’s team when implementing the activities. ACTED will ensure that regular formal and/or informal risk analyses are performed and relevant colleagues are consulted (Country Coordinator, In-Country Security Manager, Area Coordinators and project staff). Communication with external forums for security updates (INSO) and humanitarian access will be regularly conducted by its ACTED’s Country Security Manager to inform project implementation and minimize risks. ACTED will ensure the security and safety of its operations through close liaison and collaboration with local authorities. ACTED’s security staff will also be tasked with continuously monitoring interactions between IDPs, returnees and host communities in order to determine the risk level of tensions and recommend relevant adjustments to the intervention. Moreover, ACTED’s Security Plan for Iraq currently in place will be activated at relevant stages of the project’s implementation. If the security situation deteriorates to the extent that ACTED’s team is not able to implement activities in proposed areas, activities will be reoriented to accessible areas.

How is your project accountable to affected population

ACTED will ensure that community-based groups are included in the design of activities by seeking their engagement in regular FGDs; forums for two-way communication between them and ACTED. Women, men, boys, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities will be engaged to identify protection concerns and suggest possible solutions. Complementing the FGDs, a complaints mechanism will be made accessible to beneficiaries. ACTED will support existing mechanisms in camps using the monitoring and observation in the camp through the established committees and through camp management and partners with following up on the complaints once they are registered. Moreover, as not all camps have established their compliance response mechanism (CRM), ACTED is in the position to support the camp management in developing it. ACTED has established a CRM – a formalized process providing a safe, confidential, accessible and transparent channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. ACTED will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. Contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries in a variety of forms, including banners at distribution sides, contact cards and other printed materials, and complaints boxes wherever relevant.Furthermore, ACTED will inform targeted beneficiaries and communities on the selection criteria for activities, as well as on the distribution process when relevant to avoid the creation of tension. ACTED’s CRM provides a two ways communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable ACTED to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. ACTED will also ensure that its staff will comply with its Code of Conduct and serious measures will be taken in the event of violations during the course of the project.Beneficiaries’ participation will be promoted through site distribution and community mobilisation committees, namely women’s committees, youth committees, site maintenance committees, which will improve the quality of service provision within the camp with the special focus on the gender and age specific needs, while allowing IDPs to have a forum of information sharing and discussion. In order to enhance their self-governance capacity and community ownership, committee’s members will be trained on safety and environment, but also community registration, integration with host communities, the identification and reporting of issues related to gender-based violence. HH size (only with regards to site maintenance committees) and gender composition will be the main criteria in the establishment of the committees. In addition, ACTED will regularly share the results of service mapping and will keep them updated about services availability and actors available to address their needs. CCCM capacity building will be provided to IDPs and camp management, as relevant, to allow for them to take ownership over the care and maintenance of the sites. ACTED will ensure a strong exit strategy in all its activities, in particular with regards to capacity building of camp management, site and youth/women committees by empowering them with the tools to fulfil their purpose independently. ACTED will train the camp management and staff on assessments, informational management, risk management, protection and GBV concerns and community participation. During the implementation of this project, ACTED will maintain close contact with the appropriate clusters, including the CCCM cluster, SNFI cluster, as well as the Protection Cluster, included the GBV and Child Protection sub-clusters, to ensure complementarity of action with other service providers and to allow for swift and effective referrals when needed.
