Project Summary

BRAC Child Protection sector has been implementing child protection interventions since 2017 in both camp and host community with provision of psychosocial support, case management, life skills based session etc. and contributing to strengthen child protection mechanism through community engagement. For this 2020 JRP, BRAC Child Protection is aiming to strengthen child protection system and services across the camps and host community providing services e.g. play-based curriculum, structured PSS and adolescent engagement activities etc. in collaboration with community people and other stakeholders for the protection of vulnerable children and adolescent. BRAC Child Protection has targeted 19333 beneficiaries with composition of children, adolescent, community people and child protection staff in 32 camps (camp 1E-camp 27) and host community e.g. Teknaf sub-district and Ukhiya sub-district.

Needs

Child Protection services have a huge number of target population as boys and girls represent 55% of all Rohingya population (UNHCR Population factsheet, 15 September 2019) and there are a significant number of children exposed to child labour, child trafficking, child marriage, child separation as well as violence towards children in the camps. The Rohingya have adopted negative copying mechanism such as early marriage of their girls to increase food supply as well as to the host community in order to obtain legal status. Different studies informed that approximately 5-10% households marry off their children due to different reasons while one-third of these households perceive child marriage as quite normal (July 2019 UNHCR’s SPP 5 and August 2019 ISCG’s MSNA). ISCG’s MSNA report of August 2019 has stated that 3% of 5-17 years old children are involved in different forms of child labour while 51% of them are exposed to the worst forms of child labour. This is also a great concern in the host community as a significant number of working children are involved in hazardous works. Unaccompanied and Separated children also need special attention as they are more likely to be exposed to different forms of protection concern that remain still urgent. Moreover, the situation of the camps is likely to generate anxiety, aggression, depression and various sorts of psychosocial stress which need to be addressed and treated properly through a more comprehensive and structured Mental Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS). Ensuring social cohesion is another area that needs to be worked in where attempts need to be taken to decrease different forms of tension in the host community people raised as an impact of entrance of Rohingya people into this land. Child protection sector specially needs to look into the matter of recreational space and child safety. The project will, therefore, adopt a comprehensive approach catering to 0-18 years old children and adolescents, both boys and girls in different camps and host community. Through play-based curriculum BRAC will ensure holistic development and resilience of young children in CFSs as well home-based pockets, promote adolescents’ resilience, leadership and psycho-social well-being. The Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Centre under this project will serve as one stop centre where a range of CP services will be available including case management, structured psycho-social support, life skills and pre-vocational skills. It will also engage community people and other important stakeholders to strengthen the overall child protection mechanism.

Activities or outputs

1. Provide comprehensive case management and referral services to children at risk including unaccompanied and separated children, children with specific needs including disability and responding to children exposed to SGBV in collaboration with the GBV sector partners. Case Management support will be provided in for the same target group in host community as well.2. Improvement of the play-based curriculum will be continued in the Child Protection facilities with a harmonized and holistic services for children in the host community and the Rohingya community with special regards to age, gender and diversity. Curriculum as well as the facility will be assessed through the lens of inclusion to ensure the service to be inclusive. Consultation with community people at every stage will be ensured.3. Provide structured psychosocial support for boys and girls, including adolescents in the camps who are affected by memories of violence and exposure to violence as well as trauma. Psycho-social support will be provided for the same age group in host community as well.4. Provide life skills based education, leadership skills, pre-vocational skills, safety mapping and psychosocial wellbeing through centre and home based group for both Rohingya and host community adolescents where participation of adolescents will be ensured in designing the service to address their age, gender and context specific needs.5. To maximize the use of space, collaboration with other sectors e.g. nutrition, GBV, WASH and education sector will be done in referring children to participate in the child protection facilities as well as Learning Centres in the camps.6. Mainstream Child Protection activities in other sectors through capacity building on PSEA and CPSS guideline on child labor, combating child marriage and violence against children to ensure children safety while accessing the services and work with this sectors in monitoring risks that the children are facing.7. Strengthen and enhance protection environment for children in the community through Community Based Child Protection Committees who will monitor CP risks and develop community response as well.8. Children, adolescents and community people will be encouraged to plant trees and keep the environment clean for better and greener environment.9. Integrating DRR approach in child protection services through strengthening the Lost Child and Caregiver Meet up Point to act as a centre for lost children during a disaster and strengthen the CP structures to reduce the risk (DRR) as well as building capacity of staffs. Also the project will provide comprehensive knowledge on disaster risk reduction to the communities including religious leaders who can participate in emergencies situation e.g. pre-during-post disaster situation. 10. All the staffs will be oriented to and obliged by Minimum Standard for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action and Child Protection Policy of BRAC to ensure ‘Do no harm’ principle to children.11. Both in camps and host community, active participation of the members of different committees, e.g. CBCPC, Parents and Caregiver Committee will be ensured to build ownership of the services among them, initiate the change process in their mind set and finally make them able to take the lead in different activities pertinent to protection of their children. Formal feedback and complaint mechanism will be in place for the beneficiaries. 12. Support the Child Protection Sub-sector in coordination at the camp level as well as in Cox's Bazar by assigning staff as focal points for child protection and to deputize the CPSS Coordinator. 13. Project will ensure social cohesion through different sorts of activities, e.g. different day observation, arranging sports, awareness raising meeting or events etc. in collaboartion with Government and local organizations.
