Project Summary

Somalia has seen conflict, desert locust and serious flooding since June affected 191,800 people in Hirshabelle, South West and Jubaland states as well as Banadir region. Most of the 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia live in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Safety and security concerns remain a high priority for all affected populations, with displacement contributing to immediate and heightened exposure to violence, exploitation and other forms of abuse. Serious risks and threats to safety and security.
Persons most affected include those with specific needs: people with physical, sensorial, intellectual and mental disabilities, adolescent girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women and girls, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, persons from minority clans, older persons without caregivers, persons missing documentation, persons facing eviction, persons living in areas with armed conflict and violence.
The protection needs in Somalia changed and evolved over time. The current crisis affects individuals of all ages and genders, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and scale. Somalia’s societal landscape is highly granular, with numerous social groups, clans and ethnic groups and divisions between those groups constituting one of the dominant characteristics of society. Livelihood strategies vary, and some of these groups have comparatively less social capital, resulting in reduced clan-based protection and thus less financial support during crises. In the context of the ongoing conflict and the natural disasters, marginalized groups are likely to be disproportionally affected and are at heightened risk of protection violations. Inequality between groups can be aggravated in situations of displacement, where power dynamics regularly result in forced evictions. In general, women and girls are frequently victims of GBV within IDP communities, including rape and sexual assault, raising grave concerns about the safety and security in IDP sites. Families with members with specific needs are at heightened risk in such adverse circumstance and usually require specialized life-saving assistance, as well as interventions to mitigate further exposure.
GBV continues to be an issue of great concern in Federal Members States (Galmudug, South West State, Jubaland, Hirashebelle) Puntland and Somaliland worsened by multiple displacements and forced evictions due to flooding and armed conflicts. Women and adolescent girls from 18 – 50 years old (including those living with disabilities) are subjected to Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse in Middle Shabele, Lower Shabele, Gedo, Snaag, Awdal, Biadoa, Bay, Mudug and, Banadir.
The proposed project is designed to provide immediate life saving services and risk mitigation to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls through the following interventions:- Provision of Clinical Care for Sexual Assault survivors (CCSAS), Case management (CM) and Psycho social support(PSS) services including hotline services; PSS support to women and girls in safe space to support each other by networking, get information regarding GBV available services; risk reduction through engaging community protection groups, distribution of solar lanterns and dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls. The proposed project will therefore increase accessibility, quality and coordination of GBV services for the crises affected communities in Afgooye, Marka in lower shabelle, Balaad in middle shabelle and Dhusamareeb district in Galgaduud region and Banadir. The project will target 36,400 with GBV services in the six districts.

Mitigation Measures

STS will involve the leaders in the community with the affected population in all planning and preparation related to the activities proposed. STS has maintained a strong relationship with the local state governments which will allow us to approach the community with acceptance from local leaders in order to be able to gain the trust of the local community and provide the most effective services.3a. • Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. The integration of STS Protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. • STS will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect Gender practices, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build beneficiaries self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • STS will train all staff involved in the project on psycho-social support (PSS) including identification and support for disabled beneficiaries. 3b. • STS staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. •STS staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). 3c. • STS will locate distribution points in locations that are accessible by vulnerable population groups from the catchment population. 4b. STS will maintain a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained.
