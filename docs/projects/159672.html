Project Summary

According to UNHCR, 2.6 million people are internally displaced within Somalia, due to armed conflict, insecurity, and/or drought. An estimated 162,000 people were displaced from January to May 2019, which represents a 70 percent decline compared to the number of people displaced in the same period of 2018.
An estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall. An additional 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed) and engage in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. In total, 6.3 million Somalis face acute food insecurity through the end of 2019 (Somalia HNO 2019). The food security situation reflect the cumulative effects of below-average rains experienced across most parts of Somalia during the 2018 Deyr (October-December), followed by harsh weather conditions during the dry 2019 Jilaal (January–March) season and the delayed and poor performance of the 2019 Gu (April-June). These consecutive seasonal poor performances have been exacerbated by limited recovery from the extended impacts of the severe 2016/2017 drought. An estimated 2.6 million Somalis across Somalia remain displaced primarily due to conflict, lack of livelihood opportunities and drought.
The growing number of IDPs due to drought, conflict and lack of livelihoods is one of the major humanitarian challenges in Somalia. Between January and July 2019, conflict, lack of livelihoods and severe drought conditions has contributed to the displacement of 240,000 people across the country (FSNAU 2019). In southern pastoral areas, food security has widely deteriorated to Stressed (IPC Phase 2), driving by the impact of drought conditions during the Jilaal. Due to deterioration of livestock body conditions during that period, poor herd saleability caused household income to decline. However, improved body conditions, relatively larger herd sizes, and some access to milk is allowing most households to meet their minimum food needs. Poor households are continuing to engage in livestock and livestock product sales, though they have had to increase sales of non-productive livestock to compensate for the cost of atypical migration during the Jilaal. The exception is Southern Inland Pastoral livelihood zone of the Juba and Shabelle regions, where poor households have above-baseline livestock assets. Due to medium calving and milk production, households are now able to access adequate income, sustaining Minimal (IPC Phase 1) outcomes.
HAPEN has been undertaking livelihood interventions in Lower Juba aimed at strengthening food security. In the process, data collected still indicate fragile food security situation with households adopting destructive coping mechanism in order to access food. This mostly among new arrival IDPs and returnees.
HAPEN will seek to offer response to the needs that has affected both the host community and the IDPs who have sought to take refuge in these proposed areas. This will be attained through improvement of HH access to food by provision of unconditional and conditional cash transfers to the beneficiaries of the targeted districts of lower Juba. Besides this, those who are pastoralist will receive restock of animals offered with the vaccination with major focus of the female headed HH which will be accompanied by capacity building on best farming practices; veterinary care and pest control. The need to offer sustainable access to livelihood assets in hard to reach areas of Lower Juba will remain crucial and need urgent humanitarian response.
The proposed intervention aims to improve immediate access to food, protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, and to build resilience against current and future shocks for most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in Lower Juba.

Project Activities

Improve households immediate access to food:Provide unconditional (3,500HH) cash through cash voucher, Food vouchers distribution to IDPs and host community, more consideration given to women headed HH, their nutritional status, prioritizing the malnourished children (boys and girls) and mothers this will entail the cost of minimum expenditure basket (MEB) in selected districts of Lower Juba regions. Unconditional Cash Transfer will be provided to vulnerable households such as those without an able bodied breadwinner capable of participating in CFW activities. Women will be the primary beneficiaries of UCT. Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources. -Capacity building of the locals, flood affected communities both men and women, boys and girls in Lower Juba regions on pest control, veterinary services to the flood affected HH. -Farming inputs to vulnerable riverine and agro-pastrolist before GU rains giving consideration to women headed HHs.-Emergency fodder,saltblocks, water trucking and appropriate fodder production techniques to agro-pastroralist and pastroralist. -Technical Assistance to riverine, pastrolists, and agro-pastrolist. -Tractor and irrigation services to most vulnerable beneficiaries giving priority to women headed HHs and disabled. Support household and community resilience to shocks through rehabilitation and or restoration of community productive assets:•Rehabilitation of priority community assets which will be identified by the community through an inclusive process of consultations with VRCs, the local community and authorities. HAPEN will ensure a minimum 40% of women within these committees as well adequate representation of youth, elderly and people living with disabilities. Communal assets to be rehabilitated will include feeder roads as well as rehabilitation of water catchment areas. Rehabilitation of assets will be done via the CfW modality to ensure that beneficiaries earn an income from rehabilitating these assets. HAPEN will also intend to support disaster preparedness through facilitating the formation of community disaster management committees (CDMCs) in target community to undertake a hazard vulnerability and capacity analysis.

Please provide further explanation

Increase in the purchasing power through cash for work arrangement will enable food insecure households to access to food. This will alleviate effects of short term hunger besides addressing acute malnutrition to gain access to fundamental needs such as food due raised purchasing power. In addition, cash injection will help targeted households rebuild their asset bases following loss of entire livelihood support assets through past chronic droughts. The cash injection will not only allow the households to access immediate food but will enable them improve household purchasing power, which will boost the recovery process and therefore reverse the current unabated slide into the destitution by vulnerable populations. Similarly, as a result of cash injection through wages, there will be increased purchasing power among targeted beneficiaries. This will in turn ease strain on local social support networks, decrease deterioration of health and nutrition conditions. Beneficiaries will repay debts and in the process re-activate credit mechanisms. As a result of improved access to food in the market, there will be increased food consumption. HAPEN’s cash project aims to alleviate immediate humanitarian need of IDP and host communities. To mitigate the risk of the humanitarian needs reverting at the completion of the project. By developing a linkage and layering this life-saving response with longer term resilience projects, HAPEN is able to ensure that the gains to the beneficiaries is extended as they are enrolled in longer term projects. HAPEN will coordinate with partner agencies and consortia to identify their current and planned activities, to understand the gaps they identified as well as to see how this necessary life-saving response can be linked with their projects. Increased income will be used to access livestock production inputs such as drugs which will save animals from common diseases. In the past, beneficiaries sell their animals during the times of distress and so when the rains come, an opportunity for livelihood recovery is missed. Hence in the medium-term, recovery of animals which will have been spared will accelerate general livelihood recovery and enhance food security and self-reliance.In addition to the direct beneficiaries, the cash injection as provided in the project budget will have a multiplier effect to benefit traders, transporters, shopkeepers and service providers in the community, thus revitalizing the local economy. The project will also benefit the larger community, as direct beneficiaries share their income with their relatives, families and communities at large.
