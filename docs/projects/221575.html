Project Summary


The project, implemented in the districts of Metuge and Ibo, aims to improve food security and resilience among populations affected by climate shocks, violence, and displacement. Targeting internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, the intervention focuses on promoting climate-smart agriculture and sustainable fisheries.
Key activities include distributing agricultural and fishing inputs, providing technical training, and rehabilitating critical infrastructures. Special attention is given to vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, families with disabilities, and those with young children, using a gender-sensitive approach.
In Ibo, the focus is on enhancing agricultural practices and infrastructure, while in Metuge, sustainable fisheries and women-led initiatives in fish processing are prioritized. The project integrates participatory mechanisms to ensure inclusive beneficiary selection, contributing to strengthened livelihoods and community resilience.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will be implemented in the districts of Metuge and Ibo, areas deeply affected by climate shocks, including Cyclone Kenneth, tropical storms, and floods, as well as violence and extremist attacks. These crises have led to significant internal displacement, increasing food insecurity due to heightened competition for resources and the loss of livelihoods. The project aims to improve food security and resilience among vulnerable populations, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities. This will be achieved by promoting climate-smart agricultural and fishery practices, distributing essential inputs such as seeds, tools, and fishing equipment, and providing technical training to enhance productivity and sustainability. Special attention will be given to female-headed households, families with disabilities, and households with young children, using a gender-sensitive and inclusive approach to identify beneficiaries and address their specific needs. In Ibo, activities will focus on agricultural development. Farmers and IDPs will receive staple crop and vegetable seeds, tools, and training on climate-resilient techniques, including pest management and home gardening. Agricultural extension officers will also be trained to better support local farmers. Additionally, the project will rehabilitate key infrastructures, such as irrigation systems and post-harvest facilities, to strengthen local food systems. In Metuge, the focus will be on sustainable fisheries targeting Nangua, Bandar and Metuge villages. Fishermen will participate in awareness campaigns on fishing legislation and good practices, and they will receive environmentally friendly fishing nets and gear to replace harmful equipment. Training sessions will support post-harvest handling and marketing, particularly for women involved in fish and shellfish processing, with practical demonstrations on hygiene, preservation, and market access. By integrating participatory mechanisms and involving local authorities and community leaders, the project will ensure an inclusive and effective approach to beneficiary selection. Ultimately, it seeks to strengthen food security and livelihoods while building resilience to future shocks in these vulnerable communities.Target groups: - Resident farmers and fishermen of the target area, returnees and IDPs supported in agricultural and fishery activities;- District extension officers that will receive technical trainings; - Population of the target areas and IDPs that will benefit from an increased food security.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will support livelihood diversification and food security as follow: Ibo District:Distribution of agricultural inputs such as staple crop (corn, peanuts, beans, pumpkin and sweet potatoes) vegetable seeds, and agricultural tools; Capacity building for farmers and IDPs on climate smart and sustainable agriculture techniques (production, pest management, harvesting, post harvesting, storage)Skills development for agricultural extension officers: Training sessions will focus on enhancing the technical knowledge of district extension officers so they can support farmers with up-to-date information on sustainable agricultural practices. Topics may include climate resilience strategies, pest and disease control, soil health management, and the use of digital tools for agriculture. These officers are then better equipped to provide hands-on guidance and support to farmers throughout the region.Promotion the cultivation of home gardens for IDPs population and resident vulnerable population/groups with limited access to land;Rehabilitation of agricultural related infrastructures including irrigation systems for farming association, postharvest facilities and markets. Metuge District:Promote awareness-raising campaigns to strengthen fishermen's awareness of fishing legislation and good fishing practices to support measures to preserve fish stocks. Community Fisheries Councils (CCPs) and fishermen's associations affiliated to CCPs will host interactive sessions on fisheries legislation to reinforce compliance with good fishing practices. The sessions will last a maximum of one day, and will be held in two stages. The first will anticipate the distribution of nets and fishing gear that is not harmful to ecosystems. The second will be randomized to benefit CCPs and fishing associations that show a need for support to improve compliance with good practices.Support to small scale fishermen through the provision of fishing nets, gears and support for the replacement and conversion of harmful fishing gears. The project will purchase fishing net kits for emergency support to displaced and host communities in the Metuge district, guaranteeing access to essential equipment for practicing artisanal fishing to restore livelihoods.Strengthening entrepreneurial initiatives in post-harvest handling and marketing for fishermen. In order to reduce catch losses and guarantee the supply of fish on the market, in each intervention area, part of the members of each CCP and the affiliated associations, totalling 30 members, will be trained in fish handling.Strengthening initiatives led by women shellfish gatherers and fish processors through training in handling techniques (handling, processing and conservation shellfish and fish) and marketing. The project will purchase improved fish handling equipment for emergency support to displaced and host communities in the Metuge district. The groups will be trained based on practical demonstration and the topics will cover hygiene and sanitary aspects, improved drying processing techniques, the dried fish value chain and storage, conservation techniques and market access.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project adopts a comprehensive approach to identify and prevent potential risks, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the communities. Collaborating closely with local institutions, the cluster, and partners, the project utilizes existing feedback mechanisms like Linha Verde for real-time feedback on service provision, product quality, and information requests, allowing for swift response and resolution of concerns. The project actively engages communities through consultations, focus groups, and participatory data collection to identify and mitigate risks. These participatory processes will not only identify constraints but also empower communities to actively contribute to risk identification and mitigation strategies. Tailored sessions foster understanding and promote new habits to mitigate risks. In addition, Protection mainstreaming is integral to the project's design strategy,focusing on preserving safety, dignity, and equitable access. Oikos adheres to the 'do no harm' principle and Oikos staff is trained in identifying solution that respect these principles. Moreover, Oikos coordinates closely with protection actors, addressing gender-based violence (GBV) and other protection needs through referrals, ensuring a proactive approach to safeguarding vulnerable populations.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

This action plan is designed based on the inputs provided by target group identified through ongoing community engagement and participatory methods. It emphasizes collaboration with communities, valuing their resources and traditional knowledge to ensure community acceptance and active involvement. The project will create spaces for interaction, such as public consultations, focus gender groups and community data collection, to identify constraints and ways forward. Special attention will be paid to the constitution of the groups identified in order to guarantee inclusion, consolidating the participation of women, young people and middle-aged people, taking into account local customs

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project ensures inclusion of vulnerable households, especially including people with disabilities, through active participation of local stakeholders in beneficiary selection. Consultations with individuals with disabilities and specific needs allow identify specific construction requirements and adaptation. All the activities have been structured with the aim of guaranteeing access to disable people, such as the selection of the location for the training and the project staff will guarantee all the support to allow them to participate in the actions.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will provide capacity building for resident farmers and IDPs and extension officers on climate smart techniques, more sustainable and resilient to climate shocks, and on sustainable agricultural practices, that help to conserve the natural resources. The distribution of fishing nets and gears will be based on the replacement of harmful gear used by fishermen, preceded by a talk on good fishing practices. The awareness-raising will be interactive, aimed at the beneficiaries, and will include content on predominant ecosystems, the impact of harmful fishing practices, climate change and its impact on artisanal fishing, and measures to mitigate negative impacts. Therefore, the project will have beneficial consequences for environmental sustainability.
