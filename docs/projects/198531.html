Project Summary

The crisis in conflict-affected north-east Nigeria continues to be one of the gravest in the world today, marked by armed conflict, forced displacement and severe violations of civilians' human rights and dignity. They are the main drivers of vulnerabilities and protection concerns that the vulnerable populations face, including protracted displacement, human rights violations, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Internally displaced persons (IDPs) in and outside camps have weak coping mechanisms and are heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance to survive and improve their living conditions. Despite all the above challenges, the humanitarian needs overview (HNO 2022) predicted a decline in the figures of person’s in need (PiN) from 8.7 million people in 2021 to 8.4 million people in 2022. This reduction though minimal could be attributed to the contribution of humanitarian partners and the State actors in providing the much-needed support required to provide the minimum requirements to displaced populations living in and out of camps.
Yet, there has been no reprieve in the severity of the crisis or in the deterioration of the living conditions of people impacted by the crisis; they continue to face significant uncertainty, as well as deprivation well above pre-existing poverty levels and no access to basic service. People are also exposed to daily threats to their health and safety. Some local government areas (LGAs) have crude mortality rates that are higher than the rates during the height of the war. The mass exodus in 2022 of thousands of people from areas controlled by non-State armed groups (NSAGs) included thousands of fighters and their families. Despite this, violence continues unabated. Peace or security across most conflict-affected zones seems a distant prospect, especially in Borno State, as attacks by NSAGs continue at scale.
In an effort to alleviate the above-described challenges, SAF will provide Camp Coordination Camp Management (CCCM) and Shelter/NFI assistance to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Magumeri to tailor the humanitarian response. With the continuous inter-sectoral vulnerabilities and needs of people, SAF sees the need to provide necessary assistance to the affected population.
This action will target displaced populations, host communities and returnees. Activities will be implemented in displacement sites as well as host communities. Direct assistance will pertain to the following sectors of assistance; Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) and Shelter and NFI.

1 - Needs

According to OCHA’s 2022 HNO, it is estimated that 8.4 million people in BAY states will need humanitarian aid in 2022, only slightly fewer than a year ago. Those living in camps or camp-like settings display a much higher dependency on humanitarian aid because of movement restrictions and scarcity of livelihood opportunities. IDPs residing in camps and host community settlements continue to experience limited access to basic services, crowded living conditions, significant protection risks, and inadequate shelter and WASH facilities. Annually, IDPs face the rainy and dry season where their emergency shelters are affected by strong winds, flash flooding, heavy rains and outbreaks of fire have further deteriorated the situation. In Magumeri, 7 sites hosting about 33,190 Individuals where SAF CCCM is providing camp management support are classified as vulnerable to flooding with various levels of vulnerability. The sites are in the deep field areas namely Suleimanti, Gidan Breadi, Malayi, GGSS Quarters camp, Bare, GGSS Gajiganna and Ngawo Kawarlaye Camps. In addition, most sites that resulted from spontaneous settlements present some level of congestion, due to land constraints, constant arrivals of populations, access and security challenges constraining the extensions of camps and construction of infrastructure to mitigate flooding. SAF CCCM aims to ensure ground preparedness and adequate response to the rainy season, priority preparedness and response activities. IDPs living in host communities without CCCM site facilitation or out of camps are equally under the category of the most affected. According to DTM (Round 40), 40 per cent of the IDPs live in camps/camp-like settings, while 60 per cent of the IDPs are displaced among host communities. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The protection of the rights of persons displaced and affected by the humanitarian crisis has always been paramount during programming by SAF. The programmes designed not only enhance the protection of vulnerable populations, but also ensures that protection as well as humanitarian principles, such as do-no-harm, are mainstreamed into its programming. This will allow SAF to deliver assistance in a manner which promotes the protection, safety, and dignity of the affected population, and provides equity to women, girls, men and boys. SAF is also a member of the global protection taskforce as well member of the Protection Sector Working Group, SAF will take into consideration the standards and principles that have been agreed by the sector. In addition, SAF will integrate global Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) and SAF standards and practices.SAF takes the necessary steps to prevent sexual abuse and exploitation and respond to the needs of the affected population; it provides PSEA-specific training to raise awareness for its personnel, government stakeholders and beneficiaries. SAF will keep the progress to sufficiently include gender in needs assessment, outputs and activities focusing on addressing gender inequality through targeting gender and/ or focusing on gender issues to contribute to gender equality significantly. SAF's primary strategic planning documents include at least one high-level result on gender equality, where programmatic initiatives consistently include gender equality and the empowerment of women results. Any protection concerns that will be raised through the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism will also be addressed accordingly.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SAF will maintain its commitment to engaging with affected communities at all phases of the program cycle. SAF will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, SAF will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. For any distributed items, SAF will ensure the community receives proper briefing on the selection methods and the date, time and place of distributions to take place ahead of time. During distributions, demonstrations and sensitization is done on the usage of the items and the cash assistance and sampled post distribution visits. During distributions, SAF also has a separate Complaints and Response desk for receiving instant feedback. After the distribution of emergency shelter of NFI kits, SAF will conduct post-distribution monitoring. After the project has been completed, SAF will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning. SAF's Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework ensures that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with the community needs and humanitarian frameworks. SAF will utilize feedback from previous assessments and PDMs.
