Project Summary

Conflict-affected populations struggle to get access to humanitarian assistance and essential services in an inclusive and equal manner. Vulnerabilities associated with Disability, Gender, and Age (DGA) have been heightened by the conflict. PwDs are highly affected by the crisis and have a right to receive services equally with others. Children, women, and older persons are at a higher risk of exclusion. The project will work removing barriers to provide access to inclusive humanitarian assistance, reduce vulnerability and improve access of persons with disabilities, mainly women with disabilities and older persons. INGO will work to enable persons with disabilities to participate on an equal basis with others, on accessing services through provision of community approach, technical guidance for actors and reasonable accommodation for individuals (individualized support). Specifically, we are building the capacities of humanitarian organizations to be inclusive in their activities as well as building their capacities to better engage with humanitarian structures and organisations. Also, INGO will provide personalised social support and case management to PwDs as well as conducting community awareness sessions on including PwDs. As part of its work, INGO conduct barriers assessments to understand what prevents PwDs from participating on an equal basis with others. Our latest assessments found that humanitarian actors and NGO’s can lack the following: i) understanding of how to identify people with disabilities ii) clarity regarding the human rights of persons with disabilities; iii) human, operational and financial resources to provide inclusive and accessible services and we also found that people with disabilities themselves are rarely included in the program design, provision and evaluation of service provision and the delivery of the humanitarian assistance. Moreover, there are negative beliefs, attitudes and practices limiting the participation of PwDs in social life at the community and family level.
Based on this assessment, the INGO action focuses on providing guidance on how to include persons with disabilities mainly women within humanitarian assistance and to overcome risks of increasing vulnerability, as well as working to empower persons with disabilities to know their rights, having the confidence and the right tools needed to actively engage and play a key role within the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC). As part of this effort there will be activities operationalizing the IASC guidelines on the inclusion of persons with disabilities in humanitarian action
The INGO acknowledges, among other factors generating inequalities and that need to be considered, that disability, gender and age as socially constructed norms can be observed in any human group, Disability, Gender, and age are key factors to understanding different people’s capacities, needs, and exposure to risks. The impact of inclusion is not only through the direct targets, we rely on increasing the understanding of disability, gender, and age vulnerability risks, within communities, humanitarian actors, clusters, working groups and persons with disabilities themselves which will lead to the inclusion of PWD, older persons and women with disabilities in social, cultural and socio-economic activities. Moreover, empowerment and support persons with disabilities to participate through the project cycle will set an example of good practice for clusters and working groups who will be targeted within this project. Trained persons with disabilities will target wider communities, improving inclusion principles. Another target is the increase of the percentage of persons with disabilities through actor's projects. For example, for partners who will be trained, we are aiming to increase the percentage of PWD they target within their own projects up to 30% (the average percentage of PWD) as some of our partners only target between 10%-15% only.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO report, the deteriorating economy, widespread poverty, lack and loss of livelihoods, disease outbreaks needs and increase reliance on harmful coping mechanisms. PwDs face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, compounded by displacement where families/community support networks have broken down. They face stigma, social isolation, which increases risk of separation from their families, care providers, and dependent on ADs. According to HNAP, 24% aged 2 and above have disabilities. Economic deprivation and pandemics have spurred displacement and exposed PwDs to increased barriers to socioeconomic participation, accessing services. INGO assessments revealed that PwDs who live in camps in NES face significant difficulties accessing washroom facilities and being moving around and physically accessing the facilities. Another need identified was the need for a change of negative attitudes towards disability in the community. It`s also identified that older PwDs and PwDs who has children are in need of more support. Public transport remains a challenge for PwDs as they are not accessible. Also, protection risks for PwDs identified such as the need for accessible work opportunities, removing physical barriers and providing opportunities. Evidence based data reported by the INGO through barriers assessment in 2021, shows that PwDs experience reduced independence in their daily activities due to physical and attitudinal barriers (bullying).

4 - Implementation of activities

Through the implementation of a twin-track approach, the intervention will combine a package of activities to 1) empower PWDs by providing tailored support at individual level based on technical assessment of the needs of persons based on DGA criteria, persons will be targeted through community outreach approach. This type of support includes personalized social support or case management, as well as reasonable accommodation, rehabilitation services and psychosocial support through referrals. We are also providing training on inclusive humanitarian action as well providing capacity building opportunities and raising awareness about the rights of PwDs so that they can be more empowered within their communities.2) Build an inclusive and accessible living environment at the community level by removing attitudinal, institutional and physical barriers. This will be achieved by raising awareness in communities regarding disability inclusivity as well as training humanitarian actors to be inclusive in their activities 3) building the capacity of humanitarian actors to adopt the IASC guidelines and include PWDs in their activities. Tailored support to PWDs, local community’s representatives, humanitarian actors and services providers to promote inclusive environment and improve inclusion and accessibility in line with HNO 22-23.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

INGO keep low visibility in order to protect the identities of staff. In case of an attack on a location where activities are being conducted, this activates the DoC policy and staff are provided with PSS to prevent burn out. In terms of sexual exploitation or abuse, although there is no increased risk through the implemented activities, INGO applies PSEAH policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. Moreover, accountability measures are in place and ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. We conduct awareness raising activities on PSEAH and complaints mechanisms. In order to ensure that appropriate risks related to PwDs are identified, the following is applied: • Staff regularly trained on how to interact, communicate and engage with PwDs. • Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field staff are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data. • Working through partners: before becoming a partner, each actor goes through an assessment and vetting process. INGO monitors implementation through partner`s reports, field visits, feedback and response mechanism. • Collecting feedbacks from PwDs about their satisfaction, success stories and good practices is ongoing during project cycle. Additionally, there has been a recent outbreak of the cholera bacteria, however the prevention measure includes appropriate sanitation and hygiene measures which are already in place since COVID-19 pandemic.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The INGO expresses its determination to combat the sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment of children and vulnerable adults and implements measures to reduce risks on programs. Firstly, PSEAH and Child Protection policies and Code of Conduct are in place and PSEAH training and other safeguarding policies for staff and partners are mandatory; all new employees submit background checks. And accompanying family members, trustees, consultants, interns, volunteers are informed of PSEAH policy. Secondly, a whistleblower system and complaint mechanism are in place and open to all complaints incl. PSEA allegations. All beneficiaries receive information sessions at facility and community level with support of the PSEAH focal per hub as well as complaint numbers and process. Every allegation is monitored by the country Manager. The complaint follows received issues closely and addresses them accordingly. To address culturally sensitive and risks with GBV and CP, INGO will ensure gender balance of staff, with the aim of increasing number of female staff. Partners are involved in actions of abuse or harassment against beneficiaries: Any proven allegations of such practices result in follow-up of the case and termination of agreement unless the partner commits to and effectively ensures a change in rules and regulations.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Loss of access and border closure mitigated as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted. The lack of acceptance is mitigated through positive relations with stakeholders, community and staff. INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and ensure to keep close coordination with local authorities to maintain positive relations with community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure satisfaction of the community. Loss of funding is mitigated by the INGO’s close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding and adapting programs accordingly. The eventual spread-out of COVID-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this threat, the INGO and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging including DGA; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster. Logistic and fund transfer issues and supply chain interruption, mitigated by assessing transfer partners and enhancing coordination with INGOs and prepare contingency stock. Mismanagement for cash related issue is mitigated: 1) individual monitoring of beneficiaries, 2) FRM and anti-fraud corruption policy applied.
