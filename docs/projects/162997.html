Project Summary

Under the HRP 2020, UNICEF will endeavour to support the
most vulnerable and marginalized particularly, women and children. UNICEF positions
itself to work in the deprived communities with the highest level of vulnerability aiming to
address the underlying immediate and long-term causes. Based on the comparative
advantage of UNICEF’s programming and hands-on experience of 60 years in Sudan, it is
evident that a meaningful impact and results only be attained by adopting inter-sectoral
approaches given they are fully integrated.
UNICEF aims to deliver an integrated package for children by adopting a “whole child”
approach wherein, the UNICEF led sectors would work together by identifying the
geographic convergence, improving programmatic complementarity under a common
result framework thus, realizing rights of the most vulnerable children in the areas of
health, education, WASH and protection. This will allow UNICEF to enhance sustainability
within the projects and build resilience of the affected population and thus, bridging the
humanitarian and development.
UNICEF in partnership with UNHCR has developed a modus operandi for refugee
response both for in-camps and out-of-camp population. UNICEF is ensuring the delivery
of an integrated maternal child health and nutrition package along with WASH, education
and child protection in camps. Provision of out-of-camp services has been mainstreamed
with the service delivery for host communities. In the spirit of partnership and the new
way of working, UNICEF submits an intersectoral proposal – a progressive step in the
direction of achieving integration.

1 - Needs

Conflict that broke out in South Sudan in December 2013 displaced thousands of civilians.Sudan is a transit and destination country for refugees along the eastern Africa migratory route into North Africa and Europe. It is estimated that 50 per cent of the newly arrived refugees in eastern Sudan move onward. Based on information from IOM, the main reasons for migrating to other countries are economic, with 76 per cent of people interviewed highlighting leaving Sudan for economic reasons, followed by 16 per cent of respondents who mentioned targeted violence or prosecution. Sudan hosts among the largest population of South Sudanese refugees regionally, some 810,155 people. Amongst the overall refugee population, 48 per cent are children and youth (0 – 17 years old). It is estimated that around 70 per cent of the refugees live below the poverty line (less than US$3.84 per day) in Khartoum and around 60 per cent in Darfur and the Kordofans. With limited livelihood opportunities, refugees are vulnerable and reliant on humanitarian assistance to for protection and to cover their most basic needs.Over 90 per cent of refugees in Sudan are reliant on food assistance, and suffer from a lack of dietary diversity, rations gaps and lack of sufficient income and means to supplement household food stocks. Refugees’ food insecurity has contributed to ‘critical’ and ‘serious’ malnutrition across most camps and larger refugee settlements. Major refugee sites in Sudan are overcrowded and congested, and land for expansion is limited or not available. Water supply gaps and poor sanitation access and latrine coverage further aggravates refugees’ malnutrition risk. Refugees’ face poor health status and are largely without equitable access to health services. In out of camp locations – where about 70 per cent of refugees reside – there is limited access to quality secondary and tertiary medical care treatment, including no access to treatment for chronic illnesses. Refugees face mental health service gaps despite high trauma related to persecution and asylum flights, and other mental health care needs. Protection gaps related to fulfillment of basic rights and vulnerability to discrimination issues and physical threats also undermine refugees’ physical and mental well-being.Refugee camps in Sudan are very congested and are well above capacity, undermining, water, health services and other assistance, especially shelter which further undermines the dignity and physical safety of refugee families. Refugees lack of access to land, productive assets, financial services and other means of production in Sudan, limiting their access to livelihoods, income and education, and undermines their capacity for self-reliance over the long-term. These lack of access to services and livelihoods opportunities also introduces additional protection risks for refugee children, including high prevalence of child labour, early marriage and school drop-out.The HNO 2020 identified 132,642 SSR vulnerable children in basic school age 6-13 years. The project targets 75,215 children which represents 56.9% of total SSR children in need under education.The current report on UASC reveals that number of separated and unaccompanied SSR children represent 70% of the total UASC population amongst the refugees.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Impact on environment/natural resources from the perspective of:WASH • The sites for the construction of new water supply sites will be selected taking in consideration low environmental impact of water pumping through IWRM and climate change adaptation measures for balanced and rationalized utilization of water resources. • Decision makers and communities’ awareness and sector practitioners’ capacity on IWRM and climate change adaptation will be enhanced. • Geohydrological information management and monitoring system will be established with focus on the groundwater vulnerable areas. • Environment friendly water supply motorized solar systems, hand pump and concrete instead of wooden latrines slabs will be widely adopted.HEALTH:The climate change is one of the factors increasing population's vulnerability to natural disasters in Sudan. Therefore, this project considers supporting health emergency preparedness, timely response and building community's resilience as one of the main objectives. Locaties at high-risk of the natural disasters (mainly floods and drought) are fully considered and targeted by this project. Building the community capacity andsupporting services' provision through community workers- including community midwives- are among the activities planned for under this project to serve as mitigation measuresNUTRITION:Impact will be felt through waste generated from packaging materials of RUTF and Nutrition supplements.
