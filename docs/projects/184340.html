Project Summary

The emergency health response aims to save lives, contribute towards improved physical and mental wellbeing of 389,988 individuals and their families. This will be realized through provision of sustainable access to quality and comprehensive health care services .The interventions will support 71 Mother and Child Clinics, 44 Primary health units/Health posts and 3 mobile teams located in 21 districts spread across 8 regions in Somalia. The interventions are aimed at contributing towards a reduction in morbidity and mortality vulnerable Somali communities, especially among the most vulnerable groups such as children under 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and people living with with mental and physical disabilities. The project will increased access and quality of basic health services, prioritizing hard-to-reach areas.
Planned measures will include both disease prevention and management.
Prevention measures include vaccination services and community awareness raising, WV will improve child health outcome through Timed and targeted counselling (ttC) which aimed at enhancing demand and health seeking behaviour will be adopted across the target project locations.
On disease management, the proposed project will support clinical diagnosis and management of common illnesses disease outbreak surveillance, reporting, investigation and response. Health facilities will be supported with health care worker capacity building, medical supplies and equipment provision to provide communicable disease management, comprehensive basic reproductive health care services in alignment of Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP). Except for covering essential maternal and child health, the capacity building will also include medical supply chain management, case referral, COVID-19 case detection, diagnosis and management, Gender Based Violence and protection incident management as well as psychological First aid.
WV will enable PLWDs access health care services through the disability friendly health facility set up including latrines provisions .The project will utilize Community Health Workers (CHWs) who will do home visits ,beneficiary follow up and defaulter tracing with particular attention to PLWD and COVID affected HHs, facilitate their referrals and movement to the health facility to seek health care services. Special emphasis on health promotion and home follows up will be done targeting pregnant women and HHs with children living with disabilities.
Moreover, WV through continuous engagement with community leaders, disability associations and the protection unit will sensitize communities to raise awareness on health rights of PLWDs to mitigate stigma. In each community engagement, WV will ensure and facilitate representation with 15 % of participants being PLWD during all community engagement forums. Boys and girls, men and women living with disabilities attended to will be closely followed up to ensure continuity in care thus transforming their health and well-being.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

WV will put robust measures to address barriers that hinder access to health services and factors that prevent health-seeking behaviors. The measures will pay specific attention to the underprivileged and vulnerable groups in Somalia such as women and girl, youth, disabled, poor, and marginalized group. WV will work closely with the communities to identified the disadvantaged groups and include them in project design, monitoring and evaluation process. This means to ensure their specific needs and barriers are identified as well as their concerns can be heard. WV will promote active and representative participation of different groups in communities to influence decisions that affect their lives. To ensure transparency, communities will be sensitized on available services, health priorities, their rights and what is expected of community members. WV will work together with MOH to strengthen coordination mechanisms, which will ensure effective service provision and prevent duplication of efforts while reallocating and prioritizing resources to underserved areas. WV working with MOH will provide timely information to target population regarding project objective, interventions and timeline to make informed decisions and provide relevant comments. Inception meetings will be held both at regional and community level to share information on all aspects of interventions including support for SGBV, protection and evictions, The project will take into consideration Inter-Agency Standing Committee commitment to affected populations. A complaints and feedback mechanisms (toll free number, community meetings, and feedback boxes) are in place. A toll-free hotline with Interactive Voice Response (IVR) system proved to be an effective tool to receive feedback from different stakeholder. WV’s quarterly accountability report (April – June 2021) reported 86% of the feedback was channelled through the hotline. It also observes to be a more suitable tool for women and girls to provide their feedback as more than half (64%) of the feedback were received from female who may find more difficult to voice their opinion in community meeting in Somalia context. The project will leverage on existing WV Community Response Map platform that facilitates online recording, tracking, compilation and visual mapping and feedback received from community members daily.WV will select beneficiaries and design project in consultation with community members, including different community groups, and with MOH. This will not only help to ensure the appropriateness of the activities of different groups, it will also make sure the project will not put vulnerable groups such as women, boys, girls in harm by considering their needs such as the distance and safety to access health facilities.The project will utilize CHWs who will do home visits and follow up on PLWD HHs , facilitate their referrals and movement to the health facility to seek health care services. Special emphasis on health promotion and home follows up will be done targeting pregnant women and HHs with children living with disabilities. Moreover, WV through continuous engagement with community leaders, disability associations and the protection unit will sensitize communities to raise awareness on health rights of PLWDs to mitigate stigma. In each community engagement, WV will ensure and facilitate representation with 15 % of participants being PLWD during all community engagement forums.
