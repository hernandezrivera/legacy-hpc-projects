Project Summary

The project aims to improve food security, nutrition and income generation of IDPs and host communities in the districts of Montepuez, Ancuabe and Meluco by improving food production, access, availability, preservation and consumption as well as production for the market of yield crops and increasing household income.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Ayuda en Accion, through this intervention, is planning to assist around 7,500 households (37,500 people) to improve livelihoods and food security by running agricultural production and sustainable businesses. The proposed project will directly address the problems of lack of food, economic exclusion and the risk of deprivation of rights of a growing population of internally displaced persons that requires an effort of social and economic inclusion through economic empowerment to take off its capabilities.The intervention will cover around 37,500 IDPs and host communities, selected from the districts of Montepuez (Nicuapa and Mapupulo), Ancuabe (Nangumi and Marocane) and Meluco (Minapo) centers. The proposed communities will benefit from training in food production, preservation, processing and consumption, followed by the distribution of agricultural and poultry kits to improve food security and livelihoods. Similarly, from a market systems approach, linkages will be introduced with agricultural marketing companies with products for sale, which will allow for increased income. These activities have a twofold objective: on the one hand to improve the food security of the communities and seek to increase incomes and, on the other hand, to reduce dependency on humanitarian aid from a nexus approach.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Result 1: Improve food security by increasing production, access, availability and consumption for 7,500 displaced families:1. Implementation of home gardens in 3,000 households with the resettled communities of Montepuez (Nicuapa and Mapupulo), Ancuabe (Nangumi and Marocane) and Meluco (Minapo) sites.2. Promotion of cultivation and commercialization of food and cash crops for 3,500 households in the resettled communities of Montepuez (Nicuapa and Mapupulo), Ancuabe (Nangumi and Marocane) and Meluco (Minapo) sites.3. Distribution of 5,000 chickens to 1,000 households from Montepuez (Nicuapa and Mapupulo), Ancuabe (Nangumi and Marocane) and Meluco (Minapo) sites, including the construction of chicken coops; 4. Introduction of food processing and conservation methods, including creation of nutritional spaces for cooking demonstrations in Montepuez (Nicuapa and Mapupulo), Ancuabe (Nangumi and Marocane) and Meluco (Minapo) sites Result 2: Increase the income of displaced families and host communities through market-oriented agricultural production:5. Increase the production of yield crops with the families.6. Establish market linkages through agricultural marketing companies for the purchase of production.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection mainstreaming is an integral part of AeA’s design strategy, this alongside the participatory methods that AeA use to collect information help greatly to identify the risks posed to each demographic. Staff working in the field are aware of the do no harm, a principle which assists in making the most suitable decisions for the populations in question. We prioritise preserving safety and dignity in the design of field activities and in the planning of any event. Ensuring that there is equitable access for all and encouraging participation in order to empower those we are helping.Special attention is given to individuals more vulnerable.Protection risks are identified jointly with the community and followed up on by the team. Some risks and challenges are already identified and will be addressed during the project implementation, such as the following: 1. the complexity of working with young people, due to emotional volatility and life projects; 2. the cultural aspects that limit the participation of women in economic activities; 3.the instability of the region; 4. the lack of safe land for agriculture for the displaced; 5. the residential instability of the target group. Information derived from FGDs, community gatherings and committees will be processed and forwarded to the relevant clusters.In order to be able to face these challenges, the intervention that is proposed has a sensitive nature, seeking to better know the target group, their needs, capacities, assets and expectations, in the context of the local culture and local conditions, as well as looking for solutions that are adapted to their conditions.Trainings and the fostering of community structures allow for improved and sustainable agency of the displaced community, with special focus on those who are more vulnerable, in all community relevant aspects.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

During the year 2021, direct assessments have been carried out in both displaced and host communities, identifying needs based on criteria that differentiate social composition including: gender, age, disabilities, background and condition. This has been complemented by constant attention and structured and semi-structured meetings with local authorities and partners, including a study on FSL conducted in 2022 as part of ongoing NCRP (Northern Crisis Recovery Project) under implementation in those districts. The survey addresses people's condition and captures differences at different levels and sectors.The accountability will be carried out through constant monitoring with community groups of local authorities, focus groups segmented by age, gender and condition, as well as with the participation of local government services and state representations at each level. The accounts will be drawn up on a quarterly basis.In addition, Ayuda en Accion has activated a green complaints line which will be publicised with the beneficiary groups and will be operational for any eventual case that may arise. Peace Community commitees recently created will also be used to access and give a feedback on issues raised by the beneficiaries.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities are considered more vulnerable than others and will be involved in the project from the beginning of the project implementation. The two local partners (KAMPOS and MAHLAHLE) are experienced in leading interventions with displaced communities and will work with Ayuda en Accion staff to identify and include people with disabilities in all activities, according to their respective locations. Households containing people with disabilities will have priority in the distribution of start-up kits for home garden, as well as raising chickens. The selection of nutrition training locations for cooking demonstrations, will be held considering the distances to the households with people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The question of composting will be essential when providing compost for the creation of home gardens. Associations / micro-enterprises will be able to provide services for such waste management and obtain returns that allow them to be made sustainable in the future, through donor contributions, sale of products from waste, etc. Opening of wells, horticulture as well as use of production of composting will be implemented according to environmental recommendations to avoid contamination of water sources. Likewise, in production and irrigation activities, priority will be given to the use of clean technologies, such as solar pumps.
