Project Summary

The proposed project will lead to reduced suffering, increased and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for 12,720 IDPs, returnees, and host community (HC) members (3,353 women, 3,515 men, 2,857 girls, and 2,995 boys) in the Circle of Gourma, Region of Timbuktu, Mali (Ultimate Outcome 1000). This will be achieved through interventions in the sectors of Protection, WASH, and Livelihoods. Consultations with community members and other stakeholders, especially women, girls, those with disabilities, and the elderly, will be conducted to ensure a gender-sensitive and inclusive response throughout the project intervention.
IR’s WASH intervention will result in increased use of safe, gender-sensitive WASH infrastructure for IDPs, returnees, and HC members (3,029 women, 2,911 men, 2,581 girls, and 2,479 boys) in the Circle of Gourma, Timbuktu, Mali (Intermediate Outcome 1100). This will be accomplished through two immediate outcomes: increased access to safe drinking water for conflict affected IDPs, returnees, and HC members (3,209 women, 2,911, men, 2,581 girls, and 2,479 boys) (Immediate Outcome 1110) and improved knowledge of safe, culturally appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices among conflict affected IDPs, returnees, and HC members (330 women, 318 men, 282 girls, 270 boys) (Immediate Outcome 1120).
Immediate Outcome 1110 will be achieved through the following outputs: broken safe water sources rehabilitated {Output 1111; Q3, Q4}; female and male community repair agents selected and trained {Output 1112; Q3}; and female and male Water Management Committee (WMC) members trained {Output 1113; Q3}. Immediate Outcome 1120 will be achieved through three outputs: awareness sessions on culturally appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices conducted {Output 1121; Q4}; female and male WASH management committee members trained {Output 1122; Q4}; and gender-sensitive and culturally appropriate hygiene and sanitation kits distributed {Output 1123; Q4}. The community members, especially women and girls, will be consulted on WASH activities prior to implementation due to the disproportion they are impacted in this sector. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) will take place.
IR’s Livelihood intervention will result in gender responsive and culturally appropriate food production systems strengthened for conflict affected IDPs, returnees and HC members (350 female-headed HHs and 870 male-headed HHs) in the Circle of Gourma Rharous, Timbuktu, Mali (Intermediate Outcome 1200). This will be accomplished through three immediate outcomes.
Firstly, Intermediate Outcome 1200 will be achieved through improved equitable access to culturally appropriate immediate agricultural assistance for 50 female-headed HHs and 350 male-headed HHs among returnees and HCs (Immediate Outcome 1210). Outputs for this outcome include culturally appropriate agricultural inputs and seeds distributed {Output 1211; Q1} and cash transfer organized in a safe, culturally appropriate manner {Output 1212; Q1}. Secondly, Intermediate Outcome 1200 will also be reached through increased equitable access to life-saving income generating activities for 200 vulnerable female and male-headed HHs among the returnees and HCs (Immediate Outcome 1220). This outcome will be accomplished through these outputs: vegetable garden rehabilitated {Output 1221; Q1} and cash transfer organized in a safe, culturally appropriate manner {Output 1212; Q1}. Lastly, Intermediate Outcome 1200 will be accomplished through increased water availability period for animal consumption for the conflict affected pastoral population (145 women, 140 men, 171 girls, 164 boys) (Immediate Outcome 1230). This will be achieved through the following outputs: water pond rehabilitated {Output 1231; Q1} and cash transfer organized in a safe, culturally appropriate manner {Output 1232; Q1}.

Needs

Project Rationale: The protracted multi-dimensional humanitarian crisis has been ongoing in Mali since 2012 and has created a disruption in the supply and access to basic social services, such as water, sanitation and hygiene, has led to a deterioration of livelihoods and has created, as well as exacerbated, protection issues. Continued insecurity within the region has further exacerbated needs within these sectors. Since 2012, the Circle of Gourma Rharous, the proposed area of intervention, has experienced occupation by armed forces and massive population movements. WASH: In the Circle of Gourma Rharous, 33% of the population do not have access to clean water (WASH Cluster). An Islamic Relief (IR) Mali baseline survey within the Circle highlighted the following sources of water supply used as the primary means for obtaining water: 21.11% obtain water from water wells or hand/foot pump boreholes, 16.51% obtain water from fountains, 15.93% from developed wells, 15.48% from surface water, 14.28% from faucets, 7.04% from rainwater, and 5.61% from unmanaged wells. According to 70.7% of households (HHs), water is not treated which has negative impacts on the health of HHs, especially in children affecting their nutritional status. The most commonly used treatment methods are filtering water with cloth/fabric (46.6%), followed pharmaceutical tablets (19.1%), and the use of bleach. The draft of the 2019 HNO for the WASH cluster highlights that in northern regions of Mali, women, girls, and boys, are travelling longer distances in search of water and are also more exposed to violence by armed groups & abandoned explosives of war. Protection: Protection, particularly relating to physical violence is a significant concern consistently highlighted during cluster meetings within Northern Mali. In Mali, more than 56% of women are victims of GBV and 44% of married women undergo physical, sexual or emotional abuse. According to the MICS (2015 Multiple Indicator Survey), it is noted that: (i) 16% of girls are married before age 15, (ii) 17% of women aged 20-49 are married before the age of 15 Years and (iii) 49% of the same age group are married before the age of 18 years. The phenomenon is more frequent in rural areas (53%) than in urban areas (36%). Cases of women being made to forcibly marry members of armed groups are also present and young boys are being recruited by armed groups and exploited, along with disruptions in education systems leading to increases in this as well. Girls face challenges related to EFM and FGM/C. The area of Gourma Rharous is an area where child marriage, verbal abuse, and sexual violence is present at an alarming rate (IRM Assessment). Children are the most affected by the armed groups occupation at 71%. This is illustrated in the high rate of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) among kids in the area (83.9%). In October 2018, communities of Indaiatafane (in the Circle of Gourma Rharous) were highlighting the abuse and looting they faced due to Malian armed forces and armed forces of Group 5. Livelihoods: Due to insecurity in the area, the livelihoods of a vast amount of population have been disrupted. According to the findings of the National Food and Nutrition Survey of September 2018, 66.5% of the population in the region of Timbuktu are in light food insecurity, 5.69% are in moderate food insecurity and 9.2% in severe food insecurity. Only 8.8% are food secure. IRM’s baseline survey (2017) found that 40% of all HHs surveyed were food-insecure, with this being more accentuated in Hamzacoma (67%), Rharous (65%), and Bambara Maoudé (39%). Severe deterioration of the livelihoods due to climate change factors combined with the insecurity which has affected the free movement of people and goods, all cumulating in decreases in agro pastoral production on both crops and pastures. Rice and market garden perimeters have lacked maintenance due to insecurity negatively impacting yield
