Project Summary


OB and BRD plan to implement food transfer modalities to respond to food insecurity starting in Aljehad and Alsalam camps in Dana sub-district in Idlib, over a period of TWELVE months starting from January/2020. These two camps are reeling from high severity food insecurity. While Aljehad camp has been around for 6 months, Alsalam is relatively new and is not yet reported on the CCCM data though we have communicated this to the cluster, and there is already a WASH project underway with OCHA support. The project meets the main sector objective to improve the food security status of food insecure people, supported with essential complementary activities. This is to be achieved through the following modalities.
A standard food ration is provided to every beneficiary without distinction. Daily food assistance will cover distribution of vegetable hot soup and bread to 2,931 HHs for 165 days. 9 rounds of Food Baskets will be distributed to the IDPs totalling 8,793 HHs (38,250 individuals in total) over the 12 months, along with kitchen kit and fuel to support the HHs with cooking their own meals. The project will also allow volunteers from camps to partake in the operation. The project will provide basic food security to 2,931 HHs in the two camps plus further 5,862 HHs that will be found in the same Dana sub-district (Community) over the course this project. The specific objectives include daily provision of vegetable soup and bread to residents of Aljehad and Alsalam camps for survival. To sustain the food security, the project will distribute food baskets, kitchen tools and fuel.
Implementation will start with establishing 2 kitchens, one in each camp, to prepare hot vegetable soup. OB/BRD have chosen this methodology because of its nutritional value and relative ease of preparation. The equipment and food ingredients will be procured locally. The project will also involve the beneficiaries by taking on volunteers, 20 every fortnight, from the camps to be trained to support the operation. A total of 200 volunteers will be trained over 5 months. Along with the ready-to-eat, 2,931 HHs will receive 3 rounds of food baskets, 1 round of cooking utensils and 3 rounds of fuel to support themselves over the year to meet their basic food needs. The additional 5,862 HHs from new IDPs population will receive remaining 6 rounds of food baskets and fuel and 1 round of kitchen kits. Also considered in the design are the beneficiaries with disabilities (3.77% of the residents) and 3,300 under 5.
The FBs will be designed based on the general recommendation i.e. providing 2,100 kcal per person per day: Consideration will be given to adding soap to the ration to encourage hand washing and hygiene promotion.
The project will ensure that Ready-to-eat as well as the FBs meet the requirements for energy and protein, a balanced diet that is culturally acceptable, fit for human consumption and easily digestible for children and other vulnerable groups.
Management considerations will include local food habits, community participation, resources for food preparation and consumption, do no Harm, and quality control.
As for operational, financial and security risks to the project, there is low to medium risk to project objectives. With the locations being close to the Turkish border and far from active armed conflict, it is relatively safe for beneficiaries and staff, and supplies can be
sourced with relative ease. Should the conflict affect the locations, the IDPs are most likely to move towards the Turkish controlled Afrin and Euphrates Shield areas. In this case, OB/BRD can shift the operation to follow the IDPs into those areas where we have the capacity to establish the food aid.
OB and BRD can deliver this project on an estimated budget of $2,000,000 in 12 months, reaching to 38,250 total number of IDPs .

4 - Needs

Olive Branch and Basma Relief Development have targeted Dana sub-district in Idlib province. According to the database of FSL Cluster, local organizations are covering only 41% of need of the people that are living in this subdistrict. There are 8 organizations working in Dana sub district and providing food assistance but none of the local organization are covering the needs of the people living in the camps in this sub district. According to the need assessment conducted by OB and BRD in Dana sub district, it found two camps, Aljehad and Alsalam camps, with total population of 12,750 (Men:2801, Women:2912, Boys:3617 and Girls:3420). In terms of under 5, there are 1,637 boys and 1,721 girls. The people in the camps are desperate need of food security. No organization is providing any regular food assistance to this vulnerable population. BRD in partnership with OCHA are providing WASH facilities to the same camps. The residents were basically displaced from rural Homs, north Hama and south of Idleb. Since December 2019, there are daily new arrivals of IDP reaching to these 2 camps. Aljehad and Alsalam camps have been in existence since 2017. This direct need assessment as of 27th January 2020 highlights the following key data and findings. - There have been no regular food supplies for six months. - They are getting by with ad-hoc food aids and handouts from individuals and charities. - Of the total, there are 3,358 under 5. - For the adults there are no job prospects nor any form of livelihood opportunities in general in the Dana sub-district. - Foods are available in the local markets; however, the camp inhabitants have little or no access to cash money to buy essential foods. - 13% of the camp inhabitants are getting 1 meal per 2 days. - 65% are getting 1 meal per day. - 18% are getting 2 meals per day. - 4% are getting 3 meals per day. - There are 481 of people with special needs (189 Females, 292 Males).

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention by OB and BRD is constructed on the following activities. 1-Daily food assistance for the assessed food insecure population of 12,750 in the camps through providing them with hot Vegetable soup and fresh bread. 2- Distributing 9 rounds for 8,793 HHs, 3 rounds per group of 2,931 HHs of FBs, which contain Milk powder, Rice, Flour, Sugar, and Oil, plus some essential items to go with and a piece of soap. Quantity will suffice for 1 month for an average HHs.3- Training 200 volunteers (20 per 15 days) on preparation and cooking soup for the camps (200 beneficiaries). 4- Distribution of 2,931 kitchen tools for HHs. 5- Distributing 2,931 fuel vouchers for HHs (20 litres) sufficient for 1 month to go with the FBs above. This is to encourage them to take initiatives to cook for themselves. The Daily (Soup): middle of M1 to end of M6, daily provision measured to the HH size. Daily (Bread): middle of M1 to end of M6, daily provision of 1 bread per person. Distribution FBs: M2 to M12, 9 rounds of distribution to HHs, approx. 29kg. Training volunteers: start of M2, train a group of 20 volunteers each month to help in preparation, cooking and distribution. Distribution Kitchen Tools: M2 for 1st group, M5 for 2nd, M8 for 3rd group, consisting of Cooker and Utensils, for each HHs. Distribution Fuel: M2,M5,M8, 3 rounds of distribution of 20 lt per HHs followed up FBs. Implementation will start with establishing 1 soup kitchen in each camp to start delivering the vegetable soup along with fresh bread. Soup is chosen because of its nutritional value and ease of preparation to meet the emergency needs of the people in the shortest time possible. Soup delivery will be for 5.5 months starting middle of M1 of the project.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project is designed to benefit all population of the camps including women and girls. We have accounted for the children under 5 which is 26% of the targeted beneficiaries to be considered in the food basket design. For the older and PWDs, the aid will be taken to their place of accommodation. The project should reduce the risk of vulnerable women and girls from being exploited in anyway due to lack of basic life-saving aid. The HHs will be provided with kitchen tools to cook in their own place and thus reducing the risk to their safety and security. School age children will be able to attend education as a result of their basic food needs being addressed. OB and BRD also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to in the course of the operation. Basic needs will reduce domestic violence within the family and tension within the camp. Women will be able to cook meals in the safety of their own tents and feel less vulnerable. In general, OB/BRD will ensure that there is sufficient monitoring conducted by OB/BRD to avoid any kind of harm done to the beneficiaries in the distribution. Proper measures will be in place to ensure there is no abuse of power by any members of the implementation team, the volunteers or the IDPs themselves. OB/BRD will involve the women participants in the operation to decrease the protection risks in dealing with gender sensitive matters. Also, there is no risk of tension between inter-IDPs or with HCs as all beneficiaries are IDPs and there is no host community involved.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Olive Branch and Basma Relief Development have assessed the risks to the proposed intervention and can be summarised in terms of Operation, Financial, Security in addition to Protection discussed in the previous section. In terms of security risks, we estimate this to be low in that the district is sufficiently close to the Turkish border and is considered safer with easy access to the border and markets. However, the situation can change if the current escalations continue for next six months, in which case there is likelihood of the IDPs moving into Turkish controlled areas such as Afrin and Euphrates Shield. Should this happen we would move our operation to these areas since we already have capacity on the ground to operate directly. Furthermore, BRD and OB will coordinate with the local civilian authorities and the camp management to avoid any kind of interference by any armed groups in our operation. Financial risks are low as we have a well-established financial control, procurement and anti-fraud policy to ensure the duties are sufficiently segregated for the benefit of transparency, control and avoidance of any frauds. Since the materials will be sourced in the relatively stable local markets or across the border if required, there is low risk of impact from inflation and price fluctuations. In terms of disruption to our operation due to shortage of required items, we estimate the risk to be low in that the required food items and kitchen tools are available in the market. In addition, the risk is minimised by having more than one supplier to work with OB/BRD.
