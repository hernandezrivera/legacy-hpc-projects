Project Summary

NRC will target 2150 youth ages 15-24 with lifelong learning opportunities, including life skills and vocational training. NRC will also provide Education Sector-wide capacity building opportunities to 150 Master Trainers and 3695 learning facilitators from across all implementing partners. Please note that only youth are contained in NRC's overall target beneficiary number, per Sector request.

Objectives

Objective 1: NRC will provide immediate access to equitable learning opportunities to crisis-affected refugee and host community adolescents and youth by:• Developing and implementing learning, empowerment and participation opportunities for adolescents and youth (ages 15-24)• Providing education opportunities for adolescents and youth (ages 15-24) who are out-of-school in host communities Objective 2: NRC will improve the quality of teaching and learning for refugee children and youth, and increase teaching-related professional development opportunities by designing and implementing:• An integrated and holistic teacher capacity development program in the camps• A program to support the capacity development for personnel in the camps

Needs

Almost 919,000 Rohingya refugees currently reside in Cox’s Bazar District, with the majority having fled from Rakhine State since 25 August 2017. Living conditions in the camps are rife with protection concerns, particularly for vulnerable children and youth, who are regularly exposed to risks such as trafficking, drug abuse, child marriage, gender-based violence, and child labor. Currently, of the estimated 518,400 at-risk refugee children and youth ages 3-24, over 43% of all children 3-14 years and 97% of refugee youth aged 15-24 years still lack access to quality learning opportunities. For youth, the situation is particularly dire, with refugee youth currently not permitted to enroll in formal education facilities or partake in livelihoods activities outside of the camps. Overall there is a gap in quality education activities for children in the primary grades, and a lack of opportunities at all for secondary school children and older youth. Where access to informal education exists, the focus has been on primary education activities in Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs), and the proposed Learning Competency Framework Approach (LCFA) curriculum materials for children in Levels I-IV have yet to be approved. Level V is also currently under development, which is slated to cover basic literacy and numeracy for adolescents and youth. Additionally, a Secondary Data Review (SDR) by the Youth Working Group (YWG) in September 2018 found that overall, only 2% of males and 1% of females 15-17 years of age attend any type of NGO-run safe spaces (incl. Child/Youth/Woman Friendly Spaces and Temporary Learning Centers). The 4W of the YWG from the same period also shows that less than 10,000 youth ages 15-24 are currently being reached with any form of targeted activities across all Sectors. The SDR also found that 69% of female adolescents and youth have never participated in any type of educational activities at all, either currently or prior to their displacement. The SDR identified that precarious work conditions, weak gender sensitive planning, and low salaries are causing a drop-out of teachers, especially females, that could possibly affect low retention and enrollment rates among girls. This corresponds with anecdotal responses from the Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA) conducted in early 2018 which found that teachers, who themselves are simply “learning facilitators” from the community with little to no practical classroom experience, report a need for further training. Teacher professional development (TPD) on pedagogy, classroom management, life skills, and subject-specific trainings remains a critical need. Should the LCFA roll out, additional trainings on the use of level-specific lesson plans will also be required. Additionally, as most refugee children and youth have experienced traumatic events, providing quality psychosocial support (PSS) programming will remain essential, particularly as this has been noted as a gap within the Education Sector. Finally, while host community youth are afforded quality education opportunities on paper, in practice their access to schools and livelihood opportunities has also diminished with the influx of the Rohingya. Prior to the influx, Cox’s Bazar was among the lowest preforming districts in the country with regards to education access, retention and achievement, with a literacy rate in Ukhiya of 39.3% (compared to a national level of 51.8%). The burden placed on infrastructure and the economy by the new population has also begun to cause tensions between the refugee and host communities, with the depletion of natural resources and the decrease in daily wages being key flash points. Failure to address access to education and sustainable livelihoods opportunities, as well as issues of social cohesion among youth, may lead to civil unrest. There is a clear need to provide access to activities promoting life skills and job opportunities for both populations.

Activities or outputs

OBJECTIVE 1: NRC will promote youth education and empowerment through the establishment of Youth Support Centers (YSCs) that will provide pathways to lifelong learning for youth ages 15-24. YSCs will incorporate mobile, home and community-based learning, and will be established separately for host community and refugee youth. Activities will include life skills training, PSS, basic literacy and numeracy, formal school re-enrollment support, and vocational training. ALL TARGETED YOUTH will experience improved social and economic wellbeing through access to YSC activities, and will decrease reliance on negative coping mechanisms. YSCs will include life skills courses adapted from NRC’s global youth programming, and recreation and dialog activities that will bring together host community and Rohingya youth to promote peaceful coexistence. This will include principles of social cohesion as well as cooperation with NRC ICLA (falling under the Protection Working Group) implementing alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. YSCs will regularly consult youth for their feedback and will design relevant activities in cooperation with them – this will include a market assessment led by the youth, as well as a community response and feedback mechanism. HOST COMMUNITY YOUTH pathways will include access to income generating activities (IGA) - in cooperation with the YWG and Livelihoods Sector - formal employment, or entrepreneurship opportunities through technical and vocational training. Female dropouts will be targeted during YSC life skills activities using a contextualized version of NRC’s Adolescent Girls’ Toolkit to help support reintegration into formal systems. Formal school dropouts will be targeted during YSC life skills activities for re-enrollment support utilizing conditional cash programming for attendance. This will be done in close cooperation with MoPME and DPEO. REFUGEE YOUTH pathways will include modules to support access to small-scale IGA through basic vocational training. Refugee youth will be targeted with age-appropriate basic literacy and numeracy activities using the LCFA Level V. NRC will target at least 75% females in the host community and 50% females in the refugee community. NRC will ensure selection criteria are respected through comprehensive vulnerability and needs assessments, with projects targeting at-risk youth including school dropouts, youth from female-headed households, and those youths who are the household breadwinner, particularly in locations where NRC is a camp management actor. OBJECTIVE 2: NRC will provide support to education activities in the camps through the capacity building of local and national partners and their Learning Facilitators under the umbrella of the Education Sector. NRC will build on work started in 2018 whereby a cohort of 92 Education Sector Master Trainers (MTs) has been established. Capacity building will reinforce and follow-up on prior learning for technical and field staff, including key lifesaving messages on the topics of psychosocial support (via NRC’s Better Learning Program), health & hygiene promotion, and disaster preparedness. The contextualized TiCC modules will also be reinforced, as will the INEE Minimum Standards. Additionally, NRC will expand these MTs to a cohort of 150 and will support them on the use of materials curated for the LCFA, particularly subject matter and level-specific lesson plans. NRC will also provide training on PSEA, basic pedagogy, classroom and time management, and active learning, in collaboration with MoPME and DPEO. Trainings will follow a ToT with accompaniment model, whereby NRC will utilize internal expert capacity building staff to rollout and follow-up on trainings in the field alongside MTs as they train 3695 Learning Facilitators (LF). Teacher Learning Circles and teaching exchanges will be established for LFs, and continuous in-service training and monitoring schedules will be established with Sector partners.
