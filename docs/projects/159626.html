Project Summary

Overview of the existing problem: According to UNOCHA release updates: The 2018 Deyr rainy season performed poorly in many parts of Somalia. As a result, drought conditions are expected to develop in early 2019, particularly in central and northeastern parts of the country. The overall humanitarian outlook is thus expected to worsen up until the Gu rainy season begins in April, and a scale-up of humanitarian response will be required in order to prevent further deterioration into food insecurity for households that rely heavily on agriculture and livestock production.
Buurhakaba has no deep underground water sources, as there were no successful borehole drilling operations the past due to natural limitations. For that reason, all the people in the area depend on shallow dug wells and surface water catchments. Both these types of sources depend on rainfall for recharge and are subject to both biological (Pathogens) and mineral (Salinity) contamination when the recharge in late in coming and water levels drop down during the dry season and in extended dry spells and drought, and the lack of water. During the Deyr season, the rainfall was very low. Buurhakaba district faces serious water scarcities. The severe drought, insufficient and unsafe water and poor sanitation are increasing the risk of outbreaks of communicable diseases. The district being facing challenges of water scarcity and continued environmental degradation due to the major sources of water found in the district are subsurface water resources such as spring dams and shallow wells for domestic and livestock development however, the only main source of water source in Burhakaba Town is A BIG DAM (Warta Dawladda) for communities living in Buurhakaba town rapidly dried and created a crisis of drinking water. Due to the Constant deficit rainfall has definitely contributed to the current crisis,
How the problem is linked to national priorities and policies:This project is designed within UNICEF’s and Somali Federal government’s WASH development frameworks, with all activities aligned to the Somalia Rolling Work Plan. The project is in line with federal government' National Development Plan in which increased provision of equitable, accessible, and affordable social services (including WASH services) is a key priority. In addition, the project will work within South West State’s Ministries of Water, Public health and Education plans.
Annual Work Plan 2017, FGS/UNICEF gives priority to theprovision of water, sanitation,and hygiene to most parts of the country. According to National Development Plan (2017 – 2019, p.118), the government of Somalia recognizes the importance of water, hygiene, and sanitation as one of the pillars for sustainable development of the economy and management of resources. The vision, “Adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation for all” underpins the problem.
The relevance of the Programme in addressing problem identified
BTSC conducted WASH baseline survey between September and October 2018 to identify WASH needs of rural and urban communities in Burhakaba district and Bay Region.
The assessment provided substantial amount of household-level information on water, sanitation,and hygiene, according to the assessment, 90% of the households sourced their water from unprotected sources such as water catchments and open shallow wells. In Buurhakaba district Water is first priority for intervention, there is no permanent source of water, the current water crises in Buurhakaba, it is an impact of drought and it needs solution and urgently intervention to reduce the burden of water crises. Therefore, in this matter BTSC priorities firstly Emergency WASH intervention particularly rehabilitation of open shallow well with hand pumps and water treatment through Chlorination of water sources which can contribute access safe and clean water to children’s health, nutritional status,and wellbeing.

Project Activities

ACTIVITY 1. Water supply Construction of 30 shallow wells equipped with Hand pumps Chlorination of 200 water sources and trucks including orientations for chlorinators and testers benefiting 10,000 persons (10 Chlorinators for Burhakaba town and surrounding villages) Train 60 community hygiene promoters to conduct outreach hygiene promotion (at least 1 promoter per 500 people; at least 50% are female) Procurement of supplies for shallow with hand pumps(30 Hand pumps) Training of 30 community hand pump attendants to manage the rehabilitated/protected water points Training of WASH committees on management and conflict resolution for new water sources with hand pumps(30 persons)ACTIVITY 2. WASH in Schools Construct 24 gender disaggregated twin pit latrine blocks for 6 schools and 6 health facilities with hand washing facilities (triple pit latrine/school and twin pit latrine blocks for MCH) Implement WinS in 6 schools (includes construction of group hand washing stations, establishment of hygiene clubs and training for teachers and CECs) Distribution of MHM for adolescent girls for 6 schools (1480 girls) Training of 24 school teachers, regional and district educational authorities on 3-star approach ACTIVITY 3. SANITATION AND HYGIENE Conduct hygiene promotion to alleviate harmful hygiene and sanitation practices and norms in 20 communities (includes celebration of global WASH events) Sensitization training for opinion leaders includes religious leaders and elders (40 persons).ACTIVITY 4. CLTS Conduct CLTS triggering and post trigger monitoring in 20 villages in Burhakaba district Identify and train 40 CLTS facilitators Identify and Train 20 community mobilizers for service delivery

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. BTSC adopts a broad-based consultative approach in its assessments, project design and implementation. Broad-based in the sense that every section or group of the community members are consulted. Special attention is given to members of marginalized community/ minority groups in a manner to create animosity and draw hostility from the majority groups. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls, and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes. BTSC engages broad-based gender-inclusive relief community committees at village levels and project sites. Women, girls, and boys are part of these project committees and they are coached/mentored and encouraged to articulate and bring out challenges facing them to the decision-making table at the village level. 3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. BTSC’s Staff and partners are trained on PSEA. Protection is mainstreamed in all BTSC’s interventions and there is dedicated staff who conduct high-level awareness campaigns on prevention of all forms of Gender-Based Violence (GBVs) at the community level. 3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. BTSC ensures access to humanitarian assistance and services does not result in the exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence in terms of distance and location of the assistance and services. The project team will consult all gender and age groups when selecting the site for water and Sanitation facilities. Water and sanitation facilities will be located in sites that are close proximity to the homesteads. Latrines will be located in properly lit areas and with a good view of all sites. Male and female
