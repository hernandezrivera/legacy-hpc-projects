Project Summary

Safe drinking water, improved sanitation facilities and hygiene education (WASH) beyond the household, and particularly in the school setting, is crucial for the health and education of children. Children spend a significant portion of their day at school where WASH services can improve educational opportunities and decrease the potential for disease transmission between students, and girls are often the most affected because of the poor WASH conditions inside the schools. Critical challenges, such as the effects of general lack of funding for implementation of educational activities, and the insufficient operational budget have a negative impact on schools supplies and schools maintenance in GS. In line with the WASH Cluster Objective 1, the action aims to ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services with dignity for the un-served, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank through ensuring basic WASH services for conflict-affected, un- or under-served and vulnerable populations and institutions in the Gaza Strip.
The proposed intervention responds to the SDGs 6 and 4. SDG6 aims to ‘ensure available and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’ and includes targets for universal access to drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for all by 2030. This action’s purpose is to maintain WASH services that are suitable for men, women, girls and boys of all ages, including people with disabilities (PwDs). Further, the action will ensure inclusive and quality education for all and promote lifelong learning and includes targets to access primary and secondary education, improved learning outcomes and the elimination of inequalities at all levels of education. This action addresses the means of implementation and aims to build and upgrade education WASH facilities that are child, disability and gender sensitive and provide safe, non-violent, inclusive and effective learning environments for all, including, among other things, providing access to basic drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services in all schools. By constructing and rehabilitating WASH facilities from a gender perspective and bearing in mind the needs of PwDs, the intervention will follow the global UN indicator “proportion of schools with access to: basic drinking water; single-sex basic sanitation; and basic hand-washing facilities” to measure the success of the intervention, which aims to also generate improvement in the Health and Education sectors.
The action aims to achieve the above objectives through the rehabilitation of destroyed/degraded WASH facilities, addition of toilets with water point units, awareness campaign on hygiene promotion and environment protection, menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and distribution of hygiene kits.
The selected 20 governmental schools are located in ARA (access restricted areas), considered as high vulnerable areas by OCHA: 3 in Bet Hanoun, 15 in East of Gaza Governorate (Alsha'f, Alshijaia, Alziton, Al Tofah, Al Daraj), and 2 in Middle Area (Ebin Zaydoon and Buraij)
The proposed intervention will benefit a total of 20,876 people: 11,445 girls, 8,716 boys and 715 teachers (468 women and 247 men). The intervention has been coordinated with Education Cluster,

Needs

In the context of the Gaza Strip (GS), WASH facilities in institutions, especially in schools, do not meet the minimum SPHERE Standards nor the INEE standards required to ensure a healthy physical and psychological environment. The lack of adequate sanitation facilities providing dignity and privacy increases dropout rates, especially for girls. The provision of adequate WASH services in health facilities significantly contributes to the prevention of infections and spread of diseases, the protection of students, as well as upholding the dignity of vulnerable populations including PwDs.In 2018, PWA has also witnessed a deterioration of WASH infrastructures in schools. Overall and according to the recently published SDG WASH in Schools baseline from the Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP, 2018), there is only 20% coverage of all three elements of basic WASH services in schools, such as access to drinking water, basic sanitation and hygiene services. Further, over 490,145 school children (of which 240,998 boys and 249,147 girls) school children across the oPt face challenges in accessing quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment. The ongoing restrictions on the movement of people and goods, and recurrent conflict in the GS have affected all 450,192 basic, secondary and kindergarten students and teachers. Of particular concern are students and teachers in the North Directorate (80,925) and East Gaza Directorate (75,716), which were most intensely affected by the 2014 conflict. The students who attend schools in the ARA also face the risk of Israeli military incursions (HNO, 2018).Critical challenges, such as the effects of general lack of funding for implementation of educational activities, and the insufficient operational budget have a negative impact on schools supplies and schools maintenance in GS.Within the framework of the WASH Cluster response strategy for 2019, this project aims to ensure the sustenance of life saving WASH services in institutions, so as to protect the health of girls and boys in schools.In coordination with the Ministry of Education (MoE), the WASH and the Education clusters, INGOs and different stakeholders involved in the sector, several needs assessments and surveys were undertaken by GVC in GS over 2017-2018. As a result, 20 schools in the ARA were prioritized for support. The identified schools are in urgent need of rehabilitation/reconstruction of WASH facilities (supplying 20 domestic and drinking water tanks, maintenance of 17 drinking water SS tanks, maintenance of 3 desalination units, addition of 11 PwDs toilets, maintenance wastewater network, isolation of roofs and lack of SWM, etc.). The assessments also highlighted bad hygiene conditions and solid waste management, including lack of proper hand washing, and lack of proper solid waste management. Specific awareness campaigns and distribution of hygiene kits, through schools’ students and their parents, are one of the best ways to divulgate the change behaviour message improving personal hygiene and waste management.Most of the students, especially boys, are vandalizing WASH facilities in their schools. There is a need for a behavior change campaign to promote positive practices and increasing students’ involvement in the maintenance of the WASH facilities within their school premises.Lack of access to WASH services in school poses a greater burden to women and girls. In school, lack of access to WASH services has a negative impact on girls’ education especially, foremost those who are unwilling to use schools’ toilets since they are dirty and/or lacking privacy, and during their menstrual cycles. Furthermore, the menstruation is a taboo subject, which exacerbates the challenges of managing menstrual hygiene where there are no safe and private toilets with availability of water and soap. Providing MHM in schools encourages adolescent girls to continue their education, improving their long term development.

Activities

1. Rehabilitation of destroyed/degraded WASH facilities in 20 schools, including for PwDs, and the installation of gender segregated sanitary units. The works will include the rehabilitation of water supply facilities, the replacement of tiles, fountains and pipes, maintenance of desalination units, maintenance wastewater network, isolation of roofs, as well as general rehabilitation when/if required.2. Addition of toilets with water point units for girls, boys, and PwDs in 11 schools. The design will consider age, gender, privacy and dignity of users, especially for girls and students with special needs.3. Awareness campaign on hygiene promotion and environment protection (solid waste management): Conducting 80 public awareness and hygiene promotion activities benefiting 9,000 students. Specific attention will be given to WASH, hygiene and environment awareness by establishing Environmental Clubs in each school and Environmental Health Committees in each healthcare facility. This will include trainings and workshops for teachers, students, and healthcare staff. A KAP survey will be designed and conducted before the campaign and after is it finalized. The campaign is also designed considering the characteristics of each institution and the population it serves, with different needs, expectations and behaviors of girls and boys or patients, also depending on their age and abilities. If/when possible, the campaign is conducted with an innovative methodology/technology, including collaboration with Rural Women Development Society (RWDS). RWDS is a Palestinian organization based in Gaza with solid experience on awareness campaigns. GVC and RWDS have already worked together in WASH related projects in the Gaza Strip, where RWDS takes the responsibility of the soft component.4. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) will be promoted by RWDS in the targeted institutions to ensure 3,641 girls have access to adequate facilities. MHM will be an integral component of hygiene promotion campaigns5. Distribution of hygiene kits benefiting 4,500 students, jointly designed by students and teachers and in compliance with the Education and WASH Cluster standards, will ideally include specifically needed items such as soap, nail cutters, toothbrushes and toothpaste, anti-lice shampoo, combs and sanitary pads (for adolescent girls).An assessment is conducted prior to the technical implementation to investigate the main protection relates issues and needs and gaps, with girls, boys, with and without disabilities to evaluate indicators as baseline After the completion of the intervention, an overall evaluation will be conducted, including quantitative data collection and qualitative analysis through separated groups (girls and boys) focusing Safety and Dignity. Finally, a feedback mechanism is assured throughout the project implementation. When designing and defining interventions, the input of beneficiaries be taken into important consideration. During the technical visits social staff will discuss with boys and girls to collect their different needs related with WASH facilities and transfer this information to the technical staff. Awareness campaigns will foresee active participation of the students.
