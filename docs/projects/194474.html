Project Summary

The Dialogue and Research Institute DRI has vast experience in implementing protection and HLP legal services such as land rights issues, peace building and conflict prevention, GBV, human rights, justices and rule of law, women and youth empowerment. DRI will target Unity, Jonglei, Central, and Western Equatoria states and intervene in the most affected counties by flood and conflicts. The project is in -line with Humanitarian Response Plan (HNO 2022) and the Cluster Strategic Objectives. Women, men, boys, girls, and other marginalized vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities will benefit from this integrated lifesaving protection and HLP support for IDPS, returnees and host communities who are critically affected and categorized in the locations of IPC 3, 4, and 5 severities. 20,000 beneficiaries disaggregated as (7,000 women, 5,000 men, 4,000 boys, and 4,000 girls) will be reached in Unity, Western and Central Equatoria and Jonglei state targeting Juba, Bor South, Nyirol, Uror, Koch, Mayendit, Mayom, Rubkona, Negaro, and Tambura Counties. 15% of the target population will be persons with disabilities will be assisted. The HLP component of this project will provide legal aid services to 600 beneficiaries monthly in each location including men, women, girls, and boys during the project period and about 6000 beneficiaries quarterly. The general protection component will consist of 60% while HLP for 40% in the integrated proposal caseload. DRI strength in HLP and GP emanated from previous consistent records of achievement in human rights protection and protection of HLP rights through funding from the Uk-Foreign and Commonwealth Office, (FCO), USAID, Viable Support to Transition and Stability (VISTAS), Building Cross line Interdependence for Peaceful Co-existence in Rubkona, Tambura, and Bor, Tambura, Juba and Bentiu with a GP and HLP rights documentation, protection, and GBV projects. The ongoing USAID -IRC funded project Community Managing Conflict (CMC) in Unity state is addressing GP and HLP issues including land and borders breeding conflict among communities. This cemented DRI experiences in GP and HLP access to lands, housing, and properties rights from 2016 to date. DRI will implement this project in four States to enhance advocacy on HLP and GP with provision of legal aid targeting returnees, IDPs, and host communities including women and girls to enhance access to HLP ensuring gender equality and access to properties among communities with keen attention to the most vulnerable PWDs. DRI will enhance protection environment and build the resilience of the affected communities by engaging communities stakeholders such as local chiefs and administrators, women and youth groups as well as State executives and public institutions (schools and women groups, strengthening Peace Networks (PNs) and Peace Committees (PCs), to benefit from this integrated project. DRI will ensure to implement this project through a coordinated approach for wider information sharing and complementarity of the work and synergies among protection partners. DRI proposed the following activities under this integrated project:
A) Awareness creation on HLP and protection-related messages.
B) Establishment of dispute resolution mechanisms in the community involving women and local leaders.
C) Protection assessment and protection monitoring with community stakeholders.
D) Provision of PSS, group, and individual counseling.
E) Peacebuilding programs and peaceful co-existence for social cohesion and conflict resolution.
F) Strengthening protection monitoring system within the community.
G) Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. DRI will increase paralegal volunteers, Communities Land Dispute Working Groups (CLDWG), in Tambura, Rubkona, to HLP centres with solar lights for communities printing, storage of legal files.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis resulting from years of conflict, recurring natural disasters, and severe economic breakdown continues to affect and destroyed the livelihoods of vulnerable people in South Sudan. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2022) reported 8.9 Million people in dire humanitarian needs. The seasonal flooding threatened thousands of lives with critical impacts on livelihoods and the economic situation while housing Land and Properties (HLP), rights for women, girls, boys and elderly and Person With Disabilities (PWD), remains source of conflict among communities. The ongoing violence and insecurity caused by revenge killings, cattle raiding, land disputes, and negative political influence among the youth creates instabilities. Following the recent events of internal conflicts and natural disasters such as flooding which affected nearly 830,000 people for the last two consecutive years countrywide, many people lost their homes, livelihoods and the infrastructure has been badly damaged. HLP rights is limited among women as majority of displaced, returnees and host communities experiencing lands disputes while majority of PWD rights are denied due to their disabilities. The political situation has created a gap of trust, competition over resources and intercommoned violence has strained relations, spread social tension, and led to organized crime, including cattle raiding, revenge killings, and attacks that flares up repeatedly. The GBV cases continue to rise with women, girls of adolescent age are severely suffering from emotional pains as result of forces marriages, evictions form their homes due to the fact that they are culturally level as ‘’women’’ while they continue to experiences sexual violence in their daily life. The lack of access to justices on land disputes is critically affecting women and girls and PWDs. The land as a source of income and identifier of community is a contested matter. The gap between law and practice in consideration of women's right to HLP remains a source of conflict. In consideration of the above issues, DRI proposes to support the protection of displaced and conflict-affected populations through the static, mobile, and emergency integrated intervention in the targeting counties using the DRI presence in Jonglei, Western, Central and Western Equatoria, and Unity, will continue to use the existing program infrastructures such as offices, vehicle, and already established Communities Peace Networks (PNs),, and women groups to promote peace and HLP rights for women, girls, boy, and elderly person equally. DRI shall upgrade existing HLP, legal officers, and already trained Volunteers to promote safe and secure disputes resolution, protection, and access concern among communities, such as women girls and boys, and PWD. DRI’s community engagement strategy will include the provision of legal assistance to vulnerable groups, Women, men, girls, boys, elderly, and People with Disabilities, through legal aid services, an awareness session on lands cases and making follow up with authorities and providing feedback on time to concern victims and communities. DRI will provide legal aid targeting returnees, IDPS, and host communities including women and girls in Unity, Jonglei, Central and Western Equatoria on rights to lands, and gender equality and access to properties among communities. DRI will continue raising communities’ awareness on HLP rights, legal framework, and available mechanisms for HLP disputes resolution for returnees; IDPS, and host communities including women and girls, men and boys at project locations. It is anticipated that 40% of communities are on land disputes which create more violent conflict and caused destruction and displacement of women, girls, and elderly persons

2 - Project activities

A) Awareness creation on HLP and protection-related messages. DRI will train 100 volunteers to raise awareness on land rights and expands the use of local radio messaging on HLP dispute resolution mechanisms shall be strengthened to empower communities on HLP rights. B) Establishment of HLP disputes resolution mechanism. DRI will establish dispute resolution mechanisms in the community involving women and local leaders. DRI will form community Working groups on land disputes including documentation committees to address the land issues. C) Training of local stakeholders on HLP, disputes resolution, and ADR to strengthen dispute resolution mechanism including documentation committees to address the HLP issues in the community. DRI will train local authorities on the rights of the land of people of concern such as displaced persons, Land ownership, and transfer of ownership including HLP awareness-raising and outreach activities to empower communities. DRI will establish weekly sessions with communities leadership on HLP disputes resolution in communities. E) Legal assistance/counseling/case management. DRI's existing 2 legal officers will be increased from exiting legal 2 officers to 5 legal counselors who will be providing legal aid to various communities on lands disputes in all target locations. F) Protection assessment and protection monitoring with community stakeholders. DRI will rollout session for community engagement to address disputes overland's rights and plot ownership for women, men, girls, the elderly, and persons with a disability. G) Provision of PSS, group, and individual counseling. DRI will strengthen community land committees at Payam, Boma, and villages levels. encase of town DRI will work with Lands Commission to address lands disputes. DRI will put in place mechanisms for tracking communities' tension around disputes and rights of access to lands through legal aid and community lands dispute mechanisms such as traditional leadership council, Payam chief. Gender consideration will be high curb into the program activities. H) Peacebuilding programs and peaceful co-existence for social cohesion and conflict resolution. DRI will promote community social cohesion meetings at the community levels to ensure host communities, IDPs and returnees mitigate violence conflict among communities. I) Strengthening protection monitoring system within the community. DRI will train and strengthen community protection working groups and shall incorporate HLP disputes resolution mechanisms in all protection activities. DRI will assist and train communities on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding and shall provide training for local authorities and relevant institutions' staff trained on HLP, dispute resolution, and ADR among other key HLP issues in the communities. J) Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. DR will establish volunteers to assist communities on the coverage of protection for women, girls, boys, and PWDs as well as marginalized groups.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRI has a well-established community engagement strategy and community feedback engagement using FGDs, IEC materials, uses suggestion boxes stationed at the protection centers to collect feedback from the community about Land rights, Protection concerns for girls, women, men, boys and elderly, and PWD among communities. DRI will conduct interviews and groups discussions with the community members to share their reflections on protection issues, HLP rights, and DRI will share with them available channels for disputes resolution mechanisms. DRI also has an existing mobile and static complaint desk for the collection of program feedback and community engagement strategy which exists for the last 8 years including regular meetings with state and county land council, land commission, and community land protection groups. DRI community engagement strategy will include the provision of legal assistance to vulnerable groups, such as women, men, girls, boys, elderly, and PWDs through legal aid services, an awareness session on HLP rights and channel for resolution of disputed cases and shall make routine follow up with authorities, providing feedback on time to concern victims and communities, family and individual affected by HLP. DRI will provide legal aid targeting returnees, IDPs, and host communities including women and girls in Unity, Jonglei, Central and Western Equatoria on rights to lands, gender equality, and access to properties rights among communities shall be a top priority. DRI will continue to raise communities’ awareness on HLP rights, legal framework, and available mechanisms for HLP disputes resolution for returnees; IDPS, and host communities including women and girls, men and boys at project locations. It is anticipated that 40% of communities are on land disputes which create more violent conflict and caused destruction and displacement of women, girls, and elderly persons. In addition, DRI will carry out community awareness events weekly community focus group discussion on HLP outreach sessions in each of the project locations to raise community awareness on HLP rights, legal framework, and available mechanism for HLP disputes resolution targeting returnees, IDPs host and communities including women and girls, men and boys in each project location. Each awareness outreach will target about 300 persons and a total of 6000 persons in all the project locations on monthly basis. DRI will also engage communities through training of HLP volunteers and established volunteer body known as Community Land Disputes Working Group (CLDWG in all locations to address urgent lands. DRI will produce radio clips, and radio talk shows around HLP, to raise awareness on HLP rights, the legal framework, and available mechanism for HLP disputes resolution targeting communities, returnees, IDPs, and host communities including women and girls, men and boys in each project location. DRI will produce 6000 branding materials such as T-shirts containing key messages for communities on HLP rights and legal redress, PSS, referral services, and legal court representation of cases filed for dispute resolution. Similarly, DRI will support the PWD cases through legal aid officer assigned for cases involving PWD on Housing and Land Properties (HLP) rights to work with PWD victims whose lands rights have been jeopardized or compromised by conflict, army occupation of lands, schools, and farmlands and traditional ancestral lands. DRI's Legal Officers on HLP shall work with communities to address HLP cases among returnees, IDPs, and Host Communities to ensure conflict around lands rights issues are dealt with in accordance with respect to PWD, women, girls, and elderly and marginalized communities. All legal and HLP volunteers shall be 50% females and PWD shall be given all priority in any dispute resolution meeting or legal redress cases.
