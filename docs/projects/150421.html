Project Summary

The aim of the project is to support 5 communities in Ramallah and East Jerusalem governorates identified by the WASH Cluster by providing them with new water network extensions, rehabilitating current water networks, and installing transmission pipelines and new water reservoirs.

Needs

West Bank and Gaza is a very water-scarce lower-middle-income territory with a relatively water-dependent economy and is vulnerable within its geopolitical setting. Assuring water security is thus a priority. Water security requires adequate water resources that are well managed, including management of risks, and water supply services that provide sustainable, efficient, and equitable. The geopolitical context places severe constraints on the water security of the Palestinian territories. Political economy factors have constrained the ability of the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) to improve service delivery. These constraints affect the provision of services both in accessing adequate bulk water supplies and in investing in and implementing projects for developing water supply and sanitation infrastructure. In West Bank, access to piped water on premises does not translate into quality services. Consider the measure of “access when needed,” defined as “not available for at least one full day during the previous two weeks.” By this measure, access to improved water in the West Bank drops from 93 % to 80 %, with the biggest decreases in the governorates of Jenin and Nablus, as well as Ramallah and Al-Bireh governorates, where service is frequently intermittent. Sanitation coverage is high, but the connection to sewerage networks is much higher in Gaza than in West Bank. In the West Bank, despite near-universal access to improved sanitation (94 %), access to sewage connections is only 30 %, with rates varying widely by governorate, from zero (in Tubas) to 59 % (in Qalqilya). Access also varies strongly by household income: only 13 % in the poorest quintile are connected to sewer networks, compared to 42 % in the richest quintile. Only 36 % of the population of West Bank and 22 % of the population in Gaza receive a continuous supply of water every day of the month. Despite this high intermittency, nearly 50 % of the population in West Bank report no problem with their service – indicating that they have settled for low standards due to developing different coping strategies. Inefficiencies in the distribution of water through service providers add to the challenges with bulk water supply. Physical losses, theft, and unbilled water supply are very high. Overall, service providers receive revenues on only about 70 % of the water they supply; the rest is so-called non-revenue water (NRW). While a rate of 30 % for non-revenue water may be sustainable in a water-rich country, it is not in the West Bank. These levels of non-revenue water are a dramatic loss for a very water-scarce nation. According to the rapid assessment conducted in cooperation with the WASH Cluster and PWA WestBank water department, it has come to an agreement on what locations and what activities need to be implemented while ensuring the adherence to the National Water Sector Plan. The situation of the targeted locations is deteriorating; rustic old networks, high losses and spills and a shortage of water, which sometimes leads to water pollution. There have been many efforts to do the needed repairs. yet, they are not being implemented properly. The suggested interventions will protect human health, minimize water losses, which will directly support the increase in water availability and decrease the cost of purchasing. Global Communities will provide overall management and strategic direction, including on quality of performance and achievement of results, provides administrative, financial, and compliance oversight for the suggested activities; manages and provides quality assurance oversight constructing and rehabilitating of HHs domestic water supply and installing HH connections, construction water reservoirs, and carrier and advocating for hygiene promotion and protection mainstreaming activities. Further, GC will coordinate with and among partners and leading program MEL plan.

Activities

Activity 1. Rehabilitation and construction of new households’ domestic water supply networks and installations of household connections. Global Communities with the support of the WASH Cluster, Municipality of Ramallah, Jerusalem Water Undertaking and PWA will construct and rehabilitate domestic water networks, install main trunk pipeline and installations of new households’ water connections as follow: • Connect targeted households at Khelat Al-Qadi - Kafr Ein by installing household connections and sub water distribution networks through pipes diameter of "1" inch and a total length of 2500m. • Construction and rehabilitation of domestic water networks with pipes diameters of "1", "2", and "3" inch with a total length of 4000m at Umm Safa.• Construction of main trunk pipeline of “4" inch diameter with a total length of 600m and rehabilitate domestic water network with distribution pipes of “3" inch diameter and a total length of 1200m in Al-Khalila neighborhood in Al-Jib. • Construction and installation of domestic water networks of “2" inch diameter of a length of 3000m and “4" inch diameter of a length of 3500m at Sur Bahir (Al-Muntar، Wadi Al-Hummus ،Dier Al-Amoud communities) in East-Jerusalem. It should be noted that comprehensive field assessments, engineering designs, and tender documents will be prepared by Global communities and its Partner PHG in close consultation with the Municipality of Ramallah, Jerusalem Water Undertaking and PWA along with the engagement of the local communities. Activity 2: Construction of a water reservoir with a capacity of 500 m3 and installation of 2500 m carrier (feeder) – 6 inches. This activity will benefit the targeted communities by the WASH Cluster through the construction of a water reservoir in Qibya - Ramallah governorate. The work will also include the rehabilitation of existing associated water networks. Meanwhile, Global communities in coordination with PHG, WASH cluster, PWA, Municipality of Ramallah and JWU will conduct a technical assessment and hire a technical consulting firm to design and prepare tender documents for the reservoir and associated piping work. Those activities (1-2) will contribute to the well-being of women and girls in the families as they bear most of the sanitation and hygiene responsibilities, where such burden increased due to lack of access to water. Hence, this intervention will support better water access to different age and gender groups. Further, protection officer will work in close relation with the beneficiaries to measure the progress of communities in regard to WASH practices and to explain existing protection referral systems to raise awareness. Activity 3: Community mobilization and hygiene promotion for practice change. This activity is designed to target women, children, and men. The targeted beneficiaries will be provided with hygiene promotion activities and standards and protection messages using various approaches based on the communities’ assessments. This will include behavior change approach and communication for Development (C4D) approach. The households will be targeted with 2 home visits to deliver key messages (safe water handling and storage, good personal hygiene practices explain protection refer) in order to influence good WASH practices. Specialized IEC material will be adopted – including responsibility calendars, informative stickers and follow up sheets. Global Communities with its partners will distribute to specific households’ hygiene kits, which include: soap, shampoo, towels, sanitary napkins, bath sponge, etc. These kits will improve the health standards of different age and gender groups; in addition, there will be a positive impact on public health status and the environment. Men will be encouraged to take on roles in safe water handling, safe drainage of wastewater and protecting the hygiene of their community in order to reduce the burden on women and girls.

Indicators

GC MEL team designed a plan that outlines the anticipated results and the tools and mechanisms through which GC and implementing partners will monitor, audit and evaluate progress over the life of the program. This illustrative plan will provide information about the overall approach to performance monitoring, a description of the proposed methods for collecting, analyzing, and reporting high-quality program data. Global Communities’ proposed plan will ensure that gender (focused on female headed-houses) and youth are fully integrated into the MEL system. Further, an illustrative learning and adaptive management plan will be put in place to promote stakeholder collaboration and capitalize on best practices and lessons learned. Monthly written reports will be submitted by the partner to the GC will be involved in developing both the interim and final reports to the donor. In order to assess the appropriateness and effectiveness interventions in line with the overarching goal, GC has designed a holistic MEL approach that employs rigorous quantitative and qualitative methods to inform an array of performance indicators that include: index/composite indicators, indirect/proxy indicators, complexity-aware monitoring, standard foreign assistance indicators, as well as custom indicators to monitor the program’s success in designing and implementing an inclusive approach to achieving sustainable WASH outcomes. The collected data will be analyzed in a timely manner to determine unforeseen risks or/ and any reported issues, which will be mitigated in coordination with the protection officer to ensure full optimization of the project. To ensure accountability towards the beneficiaries; regular field visits and on-going monitoring will be conducted by the protection officer and Global Communities' engineers and will be verified through partner's progress reports. In regards to the supplies, they will be purchased directly by Global Communities based on the list reviewed and shared by WASH Cluster. The installation work will be carried out by contractors that will be supervised and monitored by GC and its partner site engineers on a daily basis to ensure the outcomes are as planned. GC will closely coordinate with the Wash Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure no overlapping and enhance protection mainstreaming through existing specialized support services. It should be noted that the MEL plan will be assessed and review by all affiliates (WASH cluster, PWA, PHG, and JWU). Further, GC will review previous M&E plans and assessments that were developed by WASH Cluster partners (ACF, GVC, PHG) and use them as inputs along with KAP survey and baseline and end line assessments to minimize the gaps and ensure effective and efficient M&E and protection support. For the presented project, the targeted communities (Area C, East-Jerusalem, Areas A&B that are close to settlements, checkpoints or/ and the wall) will be provided with protection support and guidance to protect the beneficiaries from any unforeseen security risks or harm that may befall them. In order to maximize its effects on beneficiaries, the project will adopt IPA and AAP practices throughout all stages of the project cycle, building on the recognized expertise gathered by WBPC: PUI, ACF, and GVC. The project partners will adhere to doing no harm principles by ensuring all staff is interacting with the beneficiaries in an appropriate way and respectful of supported individuals’ privacy and dignity.
