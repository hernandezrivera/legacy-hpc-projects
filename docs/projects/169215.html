Project Summary

The impact the Beirut blast on migrant workers is a significant cause for concern due to the particular vulnerabilities of this group under the Kafala (sponsorship) system. The blast increased protection needs that were identified as a result of the COVID-19 and the economic crisis, and magnified many of the structural issues and gaps in human rights protection and decent work associated with this system, and ILO will continue to propose sustainable solution to dismantle the system. In the meantime, immediate assistance can help prevent a situation where migrant workers are further marginalized.
Many migrant workers, have already lost their employment as a result, and many more are at risk of losing their jobs. Many have also lost their housing and others are at risk of eviction. Migrant workers who wish to return to their countries require legal and financial assistance, especially undocumented and irregular migrant workers. The majority of the migrant workers are women employed as domestic workers, and a smaller number are men who work as porters, farmers, concierge, or employed by cleaning companies and gaz stations.
The ILO proposes to invest its knowledge on decent employment, fair recruitment, and prevention of forced labour and human trafficking, building on its existing strong partnership with workers associations and civil society to provide direct support to migrant workers. Through the humanitarian assistance activities, ILO will document the vulnerabilities of migrant workers to inform future action. The project will offer immediate support to migrant workers through a number of interventions based on the rights based mandate of the ILO.
People targeted:
Migrant workers (90% women domestic workers between 18 and 65 Years old, in addition to 10% men in service sectors between the age of 18 and 55 Years old ).

Plan fields

1 - Needs

Based on consultation with workers associations and migrant workers centres, there are four main types of urgent needs for migrant workers: 1. Direct assistance in the form of cash grants, food and rental subsidies, and one-way tickets to home country. 2. Legal Aid benefiting irregular and undocumented workers and follow up on labour complaints such as unpaid wages of migrant workers resulting from the Beirut Blast. 3. Access to relevant information in their native languages.

2 - Activities

The project activities are designed to meet the most urgent needs while paving the way to decent work for migrant workers. The activities will cover three categories:1- Legal support including regularization of migration status and facilitating voluntary return of migrant workers in coordination with relevant partners without undermining rights of workers. The approach will capture SGBV and mental health vulnerabilities and refer them to the specialized partners. 2- Providing direct assistance to migrant workers to prevent a rapid humanitarian crisis. This will be a combination of ECA and Protection Cash.3-Producing relevant material in the languages of migrant workers on emerging issues related to the Beirut Blast, and documenting lessons learned and personal stories of migrant workers affected by the blast with the objective of informing future humanitarian and policy work.To implement the activities ILO will build on its existing labour migration projects and partnerships, and will design the approach to effectively respond to the need and generate information that will inform future actions and prevent deterioration of humanitarian needs of MWs.
