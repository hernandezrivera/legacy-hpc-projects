Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and the FSL Cluster priorities, NPA will support vulnerable and food-insecure Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities in IPC 4 locations in Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Pochalla & Bor South Counties of Jonglei State, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center, Cueibet, Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial Counties of Lakes State, Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State and Kapoeta North County of Eastern Equatoria State. The targeted communities in the identified counties will be supported to improve their food security and livelihoods through provision of timely and appropriate main season and dry season inputs/kits (Crop, vegetable and fishing), training in improved agronomic and fishing practices. In addition, NPA will support food-insecure with cash transfers in Rumbek East and Rumbek Center Counties in Lakes State with cash transfers to address their food needs. NPA’s emergency assistance and livelihoods support aims at saving lives and averting total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural and fishing inputs/kits, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households (HHs) thus supporting them to recover from crisis and placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute shocks. NPA’s response will address the food security and livelihoods needs and priorities identified in the South Sudan 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO).
NPA seeks to support 105,056 HHs (630,338 individuals) in the targeted Counties with livelihood kits and cash transfers to address food insecurity. Some of the kits will be provided by FAO and some procured by NPA. Training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers and fisher folk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently cascade the knowledge and skills to the farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of cash transfers and agricultural inputs and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of food production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs. COVID-19 preventive and mitigation measures will be mainstreamed throughout all activities to prevent spread and transmission of the virus. The proposed interventions are aligned to the FSL cluster objective 1: CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 and Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19.
NPA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMC’s) to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be targeted in this project.
The target communities will be the prime movers of the implementation process through engagement in the project. Through various means, NPA will gather feedback from the target communities and stakeholders to improve project implementation.

1 - Needs

Conflict and constrained humanitarian access, the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic, economic challenges and elevated food prices have worsened the food security situation in South Sudan. Communities are grappling with severe flooding that has caused widespread displacement, damage to agricultural production, destruction of livelihoods and compounded existing issues in many regions of the country. In 2021, communities in South Sudan were hit hard by the triple shocks of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a third consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Despite the hope instilled by signing of the R-ARCSS, the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence, protracted and repeated displacement, layered on top of pre-existing development challenges, continue to impact South Sudanese throughout the country. This has resulted in sustained poverty, persistent protection concerns, lack of livelihoods and access to basic services, with women and girls being disproportionately affected due to cultural and structural factors. South Sudan often experiences high levels of food insecurity, which peak in severity between May and July during the lean season, when families have depleted most of their harvest and are highly dependent on food assistance, which is almost entirely provided by aid agencies. Entrenched socio-cultural norms and practices put women and girls at greater risk of food insecurity than men and boys. In 2021, South Sudan experienced its worst food crisis since independence as seasonal flooding set in amid an economic downturn and renewed conflict that spiked despite a peace agreement and the formation of a unity government. 7.7 million people needed humanitarian assistance in 2021. In 2022, the situation is expected to deteriorate with the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) projecting that an estimated 8.3 million people will experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July) 2022). This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflicts, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. According to the 2022 HNO, the counties of most concern are in the states of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Warrap, with the most extreme case of being Fangak County in Jonglei State. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. The current economic crises are impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. Various assessments conducted by NPA in the target counties in 2022 reveal high levels of food insecurity in the target counties with households resorting to adopting negative coping mechanisms for survival.The project engages with targeted beneficiaries to learn how they are assessing their needs. The project provides targeted beneficiaries with information about the assistance to be provided. The project disaggregates needs by gender, age or population group. The project has a built-in feedback mechanism that tracks communities’ perception of assistance. The project has a built-in complaint mechanism. The complaint mechanism is address complaints of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (i.e. it is confidential, accessible and responsive).

2 - Project activities

Activity 1 - Project launch and community mobilization: NPA will conduct various meetings with project staff, local authorities, key stakeholders and community representatives to discuss the project objectives, strategies implementation plan, distribution plan and community participation. NPA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and how to provide feedback or complains to NPA. Activity 2 - Formation and training of PMCs/beneficiary selection committees: NPA will facilitate the formation of the Boma-level PMC’s. Each PMC will comprise of 10 members (5 F, 5 M) who will be trained and empowered to be a voice to and of the targeted beneficiaries as well as participate in beneficiary selection and monitoring of the project implementation. Activity 3 - Beneficiary selection: NPA adopts participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries through the PMC’s. Under the guidance of NPA, the PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food- insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. Activity 4 - Formation of AAP committees: NPA will form community representative committees or work with the existing ones to address issues affecting the targeted population, gender disparities, resolving issues related to the project.Activity 5 - Provision of cash transfers: NPA will support 2,000 vulnerable HHs with cash assistance to address their immediate food needs. The cash transfer values will be based on the Multi-Sector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB) as established by the South Sudan Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI).Activity 6 - Distribution of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs: NPA will distribute main season and dry season cropping and livelihood kits in the targeted locations. NPA will distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetable kits and tools (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine) to the 105,056 targeted HHs (630,338 individuals). 55% of the target HHs will be female-headed. NPA will ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed throughout the project activities. Provision of livelihoods kits is aimed at improving production of vegetables, crops and fish hence contributing towards addressing food insecurity and reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and widened dietary diversity options. Activity 7 - Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: NPA will encourage and facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups to encourage team work and peer to peer exchange. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided to 10,505 model farmers and fisher folk. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Activity 8 - Monitoring and reporting: NPA will conduct routine monitoring activities to gather data on the progress of the project and submit the required reports. NPA will also conduct post distribution monitoring after every distribution to ascertain the appropriateness of the response. Outcome monitoring will be done periodically to assess the changes in the target communities as a result of the project.

3 - Project indicators

M&E activities will be guided by the project’s M&E plan and logical framework. A mix of qualitative and quantitative monitoring techniques will be employed in this project to ensure that accurate data is collected and triangulated. NPA’s M&E unit will lead the documentation of the project results, success and processes based on the routine M&E activities that will be conducted during the project period. Given the fluidity of the situation and the wide-ranging activities that will be implemented, monitoring will be conducted on a daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly basis contingent on the specific activity and indicator. The data will then be submitted to the M&E unit for entry, verification, analysis and management. Findings of the monitoring activities will be used to inform the project implementation as well as measure the level of attainment of the indicators. The key data collection activities at this level will be as follows:i. Routine activity data: To be collected on an ongoing basis by NPA project implementation teams. This data will track activity implementation progress as well as beneficiaries reached through the project activities to provide evidence of what has been accomplished at the activity level. ii. Output and outcome monitoring data: The programme outputs seek to measure changes that have occurred/are occurring as a result of the activities that have been implemented. The same applies to the outcomes, albeit at a higher level. Consequently, both qualitative and quantitative data will be collected at the output and outcome levels. NPA has well established output and outcome monitoring tools that will be developed or adapted for purpose of output and outcome monitoring for this project.iii. As part of the output and outcome monitoring, PDM and beneficiary satisfaction surveys will be conducted at the end of each month following cash distribution. Enumerators will be engaged to collect data across the two targeted counties. The PDM will focus mainly on access to cash, spending decision making, satisfaction and impact of the cash transfers. The PDM will also assess cases of sexual exploitation or abuse of power related to cash assistance. Markets, traders and price monitoring will be conducted at the end of each month following cash distribution. This will focus on changes in market accessibility, availability of commodities, traders restocking/supplies capacities, trends in market prices as a result of the cash transfer and if the project is impacting non-targeted HHs. The data gathered during the project period will be used to inform program implementation as well as measuring the level of attainment of the indicators. Periodic meetings involving the relevant stakeholders will be conducted to review the findings of the monitoring activities. The following indicators will be monitored in this project: • Number of HHs (female, child & male headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits • Quantity and variety of seeds distributed • Quantity and variety of crops produced (outcome) • % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish) • Number of HHs receiving cash transfers • Value of cash received by beneficiaries (and proportion in relation to food basket) • Quantity of SAD food assistance distributed ( total cash value)

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries voice any issues of concerns and feedback on the project activities. The most vulnerable members will form part of the PDM sample to ensure that their voice is heard. NPA will ensure disability to be considered across all the implementation and monitoring of the project. People with disabilities, older persons and/or their representative organizations will be consulted during the design and implementation & monitoring of the project. The project will identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities and older persons in humanitarian contexts with considerations for intersectionality of age gender, socio-economic factors and other diversities. The project will identify, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal and Institutional barriers and enablers to participation for people with disabilities and older persons, and will dedicate budget for removal of these identified barriers. The project will have specific targeted interventions for persons with disabilities to meet specific food related needs (i.e. type of food, packaging, outreach to households without means to reach distribution) as identified in the need’s assessments.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, NPA will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of concerns and feedback on the project activities. In line with NPA's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, NPA will inform the target communities about NPA's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, NPA will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all it's communication activities, NPA will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. NPA will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across.

Complaints and feedback mechanisms will have clear channels of communications which will be made available to both female and male beneficiaries. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

While NPA has recently conducted a country-wide operational, security and programmatic analysis, NPA will conduct a further in-depth risk analysis to ascertain the operational, programmatic and security related risks alongside the corresponding mitigation measures before commencement of the project activities. NPA has identified the following protection related risks and analysis.; 8.1: Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services (Likelihood is low and impact is high). To mitigate this risk, NPA will ensure that registration is conducted and assistance provided as close to the beneficiaries as possible. NPA will work with existing community structures and local authorities to ensure that appropriate and locations are identified for project activities so that beneficiaries do not walk for long distances for registration or to receive assistance. PMC's will assist in crowd control during project activities. Activities will be implemented in line with the seasonal calendar to ensure accessibility to project locations. Inputs will be distributed and trainings conducted prior to the rainy season during which some locations are either cut off or inaccessible. 8.5: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities (likelihood is low, impact is high). As a mitigation measure, NPA will adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project, consultations, identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. Under the guidance of NPA, the PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. 8.6: Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms (likelihood is low, impact is medium). To mitigate this risk, NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. NPA will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to NPA. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and where possible use of IEC materials to disseminate information in the target communities about the CRM. 8.8: Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources (likelihood is low, impact is high). NPA's mitigation measure for this risk entails developing and sensitizing the local authorities, PMC's on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by NPA staff to ensure objectivity and impartiality. PDM's will be conducted to ascertain the objectivity of the beneficiary selection process and appropriate corrective measures will be implemented in case of any issues identified.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

NPA recognizes the essential role of women in improving HH food and nutrition security, which in South Sudan has become even more accentuated as the number of women-headed HHs increases as a result of the protracted conflict. For this reason, this project will focus as much as possible on women to ensure families, and above all women and children, reap the benefits. The project will put in place mechanisms to ensure that women, men, boys and girls equally participate and benefit from all interventions. Community mobilization, sensitization and implementation will be done through a gender lens and sensitivity by using the NPA gender check list to reflect that women and men have different needs and priorities are affected differently. To enhance the protection of women and girls during project implementation for instance, cash and livelihoods kits distributions shall consider the safety of women and ensure that they are not inadvertently exposed to additional risk. This will be done by ensuring that distributions are conducted in safe areas and during day time so that they do not have to return home after dark. Women will be encouraged to discuss issues related to the project and associated concerns. Monitoring of the project will ensure that perspectives of women and men are captured systematically. Gender and age-disaggregated data on project coverage and impact will be collected, analyzed and routinely reported on, and project activities monitored for improvements as well as beneficiary satisfaction for both women and men. The project has been designed to ensure that women and other disadvantaged members of the community benefit and participate in an equitable manner. In designing the project NPA, ensured that women’s views were also considered. NPA’s Gender Advisor evaluated the practical and strategic needs of women to ensure that the project takes into consideration women’s needs. The sensitization activities that will be on-going throughout the project will inform the communities on women’s rights and promote discussions on issues affecting women’s rights to participation, own income and ownership. The project will be implemented in ways that ensure that women’s protection needs are prioritized for example trainings involving women will be conducted in venues that are accessible and take into consideration the multiple roles and time-constraints that women face. In addition, safety audits and protection risk assessments will be conducted in the areas surrounding the AGFS as well as the cash and livelihood inputs distribution points. Furthermore, the project will ensure that women are well represented in all the committees and project activities. Gender equality and equity awareness campaigns will be mainstreamed during project implementation of all the planned project activities, for example by including sensitization campaigns on women’s rights and GBV throughout the project implementation
