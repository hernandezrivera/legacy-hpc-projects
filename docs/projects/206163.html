Project Summary

South Sudan Education indicators are one of the worst in the world with literacy levels standing at only 23% according to UNICEF. This is even worse among women and girls. Conflict, natural disasters driven by climate change and other cultural practices have been a contributor to lack of access to quality education to vulnerable populations in South Sudan.
Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Tonj East and Tonj South Counties are some that have been in conflict for quite some time that has severely affected the education infrastructure making the education system weak and unsustainable. This coupled by continuous subnational conflict and natural disasters brought by climate changes have eroded the few education amenities that existed and caused an almost fall to the education system in these counties.
The influx of returnees from Sudan has also exacerbated congestion in facilities that are few and already full begging the need to create new ones. Low capacity of teaching staffs and PTAs that are the backbone of the education system needs strengthening. The coordination of education system is low and education officials capacity need to be strengthen for effective management and coordination.
This project objectives are to provide safe access to quality lifesaving education to the vulnerable communities in the 4 counties of Warrap State targeting 8958 children, teachers and school management committee members including learners with disabilities. The project will create access by construction of 20 TLS and 20 segregated WASH facilities all fitted with hand washing equipment. The project also intends to distribute educational and recreational supplies to both learners and teachers. To support girls attending school and to improve dignity and reduce GBV cases against girl child, the project will support distribution of dignity kits to 1100 adolescent girls. 40 school inspectors and education officials capacity will be improve through training in education in emergencies in adherence to INEE guidelines. 80 teachers would be trained in numeracy and pedagogy through and in-service training. 220 PTA/SMCs members will be trained to support education.
The project will target 2300 IDPs, 3450 Returnees, and 3208 host communities with integrated education services. 980 children and youths would be trained in life skills and peacebuilding and protection and GBV issues will be cross-cutting. Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse that is a major cause of school dropouts amongst girls will be prioritised in the implementation of the project. The project will as well support formal education through distribution of learning materials and also capacity building of teachers on pedagogy.
The project will ensure that there is gender mainstreaming by conducting ensuring that there id gender equity through working closely with both boys and girls, supporting recruitment of female teachers as well as encouraging women participation in school management committees.

1. Needs

Sub-national conflict, food insecurity, the Sudan conflict and economic crisis have contributed to a worsening crisis in the education system in South Sudan. Warrap State has hit by above average rainfall that has caused flooding in most of the counties of the state. An IRNA assessment was carried out in August 2022 by partners, found an unprecedented suffering of the people displaced by flooding in the counties of Tonj East Tonj South, Gogrial East and Gogrial West with a total of 54,264 HH displaced, translating to about 173,584 individuals. Damage of farmland and food crops, collapse of shelters including schools, threat from waterborne diseases including malaria, occupation of schools by the displaced persons/IDPs and lack of safe drinking water are some of the needs that the displaced, returnees and host communities are facing including protection and GBV issues. Coupled with recurring communal conflict in Greater Tonj and COVID-19 pandemic that has caused greater suffering among the IDPs and the host communities population in these counties.An estimated 7,890 children of IDPs, returnees and host community area affected in in Tonj East Tonj South, Gogrial East and Gogrial West. Out of school boys and girls continue to be exposed to dangers of protection issues including early marriage/pregnancies among girls and boys engaging in gang/drugs or join cattle raiding groups. Food insecurity has caused rise to domestic violence among women seen as breadwinners as they could not provide enough food. Based on IPC projections published on November 6, 2023, the counties are ranked at severe levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and Tonj East, Tonj South and Gogrial East and West populations will be experiencing this effect between September 2023 to July 2024. Many women and girls continue to be exposed to sexual exploitation or GBV issues. Displacement has eroded the schools with trained personnel i.e, teachers, SMC/PTA, as many have fled their homes to other areas. The current food insecurity will lead to lack of coping in classes with the reopening of schools. The COVID-19 pandemic continue to rise among the communities as they continue not practicing prevention measures like social distancing and hand washing. This situation is predicted to worsen and there is need for robust awareness and mitigation through fixing of more wash facilities. Many schools have been looted or damaged by flood thus need rehabilitations and provision of educational supplies for both learners and teachers. More dignified and protective wash facilities needed for both boys and girls. Teachers and school authorities will need capacity building to support management of schools in emergency context and delivery safe and protective services. Protection and GBV, including COVID-19 messaging will be mainstreamed throughout the project implementation.

2. Project activities

The key project activities include;1. Establishment of 20 semi-permanent learning spaces with WASH and Hanwashing facilities 20 targeted schools in the 4 counties of Gogrial East and West and Tonj South and Tonj East counties.2. Distribution of educational, teachers and recreational materials in all the 20 targeted schools3. Training of teachers in pedagogy, numeracy and EIE4. Training of education officials in EiE5. Training of PTA/SMCs on EiE6. Monitor enrolment of and retention of learners in all the 20 targeted schools.7. Conduct integrated lifeskill, MRE, protection and GBV for youth and adolescent8. providing incentive to targeted number of teachers in all the 20 schools9. conduct back to leaning campaigns and girl child retention campaigns10. Procurement and distribution of dignity kits to adolescent girls in targeted schools
