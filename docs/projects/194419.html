Project Summary

IsraAID South Sudan is a protection/GBV leading organization, it is committed to promote and respond to communities’ resilience and Primary Basic Needs of Women, Girls, Men and Boys in South Sudan with particular focus on Protection and Gender based violence both prevention and response and Risk Mitigation in order to reduce suffering of the communities through cash voucher in Central Equatorial State (Terekeka and Juba). These locations were heavily affected by the conflict that started in 2013. Children and women, people with disabilities including elderly are the most vulnerable people during and after conflicts.
IsraAID will work in close collaboration with Gender-Based Violence Sub Cluster (GBV SC) and other GBV Focal Points in the above mentioned locations. As part of the community initiatives, IsraAID will undertake regular and rapid interventions in those areas with extraordinarily humanitarian needs in some of the areas with no humanitarian presences such as Terekeka and parts of Juba. IsraAID has qualified and committed staff with GBV and Cash and Voucher experiences to work and coordinate with other protection actors to identify the most vulnerable women and girls affected by the conflict and some natural crisis to provide response services through direct GBV services and material cash assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls. In each of the four (2) locations, IsraAID will identify together with women and community leaders a place where Community Resource Centers will be established or rehabilitated to act as a central place for women and girls, men and boys’ economic empowerment catalysts.
Those Community Centers shall be accessible by the communities to both female and male including GBV survivors and girls at particular risk of sexual violence, exploitation and abuse, denial of resources or forced or early marriage. An integrated part will be establishing new or strengthening existing referral pathway to connect women and girls to multisector GBV prevention and response services.

1 - Needs

Although the armed conflict formally ended with the signing of the 2018 Peace Agreement, women and girls in South Sudan continue to suffer from a myriad of mental and physical problems associated with conflict-related sexual abuse that includes sexual expoitation, child marriage, domestic violence, and harmful traditional practices which continue to hurt and disempower women and girls. Women and girls are deliberately targeted where rape is used as a weapon of war. Domestic violence has significantly increased due to trauma, displacement, and on-going violence. In addition, trust and cohesion among the population have significantly been affected by the on-going violence; service providers and infrastructures have considerably been weakened thus further increasing the vulnerability for the affected populations – especially women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly. Prior to the 2016 conflict crisis, IsraAID received a rapid assessment of Inter Agency Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) in Morobo i.e. Hai Marakazurban, Terkeka (Hai Wehida and Lengkango), Maridi County (Hai Malakia & Gabat) and safety audit in Juba Urban (Jopa and Kaseri). The assessments findings reveled the level of distress/trauma among the displaced and host communities is extremely high (especially among children) – resulting in nightmares, behavior changes, violence, fear, disruptions in daily life, fracture in social cohesion and collapse of traditional / community support mechanisms. GBV is rampant in South Sudan, although exact numbers are difficult to ascertain as cultural norms and stigma inhibit reporting. UNDP estimates that 475,000 women and girls in South Sudan are at risk of GBV . In 2018, 2,300 incidents were reported, likely a small fraction of the total cases. 65% of women report experiencing physical/sexual violence in their lifetime, while 50% of girls will be married before the age of 18. There are limited access to GBV services such as medical, safety and security, legal justice and psychosocial support. Women, girls, men and boys are economically crippled due to the current price fluctuations and low purchasing power which has negatively impacted their well being. Rampant early pregnancies and forced marriages have become a social norm since young girls are seen as source of income to the families. Firewood within weapon free zones have been depleted and women and girls travel far distances through unhealthy routes to collect firewood hence exposing them to dangers in the hands of unknown gunmen.

2 - Project activities

The project will focus on a ray of activities among them:a) Establishment/maintenance/rehabilitation of community centers.b) Carry out GBV awareness raisingc) Identification and formation of community based structuresd) Comprehensive case management for the GBV survivorse) Distribution of cash vouchers to the most vulnerable women and girls f) Psychosocial activities for the adolescent girls groups especially out of school girls and young mothersg) Establishment and updating of referral pathways.h) Mapping and training of different actorsi) Carrying out vulnerability needs assessment to ascertain specific needs requiring interventions

3 - Project indicators

The project will begin by carrying out a baseline survey to ascertain the status of the needs. The project will begin with a grand opening meeting to ensure the deliverables are clearly understood while on a quarterly basis, the project will carry out quarterly review and reflection meeting to gauge the extent of performance vis a vis the planned interventions. Regular community consultation meetings will be held for feedback as well as tools developed that provides feedback in a systematic manner. These accountability tools will review satisfaction from both the team and beneficiaries. There will be joint coordination meetings with government, partners and UN agencies to identify gaps and possible solutions. The issues identified as gaps will be raised with Protection and GBV sub-cluster including UNOCHA.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project design will draw from the “ Guidance for Cash Transfer Programming within South Sudan {2015)” manual. The response analysis will take into consideration the market and risk assessments tools that are included in the manual.A “do no harm” approach will guide intervention, putting emphasis not only on the risks that women may be exposed to as a result of CVA but also the negative impact that the intervention may have on local businesses. IsraAID community-based approach will be leveraged in order to further explore how local markets can benefit from the intervention and to identify enablers for a sustainable action, through a multiplier effect, so the intervention can ultimately benefit both beneficiaries and local business.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CVA is preferred to in-kind donations as they help increase the well being and dignity of beneficiaries by giving them self-determination in the choice of goods they can access. Additionally, CVA enables a more equitable resource sharing and control when beneficiaries determine what they want. This improves social capital and diminishes social exclusion due to the increase in purchasing power that the beneficiaries get. Lastly, there is evidence that women benefiting from cash transfers increase their leadership abilities and equitable communal decision making.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Different methods will be employed to collect feedback from different groups benefitting from the project. Feedback boxes will be deployed at strategic places where confidential reporting can be deposited. A hotline number will be provided to the community and that will be managed by a dedicated staff who will be able to take up urgent action whenever information is relayed. Atsome instances, one on one feedback will be encouraged in a structured manner right from the project staffs to the country director.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will develop tools of accountability and feedback. These tools will be administered on a quarterly basis or regularly as need be depending on the type of activity being measured. Quarterly stakeholders meetings will be conducted to gain general feedback of the delivery, identify gaps and respond to issues raised.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IsraAID has staffs trained on PSEA and will conduct awareness among beneficiaries to ensure they know what PSEA is, how to report and the expected results. The organizational code of conduct and PSEA policies shall be activated for all partners and contractors working with IsraAID. This will ensure the beneficiaries are protected from being exploited due to their vulnerability.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Accountability and feedback mechanism is key in getting feedback from beneficiaries. The confidential information received shall be acted on without jeopardizing the security and safety of those who volunteer to report. The feedback given during review and reflection meeting shall be acted upon and gaps identified sealed. There will be continous learning during the project implementation as well as responses being acted upon.
