Project Summary

Globally, WCUK has over 25 years of experience in conflict affected countries, including 14 years in Iraq, providing integrated child protection and education programming. During this time, WCUK has become a key child’s rights organization advocating for the well-being and protection of children and youth throughout Iraq Since 2004 WCUK in Iraq has implemented multiple integrated protection and education projects funded by UNICEF, UNOCHA, and the Dutch Relief Alliance working with IDPs in Dohuk, Baghdad, Anbar, and Mosul and has established strong relations with government bodies as well as other protection and education actors. In addition to protection and education programming, WCUK also offers its child-specific PSS expertise through our DEALs structured PSS methodology. In 2018, War Child’s DEALs methodology was officially endorsed by the CPSC as one of the 2 structured PSS programs to be rolled out across the country. In conjunction with SCI, WCUK is currently the co-chair of the CPSC’s PSS Task Force, leading and overseeing the national roll-out of these methodologies. WCUK has also been committed to supporting the humanitarian coordination mechanisms within both the CP and Education Clusters. WCUK served as co-chair of the Central/South CPWG until mid-2018 and is currently co-chairing the Ninewa CPWG next to SCI.
WCUK’s planned intervention in Iraq for 2019 aims at meeting the current needs and increasing the resilience among children and young people through an integrated CP and Education response. WCUK’s global protection framework acknowledges that service delivery is not enough to meet the protection needs of persons of concern, and considers that community engagement and empowerment is an indispensable element for any sustainable intervention. In this regard, WCUK has integrated community-based programming as a core foundation of its strategy, alongside working with formal and non-formal actors.
WCUK’s strategy to build and strengthen the resilience of children and youth in Iraq consists of a four-pronged approach:
Structured PSS programming: WCUK supports children and youth with structured psycho-social and life-skills programs through our DEALs methodology and the Adolescent Girls Toolkit, as well as parents and caregivers through positive parenting programs.
CP Case Management : WCUK identifies and supports cases of at-risk children and youth through the provision of specialized protection services as well as referrals to external services when needed. This also includes provision of legal assistance and support to access civil documentation.
Community-Based Child Protection: WCUK’s interventions are also strengthened through the establishment and strengthening of community-based child protection committees and youth groups, enabling local community members to directly participate in programming and advocate for their rights.
Integrated Education Approach: WCUK works with schools and teachers through teacher training in TICC and SEL, integrated PSS activities and school-based case management to anchor child protection in the education system and work towards sustainable, long-term recovery of protective networks through child-friendly schools. In addition, WCUK will also work with NFE programming in camp-setting to support out-of school children.
The project proposes a multi-faceted protection response, to increase the wellbeing of children and their families through (1) structured psychosocial support (PSS) programmes for both children and their caregivers, (2) specialized child protection assistance through case management services, as well as (3) community-based child protection activities focusing on local-level advocacy, (4) integrated child protection response through school-level engagement and support. This set of interventions will contribute to more durable improvements in well-being by addressing protection concerns at individual, household and community levels.

Needs

With the defeat of IS and the cessation of conflict in most regions of Western and Northern Iraq, large numbers of IDPs have returned to their areas of origin (AoO). Despite this steady increase in returns, a significant number of IDPs remain in need of critical humanitarian and protection assistance. Allegations of forced returns, secondary displacements, and conflict in AoO have further exacerbated the protection needs of this population. Conflict, violence, and prolonged displacement can degrade the psychosocial wellbeing of children, which can have long lasting impact. Without proper lower level psychosocial intervention (structured PSS and case management) psychosocial distress can become mental health disorders. Due to a fragile security, social and legal context in newly accessible areas, female IDPs and returnees remain one of the most vulnerable groups (UNFPA GBV Assessment, Iraq 2016; Care, GBV Challenge Workbook). Growing gaps in humanitarian aid have heightened the likelihood of females being engaged in negative livelihood coping strategies (DPWG MoM, Oct. 2018) (UNHCR Protection Update – July 2018). Female-headed households are particularly vulnerable to (S)GBV due to lack of financial and social resources and perpetrator’s awareness of their vulnerability. WCUK will focus on locations in line with cluster priorities and as such on areas of returns and those of continued displacement. According to estimated figures from the Returns Working Group, Ninewa is the main originating Governorate of IDPs in Iraq and is currently also hosting the majority of the returnees (1,550,000 IOM November 2018). Ninawa Governorate continues to have the highest number of displaced individuals in the country, hosting 31% of the total number of IDPs (IOM DTM September 2018). WCUK will work in high priority locations in disputed territories including Sinjar, Talafar, Talqaif and West Mosul, as well as continue our work in al-Qayyarah subdistrict, which hosts Iraq's largest IDP camp. WCUK will also provide services to returnee, remainee and IDP populations in need in western Baghdad and eastern Anbar, where WCUK has worked since the Fallujah response, and where high needs remain. Perceived affiliation of the displaced population from Mosul to IS has significantly increased their vulnerability while living in displacement. The Child Protection sub-cluster (CPSC) recognizes this as a continued and acute need and identifies Iraq as a Protection crisis. The current conditions of displacement have impacted gender and power dynamics within the population. There is a significant number of female-headed households following the deaths or absences of their husbands and/or sons.In this context, WCUK ensures a gender-sensitive approach through its programmes and actively encourages meaningful participation of women and girls in project activities and decision-making processes. Due to a fragile security, social and legal context in newly accessible areas in Ninewa, female IDPs and returnees remain one of the most vulnerable groups (UNFPA GBV Assessment, Iraq 2016; Care, GBV Challenge Workbook). Growing gaps in humanitarian aid in Dohuk Governorate have heightened the likelihood of females being engaged in negative livelihood coping strategies (DPWG MoM, Oct. 2018) (UNHCR Protection Update – July 2018). Female-headed HH (6.4% - 12.5% of the HH in Sumel/Zakho and 10-25% of the HH in Ninewa), are particularly vulnerable to (S)GBV due to lack of financial and social resources and perpetrator’s awareness of their vulnerability. Dohuk Governorate still host the majority of the IDPs in the Kurdish Region of Iraq (350,000 IOM November 2018). WCUK has worked with displaced populations in Dohuk since 2014 and has built strong networks with the local communities. As such, we can observe how the shrinking funding focus on these populations led to a decrease in available services for IDPs in these locations.

Activities

The project activities will contribute the strategic protection objective 1, as identified in the HRP. The project will aim to increase the resilience of children and their families in addressing child protection concerns at individual, household and community level in Iraq. Activities will include: (1) PSS: The continuation of psychosocial support for children and youth through WCUK’s DEALs methodology that is currently being rolled out at national level as one of the two structured PSS methodologies endorsed by the CPSC. The DEALS methodology includes three main programs tailored to the specific needs of each age bracket: Little Fellows (6-9y/o), I DEAL (10-14 y/o) and Youth DEALs (15-24 y/o). WCUK will also implement the Adolescent Girls Toolkit as part of its programs. In addition, WCUK will also complement its PSS intervention through the provision of structured PSS programs for parents and caregivers through IRC’s Positive Parenting program. (2) CM: WCUK will provide case management for at-risk children and youth through the provision of specialized services and protection assistance, as well as referrals to external services when and where needed. This includes support in accessing medical, psychological, and legal services for wide range of cases, including situations of: neglect; emotional, physical and psychological abuse; psychological distress; school drop-outs; children with disabilities in need of assistance; separated and unaccompanied children; and families with children in detention. This will be done through trained case management teams following the Inter-Agency minimum standards on CP and CM, as well as the CPIMS forms endorsed by the Iraq CPSC. These services will also include the provision of legal assistance for children and their families to access civil documentation. (3) CBCP: WCUK will work on community engagement, empowerment and local-level advocacy through the establishment and strengthening of Child Protection Committees (CPC) and Youth Advocacy Groups (YAG). The CPC is composed of parents, caregivers, teachers and local leaders with the aim of identifying, prioritizing and responding to existing child protection concerns through an Advocacy Action Plan. Similarly, the YAG are comprised of youth aged 15-24 y/o, who have identified and prioritized the main protection concerns that specifically affect youth in the area they live and set up an Advocacy Action Plan to address them. This includes advocating and liaising with community leaders and local authorities to find ways to address the identified protection concerns. (4) Integrated Education: WCUK will continue its work in West Mosul and Baghdad to rehabilitate and refurbish schools that were damaged during the conflict in Iraq. This support will be flanked with teacher training on Teachers in Crisis Context and Social and Emotional Learning and Conflict Sensitive Education training, positive forms of discipline and in-depth teaching support to teachers and headmasters. WCUK will support children’s wellbeing at school through integrated PSS and case management activities and support school-based social workers. PaTo provide the best possible start to education, WCUK will launch its Early Childhood Care and Development and complementary Caregiver support training in Iraq.

Protection Risk Analysis

During the implementation of the project the staffs and beneficiaries might face the following risks: 1. Beneficiaries data is at risk to be confiscated by security forces / confidentiality compromised 2) Community perceptions might limit or disapprove of active participation of girls and women in project activities; 3). Case management and legal/civil documentation support for children from families with perceived aﬃliation to IS could lead to an increase in vulnerability due to increased visibility to authorities; 4. Assistance and/or support for families with perceived aﬃliation to IS might put WCUK employees at risk with other stakeholders 5. Beneficiaries are at risk of abuse and exploitation by project staff 6) Beneficiaries might come to harm through insufficient safety measures on sight

Mitigation Measures

(1) WCUK has strong data protection protocols and safeguarding policy in place to ensure beneficiary data is kept safe. This includes locked security cabinets for beneficiary lists and coded case management files held on location. Physical documents with identifiable information are solely kept in Country head office. Case files are kept on password protected case management information systems in soft copy. (2) WCUK will conduct community sensitive outreach and community mobilization throughout the project and engage on a regular basis with (informal) community leaders as well as with girls, women, men and boys to achieve greater acceptance of the project and its specific assistance to women and girls. Where necessary, negotiation with families will take place to allow participation of girls and women in the project activities.(3) All case plans are assessed in strict adherence to the best interest of the child and weighted against risk assessments together with the child’s family to ensure conflict sensitive approaches and Do no Harm principles are followed. (4) WCUK will continue to build strong and positive relationships with local authorities, camp management and community leaders to ensure activities are clear and understood and occur with the community's approval. Each project will commence with a startup workshop and include feedback mechanisms and be based on continuous two-way communication to ensure towards community acceptance of activities.(5) Project staff will be trained on PSEA, Confidentiality, Informed Consent and other safeguarding principles to ensure Code of Conduct and Safeguarding Policy are followed. As for the beneficiary’s concerns, WCUK will continue to have complaint's mechanisms (feedback boxes and hotlines) as well as child friendly feedback mechanisms and will conduct regular beneficiary satisfaction surveys in order to monitor and ensure quality of services. Codes of conduct are signed by each Partner staff including PSEA references. At the start of each project beneficiaries will be informed of expectations of staff behavior and of reporting pathways. WCUK has three safeguarding focal points in country and additional support from Global Safeguarding Adviser.(6) WCUK conducts monthly site assessments to ensure that centers are safe for use for children and community members. All electrical wiring and cables are put out of reach of children and generators are fenced in. The Global Safeguarding and Safety Adviser oversees regular compliance with WCUK’s safety policies.

How is your project accountable to affected population

WCUK seeks to ensure that participation, feedback and complaint mechanisms are integrated into all projects and has included these in the current OCHA-funded project in QAC. WCUK will gather both solicited and unsolicited feedback from participants in the proposed project, using participatory and inclusive methodologies. Feedback will be captured through Focus Group Discussions, involvement of participants in evaluations and through the establishment of secure, accessible and inclusive mechanisms, such as feedback boxes and a dedicated phone hotline. War Child is currently developing a comprehensive and robust Accountability to Affected Populations Framework, framed by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations, informed by our Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) Self-Improvement Plan, and influenced by our organisational value to be “Accountable to Children”. Similarly, we are concurrently developing a policy specifically focused on Child Friendly Feedback & Complaints which will specify the standards to which we should adhere (IASC, CHS, Internal Standards), and will provide a clear and comprehensive organisational approach to feedback. Feedback from participants also functions as a significant principle of WCUK Safeguarding Policy. All staff, volunteers and associates of War Child are obliged to report all safeguarding concerns to trained in-country focal points who then refer to the dedicated Child Safeguarding Advisor. Participants are also encouraged to report concerns on staff conduct and behaviour to War Child staff. War Child employ a zero-tolerance approach to any contravention of the Safeguarding Policy and responds to all safeguarding concerns in a timely, appropriate manner.
