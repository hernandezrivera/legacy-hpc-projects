Project Summary

The project targets to reach 27,000 most vulnerable children and at-risk children in Koch and Aweil Center in former Unity and former Northern Bahr el Ghazal States. The project aims to provide critical child protection services for children at high risk and to prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. IRC proposes interventions that improve the social-emotional well-being of children using a combination of center-based, school-based and community-based approaches utilizing UNICEF’s PSS manuals and IRC’s Social and Emotional Learning manuals. This will be coupled with case management services for vulnerable children including family tracing and reunification (FTR) services for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). The project shall also build the capacity of IRC child protection workers, protection workers, government officers, and community-based child protection structures in CPiE in order to promote longer-term and sustainable change.

Needs

Prolonged conflict, displacement and sustained economic decline in South Sudan has diminished the capacity of people to ensure the safety of their children. The crisis continues to be first and foremost a protection crisis, with more than 6.1 million people in need of some form of protection from violence, abuse, exploitation and violations of fundamental human rights . Among those in need are 15,600 people located in Aweil Center and 124, 200 people in Koch. According to the Child Protection Sub-Cluster priority locations, Koch and Aweil Center are first and third tier priorities, respectively. Moreover, the September 2018 IPC analysis indicated that the population in Koch are in Phase 3 (Crisis) food insecurity while Aweil Center is in Phase 2 (Stressed) food insecurity. Furthermore, large sectors of the population have been displaced from their homes to the bush, swampy areas and islands where they have no access to adequate protection, shelter, sanitation, clean water or food, rendering them vulnerable to disease, risks of sexual violence, neglect, and distress. The armed conflict, cattle raiding and communal violence (revenge killings) in and around Koch have hampered coordinated delivery of protection and health services. This has resulted in a major population movement into Koch town, Bieh, Buaw, Bentiu and Rubkona towns. As a result, the children in Koch area are vulnerable to violence, abuse, and labour exploitation, and have been suffering with psychological trauma and distress, Child protection concerns in Aweil Center are mostly driven by the limited livelihood options, food consumption gaps, and lack of well-functioning child protection structure including constrained case management systems, weak referral mechanisms, and limited capacity of the community-based child protection mechanisms. Parents of school-aged children have been forced to move to distant locations to look for work, which has also resulted in an increased number of neglected children. Child-headed households, where older adolescents are required to take care of smaller children exist. Likewise, some children have lost parents as a result of conflict and have been left to fend for themselves. Consequently, child labour exploitation, neglect, sexual violence, child marriage, and street children in Aweil Center has been increasing at an alarming rate. Exposure to violence, malnutrition and mass displacement can have long-lasting effects on children’s psychosocial well-being and development. There is an unmet need regarding the social and emotional wellbeing of children. There is a lack of parenting skill and child development among caregivers to support their children. Community members have also limited resources and knowledge to provide adequate responses to child abuse. In these areas, unaccompanied and separated children and children living with disabilities are often vulnerable to abuse, sexual exploitation, and violence as they are at risk of over-dependence on exploitative and/or abusive relationships in order to provide for their needs. During displacement children have no information on how to access services or obtain support and they are already traumatized from the violence/conflict witnessed and the separation from family and local community support networks. The current volatile security situation, the high rate of child protection concerns, and the limited capacity of the child protection structures in Koch and Aweil Center continues to be a critical priority for this anticipated HRP. Therefore, IRC will re-establish its office in Koch (Koch town, Buaw and Mirmir), and Aweil Center (Nyalath and Aroyo Payams), and continue operating from existing office in Aweil South (Panthou, Watmouk, Nyith, Taraliet, and Nyocawany Payams) through static and mobile child protection interventions.

Activities

To provide crucial services to the most vulnerable and at risk children in Koch and Aweil Center, IRC proposes to improve the social-emotional well-being of children using a combination of center-based, school-based and community-based approaches utilizing UNICEF’s psychosocial support (PSS) manuals and IRC’s Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) manuals. This will be coupled with case management services for vulnerable children including FTR services for UASC. Where needed, UASC will benefit from alternative care placements and individualized support. The project shall also build the capacity of community-based child protection structures in the CPiE approach that promotes the participation of different community structures (Child Protection Committee (CPC), community gatekeepers, PSS club in schools, religious leaders, and teachers) in order to promote longer-term, and sustainable change. Regular consultative review meetings between IRC staff, parents, CPC, and community leaders will ensure the project responds to the continuously changing needs of the children; analyzing new challenges and designing community centered solutions. Planned activities include:• Identification and documentation of children with protection concerns• Support to girls and boys in vulnerable situations through case management and referrals to specialized services• Support of NFI and food items for vulnerable children• Training of staff on Child Protection case management • Family tracing and reunification of UASC• Identification and placement of UASC in alternative care• Training and mentoring for child protection volunteers and staff on child protection approaches • Capacity building of child protection workers and staff on CPiE• Capacity building of other relevant staff on CPiE• Capacity building of government officers on CPiE• Provision of parenting skills sessions to caregivers and community members • Provision of structured community-based psychosocial support activities • Establish Child Friendly Space/SHLS• Provision of support to community-based child protection committees to engage in child protection activities• Provision of life skills and resilience programming to adolescent girls and boys• Provision of support to adolescents to organize peer support activities with children • Mainstreaming of child protection and psychosocial support in schools through capacity building events • Awareness raising on prevention and response to child protection issues through campaigns, billboards/leaflets/pamphlets, and group awareness raising sessions• Capacity building of community-based child protection structures on CPiE• Establishment of peer to peer support groups/clubs for adolescents

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IRC will apply a center based and community-based child protection approach that promotes the participation of different community structures including CP Committee, community gatekeepers, PSS clubs in different schools, religious leaders, and teachers in the implementation of the project. Therefore, during the start-up of the project, there will be a project kick-off meeting with relevant community and government representatives, and intensive community mobilization to introduce the project to the beneficiaries with continuous updates for the community using different IEC/BCC materials and campaigns. Significant number of children will be reached through in school and out of school edutainment programs including non-focused PSS interventions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will have different information dissemination approaches for the beneficiaries considering the context of the two locations of the project, language, the age of the target groups, and literacy level. The approach includes community meetings, in school and out of school clubs, various events, edutainment activities, printed materials including posters and leaflets.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Yes, one of the primary objectives of the project is to respond to abuse, violation, sexual exploitation, and neglect of the most vulnerable children through a structured community-based child protection approach.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The project consultations will include both the representative and the broad community members depending on the situation and context of the target locations. The representatives will have continuous involvement in the capacity building interventions, meetings and at different stages of the project cycle management so that they can cascade and disseminate relevant information to the wider community. The IRC will conduct consultative meetings on the implementation of the project with the community representatives, children and youth groups, and community-based child protection structures. This provides an enabling environment for the affected community to participate in the implementation of the programs that target them. The project will have also sessions with the broader community to disseminate the project information using different message transmission approaches including face to face meetings.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IRC has organizational level feedback and/or complaint mechanisms for any potential violation of the IRC’s Code of Conduct. The complaint and feedback mechanism will be set up during the project initiation stage with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. They will receive a pamphlet containing these standards and for those unable to read or in the case of children, will receive visual illustrations of IRC accountability standards. The pamphlets will contain telephone numbers through which they can call and register their complaints. The feedback and reporting procedure will be displayed in a visible place in the project location. The complaint and feedback boxes will be placed at CFS/SHLS, schools and distribution sites.Moreover, specific to this project there will be quarterly community dialogue forums where the target community can express any concerns regarding the entire project. Besides, there will be monthly meetings with the Child Protection Committee to receive the feedback from the wider beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRC has conducted a protection risk analysis on the elements of child protection interventions included in this HRP such as PSS, Case Management, CBCPM and Cash-based intervention based on the safety and dignity, access, confidentiality, and people with specific needs. Accordingly, the analysis indicated that if the interventions for child protection are not aligned with the guiding humanitarian principles and standards including the code of conduct, the safety and dignity of clients might not be protected. To tackle the anticipated risks the following community based and humanitarian agency mitigation measures are identified. (1) strengthening of the Community Based Child Protection Mechanism; (2) meaningful participation of the clients in the project cycle management process; (3) establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries; (4) two-way feedback mechanisms between communities and humanitarian agencies; and (45) whistle-blowing mechanisms and swift agency response to reports of fraud or corruption. In addition, the IRC will conduct the safety audit for this project to assess the potential safety issues.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The beneficiary and government representatives and community based structures will identify locations for accessible service during the project opening meeting. In addition, distance is identified as one of the barriers for service access and the mobile child protection intervention is designed to tackle this problem especially in Koch. IRC has been engaged in critical response, prevention, and risk mitigation activities through static interventions in Nyal and Kol and mobile responses in Garbek, Yup, Machar, and Kuenor. This approach enables the most vulnerable and hard to reach children to access the services being rendered by IRC and the same will continue in the upcoming projects.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

The project activities are designed to tackle inequalities and protect children from abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation by providing them with a protective and safe environment, and appropriate psycho-social support. How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will have a system that can enable IRC to track the access of beneficiaries. Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

To achieve the physical, economic, social and psychological security of the beneficiaries, this project will focus on community-based protection. The project will provide support and assistance to the most vulnerable, enabling them to increase their capacity to make informed choices about their futures and safety; organizing their resources and efforts to reduce exposure to harm; develop local strategies to increase safety; and connecting with international and national actors to build capacity, accountability and a protective environment. Moreover, the project will conducts a safety assessment for survivor children in the case management process and plans safety measures based on the findings of each survivor child. The project will also take precautions while providing services such as appropriate timing and place for the service delivery, the privacy of clients, and confidentiality of client’s cases.
