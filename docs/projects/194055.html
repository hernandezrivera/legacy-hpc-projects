Project Summary

DRC's refugee response in 2023 will include Protection, Child Protection and GBV support for Ethiopian refugees in Eastern Sudan and in Khartoum. Specific activities will include establishing and supporting Community Based Protection Networks, awareness raising, training and capacity building, as well as delivering child-friendly safe spaces.
**This project has been updated to reflect changes in DRC's planning for 2023 based on the conflict which started in Sudan on 15 April 2023. DRC is now expanding its operations to respond to displaced groups, including refugees, in White Nile and Jazirah states, and increased refugee populations in Gedaref.**

1 - Needs

Protection: DRC has prioritized the collection of protection monitoring data across all intervention areas despite the considerable sensitivities and risks these activities present, as they mitigate critical gaps in access to information on protection, and guide DRC’s protection program response providing an evidence base. Both quantitative and qualitative methodologies are being used to gather data on risks, incidents, specific needs, and coping strategies, amongst other. Data indicate that context-specific analyses are required to inform response activities but consistently find that there are widespread protection risks facing crisis affected populations in Sudan. Safety risks, needs gaps, illegal substances, and problematic assistance provision have been clearly documented to be impacting the protection outcomes of refugees in Gedaref. Early marriage, rape and sexual assault, and sexual exploitation and abuse are reported to be issues facing women and girls across target areas. Data continues to build on the need for significantly increased investments in protection responses, and for increased emphasis on protection mainstreaming to improve outcomes for the most vulnerable. Some of DRC's recent assessments, which have informed the project activities, are: DRC East 2021 - Babikiri site Assessment DRC East 2021 - Hamdayet Assessment DRC East 2021 – Protection Monitoring in East Sudan (June-Aug) DRC East 2021 – Protection Monitoring in East Sudan (Sept-Nov) DRC East 2021 - PM Snapshot November – East Sudan **DRC has updated this plan to reflect a sharp increase in emergency response activities following conflict that began in Khartoum and other areas of Sudan on 15 April 2023. This plan reflects needs described in the revised HRP which was released on 17 May 2023.**

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under general protection, DRC will establish or re-establish and support Community Based Protection Networks, and work closely with these groups to deliver other activities. These will include awareness raising on protection topics, provision of individual protection services (cash and in-kind), training service providers on protection principles, and community engagement on protection issues. For child protection, DRC will deliver child-friendly safe spaces for children in the target areas (Almafaza, Alfashaga, Al Galabat Al Gharbyah, and Basundah). DRC will provide capacity building activities, including mentoring and coaching for government and NGO staff as well as community-based protection structures, such as the DRC-supported CBPNs. Children with disabilities will be reached with individual services to ensure that their care and wellbeing is improved. DRC will deliver campaigns to raise awareness about the harms of GBV, and will provide capacity building on GBV topics to key stakeholders. DRC will provide individual case management support for GBV survivors, and will ensure that SGBV survivors are assisted with appropriate support, including effective referrals and material support (dignity kits, etc.). **This plan reflects an increase in certain activities based on the change in context and needs since 15 April 2023. DRC plans to deliver more emergency response activities than previously planned, including supporting in shelter and NFI, multi-purpose cash assistance, and additional protection in emergencies activities. This will include in Humanitarian Mine Action Risk Education.**

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to affected populations is a core component of DRC’s humanitarian response. The participation of the affected population in project activities has been ensured through needs assessments, household surveys, beneficiary registration events, distribution events, focus group discussions and key informant interview processes. In the different community engagement sessions, men, women, boys, and girls participate in the prioritization of needs and identification of the most vulnerable persons for support. As part of do-no-harm, DRC staff continuously receive training sessions on PSEA in humanitarian response. The aim of the training is to ensure that humanitarian support is delivered in a safe, accountable, confidential, appropriate, and inclusive environment. To further reinforce inclusive beneficiary participation in project activities, DRC’s comprehensive complaints, and feedback mechanism (FCRM) provides pathways through which community participants can channel their complaints and suggestions to program management. Such platforms include; a toll-free hotline, WhatsApp hotline, SMS helpline, static information desks, mobile protection desks, post distributions monitoring (PDM) surveys and assessments, focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and ad hoc walk-in meetings. All feedback received from the affected populations is treated with confidentiality, logged into the DRC feedback register, and categorized according to the level of sensitivity. Feedback on very sensitive matters is immediately referred to the protection sector and/or senior management for investigation and action within 72 hours, whilst non-sensitive feedback is referred to the programmatic sectors for investigation and response provided within two weeks’ utmost. In either case, the complainant is traced, and an appropriate response or feedback is provided on the subject matter raised. Where a complainant prefers anonymity, the feedback is provided to the general community where the issue was raised. A summarised FCRM report provides highlights of total feedback received by location and sector, and includes suggested recommendations for management attention.The established FCRM channels allow DRC the project management team to maintain dialogue with affected populations and address/modify project activities in a timely manner, facilitating accountability to vulnerable populations and ensuring that their needs, interests, concerns, and rights are at the center of management decision-making.
