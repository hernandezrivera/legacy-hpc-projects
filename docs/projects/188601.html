Project Summary

Breeding milk cows is an important livelihood for a considerable number of the agropastoralist area of Idleb, which is the target area for the proposed project. An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Protracted conflict, inflation, high food and commodity prices; and poor purchasing power among the population including livestock keepers signifies the dire situation and vulnerability of people. Majority of vulnerable host communities and IDPs, among them men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and the disabled that are highly dependent on animal source foods (meat and milk) for their nutrition are at risk of hunger and malnutrition, while livestock assets are at risk of diseases.
Because of the needs and rationale above, Nasaem is requesting funding to conduct the following:
• Provision of technical guidance for breeding and feeding milk cows.
• Provision of fodder and other dietary supplements to male and female cow breeders to protect their assets.
• Provision of trainings on establishing community-based cooperatives for male and female cow breeders.
Nasaem's team will discuss and agree on selection criteria with communities' actors and communities. Following that, Nasaem will identify and register eligible vulnerable households who have one or two cows to be supported. Each household out of 1700 eligible households will receive 21 fodder bags, each bag contains 50 kg of concentrated pellet fodder, in addition to that the cow breeders (men and women) will receive 15 kg of minerals supplements and vitamins (premix) with 45 kg of mineral salt block. The total amounts that will be provided from these nutrients adequate for one cow for about 100 days. The fodder and other dietary nutrients will be distributed to cow breeders in two batches; the first batch will be distributed in February 2022, and the next one in April 2022. The quantity provided of fodder will cover at least 75% of the fodder needs for one cow during end of summer, and winter season (until Dec 2022). This quantity has been determined to first ensure coverage maximum number of vulnerable households and at the same time avoiding creating dependency.
The recent global pandemic of coronavirus 2019 (Covid-19) has put additional stress on humanitarian response in general; the project will ensure the implementation of Covid-19 preventive measures (distribute soap, sanitizer and masks, as well as maintain a safe distance during project activities).
The project will support three critical factors—resources, capacity, and motivation—are all essential to the sustainability of project activities, outcomes.
The basis for the sustainability of these interventions was that profits would provide the resources for the inputs needed to continue applying the practices farmers had learned, breeders’ capacities would be maintained through continued application of the learned practices, and beredes would be motivated by increased production and associated income. Plans will be developed to ensure linking project activities to external entities and long term livestock projects.

4 - Needs

The estimated FSL sector PiN for 2021 – of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity – has increased from 9.8 million to 14.2 million. The evidence highlights an alarming deterioration, caused by a combination of factors including currency depreciation and associated loss of purchasing power, considerable hikes in food prices, large population displacements, lack of livelihood and income generating options, losses in agricultural production and assets, all compounded by ongoing conflicts and the COVID-19 pandemic. Livestock breeders remain vulnerable since their animals are at risk of disease outbreaks; with limited pasture availability and high feed costs, many livestock farmers are forced to sell-off their animals, thus adversely affecting overall livestock production levels and compromising the long-term livelihoods of livestock keepers.Livestock production is vital in the northwest in general and northwest in particular, to sustain food availability. Livestock breeders – the vast majority of whom maintain a small number of animals - are constantly seeking ways to increase and protect their assets, and generate a good income. Unfortunately, in this time of crisis, breeders have lacked access to fodder and veterinary services, which has impeded their ability to sustain a good livelihood. Based on the rapid need assessment conducted by Nasaem team, the cattle breeder in the targeted areas suffer from what called winter feed gap.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Risk: A breach of ethics. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, and ask them to sign them. - Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the areas based on the updated service map. The project will priorities the most vulnerable persons (males and females including IDPs), considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. The project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups and not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Awareness sessions will be conducted about strengthening self-protection mechanisms, GBV services, increasing knowledge about woman and her main roles and rights. In addition, males will attend sessions about GBV risks to increase knowledge and awareness and to reduce following bad or negative coping strategies. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, NKO will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All staff and volunteers working in this project will be trained on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) aspects to understand, sign and adhere to a code of conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standardsand the rights of beneficiaries. Ensure measures in case of violation and fight impunity. NKO will ensure that mechanisms are in place for Protection from PSEA and that they are, regularly updated and are functioning effectively. NKO willcontinue to strengthen coordination with the PSEA network in addressing sexual exploitation and abuse. Respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can or need to be put in place following existing principles andprocedures recommended by the PSEA network. In partnership with PSEA network, identify dedicated focal points and raise awareness about PSEA. Provide clear information that beneficiaries do not have to provide services or favors in exchange for receiving services or accessing facilities. Ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally and without any discrimination. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against girls, boys, women or men. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.
