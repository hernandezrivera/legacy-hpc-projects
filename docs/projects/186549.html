Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen protection and emergency intervention and promote durable solutions through a coordinated multi-sectoral response by reinforcing CCCM activities. The project is aligned to the sector objectives which seek to improve living conditions of IDPs and other affected populations through site facilitation to ensure improved coordination and minimum standards are upheld. It will also focus on strengthening accountability to the affected populations through the implementation of complaints and feedback mechanism, site monitoring and reporting of protection needs and assistance, inclusion of women, girls, boys and men of different ages and from diverse backgrounds in CCCM activities through participatory approach. The project will ensure IDPs, returnees and host community are included in the intervention through the in camp and Out-of-Camp Approach. It will support site facilitation and maintenance activities for dignified lives of persons of concern, site planning, reception center management, capacity building for partners and authorities; and strengthen community governance systems, and the mainstreaming of protection, GBV, COVID-19 and other cross cutting issues in to CCCM.
The overall response will focus on enhancing multi-sectoral coordination of humanitarian response, monitoring of protection and assistance, promotion of participatory approach, community organizing, promoting, and strengthening of accountability to the affected population, ensuring infrastructure improvement and to mainstream protection and other cross-cutting issues like COVID 19 into CCCM for improved living conditions, and durable solutions of the affected populations.
Under the Camp Coordination and Camp Management component of the project, CARE AID will provide site management/facilitation support the displacement sites in MAFA, provide capacity building to community leaders, trainings for partners on CCCM, trainings for community leaders on CM related issues. This support coordination mechanisms at site level for the provision of services, as well as the enhancement of feedback and complaints mechanisms at the sites. Below are some of the proposed activities that CARE AID will implement;
• Site facilitation: -Support the authorities to effectively coordinate multi-sectoral response in project locations and report gaps to respective partners and sector leads through the Shelter/NFI/CCCM sector.
• Site Improvement: - preparation and upgrade of identified areas, including communal infrastructure within the site/area.
• Strengthening of community governance Structures: strengthening the existing site management committees in each of the sites and the identified areas through capacity building and establishing such structures where none exists.
• Capacity building: - staff, partners, and authority’s capacity building for improved accountable and effective delivery of CCCM activities.
• Community mobilization & engagement: - reinforcement of community mobilization and information dissemination through the existing Complaints and Feedback through the use of loudspeakers and IEC materials in respect to COVID-19 protocols.
• COVID-19 will be integrated into CCCM response by implementing Risk Education, Community Engagement, and support with non-medical material
• Regular intra-camp coordination meetings with different stakeholders inside the camp.
• Regular site monitoring and reporting.
• Regular mapping of camp services and actors
• Set-up and maintenance of leadership structures, sectoral groups, and community representative groups.
• Ensure that women, youth, Persons with Special Needs, elderly are represented in governance structures, community groups and camp activities.
• Ensure consultation with communities especially women, youth, Persons with Disabilities and the elderly before implementing new approaches.

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in north-east Nigeria has affected millions in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. According to the Sector specific needs analysis outlined in the NHF Allocation Strategy, the CCCM/Shelter-NFI Sector has identified the need to ensure that critical life-saving assistance continues to be provided where overwhelming humanitarian needs persist to ensure appropriate responses. According to round 37 (August 2021) of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), there were 2,191,193 IDPs (445,852 households) and 1,753,484 returnees (284,389 households) across Adamawa, Bauchi, Borno, Gombe, Taraba, and Yobe States. Borno State accounts for the largest IDP caseload with 1,633,829 compared to Adamawa (209,322). However, returnee figures are considerably higher in Adamawa (829,594), compared to Borno (740,595). Assessments of the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe State internally displaced persons (IDP) camps conducted by the CCCM, Shelter, and NFI Sectors indicate that one in four of the camps, which host 430,000 IDPs, is highly congested with per capita space of less than 15m2.According to the need assessment conducted by Care Aid in October 2021 in Mafa and Konduga Local Government Authorities, new displacements account for 70 per cent of new arrivals in camps and camp-like settings. However, the report further cites a 25 per cent caseload of IDPs coming as a result of the recent closure of camps and camp-like settlements in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) and Jere. According to the Borno State Return Strategy September of 2018, IDPs are likely to return in Mafa,Bama, Gwoza, Dikwa, Mobbar, Ngala, Maiduguri and Konduga. However, the report further notes that the returnees are likely to settle within the Local Government Areas (LGA) headquarters that are accessible to humanitarian organizations. In sum, IDP populations are steadily increasing in camps that are located in LGA headquarters in such locations as Mafa, Bama, Gwoza, Dikwa, Mobbar, Ngala, Maiduguri and Konduga. Notably, the camps were already congested prior to the recent influx.Although all IDPs are among the most affected population groups, there are groups with more significant vulnerabilities among the IDPs such as the elderly, women, and girls with specific needs, children, and persons with disabilities, and those with underlying illnesses. According to the need assessment conducted by Care Aid in October 2021 in Mafa and Konduga Local Government Authorities, 58 percent of beneficiaries were identified as vulnerable persons, with elderly people over 60 years making up 27 per cent of all vulnerable persons followed by lactating mothers (25%) and children under 5 years (19%). It is also important to highlight that IDPs living in host communities and host communities without CCCM site facilitation or out of camp are equally under the category of the most affected. According to round 37 of the DTM, 41 per cent of the IDPs live in camps/camp-like settings, while 59 per cent of the IDPs are displaced among host communities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GBV risks will be mitigated in camps/camp-like settings through the appropriate adaptation of plot allocation and decongestion guidelines, as well as due considerations given to site planning in north-east Nigeria context. Care Aid’s commitment to preventing and mitigating Gender based Violence in emergencies including in camps and camp-like settings is captured in its Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises (GBViC Framework). Care Aid’s CCCM response includes the outlined Mitigation measures in this Framework and works with various Protection and GBV actors in planning and implementation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CARE AID will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. This action is centered on 4 pillars of participation and inclusion, communication and transparency, feedback mechanisms, and organizational learning. To ensure participation/engagements, CARE AID will employ participatory methodologies at each stage of the operations management cycle to incorporate capacities and priorities of people with disability into protection and assistance activities of this action by engaging with organizations of people with disabilities. CARE AID will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision-making process. CARE AID has already established complaints and feedback response mechanisms (CRM ) in Mafa which has been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.
