Project Summary

Throughout 2022, DCA Bangladesh will be implementing DANIDA HuM and ACT Alliance funded projects, through an integrated approach. The project has identified three priorities in 2022 for our programming in Cox’s Bazar, as follows: 1. Reduce violence (household level/ intra community level/ inter community level). 2.Socio-economic empowerment of women and youth and 3. Community led programming. The programme design of DCA for this instance holds these priorities as the central component, which are: technical expertise in livelihood, and gender sensitive programming. The project, however, provides life-saving and integrated multi-sector assistance through livelihoods support, aiming to increase the access to food through increased vegetable production, and access to income. The project reinforces DCA’s engagement on gender, disability inclusion and GBV in promoting women’s participation in work-related activities and ensures that gender and protection is mainstreamed throughout. This project ensures ethical standards, and to protect staff, and every woman, girl, boy and man engaged in abuse by individuals or groups operates from the Do No Harm Principles, has an anti-corruption policy, a “zero tolerance” policy against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and a code of conduct for all staff and contractors. DCA promotes accountability in humanitarian programmes, and communities are actively involved. DCA Bangladesh is a strong advocate for better services for GBV and overall protection in Cox’s Bazar. This has been done through local coordination mechanisms, PSEA taskforce membership, Localization Working Group membership, and service auditing to set the humanitarian agenda.
The integration of lifesaving, early recovery, as well as resilience building activities in both host & refugee communities is fully in line with DCA’s focus on triple nexus (development- humanitarian- peacebuilding) approach; linking humanitarian assistance with development support and peacebuilding to sustain the outcomes and ensure relevant and significant impacts. Community resilience initiatives will be integrated into livelihood programming, DRR activities through cash for work (CfW), with programming mainstreaming activities that promote peace between the Rohingya and Host communities. Besides, Host Community vulnerabilities will be addressed through increasing household level income generating opportunities. Overall, this holistic approach that meets the distinct needs of both communities aims to increase the sense of tolerance between the two targeted communities, while enhancing their resilience through boosting local economic activities.
Throughout the project period, DCA will provide transferable skills and development (material) packages support to Rohingya women and youth for ensuring their self-reliance. Also, community infrastructures will be repaired and restored through CfW. Tree plantation will also take place for mitigating environmental losses both in Camp and HC. Household level climate smart homestead gardening and climate sensitive self-reliance interventions will also be implemented for Rohingya and Host. Furthermore, for addressing the needs of HC HHs skills development training and Investment Grants support, basic requirement through multipurpose cash grant (MPCG) to COVID-19 / Disaster affected households on stipulated time, market linkage of a number of farmer producer groups will take place under different activity interventions. Rights-holder and community-centered methodologies as well as extensive consultation with a broad range of stakeholders continuously informs project design to ensure that projects are meaningful in the targeted locations and aims to increase localization and local ownership of the project activities, alongside increasing the likelihood of it being sustained even after the project period.

1 - Identified Needs

In August 2017 a surge of violence in Myanmar’s Rakhine State saw 700,000 Rohingya flee across the border to neighboring Bangladesh. They settled in Cox’s Bazar District, joining around 200,000 Rohingya already seeking shelter in what is one of Bangladesh’s poorest and most disaster-prone districts. The total number of people in need is estimated at 1.3 million people, and four years on, protection risks are paramount in this protracted crisis. Below is a basic summary of the specific problems related to the sectors of livelihoods, gender sensitive programming and social cohesion around which DCA Bangladesh is planning to frame our programme.Livelihoods: The Bangladeshi population living in and Cox’s Bazar have fragile livelihoods. For the host communities, access to income, including casual labour, is relatively common. Unfortunately, the vast majority of families earn a very low weekly or monthly income due to erratic casual labour opportunities. Most households have members who work only a couple of days per month. Only a small fraction of Bangladeshi – and even less so for the Rohingya - rely on stable wages. Poor diet, lack of education and livelihood opportunities, precarious health and WASH conditions continue to remain a challenge to Rohingya refugees’ livelihoods and raise serious protection concerns. According to WFP Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability assessment, the coping patterns of Rohingya population have changed. A significant number of refugees have been forced to employ diverse coping strategies to meet their needs. The adoption of these negative coping strategies is higher than the last year. GBV and Gender Sensitive Programming: 52% of the refugee population in Cox’s Bazar are women and girls. Key areas of concern are increased incidents of intimate partner violence (IPV); 74% of reported GBV cases are IPV, with 98% of those reported affecting women, underreporting of GBV due to stigma, and restriction of female movement. Alongside this high risk of GBV, the majority of Rohingya women do not have a reliable income source, and report that they are unable to meet their basic needs because of a lack of job opportunities (60%), and limitations in access to markets (68%).Social cohesion: Nearly half (47%) of Bangladeshi communities hosting 900,000 Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar District of Bangladesh report being ‘unhappy’ or ‘very unhappy’ with the presence of Rohingya in their area. Common reasons are competition for services and utilities, competition for resources, threat of deadly crimes, competition for jobs, unfair distribution of support, and cultural differences. Currently, most host community households (58%) report never interacting with refugees, with only 17% report interacting every day. The main reason for contact is casual interactions.DCA’s current project is largely based on relevant lessons captured throughout project implementation in 2019, 2020, 2021. Labour market analysis, Social Cohesion assessment, Resilience and Peace Building assessment were done during previous projects implemented by DCA. These will inform the specific activities to implement under IGAs and business development in the upcoming project interventions. The market analysis focus is on vocational skills gaps, understanding existing skills, capacities and preferences of beneficiaries, and market assessment to inform employable sectors and market gaps.Addressing the challenges regarding social cohesion, it is required to strengthen the engagement of host communities surrounding the camps; particularly to reduce conflict sensitivity between the targeted two communities in the long-term. While inputs in the form of material or cash support should be complemented by building and strengthening positive relationships between the two communities, provisions provide the first step to increase interactions. Linkages should be made among potential producers, businesses, and markets as part of this.
