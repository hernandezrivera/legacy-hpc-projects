Project Summary

The WASH situation in Leer, Koch, Panyinjiar and Mayendit Counties are really demanding due to the impact of clashes and war experienced in some parts of the region in the past. Most of the existing WASH infrastructure were destroyed and now the hosts, returnees and the IDPs in these locations are in dire need of WASH services. The WASH project will address acute water shortages by rehabilitating water points and developing protected hand dug wells so that the population will access safe and adequate amount of water for drinking and other domestic uses. The rehabilitation, construction and repairs will be done after a GBV risk analysis have been conducted in the focus groups discussion with women and girls, conducted by female staff on the safety and best location to access safe equitable and sustainable water source and thus reducing cases of GBV and risk of sexual exploitation of women and girls since they are the primary beneficiary. The OTP/TSFP nutrition sites and PHCC/PHCU health facilities will be prioritized so that the children with SAM/MAM cases will not be left behind as part of the integration. The project will also ensure the vulnerable communities of Leer, Koch, Panyijiar and Mayendit Counties access improved sanitation services through rehabilitation of latrines in the safe agreed locations after discussions and consultations in the FGDs and the latrines will have locks to enhance privacy, dignity and security and hence reducing the risk of exploitation, such as sexual exploitation and abuse. The communities will also be taught by WASH staffs and Nutrition/Health staffs (trained on WASH minimum package) on how to improve hygiene situation through hygiene creation awareness campaigns with emphasis on hand-washing with soap at critical times, safe disposal of human wastes, treatment and storage of drinking water to prevent occurrence and transmission of Cholera and other water borne diseases.

Needs

According to South Sudan Human Response Plan (HRP) 2018, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains critical with an estimated 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in the second half of 2018. The situation in compounded by the effects of violence, displacement, diseases, floods and economic decline. The multiple hazards have continued to erode the capacity of people to cope and mitigate risks to life, livelihoods and well being. Humanitarian needs assessment reports done by REACH in South Unity Counties of Leer, Mayendit, Koch and Panyijiar and the recent assessment done by UNIDO in Leer and Mayendit Counties, indicates that an average of 1,250 people are served by one hand pump and most of them spend 30 minutes or more travelling more than 500 m through forests and other insecure places to access water. which is well below the sphere standard of one hand to serve 500 people spending not more than 30 minutes and walking less than 500 m. This situation has led to more breakdown of pumps and increased queuing time at the viable water sources causing reduced per capita water consumption (under 10 L/person/day), increased consumption from unprotected surface water sources, reduced time for other essential tasks and can also be a source of conflict between the residents and visiting users. This situation has also increased the burden of women and girls caretakers who are solely responsible for fetching water and exposing them to some form of GBV namely abduction of young girls and boys, forceful marriages, killing and rape. HIV and AIDS and various sexually transmitted diseases (STD) can also be transmitted to these poor women and girls. The hygiene situation of these areas is poor where about 99.% of the population in Leer, Koch Panyijiar and Payams in Mayendit North County practiced open defecation, combined with such low water consumption per capita greatly affects personal hygiene and health. Only 30% of the population mentioned both women and men are reported to wash their hands three or more times and only 13% of House Holds use soap. Additional challenges include exclusion of women (the main users of the water points) from leadership roles, as well as a lack of proper methodological hygiene promotion programs targeting women, men, girls and boys. Inadequate sanitation is also a major concern and improving sanitation will have a significant beneficial impact on health especially in household with SAM/MAM cases in OTPs/TSFP centres, in child friendly spaces and in schools. The gaps in water, sanitation and hygiene in Leer, Koch, Panyijiar and Mayendit Counties need and will be addressed through an integrated approach through rehabilitating water facilities, sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion and creation awareness which will impact knowledge to behavior change and improved health which will eventually minimize the risk of occurrence and transmission of water borne diseases including cholera

Activities

Rehabilitation and repair of boreholes- The rehabilitation of the water points will be done by trained hand pump mechanics after assessment. The number and locations of the broken hand-pumps will then be communicated and a meeting with the FGDs will be done to select the the best and urgent source to be rehabilitated this will ensure the affected population are not subjected to harm e,g exploitation and abuse. Water sources inside or near institutions will be prioritized e.g near a school, child friendly spaces, health or nutrition facility.Rehabilitation of latrines will also be done in or near institutions after a GBV risk analysis has been done and the latrines will have locks and doors to enhance privacy, dignity and security and hence reducing the risk of exploitation, such as sexual exploitation and abuse.Distribution of Water purifiers to the beneficiaries and monitoring their use. This will be done by WASH staff and also Nutrition /Health staffs trained on WASH minimum packages including demonstration on usage so that the beneficiaries will use safe water and minimize the risk of occurrence and transmission of water borne diseases.Distribution of Hygiene kits that includes NFIs and monitoring use. This will also be done by the WASH staffs, Nutrition and Health staffs to the prioritized beneficiaries like PWDs, PLM, elderly, CMAM children and their mothers etcHygiene promotion messages to influence behavior change to the community. This will be achieved by community hygiene promoters, CHPs through participatory interactions and hygiene and sanitation awareness creation campaigns with emphasis on importance of hand washing with soap, safe disposal of human excreta, treatment and safe storage of drinking water. In schools, WASH club members and patrons and school PTA members will be formed and trained so as to train children in schools on WASH.Training WASH staffs on gender mainstreaming in WASH. WASH staffs will be trained on GBV streamlining so as not to cause more harm to the beneficiaries and also teaching communities on the gender related issues.Training water management committees on ways of managing water points including guiding the elderly, sick, people living with disabilities and children at the water points. The beneficiaries will also be trained on practices which should not be practiced at the water points e,g bathing at the platform etc.Training Adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management- Adolescent girls will be trained on best way of managing their menses and Menstrual Hygiene Kits will be given to them and they will be taught on the usage both in schools and community level.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The beneficiaries will be directly involved before the launch of the project where they will be taught on the entire project cycle and duration. They will also be taught on their roles and responsibilities and they will have an opportunity to ask questions and get clarifications concerning the project. UNIDOR has been doing this so that the community gets timely information which enhances involvement and sense of ownership of the projects.The local leaders and other key stakeholders will also be invited during project launch and since they are the representatives of the community, they will give the community information about the assistance to be provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages will be context specific as far as WASH needs is concerned and will be specific on how to improve water sanitation and hygiene in the community. To ensure this, we will use our locally hired staffs selected from the community on merit who understands the context of the area and will be able to pass the information to them. Some information however will be specific to the target groups. for example discussion for safe location of water sources and gender sensitive latrines, women and girls are the target groups and the information will be specifically made to them during FGDs.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse especially to boys , girls and women when conducting all the WASH activities. For example, women and girls will be consulted through focal groups discussion on the best site for rehabilitating water sources and also latrines so that they can not be exposed when either going to fetch water or relieving themselves.Women, girls, boys and men will also be cautioned not to fetch water during the night as these may expose them to abuse, exploitation or even conflict. The Water Management Committees will also be trained and cautioned not to open the water points at night to limit occurrence of abuse and exploitation. WASH staffs will be trained on GBV and they will be able to prevent GBV cases from occurring and also report any case to the relevant authority in case it happens.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultations will be broad and inclusive to all. For example we will have consultation meetings with the community leaders, focus groups discussions which will compost of beneficiaries and other community group representatives for example with women, Public forums without any segregation and finally carrying out random sampling with questionnaires for the correspondents. We will also extent the consultations to the state cluster level concerning the issues that might have arise and not limited to nation cluster level

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes, we have developed a tool for feedback and the local leaders and representatives have the primary contacts to give their opinions and complaints. We have community Hygiene Promoters who visit household to household and they are able to get feedback from the direct beneficiary and the feedback's are addressed immediately.During field supervisions, we also have sessions with the beneficiaries where we discuss the project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach. These views from the beneficiaries we then screen and process them and we use this as complaint/ feedback mechanism.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community are engaged directly and indirectly. They are directly involved in monitoring and evaluation at the household level when they give complains, compliments and recommendations to the Community Hygiene promoters and other WASH staffs including hand pump mechanics. They will be indirectly involved when they are represented by their leaders who will be part of the team conducting the monitoring and evaluation. Locally hired staff who are selected from the community will be involved in the monitoring and evaluation and this in another way is also part of community involvement.The findings will be shared to the stakeholders upon their request for verification and the details of the community members and representatives that participated in the monitoring and evaluation exercise will be noted which can be used for reference.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project aims at promoting services that address different needs of men, women, boys and girls without any kind of prejudice. WASH infrastructure will be established after GBV risk analysis has been done through the focus groups discussions and hence ensuring safe environment for all the population (women, men, children boys and girls, the elderly including people that are physically challenged). For example we will prioritize repair and rehabilitation of a borehole that is in or near an institution to that inside the bush. The WASH projects will also involve selection and training of water management committees who will ensure the water points are secured through fencing and clearing of bushes around to prevent reptiles and other harmful animals and also ensuring that no person accesses it at night which is risky and can expose them to danger.Sanitation facilities for example a latrine will be lined to prevent collapse of any boy, girl, man or woman using the facility. It will also have locks and proper walls and door to ensure privacy and sense of dignity as compared to open defecation which exposes women and girls to abuse and exploitation.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project identifies and addresses barriers to access that different population groups could face in relation to the project for example gender sensitive latrines will be rehabilitated at agreed-upon locations and will be used in the daytime so that the privacy of women, men , girls and boys are respected unlike when they are just openly defecating.The WASH projects will be established at a central position where beneficiaries will not have to move to get the services while ensuring equality to all the gender groups without any discrimination or bias.Training of school girls on menstrual hygiene management will be done by female teacher trained by our WASH staff so that they can talk freely with the adolescent girls and train them as well as issue them with hygiene kits so as to break the barrier of adolescent girls not accessing hygiene kits to manage their menses and attending school.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address inequalities or protection threats by ensuring that the areas of operation are safe for men, women, girls and boys and can not subject anyone to threat. The projects will also include education of public on the usage of facility with inclusion of all groups so that no person will feel neglected. WASH facilities will only be accessed during the day and we will teach the community on this and adequate measure will be taken for the exceptional cases. The rehabilitated latrines and water points will be done after consultations with the focus groups to ensure their needs are addressed. Women and girls will also be advised to be vigilant and to avoid putting themselves at compromising situations for example walking to the water points in the evening alone in areas that are prone to GBV cases.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities will address inequalities/Protection threats by ensuring that men, women, girls and boys are involved in the different project implementation activities to ensure inclusivity and equality. For example Water Management Committees will consist of more women than women since women and girls are the primary users of water while hand pump mechanics trained will consist mainly of men due to the nature of the work involved and also the cultures that do not allow women to do such kind of jobs.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will measure access of beneficiaries through filling of questionnaires and feedback to the visiting community Hygiene Promoters who will provide the information for further processing. The rehabilitated WASH facilities will also be mapped and the GPS location taken and this will provide the location of beneficiaries accessing the WASH services.Field monitoring visits will also provide us with more information on whether beneficiaries are reached/accessed or not. NFIs distribution lists will also be used indicating the locations of the beneficiaries and this will be used to monitor access of beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The safety of the beneficiaries will be measured by the information obtained from the community members and the monitoring tool which will have a section to include the general security status of the area. Remote monitoring through the mobile phones will also be used to measure and determine the safety of the beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will ensure consultations are made with other sectors on ground like protection sector to provide the dignity status of the community for example number of rape cases as a result of WASH intervention. The project will provide dignity kits to the beneficiaries and the number and location that have received dignity kits will be mapped and monitored and this will provide an estimate to measure dignity of beneficiaries. Adolescent school girl's attendance list and MHM Kits distribution list will also be used as an indicator to measure dignity. The number of rehabilitated latrines with locks will also provide an estimate to measure dignity of the beneficiaries.
