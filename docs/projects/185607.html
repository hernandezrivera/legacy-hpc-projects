Project Summary

The IRC developed the project in line with its Global goal, the Strategic action plan (SAP) for Bangladesh 2022-2024, and JRP 2022 objectives. The IRC coordinates with Government of Bangladesh, PWG on protection mainstreaming, co-chairs the GDHWG, and reports through the 5 W on a monthly basis, and participates in camp level coordination meeting with the CICs, and other actors. The IRC's ProL project will be implemented in the following camps: 1E, 1W, 2E, 6, 11, 15, 16, 18, 21 and 25 where IRC has presence since 2017. IRC will work with community based protection structures to conduct protection analysis using PAF to identify protection concerns at camp level, and use protection monitoring to identify protection risks, incidents and trends over time. IRC as a major Player in the PWG will continue with disaster preparedness efforts for monsoon and cyclone responses and address challenges of COVID-19. The project will focus on systematic protection monitoring to unearth protection risks, incidents and trends relevant to protection and assistance to inform prioritisation, advocacy, program re-design, and decision making. IRC will conform to SoPs to prevent the spread of COVID-19, and work with community volunteers to collect data and information through KIIs, FGDs, and HHs interview and analyse the data which will be shared with the protection working group at Cox's and at field levels, donors, and other stakeholders; The IRC will establish and strengthen inclusive and representative community based protection structures ( 50% M and 50% F and will include persons with disability) to identify protection issues, handle non-criminal protection cases, and conduct timely referrals to foster access to services by those in need. strengthen social cohesion within refugee community and between the refugees and host community through conducting meeting and recreational activities to promote peaceful coexistence, and foster resilience among the refugees; provide legal assistance in HC (legal counselling, legal information/awareness, legal representation and strengthen alternative dispute resolution mechanisms specifically gender sensitive mediation at IRC hubs/appropriate community structures in ways consistent with the principles of fairness equality and accessible to women; conduct protection mainstreaming training, support IRC, partners, PWG and other sectors to mainstream protection through proritising safety, dignity, and do no harm, ensuring meaningful access by all to services, promote accountability, and participation & empowerment. IRC has Well established CRM in accessible places, a member of PSEA network and has focal point staff to create awareness on SEA and reporting mechanism. Provide technical support to IRC and partners' staff, protection working group, partner organisations, Health, FSL, and WASH sectors, and conduct advocacy with RRRC, and CICs to foster accountability by duty bearers. IRC will establish two-way communication to provide accurate, accessible, and life saving information both in the host community and camps. Information moderators will use Trello board to respond to information needs, collect feedback from the community on information needs, adapt the information content provided, feedback will be treated with due diligence, analysed, responded to, and used for decision making in a timely manner. The project will be developed using the AGD approach for effective program targeting vulnerable, and relevant to the context. Thus, FGD will be conducted with different Age groups (19-24, 25-30 etc) , gender and diversity to identify the needs of all the groups.

1 - Identified Needs

Now in its fifth year, the protracted crisis is a protection crisis. There are over 907,000 Rohingya refugees who have fled the conflict in Myanmar since 2017 (IRC SAP Oct, 2021). The stateless Rohingya are currently residing in extremely overcrowded camps, no freedom of movement outside the camps, restricted access to rights including education, livelihood and specific protection need which contribute to cyclical denial of identity, persecution, and displacement. COVID-19 continue to exacerbate pre-existing protection risks including coercion, violence, and deprivation as reflected by the perception of refugees of a deteriorated security situation. Measures to slow the spread of COVID-19 exacerbated protection risks such as IPV, neglect, violence, and exploitation (IRC, Protection monitoring report August 2021). The increased safety and security concerns caused by the impacts of lock down measures and suspended protection programming accelerated already rising protection risks including GBV, and child protection, weakened community structures, and intra, and inter community tension exacerbate. Furthermore, community based protection initiatives were suspended which hindered access to life saving protection information, disintegration of community structures, and weakening of community self protection capacity. In addition, in most of the camps there are a lot of challenges and the refugees are suffering the lack of mediation, and the extremist groups are taking advantage of the huge gap to propose mediation while extorting money from both parties to the conflict (IRC protection monitoring report sept 2021). The Joint Multisector Needs Assessment in Oct 2021 unearthed 30% of the host community need access to Justice and mediation services, only 54% contact law enforcement agencies, 46% contact Parishah, and a dismal 22% of the host community members have access to legal services from legal service providers. IRC GBVIMS October revealed 51% of women and girls reported physical abuse, 23% psychological abuse, 18% Denial of resources, and 95% experienced IPV. The CPIMS data unearthed 52% neglect, 23 psychological distress, 17% separated children, 14% child labor, 14 physical and emotional abuse, and 8% child marriage. The structural weaknesses in the Bangladeshi court system including limited capacity are pervasive with huge case backlog which create lengthiness, very costly, long distance to formal court system and weak alternative dispute resolution mechanisms.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Establish and strengthen community based protection structures in 10 camps. Activity 2: Conduct targeted capacity building training for community based protection structures on protection, PFA, GBV, PSEA, and CPActivity 3: Develop and implement context specific community based action plans to address specific protection issues identified in the context.Activity 4: Conduct systematic protection monitoring to identify and analyze protection risks, incidents and trends relevant for protection and assistance Activity 5: Conduct protection mainstreaming initiatives to maximize the benefits of humanitarian assistance. The capacity building training will enhance the application of and adherence to the 4 elements of protection mainstreaming and ensure that all humanitarian projects are developed with "protection lens".Activity 6: Conduct legal assistance in host community which shall encompass legal counselling, Awareness sessions on legal issues, legal mediation and litigation. Activity 7: Conduct advocacy at different levels -(camp, national, and or regional) to ensure accountability for action or inaction by the duty bearers.Activity 8: Establish 2 way communication system in camps to provide accurate, accessible and life saving information to the refugees and host community membersActivity 9: Establish community centers -Information desks, legal information, and recreational activities to strengthen social cohesion.Activity 10: Provide Individual protection assistance to the most vulnerable individuals.
