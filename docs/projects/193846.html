1 - Needs

Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa, with 1.14 million refugees and asylum seekers as of 30 June 2022. The Government of Sudan continues to generously maintain an open-door policy, with new arrivals often fleeing conflict, food insecurity, and lack of basic services in neighboring countries. Persistent insecurity in countries of origin contribute to protracted refugee caseloads remaining in Sudan, as opportunities for safe, voluntary, and dignified returns are obstructed. By mid-2022, Sudan is hosting 131,191 Eritrean refugees primarily in Kassala (103,340), Gedaref (13,840), and Khartoum (9,275) states; and 73,448 Ethiopian refugees, primarily in Gedaref (49,944), and Blue Nile (9,688) states. Within this context, persistent and large-scale needs shape the humanitarian context affecting refugees across Sudan. Ethiopian refugees have been seeking safety primarily in Gedaref since the outbreak of the Tigray conflict in 2020.Lack of legal awareness and knowledge including rights according to international law and the law of the land of persons of concern, host community, police officials and legal aid actors have contributed to difficulty to access to justice system and protect their basic rights. Inadequate number of knowledgeable legal aid actors particularly for sexual and gender-based violence against women and children including translation services for minority refugee group have contributed to the increasing number of SGBV and exploitation cases that have not been reported. Lack of legal aid specialist to work on children cases including child friendly facilities and legal process (i.e., separated interview room to separate perpetrator and child survivors, children psychologist to support the legal aid workers/court/police officials to interview children and separated detention space for children) have led to maltreatment and affected to children mentally and physically. PoCs in need of legal assistance supported. This program aims to educate and provide basic legal knowledge to women and children from refugees and host communities about women and children rights, refugees’ rights according to international law and Sudan law to mitigate traditional harmful practices against women and children through different awareness raising activities which includes campaign, focus group discussion and house-to-house visit.

2 - Activities/Outputs

- Providing direct legal services (representation in courts) to refugees and asylum seekers, with special attention to cases of human trafficking, cases of sexual violence cases, cases of arresting due to illegal entry and sexual exploitation and abuse - Visits monthly for lawyer to camps, immigration records and passport offices for the purpose of monitoring and investigating refugee cases. .

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is core in the mutawinat response and will be strengthened through continuous consultation with the affected population on the response modalities as well as their satisfaction with services provided. In addition, mutawinat is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of SEA. mutawinat will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. mutawinat partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regard to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities.
