Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to improve access to nutritious and diversified food at household level through climate smart agriculture, home gardens, income generation through gardening and improve nutritional status to the members of the households. Programme participants will receive training on vegetable production at household level through modern and climate smart techniques and environmental friendly e.g. the use of organic compost and avoiding pesticides (use of Pheromone & Yellow traps). To ensure optimum utilization of vegetable from the gardens, Concern build awareness for the programme participants through training and other supportive sessions on nutrition. The project will also aware participants on preservation of seeds, processing and storage of the vegetables by the trainings.
Aligning with nutrition specific intervention, the project will select programme participants considering vulnerability of malnutrition of children U5, pregnant and lactating women, person with disability, women headed family etc.
Major activities includes:
i. Support households to grow seasonal vegetables to improve dietary diversity.
ii. Training and awareness raising on climate smart home garden methods
iii.. Ensure cross-cutting issues including gender, disability, AAP etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Assessment shows that, 45% of Rohingya households have inadequate food consumption and 51% are unable to afford the minimum expenditure basket (MEB). This is further exacerbated by the recent reduction of food rations in the camps, straining already small household incomes: 29% of Rohingya refugees sell their humanitarian assistance items and 68% borrow money to buy food for the family; 55% of debts are associated with health care and 57% with food. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Rohingya camps is high at 12.3%, with stunting (chronic malnutrition) prevalence at 41% (compared to host community GAM at 9.8-9.9% and stunting at 20.7-25.8%). According to Concern's 2022 Barrier Analysis, and the Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) and Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) among children 6-23 months equally low (28.2% and 22.7% respectively) . Above gaps identified during programme design which leads to start nutrition sensitive food security activities in camps and host community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Considering needs and gaps for food security and nutrition, Concern developed programme strategy of nutrition sensitive food security activities which focus on the vulnerability of children U5, pregnant and lactating women etc. and aims to improve their nutritional status by increasing dietary diversity through climate smart home garden activities. Adoption of integrated approach: Concern developed an approach of integration of food security activities with nutrition specific and sensitive intervention for a better outcome and productivity. For the selection of programme participants, nutritional vulnerability will be examined aligning with and supported by nutrition specific intervention (CMAM) for each locations of camps and host community (IMCN). Ensure predictability and continuity: The programme participants will be supported through training and awareness session which will give them opportunity to build up capacity on climate smart gardening / agriculture. They can use the knowledge for long time even after closing of the project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Guided by Concern’s comprehensive protection policy, Concern uses a protection, gender and safeguarding risk tool to identify protection risks based on available evidence, community / self-protection measures that are already present. Based on all the information collected, Concern proactively identifies mitigation measures with the intention to prevent those risks from occurring. For the programme, Concern designs the activities in line with the need and suggestion of the community people. During the implementation period, regular session and mobilization meetings will be conducted with participation from the targeted community. Complaints and feedback response mechanisms (CFRM) for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries is in place through CRM box, hotline number and direct feedback during session and meeting. To ensure protection mainstreaming, all concern staff and volunteers will be trained on the Concern code of conduct and associated policies - the Program Participant Protection Policy, a Child Safeguarding Policy, an Anti-Trafficking in Persons Policy and a Whistleblowing Policy – all of which provide a comprehensive safeguarding policy framework for all of Concern’s programs. In addition, programme staff will also receive orientation from partner IRC on protection mainstreaming. For all the sessions, training under this programme, protection mainstreaming defined as an integrated agenda and will be discussed with programme participants to ensure protection of the beneficiaries and to ensure 'Do No Harm'.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project targets pregnant and lactating women (18-49 Y), husband of the pregnant and lactating women as primary target for nutrition sensitive activities. For the gardening activities, household with malnourished pregnant and lactating women, children, person with disability will be targeted. Concern and its partner will ensure participation of person with disability ensuring 'do no harm'. Concern will ensure inclusion of the beneficiaries for different ages, gender, ethnicity, religion etc.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

As part of AAP, Concern Worldwide conducted workshop with stakeholders, identified key intervention areas and modification of activities within the existing framework and will have initial meeting with the community and other key stakeholders to introduce the project its objectives and key activities. During the implementation, Concern will also share details information of the activities, the rights of the beneficiaries through this programing, so that beneficiaries can understand and be aware of their rights. The CFRM is also publicized and operationalized, such as through complaint boxes in activity sites/offices, a CFRM hotline number, email and/or in-person delivery to Concern office/staff. The community will also be sensitized on the CRM mechanisms through setting up a local help desk, and/or through household visits, group discussions etc. All feedback/complaints provided by beneficiaries through the accountability mechanisms will be recorded and resolved. During the monitoring and evaluation of the project, community will be engaged such as in the outcome harvesting, impact analysis and for reprogramming.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

All employees of Concern will receive training on the code of conduct (CoC) and P4 policy (Program Participant Protection Policy) which includes PSEA policy. Concern has established mechanisms to guarantee that individuals or any programme participants can promptly report any PSEA or SGBV-related issues using functional CFRM system. Concern has a zero-tolerance policy is in place against PSEA. All issues that are reported will be investigated and handled according to our internal policies. The policy regarding PSEA will also disseminate as a part of protection mainstreaming during sessions, meeting and trainings.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

DRR is considered as a cross cutting issue under this programme and environmental issue will be addressed during implementation, e.g. provision of organic compost, use of pheromone & yellow traps. The programme has also provision to response any DRR related emergencies aligning with the programme intervention location. Concern is a active member of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazila disaster management committee. Aligning with the committee, the programme will take part in DRR response.
