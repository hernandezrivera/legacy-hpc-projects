Project Summary

this project aims to promote oral health hygiene in North western Syria by building a much needed regional level framework for oral care focusing on 2 components:
(A) preventive component: targeting children and younger population with oral health awareness sessions and with distribution of oral hygiene kits. establish a routine check for children and refer them to dental clinic if necessary.
(B) and therapeutic component: provision of free dental care sessions by supporting a network of 30 dental clinics in the area, create new clinics where needed, and provide mobile dental clinics to access distant locations and address people with mobility disabilities.
this project targets are to provide oral health education and distribution of oral health kits for 20,000 children, and to provide 180,000 dental treatment (average cost per session would be ~5 USD) for people with need for primary oral health care services,

Needs

Displaced populations and host communities, particularly those in northwest Syria are facing an increased insufficiency in accessing to existing dental clinics, given that dental services are not always available in many primary health care centers, Existing dental services are overstretched and unable to deal with the increasing load of patients because of either a lack of proper dental/medical materials and supplies or the unavailability of dental clinics.As a result, Oral diseases such as dental caries, periodontal diseases, tooth loss, odontogenic infections, including abscesses and even orofacial infections have emerged as a major public health problem. Moreover, several oral diseases are related to chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular disease, bacterial pneumonia, diabetes mellitus, and low birth weight. Infective endocarditis is a serious and often fatal systemic disease that has been associated with dental diseases and treatment.there are few studies addressing the issue of oral health status in Syria, Except for white Smile series of oral health status assessments that was Updated in January 2019, available at : (https://www.whitesmilengo.org/needassessment) the recent assessment was based on WHO Step-wise approach form the monitoring of non-communicable diseases and customized for oral health survey, a total of 830 surveys have been collected within host communities and IDPs in white Smile clinics in Idleb and Aleppo governorates.Assessment results Indicated that : (25.54%) do miss 12 or more of their all teeth, with (0.95%) having no teeth at all, (3.70%) having less than 10 teeth. age is progressively affecting the overall number of teeth, the average sample individual would have (85.29%) chance to have more than 20 teeth when he/she is adolescent, this percentage would drop to only (29.41%) when he/she passes the age of 50.almost (55%) described their oral status as average (33.65%), poor (15.27%), and very poor (7.04%). the elderly group is having (75.32%) falling under the average or worse gum health categories. Implementation of activities

Strategy and approach :the program is to be conducted through the main phases as the following: o Phase 1: or Inception phase, renew MoU with local health directorates, recruitment for vacant positions, detailed implementation plans, timelines, programs and meal framework will be finalized. Also, baseline studies will be conducted in this phase, and consist of a mapping exercise to identify locations, priorities as per gender and age, current provided services, gaps. The second study will be epidemiologic and would consist a more in-depth assessment for oral status because as it will depend on dental practitioners to diagnose samples from targeted population, using standardized WHO tools and indicators (DMF/DMFt indicator , PI indicator , CPITN indicator, etc.)o Phase 2: at this phase, procurement of project supplies and equipment, including oral hygiene kits, dental supplies, dental mobile clinics, rehabilitation of dental clinics to be included in the project and make them accessible for disabled persons will be performed. In parallel, White Smile will develop training curriculum, and prevention sessions scientific materials.o Phase 3: implementation of awareness activities among children, dissemination of kits, including toothbrushes, toothpastes, mouthwashes, age and sex appropriate brochures and leaflets, refer cases that needs treatment to the dental care network.o Phase 4: provision of dental treatment in the clinics included in the network, prescribe necessary medications, and refer systemic cases diagnosed by the oral status exam (diabetes, hematologic disorders, cardiovascular disorders, stress, etc.) to its respective specialist.o Phase 5: closing out and end line assessments, to measure the impact of provided services and determine the needed frequency of awareness sessions to keep the children motivates. And the impact of provided dental care sessions over the well-being of local communities, hand-over activities for local health directorates will also be established. Logframe and project framework:Overall goalIncreased local resilience to oral diseases through oral health education promotion and increased access to basic dental care services. Outcomes • Increased resilience of local community through community engagement with oral health education campaigns• Increased access to basic dental services in targeted population, especially for vulnerable groups (disabled, IDPs, elderly people, women, children)• enhanced organizational local capacity (skills, structures, resources) Outputs a) Baseline report is completed and utilized to plan most prioritized intervention b) Awareness of the importance of Oral hygiene and tobacco risk is raised c) Dental treatment provided free of charge (in PHCs and mobile units) to population in targeted areasd) Service provided in a principled, professional, inclusive approach to all populatione) Improvement of local staff’s capacity with more gender balance in staff ActivitiesA.1) Conduct a baseline study to assess status of oral health, prevalence of smoking in the targeted areasA.2) Mapping areas with vulnerable groups (retirement centers, camps)B.1) Development of awareness curriculum and awareness bookletsB.2) Visits to schools, community centers, camps to conduct prevention sessions, smoking risk awareness sessionsB.3) Distribution of awareness materials and oral hygiene kitsC.1) Equip dental mobile unitsC.2) Establish dental clinics, rehabilitate them to be accessible to people with physical disabilityC.3) Create a Prioritization process for vulnerable groups in bookings for dental appointmentsC.4) Dentists and assistants jointly provide treatment sessions in PHCs or mobile unitsD.1) Develop quality standards for treatment (specially for vulnerable groups)E.2) Training for dental staff over protocols and standardsE.3) Recruit or qualify more female staff (Dentists, dental assistants)

Protection Risk Analysis

• Risk of inability of disabled persons or vulnerable groups from far locations to access the serviceo Likelihood: higho Mitigating measures Utilize mobile clinics that would periodically visit temporary camps or settlements with poor access to dental health clinics Properly establish disabled facilitation and standards inside our clinics (ex. Suitable doors width, wheel chair ramps at entrance stairs) • Risk of child abuse when afraid from receiving dental treatment: it is acceptable in some communities that parents might beat their children if they showed fear from dental anesthesia or extractiono Likelihood: higho Mitigating measures Strict policy guidelines to dentists to prevent parent from hitting their children to force them to make the treatment and apply the (Show, tell, do) approach to calm the child Education sessions with local community about the correct ways to address dental pain of their children, and to encourage regular check-ups• Risk of inequitable access to service by denying women to go to the clinic due to conservative community concerns, especially if the husband or the father is busy and unwilling to accompany the women to the clinic, or by wrong ideas about the danger of dental care for pregnant womeno Likelihood: mediumo Mitigating measures: ensure that clinics do have female health workers to avoid restriction of families to send their women and daughters to the clinics distribute leaflets and information products about the need for pregnant to access dental care and what kind of procedures should be avoided or not. correctly instruct male staff about correct ways to deal with women and ways to deal with sensitive medical questionnaires (regarding sexual diseases history for example) • Risk of GBV in overcrowded health centerso Likelihood: mediumo Mitigating measures: make sure to annex the clinic with suitable and sufficiently large waiting room with enough space between chairs adoption of appointment system to avoid overcrowding in front of the clinics• Risk of transferred infection in dental clinic (from patient to staff, or from patient to patient)o Likelihood: mediumo Mitigating measures: capacity building for staff about the principles of disinfection procedures and sterilization processes implementation Of WS infection risk policy in dental clinics (including mandatory wearing of latex gloves, medical masks, etc.) annual routine check for staff for main transmissible agents in dental clinic ( including Hepatitis B , HIV , etc.)

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Risk of attacks on dental clinics facilitiesThe primary threats in area 1 are shelling, airstrikes, and the risk of military advancement or change in the areas of control from different parties to the conflicto Likelihood: medium (dental clinics are not targeted in the same way as health clinics, and instead face threats commensurate with the general population and target area)o Mitigating measures Adopting appointment system for patients to avoid crowding in waiting rooms to minimum Choosing clinics as possible in multiple story buildings Availability of basic safety items (firefighters, first aid kits) and measures in each clinic (replacement of window glass with fiber glass) Hiring of a special safety and security officer, instructions to suspend clinics on days when security threat is high Continuous revision of security plans and preparedness with medevac plan for the staff for safer zones Ensuring the availability of internet connection to follow security updates and to stay in touch with members of staff.• Risk of kidnapping, assaults against dental staffo Likelihood: medium,o Mitigating measures Avoiding direct negotiations between dental staff and military groups, handling this issue by safety and security officer Capacity building for the staff on humanitarian principles and preserving neutrality in conflict zones, expressing opinions on social media. Avoiding keeping any valuable assets or cash in the HQ office to prevent kidnapping staff as leverage. Keeping the local communities engaged with the clinics and choosing local staff upon availability to assure social cohesion and decrease the potential of kidnap.
