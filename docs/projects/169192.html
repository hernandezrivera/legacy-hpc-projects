Project Summary

The Beirut Blast had significant consequences on the most vulnerable Lebanese and non-Lebanese communities, already struggling to cope with existing challenges, including the COVID-19 pandemic and the socio-economic crisis. Many individuals have little-to-no capacity to respond to the destruction of their homes, new medical needs, increased food insecurity or lost livelihoods. Initial reports indicate that mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are acute among vulnerable groups impacted by the blast. Children are particularly at risk. Displacement, loss of family members and high levels of stress at home can have significant consequences on children. SCI is implementing highly integrated programmers (CP, education, shelter and FSL), ensuring appropriate convergence and addressing family needs a holistic way as possible leveraging SC’s multi-sectoral expertise. Psychological First Aid (PFA), psychosocial support (PSS) and continued case management are urgently needed. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support will be prioritized integrated across child protection, education, livelihoods and shelter interventions. SC will provide immediate Psychological First Aid to caregivers and children in Child Friendly Spaces temporarily established as a specific response to the Beirut blast.
SC will continue to deliver MHPSS packages for both children and their parents/caregivers. Activities will be focusing on alleviating all the psychosocial distress suffered during explosion, COIVD-19 pandemic and on-going economic crisis, through community-based interventions as well as focused care for those children and caregivers whose needs exceed the capacity of community-based responses. The promotion of emotional wellbeing, recovery of the sense of normalcy, and alleviating the stress caused by a number of crises, and psycho-education would be the main focus on these tailored interventions. Packages to also be tweaked to the different needs of girls and boys as they can both be disproportionally affected and hence have different needs, interests, priorities, vulnerabilities and risks. Strengthening of community-based MHPSS response capacity, through PFA, Peer to peer PFA, Home-based MHPSS, Child and Youth Resilience package and Heart training.
Expanding the range of MHPSS interventions on offer, with a specific focus on person-focused interventions, through the recruitment of mental health professionals (psychologists), as well as training, coaching and supervision of paraprofessionals including CPCM teams and partners
Strengthening internal and external referral pathways and mechanisms including to specialized mental health services and paraprofessional counselling.
The PSS sessions will allow facilitators to safely identify children and/or caregivers requiring more specialized mental health support who will be referred internally to specialized MHPSS i.e for group MHPSS sessions or for individual sessions that will be facilitated by a clinical psychology. Caregivers and children under case management will benefit from this specialized MHPSS sessions. In addition, to provision of comprehensive case management for children at risk including referrals to specialized services. Children who have suffered any form of neglect, exploitation or abuse are entitled to care and non-discriminatory access to services. This will include the following: Provision of specialized case management, using components of Steps to Protect Common Approach, focusing on those most affected by the Beirut blast, the COVID19 pandemic and its secondary impacts, including vulnerable girls and boys who lost their parents and/or caregivers, experiencing domestic violence, GBV and at risk of child marriage, including adolescent girls and those who are already married/pregnant and working children. Referrals of children with disabilities and children who had new impairments resulting from the blast, to also be considered for them to access specialized services.

Plan fields

1 - Needs

Initial reports indicate that mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are acute among vulnerable groups impacted by the blast. Children are particularly at risk. Displacement, loss of family members and high levels of stress at home can have significant consequences on children. Psychological First Aid (PFA), psychosocial support (PSS) and continued case management are urgently needed to ensure the physiological wellbeing, safety and recovery of these children and the caregivers.

2 - Activities

1- Provision of PSS activities (with a focus on Resilience activities): PSS sessions will be provided to children and youth. Sessions will be based partly on the child and youth resilience manuals, and adapted to fit the focus of each session including: emotional processing, the accumulation of stressors, coping mechanisms, as well as sessions tackling other CP concerns, while continuously mainstreaming COVID 19 messages. The sessions will be conducted face to face in SFC, however if COVID 19 lockdown persists, the sessions will be adapted for remote modality. We can expect a decrease in participation if the sessions are done remotely. Targets: Recreational PSS: 225 children 2- Provision of emotional support, FPSS and MHPSS for caregivers and for children: The emotional support for caregivers can be offered in informal and group settings, creating a safe space for caregivers within the community to discuss the challenges they might be facing and find resources to cope, with the support of fellow members of the community. This approach is particularly helpful in post crisis times as a sense of proximity, support and solidarity amongst caregivers of the same community can be fostered. Moreover, a Home- based MHPSS approach will be conducted where the caregivers are the main facilitators of activities with children and can be safely done inside their homes. This approach is specifically designed for the context of the corona virus outbreak, when CFSs are temporarily suspended. Additionally, in the current Lebanese context, certain caregivers and children, who might be in need of MHPSS support following the Beirut explosion, are not engaging in group PSS sessions nor attending CFS. This intervention can be particularly helpful for them as well as for those who are injured and cannot commute to the CFS. Targets: FPSS: 100 children – FPSS caregivers: 100 caregivers – Family-based MHPSS: 150 children and 100 caregivers - Children and caregivers supported with specialized mental health service from the Psychologist are 25 child and caregivers: 25 children and caregivers. 3-Provision of specialized case management: using components of Steps to Protect Common Approach, this activity will focus on the most affected by the Beirut blast, the COVID19 pandemic and its secondary impacts, including vulnerable girls and boys who lost their parents and/or caregivers, experiencing domestic violence, GBV and at risk of child marriage, including adolescent girls and working children. SCI will consider referrals to specialized services for children with disabilities and children who had new impairments resulting from the blast. Children and their caregivers are provided with Mental Health and Psychosocial Support to overcome the consequences of multiple crises. Targets: 40 cases (also supported with emergency cash).
