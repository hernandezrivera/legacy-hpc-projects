Project Summary

This project is designed to address the pressing challenges faced by returnee single mothers. These women, often the heads of their households, confront economic instability, food insecurity, and social marginalization upon returning to their communities. This project aims to empower these women by enhancing their livelihoods through sustainable agricultural practices, specifically poultry farming and backyard gardening. Through targeted interventions, the project seeks to improve food security, increase household income, and foster community resilience. Cameroon has experienced significant socio-political unrest, leading to the displacement of many families. Returnees, particularly single mothers, often return to their communities with limited resources and support, facing high levels of unemployment and poverty. According to the World Bank, approximately 40% of returnees are unemployed, with women disproportionately affected. This situation is further complicated by cultural barriers that restrict women's access to land and financial resources.In rural areas like Mbonge and Fontem, the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) reports that over 60% of women live below the poverty line. This project responds to these challenges by creating sustainable livelihood opportunities through agriculture, promoting food security, and enhancing the economic independence of returnee single mothers.
Objectives
1. To empower returnee single mothers by providing training and resources for poultry farming and backyard gardening, enabling them to generate income and improve household well-being.
2. To increase access to nutritious food by promoting sustainable agricultural practices that allow families to grow their own vegetables and raise poultry.
3. To foster a supportive network among returnee single mothers, encouraging collaboration, knowledge sharing, and collective marketing of agricultural products.
The primary target population for this project is 300 returnee single mothers in Mbonge and Fontem Sub-Divisions. These women are often the sole providers for their families and face unique challenges in reintegrating into their communities. By focusing on this vulnerable group, the project aims to uplift not only the participants but also their families and the community as a whole.
Activities1. Training and Capacity Building
• Conduct 12 workshops over six months, covering essential topics such as poultry nutrition, breeding practices, disease prevention, and biosecurity measures.
• Engage local agricultural experts and successful poultry farmers to provide hands-on training and mentorship.
• Organize 10 training sessions focused on cultivating short-term crops like tomatoes, peppers, and leafy greens.
• Include hands-on activities where participants practice planting, soil preparation, and pest management.
• Provide training on budgeting, savings, and basic accounting to help participants manage their earnings effectively and reinvest in their businesses
• Distribute individualized start-up kits containing 25 three weeks broilers, a month’s supply of feed, and necessary equipment such as feeders, waterers, and basic veterinary supplies.
• Provide toolkits that include shovels, hoes, watering cans, and seeds for fast-growing vegetables, enabling participants to establish their gardens quickly.
• Facilitate the formation of support groups for returnee single mothers to encourage knowledge sharing and emotional support.
• Organize regular meetings to discuss challenges, successes, and strategies for collective marketing.
• Host community events where participants can showcase their products and connect with local markets, enhancing sales opportunities.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

To empower returnee single mothers by providing training and resources for poultry The Poultry and Backyard Farming Project is designed to address several critical needs identified among returnee single mothers who are facing the dual challenges of reintegration and economic instability. Through comprehensive assessments, the following key needs have been identified Economic Vulnerability:Returnee single mothers often find themselves in precarious economic situations due to unemployment or underemployment. Many lack the necessary skills and resources to establish sustainable livelihoods. The project aims to address this need by providing targeted training in poultry farming and backyard gardening, enabling participants to generate income. This economic empowerment is essential for their ability to support their families and improve their quality of life Food Insecurity:Food insecurity is a pressing issue for many households in Mbonge and Fontem, particularly among marginalized groups like returnee single mothers. Many of these women struggle to access nutritious food, often relying on unstable and inadequate food sources. By promoting backyard gardening and poultry farming, the project will directly enhance food security by enabling participants to grow their own vegetables and raise poultry. This not only provides immediate access to nutritious food but also reduces dependence on external food supplies.Lack of Agricultural Knowledge:Many returnee single mothers have limited knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices, which can hinder their ability to successfully engage in farming activities. The project addresses this need through comprehensive training workshops that cover essential topics such as poultry management, crop cultivation, pest control, and sustainable farming techniques. By equipping participants with practical skills and knowledge, the project empowers them to effectively manage their farming endeavors. Social Isolation and Lack of Support Networks:Returnee single mothers often experience social isolation and lack access to supportive networks. This isolation can exacerbate their challenges and hinder their reintegration into the community. The project seeks to build community cohesion by establishing support groups for participants. These groups will facilitate knowledge sharing, emotional support, and collective marketing efforts, fostering a sense of belonging and collaboration among women facing similar challenges. Limited Access to Resources:Access to essential resources such as land, capital, and agricultural inputs is often limited for returnee single mothers. Many women face barriers to acquiring land due to cultural norms and legal restrictions. The project will address this need by providing start-up kits that include poultry supplies and gardening tools, thereby removing some of the initial barriers to entry. Additionally, the project will explore partnerships with local microfinance institutions to provide participants with access to small loans, further enabling them to invest in their agricultural activities. Need for Financial Literacy:Financial literacy is crucial for the successful management of household income and agricultural enterprises. Many returnee single mothers lack the knowledge and skills to effectively budget, save, and invest their earnings. The project will incorporate business management training into its activities, focusing on essential financial skills. This training will empower participants to make informed financial decisions, manage their earnings effectively, and reinvest in their farming activities.This strategically designed to address the multifaceted needs of returnee single mothers,by focusing on economic vulnerability, food insecurity, agricultural knowledge, social isolation, limited resource access, and financial literacy, the project aims to create sustainable livelihoods and improve the overall well-being of participants.
