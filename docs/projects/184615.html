Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen a rights-based protection response for IDPs and non-IDP communities, as well as refugee returnees, in particular those most vulnerable through stronger advocacy, life-saving protection and assistance activities, evidence-based programming with the focus on developing and building the capacity of affected communities to cope and recover and better position them for the attainment of durable solutions. Improving coordination and quality of assessments and monitoring will also be key components of the project. The project also foresees benefiting the entire displaced population through continuous advocacy and support in the area of law and policy, fostering the successful engagement with relevant authorities and partners with a focus on the approval and implementation of the new IDP Law.
UNHCR will continue to make the best of its key membership of the protection cluster and ensure regular reporting to the clusters’ 5Ws and strong participation and contribution in the different clusters’ working groups to ensure coordination and complementarity of interventions, avoiding duplication with other members and ensuring regular sharing of protection reports with the cluster.

1. - Projects activities

UNHCR will target 1.2 million IDPs and vulnerable host communities with multifaceted protection interventions, aimed at enhancing access to strengthened national frameworks in line with relevant international standards, enjoyment of rights and improved living standards. Additionally, it is anticipated that 39,820 Somali refugees currently residing in neighboring countries including Kenya, Yemen, Libya, will return voluntarily to Somalia in 2022 bringing total number of returnees targeted by UNHCR’s intervention to 132,168 persons. UNHCR protection interventions will be guided by the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy and relevant frameworks for the protection, assistance, and solutions to IDPs. Further the interventions will contribute to strengthening system wide data collection, analysis to inform humanitarian response, that will in turn contribute to formulating an evidence base and strategic advocacy by the HCT, as well as protection mainstreaming and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). UNHCR’s interventions are fully in line with the key priority areas of “way of working” under the updated HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy and will contribute to HRP Strategic Objectives as it aims to: (i) address the protection needs of vulnerable and displaced persons through the provision of specialized assistance and services to identified persons with disabilities, and ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to services. (SO2 and Cluster Objective IDP communities, including persons with disabilities and older persons, receive quality and timely response services and benefit from risk reduction and prevention measures); (ii) strengthened protection monitoring and analysis (SO3 and Custer Objective Protection risks, human rights violations, gaps in available service and evictions, are identified and addressed through protection and eviction monitoring and analysis). UNHCR interventions will contribute to the achievement of the aforementioned HRP Strategic Objectives and cluster specific objectives: (i) protection risk assessments and monitoring to assess protection risks and vulnerabilities, needs and capacities of different groups in line with age, gender and diversity mainstreaming approach; (ii) UNHCR will continue working with partners to scale up Protection and Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), which has a country-wide coverage and is one of the key sources of displacement information. Data gathered by the PRMN includes displacement trends in Somalia, protection risks, vulnerabilities, and human rights violations, including evictions monitoring and those related to COVID-19. PRMN will also continue to contribute to Protection Cluster Somalia Protection Monitoring System (SPMS). UNHCR will ensure that PRMN results are widely used for advocacy, referrals and design of the response interventions by the humanitarian community. PRMN will also continue to be used as a protection monitoring tool on refugee returnee movements; (iii) UNHCR will strengthen the basic infrastructure to support remote IDP sites and ensure equitable access to services and assistance to persons in need at the household level with a clear focus of moving towards attaining durable solutions. This will contribute to peaceful co-existence between different groups in the community, ensure community engagement, ownership and sustainability of intervention; (iv) UNHCR will provide the support to community management structures on protection principles and promotion of age, gender and diversity approaches in the management structures, assessment and service delivery to affected communities; (v) provision of specialized services and support to the most vulnerable individuals according to their identified needs, including through direct assistance provision, referrals to service providers, and cash interventions to address needs identified; (vi) capacity building; (viii) advocacy for domestication of Kampala Convention and adoption of the IDP Act.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

UNHCR uses cash to provide basic needs and mitigate against protection risks. CBI provides greater dignity, flexibility, and choice for persons of concern in identifying their priorities and addressing their own needs. Cash-based interventions contribute to increase of protection outcomes, facilitate inclusion and solutions, and improve efficiency and effectiveness in programme delivery. Experience highlights that CBI can increase protection outcomes, it also contributes to the economy and foster positive relations with host communities. The flexibility that comes with the use of cash makes it a more dignified way of delivering assistance, an assertion that has been echoed explicitly by persons of concern (returnee refugees and IDPs during FGDs and PDMs). UNHCR has institutionalized CBIs as a corporate tool for delivering assistance and of UNHCR Operations, UNHCR Somalia is ranked amongst the top 10 CBI operations globally and the operation continues to draw from UNHCR’s strong expertise both from the HQ and Bureau on CBI programming leveraging on all the corporate tools developed over the years for improved efficiencies and empowering and giving beneficiaries a voice to own their lives and determine their future.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

UNHCR Somalia actively participates in the Cash Working Group and the joint multi-sectoral needs assessments as well as market assessments during the response analysis and planning to inform the modalities and delivery options of assistance. As a pre-requisite for CBIs, market functionality and access have been major determinants for CBI interventions. Based on the identified needs across sectors a minimum expenditure basket has been used to inform the transfer values for beneficiaries, and market price monitoring has continued to inform the transfer values. In programming for CBIs, contingency measures have been put in place in striking a balance on modalities (cash/in-kind) due to insecurity as markets can easily be disrupted or inaccessible as result. UNHCR has Framework Agreement with Standard Chartered Bank (SCB) since 2014 to implement cash-based interventions (CBIs), which is executed through AMAL Bank inside Somalia. The project will be implemented through this agreement and UNHCR’s CBI Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). As a member the CWG, UNHCR will continue to participate in joint assessments (needs and market) to inform CBIs and will strive for common platforms (joint FSP procurement, joint post distribution monitoring, joint targeting, etc.) across agencies for delivering assistance with improved efficiencies.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

UNHCR will ensure implementation of protection interventions that are fully in line with its Policy on Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD Policy of 2018) which focuses on six core areas of engagement that contribute towards achieving accountability to persons of concern and effective engagement of all beneficiaries regardless of age, sex, gender and age. Following a comprehensive participatory assessments process, UNHCR contribute to women participation in public life including participation in awareness raising campaigns and implementation of women’s economic empowerment projects in Somalia. In addition, UNHCR will employ vulnerability-targeting criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries.UNHCR will also ensure that prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse is systematically mainstreamed across the whole project cycles and that partners and community leadership structures are aware of their obligations in this regard. Communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanisms to ensure high standards of accountability are maintained and promotion of the UN zero tolerance policy to SEA and fraud. Existing community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms will be strengthened, and communities will be sensitized on existing mechanisms. Any confidential and personalized data (particularly those collected through PRMN and for the purposes of service provision/ referrals) will be restricted to authorized protection and community services staff and will be shared only on need-to-know basis and adhering to UNHCR data protection policies. Any use of data by UNHCR and its implementing partners is regulated by the general principle of confidentiality and provisions in project agreements with the partners. UHNCR will pursue projects that promote peaceful co-existence and also benefit vulnerable host communities’ members.UNHCR will conduct regular protection monitoring and continuous advocacy with relevant stakeholders to mitigate and address human rights violations and risks identified.
