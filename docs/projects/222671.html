Project Summary

Through this plan, TRI-SS seeks to mobilize $1,500,000 to provide quality gender-responsive and inclusive life-saving primary health services for 35,714 individuals affected by crises, including women, men, girls, boys, people with disabilities, and the elderly. The estimated cost per beneficiary is $42 per person. The response applies a mixed approach with static (3 PHCUs), and 6 mobile teams: One PHCU in each county and 2 Mobile teams each. TRI-SS will target those PHCUs that are not yet covered through the Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP), identified in consultation with the CHD. The distribution of the individuals targeted per county include, 19,643 (55%) in Renk, 7,143 (20%) in Pariang and 8,929 (25%).
In 2024, Renk experienced significant population movements due to the Sudan conflict, with over 373,000 individuals seeking shelter and services. The county's transit centers struggled to accommodate the influx, putting strain on resources. Despite efforts to prevent cholera, gaps in WASH infrastructure and healthcare persist. Pariang experienced displacement due to violence and flooding, while Kapoeta East experienced armed youth attacks, food insecurity, and malaria. The Rescue initiative-South Sudan (TRI-SS) is contributing to the cholera response efforts in Renk County.
This plan is aligned with the health cluster specific objectives to provide safe access to quality gender-responsive and inclusive life-saving primary health services for individuals affected by crises, including women, men, girls, boys, people with disabilities, and the elderly. Thus, protects vulnerable communities from life-threatening risks and provides specialized health services. The plan also involves consultation and engagement with affected individuals, promoting collective action and accountability.
The activities include:
• OPD consultations for common diseases in 3 PHCUs are not supported by HSTP.
• Mobile Health Teams deployment to underserved areas.
• Mental Health and psycho social support services
• Skilled deliveries & GBV Integration
• Epidemic Disease Surveillance, Prevention, and Control.
• Immunization campaigns targeting vaccine-preventable diseases.
• Regular assessment and improvement of infection prevention and control standards in health facilities.
• Health Education, Risk Communication and engagement.

1. Needs

Renk witnessed significant population movements in 2024 due to the conflict in Sudan. Over 373,000 individuals passed through Renk seeking shelter and services. In September 2024 alone, 23,713 returnees (81% of total arrivals) crossed through Renk. The county's transit centers struggled to accommodate the overwhelming influx of returnees and refugees. The high influx of returnees placed significant strain on available resources. 56 suspected cases of Mpox reported across different counties one (1) in Renk (IDSR Week 35, 2024). As of January 2024, 154 suspected measles cases were reported in Renk, with 19 (12.3%) laboratory confirmed. By November 2024, 550 suspected cholera cases were reported across South Sudan, with 166 in Renk County. Cholera prevention efforts in Renk included supplying 44,683 individuals with clean water and deploying 28 hygiene promoters at Wunthou Reception Center. Despite these efforts, gaps in WASH infrastructure and healthcare persist. The Rescue initiative-South Sudan (TRI-SS) deployed 1 clinical officer and 4 hygiene promoters to Renk to contribute to the cholera response efforts in Renk County. Pariang experienced displacement driven by inter-communal violence and flooding, contributing to a fluctuating population of internally displaced persons (IDPs). In Kapoeta East, Armed youth attacks in Kauto Payam displaced approximately 6,500 individuals to remote mountainous areas, further stressing local resources and creating humanitarian access challenges. Pariang and Kapoeta East also experienced severe food insecurity, driven by displacement, conflict, and environmental factors with Kapoeta East County in IPC phase 4, while Pariang county is classified as phase 3. Malaria, hepatitis E, and measles were prevalent in Renk, Pariang, and Kapoeta East.
