Project Summary

The project aimed to contribute to the FSL cluster CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5) and CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock in communities of Rubkona, Koch, Panyijiar and Mayendit counties, Unity state. The IRC will respond to the most vulnerable and food-insecure households classified in IPC4 or, worse, acute food insecurity phases through unconditional multipurpose cash assistance to meet their food and other basic needs. The cash assistance will target households with multisectoral needs of food, nutrition, health, and protection-related. The project will provide a sequence of food production to support the most severely food-insecure and malnourished households in the main season farming of May to July and the dry season of Nov to March. To strengthen households’ resilience, the project will mainstream gender, conflict sensitivity, and disaster risk reduction to support at-risk families focused on improving vegetable, crop, and fisheries production and bolstering resilience towards recurrent shocks and stresses. In pursuit of the project objective(s), the following activities will be implemented:
(a) The IRC together with the complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM/AAP) committees, will mobilize and engage community members to inform the needs of the most vulnerable households and selection criteria for registration together with FSL, Nutrition, and protection partners.
(b) Provide 3,870 (1,538 males, 2332 females) most vulnerable people unconditional multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) during the critical lean season months to highly vulnerable food-insecure households to meet their food and other basic needs and reduce negative coping strategies.
(c) Preposition and distribute emergency (short-term maturing) agricultural inputs (Cereal and vegetable seeds, and tools) and fishing kits to 2,580 (1024M, 1556F). The IRC anticipates FAO will provide complimentary in-kind input to support the project.
(d) To improve nutritional practices for pregnant women and caregivers of children under 5 years with MAM and SAM. Nutrient-dense vegetable seeds will be provided, demonstration gardens at nutrition facilities close to water points, and basic training on vegetable production techniques. The IRC Nutrition team will conduct nutrition awareness and food cooking demonstrations showing how to enrich households’ meals with locally available foods
(e) Facilitate capacity building to community-based extension agents (AEA’s), crop producers, and fisherfolks on sustainable production skills and provision of proximate extension services on production and post-harvest handling through demonstration gardens. Also, fisherfolks receive basic training on fish preservation and kit repairs to enhance household nutrition and income.
Overall, the project will reach 5% (129,000 people) (i.e 6,450 people) in emergency or severe (IPC 4 for 2024) in Rubkona, Mayendit, Koch, and Panyijiar counties. In Particular, Rubkona (3,850 people), Mayendit (650 people), Panyijiar (1400 people and Koch (550 people) equivalent to a total of 6,450 people (1,297 e.i 522M children, 775F children, 2,576 e.i 1,017M , 1,017M, 1559F Adult, 1,615 e.i 643M, 972F elder, 962 e.i 380M PWD, 582F PWD). By program type: 3,870 (1538M, 2332F) people to receive multipurpose cash; 2,580 (1,024M, 1556F) people to receive livelihoods inputs distribution (Crop, Vegetable, Tools & fishing Kits) and production skills

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple shocks and stressors, including insecurity, intra-communal violence, and conflict, low agricultural productivity, poor macroeconomic conditions compounded by recurrent climatic shocks (flooding and prolonged dry spells leading to low agricultural productivity), and the ongoing conflict in Sudan. The continued influx of returnees and refugees has driven high humanitarian needs, putting an additional burden on host communities who are sharing scarce resources with new arrivals. As of October 31, 2023, South Sudan received 353,175 returnees and refugees from Sudan. Between August 1 to October 21, 2023, over 69,896 South Sudanese refugees returned from Ethiopia due to the suspension of WFP food assistance in refugee camps and the deteriorating security situation in Gambella. With no end to the Sudan conflict, it is expected that the number of people seeking refuge in South Sudan will continue to increase. Food insecurity is likely to worsen in 2024 in South Sudan, with huge food gaps among returnees and refugees. This is likely to increase severe and harmful coping mechanisms like begging and borrowing, leading to the accumulation of debts; forced migration in search of jobs; the sale of productive assets, reducing the frequency of meals; and eating wild fruits, among others. The lack of food among refugees and returnees and host communities is likely to increase the strain on host communities in Rubkona, Panyijiar, Mayendit, and Koch counties as they turn to their hosts for support. It is also likely to increase tensions between the refugees, returnees, and host communities, with the risk of exacerbating conflict over available food and natural resources.According to the Integrated Food Security Classification Phase Classification (IPC, 2024), the food and nutrition insecurity in South Sudan is expected to worsen at the peak of the lean season (May – July, 2024). In particular, an estimated 129,000 people will experience emergency or Catastrophe food insecurity (IPC 4) in Rubkona (77,000 people), Panyijiar (28,000 people), Mayendit (13,000 people) and Koch (11,000 people) counties. Further recurrent flooding, droughts, and the outbreak of pests and diseases continue to impact the food security and livelihoods of vulnerable people in Unity State. as the majority of the population relies on climate-sensitive livelihoods (crop farming, pastoralism, fishing) to meet their food and income needs. Recurrent climatic shocks continue to destroy farmlands, reducing annual crop yields. Other sources of livelihood, like fishing, experience loss and damage of fishing equipment, thereby reducing fish trade, which is one of the key income sources. Pastoralist livestock continues to be affected by pests and diseases, significant damage to pasture/grazing land, and decreased milk production. Further, communities and local authorities lack the capacity, knowledge, and skills to respond to climate shocks and new emerging disasters. Thus, there is a need to provide emergency cash, food, and livelihood response to the vulnerable affected people's needs.

2. Project activities

(a) At the inception phase, the IRC will establish and train the CFM/AAP committee on their roles and responsibilities, using WFP’s/FAO standard operating procedures. The APP will include local authorities, CAD and RRC, women, and youth leaders.(b) The CFM/AAP will validate beneficiary selection criteria, and participate in the beneficiary identification, selection, and registration process to ensure equal representation of vulnerable groups, including beneficiaries from the nutrition and protection sectors. The targeted beneficiaries are the most vulnerable households, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities, including pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), caregivers with children under five with SAM and MAN, Women GBV survivors, and PWD newly arrived most vulnerable households from Sudan without food and income.(c) Provide unconditional multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) during the critical lean season months to highly vulnerable food-insecure households to meet their food and other basic needs and reduce negative coping strategies. The households will receive cash assistance equivalent to at least 70% of the Multi-sector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB), based on the South Sudan Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) values in September 202 for South Sudan. The IRC will provide two rounds of cash assistance, with a household expected to receive Panyijiar (119 USD), Koch (101 USD) Rubkona (82 USD), and Mayendit (106 USD).(d) Preposition and distribute emergency (short-term maturing) agricultural inputs (Cereal and vegetable seeds and tools) and fishing kits. The IRC anticipates FAO will provide complimentary in-kind input to support the project.(e) To enhance access to nutrient-dense food and improve nutrition and caregiving practices for pregnant women and caregivers of children under five with MAM and SAM referred by the nutrition partners. Nutrient-dense vegetable seeds will be provided to caregivers, and demonstration gardens will be established at nutrition facilities close to water points for training on vegetable production, harvest, and preservation techniques. (f) The IRC will conduct nutrition-sensitive awareness and cooking demonstrations on preparing nutritious meals, particularly for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5, using vegetables from gardens and other locally available foods. Nutrition-sensitive messages will be shared on hygiene promotion to caregivers. (g) To build local adaptive and absorptive capacity of target households to enhance food production, IRC will identify and train AEA’s who will cascade training to beneficiaries and extension services. The Farmer Field school training will be conducted at the demonstration sites by AEA using the FAO training manual. The farmers will receive training on climate-smart agriculture practices, including composting/manure management, mulching, pest management, and post-harvest handling.(h) Support livestock mass vaccinations, treatment, and surveillance through CAHWs: FAO, through the Ministry of Livestock, oversees the coordination of livestock disease surveillance and response with partners. In contribution, the IRC will engage the FAO, and train CAHWs and county agriculture and livestock departments to contribute to livestock disease surveillance and monitoring, facilitate vaccination campaigns during disease outbreaks, and plan mass vaccination exercises and treatments for livestock. (i) The IRC will deploy a team of technical staff to implement and monitor the project activities in the field directly. The project will engage beneficiaries and local authorities for participatory joint monitoring every quarter. Project M&E systems will include periodic data collection and tracking through standard to FOA WFP and IRC. The IRC will engage relevant stakeholders (CAD, RRC, beneficiaries, and AEA’s/CAHW) to document lessons learned that the IRC and partners can use in designing other projects.
