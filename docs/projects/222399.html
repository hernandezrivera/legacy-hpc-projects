Project Summary

The overall goal of the project is to build resilience against factors that have caused perpetual food and nutrition insecurity for multistate communities in South Sudan. The project is multi-interventional package consisting of cash or voucher assistance for food in emergency, agriculture, nutrition and climate change education, incorporating community outreach to improve community food security and nutrition. According to the IPC analysis for 2024, between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 includes 10,000 people in Malakal County in Upper Nile State and approximately 31,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan. the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season in the projection period of April to July 2025. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. ADRA is proposing to work in two of the worst affected states (Upper Nile and Warrap states).
Good food security and nutrition relies on healthy food systems and clean environments. A healthy food system includes the production of plant and animal food, addressing biodiversity, healthy diets and eco-friendly environments. The project will be a platform for food security, environmental and nutrition behavior change communication that capacitate smallholder farmers and their support systems including livestock with alternative sources of income to meet community needs for other non-food items that jumpstart sustainable livelihoods, productivity and household incomes. ADRA will use the farmer-to-farmer group approach comprising of men, women and the youth to effect household food production and food basket and improve nutrition habits among vulnerable families. improved agricultural and veterinary practices, income generation, natural resource management and peace building with a focus on gender parity and community engagement. The reverberation of the Sudan Crisis in April 2023, has increased the number of people migrating into South Sudan through the Renk border. Migrants who consist mainly of returnees, are moving from Renk into counties in Upper Nile including Longichuk, Maiwut, Nasir and Ulang with multiple needs. The populations of incoming migrants are faced with challenges of food insecurity, malnutrition for children under the age of 5 years & pregnant and lactating women, poor water- sanitation & hygiene, limited shelter, disease outbreak, lack of education for children of school going age and natural disaster such as flooding. According to a report by IMC in August 2023, for women and children fleeing Sudan and host communities, conflict, displacement and rising costs are worsening malnutrition rates. Returnees and IDPs have lost their livelihoods due to movement to safer areas, forcing the populations to depend on humanitarian assistance. This is further aggravated by flooding in Greater Tonj and the Sobat corridor that includes Nasir and Ulang which began in October 2023.

1. Needs

A complex interaction between and among climate change, conflict and development has left South Sudan consistently ranking among the world’s hungriest countries. South Sudan remains the topmost food insecure country globally, with needs expected to rise to record high levels and some areas facing a risk of famine during this rainy season. Frequent reports of localized famine conditions have surpassed all countries globally in the last seven years. The agro-pastoralists smallholder farmers in South Sudan are half sedentary and food-foraging society relying most on some crops, livestock like cattle and cattle products. Food security vulnerability in South Sudan is compounded by inadequately managed livestock and agricultural systems that have had a damaging effect on land, water and atmospheric system resultantly limiting the accumulation of surplus food and possessions. Movement has further contributed to the loss of household labor due to travel and health emergencies. These factors, combined with other factors such as conflicts, climate change and variability have posed unpresented threats on food security, exacerbating it. With the world past the halfway mark from 2010, the global agenda 2030 as elaborated in the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), will be missed. Most smallholder farmers and agro-pastoralists in South Sudan live in areas that are marginal, prone to high heat stress, drought and floods with no infrastructure that can support life and or enterprise. There are several problems bordering around sanitation, resilience and conflicts, a situation that has created a caste-like, food insecurity and vulnerability among the agropastoralists and smallholder farmers in South Sudan. According to the IPC analysis for 2024, between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 includes 10,000 people in Malakal County in Upper Nile State and approximately 31,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan. the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season in the projection period of April to July 2025. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, approximately 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) will be observed during this period. About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. ADRA is proposing to work in two of the worst affected states (Upper Nile and Warrap states).

2. Project activities

1. Distribution of inputs: vegetable, crop and fish inputs will be distributed to all targeted individuals from the cluster target households falling under IPC phase 4 (emergency) in the eight counties. Of these households, 60% of the target group will be vulnerable women and men smallholder farmers including returnees. IDPs will constitute 20% of the total while young people also constitute 20%. 2. Demonstration sites: The project will establish a minimum of 12 crop and vegetable demonstration sites across each Payam. These will be centres for interaction for farmers, extension agents and private sector players (input suppliers, agro equipment suppliers) for knowledge sharing and field level testing of appropriate technologies. 3. Exposure and learning visits and events: The project will support field days, intra- and inter-county learning visits with the latter bringing together farmers who experience the same physical, climatic, social, economic and cultural characteristics. 4. Training in agronomic practices: One lead farmer will be selected from each group of 20 farmers formed through self-selection. The project will facilitate a Training of Trainers (ToT) for Agroecology and Farmer Field Schools (FFS) for four Agriculture Extension officers and a Crop Specialist at County level. 5. Promote Improved Post Harvest Management Practices/Value addition, preservation and storage: Famers in South Sudan loose about 30% of the harvested grain due to lack of appropriate post-harvest and storage facilities and knowledge. Farmers are forced to sell most of their grain soon after harvesting at very low prices, leaving them poor and vulnerable. ADRA will use its experience from previous interventions to implement the use of post-harvest equipment which includes solar driers and use of crop hermetic bags. 6. Pasture and Grazing Management (fodder banks/grazing land reinforcement) and Livestock Marketing Improved: The deaths due to limited grazing of cattle, overgrazed pastures and poor-quality pastures presents an opportunity to lure men into mixed farming strategy that can respond well to food insecurity that is affecting the region. Communities will conduct a participatory assessment of the grazing area to establish the state of the rangeland in order to come up with activities that address the immediate challenges. These challenges will be addressed through the FFS and demonstrations for pasture and grazing management. A TOT will be conducted for lead farmers on fodder production, management, harvesting and preservation. Training will include silage making, hay making, preservation and storage. 7. Monitoring and Evaluating Project Activities: The project will develop an MEAL Framework that includes an MEAL plan, indicator tracking, survey data where all the payams/ bomas will feed information. Key to sustainability is the farmers’ ability to monitor their own project, evaluate their performance and proffer solutions. Lead farmers/Farmer facilitators with the assistance of government extension agents at Payam level and ADRA will form community-based monitoring and evaluation (CBME) committees. These will be trained on basic participatory MEAL tools, data collection and the interpretation of collected data. 8. Food distribution (nutrition enhancement foods) will be conducted to pregnant & lactating women and children under 5 especially in IDPs and among returnees.
