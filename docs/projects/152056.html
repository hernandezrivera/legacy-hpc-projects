Project Summary

UNHCR proposal to provide livelihoods training for women, men, boys, girls and adolescents internally displaced persons (IDPs), refugee returnees and host community members aims to ensure all persons of concern are able to make a safe and sustainable living that meets their basic needs, contributes to their dignity, reduce risk of sexual and gender based violence and provides for the full enjoyment of human rights. The action will be implemented in Borno (Ngala, Mobbar, Maiduguri, Jere and Bama), Yobe (Potiskum, and Damaturu) and Adamawa States ( Mubi North, Mubi South, Fufore and Maiha) of North-East Nigeria and is aligned with the early recovery and livelihood strategic objectives of the HRP 2019 and the centrality of protection.
Tin line with the early recovery and livelihood sector strategy, the project objective is to Support livelihood stabilization and income recovery. UNHCR will ensure the achievement of this objective through the following means;
I: Build capacities of affected and vulnerable internally displaced persons (returning), refugee returnees and host community members particularly vulnerable women and men and youths through livelihoods opportunities like vocational skills training and training in agriculture/livestock/fishery production to support economic self-reliance and reintegration.
II: Promote business-oriented community-based organizations which will inform and strengthen formation of beneficiaries in cooperatives, who will be provided with financial managing and entrepreneur training and provided with cash grants and starter kits in various skills to enable undertaking of collective production and marketing for long-term sustainability.
Another objective that will be achieved by this project is Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in HC, returnees, and host communities through facilitation of intending returnees (women, men and youths) to assess conditions in their home areas and to plan their return together with receiving communities and support the return and reintegration process. And provision of livelihood opportunities, capacity building and community-based responses.

Needs

Now in its ninth year, the conflict in North-Eastern Nigeria continues to have a devastating impact on civilians. The crisis is among the most significant developmental, humanitarian, and political problems in Africa that have led to massive numbers of causalities, economic disfranchisement, mass displacement of the population, and threatens the future generation across Nigerian and its neighbours. It is projected that the affected states and Nigeria as a whole have lost over eight years of human development progress since the crisis began. Since the start of the conflict, more than 20,000 people have lost their lives, over 4,000 people were abducted, and currently, over 2.5 million people in the region have been displaced, of whom 1.9 million are internally displaced in Nigeria according to the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 24 of August 2018. The complexity of the crisis means that civilians, including displaced persons, vulnerable host communities and those trapped in conflict areas, face grave protection risks and violations which leads some of them to engage in negatives coping behaviours. A number of the vulnerable women, men and youths (boys and girls) are sexual exploitation and abuse, arbitrary and extended detentions, family separation, exposure to unexploded devices and IED attacks, restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity, all of which are exacerbated by insufficient essential services and psycho-social distress. In alignment with the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the centrality of protection and the Nigerian Governments development plans, UNHCR continues to lead and coordinate efforts to deliver life-saving assistance. Additionally, UNHCR provides community-based interventions, conducts protection monitoring, advocates for and strengthens the capacity of national and local authorities to protect IDPs and provides technical support to other sectors on protection. Furthermore, UNHCR and UNDP finalized the durable solutions and returns strategy to foster voluntary returns and reintegration of returning refugees from across Cameroon and IDPs. Central to achieving/ensure a safe protection environment is the provision of livelihood support to vulnerable women, men and youths IDPs and refugee returnees as well as host community members. UNHCR through its activities to promote self-reliance and improved livelihoods is anchored on the HRP objective that seeks to foster resilience and early recovery, and strengthen the humanitarian-development nexus by working towards collective outcomes. Resilience building activities will help ensure that the humanitarian response also contributes to longer-term objectives, more resilient communities and durable solutions. With the progress made in terms of liberating previously occupied communities by non-state armed groups, many more of the displaced people of the region are slowly returning to their homes and communities. Over 90% of the displaced have returned home in Adamawa state with about 40% for Borno and 60% for Yobe State. Most of these returnees are facing prolonged food insecurity, lack of livelihoods with jobs lost and previous means of livelihoods no longer available, particularly for women and girls who have been negatively impacted by the crisis and exposed to risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, etc. With the return of many IDPs to their homes, there is the urgent need to provide livelihoods support so that they can cater to their needs. In 2017-2018, UNHCR and its partners trained and empowered over 10,000 individuals with life-skills, agricultural production, vocational training and enterprise development. Additionally, there is a lack of technical and vocational training. Indeed, the number of persons of concern receiving life-skills training for livelihoods purposes is very limited, especially given the low presence of civil administration and other socio-economic structures in return locations.

Activities

Access to self-employment/business facilitated: The formation of cooperatives shall be developed through which women, men, boys and girls of concern will receive guidance to improve their living or invest in a business. Cooperatives will receive counselling, mentorship, technical and operational support to grow their businesses. Starter material kits and financial support (operational/start-up capital) will be provided to form small-scale enterprises or income generating activities. The project will employ cooperative monitors to provide selected by the members from within the community to provide relevant guidance and closely monitor that all cooperatives run their respective businesses successfully. Vocational Skills training: UNHCR intends to reintegrate women, men, female and male adolescent’s returnees, IDPs and host communities through vocational training skills making use of existing or previously technical training platforms. The project will design and roll-out market-driven skills to strengthen livelihoods and the local economy. Returnee, IDP and host community men, women and adolescents will be trained to acquire skills in art and craft, soap making, knitting, use of computer software programming, carpentry, brick making, briquette making (as cooking fuel), welding and phone and digital repairs. All beneficiaries of vocational training programmes will benefit from Financial Literacy and Business Development training (FLT &BD) in all three States. The FLT enables the beneficiaries (individuals or families) gain the basic understanding of banking, savings, and the importance of good credit. It increases the likelihood that the beneficiaries (women, men and youths) who are form cooperatives and are support with cash grants and/starter kits will utilize the banking/local thrift services and access more finances to support their business. Peaceful Coexistence with Local communities Promoted: UNHCR will undertake sustained advocacy and engagement with communities about the vulnerabilities and risks of specific groups through engagement with religious, traditional and community leaders - local champions who can influence others in the community to accept and support returnees. This initiative will create a platform for overcoming barriers to community reintegration.
