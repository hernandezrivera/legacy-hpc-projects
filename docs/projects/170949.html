Project Summary

The general objective of this proposed MdM project targeting the North West Bank (NWB) and Ramallah Governorate, is to contribute to a comprehensive Protection system of vulnerable communities that are exposed to conflict-related violence, but are also impacted by the stressors and the uncertainty accompanying the COVID-19 pandemic.
The MdM project comes with 3 main outcomes as follows:
1. Mitigate the impacts of conflict related violations (settler violence, demolitions, excessive use of force by Israel Security Forces (ISF), displacement) on the mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS) wellbeing of Palestinian communities living in NWB including the North Jordan Valley (NJV) and Ramallah Governorate.
2. Strengthen the resilience and positive coping mechanisms of Palestinian communities living in NWB including the NJV and Ramallah Governorate.
3. Third States Parties, notably France & EU Members are mobilized and take systematic and concrete measures toward occupation practices which negatively impact the health and protection of Palestinians and their effective access to services in the WB.
Within this action, MdM will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objectives 1 and 2, both relating to the Strategic objective 1 of the HRP.
In relation to Objective 2 of the Protection Cluster “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the
effects of the occupation and conflict related violence”, the MdM intervention will focus on the provision of MHPSS support with protection, child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) case management interventions mainstreaming, despite if the case is related or not to occupation. Further, we will address the health and protection needs of People with Disabilities (PWD) through the adaptation of interventions to their needs, accompanying their follow up, and ensure they are reaching the necessary services. To address the community stress and anxiety related to COVID-19 pandemic and to limit the spread of the virus, awareness raising and MHPSS related to COVID-19 will be also mainstreamed within this action.
MdM advocacy efforts will also contribute to Objective 1 of the Protection Cluster “Increase Respect of International Humanitarian Law & International Human Rights Law and Accountability for Violations”. The advocacy contribution of MdM will be through field briefings to diplomats and international media, production of periodic reports and factsheets to reflect the impact of the violations in the MHPSS of the Palestinian communities, in addition to a lobby tour or virtual meetings with targeted diplomats in Paris and Brussels. Moreover, MdM will feed and contribute to all advocacy initiatives of the Protection Cluster and its Areas of Responsibilities (AOR) covering the MHPSS field.
MdM interventions within this action will be provided directly and through outreach program to all targeted communities, keeping community participation and coordination as one of the main pillars of its intervention. As a medical organisation, MdM is working with Specific Operation Procedures (SOPs) related to COVID-19 that enable us to safely implement this outreach program. MdM team members are also trained and prepared to provide remote MHPSS interventions by phone in case of COVID-19 related restrictions.
MdM MHPSS interventions will be under the umbrella of, and in close coordination with, the Protection Cluster and its sub-working groups. A specific Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) plan is already developed and functional for this kind of intervention. MdM will ensure close coordination, avoid duplication and ensure complementarity of services with other protection actors in the targeted area.

1 - Needs

Through direct outreach, MdM is providing MHPSS Emergency Response (ER) to victims of critical incidents related to occupation since 2012, targeting Nablus, Tubas- North Jordan Valley, Salfit and Qalqilya governorates, in addition to hotspots in the eastern part of Ramallah Governorate. It was identified by MdM team members that the impact of the coercive environment is significant in terms of fostering a prolonged environment of fear and intimidation, accompanied with different kinds of violations related to the occupation, including Israeli settlers' violence, demolitions and excessive use of force by ISF. MdM identified a clear increase in the number of critical incidents within the first 9 months of 2020 when compared to the same period of 2019, MdM intervened following 282 incidents within 2020 (187 settlers related, 19 ISF, 85 Israeli Civil Administration (ICA)), compared to 130 incidents (76 settlers, 10 ISF, 44 ICA) within the first 9 months of 2019. The increase of incidents was also reflected in the increase of victims in need for MHPSS ER, within the first 3 quarters of 2019 the number of victims who received MHPSS ER was 1,036 victims (440 Females, 381 males, 105 girls & 110 boys), compared to 1,752 (552 Females, 817 males, 185 girls & 198 boys) within the same period of 2020. Despite the COVID-19 pandemic, there was a clear increase of critical incidents since March 2020. The average number of incidents within the first 3 months of 2020, was 18, meanwhile it was increased to 35 incidents per month following the pandemic. The data analysis of MdM for the period from January to September 2020 showed that Nablus Governorate is still the most affected by incidents (120 incidents), followed by Salfit (58) then by Tubas -Northern Jordan Valley (41), then by Ramallah (33), and Qalqilya (30). In a context of an escalation of critical events, the provision of MHPSS ER is crucial to both minimize the acute impact and to strengthen resilience of communities. A key in organizing MHPSS is to develop a layered system of complementary actions that meets the needs of different groups. Based on IASC guidelines, the basic services and safety are the first layer of MHPSS interventions, meanwhile the MHPSS interventions come as a second layer. The well-being of victims impacted by critical incidents should be protected through both addressing the basic protection and MHPSS needs, which are equally important. Based on the MdM data analysis done between January and September 2020, anxiety was the most common symptom within adult males and boys, trauma (flashbacks, avoidance and intrusive feelings) symptoms were most common among adult females, sleeping problems and insecurity were the most common symptoms reported among girls. Basic and protection needs were clear, 16% (292 /1,752) of beneficiaries (85 males, 100 females, 46 boys & 61 girls) were referred to protection services, 69% were referred for financial support, 20% for livelihood , 10% to agricultural and 1% to legal. 94 victims (31 females, 13 males, 29 girls & 21 boys) were referred to advanced MHPSS as they were at risk of developing MHPSS Disorders. Among these numbers, recently the team in the field are detecting an increasing need in terms of response capacity to GBV cases.Marginalized women and girls in Area C remain more vulnerable during the COVID-19 health emergency due to their lack of access to early-warning systems as well as limited access to technologies and health services. In total, these challenges generate complex protection threats for women and girls, including heightened risks of GBV, intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and forced and early marriage (UN WOMEN-Gender alert). Addressing those needs, MdM team will ensure a safe referral system and a close follow for GBV, Child Protection and People With Disabilities cases identified with MdM action. MHPSS ER is important to mitigate legitimate distress and diminish the chances to develop severe disorders and it is a key intervention in the Palestinian context. Insufficiently skilled MH staff at primary health care level, together with the reluctance of the individuals to seek advanced services mainly for stigma, but also due to economic reasons and geographical access, make necessary to reinforce MHPSS services at community level through innovative approaches, such the capacity building of community key actors and school counselors. Following the evaluation conducted at the beginning of 2020 by MdM and its partner, the Palestinian Counseling Center (PCC), along with the Ministry of Education Higher Education (MoEHE) PSS teams supported by MdM, school counselors reflected the importance of the continuation of technical support, supervision and accompaniment by MdM and PCC, enabling them to continue the provision of PSS support within their school environment. Moreover, and due to the increase of GBV and Child Protection needs following the COVID-19 pandemic, child protection actors including school counsellors and other CBOs and NGOs staff working in MdM targeted area will benefit from stress management sessions enabling them for a proper continuation of their services. In complement to the above actions which aim to treat moderate psychological problems, other complementary activities aiming to strengthen the resilience and coping skills of individuals and communities are needed. Psycho-education sessions play an important role, as there is still lack of information and knowledge about how critical incidents, sexual and gender-based violence, a limited access to education, and disabilities, have a heavy impact on mental health and daily functioning of individuals. As reported by Bedouin and herder community of the North WB, 84% reported stress and fear from the COVID-19 pandemic. To alleviate this stress and to limit the spread of the virus, MdM will integrate the MHPSS support and awareness related to COVID-19 within its intervention.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Increased access to immediate MHPSS and to a holistic protection response through case management for the victims of critical events. The objective is to mitigate the psychological and psycho-social impacts of the critical incidents on individuals, families and communities. These cases are identified by MdM from different sources including direct contact by the community, OCHA, Child Protection /MHPSS AOR and other Protection actors. The main activities will be: (1) Support visit including PFA within 72 hours. (2) 2nd Support visit including a Psychosocial Assessment within 10-15 days. (3) Basic and protection needs, child protection, PWDs GBV needs will be assessed, referred, documented and followed through the case management approach. (4) Low intensity psychological interventions for individuals affected by mild psychological problems such as anxiety and/or depression are directly provided by MdM specialized team (5) Referrals to MH specialized services of detected mental health conditions as moderate, severe depression, PTSD, suicide attempt in need of advanced support to MHPSS working group actors and MoH according to geographical services in the area. Within all the above-mentioned activities, MHPSS and awareness raising related to COVID-19 will be mainstreamed, MdM team will provide awareness and facts about the pandemic and address all the questions of community members related to the virus and orient them towards supportive positive coping mechanisms. All COVID-19 interventions will be documented and reported through the COVID-19 5Ws Dashboard. Output 2: Increased Resilience capacity of the communities through Community Psychoeducation and MHPSS preparedness at community level: (1) Family/household-based Psychoeducation: the main aim is to make the communities aware about the impact of traumatic events on their MHPSS wellbeing and mental health as well as the resources and services available to alleviate it. These sessions usually address critical incidents impacts on women, men, girls and boys and the potential ways on dealing with it. Moreover, awareness will be raised and mainstreamed on MH, Sexual and Gender Based Violence, child protection related issues, and specific needs of PWDs and more specifically in this time of epidemics. Specific cases will be assessed within MHPSS sessions, identified and refer accordingly. In order to assure an universal access to MH support, and the proved difficulty in reach men, “safe” focus groups will be organized in the field, involving communities’ key men in order to assess their needs and shape together with them the more adapted MH response. (2) MHPSS Preparedness at community level: the objective is to reinforce the community emergency response capacity, through school emergency PSS teams and in mapping reliable MHPSS community actors. MdM and its strategic partner PCC will reinforce the capacities of 6 Schools MoEHE mobile PSS teams to respond to critical incidents in the NWB and to identify and refer safely GBV, child protection and PWD cases. This activity will include the capacitation of MoEHE teams on MHPSS emergency provision, their supervision, accompany and support with material needs. PCC will be in charge of the technical supervision of the school counselors’ supervisors, the accompany of the PSS teams and will participate in the development of specific intervention protocols Moreover, MdM will map MH reliable community actors, provide capacitation on provision of in person and remote PFA, GBV, child protection and PWD cases’ needs of specialized support , structure and assure a safe beneficiaries’ referral pathway to MdM secondary line MHPSS support. Stress management sessions will be provided to the MoEHE and local protection actors operational in MdM targeted area. Local protection actors will be identified in cooperation with the CP/MHPSS AOR, a specific assesment will be conducted with the partners staff before the implementation of this activity. Output 3: Advocacy initiatives that aim to enhance the accountability for violations of HRL & IHL and the engagement of Third States Parties. MdM collects quantitative and qualitative information to raise awareness of public opinion and mobilize EU decision makers to effectively act according HRL and IHL standards. The activities will include: (1) Production of MdM own documentation, reports and testimonies about the impact of critical events on MHPSS wellbeing, awareness and access. (2) Contribution to joint reports (Settlement expansion and Practices Group, AIDA, UN mechanisms). (3) Organization of field tours and contributions to decision-makers briefing. (4) Lobby tours in Paris and Brussels (if there is continued movement restrictions due to COVID-19 crisis, creation of digital content and platforms for online engagement and knowledge sharing).

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by the MEAL officer who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular feedback / complaint and sensitive feedback. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint which relates to program design and implementation/service delivery, such as changes in location of provided service, working hours of MdM outreach program/service, waiting time for receiving the services, unavailability of certain service, inclusion of a certain service, etc. Sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse of authority by MdM staff, derogatory/immoral behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal abuse/physical assault, discrimination of beneficiaries based on race, gender, sexuality, creed or religion, harassment of beneficiary or any act that might harm MdM’s reputation, financial corruption, misuse/fraud of money or material, child abuse and neglect, counterfeiting, threats, blackmail. Based on that will take up the feedback to coordination level, if feedback is considered relevant according to MdM criteria, MdM will proceed with further actions, a review ethical committee will be formed (in which members of the community involved will take part) to analyze the case and make decisions. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. The user(s) who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms (letter). All complaints will be registered anonymously by the MEAL officer in a specific data base, periodic analysis of these feedbacks and complaints will be done and will be shared with MdM coordination and headquarters team to be taken into consideration within program and strategy.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MdM team providing the MHPSS ER to victims and vulnerable community members is a well-trained, supervised and gender balanced team, who are updated about the cultural, religious, context and security situation in MdM operational area of coverage including towns, villages and Bedouin communities. MdM interventions are provided through outreach to the households of the victims that reduce the exposure of the victims to harm and provide equal access including the access to people with disabilities. MdM security department and team are continuously assessing the locations of the activities conducted in the field to ensure safety and security of beneficiaries and MdM team. When MdM team receives a trigger of critical incident from OCHA, Protection cluster, community members, etc, the team ensure gathering the proper and enough information about the incident from different sources to avoid the repetition of the story by the victims and avoid further exposure to trauma, furthermore MdM team is coordinating the activities with the village councils and community leaders and cooperate with the needed stakeholders to avoid community tension. Confidentiality and privacy of MHPSS interventions is a crucial principle applied by all MdM team, within their interviews, data and information collected and shared even when referring beneficiaries to other services, a clear referral protocol developed by MdM and MOH in 2015 is used for that. MdM is always ensuring that individual sessions are conducted in privacy, even from same family members. MdM is using digital data collection tools and using a barcode system to anonymize the data collected and ensure beneficiaries data is protected. MdM MHPSS team is programing the interventions based on gender needs, experienced female team members to intervene with women and girls and experienced male members to intervene with males and boys, moreover they are reaching community members in appropriate time, intervening with women within the morning hours, with children after the school time and with men within the evenings and the weekends when needed.Within its case studies and advocacy documentation, MdM is keeping the publications anonymous to avoid the identification of cases, written consent is received from the beneficiaries for use of testimonies in published materials (even when remaining anonymous). Within its psychoeducation activities, MdM is reinforcing the local community’s positive coping mechanisms and mobilizing the community resources towards community protection. MdM is counting with a functional feedback and complaint mechanisms and working closely with beneficiaries and community representatives involving them in the design, implementation and monitoring of the action. MdM team is sharing the protection concerns of the individual and communities targeted within its interventions with the relevant protection actors, and in close coordination and cooperation with Protection cluster. Gender based violence and child protection are mainstreamed within MdM outreach, in addition MdM is ensuring all of its staff are sensitized and oriented on our policy on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, sexual harassment, moral harassment and abuse of authority.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender and inclusion are adopted by MdM within its intervention in Palestine. Within its intervention, when MdM receive a trigger about a critical incident, MdM team gather all the related information about the community members affected, communicate with the beneficiaries and set an appointment for the intervention. Female members of MdM team intervene with females and girls and male members intervene with males and boys, respecting the culture of traditions of the local community. Within its psycho-education activities, MdM is conducting separated sessions for males and females reaching them by having 2 team members attend each household, 1 female team member to work with females and 1 male team member to work with males, the content of the session is adapted based on the age and gender of the targeted community members visited in their households. Within 2021, MdM will reinforce the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff on gender, GBV and inclusion of PWD. A specific SOP was developed to address the needs of GBV and PWD cases identified and ensure proper referral, follow up and documentation of these cases. Moreover, identification of child protection, neglect and abuse was reinforced within this intervention, a clear referral system for child protection is established with the Child Protection Networks and other NGOs under the umbrella of the CP/MHPSS AOR. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based upon a gender-based approach (gender specific health), for instance, developing different IEC for women, men and children, developing different protocols based upon gender and also addressing all the barriers preventing PWD to access services. By its outreach program MdM is able to reach the majority of the victims affected by the critical events, by the support provided by a well-prepared logistic department, MdM team is able to reach the scattered Bedouin communities, farmers and shepherds in their communities and place of work. To ensure basic and protection needs, MdM is using several referral approaches through the Protection cluster and its working groups, by liaising with the local authorities or by mobilizing the community resources in cooperation with CBOs and village councils, ensuring access of community members in need for such services. By having an outreach program, MdM MHPSS emergency response team is targeting and reaching victims of critical incidents including PWD in their households and schools. PWD are referred with a closer follow up to basic and protection services in coordination with operational protection actors under the umbrella of the Protection Cluster, the Ministry of Social Development and other local authorities. MdM MEAL process is taken into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities and targeted locations, all beneficiaries referred to any external service are documented and tracked accordingly, by 2021, MdM will add PWD to all its monitoring, evaluation and reporting process.
