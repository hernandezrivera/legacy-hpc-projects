Needs

Although there are slight improvements in humanitarian support in Banadir region needs persist. Specially in IDPs in Banadir Malnutrition rates remain high, especially amongst IDPs. Some 294,000 children are acutely malnourished, including 55,000 who are severely malnourished. Currently 5.4 M people are in need Health Services across the country while 2.6 M people are displaced. Acute Watery Diarrhea/Cholera cases are on the rise due the floods that have affected many parts of the country such as Beletweyn, Balcad and other riverine areas in June 2018 alone 1,243 cases of AWD/Cholera including 13 deaths were reported. 5,582 cumulative AWD/Cholera cases including 40 deaths (CFR 0.7%) were reported from 23 districts since December 2017, Islamic relief is one of the partners that have been working on the health sector for the last 10 years in Banadir and Middle Shabelle. The food security outlook remains particularly worrying among rural populations in the north-eastern parts of the country, which received below-average rainfall during the last Gu rainy season. Mogadishu remains a home of several IDPs most of whom conflict, violence, human rights violations and natural disasters (drought) that compelled them into displacement. These are part of an estimated 1.1 million Somalis – approximately a tenth of the population – who are internally displaced. Most internally displaced persons (IDPs) continue to live in dire conditions in protracted displacement. Whereas they strive to live, prospects for durable solutions remain uncertain. There is a severe shortage of health manpower and supplies let alone public facilities in most parts of the country. Health services are essential in combating the outbreak of epidemic and infectious diseases. The protracted crises and political instability in Somalia has disrupted the health systems leading to Gross under performance. Persons displaced by the floods, drought and conflict are living in temporary IDP camps across the country

Activities

- Provision of primary health care services through health facilities (Balcad, Daynille and Bondhere). - Provision of primary health care services through mobile outreaches for IDPs. - Provision of essential medicines and equipment. - Secondary care via hospitals and referral health centers for EMOC. - Facilitate complicated and serious case including MCH/ pregnancy cases through referral system. - Identify and training community health workers. - Conduct health awareness raising sessions on health related issues through community health workers. - Screening of children for malnutrition and refer/link the severe/acute malnourished children to the service providers. - Developing, printing and distribute culturally appropriate health IEC material. - Close coordination with the relevant government and humanitarian actors through cluster/coordination meeting - Sharing updates with government and Cluster- Strengthening of health information system.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The programme approach and strategy are designed in a way that is intended to mainstream sustainability in all activities and to build the capacity of institutions at all levels including community-level so that the benefits can continue after the defined programme lifespan. Capacity development and training activities are specifically designed to increase individual and collective self-sufficiency in a way that will drive local ownership. This will be backed up through systems strengthening to develop the management information and management capacities of state and community institutions, so that they can function effectively and independently of the programme. Furthermore, accountability mechanisms that are created will help to ensure that the delivery of basic health services continues to be accountable to communities and that there is effective engagement between community management structures and government organisations at all levels. Care will be given that information, training and knowledge can be passed down through organisations and government structures to mitigate the risk of outcomes being lost through turnover of staff. Central to the sustainability strategy is the support to the development and training of community health workers to act as representatives of communities to government bodies. Training of these will ensure that they are able to operate effectively and independently following the programme and will have an on-going effect on communities’ ability to engage in basic health service delivery. Specific programming activities in this workstream are also designed to encourage sustainability and enable a smooth transition. The development of effective management information systems across sectors will facilitate greater ownership of basic service delivery by woreda and regional organisations and training will be designed to ensure that this approach is followed. The programme will plan and hold meetings at an agreed point with stakeholders from the government and communities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Since the project is implemented in insecure area there are security and safety constraints including physical and mental health of staffs and partners which may affect the pace of implementation. IR Somalia has risk Management Manual which outlines the processes and procedures that will be applied to identify and mitigate programme risks. The manual outlines the programmatic approach to the identification and management of all risks affecting the programme and confirms the necessary roles and responsibilities. The manual is to be used alongside the Programme Risk Log, a live document which documents risks, assesses their risk rating, outlines appropriate mitigating actions, and assigns owners. The identification and reporting of risks begins at the workstream level and potential risks will be filtered and escalated upwards through programme management level to steering committee level dependent upon the severity of the risk and the agreed escalation principles. Through using the manual and the risk log, risks are tracked and can be reported to internal and external programme stakeholders. The risk management process is summarized below:• Weekly meetings – weekly programme management meetings will be held. Risks of high likelihood or impact will be verbally reported in the meeting and updates given on any mitigating actions taken since the last update. • Monthly reports –the PMT will produce internal monthly reports to include progress updates. Appended to this will be the programme risk log and dashboard, whilst any ‘extreme’ or ‘very high’ rated risks will be included in the main report. • Quarterly reports –the Programme Risk Log will be appended to the quarterly reports. The quarterly reports will include more detailed information relating to progress against programme work plans and risks and issues arising. The report will summarize where problems either have arisen or could potentially arise.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

During the implementation of the projects Women’s involvement will be strongly encouraged through targeting a 50% component of female members to join Water Management Committees as well as Community Hygiene Promoters. This will ensure that women benefit from WASH related capacity building and play an active role in program activities. Women will be heavily involved in hygiene sensitization activities and water point management to improve local water resources and to increase women’s participation in public decision-making. The projects will have a higher female representation so as to reach a greater number of women and children, who have major roles in hygiene and sanitation at household level. The composition of hygiene kits to be distributed in the framework of the WASH intervention will be gender sensitive and will address women-specific hygiene needs. When distributing hygiene kits, female-headed households will be majorly targeted as they are tasked with collection of water and storage. Community leaders and local administration will be sensitized on inclusion of the most vulnerable groups including women, minority groups, pregnant and lactating women, female-headed households, older people and other special needs groups locally identified with communities. A complaints response mechanism will enable women to register complaints privately by phone. Islamic Relief has directly employed several women as community mobilizers; this trend will be encouraged by recruiting more women as staff members/community mobilizers as the security situation allows.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Collateral exposure to different forms of violence for IR staff (armed clashes, explosive devices, etc.). IR has been operational in the target area and understand the different dynamics. IR has security procedures that all staff strictly adhere to mitigate the risks. IR will conduct thorough analysis and plans which will help to avoid proximity to conflict actors, assess possible causes of conflict events and ensure proper risk analysis is made. IR will keep communication with other actors in the area and regularly attend INSO briefings and will closely liaise with government for access and getting the latest security updates.Also, allocation of resources and beneficiary selection could be another source of conflict amongst communities and may trigger potential security risk. IR will ensure that beneficiaries section is made through transparent vetting system. IR will also ensure a sound and transparent management of resources is in place by following standard procedures. This will mitigate any potential conflict that might have been triggered by beneficiary selection etc. by doing this the local communities, community leaders and local authorities will understand the fair process and ensures acceptance by the different stakeholders.
