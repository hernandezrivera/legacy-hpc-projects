Project Summary

In 2021, Action Against Hunger will continue to serve the information and communication needs through its CwC information centers, address existing gaps and ensure that the voices of the refugees and host communities are taken into considerations in program design, implementations, and decision-making process. The information centers will continue to raise awareness on COVID-19, nutrition, health, livelihoods, and disaster risk reduction, mental health, gender, gender-based violence, protection, and SRH issues to ensure the overall wellbeing of the targeted populations.
Action Against Hunger’s partner BBC Media Action will implement the Common Service component of this proposal, which will focus on community engagement and accountability through key areas of research and development of knowledge products, training and mentoring as well as the production of content and tools to support improved community engagement to the Rohingya and host communities. The common service will support community-based organizations with a focus on vulnerable populations including women, youth, older people, and people living with disabilities, key influencers within the community who people go to for information including Imams, Majhis, and government officials. Key themes will include improving people – especially women’s ability to provide feedback, health and Nutrition, rights, peaceful coexistence, and social cohesion as well as health as it relates to the Covid19 pandemic.

1 - Identified Needs

Since 2018, the information centers/hubs have been working as a catalyst in the humanitarian response to the Rohingya refugee crisis by ensuring that people had access to vital information and communication resources, enabling space for people to share their inputs, feedback, and lodge complaints. JRP 2019 midterm review reported that 92% of refugees say they have adequate information to make decisions about their daily lives, compared to 23% in 2017 and 84% in mid 2018. This is an impressive progress where the CwC info hubs have played a vital role as part of the response. However, information needs are constantly changing due to changing contexts- repatriation/relocation issues, monsoon, AWD outbreak, COVID19 pandemic etc. ISCG’s Rapid Gender Analysis (May & Oct 2020) reported that the COVID19 crisis has further exacerbated the pre-existing socio-cultural gender norms with negative consequences for women and girls. Access to information also varies topic wise but some groups –esp. women, girls, elderly and people with disabilities continue to be left behind. These groups continue to face challenges in both host and refugee population and rely on neighborhood based approach. Whereas, men and boys prefer information dissemination via loudspeakers and public meetings. Considering varying levels of literacy, comprehension skills and other barriers to access information, Action Against Hunger (ACF) will develop context-specific, user-friendly pre-tested audio-visual contents and disseminate vital messages through center and neighborhood based activities. Alongside, ACF's strategically positioned Info Hubs within Nutrition and other program integrated centers will allow both direct and indirect beneficiaries, particularly PLWs, to get services from info hubs. ACF’s CwC program works in line with AAP principles and ensures active and meaningful 2 way communication between humanitarian actors and target group. To address tension between refugee and host communities, ACF will implement its community engagement activities including behavior change awareness, feedback sessions, to address low levels of social cohesion between the communities and promote peaceful coexistence within and between the two communities. In addition to accountability mechanisms, ACF will use participatory community engagement to raise awareness on COVID19, nutrition, health, livelihoods and actions for disaster risk reduction (DRR), mental health, gender based violence, protection and SRH issues to ensure wellbeing of the targeted group. Data collected from these sessions will be consolidated and shared with CwC Working Group (WG) to support the sectors’ decision-making. The J-MSNA found that protection and security concerns have increased during COVID19 pandemic and households headed by women were found to be at more risk of such issues. Many refugees reported of not knowing the contact-points. In 2021, ACF will strengthen its inter-agency reference mechanism to ensure that refugees are aware of community-level first-points-of-contact to seek support when they encounter any protection/security concern.This project will undertake and contribute to research activities to understand evolving needs, preferences and delivery of humanitarian assistance to different age groups. As ACF runs multi-sectoral response, such research will strengthen integrated programming and gender and age specific response. ACF will consult with CwC WG members during the research procedure to maximize the quality and impact. In 2021, ACF will continue to implement environment-friendly actions -manage data digitally to reduce use of paper, use solar-energy, and promote eco-friendly ideas in awareness sessions. ACF and its partners will continue to serve information needs through its CwC info hubs, address existing gaps and ensure that voices of refugees and host communities are taken into consideration in program design, implementation and decision-making process.
