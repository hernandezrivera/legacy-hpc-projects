Project Summary

The goal is to improve host communities and refugees living condition by developing alternative construction materials and building components that will provide effective, cost efficient, affordable, and easy to use building options to improve current infrastructure in both contexts whilst minimizing maintenance requirements. HFHB will investigate the possibility to implement Shelter activities in the camps with a national or local NGO.
20 Rohingya refugees and residents from the host communities will be trained at the Shelter Lab and 20 shelters pilots will be built using building components designed and produced at the Lab. HFHB is planning to build the pilots in Ratna and Raja Palong (Ukhiya Upazila) as it already works in these two areas. Local authorities (UNO) are already informed of the project. HFHB will conduct household assessments in the two Unions to identify the families the most in need and get their consent to receive a new home after explaining the design and the materials used.
In addition, 750 MTS shelters will be built in camp 19 and 250 will be improved in Ratna and Raja Palong host communities. Construction and maintenance training will be provided to a member of each household (750 refugees and 250 members of the host community) in order to build up construction skills and ownership.
With the support of a local organization, 1,500 emergency shelter kits will be stored and distributed in camps and host communities following natural and manmade disasters.
HFHB will ensure to actively participate in the SCCCM meetings and will keep the sector inform of the activities implemented or planned by completing on time the 4W and other coordination tools. HFHB will suggest to set-up a task force specifically dedicated to the development of alternative construction materials, for the Shelter lab to provide regular update and brainstorm ideas with partners. Consultation and coordination in the field (camps and host community) will be ensure by HFHB filed team and a staff will be specifically assigned to maintain shelter focal points in camps and local authorities (UNO & CIC) of the evolution of the activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Shelters in the camps and host communities are exposed to monsoon rains and face high risks of floods, landslides, fire and cyclones. Despite improvements in shelter design (bamboo posts raised up from the ground on steel footings) and the use of some better-quality material (treated borak bamboo), due to conditions in the refugee camps and surrounding communities, weathering and the lack of quality materials and disaster resilient building techniques, shelters have still a very limited capacity to resist these events and consequently require regular maintenance and replacement. Current shelter assistance approaches are limited and neither effective nor cost efficient due to frequent repair, replacement needs and costs. According to the SCCCM 4W dashboard, as of July 2023, only 21% of the JRP 2023 refugee households target, have been reached with shelter assistance. The durability of the tarpaulins and bamboo used in the construction of the shelters in the camps is very limited. In addition, both materials are extremely inflammable. The Shelter and NFI strategy 2023-2025 (IOM Sept 2022) "support diverse shelter interventions to avoid depletion of local bamboo reserves..." and recommend the preposition of ESK to respond to disasters (flood, fire, cyclone and landslide). With the decrease of funding toward the Rohingya refugee crisis and the reduction of organizations' operations, there are urgent needs in finding alternative and more sustainable construction materials and building components.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Shelter design and materials provided by HFHB aim to enhance the safety and security of families living in the camps and host communities by increasing the sustainability and weathering resistance of the shelters. Materials will be sourced from registered suppliers and their origin will be verified to ensure there are sustainably farmed. Local communities will be involved at every steps of the program, from the design to the handover. Labor from the community and vulnerable groups will be recruited and trained for different roles throughout the implementaiton of the activities(unskilled labor, carpenters, assessors...).HFHB will host consultations with vulnerable groups to identify specific needs and develop innovative and protection sensitive building designs, production standards and processes (safe spaces, infinity, etc…). HFHB will ensure that the construction team and other team members involved in the process are trained on the basics of GBV and PSEA.HFHB will consider time, location and access for shelter materials distributions. In addition, HFHB will give special consideration to all women or households with many women and girls to ensure the mitigation of GBV risks.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In the short term (12 months) the project is planning to support and work with the youth and persons with disabilities. They will be recruited, trained and involved in the development and production of building components at the workshops and during the repair or reconstrucito nof shelters in camps. In the long term (>12 months), when shelters will be repaired or constructed at a larger scale, youth, women and LGBTQ will be consulted and involved in the design and construction of the shelters. Shelter support will given in priority to the most vulnerable groups.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

There will be series of consultations with local authorities, the private sector and communities to identify construction habits and challenges. Materials and designs will be identified with all stakeholders throughout the development process of building components and pilots. HFHB will ensure consistency in the items distributing in the shelter kits and that the shelter designs built are compliant with sector and Government specifications. Committees will be set-up within the communities benefiting from HFHB assistance to give the opportunity to the beneficiaries to play an active role in the activities implemented within their community. HFHB will conduct participatory shelter and building component designs exercises within the communities to demonstrate purpose of the designs and collect feedback. The beneficiary selection process will go through multiple steps including meeting in the communities, with residents and community leaders. The selection methodology and criteria will be explained. Complaint mechanisms will be set-up and explained to the community. Communication will be maintained with community committees and local authorities. If any changes occur in the project, the information will be shared immediately. Awareness sessions will be conducted in the communities where HFHB activities are implemented. The session will take place at the beginning of the project to inform the communities of the type of activities, budget, timeline, donors, selection criteria…HFHB staff will be trained on accountability and will be in charge of informing local authorities of the HFHB interventions. HFHB will listen to and record beneficiary initiatives and comments on HFHB interventions by designing monitoring tools for community mobilizer to collect feedback.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

HFHB will ensure to create and maintain a safe organizational culture for those who work for and with HFHB, as well as vendors and communities where HFHB implement activities. HFHB will put in place strong prevention and response work, offering support to survivor and holding persons responsible for committing sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse.HFHB will use safe and accessible channels for employees, related personnel, vendors, beneficiaries and others to safely report sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse. All stakeholders will have the necessary information about how to access safe reporting. HFHB will provide training to staff and related personnel for receiving complaints to ensure they understand their obligations. Emphasis will be made on confidentiality.HFHB will respond to all concerns or allegations by conducting independent investigations, ensure proper recording and management of cases, take appropriate actions against employees or related personnel and provide support to survivor.PSEA will be integrated in HFHB programs through recruitment, by conducting background check and request ISCG support. HFHB PSEA policy will be integrated in staff contract. Hence, by signing a contract, staff will commit to comply with the policy. Orientation/training on policy, reporting and response will be provided to newly recruited staff. In addition, refresher training will be organized throughout the project timeline. HFHB employees will be supported to take pro-active programming measures to increase awareness and maintain a safe environment.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes.Materials and designs that will be developed at the Shelter Lab will be tested against fire and water. Hence, the shelters built with these materials will be flood and fire resistant and the shelter structure will be studied and developed to resist strong winds.
