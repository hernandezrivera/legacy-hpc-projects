Project Summary

The crisis currently affecting the Lake Chad Basin states results from a complex combination of factors, including conflict with Non-State Armed Groups, extreme poverty, underdevelopment and a changing climate, which together have triggered significant displacement of populations. Conflict over natural resources in the Far North has intensified. In August 2021, intercommunal clashes in Logone-Birni (Logone et Chari) over access to water displaced over 21,000 people, including almost 9,000 who sought refuge in Chad.
In the year 2022, 4 million people in Cameroon will need humanitarian assistance, 1.2 million people are in the Far North, and 477,000 people in relation to the CAR refugee crisis. Another 200,000 people will need humanitarian assistance due to the impact of COVID-19 and other epidemics. A total of and 776,000 people are targeted in the Far North (63 per cent of the PIN).11
The NRC programme will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, households who need shelter assistance to improve their living conditions in the project implementation locations. Particular attention will be given to households that meet the following vulnerability criteria:
• IDPs and returnees living in makeshift camps with shelters exposing them to bad weather;
• IDPs and returnees living in host communities where they scramble for the very limited space available and share sleeping spaces;
• IDPs, Primary, Secondary Multiple Displacements and vulnerable Host community members
• Returnees (former IDPs), where houses have been vandalised or destroyed due to the crisis;
• Schools in conflict affected areas that are used as collective housing by the displaced communities.
Target group
Beneficiary targeting will also aim at reaching the most vulnerable households based on agreed upon selection criteria. Priority will be given to female headed households (HHs), unaccompanied minors and child headed HHs, and HHs with disabled members. Having clear beneficiary selection criteria will ensure that deserving beneficiaries have equitable access to NRC’s shelter assistance, and hence mitigate the risk of tensions if the assistance is solely addressed to a particular group.
The Shelter response in the Far North aims to reach a total of 2,857 Households, some households will benefit from different services at the same time hence the households are with double counting. In Total, the shelter response is expected to reach a total of 20,000 (9,800 Male 10,200 Females) persons.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Shelter and NFI The shelter infrastructure in the Far North region remains insufficient to meet the needs of the displaced populations (IDPs and returnees) due to either having been destroyed during conflict or poorly maintained. Majority of the affected population do not have legal documents (with majority of the affected population having lost their documents when fleeing) proving that they are the owners of these properties. This makes it difficult for them to claim ownership to the property and thus they find themselves unjustly thrown out of their properties. This is particularly the case for women. Lake Chad crisis in the Far North region:Overall, 607,252 people are displaced in the Far North as of 30 September 2021: 357,631 IDPs (IOM, DTM, July 2021); 114,364 Nigerian refugees (UNHCR, September 2021); 135,257 returnees (IOM, DTM, July 2021). Rehabilitation and construction of new facilities is required for displacement affected populations to have access to adequate safe and protected social services. The locations where the IDPs have fled for safety include areas where they are living with relatives and friends in homes whose space is insufficient to provide the needed separation and privacy between the different ages and sex. Social services and infrastructure in the Far North region are insufficient to meet the needs of the displaced populations (IDPs, returnees and Vulnerable host community members) due to either having been destroyed during conflict or poorly maintained. The attacks and lootings by BH in the region have also led to the destruction of houses and property worsening the suffering of the most vulnerable populations. Due to insecurity, populations, whose main source of income is agriculture and livestock, have left their homes seeking refuge, living in informal settings with makeshift undignified shelters, and traveling long distances in search of water, increasing the risk of exploitation and no longer having access to land and livelihood opportunities. Local host communities have little capacity to support returnees and IDPs – the Far North is Cameroon’s poorest and most fragile region, with 74% of the population living below the national poverty line. Host communities have also seen their livelihoods and access to basic services severely impacted by the current crisis. Displaced populations have also either lost their identification documents during displacement or members of BH seized and destroyed them, leading to movement restrictions out of fear of police controls.
