Project Summary

IOM leads the CwC Sector, serves on the Strategic Advisory Group, and implements a comprehensive set of CwC activities across all IOM Site Management Camps directly and through partners. In coordination with IOM Site Management, dedicated outreach and community engagement teams routinely hold meetings with key stakeholders, runs audio-listening programs, conduct awareness raising sessions, holds consultations, and operate a CFM system from dedciated Feedback and Information Centres. These activities are all designed and implemented with active participation and inclusion of different Rohingya populations and have been redesigned to improve their impact and overall effectiveness. IOM’s community engagement volunteers routinely conduct outreach sessions and community meetings with different community leaders and stakeholders on a range of health, DRR, WASH, Site Management and protection related needs. Women, girls, boys and disabled populations’ information and particular needs are met through community outreach teams, who ensure their feedback is entered in the system, and are targeted with specific and gender, age, and disability appropriate information sessions. Regular consultations about different needs and issues within the Rohingya population are conducted and disseminated on a regular basis in accordance with AAP best practices to ensure affected populations voices are included at all stages of relief delivery. IOM also ensures commitment to the Accountability commitments of the Grand Bargain through a harmonized CFM system that provides routine analysis and overview of community feedback to different actors and sectors within the response. In 2020, IOM will bring on-board other Site Management partners into a harmonized CFM system and work to support the CwC Sector in developing standardized referral pathways and data fields for community feedback within each sector in order to improve overall accountability and operational effectiveness of responding to community feedback.
This work will coincide and support facility monitoring and consolidation of services according to the Macro Site Development plan; IOM will work closely to support and coordinate CwC services and activities across the IOM AoR. IOM’s JRP plan was prepared in close coordination with the Government of Bangladesh and the RRRC. Regular meetings and consultation are held with all relevant Government stakeholders on a routine basis. Activities in the field are conducted in close coordination will all relevant camp level actors and IOM’s CwC program is conducted through and alongside IOM’s Site Management coordination and Site operations programming. IOM operates these activities in a harmonized approach in coordination with other IOM programming units covering a range of sectors and key issues, including health, disaster preparedness, site management, and WASH. In the event of reduced JRP funding, IOM will continue to implement standard CwC activities and funding but will reduce scope of its activities and number of field staff and volunteers implementing activities.

Objectives

'SO1) Reinforce coordination, advocacy and technical support across different sectors and stakeholders for mainstreaming CwC and AAP principles enabling affected peoples to have access to life-saving information and knowledge on rights and services.SO2) Further improve access to information – with a focus on currently underserved areas and groups – through rationalised, community-centric and evidence-based communication and community engagement approaches. SO3) Improve participation of and accountability to affected people through adoption of minimum standards for the referral and resolution of community feedback; and through increased use collective data analysis.

Needs

Rohingya affected person’s living in displacement camps of Cox’s Bazar have a range of information needs based related to their displacement, current context, and future durable solutions. The July 2019 Light MSNA, 21% of Rohingya still report barriers to communication due to language differences and reliance on humanitarian jargon. There is also a continued lack of understanding and focus on achieving specific learning outcomes of outreach and awareness raising activities, with many Rohingya in the camps being unable to fully comprehend all key messages related to disaster preparedness, health, and WASH related messaging. Rohingya are in need of better engagement strategies to accurately ensure that important life saving messages are conveyed in detail and comprehended by affected persons. There are a range of challenges related to the achievement of these objectives, from coordination between actors, failure to test messaging prior to dissemination with different sex & age groups within the Rohingya population, and weak facilitation skills of outreach volunteers and staff. IOM’s internal assessment highlights the continued need for tailored engagement and CwC interventions based on participatory approaches to ensure Rohingya’s inclusion in all aspects of the delivery of humanitarian relief. There is also a lack of detailed social research on cultural communication practices, community structures, and religious traditions to inform the wider response and ensure and inform consultations and community engagement programs. Consultations with different Rohingya stakeholders is greatly hampered by a lack of representational structures, and consultations need to be conducted with respect to the unique needs and perspectives of refugees based on their gender, age, ability, and place of origin. The humanitarian response for the Rohingya involves a range of different actors working across multiple sectors in order to provide services; many of whom routinely collect community feedback and conduct consultations. However, agencies and sectors have failed to harmonize these approaches. As a result, referral of community feedback and response is not systematized and responses to the community are weaker as a result. Despite strong commitments to accountability to affected populations within the Grand Bargain, there are no harmonized or established operational principals surrounding the handling and response to community feedback between agencies.

Activities or outputs

Audio/Podcast/narrowcast program disseminated & Listener group session conducted – IOM will conduct listener group sessions through its new Aa’rar Hal’Hobor format. This listening format invites Rohingya to sessions to select information generated by a range of audio content developers that best matches their needs through voting. At the end of the session, discussions are held on the content to reinforce learning and comprehension. All programs are approved by the CwC Working group and Government of Bangladesh for dissemination. During critical periods, IOM also disseminates important response-wide messaging related to disasters and potential durable solutions for Rohingya. Help Desk (permanent/static) operated & Feedback/Complaints received – IOM operates along with partners feedback and information centres and routinely receive community feedback in all IOM AoR camps. IOM also collects community feedback through door-to-door outreach activities. IOM is engaged in the creation of standardized referral pathways for community feedback in a joint led Technical Working Group between the CwC and Site Management sectors. In 2020, IOM will work to harmonize and standardize reporting and referral of all community feedback through these established channels across IOM AoR. It will also share IM outputs highlighting trends and received community feedback across all these areas. IOM operates it's Help Desks for any refugee or host community member who wishes to use their servicesOther Surveys – IOM’s CwC unit will support and continue to hold routine consultations and format these into researches and reports to share findings and opinions of the Rohingya affected population with the wider response. These will be coordinated with needs of other IOM units and support other sectors response plans. Meeting conducted with Community level committees (existing/pre-formed) – IOM holds routine meetings with different camp level committees and leaders, including Imams, women, girls, boys, and men. These meetings serve to disseminate and share important information from Site Management related to camp operations, waste management, health, WASH, and disaster preparedness. During these meetings, feedback is also gathered from the community and streamlined in IOM’s new CFM system for referral to relevant stakeholders in each camp. Radio program developed – IOM will develop various audio programs for dissemination through its own listening groups. These programs will focus on stories from Rohingya about their own culture, history and backgrounds. Community Volunteers operational – IOM will retain a standing set of community volunteers from both Rohingya and Host community to engage in and conduct CwC activities described in this proposal. These volunteers will receive regular training and mentorship on community engagement and facilitation best practices in order to ensure accountability and inclusion of diverse voices in IOM’s programming.
