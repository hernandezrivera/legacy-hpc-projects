Project Summary

UNHCR figures from the December 2021 factsheet of CAR refugees in the Adamawa and East regions reveal that there are 281,898 refugees from CAR, of which 79,583 live in the camp of Borgop, Gado, Lolo, Mbile, Ngam, Ngarisingo and Timangolo with 12,499 children under 5 years of age living in these refugees camps. The large presence of refugees in these communities have aggravated the prevalent food insecurity situation among host communities where opportunities for income-generating activities, such as subsistence farming, livestock and small businesses are limited.
The project aims to Improve the nutritional status of affected populations through the strengthening of agri-food chains and local processors, communication for social and behavioral change (SBCC) in the Adamawa and East regions by Strengthening social and behavior change communication (SBCC) of beneficiary populations on appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF), maternal nutrition and other family practices to prevent all forms of malnutrition, promoting sustainable livelihoods by supporting farming and income generating activities groups in favor of the most vulnerable families to strengthen their self-reliance and support early recovery and Build local capacities for sustained implementation of complementary nutrition activities.
During the implementation period, the project will focus on the development of mechanisms allowing access to safe, adequate and nutritious food to crisis-affected populations that include refugees and the host population in East and Adamawa regions during and after crisis with an interest in prevention of the risks of GBV and on livelihood activities with the aim to strengthen their self-reliance and support early recovery. This will lead to building capacity of households in agricultural and livestock techniques, including setting up small businesses, which will enable them to meet their basic needs to achieve food self-sufficiency to contribute to the empowerment of these households and to reduce malnutrition at the community level.
Community health workers (CHWs) will mobilize the community for any nutrition education sessions so all members of the community, have the opportunity to participate, including community leaders, men, women, and adolescents (girls and boys). Furthermore, nutrition education will also be provided to PLWs who are visiting the health facilities either for treatment or for ANC or postnatal care. These education sessions will be organized for groups and individuals. IYCF counseling and skilled support for those with difficulties with breastfeeding and/or complementary feeding will be provided through the health facilities. Cooking demonstration will be conducted at community level based on local foods, tools and materials will be provided by the community.
IMC UK will facilitate the establishment and support of Community-Based Organizations (CBO). These CBOs including CHWs will be trained and involved in information dissemination, identification and addressing of malnutrition in the community. Through the Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA) and “Economic and Social Empowerment” (EA$E) curriculum, IMC UK will continue to build the capacity of women and girls in order to allow them to implement small-scale projects and to address food security among communities. These VSLA members will be train on the VSLA and EA$E approaches with the support of MINPROFF, MINADER and MINEPIA in order to help them to have the appropriate techniques.
Priority assistance will be given to households with children under 5 years and women living with disabilities.
Mass screenings will be conducted on a quarterly basis for all children aged 6-59 months and all children who are detected with severe acute malnutrition will be referred to the nearest CMAM program

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon has been significantly affected by three complex crises: armed conflict between non-state armed groups and state forces escalating in the Northwest and South-West regions; non-State armed groups (NSAGs) insurgency in the Lake Chad Basin resulting in an influx of refugees from Nigeria as well as internal displacements in the Far North Region and the influx of Central African Republic (CAR) refugees in the East, Adamawa, and North regions. These resulted in over 1 million internally displaced people and more than 449,000 refugees.In March 2022, WFP estimated in its country brief2 that, 40 percent of Cameroon’s 27.2 million people live below the poverty line, rising up to 52 percent for women. The human development index remains low (0.563 compared to a global average of 0.737 (UNDP, 2020) and Cameroon ranks 141 out of 189 countries in the Gender Inequality Index (UNDP, 2020) with a global gender gap index of 0.69. The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP March 2021) revealed that 4.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in Cameroon, with more than 2.4 million estimated to be food insecure between October and December 2021 (Cadre Harmonisé November 2021 figures). The vulnerabilities have been aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic. According to the Centre for Disease Control, CDC, as of 22 March 2022 there were 119,544 confirmed cases and 1,927 COVID-related deaths reported since the pandemic began. About 7.0 percent have received at least one dose of the vaccine, with only 5.4 percent having received two doses.As of December 31, 20215, Cameroon hosted 342,877 Central African refugees amongst which, 281,898 hosted in Adamawa and East regions including 202,315 off site and 79,583 in developed sites. At the epicenter of crises are women and children, who are disproportionately affected by loss of livelihoods, lack of social services and breaches of protection. In fact, 52% of the total number of refugees are women and girls, while 55% are children. In addition, 52,914 of the total Central African refugee population are people with specific needs, including 9,829 women at risk; when it is known that insufficient food not only exposes vulnerable people to different diseases, but makes them even more vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse. High rates of stunting are correlated with suboptimal IYCF practices. Particularly in the East and Adamawa regions, infant and young child feeding practices remain low with an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 53.8% (Adamawa) and 46.2% (East). Early breastfeeding initiation is practiced by only 17.5% (Adamawa) and 35.1% (East) of women. The minimum dietary diversity in children aged 6-23 months is 45.2% in Adamaoua and 31.3% in the East. The dietary profile of children aged 6 to 23 months shows that in Adamaoua, only 47.3% of these children consume meat, fish and eggs, while in the Eastern region, this proportion is 62.5%. 27.6% of children aged 6-23 months do not consume vegetables and fruits in the Adamaoua region and in the East region, the rate is 36.2%. The 2021 Fill the Nutrient Gap (FNG) analysis found a strong correlation between the prevalence of stunting and the level of affordability of a nutritious diet. This study found that 61% of the population in the Adamaoua region and 70% of the population in the East cannot afford a diet that meets their nutritional requirements, indicating that not having economic access to a nutritious diet is an important underlying factor for malnutrition in Cameroon. Moreover, examples from other countries show that women's empowerment and literacy are also strongly correlated with better child nutrition.
