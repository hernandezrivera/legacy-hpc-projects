Project Summary

Conflict in Ethiopia’s Tigray, Amhara and Afar regions has contaminated vast swathes of land with explosive remnants of war (ERW). As many internally displaced persons (IDPs) people are starting to return to their homes, their movements increase the risk of ERW incidents. An immediate mine action response is needed to address the risks faced by returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and the local population, and to allow humanitarian partners to deliver aid in a safe manner.
Under the proposed project, UNMAS will:
Assess the extent of the explosive threat in Northern Ethiopia
Conduct explosive risk reduction activities
Strengthen the coordination of the Mine Action sector to enable more effective aid delivery
The project will be delivered in two phases:
Months 1-6: Mobilization and Threat Assessment
The UNMAS Operations Team will conduct analysis to determine the pattern of conflict, the nature of weapons used and where ERW may be located. The team will triangulate this information with the operational plans of humanitarian partners, to generate a list of priority areas that must urgently undergo Non-Technical Survey (NTS) to identify and detail present risks and to plan how to minimize their impact on the effective delivery of aid programmes.
Months 4-12: Explosive Risk Reduction
Mine action teams will deploy to priority areas identified in phase 1 to conduct a more comprehensive study on the nature and location of ERW, using NTS - a cost-effective way to gain information about the presence or absence of explosive hazards, using local knowledge and typical warning signs. In addition to confirming the presence of hazards, NTS also quickly ascertains whether areas suspected to be contaminated are actually low risk and can be immediately released, for safe use by communities and aid agencies alike. During NTS, if a single or a small group of ERW is located, it can be moved without slowing down the pace of survey, through swift disposal operations. If the hazard is large or complex, it will take more time to be removed and will be marked using internationally recognized tapes and signage, until another mine action team can be tasked to clear the area.
In addition, the mine action teams will deliver explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) to communities, using training materials that are gender-responsive and contextually relevant. EORE equips its beneficiaries with the knowledge and information to recognize and avoid ERW and to report their concerns to the mine action team, thus informing the teams’ survey. ERW affects women, men, girls and boys differently; each group will receive targeted messaging, the imagery used during the training will be inclusive, and a diverse range of voices will be heard to inform the NTS process. To amplify EORE messaging, UNMAS will identify focal points within humanitarian organizations to train as trainers, so that EORE can be incorporated and replicated into their communications with communities.
Additionally, UNMAS will ensure cross-cutting humanitarian coordination throughout the project, as lead of the Mine Action Area of Responsibility (MA AoR) wich was activated in Ethiopia in August 2021. While mine action operations are ongoing, information will be continuously shared with aid agencies operating in the area through the inter-cluster coordination group, humanitarian country team and other fora. UNMAS will also support the delivery of explosive ordnance awareness training and other types of support to humanitarian partners to mitigate explosive risk, including taking part in humanitarian assessments and accompanying convoys.
Finally, as part of these efforts to strengthen the coordination of the mine action sector from a Nexus perspective, UNMAS will provide technical assistance to the Mine Action Office to develop its quality management, accreditation and information management capability, as well as its operational capacity for explosive hazards survey and clearance.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

UNMAS will work with national implementing partners who will have strong knowledge of the intervention areas and of the affected population’s cultural norms, languages and habits. For both explosive threat assessment and EORE activities, community liaison will be a key component as well as information gathering and sharing with CSOs, grassroots organizations and community leaders in the intervention areas. In addition, as EO affects women, men, girls and boys differently, each group will receive targeted messaging and the imagery used during EORE sessions will be inclusive. The EORE materials will be adapted to the local languages, field-tested to ensure they are easily understandable by the local community, and adjusted as necessary. Finally, affected populations will be able to report concerns and provide feedback to the EORE teams, and the AAP/PSEA reporting channels of the wider humanitarian system will be systematically shared.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Affected populations will be able to report concerns about explosive ordnance to the operations teams. The teams will use a variety of awareness raising and data collection formats including community sessions, focus-group discussions (with groups of women, youth and the elderly for instance), child-friendly sessions at schools, door-to-door outreach to ensure people with mobility impairment or household chores are not left behind, and leaflet distribution and/or radio broadcast in harder-to-reach areas. The teams will also seek to collect feedback on EORE materials as well as on the content and methodology of the EORE sessions, to ensure that they are adapted to the affected populations’ needs. As women in affected communities often feel more comfortable to report their concerns to other women, implementing partners will be encouraged to recruit gender-balanced teams. Finally, the teams will systematically share the AAP/PSEA reporting channels of the wider humanitarian system with affected populations.
