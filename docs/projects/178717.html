Project Summary


In 2021, our NGO aims to address urgent and life-saving nutrition needs for mothers and children in NW Syria. Nutrition activities during the year 2021 will continue scaling up life-saving curative and preventive nutrition interventions to the mothers and children living in Al Rayyan camp and Atareb sub-district in Aleppo governorate, and in Atmeh camp cluster, Al Karameh camp cluster, Maaret Tamasrin sub-district, and Idleb city in Idleb governorate. Nutrition activities will prioritizing the following responses for the year 2021: supporting and following up the pregnant and lactating women and caregivers with children between 0-59 months of age and disable mothers through individual counselling sessions, group awareness raising sessions, re-lactation sessions, postnatal support sessions (breastfeeding initiative within first hour after delivery, the benefits of the first touch between the mother and new-born infant, solving breast problems, and the benefits of the colostrum feeding), the correction of misconceptions regarding exclusive breastfeeding and breastfeeding in general, and providing information on complementary feeding. -Strengthen adherence to operational guidance for response adaptations in the context of COVID-19 to mitigate risk of infection including roll out of the family MUAC approach to increase early acute malnutrition case detection and referral for treatment.

4 - Needs

In NWS 20-37% of mothers are malnourished and at risk of suffering from various consequences of malnutrition. This includes reduced immunity and mothers risking exposure to COVID-19 infections, which will impact their ability to properly feed and care for their infants, and could lead to premature birth, and to the birth of low-weight infants. As such, the chronic Malnutrition (stunting) rate among children under 5 years in NWS is showing an increase throughout 2020, According to the Nutrition surveillance system report (March 2021), the proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate was found to be 2%, (MAM) at 1.9%, and (SAM) at 0.1%; proxy GAM was also higher in Idleb (2.72%). Poor dietary diversity among mothers, as well as children, will impact their capability to optimally breast feed, which is already evidenced by the current low levels of poor infant feeding and caring practices. In January 2021, our NGO conducted a MSNA to identify nutrition needs in the target locations of the proposed program. The assessment findings identified significant gaps in basic service provision across the assessed areas According to MSNA, 29.1% of households with children under 6 months of age are not exclusively breastfed. Community members attending IYCF sessions on how to feed infants and children during the past 12 months, 37% stated that there were no such sessions in their community, 9.8% stated that this is not something they would like, while 13.9% did not know what these sessions mean. Among those who attended the sessions, 64.8% stated that they attended one session only compared to 35.2% who attended the routine sessions, which indicates a gap in preventive nutrition activities and gaps in the delivery of IYCF messaging.

5 - Implementation of activities

The NGO will support two IYCF facilities and four CMAM clinics, besides conducting IYCF activities in one maternity hospital and five Primary Health Care facilities PHCs. Over the course of the project timeline, the NGO’s Nutrition team will support facility and community-based Infant and Young Child Feeding activities through providing consultations on WHO-recommended infant and young child feeding practices in two supported IYCF facilities, five primary health care facilities, and one maternity and child hospital, These consultations will be provided by trained IYCF facilitators specialized in working with Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and other caregivers. The NGO will ensure to include PLWs who may also be persons with disabilities or injuries in its activities. Moreover, Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) doctors and nurses will provide Women and Children through four supported CMAM clinics with Micronutrient supplementation for the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies. The NGO will also support facility and community-based screening for acute malnutrition through Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening for PLW, and through Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) and z-score charts for children aged under 5 years with detection of the intensity of edema. CMAM clinics will provide treatment for moderate acute malnutrition cases among children and PLW through Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP), and Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) for severe acute malnutrition cases among children.Depending on the activities of the program, the nutrition program targets many adolescent mothers through individual counseling and group sessions.
