Project Summary

This proposal aims to meet the urgent needs of strengthening livelihoods in the increasingly unstable south-western region of Cameroon on the security front. This instability causes a deficit in the socio-economic level of the population, more particularly young women and men. This degradation of the socio-economic situation and this fear installed at the level of the population creates major distress among young people in particular. Through this project, Action Against Hunger (AAH) aims to revive and strengthen livelihoods through the establishment of a mechanism for the economic. Beyond the aspect of emergency socio-economic integration, AAH aims to consolidate the achievements of previous projects in relation to nutrition / health by taking into account experiences for adapt the approach and targeting of these young people and their households. This project aims to strengthen local capacity through the positive economic benefits of income-generating activities (IGA) that will be put in place to support young people. The municipalities will be targeted according to pockets of needs and in relation to the Harmonized framework of October 2022. The aim is to cover the real needs in relation to the changing context of the region. This support targets 1,000 households (ie 7,000 displaced women and men) in the most vulnerable villages in the region. The targeting criteria will be adapted to better capture the following people:
 Young women and men aged between 18 and 30 in households with non-existent sources of income and who have experienced a direct impact of the Anglophone conflict in the region.
 Young people, among the criteria mentioned above, showing signs of psychological distress
 Young single-parent household head with no sources of income
 Household with at least one youngster with one or more malnourished children
 Households with a high adaptation strategy above the acceptable threshold
 Young women and men internally displaced to flee conflict and without any means of subsistence

1 - Besoins / Needs

The situation in the North-West and South-West regions (NWSW) remains volatile with a one-week lockdown imposed inAugust and a two-week-long lockdown in September imposed by NSAGs. In some localities where the lockdown was not strictly observed, it was enforced through roadblocks and violence targeting communities, including abductions, and killing of civilians. The insecurity led to multiple population displacements across the NWSW with over 8,842 persons fleeing their homes including 1,235 people who fled to the neighboring Centre and Littoral regions.Meme division combined hosted over 14, 3 % of the total displaced population across the southwest with 52,548 displaced people. In the southwest region, violence between government forces and armed separatist movements erupted mainly along the main roads and, in most cases, forced the local population to flee for their safety, either in the surrounding forest, or to large urban centers under government control, such as Buea and Kumba. Only 12% of displaced households could afford some form of rented accommodation, while the vast majority (68%) would live with host families, in turn indirectly suffering the consequences of displacement.These displacements caused a loss of capital, not only for the households which fled the clashes leaving all their means and resources, but also the households which welcome these internally displaced persons or the pressure to increase on their meager resources already insufficient because of the rate. High unemployment, the increase in food products, the COVID pandemic that considerably limits trade. According to the OCHA report of November 01, 2022, 600,300 internally displaced people need emergency assistance to strengthen their livelihoods. In this figure, we count nearly 40% of youngWomen and men in whom the food emergency is very high with irreversible adaptation strategies due to lack of entry of economic resources. In addition to this situation, there is an evolution of physiological distress due to fear, uncertainty, and the loss of all their resources within this target group of already vulnerable people. All of these recurrent and interrelated shocks are reflected in the classification of food insecurity and livelihoods in the region. Indeed, the prevision classification of the “Cadre Harmonisé” of October 2022 puts the Southwest region in a phase of food and livelihood crisis (i.e. 615,455 people).In this context, it is more than urgent to improve the income of the population in order to reduce the adaptation strategy, which is harmful in the medium term, and in order to reduce household food insecurity. The project therefore offers support for the economic integration of vulnerable households by providing young women and men with profitable income-generating activities (IGA) to improve their living conditions standards. These IGAs will bring together the activities of small buoyant businesses in the region, which are: Sewing The cattle trade Transportation Peanut processing And others that will be evaluated during the starting project Waiting Result:Young men and women of the South West region, internally displaced, have access to a stable income to help cover the food needs of their households Activities:Activity 1: Identification of promising IGAs in the intervention areas and feasibility studyActivity 2: Identification of beneficiariesActivity 3: Training of beneficiaries on the management of individual / group IGAsActivity 4: Provision of kit for IGAsActivity 5: monitoring the evolution of IGAs and their turnoverActivity 6: Community mobilization and sensitization to involve the community in the support of IGAs
