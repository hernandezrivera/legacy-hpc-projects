Project Summary

The first result aims to strengthening the existing coordination mechanism through the application of the Community Protection Approach (CPA), which has been designed in collaboration with ECHO to provide evidence-based community and individual data to inscribe existing interventions in an integrated protection multi-stakeholders´ strategy. GVC and its partner MIGRACE will carry out an assessment covering the municipalities of Tripoli, Ubari, Ghat and Murzuq. The assessment will focus on information gaps and verification, in order to extract the Integrated Protection System of Indicators, returning indicators in 12 sectors benchmarked with international standards, a protection risk, threats, vulnerabilities, capacities, and dignity and safety indexes. The questionnaire (MQ) used provides as well an automatic mapping (triggers) of individual, families and households facing specific vulnerabilities and risks, to establish an Individual Protection Approach (IPA). The IPA is an automatic system of multi-sector referrals to external and national actors, as well as a management system of individual most-vulnerable cases. The IPA allows the identification of extremely vulnerable and urgent cases, as well as unmet needs.
A CPA web-based Platform is already established for Libya and will be used for the Action. The Platform is purposely designed to provide multi-stakeholders access and management of community and individual data and analyses, as well as the multi-sector activities identified. The Platform is compliant with the GDPR and Global Protection Cluster PIM standards, ensures remote management and monitoring, and tailored access can be provided to different actors.
GVC has developed the Community Protection Approach (CPA), a community engagement and empowerment instrument used to design Integrated Protection Programs (IPP), covering humanitarian and developmental interventions. The Multi-Sector Questionnaire (MQ) and the Individual Protection Approach (IPA) are at the core of this methodological process. While the MQ is a multi-sector questionnaire aiming at capturing community level phenomena with a protection focus and orientation, the Individual Protection Approach offers system to identify and assess people whose immediate physiological, dignity and safety needs are not met supporting through guidance or mediation the linking of right-holders to an appropriate service provider and by strengthening right-holders’ resilience capacities to claim their right to assistance.
The MQ is designed to be operationally feasible in a wide array of humanitarian contexts. The IPSI and triggers to identify specific vulnerable groups facing protection risks and the gaps in essential services provision offers an initial indication for humanitarian programming. The data collection is purposely designed to engage different groups according to the Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) approach, introduced by UNHCR in 2004. By analysing the AGD factors and the intersections among them, a better understanding is gained of the specific, complex protection risks faced by different individuals and groups, and it is possible to target the action to address them more safely and effectively. Among all indicators, 29 indicators have AGD relevance, because they give a direct measurement of gender/ability-related issues and because they can be disaggregated in sub-indicators by age, gender and/or ability. Based on the gender-related ones, a Gender Index is built, measuring the Protection Risk in the Gender Sector, which is conceived as transversal to all sectors of action.

1 - Needs

It is estimated that 448,848 individuals will need protection assistance in Libya for 2020. Libyans and non-Libyans face significant protection risks, stemming from conflict and violence, including violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. The response must be axed according to the following needs: 1- The protection of civilian affected by the armed conflict, including the respect and promotion of International Humanitarian Law and refugee Law by all parties to the conflict un Libya;2- The protection of human rights of IDPs, persons in need of international protection and persons at high risks of violation and abuses, which include those affected by conflict related protracted forced displacement;3- Ensure the promotion and respect of principled based humanitarian action, which include the establishment of a framework defining protection modalities and standards for humanitarian assistance and delivery of service, including through the identification of protection risks, human rights due diligence challenges in engaging with public institutions, security forces and entities affiliated to the parties to the conflict and adapted mitigation measures.4- Prioritizing peacebuilding and reconciliation efforts. Confidence and trust building between affected populations and the government is an urgent and critical component for progress towards durable solutions for IDPs.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Through the CPA, population engagement is designed as a process of empowerment, following the Ladder of Citizen Participation and is adaptable to each context. The initial engagement step is low-risk and tailored to identify aspects influencing and biasing data and information; this profiling, along with safe communication channels created with the community, is used to establish a mechanism continuously monitoring the changes in the population’s needs and problems. The monitoring uses digital tools returning automatic analysis of the quantitative and qualitative data, in forms tailored to existing humanitarian and development sector standards. A mechanism for identification, assessment and referral of families and individuals ensures additionally meaningful access to assistance and services for these people.All throughout the CPA process, different Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) groups in the community are engaged, including the most vulnerable ones. More specifically, different AGD groups are identified based on a sampling approach and classified according to a scale of prioritization, with a rationale that focuses on the characteristics of each groups and the specific protection risks associated with them. The widest possible representation of AGD groups is sought. In particular, participation is fostered at needs assessment level, through the collection of quantitative and qualitative data, employing different participation techniques (FGDs, interviews, transect walks); during the design of the intervention, as the inputs of the community are integrated in the Action Plans; and in the monitoring phase, by means of regular feedback collection and meetings with the community.GVC will ensure the accountability to affected population (AAP) through close monitoring and evaluation on the activities undertaken by the implementation partner, through regular contact, training, and reporting. Moreover, the Individual Protection Approach guarantees the follow up of cases and the implementation of the Information Management System.Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Child Protection guidelines will be provided through capacity building activities. In addition, systems of GBV prevention and response (Codes of Conduct/PSEA and CP protocols; referral pathways, complaint and reporting mechanisms, psychological first aid, and how to make referrals) will be established. Finally, the community will be engaged in choosing the location (including by means of activities such as safety mapping) and adapting the facilities, to ensure the safety of children and staff (e.g. WASH facilities that are lighted, accessible, lockable, private, sex segregated). Moreover, GVC Libya will pay attention to apply GVC Policy such as the “Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy”.
