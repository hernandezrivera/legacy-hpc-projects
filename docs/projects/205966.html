Project Summary

The Medair planned health activities are proposed to be implemented in Pibor and Leer counties, inclusive of scaling up emergency response activities through an emergency response team (ERT) to locations with public health emergencies which are accessible and feasible for program activities. According to the Health Cluster’s severity scale for 2023, Leer and Pibor are level 4 severity. The project is designed based on Core Humanitarian and Sphere Minimum Standards to ensure that affected beneficiaries receive timely, coordinated, adequate, resilient, and impartial basic quality healthcare services inclusive of reproductive health, mental health, and psychosocial services (MHPSS). The proposed health project will seek to reduce morbidity and mortality by improving access to quality essential primary healthcare, including reproductive health, services to survivors of SGBV, and mental health services, to girls, boys, men, and women in emergency situations, with a specific focus on accessibility for persons living with disability and the elderly, as well as responding to public health emergencies. Theproject aims to build the resilience of communities and the MoH to prevent, detect, and respond to disease outbreaks.
Methodology and target: Medair's project focuses on the provision of primary healthcare in areas of high need with attendant service delivery gaps, including Leer and Pibor. Supported activities include the provision of curative consultations in primary health facilities; community-based treatment of common illnesses in children under five; routine vaccination; reproductive and sexual health including the provision of ANC, safe delivery, PNC, and clinical management of SGBV; management of at-risk infants through MAMI; and mental health treatment. Medair conducts ongoing training and supportive supervision for capacity-building staff of Medair, CHD, and other partners to improve the quality-of-service provision. Medair primarily targets children under five, women, and people with disabilities and mental health conditions, due to their unique vulnerability, but not exclusively. Through the ERT, Medair can respond in any area of South Sudan in response to disease outbreaks, acute displacement, reports of increased morbidity and mortality, nutritional emergencies, flooding, and other natural disasters. Medair ERT aims to reduce morbidity and mortality of vulnerable populations through outbreak response such as vaccination campaigns or case management, and provision of primary health care services where these are inadequate. Through training and community engagement, Medair aims to increase the capacity of long-term health partners and the local community to be prepared for future shocks.
Medair's health activities are integrated with WASH, Nutrition, Emergency Shelter/NFI, and Protection activities to enhance the effectiveness and address underlying causes of morbidity and mortality.

1. Needs

The Health Cluster in the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 identified 6.1 million people in need of healthcare, an increase from 5.5 million people in 2022. Furthermore, the Health Cluster projects for 2024 that 6.3 million people need basic healthcare services which is an increase from the previous year's 6.1 million, with the target to reach 3.2 million people with essential lifesaving health services. South Sudan continues to suffer a severe health crisis, complicated by multiple factors such as flooding, conflicts, displacements, returnees, and disease outbreaks. Service delivery gaps continue to be more noticeable because of the combination of challenges including a lack of funding, inadequate staffing, medicine, and equipment to support quality service provision. As of 16 October, 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April 2023. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5 percent of all arrivals. (South Sudan: Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 16 As of 20 October 2023). 80% of the population report barriers to accessing health services, the main barriers include insecurity, distance to health facilities, and flooding. Even when able to access health services, only 8% of 1869 facilities provide the full basic package of health and nutrition services. Malaria continues to be a leading cause of morbidity and mortality (50.2% of cases and 39.4% of deaths attributed to malaria), and the number of AWD cases is increasing across the country, with cholera anticipated in identified hotspot counties, especially with the increased influx of returnees and refugees resulting from the Sudan crisis. On 26 September 2023, the Federal Ministry of Health in Sudan officially declared a Cholera outbreak in Gardafi state, and a cholera outbreak has been reported in Malakal, South Sudan. South Sudan also continues to experience vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks such as Hepatitis E, and measles. Routine immunization and reproductive health services across the country continue to have very poor coverage, with only 66% of children vaccinated and numerous measles outbreaks reported since December 2022. Women continue to suffer the most as regards gender-based violence and report some of the highest numbers of cases of depression. Pibor continues in 2023 to be classified as severity score 4 areas with catastrophic health needs. Ongoing insecurity in the areas continues to impact people’s access to health care, due to population displacement, looting of health facilities and facility closures due to insecurity. Geographical impediments also impact services, with areas cut off for significant periods of time due to seasonal flooding. Reproductive health services remain inadequate due to a lack of qualified staff in the area and a lack of referral mechanisms for complicated cases. Mental health and psychosocial support needs are completely unmet in most health programming. Leer’s classification of health needs is ranked as severity 4. This is a result of increasing insecurity and catastrophic flooding, both leading to massive displacement of the population. Insecurity has led to looting and the destruction of health and nutrition facilities. Existing health services continue to be inadequate to meet the high needs of the community and there is currently a need for emergency response across all sectors. Mental health services continue to be a significant need in Leer, with acute and chronic insecurity increasing the vulnerability of the population to poor mental health and reduced psychosocial well-being, impacting families and communities. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will be deployed for a period of up to 3 months to respond to health emergencies by providing facility and community-level basic life-saving primary healthcare integrated with nutrition services.

2. Project activities

The Medair project will consist of both acute emergency response capacity and longer-term primary healthcare interventions. All activities will be conducted through multi-sectoral responses to maximize impact on acute and chronic public health emergencies. The ERT will assess and respond to acute public health emergencies in all areas of South Sudan, including disease outbreaks, large-scale population displacement, emergency GAM rates, significant increases in morbidity and mortality, and natural disasters. The ERT health team has the capacity to conduct more than one interventionat the same time, in different locations. The emergency response team carries out short-term responses aimed at managing the initial shock of a new emergency, with interventions of up to three months. Efforts are made throughout the response to plan for exit, and handover is done as needed to long-term partners or MoH/CHD. Medair will remain flexible to respond rapidly to emergencies using different modalities including temporary or mobile clinics, iCCM, and mass vaccination campaigns, and provide support to existing health partners or MoH in pre-existing facilities through technical and operational support. In all interventions, Medair will focus on capacity building of local health staff and increasing the resilience of community members through training, education, and community engagement. In Pibor, Medair will continue to work to improve access to essential primary health care in a community that has experienced multiple compounded shocks in recent years, including ongoing insecurity and geographical access challenges. Medair will continue to support two PHCUs in Gumuruk and Lekuangole with OPD, EPI, and maternal health services, with a focus on scaling up maternal health care to provide the basic package of maternal and neonatal care. ICCM services will continue to be provided in the two payams, as well as Cascade groups which will aim to improve health, nutrition, and WASH knowledge and practices in the community. Medair will provide supportive supervision, technical quality assurance and operational support to all activities and work towards providing all services according to local and international standards. Medair will also introduce MHPSS programming in Pibor, with community-based psychosocial support and MH treatment following mhGAP-HIG protocols in 3 health facilities. Medair will also provide mobile outreach services to communities with limited access to existing health facilities. In Leer, Medair will continue to implement community-based programming through iCCM, treating common illnesses in children under 5 and working closely with other partners for referral. Medair will also continue to provide mental health services in three primary healthcare facilities following mhGAP-HIG protocols, services for survivors of SGBV, and psychosocial support services through community cascade groups. Medair will support primary health care services through ongoing support to Dindin PHCU, including OPD, EPI, and RH services. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will aim to provide support to facility and community level basic healthcare working to address gaps in BPHNS in emergency settings such as displacement, outbreaks or nutritional emergencies depending on available local capacity. The actual response will depend on the needs and gaps identified in the rapid needs assessment. Emergency health interventions will work towards national and global health guidelines.
