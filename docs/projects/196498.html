Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to ensure a proper learning environment for communities where girls and boys are learning in unsafe or unconducive learning environments by providing sustainable access to protective learning spaces through the rehabilitation of 8 damaged public schools in the Eastern and Southern Aleppo countryside, which are areas with high numbers of returnees, IDPs, and host populations.
These 8 schools will act as Child-Friendly Schools (CFSs) for promoting gender equality, protecting children‘s rights, re-defining education quality and creating a positive psycho-emotional environment at schools, promoting quality education in schools for all children – especially among the most vulnerable and hard-to-reach populations
Over the past years, Fouadi has been supporting learners by organizing the Self-Learning Program (SLP) to reduce their learning gap and supporting their enrollment in formal education and preparation for national examinations. Further to SLP, Fouadi has been supporting learners by providing safe learning spaces to children enrolled in formal education through its continuous projects of schools rehabilitation.
The current plan of Fouadi for the expansion of educational access is depending on promoting a UNICEF approach called Child-friendly school, or CFS, which is:
- Proven approach to promote quality education for all children especially among the most vulnerable populations.
- CFSs provide safe spaces and a protective environment for children‘s emotional, psychological and physical well-being, both in primary and secondary schools – especially for girls.
- CFSs support teachers and school personnel in providing quality education.
- CFSs spread a love of learning and create a sense of harmony between the school, the surrounding community, and the environment.
- CFSs promotes sports for development as part of its psychosocial support role. Both girls and boys are encouraged to participate in sports activities, so, playgrounds will be rehabilitated.
Fouadi will implement this education intervention directly in line with EDU sector criteria :
1- If the number of classrooms in the targeted schools is enough, room for ECE will be rehabilitated, taking into account its standards and conditions.
2- Rehabilitate/construct a toilet that is suitable for children with disabilities and ECE.
3- Rehabilitating a residence room for teachers if a room is available within the targeted schools.
4- Providing solar systems or alternatives to electric energy.
5- Repairing of wooden boxes and metal structures for school desks.
6- Following the measures to prevent COVID-19.
7- Establishing remedial classes for children in schools outside official working hours and according to placements tests.
8- Training of DoE education staff.
9- Rehabilitation of targeted schools entrances to suit Children with disabilities.
10- Rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities in the targeted schools and providing the targeted schools with safe drinking water.
11- Expanding the rehabilitation works to include playgrounds, parks, and theaters.
The targeted schools will support the (re-)integration of about 5,000 children, adolescents, and youth into the public school system over a period of one year. This project aims to work to address the following education needs:
1- The ECE helps pre-school-age children to get prepared for school by acquiring basic knowledge.
2- The Remedial Education Programme.
3- Support teachers in getting the needed skills to implement the CFSs approach by training them on, Active learning, School Violence, PSS, Life skills, and Class Management.
Fouadi will implement this education intervention directly in line with the EDU Sector’s HRP objectives and activities, MoE standards, and EDU sector criteria.

3 - Needs

1- In the targeted areas via this project, learning environments continue to be unconducive due to damage, overcrowding, lack of school furniture, school supplies, heating facilities, and insufficient WASH facilities. Learning loss continues due to irregular attendance for both teachers and students, shifting, and disruption/closure in education.2- Findings of Fouadi’s rapid assessment indicate that the back-to-school rate according to attendance rate has exceeded expectations but still suffers from the shortage of rehabilitated school buildings, lack of teaching and learning materials, and insufficient numbers of teachers.3- The sources of assessment information were derived from observation, interviews with boys and girls, teachers, managers in the targeted villages and based on the needs of the Aleppo DoE. 4- The assessment found that school buildings are often dilapidated and inadequate, sometimes even dangerous (classrooms, WASH facilities, playing grounds, roofs, boundary fences, and other facilities). As a result, protective and safe learning environments are not available.5- The assessment found that: 1) about 100% of four-year-old and five-year-olds did not attend any form of learning, 2) Teachers and other education personnel need support that is commensurate with the criticality of their role, 3) The lack of access to secondary education in many areas limits the ability of adolescents to continue their education.

4 - Implementation of activities

1-Prioritize inside the sub-districts will be given to villages of the severity scale scores (3-4-5) and where there is a damaged formal school with cost-effective rehabilitation. 2- Using multiple options to ensure educational access to the targeted children especially for ECE, basic, and secondary education levels. All possible options would be activated via multiple pathways such as:-Unmet education needs of students with learning gaps in the targeted locations will be addressed through catch-up/remedial classes inside the rehabilitated schools. -The project will activate the option for using a referral system to accommodate remedial classes within available Child-Friendly Spaces and other community learning centers self, which was established by Fouadi in cooperation with other partners.- Launch ECE classes in a way that does not pressure or limit the access to basic and secondary education In the targeted villages, and if the number of available classrooms is not enough within the targeted schools, establish ECE classes in other community facilities would be an alternative implementation option. 3- The project will work in close coordination with community-level stakeholders, including DoEs, school headteachers, teachers, members of PTAs. 4- Delivery modalities: direct service delivery method will be used to implement the planned programs, while in-person learning remains the dominant form of remedial classes and ECE.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- All targeted schools are in secure locations, safety protocols are also in place. 2- Targeted schools pose threats to students and school personnel. Some of them are structurally unsafe, and all of the school latrines are inadequate, open defecation is rampant, which could lead to the spread of disease. Boys & girls in school are highly vulnerable to these public health risks. For schools assessed, the latrine user ratio is poor, not sex-segregated, have not enough water for drinking & handwashing, and this affects attendance. Girls in particular lack access to protected latrines. 3- Targeted schools are insufficiently protective from harsh weather with missing windows, doors, walls, or roofs, schools do not have heaters, power or water, etc. All measures would be taken to ensure that learning takes place in safe and welcoming facilities. 4- Risks related to bullying, corporal punishment, and other threats would be addressed. Child protection measures including violence prevention, feedback mechanisms engagement with parents and caregivers, and appropriately supported teachers and education personnel is the fundamental key to mitigating these threats. 5- Ensuring safety for children and youths when accessing schools (Their routes to and from schools ): these risks include: unexploded ordinates, harassment, violence, and COVID virus. For any of these risks, risk education would be provided when needed and the project will work to mitigate these risks.
