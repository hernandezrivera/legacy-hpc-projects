Project Summary

This project will support 2500 farmer households with agricultural inputs and technical support for wheat crop planting. This support will improve bread production (the staple food for Syrian people) which has been negatively affected by the lack of wheat and high prices of production supplies.
The project will strength local production capacity and local markets, therefore, the self-reliance of the community to be stable against external conflicts. Also, the project will contribute in enhancing the quality of production as we will support farmers by high-quality seeds and all needed agricultural inputs (fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides, irrigation fuel), in addition to technical support through training and monitoring visits to farmers’ fields and providing guidance advices in order to have highest and best production.
This project will also support 4000 vulnerable households including female-headed and child-headed households living in peri-urban and rural areas to produce their own nutritious food through distributing vaccinated chicken and chicken feed to 2000 families, and distributing sheep and fodder to 2000 families, in addition to supporting them with the required knowledge and basic advices to achieve the best possible production of eggs, milk and meat.
This will strengthen and re-establish productive small backyard chicken and sheep flocks to support local food production and to improve household nutrition, especially for children and pregnant women, and to open up additional sources of income for rural and peri-urban women through small-scale marketing of eggs, milk, and meat.

Needs

HNO 2019 states that 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. The main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods, reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power, and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations.The agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25-30 percent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. Therefore, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen the resilience of Syrians. Between 2011 – 2016, cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, as the worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with the lowest wheat production at 1.2 million metric tons since 1989. There is a shortfall of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tons. Regarding livestock, there are unprecedented asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies. In addition, livestock production is also further constrained due to the reduction of livestock number as compared to the last comprehensive livestock census in 2010 (HNO 2019). According to the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform, sheep numbers fell by 45 percent, goat numbers by 30 percent, cattle numbers by 40 percent, and poultry numbers by 55 percent during the first three years of the crisis. The main challenges continue to be high fodder prices and insufficient coverage of veterinary services. 200,000 breeders’ households are suffering from the loss of productive assets (livestock) and damaged infrastructure (land, irrigation infrastructure, facilities, etc.) which is affecting both farmers and breeders. BINAA conducted need assessments and consultation with the affected community, and discussions with local councils and the local communities, we found that it is necessary to improve the food security status in order to avoid depleted coping strategy and consumption gaps for food-insecure people and to prevent falling into food insecurity for others. This goal will be achieved by empowering their resilience and supporting their livelihood by creating outcome resources for (12250 men, 12850women, 15650boys, 16250girls) (57,000 individuals)

Protection Risk Analysis

- Protection risk: The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points, or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: Medium. - Impact: High. - Mitigating measures: 1- We will not distribute in a late time of the day.2- We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute in each village separately.3- During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men.4- Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, with two phone numbers: one for men and other for women.- Protection risk: The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. - Likelihood: weak. - Impact: High. - Mitigating measures: 1- We will choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities.2- Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, with two phone numbers: one for men and other for women.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

*Risk:Beneficiaries need assistance after the end of the project*How to mitigate How to mitigate:- Coordination with cluster and responses organization to provide the needed assistance.- Survey all assessments and analyze reality and prepare project proposals to achieve an effective and sustainable response. *Risk:Beneficiaries movement (IDPs) from their location.*How to mitigate How to mitigate:- Providing assistance to needier persons, those are after them in the lists - Update the needy lists and share it with the cluster. *Risk:The spread of IDPs to multiple areas*How to mitigate How to mitigate:- Distributing items to beneficiaries in suitable modality.- Coordination with local communities to Provide the service in suitable conditions and quality. *Risk:Compression of beneficiaries on the field team, and community requests for greater assistance*How to mitigate How to mitigate:- Clarifying the project scope of work and the services which will be provided.
