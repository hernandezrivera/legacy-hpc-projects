Project Summary

Primary Education:
The main objective of the project is ensuring children’s right to access to education and the basis for further and higher education. Scaling up safe and equitable access for 180 students, 95 girls & 85 boys, 5% of them are CwDs, (7-13 years old) to a regular formal protective education environment. Payments of monthly incentives for the teachers in non-formal settings.
The project will mainly contribute to mainstreaming child protection mechanisms within the schools through the PSS (Psychosocial Support) activities, support, and provision of activities that will reduce child protection risks.
There will be leisure activities for the students (sports activities, music activities, theatre activities, etc.) in addition to recreational trips if the circumstances permit.
Those activities are for 2022 and 2023. Provision of non-formal education will be supported by providing teachers (7 males & 3 females) with monthly incentives.
Medical Education:
The project is aiming to enhance the future job opportunities for the educated females in NW Syria via providing official education and in enabling them to graduate with official certificates accredited by the Turkish Ministry of Education. The students who will graduate from the health school can work in clinics and hospitals if they do not want to complete university education. In addition, students graduating from the health and public school will be able to complete university education in Turkish universities after learning the Turkish language.
The project will mainly contribute to mainstreaming child protection mechanisms within the schools through the PSS activities and provision of activities that will reduce child protection risks. The school counsellor will work with students individually when needed and helps create a safe learning environment for all students that meets each student's individual needs.
Furthermore, HIHFAD will provide small groups in the school with structured PSS sessions aiming to enhance the resilience of the students through learning life skills. This PSS intervention takes a holistic approach based on the principle that long-lasting, effective improvement in children’s lives can only be achieved with the full participation of children, their parents or caregivers and others within the communities in which they live.
There will be recreational activities for the students in addition to recreational trips if the circumstances permit. The high school will have two sections, a general secondary school, and a health secondary school. The health secondary school will have two branches, the first is nursing and the second is laboratory. The school year will start in mid-September, and the first semester will continue until the end of January. The second semester will start from mid-February until the end of May, as there will be a holiday between the two semesters from the beginning of February until mid-February. In addition, there is a summer vacation starting from Beginning of June until mid-September, during the summer vacation an English and Turkish language course will be given to students.
During 2022, only formal education will be provided. HIHFAD will start providing medical education in 2023. The secondary education will target 240 female students, 120 for formal and 120 for medical, 5% of them are CwDs. In addition to 10 teachers in 2022 & 20 teachers in 2023.
HIHFAD will use individual interviews and FGDs to evaluate activities in addition to collecting observations from the field. HIHFAD will also analyze student data and the results of the quarterly tests, all beneficiaries will be able to submit complaints and suggestions through the complaints boxes and hotline (WhatsApp), and through the M&E field teams.
Overall target for 2022: 180 students + 10 teachers (primary) + 120 students + 10 teachers (secondary) = 320 individuals.

4 - Needs

In Syria, 6.9 million people need humanitarian education assistance, 2.45 million children aged 5-17 are out of school and 1.6 million children are at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, the access of education is still limited (HNO 2021). According to the HNO 2022, over 6,609,775 million people in need of education support, 18% (3-17 years old) are out of school, and half of the children are not in school (30%), Idleb (28%), Aleppo (26%). In the census before the Syrian crisis, Jarablus had a population of 11,570.2 In the past year, the population living in Jarablus has increased significantly. According to the latest Census Data provided from OCHA, Jarablus total population counts 58,889 individuals. Since the Turkish intervention into Syria in August 2016, the Free Syrian Army has full control of the area under the supervision of Turkish authorities. Due to the deprived situation of many families, children are even more exposed to the risks of exploitation, child labour and early marriage. Therefore, the most vulnerable children have been targeted in this project to provide them with regular formal and medical education and a safe environment that gives them the feeling of protection. HIHFAD selects beneficiaries according to a selection process and in line with the scope and goal of the project.

5 - Implementation of activities

HIHFAD will assign Education Field supervisor to support and supervise all education activities in primary and medical education under direct supervision and support from Education officer in Gaziantep Office in addition to PSS Facilitator. Signing contracts with teachers and education personnel to provide remedial classes after coordination with ED in Gaziantep and Jarablus to provide incentives for teachers. Education Supervisor will provide regular training for teachers. PSS facilitator will provide training for teachers and will provide PSS sessions to students and caregivers in coordination with Protection department in HIHFAD.Provision of formal education will be supported by HIHFAD by providing running cost materials for schools (stationery, fuel for heating, cleaning materials, …, etc.) in addition to provide needed technical support for teachers and education personnel.Provision of non-formal education will be supported by providing teachers with monthly incentives, providing needed materials and stationery for school and students in addition to technical support by HIHFAD’s education team.PSS and recreational activities will be provided to students, caregivers as well as teachers and education personnel by PSS facilitator taking into consideration special occasions such as spring break and teachers’ day.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Learning facilities attacked by parties to the conflict or by communities.M1: HIHFAD will suspend the activities in targeted areas to keep students and staff safe. HIHFAD will undertake an informed process before it decides to de-conflict or not de-conflict. Parents and caregivers are informed by HIHFAD’s team of the learning facility’s safety protocols and what will happen in case of an attack. R2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students.M2: All teachers, education personnel and all staff in HIHFAD organization is committed to signed enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying, corporal punishment, and physical, verbal and sexual abuse. HIHFAD trained all teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, violence, physical punishment and bullying and the use of positive discipline. R3: Students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers.M4: HIHFAD encourages students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers. HIHFAD assures provision of sufficient safe and clean bathroom facilities sex and age segregated. HIHFAD provides regular psychosocial support services for students, teachers and other education personnel. Those services have the capacity, ability and resources to address basic needs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA risks increase in emergencies settings due to the nature of power dynamics between humanitarian staff and beneficiaries which allow staff to perpetrate while beneficiaries are vulnerable to becoming victims.Aid workers always have more power over those we are there to serve.besides the strong PSEA policy that HIHFAD has which includes mandatory training and a PSEA COC to be signed by all the staff members, HIHFAD is implementing a set of PSEA activities through a strategic approach based on capacity building for the staff and raising awareness for the community using the following activities:-PSEA raising awareness sessions for the community.-Identify a PSEA field focal point.-Applying the PSEA field and organization risk and mitigation checklist.-Conducting PSEA training and refresher training as a part of the CB of all staff members.-Including the PSEA and PSEA COC in the mandatory induction training for any new staff.-Allocate specific safeguarding private numbers for beneficiaries has female and male well-trained respondents available to respond to any SEA complaint.-Distribute PSEA raising awareness materials in the project locations and in social media includes the safeguarding numbers.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risk of shelling and/or infighting between different armed groups continue causing loss of access to the targeted locations:Mitigation:HIHFAD will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again. 2- Schools are closed and quarantine enforced:Mitigation:We will continue distance learning methods, but if the schools are re-opened, HIHFAD will start in providing the normal physical learning and make sure that all protection measures of COVID-19 are in place and applied in schools (hygiene, sterilization, etc.). 3- The heavy shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies for a long time (months) or the target locations becomes under its controlMitigation:Shifting locations to more accessible areas in coordination with the Education cluster and other partners and relevel stakeholders. 4- Change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria.Mitigation:As a registered NGO in Turkey, HIHFAD is expecting that this legal framework will give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance changes, HIHFAD will adapt their modal of operation to new requirements that may arise to ensure continued services provided, although HIHFAD has its operational offices inside Syria that can continue following up all the needed activities with the remote management from HIHFAD’s office in Turkey.
