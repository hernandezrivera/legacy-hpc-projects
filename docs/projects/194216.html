Project Summary

According to Round 12 of IOM DTM’s monitoring of displacement and return movement, 2,229,657 people remain displaced in South Sudan, 78% of which are sheltering with already crisis-affected host communities. In a number of displacement sites and collective centers, including current and former PoCs, more than 440,000 individuals continue to live in situations of protracted displacement. Displacement figures have continued to increase despite the signing of the R-ARCSS in 2018 due to an increase of displacement-inducing incidents such as sub-national conflict and unprecedented climate-related disasters.
IOM CCCM aims to target 612,000 people of the total 1.4 million people targeted under the CCCM Cluster Response Plan. IOM will continue to provide displaced communities with basic and emergency camp management services, ensuring that the provision of protection and cross-sectoral humanitarian assistance is equitable, coordinated, monitored and adhering to standards. Of critical relevance to the current displacement environment in the country, IOM CCCM’s interventions will also integrate a robust, community-led disaster-mitigation lens in all care & maintenance interventions and capacity building exercises to enhance protective environments to increase resilience of infrastructure and communities to climate shocks. Further, fully committed to ensuring that AAP is streamlined in any and all activities, IOM will continue to develop and improve AAP mechanisms to ensure that the organization is accountable to the people that it serves and that programmes are informed by the voices of the displaced communities.
IOM intends to contribute to the sectoral objectives through the following interventions:
1) Continued and adapted provision of critical CCCM activities to IDPs living in Bentiu, Naivasha and Hai Masna IDP Sites as well as critical care & maintenance activities in Malakal PoC.
2) Deployment of emergency CCCM teams to deliver sustainable mobile responses promoting self-governance, community ownership and resilience in hard-to-reach locations following triggers at the ICCG-level.
3) Co-lead the CCCM Cluster at national level and continue acting as SFP in WBeG, Jonglei and Upper Nile States in order to support national and state-level partner agencies in accountable CCCM responses and systems.
4) Continue chairing the CCEWG in order to provide technical CCE support to partner organizations and to operationalize outputs under the 2021 South Sudan AAP Framework.

1 - Needs

According to Round 12 of IOM DTM’s monitoring of displacement and return movement, in addition to the 331,000 refugees in the country (OCHA, December 2021), 2,229,657 people remain displaced in South Sudan, 78% of which are sheltering with already crisis-hit host communities. At the same time, the other 22% (440,000) are currently taking shelter in displacement sites around the country’s 10 States and 2 Administrative Areas, including former and current PoCs. Despite the signing of the R-ARCSS, sub-national conflict, cattle raiding and the unprecedented scale of natural disasters have displaced an additional 551,967 people between January and September 2021, topping 317,774 and 155,890 people in 2020 and 2019 respectively. According to OCHA, as of 28 October 2022, over 1 million people are affected by the current floods, with the affected population centered in Northern Bahr el-Ghazal (251,763), Warrap (208,133), Upper Nile (140,107), Unity (123,966), Western Equatoria (111,403), Lakes (93,193), Jonglei (67,409) and Eastern Equatoria (8,400) States. In addition, despite being at the frontlines of the flood response in Bentiu and Rubkona Towns since the onset of the crisis, IOM has worked hard with partners during the rainy season of 2022 for, in addition to the flood-affected host communities, the 57,924 people (DTM, July 2022) sheltering in the Bentiu displacement sites A-E and the 104,581 individuals (DTM, July 2022) already living in a protracted situation in the Bentiu IDP Camp. Accessibility for humanitarian responders remains a challenge due to cut supply lines and the increased price of commodities. The same can be said about the GPAA, where in addition access has been interrupted numerous times due to communal violence and threats to humanitarian organizations by youth associations in the area. Moreover, sub-national conflict in Warrap, Jonglei, Unity, Lakes, Upper Nile and WES occurring in 2021 have displaced around 220,000 individuals according to the 2022 HNO. A sizeable number of the displaced find shelter in informal settlements and collective centers – located in hard-to-reach locations and cut off by the effects of climate shocks like floods. The ability to quickly mobilize and respond to immediate displacement due to conflict and climate shocks is critical to reach people in need – and as such flexible and agile mobile responses, geared towards self-governance, empowerment and resilience will be essential. Displaced communities are in need of urgent humanitarian support, with most of their emergency coping mechanisms exhausted due to protracted displacement and repeated shocks. Regardless of whether the affected population has settled in formal IDP sites or informal settlements, the need for appropriate monitoring and coordination of humanitarian service provision and protection remains a critical enabler in aid delivery. Furthermore, the increase in both the frequency and intensity of climate-related shocks call for vigorous site development, care and maintenance interventions to be implemented by CCCM’s engineering teams to ensure and maintain a protective environment where humanitarian aid is accessible. These interventions need also be community-led to ensure that adequate capacity is built amongst the community to promote community resilience and the sustainability of responses. IOM CCCM’s core services and responsibilities will cater to the various displacement contexts under IOM’s AoR, including Bentiu, Naivasha and Hai Masna IDP Sites, Malakal PoC for Care & Maintenance, as well as targeted mobile responses in Warrap, WBeG, Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei, attempting to reach a target of 600,000 individuals.

2 - Project activities

The project activities will work to contribute to all 3 objectives as stipulated under the 2023 CCCM Cluster Response Plan. IOM CCCM will be conducting the following activities: 1) In former and current PoCs under IOM management, improve the living conditions of IDPs by ensuring practical gaps analysis, well-coordinated services in the camps and monitoring the equitable access to cross-sectoral humanitarian assistance, through CCCM core services, DRR-adapted site care and maintenance interventions and meaningful AAP-oriented community engagement activities. IOM CCCM will a) provide Camp Management coordination and information-sharing to partners including the CCCM Cluster, monitoring services to ensure equitable access to aid and protection by using referral mechanisms, providing capacity development and enhancing resilience to the displaced population including Dead Body Management (DBM) and fire prevention and response mechanisms; b) engage daily with representative governance and community protection structures – including women, PwD/PSN and youth; c) conduct daily CwC activities such as door-to-door sensitization, townhall and awareness sessions on relevant topics and camp services and improve access to information; d) maintain, improve and expand the use of communication centers in order to enhance CFM systems and accessibility to meaningful and effective feedback mechanisms, including through block-level outreach CFM; e) implement appropriate regular and emergency care and maintenance activities geared towards improving access, protection, sanitation conditions and congestion as well as mitigating the risks and impacts of climate-induced shocks such as floods; f) coordinate with Camp Administration (RRC) to ensure adequate coordination of services, including in identification, mitigation and response to protection risks, in close collaboration with Protection Cluster and GBV Sub-Cluster; g) lead and update elaboration of contingency plans in coordination with service providers; h) Strengthen resilience, prevention and response capacities of the affected population to climate shocks and epidemics through human-centered design (HCD) workshops, capacity building and information-sharing; i) collaborate with the CCCM Cluster and other CM Agencies in mapping displacement sites in IOM’s AoR; j) advocate for durable solutions and work towards sustainable and empowering interventions in CCCM’s programming, emphasizing AAP and the HDPN; k) provide CfW in planned and emergency community-led care and maintenance activities; l) support DTM in registration, maintenance and verification exercises as well as maintain and improve information management systems to monitor service provision and identify gaps; m) mainstream protection and AAP through satisfaction surveys, consultations and building trust with the community.2) In coordination with CCCM Cluster and the ICCG, deploy flexible mobile response teams to respond to new displacements due to conflict and natural disasters in hard-to-reach and under-served locations, prioritizing sustainability, ownership and self-governance. IOM CCCM will a) lead or participate as applicable in inter-cluster needs assessments and any other fact-finding missions; b) conduct CCCM Mobile Assessments in priority locations; c) produce service and site mappings, 3Ws and gap assessments in assessed locations; d) conduct capacity building, training and HCD workshops to representative governance structures, including women, PwD/PSN and youth, geared towards self-governance, entrepreneurship, improving life-skills, sustainable and sustainable site care and maintenance activities to prevent and mitigate risks as well as climate-induced shocks; e) set up CBCM committees in order to operate a meaningful and responsive CFM in hard-to-reach locations following a protection-reviewed SoP in coordination with partners; f) provide CfW in planned and emergency community-led care and maintenance activities.

3 - Project indicators

Monitoring for the success of this project will be facilitated by robust M&E mechanisms, overseen by a dedicated M&E and Reporting Officer, and will include methods such as beneficiary satisfaction surveys, perception surveys, regular consultations (FGDs and KIIs), weekly project indicator monitoring using kobo applications as well as monthly 5W mapping tools. IOM CCCM will contribute to fulfilling the Cluster’s indicators of success, namely the # of site population reached by CM services, % of sites improved through care and maintenance interventions, including community-based infrastructure rehabilitated through CfW initiatives, % of sites with adequate CM support and information dissemination, % of sites with functional referral pathways in place to ensure that PSNs receive the assistance and protection required, % of site population who are satisfied with overall levels of service, % of site population who consider the CFMs accessible, effective, confidential and safe, # of safety audits conducted in coordination with site level protection actors, % of recommended mitigation actions from safety audits directly integrated into site maintenance and addressed (including through robust mitigation measures), # of persons living with disabilities reporting to be satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence site decisions, # of displacement sites where planning is done with the participation of the camp population, # of site level care and maintenance activities implemented with a specific focus on PSNs, % of displacement sites where sex and age disaggregated data is regularly collected and shared with partners, # of community engagement initiatives at site level conducted i.e town hall meetings; elections; information campaigns, # of sites with established phase-out community participation structures and supported with self-management tools, # of displaced people in sites engaged in community level self-reliance activities, % of female committee members who feel their views are taken into account during decision-making processes, # of sites phased out and handed over to the community for self-management, % of members of site governance structures participating in site phase-out activities, IOM CCCM will also measure success of its activities through the HRP Accountability indicators in order to ensure strict and full adherence to AAP principles in its interventions. In line with objective 2.6 of the HRP, these indicators include # of beneficiaries who have access to safe, child and gender-sensitive CFM and reporting channels in humanitarian response sites for reporting sensitive issues and concerns in aid delivery and misconduct by aid workers disaggregated by age, sex and diversity, % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by age, sex and ability) who report they have access to CFMs, % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by age, sex and ability) who feel their opinions are taken into account in decision-making by humanitarians prior to the interventions (as well as during), % of cases received in a CFM that have been “considered as resolved”, % of national and local organizations and community members involved in decision-making mechanisms.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Any CVA intervention will be specifically tailored to the market conditions of that specific location. Any and all payment of work implemented in the camps will be valued according to the harmonized CCCM Cluster payment rates that are updated on a monthly basis. Most established markets in camp locations are formal and regulated by a Traders’ Union – with whom CCCM teams have regular engagement to discuss market-related issues. Due attention will be made to localized market conditions – and CVA interventions are implemented with the objective of facilitating economic recovery and enhance economic empowerment and resilience of the affected population.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

IOM CCCM will be using CVA intended to allow for specific and targeted community-led site care and maintenance activities in static and mobile locations. Care and maintenance activities remain a staple of all CCCM interventions for a number of reasons, including but not limited to the maintenance of drainages and culverts so as to improve sanitation conditions to prevent public health issues, the construction and rehabilitation of pathways and roads to improve humanitarian as well as community accessibility, improvements of protective environments, leveling of grounds for internal relocation exercises as well as garbage collection campaigns. With the scaled-up mitigation works to adapt to new climate behavior, IOM CCCM will also implement climate-adapted site development activities so as to mitigate risks of floods and other climate-related disasters, especially in anticipation of the rainy season. This includes the construction and reinforcement of dykes as well as the maintenance of drainages and water retention basins to ensure that storm water flows and is evacuated by CCCM trash pumps. Community-led activities, that are supported by institutionalized incentives, are key for a provision of temporary livelihoods and a sense of empowerment in the decisions that impact on the lives of the affected population. Specific CfW schemes that require different degrees of engineering/site planning knowledge will also be accompanied by trainings aimed at building capacity for longer term impacts of interventions. It is also worth noting that representative community structures, including elected leadership, women, youth and PwD/PSNs, will be consulted in the planning and implementation of these activities. The CfW also aims to support the families in addressing the unmet needs especially of the most vulnerable. The provision of cash assistance through CfW schemes will be informed by protection measures so as to ensure that beneficiaries are not over-exposed to protection risks in the receipt of cash. Providing cash over in-kind will allow for beneficiaries to earn a living and receive money in order to provide for their immediate needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In order to reach all segments of the displaced community, IOM CCCM implements an outreach CFM modality in order to adopt a proactive approach in the collection of beneficiary feedback. Community mobilizers are trained to receive cases in a sensitive manner in order to make the community member comfortable submitting feedback, complaints and requests on humanitarian services and assistance. Regular community engagement meetings are held between CCCM teams and representative governance structures, including elected community leaders, women and youth forums, as well as PwD/PSN committees – ensuring that any and all consultations are representative of traditionally marginalized groups. All communication and community engagement strategies, including beneficiary satisfaction surveys, will include an inclusion lens.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Embedded into core CCCM activities, CwC interventions will be mainstreamed throughout the project, with a strong emphasis on accountability. This will be done through different means, including but not limited to door-to-door or block-level (neighborhood-level) sensitization on key issues impacting the affected population (availability or changes in humanitarian assistance and delivery modalities, sensitization on existing epidemics, updates on humanitarian conditions, etc.), regular and constant engagement with representative community structures such as elected leadership, women, PwD/PSNs and youth in order to build trust, discuss and consult on key issues affecting the displaced population, capacity building and training on CCCM, PSEA, humanitarian principles, site development and care and maintenance as well as the implementation of Human-Centered Design Workshops in order to provide community groups the opportunity to identify issues within their own community and find solutions to these gaps through trouble-shooting and innovative entrepreneurship with CCCM support, the implementation of perception consultations through FGD and KIIs in order to maintain dialogue and ensure that the response is informed by accurate qualitative and quantitative evidence. Central to IOM CCCM operations are the development, maintenance and implementation of effective, responsive and culturally appropriate CFM in static locations and community-based complaints mechanisms (CBCM) in mobile locations. Based on a structured SOP and in collaboration with IOM's Global CCCM AAP team, IOM CCCM operates a kobo-based CFM application called Zite Manager in static locations - with communication centers and feedback desks located in safe and strategic locations - in order to receive complaints and feedback from the community on any and all humanitarian services and assistance. Zite Manager is also currently being implemented in Bangladesh and Mozambique - with planned expansion to Somalia. CCCM Community Mobilizers also conduct outreach CFM at block-level on a daily basis in order to reach the less mobile segments of the population and increase intake of the system, following the “reach out to the community” instead of the “wait and receive” approach. Cases are securely referred through a database on a weekly basis to responsible service providers for their feedback on prospective action taken to resolve non-sensitive complaints. Once feedback is received, CCCM ensures that the feedback loop is closed and provide a reply to the community based on feedback received. Reports are produced in order to inform the response and advocate for additional resources when gaps are identified.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM CCCM closely collaborates with IOM’s Protection, Gender Equality and Inclusion (PGEI) Unit, including IOM’s GBV specialists, in order to ensure that all guidelines, SOPs, ToRs and intervention plans are in line with humanitarian protection principles. In addition, as chair of the CCEWG, IOM CCCM works to constantly improve and provide teams with updated PSEA referral pathways in order to ensure adequate, confidential and effective handling of cases. PSEA focal points are designated in each project location and are tasked with confidentially receiving cases if any, raising awareness about PSEA amongst team members and the communities that IOM CCCM works with, and provide regular trainings for old and new staff brought on board. Their responsibilities also include training and sensitization sessions to the representative governance structures of the IDP communities that CCCM works with, including elected leadership, women’s committees, youth forums as well as PwD/PSN committees. Raising awareness about PSEA also extends to the CBCM Committees that IOM CCCM works with in hard-to-reach locations during mobile responses. Ensuring adequate sensitization and that all teams are aware of updated referral pathways remains a challenge – however, IOM CCCM continues to liaise with the Protection Cluster, the GBV Sub-Cluster as well as the PSEA Taskforce in order to ensure that all PSEA considerations are integrated into CCCM interventions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IOM CCCM, with additional technical support from the IOM Global CCCM AAP team, continues to work on improving the CFM system operating in static locations as well as the CBCM implemented in the various mobile responses in the country. In static locations, IOM CCCM operates a kobo-based CFM whereby complaints, feedback and requests are collected by trained community mobilizers in communication centers, feedback desks as well as through outreach CFM. Community mobilizers are trained in CCCM, CFM, as well as all core trainings including data protection principles, PSEA, standards of conduct, as well as the referral of sensitive cases. All cases received are integrated to the secure digital database maintained by the database clerks as well as the CCCM IM, M&E and Programme Officers under the supervision of the Programme Manager. On a weekly basis, cases are securely referred to the relevant service providers for their feedback and action. Upon receiving feedback from the relevant organization, the CFM team provides a reply to the complainant in order to close the feedback loop. A monthly internal report is produced with various analytical indicators for all teams to visualize the gaps experienced in specific locations – that then serve as strong evidence-based advocacy for allocation of resources and requests for improved support. These internal reports are shared to IOM teams operating on the ground but are also analyzed at national level in Juba in order to adequately monitor the situation in IOM CCCM’s various areas of operation. In addition to this, IOM CCCM is working hard to harmonize the CFM system operated in all static and mobile locations that currently being operated in order to ensure that the “same language” is spoken when implementing CFM and enhance the as well as AAP mechanisms and information management of the system to improve the delivery of services through advocacy and adequate overview of gaps in specific locations at given times. Perceptions surveys are conducted on various thematic issues affecting the communities - with reports shared to partners and community to strengthen programming. Beneficiary Satisfaction Surveys are implemented on a bi-annual (planned for thrice/year) basis in order to measure the quality of IOM CCCM services in the locations that are worked in. This exercise also includes qualitative consultations such as KIIs and FGDs in order to truly capture needs through transparent dialogue with different segments of the affected population. In mobile locations, CBCM Committees are formed and community mobilizers trained in line with the IOM CCCM CBCM SOP in order to receive cases in hard-to-reach and under-served areas of intervention and ensure efficient and appropriate referral to the responsible service providers.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

In order to mitigate the risks posed to the affected population, to ensure that the intervention adopts a do-no-harm approach and that it emphasizes the centrality of protection, any and all interventions will be preceded by robust risk and protection risk assessments conducted by multi-cluster assessments, the office for staff safety and security (OSS) and IOM’s dedicated team of conflict analysts that provide analysis and recommendations in order to adopt a do-no-harm approach taking into consideration all political, social, cultural and economic aspects of the location. These analyses are also complemented and informed by ICCG assessments conducted by specialized clusters and sub-clusters as well as the UN Department for Safety and Security (UNDSS) and UNMISS. IOM CCCM regularly coordinates interventions with security and protection teams to be appropriately informed about risks – and hence work on effective community-based protection measures that are aimed to improving the safety of the affected population, especially women, girls, boy, the elderly and PwD/PSNs – and reducing the risks of violence and GBV in humanitarian settings. Safety audits will also be periodically conducted in collaboration with the GBV sub-cluster in order to improve accessibility and the safe delivery of CCCM services, including site planning, care and maintenance activities. In mobile responses, IOM CCCM will coordinate and ensure regular information-sharing with protection and conflict sensitivity partners in order to better comprehend the conflict, political and cultural dynamics at play in the various locations of intervention. Interventions will mainstream the centrality of protection and AAP and will address the risks posed by PSEA through regular staff training, community-level awareness raising, the update of referral pathways. To reduce the risk of violence, including GBV, during humanitarian activities such as distribution points, IOM CCCM will ensure that protection partners are present during such distributions to monitor and provide a focal point to resolve any and all protection issues that may arise. In CCCM mobile responses, in order to mitigate the risks posed by HLP issues, IOM CCCM in collaboration with IOM conflict analysts will conduct assessments in order to identify political dynamics at plan in regard to land as well as possible risks of disputes on land ownership. Consultations with relevant stakeholders such as community leaders, the government, OCHA and other partners concerning land usage will ensure that no political ambitions will be unintentionally supported during relocation or evacuation exercises.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

In Bentiu and Rubkona, since August 2021, IOM CCCM has been at the forefront of and leading the Flood Response in collaboration with the State Government, UNMISS and other partners. IOM CCCM has deployed heavy-duty machinery (HDM), including 3 excavators, 2 dump trucks, a grader, a backhoe, a roller compactor as well as trash pumps in order to support the flood response in Bentiu and Rubkona Towns and the Bentiu IDP Site. 2 additional excavators are currently being transported to Bentiu in order to scale-up flood response and mitigation activities ahead of the rainy season. Any and all site care and maintenance activities, community-informed or community-led, are implemented with a disaster-risk reduction and durability lens in order to mainstream resilience to climate shocks. These activities are implemented not only in static locations (Bentiu, Naivasha, Hai Masna IDP Sites and Malakal PoC), but also in mobile displacement locations that IOM CCCM is currently responding to and any future priority locations (Jonglei, Upper Nile, WBeG and Unity).
