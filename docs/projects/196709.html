Project Summary

Psychosocial support activities through the rehabilitation of 1 child friendly space (CFS) in Areesha and Washokani Camps each in Al Hasakah. The CFS allows children (boys and girls) from the ages of 8 - 24 to access daily PSS and recreational activities to strengthen resilience and enhance protection. This project builds on the previous projects implemented by the Peace and Civil Society Center (PCSC) and in collaboration with Camp Authorities and Camp Management as well as other protection Partners in the targeted camps. This project takes into account the participation rates is 50% for boys and 50% for girls. The priority of selecting the children are those most in need and most at risk, such as displaced children and children with disabilities. The CFS will have a Safety Box set up in a confidential, accessible and safe space identified based on participatory and consultative sessions that were held with children and caregivers. The Safety Box, is outside of the CFS and allows children to report incidents relating to violence and all complaints issues. This will not be only complaints mechanism for children and other suitable reporting channels are available for children with disabilities and children who cannot report due to illiteracy or language barriers. Peace and Civil Society Center has hotlines and e-mail to allow to record any complaints arising from staff or others. Complaints will be tracked through the complaints and suggestions system, which PCSC trained CP focal Points at the sites and will manage and follow up on confidentially. The FPs are trained on procedure for complaints, in addition to referral to specialized services in the two camps, which will be primarily followed up through by the case management department and the Community Workers Section to make referrals to receive appropriate services. The project aims to support children, especially children who have been subjected to physical violence through the awareness and PSS sessions and workshops that will be held targeting children and caregivers with positive behaviors and changing of attitudes towards childcaring and child raising.
also project aims to increase the awareness of caregivers and teachers about positive parenting and the negative effects of using physical violence as a tool for educating children and behavior correction. through Positive discipline training workshops targeting groups of parents and teachers, enabling them to know safe alternative methods to physical violence and enabling them to deal with their children in a non-violent manner. In addition to awareness sessions for children and Caregivers about rights and duties through sensitizations on early marriage consequences, physical violence, bulling. Boys and girls who participate in the cfs will be sensitized with information on child rights as well as information on services, schools and education and to report on incidents of violence.

3 - Needs

Several activities have been designed to ensure a positive treatment of the problems of physical violence against children through appropriate preventive and curative interventions that are based on minimum standards for the protection of children in emergencies, and to provide opportunities for boys and girls to access psychosocial support activities through the establis and activation of child friendly spaces that enable the implementation of PSS activities daily with boys and girls.Based on the previous observations, PCSC conducted a questionnaire about physical violence and its prevalence in the city of Hasaka in each of the neighborhoods (Tal Hajar, Nazareth and Al-Kalasa) and five schools in the neighborhood of Tal Hajar, at the beginning of June 2022 for 300 boys and girls, through interviews Individuals, including 141 boys and girls, were subjected to physical violence, i.e. 47%, and these practices are carried out in homes, schools and in open spaces by parents, teachers and peers. based on HNO 2023 more than 6 million children, including with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, and need critical child protection services. ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement and limited access to basic services continue to have a significant effect on physical and mental well being of children and caregivers

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will commence by launching of activities in the presence of the entire project staff. one day for the project management and support departments, and the second day with the project staff. 14 facilitators will continue to work on this project from 2022. Trainings and on-boarding sessions will be delivered to new staff to have a mainstreamed approach on child pss activities.centers in areesha and washokani will be rehabilitated and equipped for winter season to provide appropriate conditions for children cfs. Resilience, positive discipline and recreational activities for the protection of children will be facilitated based on minimum standards. Project will accommodate at least 400 boys and 150 girls at risk identified based on previous projects.The cfs will provide pfa support and pss activities, recreational activities and pss kits for children. based on previous assessments, 5% of children affected by protection risks link them to specialized service providers for CP case management. PCSC will collaborate and coordinate with other protection partners in camps and CP sWG. regular reporting on activities by MEAL staff to make sure of project delivery and report under 4w. project will have 4 child Clubs. 20 children from age 9 - 12, boys and girls, will participate in sports and recreational activities 3 - 4 months to have positive coping mechanism for children and pss. activities designed by age and organised by school shifts

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks and Obstacles inside the Camp:- The spread of narcotic pills among the youth group in the camp, and they must be sensitized.throuh awareness campagin and in collaboration with CA and CM- The appearance of rabid dogs in the camp, and the number of cases that were bitten by a dog reached 5 and they must be sensitized.throuh awareness campagin and in collaporation with CA CM- delay in arrival of food supplies to Areesha camp, distribution has not taken place since the seventh month, will be an obstacke for caregivers to access the center because they need to work for food.- Sewers and rain are exposed, and they impede the movement of children, children with disability and adults. there have been several accidents due to this, especially present in Stage 3 and 4 at washokani camp.- large number of child labor in the camp may be a challange to enter the safe space- increase child marriages in the camp, due to customs and traditions, as well as the difficult economic situation inside the camp.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Refreshes are made for all employees, workers and contractors working with PCSC. PCSC has a hotline and whatsapp number to record complaints as well as PSEA FP at the site to receive complaints.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks :1- Non-approval of the Organizations Affairs Office to implement the project:2- The winter season affects the children’s participation in the project’s activities.Measure: Providing the friendly space with safe heating methods3- School times: The center will work in two shifts, morning and evening, in line with the children’s school hours, according to the age groups. each age group will be targeted with the end of their school shift to make sure the activities dont clash with the school time3- The security situation is tense as a result of the events in the region: 4- Parents’ lack of acceptance of sending their children to the center’s activities.Treatment: Conducting a community mobilization through holding meetings with committees in the neighborhoods and holding meetings with the people to familiarize the neighborhood residents with the center and project activities, preparing a brochure containing adequate information about the center and its activities and distributing it to the neighborhood residents.
