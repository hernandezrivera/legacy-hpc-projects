Project Summary

The project will aim to provide essential health care services for the IDPs in the camps and returnees in the remote locations where the routine health care services have not been established yet. In addition to that, the project will emphasize the transition and integration of humanitarian health care services into the national health system.
The response strategy will include providing the following services to the target population
1. Provide comprehensive primary health care services including improved physical and mental health screening facilities
2. Continue and improve referral health services with standard physical and mental health services
3. Transpose the Early warning and response network of communicable diseases and initiate integration of early warning system into the routine disease surveillance system
4. Conduct assessments, surveys and case studies to enhance information management, evaluate the quality of services and plan for evidence-based interventions through an integrated health care service.

Needs

The conflicts in Iraq internally displaced more than 5.9 million people by December 2017. The Displacement tracking matrix (DTM) of IOM published in Oct 2018 records, 1,866,594 people (31%) remain displaced across the 18 Governorates of Iraq. Among them; 558612 (30%) are living in IDP camps and 70% are living in the host communities. The updated distribution of all IDPs, by geographic location, shows that nine governorates host 94.77% of all existing IDPs. Ninawa governorate hosting the highest number of displaced persons with 584,520 individuals, followed by Dahuk 347,232, Erbil 213,402, Salah Al-Din 151,920, Sulaymaniyah 150,876, Kirkuk 113,688, Baghdad 75,048 and Anbar with 70074 individuals. The updated figures of returnees as of October 2018 shows that 4,113,624 individuals have returned to their homes of origin across Iraq (that is 69% of overall IDPs) have returned to their place of origin. The highest number of returnees recorded in Ninewa governorate with 1,591,734 returnees, followed by Anbar with 1,283,292, Sala Al-Din with 575,676 and Kirkuk with 313,788 individuals. The updated hospital functionality assessment reveals that the bed capacity of the existing hospitals have been reduced by half from the capacity they had before 2014. The data also reiterate that 16% of the hospitals are not functional or completely damaged, 44% of the hospitals are partially functional with limited services and 40% are functional with a limited number of beds. The IDPs including those who are in the camps, dispersed within the host communities, returnees returning to their place of origin and vulnerable people from the host communities continue to be in dire need of essential health care services that have been impaired due to the conflict. Hence the secondary lifesaving referral health care services in the IDP and returnee locations have to be prioritized and restored as early as possible. Besides, continuing conflict in the disputed areas and prevailing political and economic situation in the country is anticipated to hinder the resettlement of the IDPs in some parts of Ninawa and Anbar governorates. Thus, the biggest IDP camps existing in Ninawa, Salah Al-Din, Dahuk, Kirkuk, Erbil, and Anbar are supposed to be operational until December 2019. Concurrently, a considerable proportion of the IDPs returning to Ninawa, Anbar, Salah Al-Din, and Kirkuk have limited access to essential health care services including primary and secondary referral health care services as they have been severely damaged during the recent military operations. The Iraqi population has been subjected to constant conflict, murder, violent attacks, torture, kidnapping, child soldiering and other crimes, making the need to create a strong, adequately structured, properly funded, and staffed mental health system in its post-conflict transitional stages. In one study by the Iraq Ministry of Health, over 70% of primary school students were affected by trauma-related stress, most of these children remain untreated. Women are also in an extremely vulnerable position as they are in constant fear of being kidnapped and raped because of the recent violence and security problems.A 2016 Report on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities in Iraq identifies a constant increase in the number of persons with disabilities throughout the country and recommends that the specialized health-care/psycho-social support should be improved. One of the assessments done in three IDP camps in January 2018 revealed that there are almost two out of 1000 people who are physically disabled and needed support. This estimation would make at least 12,000 people among the IDPs and returnees in need of physical rehabilitation support. However the physical rehabilitation centers in the conflict-affected governorates are dysfunctional and temporarily supported by the center in Sulaymaniyah, that could serve only 360 persons per year. Hence revitalize the physical rehabilitation service in the governorates identified as a priority.Public health services such as childhood vaccination, water, and sanitation services are damaged in the conflict-affected locations and increasing the risk of vaccine-preventable, waterborne, water washed, and vector-borne diseases. The EWARN recorded more than 2.2 million communicable diseases, 92 alerts and 2 major outbreaks among the population in the IDP camps and host communities in 2017. Considering the number of cholera cases (505) in 2017 and the serotypes (Inaba and Ogawa) and the number of governorates (11 out of 19) reported with cholera cases, the risk of another cholera outbreak during 2019 is very high. Evaluations are done by WHO revealed that the medical waste management and water quality monitoring in the PHCCs are not up to the standard, this could increase the risk of transmission of water and blood-borne diseases. Hence there is a need to strengthen communicable disease prevention, surveillance, early detection, and response.

Activities

WHO believes the following interventions could address the needs of the existing IDPs, returnees and host communities.1.1. Cover the operational cost of seven existing PHCCs in selected IDP camps benefiting an estimated 200,000 IDPs with essential primary health care services. 1.2. Cover the operational cost of 20 MMCs in remote returnee locations benefiting 240,000 returnees and host communities in Anbar, Kirkuk, Salah Al-Din and Ninawa governorates with essential primary health care services. 1.3. Establish five fixed PHCCs (using prefabricated caravans) that are damaged during the recent military operations and nonfunctional or partially functioning in Ninawa, Anbar, Kirkuk and Salah Al-Din. 1.4. Establish and support the operational cost of five standard medical waste treatment units in Anbar (2), Salahaldeen (1), Ninawa (1), and Kirkuk (1). 1.5. Provide essential medicines, kits, medical supplies and diagnostic equipment to support partners who are providing humanitarian health care services.1.6. Support referral capacities of five hospitals in Ninawa, Anbar, Kirkuk, Salah Al-Din and Dahuk by covering gaps in operational cost as well as providing the necessary medicines, kits, medical supplies, and equipment; 1.7. Adopt standard uniform guidelines, tools, and SOPs as well as provide training on mental health, physical rehabilitation, and rational use of medicines for all humanitarian health care service providers.1.8. Prevention and control of communicable disease outbreaks in high-risk locations through early warning alert and response network (EWARN) 1.9. Conduct assessments, surveys, and provide information management services that would enable WHO and its partners to evaluate their interventions and develop evidence-based response plans for the future.

Protection Risk Analysis

The displaced and returning children affected by the conflict have not been had routine vaccination coverage and under the risk of infected with vaccine-preventable diseases.The fertile women among the IDPs, returnees and host communities without proper PHC services are under high risk of pregnancy related complicationsThe men, women and children affected by mental disorders are under high risk of abuse and exclusionThe disabled men, women and children are under high risk of abuse and exclusion too.

Mitigation Measures

Most of the project activities will reduce the protection risk by providing preventive and curative health care services to vulnerable groups and providing mental health and physical rehabilitation services.Particularly vaccinations services for the infants and children, antenatal and postnatal care for pregnant women, mental health services for women, children and men and physical rehabilitation services for the disabled will contribute to the protection of the vulnerable groups.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The affected population is consulted during assessment processes to identify the current need and they are included in the service provisions such as vaccination campaigns, awareness campaigns, screening process as volunteers. In the past, the health care workers and social workers among the IDPs are recruited for the PHC service delivery in the camps, returnee locations and host communities, the practice will be continued in the current project as well. The beneficiaries and the community leaders are always included in the rapid assessment and M&E process through interviews, and group discussions in the past and the practice will be continued during the current project as well. Further, the call centers and complaint boxes in the IDP camps provide an opportunity to hear the concerns from the affected population.
