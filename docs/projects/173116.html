Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in increasing safe access to FSL, and WASH Services to Refugees and Host Communities, HOPE will target 4 refugee camps and hosts community in Elsalam and Kosti localities in WN, and 2 communities in Jebel Aulia of KH state (Bantiu & Dar-alsalam), and 2 refugee camps in Gadaref state (Village 08 & Um Rakuba), 8 communities in total. In the FSL component, HOPE will focus on conducting trainings on vocational & language training, financial literacy, business, and entrepreneurship followed up with coaching and mentoring support can help refugees to benefit from their existing skills and build on them to further increase their income, support livelihoods opportunities in the agricultural sector including the provision of inputs and productive assets to increase farming outputs, agricultural extension services will be pursued to facilitate access to seasonal labor and farming opportunities including training and can community-based farming, provision of income-generating support and business training for women with revolving loans VSLA as well as establish and functional literacy classes using REFLECT approach for women and youth-adult education. In WASH component, HOPE will focus on the construction of shared HH latrines, promoting COVID-19 prevention protocols and increase the availability of handwashing stations at schools, conduct training on Hygiene Promotion, provide hygiene services through hygiene promotion messages, provide menstrual hygiene materials for women and girls, establish hygiene clubs for children in schools, provide basic water services through operation, maintenance, modification of water distribution system and rehabilitation of Water Yards, installation, and connection of water tank in basic schools, and training of Water User Committee on O&M.
HOPE will consider accountability to people affected through full community participation/involvement, set feedback & compliant mechanism, strengthen community mechanisms, as well as do, follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify good practices & lessons learned and give recommendations to improve project outcomes.

1 - Needs

Many refugees are concerned about the prevalence of SGBV in their communities. SGBV risk is aggravated by inadequate lighting in camps/settlements, and water supply gaps that require women and girls to travel long distances to collect water and firewood, and lack of toilets. The lack of livelihood opportunities, the deteriorating economic situation, and the COVID-19 pandemic further undermine the safety and well-being of refugee women, girls, and boys. In early Nov 2020, the refugee from Ethiopia influx to East Sudan, on 7 Dec the total refugees arrived in Sudan are 49,248 (12,565 of them arrived in Um Rakouba – Gadaref state).The available livelihood opportunities for refugees are largely in the informal sector with high uncertainty of daily labor accompanied by high risks of exploitation, sexual harassment, and abuse. Consequently, the lack of income also undermines refugees’ housing security, with many reporting being unable to cover increasing costs for services and food.In many parts of Sudan agriculture including the White Nile is the main source to earn a livelihood for refugees and host communities. For this reason, there is a need to develop capacity in the agricultural sector along with the provision of inputs and strengthening of extension services. Small scale home gardens are needed to provide opportunities for women and are also suitable for urban settings. Access to extension services needs to be strengthened to increase value-adding opportunities for farmers which go hand in hand with awareness-raising on existing services and how to access them.Access to sufficient safe water is a fundamental human need. While water supply quantities average 14 liters per person per day for South Sudanese refugees across the response, some areas remain well below response standards with just 7 liters per person per day, especially during the dry season. Reduced water quantities are also a result of erratic fuel supplies on the local market. The solarization of some water facilities is urgently required to increase sustainability and reduce the cost and environmental impact. High latrine usage ratios remain a major challenge in Bantiu and um Rakouba, open defecation due to poor latrine coverage and poor hygiene practices remains an ongoing challenge. The record rains and flooding in 2020 has worsened the latrine situation across the country, with some latrines having collapsed, worsening the plight of the refugees. To date, 962 latrines have collapsed (187 out of them in Bantiu). Household latrines remain the preferred approach together with family shared latrines where space availability is a challenge. Jerry can replenishment and cleaning, and hygiene promotion are urgently needed. Khartoum “Open Areas” have largely remained underserved with very limited partners with limited funding to cover the overwhelming sanitation needs in all the refugee-hosting locations. Consistent and regular distribution of Personal Hygiene items for Menstrual Hygiene Management remains an area requiring urgent attention for all refugees. Acute watery diarrhea (AWD) risks still persist across many States. Investments in innovative low-cost household and hygiene awareness are urgently needed to mitigate ongoing risk. Installation of handwashing facilities at all schools, health facilities, and marketplaces and ensuring the consistent provision of soap of at least 450/p/month across all locations is highly recommended to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and other diseases. Most schools do not have adequate WASH facilities. Many of these schools also do not have access to water that would enable them to comply with the new COVID-19 protocols set out by the Federal Ministry of Education (FMoE).Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for the provision of FSL, and WASH Services to Refugees and Host Communities in Khartoum, White Nile, and Gadaref States.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design, HOPE assessed the safety and security for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to WASH and livelihood opportunities. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to WASH outreach and awareness-raising. All WASH volunteers will be trained on O&M and water yard management and linking directly to experts from other agencies and the government is already supporting the WASH sector. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy for HH shared latrines will be considering. Awareness-raising during community mobilization on WASH, REFLECT approach for adult education as literacy classes a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. Conducting community safety audits and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
