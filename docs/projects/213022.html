Project Summary

The project will support the most vulnerable population in NWS and NCS through; providing business skills, vocational and life skills training ending with financial support to small and medium enterprises and provide short-term employment opportunities through cash-for-work support, apprenticeship and interimship. Additionally, the project will provide the most vulnerable HHs with market-based assistance. The project will promote the usage of renewable energy by supporting farmers with solar-panelled irrigation solutions.
SR will provide financial support to 500 new entrepreneurs to start their first entrepreneurship by providing small/medium cash grants. The grant will be delivered in 3 instalments according to the business plan. The completion of each stage will trigger the release of the following instalment, SR will measure the progress of the business plan implementation through multiple evaluation methodologies, including; field visits, review of business cost/benefit analysis and reviewing the remaining period plan to ensure business success.
SR will enable 2000 workers (12000 individuals) to meet their basic needs by providing Monthly wages over 6 months. Each HH will receive (5$ unskilled and 8$ semiskilled daily) on an average 130$ per month (as per ERL cluster recommendation). Due to the deteriorating status of both IDPs and host communities, SR will target both groups. To ensure that the most vulnerable HHs selected, SR will conduct 100% door-to-door registration and verification and select the workers according to SR eligibility and vulnerability scoring system.
The project will provide the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households mainly), on average 1000 HHs, with seed funding to start their own business in their homes, which will enable them to earn their income and become self-resilient. SR will conduct community consultation sessions to identify common skills for females in the targeted locations and potential ideas to be implemented under this activity and will construct the vulnerabilities and eligibilities together with community representatives.
Additionally, the project will enable 3000 farmers (15,000 individuals) to improve access to irrigation by providing solar-panelled irrigation pumps, which will reduce the cost and improve the yield quantity/quality and will be reflected in a higher profit for farmers.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

SR aim to support 125 entrepreneurs by providing cash grants to kickstart their first business ventures. These grants will be distributed in three phases, contingent upon the successful completion of each stage of the business plan, with progress evaluated through various methodologies. To address immediate needs, SR will offer financial assistance to 500 households (comprising 3,000 individuals), ensuring they can meet basic needs through monthly wages over six months. Payments are structured to reflect skill levels, with unskilled workers receiving $5 and semi-skilled workers $8 per day, averaging $130 per month in line with ERL cluster recommendations. This initiative aims to support both internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities amidst worsening conditions. Additionally, SR plans to empower particularly vulnerable groups, such as female-headed households (approximately 250), by providing seed funding to start home-based businesses, fostering self-reliance. Community consultations will help identify viable business ideas and skills prevalent among women in targeted areas. To enhance agricultural productivity, SR will equip 750 farmers (4,500 individuals) with solar-panelled irrigation pumps, promoting sustainable practices and reducing operational costs. Across all project stages, SR is committed to integrating protection and gender considerations, conducting needs assessments, and ensuring equitable access to resources. Special attention is given to preventing sexual exploitation, abuse, and gender-based violence, with specific measures like training for staff and vendors, awareness campaigns, and establishing a complaints and protection reporting mechanism. Operational risks such as market dysfunctionality, currency fluctuations, and potential lockdowns due to health crises are acknowledged, with strategies in place to mitigate these challenges. Security risks are also considered, with contingency plans for maintaining project integrity in the face of threats. This comprehensive approach demonstrates SR’s commitment to not only providing immediate financial relief but also fostering long-term sustainability and resilience among vulnerable communities.
