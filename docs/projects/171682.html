Project Summary

Recurring climate shocks, including drought and flooding as well as ongoing conflict contribute to growing internal displacement in Somalia, with an estimated 2.6 million women, men, girls and boys currently displaced. The IDPs are currently residing in urban and Peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. They remain in need of multi-sectoral support services including protection from harsh climatic conditions, improvements to their living conditions and improvement to access livelihoods and durable solutions. The most vulnerable groups, who are exposed to the highest risks and are consistently socially excluded, include women, children, the elderly, child- and female-headed households, the physically and mentally disabled people living in camp and camp-like settings.
The Conflict induced crisis in Juballand between AMISOM/SNA on one hand and Al-shabab on the other hand may even continue to perpetuate more displacement in 2021 and present more protection concerns on displaced persons, including women and girls resulting an alarming levels of SGBV in IDP settlements.
As per the last IDP site verification exercise in Kismayo on September 2020 conducted by the CCCM partners and JRIA, there are 144 IDP sites hosting 65,051 persons in the four IDP sections in Kismayo town. The IDPs in Kismayo and Dollow are concentrated in sub-standard sites which are either privately owned land or government owned without any formalized land tenure agreement. This precipitate frequent evictions and continued displacement that contribute to protection risk particularly women and girls. There is a strong need to assess risk and observe vulnerability of displaced populations in Kismayo and Dollow. The Shelter conditions of the IDPs are made up of locally made materials and old pieces of clothes that expose harsh climatic conditions and infringe privacy. Limited knowledge on fire prevention and awareness, poor drainage systems and lack of protection emergency items such as solor lamps, poor physical structure such as fence remains priority as part of routine site maintenance and site improvement activities.
Through this project, SFH aims to address immediate needs and improve the living conditions of the newly displaced and the protracted IDPs living in a deplorable conditions in Kismayo, Dolow and Afmadow districts of Lower Juba and Gedo region by strengthening protection referral pathways, support and participate coordination mechanism that advocate for provision of essential social services to IDPs, and contribute to community-driven site maintenance activities including infrastructure rehabilitation and safe spaces, build existing governance structures on CCCM principles and participate safety audits assessments in the target sites while working towards transitioning to durable solutions.
SFH will train 50 duty bearers and gate keepers on HLP (Housing, Land and Property) to improve their knowledge and understanding of HLP issues. SHF will also train its CCCM staff on site planning and layout to ensure that all site maintenance projects implemented in the target district meet the required standards.
Assistance will be provided in accordance with minimum humanitarian standards of service delivery and protection while ensuring every member of the IDP community participate in the activities directly or through inclusive representation including persons with disability. SFH will conduct regular monitoring to track population changes and communal facility inspections to ensure that the camp is properly maintained and/ or upgraded to meet Sphere standards and the changing needs of the IDPs. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to content with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. Relevant consultations will be made with the Protection and GBV sub-clusters.

Project Activities

Camp management is about providing Assistance and Protection to the camp dwellers in accordance with the relevant laws and minimum humanitarian standards while ensuring inclusive participation and equitable access to assistance without exclusion or discrimination. In line with this, SFH will contribute directly to the cluster objectives of 1) Improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance and de-congestion initiatives. 2) Strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations. SFH will coordinate with camp management agencies and CCCM cluster to ensure realization of objective 1 of the cluster through active participation of the cluster operations and joint advocacy on the needs of the IDPs.SFH will improve the living conditions of the displaced populations through site care and maintenance activities that will enable them access income through conditional cash for work approach involving rehabilitation of communal infrastructures, safe spaces, drainage systems, operation and maintenance of Water and Sanitation facilities including desludging of sewerage systems and participating paid communal clean-up and Solid waste management activities.SFH will support camp management and camp coordination activities taking place in Juballand and contribute to site level actor’s and camp management agency's effort to address the increasing needs for the displaced families. SFH will also participate site identifications, mapping, monitoring and help de-congest site and relocate the IDPs where applicable. Through routine monitoring exercise and coordination, SFH will help increase CCCM coverage through monitoring the displacement conditions in Juballand and provide baseline information on multi-sectorial issues and needs of the displaced population which will be captured in the master database that will be shared with the humanitarian actors for planning and response. Where feasible SFH will support the site development (preparation, improvement and expansion) in Juballand to de-congest sites and mitigate furthers risks in protection and health.To enhance protection and ensure physical and material security of the displaced population particularly women and girls, SFH will conduct safety audits to identify the specific needs of the women and girls and locate routes associated with high risk of GBV. SFH will Coordinate with protection and GBV partners and establish strong referral pathway. Organize inclusive sensitization session with SMC, Community leaders, men, women and person with disability and disseminate information about availability of humanitarian assistance including Clinical management of rape, Psycho-social and mental health counseling and dignity kits. SFH will Install Street solar lights at risk prone locations identified as result of the safety audit assessment and Provide Household Solar lanterns to 2000 most vulnerable households that are far from street light and at high risk of GBV. Establish responsive complaint and response feedback mechanism that will enable beneficiaries provide timely and confidential feedback.SFH will train 50 duty bearers and SMC on HLP (Housing, Land and Property) to improve their knowledge and understanding of HLP issues. SFH will also train its CCCM staff on site planning and layout to ensure that all site maintenance projects implemented in the target district meet the required standards.To enhance the capacity of the existing governance structures,SFH will Conduct 5 days capacity building on CCCM modalities that involves: Humanitarian principles, actors coordination mechanism, camp management house( assistance, protection, law and standards and participation-which are all essential throughout the 3 phases of camp life (set-up, care and maintenance and closure), roles and responsibilities, data collection and information management systems, prevention, monitoring and referral of protection risk.

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.Mitigation Measures:a) Distribution of protection emergency items such as solar lamp will be distributed at day time and accessible area with the support of local authorities and protection committees.b) SFH will liaise and coordinate with mine action actors to map routes with safety risks and associated with landmines, explosive remnants of war (ERW)and busy roads where applicable. As a member of Jubaland mine action network consortium, SFH will pay special focus on this subject and ensure camps and camp-like settings are not placed in areas associated with mine and UXOs.c) SFH will Coordinate with protection actors to identify paths and routes that are highly associated with protection risks and particularly GBV and will subsequently share this information with the SMC, community leaders, men, women and girls for prevention until durable solutions to reverse the situation is achieved.2). Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.).Mitigation
