Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to increase the access to quality education and PSS services in target areas by implementing a combination of high quality formal and non-formal education activities along with a comprehensive package of support that complement each other where possible. PIN takes an integrated and holistic approach to address Education in Emergencies (EiE) and Child Protection needs. PIN employs a dual approach to supporting education through establishing temporary learning centres (TLCs) for provision of PSS and non-formal education using different modalities such as the Self-Learning Programme (SLP) curriculum and accelerated learning programme (ALP) in areas with acute concentrations of OOSC – including in IDP camps and informal settlements; and assistance to formal schools where education has been disrupted through providing construction and repair works, school furniture and equipment, teaching and learning materials, support for running costs, PSS sessions are provided to all enrolled students and teachers as its been integrated to schools regular timetables.
Additionally, provision of after-school catch-up and remedial classes to support pathways into education and reduce drop-out. The successful transition of children to formal schools, emphasizing the importance of providing Psychosocial Support Services (PSS) to aid in this process. This support is not only crucial for children who have faced significant challenges in recent years but also essential for helping them adjust to their new circumstances. Additionally, our focus extends to caregivers, who play a central role in the well-being and resilience of the children under their care. PIN takes an evidence-based approach to programming based on rigorous feedback loops to promote accountability and enhance impact. All activities will be implemented in line with relevant Education cluster tools, materials, and approaches, in addition to core INEE and Sphere standards, with a focus upon promoting safe learning spaces and essential WASH facilities and maximizing accessibility for those at risk of education disruption, including girls and children living with disabilities. All our NFE activities are aligned with the NFE matrix developed by developed by the Non-Formal Education Task Force and thoroughly reviewed by the Technical Working Group. Also , involvement of PIN in assessment led by cluster to ensure evidence based approaches. Teaching and student kits will be provided to facilitate quality learning opportunities and minimize education barriers for children from low-income HH.
Additionally, in efforts to support community cohesion and raise awareness PIN will be supporting schools and the surrounding communities through caregiver awareness sessions, training for teachers, school management and cleaners. The process will ensure the participation of relevant stakeholders to consider local priorities while also ensuring that transparency is at the forefront and that PIN is accountable to the populations in its areas of intervention. Assessments will be conducted by PIN staff and engineers in collaboration with local authorities, where relevant. PIN will target communities where education services have been disrupted due to conflict, where there is no, or limited, access to education. Experience highlights that significant gaps also exist in such areas to meet teachers’ and students’ needs for safe and quality learning. There will be a strong focus on community outreach and engagement throughout the entire cycle, including caregivers. All activities have the final aim of reintegrating children into education through formal and non-formal accelerated learning programmes and the creation of child protection safety nets. PIN is providing a holistic approach by providing access to Education the learning enters and providing numeracy and literacy condensed courses.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Schools and Temporary Learning Centres (TLCs) will be supported and established, providing non-formal and formal education with integrated PSS activities. Trained schoolteachers and facilitators provide teaching for classes with support from PIN and identify cases with protection concerns and physical disabilities to be referred to external service provider. Activities foreseen include setting up non-formal learning centers, construction work and light rehabilitation for schools and classrooms, monthly operational costs, and school supplies including water trucking, Fuel for winter months, cleaning kits, teaching and learning materials for formal and non-formal settings. PIN will provide quality physical classes, distributing essential items for quality learning and ensure safe access to integrated PSS activities. PIN will also run mobile PSS activities to reach children who can't access TLCs. In 2024, PIN will implement community PSS intervention for adolescent and will also invest more in empowering students’ caregivers and promoting awareness through conducting parenting sessions. Learning outcomes will be monitored by ASER tests. Learning outcomes will be measured by PIN's monitoring and evaluation team by ASER tests. The ‘Annual Status of Education Report' (ASER) tools are internationally recognized as a reliable tool to measure reading fluency and comprehension as well as arithmetic and basic numeracy. PIN coordinates with other clusters to make sure assistance is not duplicated. Based on HNO 2024, the education system in Syria remains overstretched. There were a total of 25 attacks on educational facilities in 2022. 6.9M Syrians are in need of educational services with 97% being children. HNO assessments reveal that 12% of children are out of school. This assessment confirms the need for comprehensive educational support, especially the establishment of learning spaces and provision of learning materials, and training and incentives for qualified teachers. As in the previous years, the number of children attending school is the lowest for the IDP population in sites/camps. So far, PIN has addressed these needs by beginning the process of setting up and repairing learning centers in communities where high numbers of children are out of school, conducting teacher training and promoting PSS activities. PIN is also conducting parenting sessions to promote awareness among caregivers. Additionally, PINs protection team will continue to build strong relationships with community members and to closely coordinating with community leaders and key focal points through regular meetings and field visits to ensure that Protection mainstreaming standards are implemented and met as they are integral part of the program's design and implementation.
