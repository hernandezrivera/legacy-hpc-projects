Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Sudan is not showing improvements as access to some of the basic services are becoming more and more challenging for the population. The humanitarian crisis driven by the annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis is manifested by some of the key indicators. According to the health cluster October 2021 update, availability of emergency medicines has reached 43% and the Pentavalent vaccine drop out this year is 8%. The available health work force is enough to cover only 17% of Sudan population and the price of medicine has increased by 1000%. .Although the signature of a Peace Agreement by some armed groups on October 3rd 2020 is encouraging, the situation remains fragile and volatile.
Specifically, South Kordofan is highly vulnerable and weakened by several years of war that have aggravated structural development problems that were previously present. The crisis has led to a breakdown in the administrative system and the deterioration or collapse of public services in the region. Many health facilities were destroyed and are still not functioning.
The presence of PUI through medico-nutritional assistance interventions makes it possible to follow the evolution of the needs of the most vulnerable populations. Through the monitoring of activities by its teams at the field and coordination level, the work in collaboration with the health and administrative authorities, and the different NGOs , PUI reaffirms the fact that it is essential to continue supporting the most vulnerable populations through the improvement of access to free and quality medico-nutritional care in the South Kordofan, in particular children and women by extending its coverage through the reinforcement of the mobile strategy.
Taking in to account the humanitarian need in the region, PUI will expand its geographical coverage and intensify its integrated response to the humanitarian needs of the conflict affected population in South Kordofan in 2022. Nutrition is among the most needed interventions and the organization will provide supports for more OTP/TSFP centers in Elabassia, Rashad and El Tadamon localities of the state targeting up to 10 centers. Additionally, awareness raising on nutrition topics will be intensified by use of community volunteers in targeted villages and by IYCF approach. The community volunteers will ensure proper screening of the under five children and PLW for malnutrition. Referral support will be given to those children who have SAM with medical complications to go to stabilization centers for treatment.
By doing this, the organization will contribute to reducing the morbidity and mortality in the affected population in the region.

1 - Needs

South Kordofan is the southernmost state in Sudan, bordering South Sudan. The state has a total population of 1,959,187 of which 585,029 (29% of the whole population) is in need of humanitarian assistance for health according to the health sector forecast for 2022. The state has been reported to have 217,683 IDPs and 38,904 South Sudanese refugees according to HNO 2021. In 2011-2015, thousands of families were displaced and still are, as of today: the population of Abassiya town, for instance, is expected to be composed at 30% of IDPs according to local authorities and sources. In South Kordofan, due to the conflict and the destruction of infrastructures especially in the 2011 to 2015 period, only 167 facilities over 302 are operational (according to the health cluster). The region is also one of the four states that face the most chronic shortage of medicines and medical supplies with only 31% availability, compared with the already low 43% availability in the national medical supply fund across the country (HNO 2020). Sexual and Reproductive Health indicators in South Kordofan are among the worst of the country. According to the hospital staff, Al Abassiya Hospital alone records 2 to 3 maternal deaths on average (over 100 to 200 live births a month) and 6 to 7 infant deaths. The hospital itself has only one old delivery bed which is broken. The delivery room has no running water and has no any infection prevention system in place although it is the referral facility for all health facilities of the district.According to the 2021 Sudan Nutrition cluster HRP, South Kordofan State has a GAM rate of 7.7% for under-five children, with the highest being Talodi (12%). Rashad District has GAM rate of 9.8 % according to HNO 2022. SAM rate for South Kordofan is 2.18% while among which some of the localities have up to 5.5% (Talodi) and 4.1% (Reif Ashargi). The pregnant and lactating women GAM rate for South Kordofan according to HNO 2022 is 8%, while some localities have up to 15% (Habila). Rashad and El Abassiaya also have more than 8% according to the HNO 2022 estimates.Although there are some OTP centers responding to the nutrition needs, those which are not supported by NGOs are not performing well due to lack of resources. Both the health facilities and population have huge gap in terms of access to clean water and sanitation services. In most of the health facilities, there is no functional latrine and hand washing facilities and community relies on water from unclean sources or has to travel for hours to get clean water. Rashad, Abujebiha and Tadamon are in similar situation and some of them are even worse. There is very limited presence of humanitarian organizations in Tadamon and most of the hard to reach areas in Rashad locality have no access to any basic services. Based on recent assessment of PUI, tens of thousands of returnees are settling in their old villages after decade of displacement and there is no any health, nutrition, education, WASH or protection services. Of the assessed villages, Tagelbo, 34 km on vey difficult road from Rashad has its health facility totally destroyed and only one of the 5 bore holes is functional. The population is badly in need of WASH and hygiene kits.Lack of protection services has also been underlined due to the situation of security in the area. In the areas assessed by PUI team, thousands of people are returning to very isolated areas abandoned many years ago where there is no social services such as administrative body. Women and girls perform gold mining and travel a long distance looking for water which puts them at risk of some forms of violence. The decade old conflict in the area has aggravated the vulnerability of the entire population to unsafe environments including land mines and frequent inter-clan clashes which resulted in loss of many lives among the community.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. HealthActivity 1: Strengthening the technical platform of health facilities Improving access to basic health and nutrition services. - Curative health care (management of cases of disease) - Preventive care (vaccination, prenatal consultations, post-natal consultations, family planning) - Reproductive health PUI will strengthen the technical platform of the health structures supported notably through the provision of medicines to health centers according to the country's import rules for medical consumables and small equipment. This strengthening is intended to ensure that beneficiaries have access to the treatments prescribed by health workers and to prevent breakdowns in essential molecules. Within this framework, PUI will also train health workers on supply chain management, compliance with drug ordering procedures and also on rational prescription of these drugs. Donations of medicines will be supplemented by a supply of small medical equipment and equipment (consultation table, delivery table, small medical materials such as stethoscopes, blood pressure monitors, scales, measuring rod, etc.) in order to guarantee the minimum equipment for the realization of preventive consultations, curative consultations and deliveries. This element is essential in the context of reducing the risks of maternal-infant mortality. Finally, in order to improve retention and reduce absenteeism of qualified staff in health centers, induced or aggravated by the crisis, PUI will provide financial support for the payment of staff salaries according to the national grid in force in the country and harmonized with other partners to avoid competition, with a view to ensuring the attractiveness of the positions and also to enable the recruitment of missing staff where necessary. This financial support will consist of a monthly incentive bonus that will be added to the salary that health personnel should receive. The absence and/or lack of qualified personnel (especially female personnel) impacts the quality of primary health care, particularly reproductive health care activities and the use of traditional birth attendants in precarious health conditions, due to the lack of access to functional structures for pregnant women. In connection with the current Covid-19 pandemic and the recurrent epidemics that the country may face (cholera), the health personnel and centers will be equipped and the staff trained (see Activity 2) in ICP equipment enabling them during consultations and waste sorting, to be effectively protected (masks, gloves, gowns, alcoholic solutions...) and to have at their disposal the necessary equipment to carry out sorting, collection and adequate management of waste in order to prevent and fight against infections. Medical waste will be incinerated according to the standard procedure at the health center or hospital level (at the district level). 5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

PUI is strongly committed to monitor the potential environmental impact of their interventions. The organization will implement a waste management plan that aims at mitigating the risks associated with hazardous waste. These risks could be significantly reduced through simple measures (reduction and sorting of waste, reduction of storage and transportation, training and sensitization). The reduction of waste is also encouraged by reducing the amount at source (choosing products that generate less waste, equipment that can be reused).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI strive to implement the principles of cross-cutting protection in the implementation of their activities, namely : - The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries - Ensure effective, ethical and meaningful access to the assistance delivered - Accountability, participation and empowerment of people During the construction and rehabilitation of the various targeted services (health centers, latrines, schools, etc.), special attention will be paid to the accessibility of these sites to people with reduced mobility. Specific adjustments will be made to the facilities in order to guarantee identical access to all persons from the community. When delivering information messages (selection of beneficiaries, presentation of intervention modalities, complaint mechanism, etc.), organizations will ensure that the information is heard and understood by all, without retention or bias. Their representativeness and consideration will therefore be ensured at all stages of the project cycle. Prevention of negative effects of assistance provided PUI will take particular care to avoid any negative effects or tensions that could result from the process of selecting beneficiaries and/or localities to be targeted ("do no harm" principle). The inclusion of communities in the decisions helps prevent unintended negative impacts while guaranteeing the dignity and supporting the legitimacy of local leaders. Access to assistance and services is tailored to all individuals and groups in order to prevent potential restrictions in accessibility caused by physical or social vulnerabilities that beneficiaries may suffer. PUI will ensure that the most vulnerable people are not left out of the selections and have equal access to the services targeted by this project by, for example, conducting individual household visits and collecting potential complaints and claims regarding vulnerability criteria and the selection process.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The complaint mechanism is an essential component of the accountability of programs at PUI. The mechanism covers a wide range of stakeholders, starting with affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors. Complaints are a formal expression of dissatisfaction or dissatisfaction with something and / or resentment of someone who calls for an answer. They may to carry on: i) The quality of the work carried out on the program. This may include: timeliness, safety and relevance activities. This includes complaints about targeting, the quality of items or services, and the failure to keep promises. (ii) The behaviour of staff or volunteers. This may include: a lack of respect for the members of the community; allegations of corruption, nepotism, favouritism or discrimination; or allegations of allegations of sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation. The process is carried out in real time to allow external stakeholders to express their concerns, complain or provide feedback on PUI activities in a given field at any time. Three different "entry points" are used for this purpose: the establishment of a telephone number, a "complaints box" in health centers, and a dedicated e-mail address for collecting comments. The PUI complaint mechanism is characterized by four criteria, it is: 1) Clearly defined: since the term "comments" is a broad concept, the complaints mechanism is framed in such a way that stakeholders are fully aware of "what they can comment on". 2) Inclusive: the mechanism allows all key program stakeholders to access and use it. Special attention is given to " give voice" to the most vulnerable people in the communities and those who may have difficulty for some reason in expressing their reactions/views. 3) Safe and confidential: Individuals, groups or entities must feel safe in their interactions with the mechanism set up by PUI. The potential fear of retaliation is clearly addressed and mitigated. 4) Responsive: PUI has a responsibility to provide stakeholders who provide feedback with an effective response to the comment. Received. Trainings related to the MEAL Pack are regularly carried out by the HQ or the field. In fact, these tools are already understood and appropriated by the teams. However, discussions are regularly organized to discuss ways to improve the mechanisms in place and to adapt them to the context and needs.
