Project Summary

Tdh began implementing health programming in South Sudan in 2017, with support to the health system through an intervention in nutrition, mother and child health, supported to a community based health system and the provision of drugs and equipment to health facilities. The program supported the South Sudan Health Association (SSUHA) to deliver the basic package of health and nutrition services (BPHNS) in 4 primary health care centers (Logwili, Loka Round, St Luke and Lainya PHCC) and 6 mobile clinic sites (Wonduruba, Nyapuro, Barakole, Bereka, Kenyi and Limbe), in Lainya County of Central Equatoria (Yei River State).
Health stands as one of the main priority interventions for Tdh, along with child protection. In 2016, Tdh’s MCH projects benefited 2,000,000 children and their relatives in 17 countries, both in emergency and development contexts. Health projects in emergencies address the following priorities:
-Dedicated human resources and capacity building. Deployment of health experts to the affected-areas along with capacity building for health personnel. By strengthening the capacity of local staff, Tdh allows the provision of services, especially in areas where security constraints do not allow direct intervention of international humanitarian actors.
-Material support to local health structures with needed medical equipment, supplies and medication. In some cases, Tdh sets up mobile clinics to reach isolated populations.
In South Sudan, Tdh will ensure emergency and essential health services are available in Wonduruba County to address immediate health needs of the population currently deprived from health care system due to the conflict. Through this project, Tdh will contribute to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by the lack of access to primary health care and reproductive care, with a focus on pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years old. Tdh’s objective is to address the most urgent primary health care gaps, by supporting existing health structures, by developing the capacities of a local NGO partner and coordinating with the MoH. Tdh is reinforcing:
• the PHCC capacity through technical support and direct supervision, the provision of drugs, nutrition supplies and equipment;
• the referral system from the outreach locations to the primary health care centers and to County hospital, for emergency and SGBV cases.
Tdh, as a Child Protection agency, has embraced a holistic approach to programming whereby integrated, multi-sectoral programs aim to improve outcomes form children and contribute to improving the broader child protection indicators.

Needs

South Sudan has one of the weakest health care systems in the world and it largely relies upon international funding to function. NGOs are responsible for close to 80% of health service delivery across the country. In Lainya county the on-going context of the conflict has internally displaced or severely affected more than 120,000 people. The insecurity, displacements of populations and limited commercial access have critical stressors on health systems in Lainya. Tdh conducted a health and nutrition assessment in 2017, which highlighted a rise in acute malnutrition cases, the absence of a malnutrition management system and the disrupted conditions of the primary health care services. Lainya was considered a nutrition emergency area, in phase 3 of IPC. The availability of medical services were reduced greatly from July 2016. Many health facilities are non-functional, having been looted and vandalized as a result of the conflict, especially Lainya County Hospital.In April 2017, Tdh supported delivery of health services, with SSUHA, in 11 locations.• 2 accessible PHCC: Lainya PHCC and Logwili PHCC.• 5 mobile clinics in locations that had previously had a functioning PHCC/U which were damaged or evacuated (or occupied by the army as in Limbe) during the crisis. Outreach was done on a weekly basis to reach people from outlying rural areas. Mobilization of the population was done in coordination with the local chiefs. With regard to sexual and reproductive health, pregnant women do not usually go to the ante natal care and tend to deliver at home with the help of a traditional birth attendant. This is due both to cultural norms and lack of quality services. The health facilities are reached only in case of medical complications. The utilization of post-natal care is still low and the rate of delivery by skilled birth attendant is low due to the lack of qualified staff. The management of SGBV - a vital service in this context - is lacking. The level of reporting of cases is low, due in large part to social norms and actual or perceived stigma, but also a lack of community awareness and referral procedures. For instance, the three victims were referred to Yei hospital once they were already pregnant - highlighting the lack of awareness and late detection around the SGBV problem.Compounding this, community health activities are poorly developed, and surveillance and sensitization initiatives are not able to disseminate information to rural communities. In parallel, Wonduruba town has been heavily militarized by the SSPDF, causing the displacement of 2,397 households for a total of 14,861 individuals. Prior to 2014 there was not a humanitarian presence in the area. The reduced capacity of the County Health Department (CHD), the multiple displacements, the destruction and looting of health facilities, has hindered the Wonduruba health system. Tdh was alerted to the prevailing situation in health in Wonduruba by the County Commissioner who facilitated two field visits by Tdh in November 2017 and February 2018. Tdh identified 35 community health workers but only 12 currently active in the 2 operating facilities (5 in Wonduruba and 7 in Katigiri). Moreover, the 2 health facilities are not equipped with the basic essential drugs and medical equipment. There is no routine expanded program of immunization (EPI), no basic reproductive health services such as antenatal, postnatal care and family planning services, and no skilled staff (including birth attendants). Reactivation of the health system and improving access to care are essential.

Activities

To reach the program goal, Tdh will focus its intervention on 3 main aspects - Health systems and clinical support; Reproductive health; Community Health Education/Behavior Change. The intervention will include rehabilitation and refurbishing of identified health facilities, restoring health care services through provision of medical kits and supervision to Community Health Workers, training and supervision of the CHWs, awareness raising and education to communities on healthier behaviors. Tdh will ensure basic rehabilitation works in facilities, and in addition supply needed missing medical and hygiene material, along with training and supportive supervision of staff, to ensure essential services meet the minimum standards. Tdh will ensure that in each of the facilities, basic WASH infrastructures are available such as hand washing points, access to safe water, latrines, and proper waste management. Tdh medical team supporting the restoration of essential health services in Wonduruba County will work in close collaboration with the County health department, will supervise all activities, provide on-job training to County medical staff, train and support them and encourage the resuming of health care services in locations where they have been interrupted due to the crisis. Tdh will focus on improving or making operational health facilities that have suffered looting, destruction and/or health professionals fleeing due to the conflict. On the other hand, for more remote locations where there used to be PHCU’s but that are not operational anymore, Tdh will ensure essential health services are restored through the CHWs. At the same time, Tdh will ensure that access to primary health care system for people living in remote areas is restored. In remote areas, Tdh will implement mobile health services, working with existing structures of CHW. In all the locations where the mobile clinics and PHCs will be established, community health workers will be selected and trained on hygiene, health and nutrition key messages (e.g. vaccinations, health behavior, post natal care, family planning, hand-washing with soap/ash, safe excreta disposal, safe water and Infant and Young Child Feeding practices) with the purpose of disseminating information and encouraging healthier behaviors among the population, with a special focus on pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years old. Overall, the community health workers will ensure the efficiency of the link between the health facilities and the communities. Under the supervision of the Tdh team, the community health workers will be responsible for: active case finding of malnourished children, performing MUAC screening and referring beneficiaries in needs to the mobile and/or static clinics, identifying pregnant women at high risk of complications and encouraging their attendance at the health facilities for ante natal care and post natal care. Protection considerations will be integrated to project in accordance with the Minimum Standard of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action and Tdh staff will be trained on child protection mainstreaming. Tdh will improve the capacity of Community Health Workers to monitor, report, and offer referrals on protection issues.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The intervention will consult the population in order to direct possible changes in the health services, through customer satisfaction surveys and interviews with relevant informants. The surveys and other interactions will specially focus in the interests of mothers and children. One of the main objectives of the intervention is to change social and health seeking behaviors of communities through a community-based surveillance system. Through a network of community health workers, the beneficiaries will receive promotion messages on hygiene, health and nutrition. This activity aims at changing the current practices which endanger people’s lives and will be the propeller to create awareness among the communities. These activities will implicate beneficiaries directly. The community health workers will be selected among the communities, so that they can better link between the latter and the project staff and medical staff of the health facilities. They will be the eyes and the voice of their own communities.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The programme will target both male and female (children, adolescents and adults) and will focus on ensuring that gender and equity are promoted throughout its implementation. Girls’ engagement in the activities, especially for adolescent girls will be prioritised. The focus on gender equity approach will be taking into consideration in order to avoid harmful interventions. As the project implementation planning will be participatory with the community, Tdh will prioritize with community input both how to best reach specific target groups and what messages are most adapted to men, women, girls and boys

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The locations proposed by Tdh were previously assessed and a multi sectorial analysis was conducted. Tdh is deeply engaged to ensure comprehensive analysis of protection and child protection into all its programs. To make sure all groups were consulted and that the risks they faced were taken into consideration assessments and beneficiaries selection process were done through focus groups discussion with IDPs, host communities, women and children. Tdh recognizes CBI can increase protection risks and will mainstream protection elements throughout the program that mitigate these risks and account for the local contextual challenges. Additionally, taking into account the protection considerations, including risk analysis related to child labor (road construction or increased farming capacity requiring children to work.) and opportunities mapping, including early childhood development/CFS training and paid jobs for mothers In Yei through its Child Protection activities and involvement in Protection coordination Tdh is also well informed about the protection risks faced by the distinctive groups.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Tdh context and security analysis provides a broad picture of the access barriers faced by populations to access humanitarian services and by humanitarian actors to access populations in need. The prevailing context, coupled with the scale and duration of the conflict have severely impacted the livelihood and overstretched resilience of affected communities. Further, the conflict has exacerbated tension along ethnic lines, vulnerability of women and children through sexual assault or kidnapping and discrimination and deprivation of basic rights. Tdh regularly updates a risk analysis of its area of operations looking at context and conflict evolution, involved actors’ strategy and perception of our interventions, impact of our activities on the communities we serve or do not serve, and expectation of the same communities and actors. For the proposed program design, implementation and monitoring, including complaints and feedback mechanisms, will ensure stakeholder input into the access analysis. Tdh will also aim to provide humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations in areas that have received limited humanitarian response and hence prioritizing people having limited/no access to assistance.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Through its M&E and data collection team the proposed intervention will be designed to make sure targeted vulnerable populations are integrated in the response. To do so a close collaboration and communication with all stakeholders will be enforced.Project monitoring tools will be adapted to collect data on the safety and security of beneficiary populations when they access services, including identifying any potential harm that may come from benefitting from the project. Additionally, a complaints and feedback mechanism (which will include comment boxes, help desks, focus group discussions, etc) will be put in place to capture input from beneficiaries and stakeholder. Tdh will use the information collected through these sources to adapt the program approach where needed if barriers to access are identified.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Through FGDs, feedback and complaints mechanism, and surveys Tdh will ensure beneficiaries input to design safer and “Do no harm” programming methodologies. In order to mitigate any identified risk, place of distribution will be chosen in coordination with heads of communities and in compliance with Tdh security policy. Additionally, safety considerations for the most vulnerable people (elderly, children head of households and disabled people) will be tailored according to their needs. During Tdh intervention protection mainstreaming sensitization will be undertaken in order to mitigate the risks that children and women face on a regular basis (worst form of child labor, GBV).

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

To ensure dignity of the beneficiary the project will adopt a community based protection analysis by using participatory methodology in the beneficiary selection and will engage with the community in the design and implementation of the project and will strengthen the community prevention and response mechanisms at the local level through its child protection project and protection mainstreaming measures. Moreover by including local partners in its intervention Tdh will ensure the project is designed and implemented accordingly to the local needs. The different stakeholders (community members, local actors, state representatives and local partners) will have access to feedback mechanism that will be signed to assess whether dignity of beneficiary have been respected, Moreover survey and final evaluation will also assess this component.
