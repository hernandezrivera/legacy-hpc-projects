Project Summary

Through this project, AFAQ is going to ensure continuity of care by providing integrated primary health care, nutrition, WASH, and child protection services and increase the accessibility to essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services for all age and sex groups; boys, girls, pregnant and lactating women, and other caregivers (other women, and men) with attention to people with special needs without any sexual discrimination in the areas of high IDP flow in north west Syria reaching to 108267 children and caregivers including PLWs, through the following modalities:
1.⁠ ⁠Deploy community outreach team /6 RRTs/ 20 CHWs to cover 8 communities and 57 camps in Salqin , Harim, Dana, Atareb subdistricts in both Idlib and Aleppo governorates, reaching a 17,275 boys and 13,032 girls under 5 years of age, and 8,954 pregnant and lactating women and adolescent mothers.
2.⁠ ⁠Integrating nutrition services in 4 PHCs and one mobile clinic in northwest Syria through activating OTP/TSFP services within the PHCs reaching to 36000 children U5 and 14000 PLWs.
•⁠ ⁠AFAQ Dier Samaan PHC (B0200221) in Aleppo/Jebel Saman/Daret Azza/Dier Samaan
•⁠ ⁠AFAQ Eastern Marj Akhdar PHC (G0704030) in Idleb/Jisr-Ash-Shugur/JisrAshShugur/Eastern Marj Akhdar
•⁠ ⁠AFAQ Jdidet Eljisr PHC (G0704044) in Idleb/Jisr-Ash-Shugur/Janudiyeh/Jdidet Eljisr
•⁠ ⁠AFAQ Mobile Clinic (G0700153) in Idleb/Harim/Dana/Dana
•⁠ ⁠Alsahhara PHC (B0200052) in Aleppo/Jebel Saman/Atareb/Alsahhara
3.⁠ ⁠Expand and enhance nutrition inpatient services for children with acute malnutrition with medical complications through the continued operation of a stabilization center at Save the Soul Hospital in Salqin subdistrict servicng about 100 children under 5 with capacity of 5 beds.
4.⁠ ⁠Provision of nutrition Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) for PLWs and children 6- 23 months using all 4 approaches of nutrition cluster guidance for about 500 families with PLWs / children 6-23 months. This will be applied at fixed PHC centers/ OTP and SC.
5.⁠ ⁠Establish two mother-baby areas (MBA), one in the Dana community linked to HA AlIman Hospital and one in Kafar Takharim community linked with WSA Women and Girls Safe Space center (WGSS) serving 5280 PLWs, and 7920 children.
The main activities of nutrition program
•⁠ ⁠Timely Screening, referrals and follow up of children 6 – 59 months and pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition.
•⁠ ⁠Treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition for children 6 – 59 months with and without medical complications.
•⁠ ⁠Treatment of moderate Acute Malnutrition for children 6 – 59 months and for PLWs.
•⁠ ⁠Provision of IYCF messaging and counselling PLWs.
•⁠ ⁠Provision of WASH key messages.
•⁠ ⁠Deworming for children 12 – 59 months.
•⁠ ⁠Distributing micronutrient and iron Supplementation for PLWs and Micronutrient for children 6 – 59 months.
•⁠ ⁠Iron folate supplementation for pregnant and lactating women.
•⁠ ⁠Referral of PLWs for ANC and PNC (Health) services.
•⁠ ⁠Screening for visible signs of GBV, PSEA and child neglect and referral to appropriate service delivery sites.
•⁠ ⁠Screening and linkage to services for immunization (zero dose, defaulters)
•⁠ ⁠Provision of CVA services for PLWs and children 6-23 months
•⁠ ⁠Provision of blanket supplementary feeding program BSFP for children 6-23 months.
AFAQ will coordinate with local stakeholders, including health directorates, local councils, and NGOs, to ensure smooth operations and prevent duplication of efforts. Additionally, AFAQ will collaborate with international organizations such as WHO, WFP , UNICEF and UNFPA to procure necessary medical kits and nutrition supplies. These partnerships aim to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of our humanitarian health interventions in northern Syria. By achieving the above components, AFAQ will ensure the continuation of essential and life-saving nutrition interventions in NWS.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In line with AFAQ's strategy, our responsibilities include direct implementation of this project in target locations, coordinating closely with health authorities in Northwest Syria. We will leverage their extensive network to facilitate access. Local communities will actively participate in our capacity development efforts through appropriate training initiatives.AFAQ will ensure adherence to quality and accountability standards, providing necessary training to staff and fulfilling contractual obligations. Additionally, we will manage all program and financial reporting to the donor. Our coordination efforts will extend to relevant clusters and coordination bodies in Turkey.This program will progress through the following phases:• Securing essential materials, devices, consumables, and nutrition supplies from UNICEF and WFP and conducting the required training.• Implement specific nutrition activities for children under 5 and PLW through operating the community mobile team (6 RRTs and 20 CHWs), 4 OTPs integrated in 4 PHCs, and one Stabilization Center (SC), in addition to establishing two mother-baby areas (MBAs). • Regular reporting, data analysis, monitoring, and evaluation. This will involve the development of the Indicators Performance Tracking Table (IPTT), Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) work plan, and a disaggregated database. The reporting will be according to guidelines agreed by the technical group of the nutrition cluster (4Ws). Needs, PRA, Risk, Assumption, and Risk Mitigation, and PSEA:According to SMART Survey conducted in NWS; Non-pregnant women Anaemia 24.8% and 47.04% of children (6-59 months), 22.5% of children (6-59 months) are stunting, the overall GAM rate 4.4 % with average (SAM) rate of 0.5%, average (MAM) rate of 3.9% and a MAM rate of as high as 9.4% among the PLWs, and the IYCF practices showed, Minimum Acceptable Dietary (MAD) rate was only 5.5%, Minimum dietary diversity (MDD) rate was 10.2%, Minimum meal frequency (MMF) rate was 42.6%, The low indicators of complementary feeding practices (MDD, MMF, MAD) show a problem in the availability and the diversity of the food.Project will ensure rights and respect with protection principles integrated. Tailored activities prioritize safety, dignity, accessibility, and inclusion. Staff trained for PFA, CP, PSEA, and GBV. Data disaggregated. Mitigation measures implemented. Empowerment and accountability are emphasized.All staff members sign the AFAQ PSEA code of conduct upon recruitment.Regarding risk assumption and mitigation: Access to services for all; Security situation remains stable; Timely nutrition supplies. Risks: Women's limited mobility; Security threats; Supply shortages. Mitigation: Mobile outreach, security protocols, contingency stock, community engagement, staff training.
