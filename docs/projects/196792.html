Project Summary

IOM will continue to provide and expand its provision of integrated protection services to conflict-affected populations across NW Syria.
Activities include:
• Child protection prevention and response services, including case management,
• Gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response services, including case management/cash assistance;
• Structured psycho-social support (PSS)
• Mental-Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS) services,
• Individual protection assistance (IPA);
• Protection monitoring (on-site and mobile teams);
• Legal advisory services (civil status documentation and HLP) and HLP Due Diligence;
• Community mobilization and outreach;
• Protection capacity building for humanitarian actors;
• Unexploded ordinances (UXO) risk education;
• Referrals to specialized and non-specialized services
Overall, 2023 IOM will directly support 84,400 beneficiaries (23,750 men, 30,250 women, 14,600 boys and 15,800 girls) across NW Syria through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions, targeting the most vulnerable including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with disabilities and the elderly. The overall objective of IOM’s intervention is to mitigate and reduce protection risks and address rights violations. Specifically, activities will comprise of the full range of integrated protection services, including specialized GBV and CP prevention and response services, PSS, MHPSS services, UXO risk education and legal advisory services. These will be provided through static facilities as well as mobile outreach teams in order to ensure coverage of underserved, hard to reach areas across NWS (e.g. close to frontlines).
Protection service will also be provided in IOM’s Planned Camps, including GBV and Child Protection (CP) prevention and response services, safety audits, legal support, IPA, risk education and structured PSS through on-site as well as mobile teams.
Legal clinics/mobile outreach teams will provide legal awareness and individual legal counselling on civil status documentation (CSD), as well as HLP to conflict-affected populations in Idlib and Aleppo Governorate and HLP and CSD trainings for humanitarian workers in NWS and Turkey.
In addition, HLP Due Diligence support across XB Clusters will be continued throughout 2023, including through the formulation of sector-specific guidance on how to minimize the risks of HLP grievances, and legal protection trainings of humanitarian workers. This work will be closely aligned with the Gaziantep HLP Technical Working Group.
IOM will provide capacity support and daily follow-up for its IPs in all aspects of programming and implementation, including technical supervision support for GBV case management. Teams will be equally comprised of male and female staff. IOM and its partners prioritize community participation and outreach throughout the project cycle, including through regular consultations and support to community structures. All staff are trained in WGQs for identifying, involving and assisting persons with disabilities (PWDs) which constitute 15 % of all project beneficiaries. All static service points will be equipped with ramps/handles to reduce access barriers for PwDs.
Third-party monitoring will be utilized to monitor and evaluate all project activities, including outcome monitoring for GBV/CP case management services in line with GBV SC M&E toolkit (e.g., through satisfaction surveys). Disaggregated beneficiary numbers will be verified through 4W reporting to the Cluster.

3 - Needs

The scale, severity and complexity of protection needs of displaced Syrians and host communities in NWS continue to increase, with 42% of assessed communities reporting the presence of at least one protection issue. 78% of key informants (KIs) reported that child marriage occurs within the community (MSNA 2022). 57 per cent of IDP sites in north-west Syria do not have access to primary schools and 80% to secondary schools, which severely hinders their access to education (HNO 2023). Overcrowded, unsafe and undignified living conditions in camps affects women and children disproportionally and heighten GBV risks, particularly, in NWS where 79% of camps are critically overcrowded (HNO 2023). According to HNO 2023, 70% of child Protection grave violations are in northwest Syria. Child labor is a growing concern with 92% of KIs reporting that child labor prevents school attendance (MSNA 2022). 77% of HHs surveyed across NWS report at least one HH member showing signs of psychological distress, in particular children, while PSS/MHPSS services are widely lacking. HLP and CSD issues remain key challenges, with 89% of KIs across NWS reporting lack of civil documentation and 78% reporting HLP concerns (MSNA 2022). Widespread UXO contamination affects all of NWS; yet 71% of KIs reported that communities had not received any risk education (MSNA 2022).

Teams will also provide HLP Due Diligence support and conduct legal research on HLP and CSD concerns in NWS. 1.4 HLP support IOM will continue its HLP Due Diligence Support across Clusters, including analysis and development of operational guidance.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1. Creating expectation of response; risk of serving specific individuals at the exclusion of others.2. Physical security risk due to conflict; accessibility barriers for PWDs.3. Limited judicial mechanisms for redress; risk that by providing information about documentation is perceived as endorsement of said documents/legal system.4. Lack of quality services and referral expose GBV survivors to harm 5. Resistance/backlash from sections of community/family on GBV messaging. 6. Risk that existing community structures conflict with CP principles.7. Familiarization causing beneficiaries to become overconfident to the threat of explosive hazards. 8. Exploitation/abuse by staff when accessing assistance. Mitigation measures: 1. Manage expectations about available services; receive feedback on the quality of the information provided.2. Collaboratively identify access barriers and engage in accessibility mainstreaming of centres for PWDs; train staff on WGQs, PFA and first aid.3. Regularly map existing forms of violations redress available4. Train relevant staff on GBV core concepts and GBV SC ethical closure tool; service mapping including only survivor centred services.5. Culturally sensitive parenting awareness sessions/community outreach activities.6. Ensure proper environment to preserve comfort/confidentiality.7. Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education.8. Reinforce that humanitarian services are free, train all staff on CoC


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In order to ensure that sexual exploitation and abuse risks are properly mitigated, the organization maintains minimum PSEA standards ensuring it’s a) code of conduct includes PSEA language, b) all staff are trained on PSEA, c) internal SEA reporting mechanism and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization and d) PSEA awareness raising activities with beneficiaries and local community are conducted during the activities. To reduce the risk of SEA by service providers when accessing assistance and services, IOM will provide regular staff training for code of conduct and PSEA through high quality trainings based on the IOM and Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) training packages. IOM and partners are also taking the necessary measures to mitigate SEA risks, including the broadcasting of PSEA video and messages on posters, and the interagency SEA hotline on display. All of IOM’s IPs are members of the WoS PSEA Network which provides in-depth training on PSEA and the existing prevention and mitigation tools. IOM and its partners will also conduct regular community consultations and events that enhance awareness on PSEA inside Syria as well as the SEA hotline. To address the risk of SEA posed by non-traditional humanitarian actors, IOM will specifically consult, sensitize and train local authorities, camp management and community leaders on PSEA principles whose exposure to PSEA programming has been limited so far.
