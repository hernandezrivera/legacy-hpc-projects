Project Summary

South Sudan continues to grapple with devastating conflict that has led to an unprecedented humanitarian crisis. The main drivers of the protracted humanitarian crisis include political and communal violence, climate-induced shocks including floods, disease outbreak and economic hardships. The ongoing flooding remains the worst recorded in the history of the country . Climatic shocks (flooding, dry spells, and droughts), insecurity (sub-national and localized violence), population displacements, and high burden of diseases and disease outbreaks as well as the economic crisis are the drivers of the food insecurity. As a result of the high food insecurity, high disease prevalence, poor quality and diversity of food, poor access to health and nutrition services and covid-19 related disruptions, 2, 175,546 are at risk of acute malnutrition in 2023.
Unity State was disproportionately affected by flooding in 2022, with 80% of its surface area (7 out of the 9 Counties) flooded and 240,000 people displaced by floods and 120,000 displaced by conflict. The most affected counties of Rubkona, Mayom, Pariang, Abiemnhom and Koch, where CARE runs its largest Emergency Nutrition Programme, have the majority of those displaced. There are also 124,004 Refugees are in Pariang, in addition to Returnees and vulnerable host communities who are particularly hit hard by the crises in the state, given their vulnerability and lack of coping mechanisms. Particularly concerning is that floodwaters have not showed any sign of receding 10 months later and the stagnant flood water is now responsible for an upsurge in communicable diseases (Cholera, Acute Watery Diarrhoea and Malaria) in the Counties of Rubkona, Koch, Pariang and Mayom. 10 Nutrition Facilities have been destroyed, leaving 1,650 children at high risk in Rubkona . In addition, WFP has in the recent has a funding cut that resulted in the reduction in the food basket provided to the population.
CARE international is well known for its high-quality Nutrition Programmes in South Sudan. In 2022, CARE reached over 262,461 children under the age of 5 and Pregnant and lactating women/Girls with Nutrition Services, as well as 330,000 with health services across the 5 counties within the state. CARE is also a member of the Health and Nutrition Clusters, as well as the Strategic Advisory Groups (SAG) for both Clusters. This project will provide Integrated, lifesaving, emergency nutrition interventions for children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), elderly men and women. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and Returnees will be of special interest to this project because of their high levels of vulnerability. To do this, CARE will implement all the components of CMAM as follows:
1. Community mobilisation
2. Outpatient Therapeutic Programme for management of SAM without medical complications (OTP)
3. Outpatient Therapeutic Programme for management of MAM (SFP)
4. In-patient care for management SAM with medical complications (SC/ITP)

1 - Needs

Using the FSNMS round 28, and the SMART Survey results, it is estimated that a total of 2,175,546 people (1,469,919 in the host community, 315,503 returnees and 390,125 IDPs) will be suffer from of acute malnutrition in 2023 in South Sudan. These include a total of 1,437,735 children under the age of 5 years (733,245 girls and 704,490 boys) of which 353, 606 will have SAM (180,339 girls and 173,267 boys) 1,084, 129 (552,906 girls and 531,223 boys) will have MAM, and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will suffer from acute malnutrition. Unfortunately, more children and pregnant and lactating women will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2023 compared to 2022 (an increase of 158,201). in the counties where CARE works, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates have been above the WHO threshold of 15% with Abiemnhom at 18.2%, Pariang at 22.3 % and Rubkona at 23.6%. The remaining two are Mayom at 22.7% and Koch at 24.1%). The key drivers for the high malnutrition levels include Flooding, Conflict, Supplies stock outs, Food insecurity and Morbidity. The 6 counties of Rubkona, Mayom, Abiemnhom, Pariang, Koch and Twic East with a combined population of 911,098 (Nutrition Cluster PIN targets for 2023), have one of the highest malnutrition burdens in the Country. The 6 counties have a SAM burden of 35,472, MAM Burden of 121,441 and PLW burden of 56,323. The burden for Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition within the 6 counties is 109,332 and for Vitamin A Supplementation is 154,886 and for Deworming is 127,554. For children between 6-23 months and PLWs, the BSFP burden is 54,666. The SAM, MAM and PLW burden among the Internally displaced persons in the 6 Counties is as follows: Rubkona (SAM 8,834; MAM 27,170 and PLW 13,269), Pariang (SAM 818; MAM 2,584 and PLW 1,051), Mayom (SAM 609; MAM 2005 and PLW 968), Koch ( SAM 1,447; MAM 4,565 and PLW 2,693); Abiemnhom (SAM 421; MAM 2,512 AND PLW 1,256) and Twic East ( SAM 129; MAM 703 and PLW 211). The ongoing flooding that has affected much of the country and the 6 counties where CARE implements its nutrition programmes has had a devastating impact on the Nutrition Programming. It has destroyed crops, cut off communities from accessing Health and Nutrition facilities, markets and other vital commodities and led to deaths of livestock which are the lifeline of the population in the affected counties. This is expected to lead to severe food insecurity and the attendant malnutrition among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women in 2023. In addition and more worryingly, flooding continues to impair access to health and nutrition services in Rubkona, Twic East, Koch and parts of Pariang. Roads have been flooded or destroyed and most of the affected facilities can only be accessed by either speed boats or canoes, therefore making transportation difficult and expensive. The flooding and the associated poor access to Health and Nutrition services will further impact negatively on the Status of malnutrition across the 6 Counties. The Economic crisis, currency devaluation and reduced market access and functionality are also expected to play a role in the deterioration of the food security situation in 2022. Incidents of insecurity related to inter-communal violence and cattle raiding within the 6 counties are likely to be experienced during this period, further contributing to the projected severe acute food insecurity and the associated malnutrition.

2 - Project activities

CARE will employ the mobile strategy whenever needed to ensure access to populations displaced by either floods or conflict, so the number of mobile Nutrition facilities may increase based on the needs and malnutrition trends through the year. 3 - Project indicators

A participatory gender sensitive monitoring and reporting plan will be developed and established for this project. The M&E Coordinator in collaboration with the CARE Nutrition Team will develop the M&E plan. Routine monitoring will be undertaken by CARE M&E and Nutrition teams and will involve relevant stakeholders including the State Ministries of Health and the County Health Departments, as well as other partners working in the 6 counties. Monitoring results will be shared monthly and quarterly and will inform the extent to which inputs were appropriately used, the extent to which planned activities were carried out as per the detailed 2023 HRP implementation plan, and the quantity and quality of outputs resulting from the activities. During project implementation, all approved South Sudan Ministry of Health and the Nutrition Cluster tools for data collection will be used supplemented by internal project monitoring framework ensuring weekly analysis and sharing of data. Quarterly data quality audits will be undertaken by a joint team consisting of; the SMOH, CHD, community representative and CARE technical staff to ensure integrity and credibility of information reported. The analysis of the data on a monthly basis will inform program decision making as it pertains to progress made in the implementation process. Analysis on a quarterly basis will inform decision making on program strategies in terms of best practice and review of lessons learned in the course of implementation. Data generated from GBV cases treated will be handled with confidentiality adhering to privacy standards required. The window of opportunity during the dry spell towards end of year will be fully utilized to provide 3 monthly technical monitoring visits done jointly by the program staff, community representatives and the CHD to supported health facilities ensuring that services are in line with positive local norms, national treatment protocols, quality standards are upheld and a proper application of the skills and concepts covered during in service trainings conducted. CARE will use its supervision checklist in addition to the QSC (Quantified Supervision Checklist) for the joint supervision visits as a tool to monitor progress. CARE will also put in place suggestion boxes at places of service delivery in order to get community views on services provided and provide feedback to promote accountability to the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARE will use their community engagement strategies to ensure that the vulnerable members of the community (people with disability, women and girls) and ensure that their voices are heard. This will be done by taking advantage of the existing feedback and complaints mechanisms structures that are in existent at the community level (complaint boxes, M&E staff, focal persons, community leaders etc). The above groups will be informed of these options and will be encouraged to provide feedback through any of them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

to provide CARE with their feedback and/or complaints, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from CARE, is an important part of being accountable. To this end, CARE will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the Accountability to Affected Populations Mechanism

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARE has ensured that the PSEA Focal Person is known to all staff and beneficiaries and that contacts are made widely shared. Additionally, CARE provides awareness-raising sessions on sexual exploitation and abuse for all staff and beneficiaries and other personnel in CARE on a regular basis. The Human Resource department makes appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing PSEA prevention efforts.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During the life of the project, CARE will put in place a robust system of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints from the community. In addition, CARE will aim to provide a safe, non-threatening and easily accessible mechanism that enables even the most powerless to make a suggestion or complaint. To avoid marginalizing the vulnerable community members, active channels such as community meetings, focused group discussions and suggestion boxes will be made available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Analysis and results of the feedback and complaints provided will be shared monthly and this will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence based decision making in order to improve programme quality and performance. To this end, CARE will not only strive to address and respond to all complaints, we will remain timely and transparent in our decisions and actions. The opportunity for communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to provide CARE with their feedback and complaints, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from CARE, is an important part of being accountable. To this end, CARE will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the Accountability to Affected Populations Mechanism. CARE will work with the affected communities to select the nutrition sites where the mobile teams will be working from. CARE will also discuss with affected communities the target criteria and mechanisms for referral from the mobile clinics to the static health facilities or stabilization centers. Regular project reviews will be conducted together with the relevant stakeholders and affected population to ensure that the project is on track.
