Project Summary

The armed conflict in Syria has crippled all the sectors, and education is no exception. The lack of a reliable local authority, the constant displacement of people from their homes, and the physical destruction caused by the bombings has made the schools, and education as a whole, very paralyzed. For a school to teach its students, it needs supplies, a suitable teaching environment (venue), and financial support to keep the school going. But schools in Northeast Syria lack all of these.
As the de facto local authority cannot provide the support schools need, schools are left without supplies, and the old supplies have depleted. There is barely any furniture for students to sit on, blackboards have become a luxury, and textbooks and notebooks are fantasy to some of the students. This is not to mention the damages in the infrastructure which has made many schools inoperable. Furthermore, schools being in such a poor condition as they are, the latest displacement waves have put even more strain on the schools as they have to enroll students from displaced families who their older schools are either completely destroyed or turned into shelters. The recent assessments show that most people have access to education, but the main needs identified in all of them were lack of school supplies, damaged infrastructure, WASH facilities, unsuitable school environment, and lack of proper furniture.
SWEDO intends to identify and support 60 most-vulnerable schools (around 48,000 beneficiaries) in Raqqah and Hassakeh governorates. The project beneficiaries will consist of around %30 IDPs and %70 Host Community. This project will rehabilitate the damaged school buildings to make them a suitable education environment, and supply them with the necessary supplies needed for teaching. The impact of this project would be that schools can serve their purposes, and education is delivered as it is supposed to. At the same time, families who refrained from sending their children to schools because of the unsuitable education conditions will likely be willing to send their children back to school after the successful intervention of this project.

4 - Needs

According to NES Forum’s “NES Emergency Update” reports, there are 6,490 HHs (32,357 ind.) living in 152 collective centers/sites in the governorates of Al Hasakeh, Ar Raqqa and Ayn Al Arab city (Kobane) in Aleppo Governorate. Most of the collective centers are school buildings that have been turned into shelters to accommodate the IDPs. As this makes these schools unusable, their students are forced to go to other schools which put immense pressure on those schools. The schools in the mentioned areas need repairs, new furniture, rehabilitation of WASH facilities, fuel, and other basic needs. According to HNO 2020, around 1.3 million children are at risk of dropping out of school, and an estimated one in three schools are either damaged or destroyed Moreover, according to the Ministry of Education in the mentioned areas, the situation of the schools is as below: Ar Raqqa:111 Schools are fully destroyed.122 Schools are Partially destroyed and need rehabilitation.150 Schools need repairs (replace windows and doors, WASH facilities, ceiling, and floor repairs). Ayn Al Arab (Kobane):There are about 400 Schools that need minor repairs, fuel, and related equipment. Al Hasakeh Governorate:More than 500 schools need minor repairs, fuel and related equipment, replace windows and doors, WASH facilities, ceiling and floor repairs. Deir Ez Zor:40 Schools are fully destroyed.200 Schools are Partially destroyed and need rehabilitation.

5 - Implementation of activities

SELECTION CRITERIA FOR SCHOOLS:The criteria for the most vulnerable schools are those having the largest number of students and the lowest amount of supplies, as well as the schools that are either fully or partially inoperable due to infrastructure damages. All 60 schools which the project intends to target will be supplied with stationery kits and blackboards as necessary. Additionally, depending on the condition of each school, the building and WASH facilities will be rehabilitated. PROJECT ACTIVITIES:- Coordination: coordination will be made with the Education Cluster, HAO, Education Committee and partners in the region to make sure that no other entity is planning to address the same gaps we are intending to address. - Verification assessments: SWEDO will arrange field assessment visits to potential schools in the target locations to make sure the gaps we intend to address still exist and afterward identify 60 most vulnerable schools for the project. - Assessment of exact needs: SWEDO engineers and field monitors will assess the rehabilitations needed for each identified school, as well as the supplies that the schools lack. - Coordination of the procurement of supplies: market research will be done and it will be decided whether to purchase the supplies locally or from the Kurditan Region of Iraq and transport them through Peshkhabour/semalka border. - Procurement and delivery of supplies: tender announcements, evaluation of offers and suppliers’ capacity, and preparation of a contingency plan to ensure the timely delivery of supplies and adequate cash flow to suppliers. - Tendering of Rehabilitation Works: tenders will be announced for the rehabilitation of schools. The tenders will include the BoQs which were prepared using the assessments. Following the tender announcements, contractors will be selected and rehabilitation works will begin.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project directly addresses Do-No-Harm principles by ensuring extensive communication with the community (including leaders, women, youth, and children) to determine the needs that can best be supported by SWEDO in the implementation period. Additionally, issues such as the hiring of staff, the procurement of materials, and the selection of vendors will be looked at in a manner that directly addresses the context and maintains the Do No Harm principles by not discriminating against any specific populations (both host community and IDP populations are very intentionally targeted) and by being aware of the dynamics present. GBV Training will be provided for SWEDO staff to prevent improper act (Zero tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse). RISK: “Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving.”MITIGATION: All SWEDO staff sign the organization’s code of conduct and receive training on sexual and child abuse. At the same time, SWEDO has a zero-tolerance policy for such acts. Additionally, the phone number of SWEDO’s MEAL officer will be circulated in the schools in case of any improper acts, beneficiaries can contact the phone number and report the misconduct. RISK: “Humanitarian staff, volunteers or associated personnel are not able to make informed decisions.”MITIGATION: SWEDO has clear Duty of Care policies that clarify the organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff, volunteers and associated personnel such as facilitators, teachers and other education personnel. The Duty of Care policy is clear to the organization’s staff, volunteers and other personnel such as facilitators, teachers and other education personnel and they understand what the organization will, and will not do, for their safety, security and wellbeing.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Further deterioration of the security situation might delay project implementation.MITIGATION: Monitor the security situation closely and plan carefully for project implementation. Also, keep the donor updated with any changes in the work plan. RISK: Further increase in the unit cost school rehabilitation from the current estimate of market price might reduce the number of schools from the target. MITIGATION: The average cost will keep within the budget, but school costs will be varied between different schools based on the scope of works. RISK: Exchange rate of Syria Pound worsens even further:MITIGATION: This will not affect the project as SWEDO will use USD in all of its procurements and tenders. RISK: Access roads may be in bad condition.MITIGATION: SWEDO shall plan for the activities to take place on days having good weather. RISK: Lack of experienced staff members in the NGO field.MITIGATION: Capacity building training will be given to the staff. RISK: There may be a lack of supplies locally or a lack of contractors.MITIGATION: SWEDO allows individuals and small businesses to participate in the tendering. RISK: Duplication of services may occur in the intended schoolsMITIGATION: SWEDO will ensure proper coordination with the Education Cluster, HAO and the Ministry of Education.
