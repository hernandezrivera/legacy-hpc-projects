Project Summary

The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. Report from the Food security and livelihood sector for the month of January, 2022 shows that Dikwa Nganzai and Magumeri local governments are currently in a state of Acute Humanitarian Need. Food security situations worsen as affected population with very limited to safe access to efficient stoves etc. Access to energy is vital to ensure food security and is often highly constrained in conflict-affected areas. When energy resources are not readily available, this can have a considerable impact on the nutrition status of vulnerable populations.
The return strategy being implemented by the Borno state government in an attempt to decongest the IDP camps. However, the returnees have to virtually restart all over making their conditions not much better than the IDPs situation. With virtually nothing to start with and the prevalence of climatic extremes, crop and livestock pests and diseases, sustaining the hitherto agriculture-based livelihoods in the return environments is apparently elusive. This is further exacerbated by the impact of COVID-19 pandemic, making the crises many folds and Despite the ongoing efforts, without sustained and expanded food security and livelihood support 3.48 million people in the Nigerian states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe will experience acute food insecurity by onset of the 2022 lean season (June – August 2022). Moreover, the prevalence of acute malnutrition is expected to remain proportionately high. The problems of the households are poverty and livelihood insecurity. Targeted poor households face lack of capital and productive assets; do not have required skills and capacity to involve with farming and non-farming activities. They do not have access on cultivable land. High levels of inequity and social marginalization exacerbate overall poverty and livelihood insecurity, denying access to natural resources and services. Patriarchal system and gender inequity results in further marginalization of women. Lack of demand for agricultural labor in the raining season and drought season, combined with extremely limited access to land and to markets severely limits employment. Male members engaged in labor temporary work, which makes the life and livelihood of the poor women in the areas dependent and vulnerable. Women have limited mobility and traditionally not been engaged in commercial or trading activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Involving communities and beneficiaries in humanitarian responses is essential to “providing assistance in ways that best meet their needs”. Participation and accountability were mainstreamed in all project phases from planning and design to monitoring and evaluation to enhance programme relevance, ownership, accountability, as well as effectiveness by providing channels for complaint and on-going feedback from beneficiaries. The complaint and feedback mechanism (CRM) that GREENCODE/FAO devised during the implementation of the raining season project in Nganzai LGA to provide beneficiaries with three channels through which they can raise complaints and feedback. This was design for participation and accountability to conflict affected beneficiaries’. The three channels are as follows: 1. Participation and Accountability to beneficiaries who could read and could afford the cost of a phone call: Banner in English, Hausa and Kanuri were pasted in strategic locations in the communities, vendors shops. The banner has the CRM line of GREENCODE for complaints 2. Participation and Accountability to beneficiaries who could write: Complaint boxes were placed at strategic locations within the project communities as well as at vendor shops across the project locations. 3. Participation and Accountability to beneficiaries who could not read, write, or afford the cost of a phone call as well as poor network in Gajiram: A complaint/help desk manned by GREENCODE (CRM) staff was placed at strategic location. This channel also served to address complaints from community leaders and beneficiaries. Furthermore, follow up face to face visits to beneficiaries, FGD, monthly beneficiaries sensitization are carried out during the implementation of the project.
