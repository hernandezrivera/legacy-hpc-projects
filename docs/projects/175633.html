Project Summary

Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) operates with vulnerable communities to alleviate the suffering caused by both man-made and natural disasters such as displacement by war or flood, destruction of shelter and property, damaging of previous season crops, disease, and hunger, among others. HACO plans Livelihood activities 2021 in Unity State in the Food Security and Livelihood Sector. The response plan proposes to provide direct distribution of emergency livelihood kits (vegetable, crop seed, fishing kits, support to 20,000 households in greater Unity State (Mayom and Panyijar Counties), whereas each county would provide services to 10,000 HHs including the returnees, IDPs and host communities in vulnerable situations. Inclusive pointer to IPC 4 & 5 communities affected by various factors such as flooding, displacement, and lack of livelihood chances which have been reported in Unity State (Mayom & Panyijar/Nyal Counties).
Humane-Aid for Community Organization (HACO) has been implementing the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the United Nations Emergency Livelihood Response Project (ELRP) in Panyijiar County/Unity State in 2020. The project focused on the distribution of emergency livelihood kits and training to support 7,000 households in the Main Season Response (MSR), and (DSR). The project objectives were to distribute crop seeds, vegetable seeds, fishing kits, training on good agriculture practices and fishing practices as well as the distribution of agricultural hand tools.
HACO has previous experience in Peacebuilding, GBV (Support to Women and Girl friendly space in Ganyliel through Livelihood activities (pastry making, weaving, Knitting, and Basket making), and WASH activities in Unity State since 2015 in Panyijar County. HACO plans more livelihood surpport activities, given the fact that the organization has proven its expertise background in agriculture sciences (Agronomy, fishing practices, and capacity building) albeit the former intervention having encountered flood catastrophe which had resulted in crop-damage, loss of crop yield and livestock, and forceful displacement of local communities.
HACO is Accountable to Affected Populations (AAP), both for achieving results in addressing hunger and implementing donors’ projects as a service provider throughout the project cycle. This requires thoroughly and eloquently engaging people, including the most marginalized, women, youth, and children in all stages of the project cycle, and ensuring that they have a voice in the decisions that affect their lives. The beneficiaries are the rightful owners of the project as all the available resources are to be spent on them. HACO is committed to AAP based on the key two principles: First, affected people have a right to be actively involved in the decisions that affect their lives. Ensuring that programs are accountable to affected people is therefore a key feature of rights-based programming. Second, engaging affected people in program decisions to make livelihood surpport to be more effective. Basing programs on the views of affected people helps ensure that needs are correctly identified and understood and that programs are designed in a manner appropriate to the context. Engagement with affected people can also help flag problems in program implementation allowing amendments to take place. HACO functionalizes its commitments to AAP by focusing on three key components: information provision, consultations, and complaints and feedback mechanisms-through setting up AAP complaints and feedback Desks.

HACO HRP aims to save lives, protect livelihoods, enhance food production and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities by building their coping capacity, thus setting the foundation for enhancing the long-term adaptive capacity of communities and strengthening synergies among the activities.

1 - Needs

Panyijiar County has a population of 117,105 individuals it has been facing challenges, namely; insecurity due to cattle raids, revenge Killing, chronic food insecurity, high rates of malnutrition, water-borne diseases, and gender-based violence among many households. The flooding which began in Mid-June 2020 affected women, men, girls, and boys. The affected populations were displaced from lower to higher grounds from Tiap, Jiech Kanhial, Chuk, and Malouk to higher grounds in Paliay village and West of Kanhial where they got exposed to cattle raiders from other States leading to loss of lives and property. The affected population are also having meager food stocks and is predisposed to GBV cases since women and girls are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse by going out in search of food while children are sent out on fishing errands. Overall the flood-displaced 5,791 HH; Greater Nyal 3,577 and Greater Ganyliel 2,214 HH in Tiap Payam, Jiech, Chuk, Malouk, Kanhial, Pachar, Pachienjok, Maluak, and Pachar (IRNA 2020). The multiple shocks that affected Panyijiar County; floods, crop failure, and loss of Livestock have exacerbated the food security situation in the County. About 93,684 (80% of the population in Panyijiar County is in IPC 3+) and having GAM rates of more than 30% (IPC 2020).HACO, therefore, plans to distribute emergency livelihood kits (crop, vegetable seed, fishing kits, agricultural tools and support training to 20,000 most food insecure HHs in Mayom and Panyijiar counties. The training will be mainly on agriculture practices (crop and vegetable), good fishing practices, Integrated Pest Management, Fall Army Worm, climate change adaptation. Training of Service Providers (SP) field FSL Assistants and field monitors. training on fish production, postharvest fish handling, preservation, utilization, and nutrition post-planting and post-harvest monitoring. Training on vegetable demo setup, production, and gap/IPPM. Training on vegetable post-harvest handling, utilization, and nutrition.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HACO shall take all appropriate measures to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of any beneficiary while implementing FSL activities, or to any persons related to such beneficiaries, by its employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by HACO to perform any services under its Livelihood surpport activities. For these purposes, sexual activity with any person less than eighteen years of age shall constitute sexual exploitation and abuse of such person. In addition, HACO shall refrain from and shall take all reasonable and appropriate measures. The project management team will make sure that gender equality is observed, curb PSEA, and a safety audit has to be conducted before any activity to address any sexual malfeasance that could be encountered, i.e. during the distribution of the livelihood inputs and/or registration process, training of the beneficiaries. The trained group members of AAPS are direct monitors to all steps. Furthermore, the involvement of local authorities, and line stakeholders working on the ground will be an additional surpport to forestall SEA by Project staff.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HACO will make sure that all Households having persons living with disabilities are taken care of by being targeted for registration, recruit volunteers to assist as translators and whenever possible use local languages during FSL Project implementation activities. Prioritization of women in all Livelihood surpport activities, especially pregnant and lactating mothers and girls. HACO shall form AAP committees, to address issues affecting the targeted population, gender disparities, resolving issues related to the provision of the services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HACO FSL Team will set up AAP committees and establish complaints and feedback desks. The desks will give beneficiaries information about the purpose, goal and duration of the project as well as beneficiary targeting criteria. The purpose of the information desks created will be for giving out information about the project and also responding to questions from project beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary Feedback Mechanisms (BFMs) will provide a method for strengthening HACO'S accountability to the affected communities where HACO livelihood surpport activities will be taking place. BFMs provide a channel for community members to easily raise questions, suggestions, and concerns and response mechanisms about HACO FSL activities. In this way, affected community members will hold HACO to account for her actions and ensure their answerability for how resources are used in their community. Transparency will be the foundation of accountability; for communities to provide relevant feedback, they first need to know what the main services or activities are that they are being asked to give feedback about, who is being targeted, and what commitments or standards HACO can be held to account for. Community members also need to know that it is their right to provide feedback, how they can provide feedback, and what will happen to their feedback. In this way HACO will be able to adapt feedback to project assistance.
