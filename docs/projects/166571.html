Project Summary

The submitted project is aiming to reduce food insecurity by leveraging the organization’s extensive background in climate smart agricultural practices, emergency response and food security. The overall approach of the project is hinged on improving the capacity of vulnerable households to recover from shocks without resorting to negative coping mechanisms. ADRA is using the promotion of drought tolerant early maturing crop varieties, conservation agriculture and capacity building of lead farmers – which have all proven successful for achieving food security and recovering farmer capacity in ADRA’s Drought Mitigation and Food for Assets Projects. Adaptive capacities to counter weather extremes are being built through the mainstreaming of climate smart agriculture techniques. ADRA is strengthening and expanding current livestock support programs that prevent further deterioration of livestock value and livestock deaths through the promotion of fodder production and preservation, which comes highly recommended by ZIMVAC. Building on experience & capacity, ADRA was promoting seed fairs through vouchers to manage the distribution of drought tolerant and early maturing seed inputs, and building the capacity of farmers to diversify food sources and develop sustainable agricultural practices.
The advent of the COVID has negatively affected mobility, further undermined the purchasing power and access to input and output markets. Income has decreased by 40-65% because lockdown restrictions on movement are curbing poultry and horticulture farmers’ access to markets and input suppliers. Limited post-harvest handling, storage and cold chain transportation facilities have resulted in produce spoilage, 30-80% losses of highly perishable produce and increased mortality of chickens (Dead On Arrival) at the markets. Retailers, processors, and integrators have been unable to take the produce as scheduled due to reduced business performance as a result of decline in demand; staff shortages; and produce transportation delays. Closure of borders and companies has affected input suppliers’ ability to supply seeds, fertilisers, chemicals, day old chicks, and stock feed in a timely manner, leading to disruptions in business continuity at farm level. This is raising the risk to produce crop and livestock below capacity and further exacerbating food insecurity triggered by prevailing drought and economic turmoil. Limited social distancing and lack of PPEs at farm level and exposes farm workers to contracting COVID-19.
Targeting: ADRA to further support 8,000 households (40,000 individuals) in IPC 3 and above targeted in the previous submission from 4 districts namely Bulilima, Tsholotsho, Gokwe North and Mbire. The main objective of the project is to prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population in the 4 districts, by providing COVID response prevention and control activities, stimulating production and facilitating access to markets.
The project aims to strengthen the poultry and horticulture value chains in the Midlands Province to mitigate impacts of COVID-19 and build resilience for future crises.
Specific Objective:
SO1: The project aims to strengthen the crop and livestock farmers to mitigate impacts of COVID-19 and build resilience for future crises.
This project proposes to reduce vulnerability of farmers to challenges faced by farmers because of COVID due to own safety issues arising, decreases in demand and logistical challenges in accessing formal and green informal markets (musika) in a timely manner.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

a) Provide PPE/guidelines for COVID-19 prevention at farms and collection points or during market visits. This includes sensitisation of farmers on the prevention and control methods being used. Continuous promotion of social distancing and use of protective wear, promote hand washing.b) Provide guidelines for COVID-19 prevention on biosecurity/safety of market produce. It is anticipated that the market packaging requirements will evolve given the safety and ease of disinfecting of produce. Farmers’ capacity will need to be built in response to the trending market packaging requirements of respective products. d) Build farmers’ capacity in adjusting to the “new normal” way of trading which requires minimal human contact. This requires use of digital platforms for transacting or accessing information/ services. Farmers need to be sensitised in this regard and encouraged to invest in ensuring they can transact.
