Project Summary

The project intends to restore access to safe, clean and sufficient water and improve hygiene practices in 3 districts of Manicaland. The project will raise awareness on health and hygiene among the communities including religious groups in the target districts.There is need to stop the advance of water borne diseases in the province to save the lives of children, women and other vulnerable groups of the selected communities.
The project will be implemented in collaboration with the government technical ministries, community based organisations and the private sector. The district and provincial rapid response teams will be strengthened through capacity building interventions to ensure effective response in case of disease outbreaks.

Needs

Zimbabwe is at risk of multiple hydro-meteorological hazards namely drought, flooding and diarrhoeal diseases epidemics especially cholera and typhoid. The projection of the El Niño phenomenon will negatively affect the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities of Manicaland Province. Recently, the country was experiencing a cholera outbreak in Harare and the 3 target districts of Manicaland were equally affected, but with Buhera being the hardest hit by the outbreak. All these outbreaks have been linked to poor water and sanitation service delivery in the areas. There is need to restore and improve the availability of water in areas that have been affected by cholera and likely to be adversely affected by the pending El-Nino induced drought predicted. Apart from supporting various livelihoods activities, the water should also be safe for consumption in order to prevent the spread of diarrhoeal diseases. The programme will ensure that water is accessible to all vulnerable groups with the community that is children, women, elderly and the disabled in the target districts. Perennial water sources will be strengthened and protected to ensure that the number of beneficiary community remains high enough to keep it sustainable. The project will also seek to distribute NFI hygiene kits to affected communities in an effort to support safe collection, storage and utilization of collected water through promoting point of use water treatment. PHHE will be adopted by the project to support behaviour change and adoption of safe practices at household level, and this awareness will promote latrine construction among beneficiary communities and create defecation free environments.

Activities

Rehabilitation, improving and upgrading of existing water sources and drilling of new water sources. (Drilling and equipping of 8 boreholes, Rehabilitation of 60 Boreholes, water quality testing 75 boreholes, Rehabilitation of 3 piped water schemes and equip with solar energy, one per district). Hygiene Promotion with a focus on safe hygiene and sanitation practices, participatory health and hygiene education and water conservation will be adopted as the major strategies for the project. (Training of EHTs/Extension workers/school health coordinators and VHWs on PHHE lifesaving messages. Dissemination of critical hygiene messages, IEC material development & distribution, Dissemination of messages focused on sanitation improvement, household water treatment and water conservation, Monitoring diarrhoeal diseases, PHHE sessions in institutions (schools and clinics) Distribution of NFIs to 7,000Households, including logistics and monitoring.
