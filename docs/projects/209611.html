Project Summary

The latest WASH reports indicate 46% of Syrians rely on unsafe water alternatives, and a 4% rise in water-borne diseases occurred in 2022 compared to 2021 illustrating the continued gap in access to and quality of water. These crises, compounded with epidemic outbreaks, as seen with 96% of confirmed cholera cases in NES in October 2022 continue to highlight the fragility and weakness of WASH services. Additionally to the conflict, North East Syria is currently facing a major water crisis, known as the triple water crisis. Water resources are limited in North East Syria, and the region faces the challenge of over-extraction, pollution, and competing demands for water from various sectors, including agriculture and industry.
To alleviate these issues the program will provide water trucking services, rehabilitation of water systems, and conduct water quality testing. The program will rehabilitate water supply systems including repair and rehabilitation of water stations, storage tanks, and chlorination systems in some of the hardest to reach areas. Also, the program will provide water treatment materials: mainly chlorine, aluminum sulphates, and active carbon powder. Besides that, the program will support local authorities to implement strong operation and maintenance systems for those rehabilitated water stations, which includes provision of Operation & Maintenance (O&M) tool kits, day to day materials and trainings. The program will also provide capacity building tools and materials to Water Departments at the governorate level to ensure quality water monitoring and water safety plans development. With the continued need for water trucking, after 10 years of conflict, is driven by Housing Land and Property (HLP) issues which restrict the opportunity for sustainable water supply solutions to the informal settlements and other camp like situations, the program will support those communities with water trucking.
Sanitation activities will include installation of latrines and handwashing stations, cleaning and disinfection of WASH facilities, and solid waste management campaigns, especially in camps where the program is the main WASH partner. Furthermore, given the cholera outbreak, the program will ensure adequate sanitation standards in Health clinics, by providing light rehabilitation, to improve access for patients and staff to safe sanitation facilities, and limit the risk of infection. The rehabilitation will include, but not be limited to, the connection to local water and sanitation networks, and repair/ rehabilitation/ construction of latrines and handwashing facilities.
Because of recent Cholera outbreak in September 2022, the program will distribute hygiene and cholera kits as a prevention, preparedness and response intervention to limit the spread of infectious diseases in prone areas. Distribution of emergency hygiene kits will be triggered in response to sudden emergency onsets for example in the event of population displacement, combined with other emergency food assistance responses under this project (RTERs) and emergency kits. In terms of cholera kits, the program will pre-position the kits at the program warehouses, distribution will be based on the NES WWG Cholera SOP, triggered based on the monitoring of data from the WWG. Furthermore, the distribution of cholera kits will be combined with hygiene promotion sessions at the household level.
Lastly, the program will disseminate Hygiene Promotion key messages to improve knowledge on health risks related to WASH and prevention methods, the program will integrate hygiene sessions – as well as cholera risk awareness - in all WASH activities to achieve behavioral change and disseminate good hygiene practices.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The proposed WASH interventions responding to access and quality is based on the severity of need for the WASH WG within NES. For example, prioritizing areas where there continues to be no other NGOs and urgent WASH needs continue to go unmet such as the informal settlements and Dier ez Zour.The WASH activities are but one of the INGO programs, designed to work together to increase the synergies of the actions; the goal of the activity is to promote integrated resilience building and positive coping mechanisms amongst the most in-need and underserved communities in Northeast Syria through an improved coordinated response.Regarding the needs, In 2023, 13.5M people are in need of WASH assistance, causing 52% of the population to rely on unsafe alternative water supply (HNO, 2023). Challenges in accessing drinking water included unreliable quality (44.3%), cost (39.3%) and unreliable supply (36.1%) in Ar-Raqqa, Aleppo, and Deir-ez-Zor governorates (the INGO, 2022). This is taken as the justification for the water supply activities the project will do.Regarding the justification for the sanitation activities, the needs are also evident. Not only the cholera outbreak, but also there has been a 4% rise in water-borne diseases in 2022 compared to 2021 (OCHA, Response to water crisis, August 2022). As per the WASH and Health Working Group database, between Sep.-Nov. 2022 approx. 10,094 cases of acute diarrhoea were identified in Ar-Raqqa, Aleppo and Deir-ez-Zor governorates; >60% of these cases were as a result of the quality of water linked to the low level of the Euphrates River (the INGO, 2022). NES suffered 96% of all suspected Syrian Cholera cases by October 2022 (HNO, 2023).For vector control activities, specifically leishmaniosis, a 59, 6% of the households in DEZ report having at least one case during 2023 (HSOS Trends Dashboard), almost two thirds of the population. During past year the INGO's vector control campaign in villages in Susat and Hajin found 2,171 cases of leishmaniosis.
