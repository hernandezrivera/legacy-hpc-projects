Project Summary

The WoS Food Security Cluster/Sector of Syria links the hub-level clusters/working groups (NES, NWS and Damascus) offering technical and operation support and provides analysis of needs, response and gaps at a WoS level. Mercy Corps currently hosts the FSS NGO co-Coordinator.
In 2021, the sector will build on its work of previous years and will focus on reviewing and updating needs periodically, response and gap analysis to operational planning and
developing contingency/preparedness plans. The sector will continue to harmonize all aspects of the response such as assessments, analysis, geographical targeting, selection
criteria (including targeting youth for livelihoods) and monitoring. The sector will lead cross-learning initiatives on outcome indicators monitoring, response modalities and
cross cutting issues. The sector will also deliver on the key outputs for the Humanitarian Programme Cycle as well as feed into strategic discussions and decisions. Inter-sector
work will focus on a variety of areas including (1) linkages and collaboration with WASH, NFI, Nutrition, Early Recovery and Protection sectors (2) deeper focus on protection
analysis with the inclusion of the protection matrix at sector and projects level and ongoing collaboration with the Protection sector (3) an integration strategy aimed at
linking people on food assistance with livelihoods support from other sectors such as the Early Recovery and Livelihoods sector (4) joint needs analysis and advocacy, common
messaging to communities and assessing convergence at household level with nutrition and other closely linked sectors (5) improved linkages and coordination with ongoing
cash-based transfer activities and to help guide new activities as well as learn from ongoing and past activities.

4 - Needs

The Syrian economy has suffered significantly since the onset of conflict in 2011. Gross domestic product in 2020 sits at just one third of its pre-conflict level in 2010, while both internal and external economic shocks have further undermined domestic trade, financial markets and export markets. Internally, the country’s two main sectors, agriculture and oil, have been hugely affected by the protracted conflict and its impact on extraction, production and national and international trade. The agricultural sector is largely unproductive, with damaged irrigation systems, trade routes and a lack of access to primary production inputs, while Syria’s major oil producing regions in the northeast are now under the control of the Self-Administration. According to the HAT Qualitative Report from January 2021 the typical unskilled worker in Syria must work an additional two and a half weeks to purchase a basket of essential goods at the same relative cost as they did before the rapid depreciation period; however, affordability dynamics vary by zone of control. In response, many households have adopted food and income coping mechanisms to compensate for their diminished purchasing power. A lack of purchasing power caused by an enlarging gap between wage growth and inflation has led to many rely on negative coping strategies, and in areas outside of Syrian government control, completely adopt foreign currencies as a mode of exchange.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the MercyCorps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection riskanalysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a cornerstone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff are trained onkey principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. All coping mechanisms, with the possible exception of sending children to work or to beg in opposition-controlled and Self-Administration areas, increased at a steady rate since October 2019. In particular families subject their daughters to early or forcedmarriages in conflict situations as a means to relieve financial burden, and because a husband serves as additional protection from pervasive sexual violence. However, girls married atyounger ages are at high risk of complicated pregnancies and deliveries, the children have worse health outcomes, are at higher risk of intimate partner violence, and on average receiveless formal education than girls married as a consenting adult.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and clusteranalysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2021 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria.Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence has been reducing, creating potential destabilizing event in the delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions ofoperational viability across northern Syria in 2021. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enableand adaptable programming. In 2021 Covid-19 infection rates are rising across Syria, particularly high in Damascus, Rural Damascus and the coastal region. With hospitals quickly reaching capacity and the cancellation of non-emergency treatments to dedicate more resources to combat COVID-19, the GoS and other authorities’ mismanagement of the pandemic may lead to the situation further spiraling out of control in all areas.. If local authorities fail to enact policies that prioritize public health and enforces precautionary measures, then local healthcare systems in governorates, who notably have very limit capacity, will face a grim scenario.
