Project Summary

IAC has been active as a humanitarian NGO and continuously implementing in Idlib, Aleppo for the past 4 years.
IAC has access in northwest side of Syria and gained widespread acceptance where it operates and has established good relations with stakeholders such as local communities, council structures, and IDP sites during implementing several projects and responding to newly IDPs in different camps in targeted areas. This facilitates a proper access for all of our teams into all the location of implementing the project and monitoring them.
IAC will consult and coordinate with the local authorities and CCCM cluster to select targeted sites and make sure there is no duplication the in the selected sites.
Project will target 20 camps in several districts in Northwest Syria (A’zaz, Jebel Saman) in Aleppo governorate.
The project is considered as a planned and will target 20 camps in Aleppo governorate.
IAC will take into consideration the HLP issues if necessary by making both the camps’ managers and the landlords sign consent forms which expressly provide the permission to conduct the project activities without any legal consequences.
Activities will be implemented according to the agreed criteria in CCCM cluster.
At the onset of project, field teams will meet with camps representatives to explain project and its goals, take any suggestions which help in success of project and ensure their support.
The key administrative and critical humanitarian infrastructure will be developed including admin offices, distribution points, and warehouse, safe spaces for women and children, supplying camps with necessary safety equipment, firebreaks & fire points, in addition to support the camps' management by providing trainings and supplies, and supporting forming the committees inside camps, in addition to contingency planning.
- Reinforcing areas of the sites impacted by flooding or other incidents through small scale intervention such as, replacing damaged tents, cleaning of sanitation and communal facilities - Ensuring access to facilities (latrines,showers) for women and children, mitigating protection risks by improving and maintaining including repairing of lighting, fence, organize camp gate in sites, and other camp facilities, and providing fuel for running generators and other maintenance cost.
IAC will take over the camps management for the first 3 months, in parallel with training and preparing the camps management committees to take over the management for the rest of the project duration. IAC organization will train the camps management committees on the necessary skills that will facilitate the care and maintenance activities including reporting tools and respond during emergencies.
Camp management committees will be strengthened through capacity development, and trainings on aspects of camp governance, gender, inclusion, and protection mainstreaming on both management and field level.
Field officers will send daily reports to project officer at HQ on project activities, the M&E field officers also will send daily reports to M&E officer at HQ to produce monthly reports including implemented activities, challenges, learned lessons, and planned activities for following month. Monitoring tools include surveys, interviews, pictures, recordings, and direct observations. M&E officer is also responsible for managing complaints mechanism so users can submit complaints and suggestions anonymously through complaint boxes, phone number, an email, or by direct communication.
Project will target about 20,000 BNFs in 20 camps by developing and reinforcing the key administrative and critical humanitarian infrastructure.
In case of underfunding, the number of targeted sites and BNFs will be reduced, and thus the expected results of the project will be reduced as well

3 - Needs

According HNO 2022 2023 14.6 M people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 M from 2021.A decade of conflict in Syria has been characterised by protracted displacement. 4 out of 5 IDP households have now been displaced for at least five years.In 2021, the total number of IDPs has increased from 6.7 M to 6.9 M. However, the number of new displacements decreased significantly from 2020 with only 346,995 new displacements registered from January to August 2021, compared to 1.63M for the same period in 2020.According to CCCM report on September 2022, the number of IDPs reached 2,661,051 in north-west Syria region, 1.82 M live in sites registered in ISIMM.IDP sites are built on private lands, there is a constant risk for populations living there of eviction, which is a special risk for women and children. IDPs are increasingly trying to access formal camps, many of whom reside in self-settled IDP sites which often lack camp management systems. Population density in last resort sites is of great concern with a growing IDP population, as they are particularly vulnerable climactic and epidemiological shocks. IAC team have implemented rapid need assessment in target locations to assess main needs of IDPs. The main findings of this RNA indicates that most of IDP sites are self-settled, and lack proper site planning, infrastructure and camp management systems and providing only a minimum level of services.

4 - Implementation of activities

20 camps in A’zaz and Jebel Saman districts will be targeted through project's activities.Activities will be implemented according to CCCM cluster guidance.Activities aim to achieve 2 main objectives:1-Improving the management quality and accountability of camp management:-Establish CM committees.-Assisting in development of selection process for committee members.-Training CM members on camp governance and protection mainstreaming.2-Monitoring and coordination the provision of humanitarian assistance in IDP sites and strengthening basic infrastructure:-Establishment of contingency planning with firefighting system and evacuation plan.-Reinforcing areas of the sites impacted by flooding or other incidents through small scale intervention such as, replacing damaged tents, cleaning of sanitation and communal facilities.-Providing camp operational and regular maintenance activities for camps including repairing of lighting, fence, organize camp gate in sites, and other facilities, and providing fuel for running generators and other maintenance cost.-Establishment of feedback/complaint mechanism.-Reporting and monitoring the provided services in camps.-Conducting needs assessments in camps.-IAC will take over camps management for first 3 months, in parallel with training and preparing camps management committees to take over the management of camps.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1:Lack of fire prevention and response would be critical for the safety of IDPs,women and youth are often the first to identify and respond to an incident but they may not participate or have access to information/trainings on fire mitigation and response.Likelihood High Impact HighMitigation1:strengthening Fire Mitigation and Response Measures maintaining the CCCM and SNFI Cluster technical guidance,preparing contingency plans,conducting fire awareness campaigns and trainings with promoting participation of women and youth.Risk2:An increase in tensions within the camps,as space is occupied by these infrastructure and create differences between the camps in terms of services.Likelihood High Impact MediumMitigation2:residents should be consulted before the construction of any infrastructure and be made aware of the risks that the current project was attempting to mitigate,portion of unskilled construction workers are composed of residents,involving IDP Committees in engaging with service providers and also the residents to report to the feedback through complaint mechanisms.Risk3:The management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities.Women, diverse and vulnerable groups may not be included or able to access structures.Likelihood Medium Impact MediumMitigation3:management structure is representative of the entire community with strong selection criteria, including the most vulnerable groups, elderly&PWDs


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff are in direct contact with the beneficiaries. All field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: 1- IAC will make sure that All staff, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct.2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA Training.3- IAC will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms.4- Activating complaints mechanisms to protect PSEA in particular, placing them in easily accessible places, designing awareness-raising banners, placing boxes for complaints and suggestions, and activating the hotline number (WhatsApp).5 - provide awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA and methods for reporting them and gaining the trust of society and spreading humanitarian principles and stick to them.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

IAC conducts a security assessment for its projects by receiving information on security situations from its network of local councils, camps admins and civil society activists, as well as by remaining continuously apprised of security situations through bulletins released by the international community. For emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises that threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer location until the area is safe enough to conduct the project activates.Assumption1: All papers and permissions on matters related to the camp's land are available at the camp administration in the area.Risk1: lack of ownership papers of the camp land and no permission to carry out activities.Mitigation1: Obtaining land ownership papers and having camp managers and landlords sign approval forms that which expressly provide permission to conduct project activities without any legal consequences before starting project's activities.Assumption2: All needed materials are available and in sufficient quantity.Risk2: As most items and materials are imported from Turkey, any closure of the border crossing points with Turkey will result in a lack of such items and materials.Mitigation2: IAC will ensure to launch procurement directly after the signature of the award for this project. In addition,contractors’ in-house stock capacity will be considered as a key criterion for selection during the bidding process to ensure bulk deliveries.
