Project Summary

IOM aims to facilitate the immediate, adequate and equitable delivery of services to the displaced population in camp and camp-like settings through camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) services, information management mechanisms, and sector coordination of partners and stakeholders.

CCCM activities will continue to provide site facilitation support and site level coordination in formal and informal camps in the following Borno locations: MMC, Bama (including Banki), Dikwa, Gwoza (including Pulka,) Ngala, Monguno, Mafa, Jere, Kala Balge (Rann), and Damboa; and in the following Adamawa locations: Yola North, Yola South, Girie and Song.

The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) will continue to provide mobility tracking in the six conflict-affected northeastern Nigerian states, with the Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) deployed mainly in Adamawa, Borno, and when necessary, in Yobe due to fluidity of population movement. Biometric Registration will remain a priority in selected camps to assist partners for verification (WFP) and support other organizations and their distributions. Finally, detailed assessment surveys for return locations will continue in selected areas. DTM will strive to strengthen regional collaborations in place with emerging needs as well.
As Sector Co-Lead, IOM with UNHCR, is accountable for the overall CCCM, Shelter and non-food items (NFI) responses in North-East Nigeria. The sector will continue to ensure access to multi-sectoral assistance in camps and camp-like settings, advocating and identifying durable solutions for the displaced families and affected communities; and building national capacity through CCCM trainings for national authorities and local partners.

1 - Needs

IOM aims to facilitate the immediate, adequate and equitable delivery of services to displaced populations in camp and camp-like settings through camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) services, information management mechanisms, and sector coordination of partners and stakeholders. CCCM activities will continue to provide site facilitation support and site level coordination in formal and informal camps in the following locations in Bornos state: MMC, Bama (incl. Banki), Dikwa, Gwoza (incl. Pulka,) Ngala, Monguno, Mafa, Jere, Kala Balge (Rann), and Damboa; as well as in the following locations in Adamawa: Yola North, Yola South, Girie, and Song. The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) will continue to provide mobility tracking in six conflict-affected northeastern Nigerian states, with the Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) deployed mainly in Adamawa, Borno, and when necessary, in Yobe, due to fluidity of population movement. Biometric Registration will remain a priority in selected camps to assist partners for verification (in collaboration with WFP) and support other organizations and their distributions. Finally, detailed assessment surveys for return locations will be continued in selected areas. DTM will also strive to strengthen regional collaborations in place with emerging needs. As Sector Co-Lead, IOM, with UNHCR, is accountable for the overall CCCM, Shelter and non-food items (NFI) responses in north-east Nigeria. The Sector will continue to ensure access to multi-sectoral assistance in camps and camp-like settings, advocating and identifying durable solutions for the displaced families and affected communities, and building national capacity through CCCM trainings for national authorities and local partners.

2 - Activities

IOM will continue to provide and enhance its site facilitation capacities in formal and informal camp and camp-like settings to ensure that IDPs have access to basic services and have improved living conditions and that provision of basic services at camp level is better coordinated. This will be achieved through continuous deployment of mobile camp management teams. IOM CCCM operations will be guided by the CCCM SOPs and agreed humanitarian standards in North-East Nigeria and ensure community participation in all levels of camp life. The presence of camp management staff will continue the following activities in camps: Coordination of partners and stakeholders; Monitoring of service delivery based on standards; Sensitization and awareness of communities on life-saving interventions; Site improvement and care and maintenance of infrastructures; Drainage construction and improvement; Setting-up and maintenance of community groups, governance structures and site maintenance committees; Management of site-level multi-sector information to share to partners and stakeholders; Improved two-way communication with affected population (to ensure that IDPs have access to critical information regarding the displacement situation and available services and are able to provide complaints and feedback on needs and services); Coordination of protection services/ response to identified protection needs and gaps and referral of the same in collaboration with protection actors.IOM CCCM will continue to manage reception centres for new arrivals ensuring harmonized reception procedures and guidelines based on agreed standards. Capacity building will continue for CCCM practitioners in north-east Nigeria, IDP communities, national and local authorities, key stakeholders and partners. DTM will continue its comprehensive and periodic assessments in over 2,400 locations to enable a better understanding of movements caused by a range of factors, including communal clashes, escalating crime, and natural disasters. DTM will enable an improved understanding of the numbers and needs of the affected population as well as an analysis of sector-wide needs.DTM will also ensure improved information management mechanisms for displaced populations in north-east Nigeria to facilitate planning, gap analysis and rapid response:• Displacement Tracking Matrix: track and monitor displacement, including gender and age disaggregation, service delivery, and key needs and gaps across communities.• Biometric registration of IDPs and affected populations in the new humanitarian operational areas through registering of new caseloads as well as update of previously registered caseloads to include births and deaths.• Reinforcement of monitoring and first alert mechanisms for rapid referral in close coordination with sector partners and Government counterparts through rapid paper registrations in order share data within 24 hours of displacement (not including personal data).• Use IOM distribution application to track assistance provided to beneficiaries over time to prevent duplication of efforts by organizations and better enhance assistance provision.• Continued return intention surveys and case by case analysis to identify the return conditions in areas of origin and further advice government and developmental partners on areas of focus. IOM will continue the establishment and operationalization of a stability index that looks into stability of return in areas where large-scale returns are witnessed.• Provision of data and sharing with respective local authorities to ensure beneficiaries without them receive better documentation. IOM, through its role as Sector Coordinator, will improve support for field sector/operation coordination in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe through coordination of sector partners and continued support to coordination mechanisms set up for deep field locations and hard to reach areas.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons With Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the camp management committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. IOM has already established CRM in many sites across Borno which has been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors. IOM has already taken several steps to ensure accountability to affected population: findings from beneficiary surveys, some of which have already been incorporated into the design of this project. IOM also actively uses practical recommendations from beneficiary feedback surveys as advocacy tools at the sector level to influence change.
