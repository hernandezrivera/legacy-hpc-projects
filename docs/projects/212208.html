Project Summary

WASH services in Northwest Syria have been severely impacted by a series of challenges, including the Turkey- Syria earthquake in February 2023, ongoing conflict, economic downturn, and power shortages. These events have caused significant damage to WASH systems, greatly reducing access to water and sanitation for affected communities. The economic decline has further hampered service providers' ability to maintain these systems, resulting in limited access to essential WASH services for households. Drought conditions have exacerbated freshwater quality and availability issues, worsening sanitation problems, and increasing public health risks such as AWD/Cholera outbreaks, leishmaniosis, and malnutrition. In northern Syria, inadequate toilet facilities, especially in IDP camps, present significant challenges, with many lacking gender segregation, lighting, and security measures. This disproportionately affects women and girls and heightens the risk of GBV incidents. Recent conflicts and bombardments in northwest Syria have only compounded this already dire situation.
ATAA seeks to save lives, uphold rights, and promote access to key institutions and services by providing particular WASH services.Basic WASH assistance is delivered to the targeted community by taking into account the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls, in addition to taking into consideration the special needs of disabled people in all the project activities, especially during the infrastructure designs.
Therefore, ATAA aims to reach 332,850 beneficiaries, 66,570 households (HHs) living in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates in 4 districts and 70 camps with basic WASH services.
The proposed project is targetting communities and IDPs camps as follows:
- all communities are located in (Dana, Armanaz, Harim, Kafr Takharim, Salqin, Qourqeena, Azaz, Jarablus, and Idleb sub-districts) in the prioritized locations based on the 2024 HRP.
- 70 camps are located in four main districts (Jarablus, Azaz, Harim, and Idleb); ATAA has proposed Dana and Jarablus sub-districts target 12 camps in significant need of water and sanitation network, in addition to a renewable system to operate the water station inside the camp.
The project will comprise the following components:
a) Rehabilitation/upgrade of 10 water pumping stations, including equipping the stations with electrical and mechanical equipment and installing solarization systems. As well as train the local communities and apply water safety plans.
b) Provision of WASH emergency services for individuals living in camps, including water trucking and water quality monitoring, water containers, construction/ repair of emergency latrines, providing cleaning materials for latrines and latrine cleaning, desludging of septic tanks, and solid waste management.
c) Rehabilitation of water networks and sewage networks to connect the remote sites, camps, and schools in addition to replacing the damaged lines.
d) Support the local operators and municipalities in continuing to collect and dispose of waste from 20 communities, schools, and surrounding IDP sites.
e) distributing hygiene kits and conducting hygiene promotion sessions about the improvement of hygiene practices and behaviors.
f) Cholera prevention through applying the CATI-CORT approach in and off camps.
h) Provide WASH services to 30 education centers and apply the WinS approach.
The project design was done according to SPHERE standards and WASH guidance in line with current HRP priorities.
The planned intervention will mainstream protection principles, especially (Do no harm), throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, and older adults. The provision of sanitation services (latrines, washing facilities, etc.) will be consulted with the targeted camps and communities, and each group's specific needs will be considered.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ATAA is committed to sustaining lifesaving interventions in camps and IDP settlements, focusing on Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) activities such as water trucking, solid waste management, desludging, and hygiene promotion. Coordinating with the ERL Cluster, ATAA aims to provide sustainable solutions for communities, camps, and residential areas, including the extension and construction of sewer and drinking water networks. Solarization will be incorporated to reduce climate change effects and ensure sustainable access to services. The Three Star Approach to WASH in Schools will be applied in 30 schools in northwest Syria, promoting healthy habits and following global guidelines. This intervention aims to overcome the epidemic of cholera and create a healthier environment for children and underserved groups. To address open wastewater and solid waste issues, ATAA plans to design and implement wastewater treatment interventions for agricultural reuse or safe disposal in public networks. Adequate landfill design and implementation will also be a core focus. ATAA employs a scientific risk management approach by developing a risk matrix during project design that considers all potential hazards and mitigation measures. As part of the management of this risk, the S&S department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects and building a trust relationship with the local authorities by clarifying for the local authorities the impact of the project on the region. Due to the unpredictable nature of conflicts, such as shelling or armed clashes, the S&S department closely monitors the project area, conducts daily risk assessments, and communicates potential scenarios and appropriate responses to the crisis cell. In addition, the department updates evacuation and contingency plans and provides field staff with training on expected scenarios and adherence to approved SOPs for a secure operational environment. ATAA is considering the PSEA in the top of priorities by active membership and participation in PSEA networks activities, and developed internal framework and control system to enhance PSEA including all the core standards, in addition to include the prevention measures in all the humanitarian interventions of ATAA.Additionally, ATAA created a solid response system starts from the active and accessible reporting channels, and investigation planning and procedures with qualified and trained staff and network of the focal points with gender balance to enhance access to survivors and provide the response interventions based on survivor based approach.Ataa is updating PSEA assessments with partners and received a UNICEF assessment result as full capacity low risk partner since mid of 2023.
