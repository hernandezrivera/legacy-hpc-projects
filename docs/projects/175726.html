Project Summary

Despite the signing of the peace, the impact of the previous crisis in South Sudan effects continues to be felt in various dimensions, despite the signing of the peace in the year 2018. Many parts of South Sudan continue to experience violence, climatic shocks, and low access to resources and opportunities such as education. Education remains a concern in South Sudan with high rates of school dropouts and low rates of enrollment and re-entry, this largely resulted from past challenges and occasional humanitarian crises, that disrupted education systems, and infrastructure.
It is against this backdrop, that CAO seeks to implement improved and equitable access to safe and protective education services projects. This will entail building capacity of the school community and establishment of safe learning spaces for 18, 578 boys and girls (50%girls) school-aged children including children with disability, youth, and adolescents, from the host communities, IDPs, returnees, and IDP-returnees in, Akobo west and Pochalla counties in Jonglei state, Panyikang and Nasir counties of Upper Nile state, and Twic county in Warrap state, for a period of one year. The project will establish and rehabilitate learning spaces, ensure latrines for boys and girls are constructed and segregated through liaising with our WASH department. We will ensure the latrines have sufficient lighting and remain safe and lockable. The intervention will seek to identify and analyze the barriers, and challenges being faced by learners that limit access to safe and protective education for different ages and sexes. The program will target and involve the following stakeholders: Education stakeholders: the teachers, the community's local leaders, caregivers, relevant government officials, and learners and capacity build them through training on PSS, COVID -19 preventive measures in line with cluster objective. The intervention intends to increase the enrolment of more girls, boys, and learners with disabilities in schools and reduce the rate of school drop out by ensuring appropriate structures and systems are in place and support from the community. An integrated response will be adopted with other sectors such as GBV and Child Protection, Nutrition, Health, and WASH in which CAO is a strong and active member being mainstreamed in the entire project. C.A.O has a strong presence in the above six targeted counties implementing Child Protection, WASH, FSL, and Nutrition projects. Another approach will entail bringing on board the multi-sectoral emergency stakeholders to collaborate with partners especially on awareness-raising. CAO intends to implement in hard-to-reach communities in these locations underserved avoiding duplication with already existing partners.CAO will focus on durable long term solutions after CAO pulls out as we purpose to empower School management committees (SMC) and PTAs by involving youths, women leaders, religious leaders, and Payam administrators in running of schools to ensure education is prioritized in the community even after the end of the project duration. CAO will also lobby for funds for school feeding programs to curb the school dropout rate.

1 - Needs

In Walgak Akobo West, there have been consistent new arrivals from neighboring country Ethiopia and other counties, this is as a result of the signing of the peace agreement in 2018. CAO has been implementing the Child Protection project under UNHCR since last year (2020) in walgak and has evidence-based gaps in Education thus seeking to help bridge the gap and serve the unreached populations in Akobo West. Apart from the big population moving from Akobo West to seek refuge in the neighboring counties and Ethiopia due to conflict between IO and IG, especially when IG settled in Waat, which borders Akobo west, Akobo west residents have experienced the Murle fighting, cattle raiding, revenge killings and communal clashes. As a result, many partners pulled of the area due to insecurity. In the process of implementing CAO staff have reported child and /or forced marriage, under-age children pregnancies, recruiting boys under 15 years into cattle raiding, rape, denial of resources,s and many other harmful practices in the community. The intervention seeks to enroll learners in school, through organizing back to learning campaigns in the community. CAO being a strong and active member of the GBV sub-cluster believes strongly a school is a place where cultural norms can be challenged and reshaped to support gender equality and prevent GBV. In Pochalla, CAO is supporting CP under UNHCR and data from the county Key informants, state that, teachers in Akela, Burator, Adongo, and Pochalla Payams end up abandoning their duties at school to engage in other roles that help provide food for their families including joining the armed forces. CAO plans to close this gap by motivating teachers in this county, through paying incentives, teacher training on EiE, and learner-centered methodologies, teachers' learner's training on updated PSS, teachers and education personnel training on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19, and provision of IEC. The project will conduct back-to-school campaigns, capacity build the education system’s in COVID 19 preparedness on learners, teachers, and communities, in line with the education cluster objective. CAO plans to prioritize synergy with other education actors to ensure that, there is improved equitable access to education services to the learners while avoiding duplication of services, as complementary assistance along with the ongoing activities in schools already. Education in Pochalla, we have FHSS supporting girls in GESS while NPA is implementing feeding programs in schools. This will be complimentary assistance along with the above ongoing activities.Jonglei, Upper Nile, and the Warrap States continue to experience core effects of conflict. Massive displacements and destructions of TLSs and classrooms were witnessed due to heavy flooding last year in most of the areas. December 2020-March 2021 alert estimate 24,000 people in catastrophe(IPC phase 5) acute food insecurity. Following the signing of the peace agreement in 2018, thousands of returnees who had earlier fled to the neighboring countries as refugees and others to different parts of the country as IDPs had to return back to, Panyikang, Nasir, and Warrap. The four counties targeted in this one-year project have been greatly affected due to the shortage of social services in the areas and with the overwhelming numbers of returnees, IDPs, and IDP-returnees coming in to join the host communities and already existing IDPs, there is an urgent need for an integrated intervention in these counties.

2 - Project activities

CAO intends to do the following activities in the targeted locations, to provide improved equitable access to education services to the affected population: • Organize back to learning campaigns. • Enroll 18, 578 children (3-17 years old) in the preprimary, primary, and secondary learning centers.•Establishment of 16 temporary learning spaces/classrooms.•Rehabilitation of 10 temporary learning spaces/classrooms.•Provision of ECD kits to children of ages (3-5years).•Construction of gender-segregated latrines and water points in 16 schools in liaison with CAO, the WASH team.•Distribution of sanitation and hygiene materials (handwashing soap, sanitizers n masks) to39,583 learners.•Distribution of sanitary hygiene kits to 22,000 girls and female teachers under reproductive age.•Conducting training and workshops for 350 teachers and educational personnel.•Distribution of IEC materials.•Conducting campaigns on COVID -19 preventive measures.•Pay teaching staff incentives.•Work with education cluster and ministry of education in the state in development and implementation of curricula.•Provision of learning and teaching materials in schools.•Support in the development of learning /teaching material developed internally /local materials.•Equip youth and adolescents with vocational skills•Conduct training/workshop for SMCs, PTAs, education authority management, and the government.•Training on PSS•Monitoring of attendance and performance.•Payment of school fees.•Raising awareness on the importance of education for both girls, boys, and children with disabilities.Raising Awareness on cross-cutting sectoral issues in the community

3 - Project indicators

Monitoring and reporting will be essential to ensure that weekly and monthly progressing achievements are reflecting indicators .M &E team will regularly visit sites of the projects, to ensure project activities are tracked for the smooth running of the project. The M & E team will work closely with the education program to ensure timely inputs and Workplan has adhered to. M&E will support the collection, analyzing and sharing of the data obtained, this data will be used to assess the progress of the project, in case of any disparity between activity sheet and work plan then realigned towards the proposed achievements will be addressed. A monitoring and reporting plan will be designed before the start of the project. The data collection tools will be in line with the project objective and segregated by age and gender. The data collection tools will capture the following: • Training attendant sheet • Activities monitoring sheet. • School enrollment data. • Teachers’ daily clock book • Learners’ daily attendance register • Monthly record of girls and teachers under reproductive age receiving dignity kits • Monthly school data collection form to end month report. These data collected will be shared with all education stakeholders and used in programming activities to make an informed decision and used to measure the indicators, the number of learners accessing schools, the ratio of male /female teachers, and training undertaken. CAO will facilitate education manager monthly visits to monitor and build capacity for the staff working under the program. At the end of the project, a final assessment report will be conducted to analyze the achievement of the project, lessons learned, and success stories.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the inception meeting, CAO will explain the organizations’ policies and all reporting channels of PSEA and related issues to the beneficiaries. These include PSEA policies, Child safeguarding policies, and code of conduct. The project design highlight all the needs of the affected community hence activities spell how to handle protection issues and GBV risks through engaging with community existing systems to champion a community protection mechanism. CAO recruiting process is also vigilant, recruits are taken through PSEA and child safeguarding policies and understanding code of conduct before signing it.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Staff is expected to be extra vigilant in the process of project implementation.CAO has a culture of ensuring beneficiaries are protected against SEA.CAO staff will be responsible for championing good practices and maintaining an organizational culture that prioritizes and safeguards all against SEA and discrimination. Managers at all levels have responsibilities to support and develop systems that maintain an environment that facilitates the implementation of this Policy and which prevents SEA. Training will be done to staff and program participants to understand the importance of a feedback and complaint mechanism. Special attention will be given to individuals living with disabilities, and even those who are illiterate both women, men, boys, and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The intervention seeks to uphold a community participation integrated approach throughout the project activities. This is to ensure quality aid delivery, active accountability, and create meaningful engagement with the beneficiaries. CAO will ensure that feedback and complaint mechanisms empower the community through open forum participation and FGDs with beneficiaries. The feedback and complaint mechanism will be shared with beneficiaries trained and allowed to contribute to the design of the monitoring mechanism. These mechanisms will be periodically accessed and modified if necessary, to address the needs of the affected communities and education cluster objectives. CAO will constantly hold open forums with different education stakeholders. It will provide a means for education stakeholders: the local government the PTAs, SMCs, teachers, and other groups at-risk to provide comments and voice complaints on the program. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will provide valuable insights and data for the ongoing monitoring and periodical evaluation of a project/program. Appropriate methods of involving people with disabilities will be formulated. CAO will put a suggestion box in every school for complaint confidentiality. All age groups and gender will be considered during the monitoring.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Ensuring that accountability is not compromised, respecting beneficiary's rights as promised. From beneficiaries' feedback, the organization will assess if the project remains committed to community support or if not adjustment will be necessary. The organization will measure the credibility of the project to the local government and donors too. Through this feedback help, the project will be keen to maintain a good relationship with the community through being accountable relevant to their needs and building trust with the affected community. Provision of insight and data for the monitoring and evaluation of the project.
