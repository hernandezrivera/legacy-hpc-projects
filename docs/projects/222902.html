Project Summary

This project focuses on empowering youth, especially girls, in the Rohingya refugee camps in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh. It addresses the significant challenges these girls face, including social and economic restrictions, gender-based violence, and lack of educational opportunities. Through vocational and life skills training, the initiative aims to enhance self-reliance, foster safety, and improve mental well-being, helping these girls build a foundation for a more secure and independent future.
Key elements of the project include practical vocational training in areas like tailoring, handicrafts, household repairs, and agricultural production, offering sustainable livelihood skills. Additional training covers sewing machine operation, carpentry, and cleaning product manufacturing, promoting diverse income-generating skills. Soft skills workshops emphasize life skills like communication, leadership, menstrual hygiene, and psychosocial support, utilizing the Girl Shine model and Gender Equality Movement Skills (GEMS) to foster resilience, self-esteem, and adaptability. Select adolescent girls are also trained as community change agents, enabling them to challenge restrictive norms and advocate for gender equality.
In partnership with a local organization, CARE will coordinate with relevant sectors (Gender and Protection, Climate Action, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) to ensure a comprehensive and gender-responsive approach. Guided by a Rapid Gender Analysis, the program targets about 1,000 girls, aiming to equip them with the skills needed for economic resilience, safety, and leadership, ultimately fostering a stronger, more supportive community for adolescent girls in the camps.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The need for livelihood programs tailored to Rohingya refugee adolescents and youth is urgent, as they face numerous barriers to sustainable employment and skill development (Assessment of Economic Opportunities for Young Rohingyas in Bangladesh, 2022). Limited literacy and vocational skills make it difficult for many youths, especially those over 18, to access formal training opportunities (Guidance Note on LSDS Activity Matrix 2025). Programs like the Accelerated Adult Literacy (AAL) Package aim to address this gap, offering foundational literacy and numeracy skills with an emphasis on livelihood-specific terminology. This helps young refugees prepare for further vocational training, making them more eligible for meaningful employment (Guidance Note on LSDS Activity Matrix 2025). Vocational training programs, including competency-based training (CBT) packages, are designed to meet the specific needs of Rohingya youth by providing them with practical skills in recognized trades (Guidance Note on LSDS Activity Matrix 2025). These programs offer formal certifications that align with national qualifications frameworks, enhancing employability both within and outside of camp settings. This structure ensures that Rohingya youth can pursue economic opportunities with credible qualifications, increasing their potential to access sustainable livelihoods (Guidance Note on LSDS Activity Matrix 2025). The programs also emphasize gender and disability inclusion, creating accessible and safe spaces for all participants (Include people with disabilities & older people in Livelihood & Skills Development Program/ projects). Gender-sensitive practices, as outlined in the Joint Response Plan (JRP), encourage female participation by providing flexible schedules and increased female staff. Efforts are made to accommodate youth with disabilities through adapted materials and inclusive approaches, while initiatives like the JRP’s pathways to economic empowerment offer support for self-employment and small enterprises. By prioritizing inclusivity and providing targeted training, these programs help Rohingya youth gain critical skills and move toward greater self-reliance and community involvement.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project aims to ensure that protection principles are a core part of all community activities and services, especially in youth livelihood and skill development programs. By including protection measures, it aims to help young people become economically independent and protect them from exploitation, abuse, and harm. Protection mainstreaming includes setting up easy ways for young people to report issues and ensuring equal opportunities for all youth and vulnerable groups, such as young women, youth with disabilities, and other marginalised groups.The project will set up robust systems to track the safety of participants, identify risks, and ensure the programs are consistently meeting the needs of young people. We will also create transparent and confidential ways for anyone who experiences abuse or exploitation to get the needed help. We aim to provide a safe space where young people can develop skills, find work opportunities, and succeed without fear.In short, this project will create a safe, supportive, and empowering environment for young people by prioritising protection in our livelihood and skill development programs. This approach will help them achieve long-term economic success while respecting their rights and ensuring safety.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This project aims to address the diverse needs of youth, adolescents, and women-headed households, tailoring interventions to meet the specific requirements. For youth and adolescents, the focus will be on skill development and creating livelihood opportunities to ensure dignified and sustainable livelihoods. These initiatives will empower young community members to become active contributors to their development, equipping them with the necessary tools to succeed. According to the UNHCR-GoB factsheet (September 2024), 3.02% of the affected population are persons with disabilities, with 79.21% of them facing significant barriers in accessing humanitarian assistance due to their impairments. This project will engage youth volunteers to support identification of people with disabilities. It will also specifically target select groups of persons with disabilities, providing them with training and resources for livelihood activities such as handicraft production and traditional mat-making. Crucially, this project will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all project initiatives. This commitment will be reflected in designing and implementing community-led interventions, where people with disabilities will have an active role in decision-making processes. By involving them directly and regularly, we aim to create tailored solutions that address their unique challenges, enhancing their participation and ensuring they can fully engage in community life.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This project is committed to reinforcing a strong Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) framework, with a particular focus on improving access to the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM). According to Inter Sector Need Assessment (ISNA ) data, 27.76% of the affected community are unaware of how to file a complaint, and 19.11% of individuals only register their complaints through informal channels, such as complaining to Majhis or Religious Leaders. This project will address these gaps by conducting extensive awareness-building activities to inform the community about the CFM and its purpose.In addition, the project will host various inception meetings and progress-sharing sessions with youth participants. These meetings will serve as a platform for ongoing dialogue, allowing community members to provide input, express concerns, and track real-time project progress. This will further ensure proper engagement of youth, keeping the community at the centre of decision-making and implementation processes.By prioritising accountability and responsiveness, this project seeks to cultivate a culture of trust, transparency, and mutual respect, resulting in improved satisfaction and better overall outcomes for the affected population. The active engagement and inclusion of youth participants and the community throughout the project lifecycle will enhance the effectiveness and impact of the interventions, ensuring they meet the actual needs and expectations of those they are designed to serve.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

This project will integrate the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles into all community engagement efforts. We will provide targeted training to volunteers and staff, equipping them with the skills and knowledge to identify, prevent, and address incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. These trained youth volunteers will serve as key community advocates, helping raise awareness and promote a culture of respect, safety, and accountability.In addition, we will conduct block-level awareness campaigns (such as workshops, informational sessions, and community discussions) to engage a broader segment of the community. These initiatives will educate residents about PSEA principles while emphasising the importance of formal reporting channels over informal ones. We will focus on raising awareness about the proper mechanisms for reporting PSEA cases, ensuring that youth and community members are informed about the formal processes available to them and the importance of using these channels to protect their safety and the integrity of the response system.By engaging youth and community members at multiple levels, the project will create a network of informed and active individuals equipped to contribute to building a safer environment, ensuring all community members understand their rights, know how to report concerns through formal channels and feel empowered to take action. This approach will strengthen the community's resilience against sexual exploitation and abuse and foster greater trust in the reporting and response systems in place.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will introduce agricultural activities (homestead gardening in sack bags/geo-grown bags/directly in soil) to promote green skills and strengthen women's livelihoods. These agricultural activities/green skills will contribute to climate-smart land use, soil conservation, water retention, and biodiversity preservation, reducing severity of the climate change's negative impacts. As an adaptation, the project will do knowledge-building activities for women groups to adapt water-efficient, space-intensive methodologies and ensure year-round production amidst climate-induced natural hazards and calamities (heavy rainfall, extreme heat, water logging, etc.). The project will promote improving fertiliser applications during agriculture activities like organic composting and carbon sinc gardening to improve soil carbon sequestration and health (through increased organic matter content, reduced erosion, and enhanced nutrient cycling). The project will ensure necessary knowledge-building activities for participants to apply these climate innovative methodologies, which will increase crop production, reduce soil degradation, reduce the risk of crop failure due to extreme weather events and thus ensure food security, increased income and improved livelihoods. Finally, increasing agricultural productivity and incomes will lead to adaptation and build resilience to climate change.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

This project will prioritise and continue training for staff, volunteers, and youths involved in the initiative, focusing on basic emergency preparedness and response. By equipping these key community members with the knowledge and skills needed to respond effectively to emergencies, the project aims to enhance the community's overall resilience. Trained volunteers and youth will be better prepared to handle shocks such as natural disasters, health crises, or other emergencies, ensuring they can provide critical support and assistance.The training will cover essential areas such as risk assessment, emergency protocols, first aid, and coordination with relevant agencies, enabling participants to act swiftly and efficiently during emergencies. This proactive approach will empower local volunteers and youth to play a crucial role in protecting and assisting vulnerable populations during times of crisis, reducing the immediate impact of emergencies on the community.The project will closely collaborate with the Site Management Support (SMS) agencies, providing essential support before, during, and after emergencies. This partnership will strengthen the overall response capacity, ensuring the community can access timely resources, assistance, and information. Together, these efforts will build a more resilient community capable of effectively managing and recovering from shocks and emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. This project focuses on empowering youth through skill development, providing them the tools to access sustainable and dignified livelihoods. The skills gained will open doors to stable income-generating opportunities while equipping youths to play a vital role in connecting CARE with the broader community. Trained youths will act as key facilitators in ensuring effective community engagement, gathering feedback, and supporting the implementation of future programs. Involving youth fosters financial independence and leadership skills, empowering them to advocate for their peers and represent their community's needs. These youth will also facilitate communication between CARE, implementing partners and the community, ensuring that all voices are heard and that initiatives are tailored to the community's unique context. This approach promotes a sense of ownership among young people and strengthens the collaboration between CARE and the community, ensuring that solutions are relevant and effective. By leveraging youth's energy, creativity, and leadership potential, the project aims to build a more resilient, engaged, and proactive community, driving positive, sustainable change that benefits everyone.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The proposal aims to empower Rohingya adolescent girls in Cox’s Bazar by promoting self-reliance through a comprehensive program of vocational and life skills training. Focusing on economic empowerment, it provides training in handicrafts, tailoring, and agriculture, linking these skills to certification programs to enhance job opportunities. In addition to vocational skills, the program includes soft skills training in leadership, communication, and menstrual hygiene management to boost confidence, decision-making abilities, and resilience. To address issues of protection and rights, the program trains girls to become community change-makers, equipping them to tackle early marriage and gender-based violence in alignment with CARE’s broader gender equality goals. Psychosocial support services are also included to assist with trauma recovery, contributing to improved mental health.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

“Nearly half (42.1%) of Cox’s Bazar’s population comprises young adults aged 15-29. Adults, classified as 30 and above, constitute 18.4% of the population, and the remaining 39.5% are children aged 0-14. ILO data show that the literacy rate of the district is 39.29% (40.32% for males and 38.22% for females), which is significantly lower than the national average of 51.77%” (BBS estimates, 2017). This project strongly involves youths and adolescents in the program and activity design. The program will focus on agricultural crop production that aligns with the agrarian growth priorities of Cox’s Bazar district. Empowering youths and adolescent girls is the prime focus of the project and will be equipped with diverse livelihood opportunities, so—in the long run—it will assist the district in economic empowerment and reduced poverty.
