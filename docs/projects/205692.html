Project Summary

This project will contribute to the overall strategic objectives of the 2024 HRP and the three sectoral FSL cluster sector objectives through support provided by UNCDR’s donors and Food security and livelihood partners in order to improve food security and livelihoods situations during an emergency and resilience building of 32,214 (5,369HH) vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls by providing improved access to livelihood inputs for food production, consumption, and dietary diversity.
The outcome of this project is anticipated to increase agricultural food production, protect and build productive assets and restore livelihoods for the vulnerable individuals that are affected by floods, inter communal conflicts, violence, climate change and physical access among vulnerable populations in high food insecure counties of Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut counties whose populations are currently in IPC phase 4 and has high disease morbidity due to preventable diseases, high GAM rates of Malnutrition.
UNCDR is proposing a 12-month FSL project targeting 5,369 HHs (32,214 vulnerable individuals; 1,309 IDPs, 1,800 returnees, 2,260 host community members; of whom 1,900 Women, 1,000 Men, 969 boys and 1,500 girls). UNCDR is an active FSL national partner having implemented FAO and RRF emergency food security and livelihoods in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi & Maiwut Counties.
In line with the FSL cluster and 2024 HRP strategic objectives, UNCDR will scale up response in the target locations by distributing main season and dry season response kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), train and develop the capacity of child protection workers, WASH staff, CNVs, and health workers on the establishment of Kitchen gardens and modern agronomic practices, livestock vaccination, training of CAHWs, assets creation, and business (VSLA) creation at the community level.
The overall objectives of UNCDR’s planned FSL project are to; 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in line with the HRP SOs and FSL cluster objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. This will help to reduce vulnerabilities and risks associated with the current severe food insecurity, poor economic conditions, climatic shocks or floods, and reduced market access due to the poor road conditions among the affected population in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi & Maiwut counties.
In this proposed project, UNCDR has targeted the following counties; Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut in Jonglei and Upper Nile States currently in IPC phase 4 & 5 (IPC, Feb 2023). The project will contribute to protection and recovery of livelihoods of the vulnerable population in Jonglei state.
In line with strategic cluster objectives 2: CLO2 Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support and
objective 3, SO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks, UNCDR intends to reach out to the target population with the following activities; 1) Identification and validating the vulnerable households for their eligibility using a standard criteria, 2) Distribution of on-farm inputs crops and vegetable seeds and to enhance access to food production technologies, 3) Distribution of off-farm inputs/kits to enhance access to off-farm food production inputs and 4) Link the beneficiaries to already available health facilities and referral pathway for effective case management to ensure effective integration.
UNCDR will target vulnerable households in consideration of gender and people living with disabilities, and ensure protection and Accountability to the affected population. UNCDR has a technical FSL specialist, is implementing FSL and protection with SCI in Ayod & Maiwut.

1. Needs

South Sudan is one of the world’s top five countries most vulnerable to climate shocks, including drought and floods. The South Sudan IPC report informs that, 7.10M (62% of the population) People were facing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between April to July 2023, with 87,000 People likely in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) acute food insecurity in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Fangak counties in Jonglei State; Pibor County in Greater Pibor Administrative Area; Cueibet and Rumbek North counties in Lakes State; and Leer and Mayendit counties in Unity State (South Sudan IPC, April - July 2023). The current security situation is characterized by Sub-national and localized violence in many parts of South Sudan and this continue to drive internal population displacements (WFP report, July 2023). The combined effects of these multiple factors continue to result in a loss of human lives and assets, displacement, disruption, and destruction of livelihoods coupled with consecutive years of high floods that submerge human settlements, farmlands, and pastureland as well as disrupted markets and delivery of humanitarian assistance to the flood-affected populations (HNO, 2023); the continuing and worsening macro-economic crisis that is resulting in food price hikes due to the devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound have disrupted livelihoods and slowed the flow and delivery of both commercial and humanitarian supplies and services. This is attributed to persistent conflict causing loss of assets and life, disruption of livelihoods and agricultural activities, immense floods destroying farmlands and crop fields, poor economic conditions due to the continuous depreciation and devaluation of SSP currency, indirect effects of COVID-19 and Russia-Ukraine conflict impacting delivery of humanitarian assistance during the peak of the lean season (June - August 2023) when food shortages were typically at worst levels. Most populations in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut counties are in IPC phase 4 and 5 with critical food insecurity and thus are in urgent need of food and livelihood assistance. The likelihood of improving food security in the entire county remains uncertain owing to the severe effects of the recent floods, ethnic conflicts, and violence, rampant insecurity that left thousands of people displaced. Therefore, there is urgent need for emergency and humanitarian food assistance to avert the worsening food insecurity in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut counties. As the lean season peaks, the food security situation in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut counties are projected to worsen in the first quarter of 2023 (January - April) with more populations moving back to their settlements with virtually nothing to begin a new life in the face of a poor economy. Additionally, New Fangak is currently experiencing the effects of 3rd and 4th wave of immense floods which resulted into mass displacement of about 25,000 people as of September 2022. The situation has been exacerbated by new arrival of 1,000 displaced people from Tonga in Upper Nile as a result of conflicts (Inter-cluster conflict spot-check assessment mission report, September 2022). Overall, Jonglei is likely to face extreme food insecurity with scores in catastrophe (IPC phase 5) (S. Sudan IPC Dec 2022 - July 2023). Many women and children are being highlighted as the vulnerable populations bearing the extreme effects of the multiple factors that drive food insecurity in Fangak. Currently, there are ongoing peace dialogues in Ayod and the situation seems favorable to rebuild resilience owing to relative stability and reduced fighting. This could provide an enabling environment for UNCDR to provide food assistance, re-build and enhance households’ capacity to produce their own food and adoption to safe and positive livelihood coping strategies. This, calls for increased support to households to cultivate by provision of quality emergency livelihoods inputs.

2. Project activities

UNCDR proposed activities, Result 1: 32,214 vulnerable populations (men, women, boys, and girls including IDPs, host communities and returnees) have improved access to food, consumption, and dietary diversity. 1.1. Conduct community consultations for needs verification/prioritization and beneficiaries’ selection process 1.2. Beneficiaries’ identification, verification, and registration exercise 1.3. Rapid Market assessment 1.4. Formation of project management and targeting committees 1.5. Food/cash distribution to the most vulnerable households (restricted, unconditional cash) 1.6. Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring assessments. Result 2: Production & Availability of Livelihood Inputs Supported, with Increased Knowledge of Food Production to Insecure HHs. 2.1. Beneficiaries’ identification and registration 2.2. Conduct livelihood inputs availability assessment in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut counties 2.3. Distribute crop, vegetable, and fishing kits (direct distribution and input trade fairs) 2.4. Conduct farmers trainings on livelihoods (crop, vegetable, and fishing) 2.5. Sensitize households (esp. women) on messages regarding proper nutrition, hygiene, and protection, especially for lactating/pregnant women and for children 2.6. Carry out post distribution monitoring of the livelihood inputs in Ayod, Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut Result 3: Increased livestock production and productivity for the pastoral population affected by the crisis 3.1. Conduct community consultation and community based participatory planning 3.2. Beneficiaries’ identification, verification, and registration 3.3. Carry out Community and household assets creation process through FFA activities 3.4. Formation of voluntary saving and loan associations (VSLA) approaches 3.5. Formation of farmers’ producer groups and training on GAP through FFS and other extension methods 3.6. Capacity building of government extension workers through refresher training 3.7. Training on draft animal technology (ox-plough) and supply of ploughs to farmers’ groups 3.8. Conduct rapid market assessment 3.9. Provide unconditional cash/food to support household assets creation 3.10. Conduct training for women groups in nutrition-sensitive food production especially vegetable production for dietary diversity and incomes.
