Project Summary

The overall goal of the project is to improve vulnerable women’s access to quality multi sectoral services including multisectoral SGBV services, cash enablement, and sexual and reproductive health services and foster women-led humanitarian response (including in response to COVID-19) in oPt. The project responds to HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
This would be achieved throughout three results: 1) To Improve vulnerable women's and girls' access to quality multi sectoral health and protection services 2) To improve the quality of SGBV services provided to vulnerable women and girls 3) Advocate for equitable access to healthcare and protection services, especially for the most vulnerable population to both SGBV and COVID-19 pandemic, through meaningful women’s and girls´ participation.
UN Women will capitalize on the achievements and lessons learnt of the last years interventions in addressing SGBV needs, supporting partners to improve quality, accessibility and responsiveness of the services to the needs of those who are most vulnerable, including to COVID-19 and to conflict-related consequences. UN Women proposes to implement this 12-month project to improve most vulnerable women’s and girls´ accessibility to multi-sectoral healthcare and protection services, cash based initiatives, decision-making, awareness-raising and accurate information on COVID-19, on SGBV, and on service availability.
UN Women’s interventions will be carefully tailored to address women’s and girls’ needs following rights-based and survivor-centred approaches. Interventions are designed to address the needs of the most vulnerable women and girls i.e. women with physical and intellectual disabilities, elderly women and those with non-communicable diseases, women at risk of forcible transfer, women heads of households, women SGBV survivors, etc.
Through its local partners in the oPt, UN Women will work to couple SGBV services with cash assistance and cash for work programming to ensure a maximized benefit for women most affected by conflict and COVID-19. Capacity building, case identification, management and referral will be also strengthened with a special focus on community protection mechanisms to deliver healthcare and protection services. At the same time UN Women will support access to comprehensive, accurate and updated information to partners on COVID-19 and its gendered implications. This information will be disability-adapted and produced in several cultural and age friendly formats and disseminated to the population through the CSOs, and the media.
To change social norms and behaviors around SGBV and women’s protection, men and boys will also be engaged through positive masculinity trainings and specialized trainings on GBV and GBV services.

1 - Needs

The May 2021 escalation of hostilities and the COVID-19 pandemic have reinforced pressures facing women, girls, boys and men in the OPT. The pandemic has also exacerbated inequalities, inequities and discrimination based on location, age, and geography. Women and girls are disproportionately impacted by gender norms and pre- existing inequalities. A CARE rapid needs assessment undertaken in Gaza after the escalation revealed that 76 per cent of those surveyed reported an increase in security concerns facing women and girls since the conflict began while 88 per cent of women surveyed expressed the need for psychological support. In Gaza, service providers underscored “crucial gaps regarding the provision of specialised health services for more serious mental health cases” stating that “There is a lack of specialised services in the community and a shortage of qualified staff to deal with the trauma of ongoing violence”. Service providers for gender-based violence (GBV) against women and girls have reported a drastic increase in demand for services, particularly psychosocial counselling and assistance in cases of severe GBV since the start of the pandemic. The WHO has reported that “helplines for survivors of gender-based violence reported a 70% increase in calls received”. In the Gaza Strip, it is reported that GBV incidents, particularly against women and girls, have been increasing as have negative coping mechanisms, such as school drop-outs and early marriage. In Gaza due to the recent escalation, essential GBV response services (such as MHPSS, health services, and legal aid) were suspended and the two main shelters were closed during the bombardment with GBV survivors in the shelters sent home. The Safe Space in the Jabalia refugee camp was damaged by shelling. A rapid needs assessment found that 85 per cent of those surveyed were not aware of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) complaints system and reporting mechanisms. In the oPt men are overwhelmingly the main touchpoint for cash and voucher assistance. For example, of households receiving Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) assistance, 78.8 per cent are headed by a male family member while only 21.2 per cent are headed by women (e.g. mother, daughter, or grandmother). Recent CVA in the Gaza Strip have demonstrated that beneficiaries have used cash buy food and pay off debts. Ongoing CVA in the Gaza Strip have highlighted the challenge of ensuring women obtain and control an equitable portion of resources received by a household as these decisions are subject to household dynamics which, as highlighted above, tend to reinforce a system that favors men having the ‘final say’ on family matters. All of this, therefore, demonstrates how CVA may contribute to building women’s access to cash, knowledge, social capital, and interactions with institutions and thus introduce additional opportunities for other benefits including self-esteem, self-confidence, self-perception, autonomy, and mobility. Boys and girls in Gaza live in a protracted protection crisis under occupation, magnified by the May 2021 escalation, a 14-year blockade, and internal divisions between the PA and Hamas. UNICEF has estimated that 250,000 children were in need of mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) due to trauma and fear from “injuries, widespread destruction and the loss of lives as a result of the escalation”. Against this backdrop and continuing its strategic cooperation with humanitarian actors (mainly sectors and clusters) and CSO partners, UN Women proposes to implement a one year project to a) improve vulnerable women's and girlsaccess to quality multi sectoral health and protection services b) To improve the quality of SGBV services provided to vulnerable women and girls especially women GBV survivors and women affected by COVID-19 c) Advocate for equitable access to healthcare and protection services, especially for the most vulnerable population to both SGBV and COVID-19 infection through meaningful women and girls´ participation.

3 - Indicators

UN Women will work closely with CSO and cluster partners to make sure the project is implemented according to project workplan, log frame and set indicators and targets. The Project will establish a Project Management Committee (PMC) which will consist of UN Women and all partners. The PMC will meet on a quarterly basis and is responsible for the overall monitoring and quality control of the project. The PMC will facilitate planning and setting overall direction for the project as well as generating and facilitating discussion amongst all partners to help coordinate efforts and avoid overlapping. UN Women will ensure that the interventions will focus on addressing women’s immediate needs in a way that decreases their exposure to various risks. Risk factors within Cash For Work (CFW) are varied according to the setting, geographical area, and type of placement, type of vulnerabilities…etc. UN Women, through systemize documentation of the good practices/lessons learned from previous CFW interventions, will work closely with partners to mitigate risks. E.g one if identified risks is that cash for work beneficiaries face spousal tension or domestic violence as a result of receiving cash (external), the while the mitigation is that coupling the cash for work with GBV assistance and also targeting men and family members of women beneficiaries. Addressing risks will also go hand in hand with strengthening women’s capacities and role as actors of change in their communities and their role in informing/guiding humanitarian efforts at large. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

As a member of the Humanitarian Country Team in the oPt, UN Women operates under the SPHERE principles . Do No Harm is a key humanitarian principle under this framework, and guides UN Women’s programme development and implementation.Thus UN Women ensures that assistance does no harm, or minimizes the harm that may invariantly be done. In this regard, UN Women will work with partners to ensure that the cash for work is coupled with protection-related interventions such as GBV case management and awareness raising of men and that to ensure a protective and enabling environment for women where income becomes an enabler to live a life that is free from abuse. On community sensitization, UN Women will support the partners to carefully develop their messages and pre-test them with the target groups and with victims to ensure that they will be effective to the target audience and will help prevent any unintended harm. As for psychosocial nad legal counselling, UN Women will ensure it is provided as per IASC guidelines and that of the MHPSS cluster and the legal task force. The complaint mechanism will have a primary focal point within UN Women, though outside of the programme/implementation team. Partners will ensure to have a box that is locked in each implementation site, to register complaints. Any allegations related to sexual exploitation and abuse by UN Women staff, partner staff, contractor staff or by cash for work beneficiaries should be reported immediately.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UN Women and CSO partners will adopt a survivor-centred approach in supporting women victims and survivors of GBV. This will ensure that survivors’ rights and needs are prioritized first and foremost. The survivor-centred approach aims to create a supportive environment in which the survivor’s rights are respected and in which women survivors are treated with dignity and respect. The approach helps to promote the survivor’s recovery and ability to identify and express needs and wishes, as well as to reinforce capacity to make decisions about possible interventions.
