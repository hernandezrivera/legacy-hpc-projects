Project Summary

Refugees Women and girls are at risk for GBV due to insecurity, low economic status and lack of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women’s rights and GBV, and negative coping strategies. Due to social stigma and fear of shame and retaliation many survivors of sexual violence are unable to access RH and GBV services. The protection response is often hampered by difficulties of timely response to the needs of survivors. Given this situation UNFPA will support integrated GBV life-saving prevention and response as well reproductive health services for South Sudanese refugees other refugees including Ethiopian, Eritrean and asylum seekers with focus on women and girls and including boys and men also, based on 2019 prioritized locations. The project consists of a reproductive health (40%) and GBV (60%). The proposed project will focus on existing interventions in the field to the extent possible, enhancing GBV referral pathways and psychosocial support, and training of services providers, strengthening of community protection networks ,establishing women centers , skills training and material support, awareness sessions on RH, HIV/AIDS, and GBV targeting refugee populations , Procurement t of RH and GBV kits, training of health care personnel (infection prevention, safe motherhood protocols standard and emergency obstetric care; clinical management of rape), procurement component of basic personal hygiene kits for the most vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age with a planned distribution of 70% of the kit for women and 30% of the kits for adolescent girls. The project will link RH and GBV interventions in so far as the GBV referral pathways builds on the availability of existing services including medical treatment of survivors (clinical management of rape).Due to the current economic situation that affected both the refugees and the host community therefore 10% of the target of the host community be benefited from the project services. Implementation of activities will be through NGOs and Gov partners with a strong field presence in comparative technical advantage.

Needs

Despite the lower arrival rates in 2018, the capacity of inter-agency partners to respond continues to be stretched, exacerbated by a critical funding gap as well as Sudan’s ongoing economic crisis. While progress has been made, many areas are still below emergency standards and require continued investment and scale-up in order to meet the protection and basic service needs of refugees. The South Sudanese refugee population in Sudan have an exceptionally heightened vulnerability with over 60% of new arrivals being children, and women and children comprising 82% of the population. Case management gaps across the response continue to compound protection issues. Participatory Assessment findings for the Darfur, Kordofans and White Nile States indicates that many refugees are concerned about the prevalence of SGBV in their communities. SGBV risk is aggravated by inadequate lighting in camps and settlements, and access to energy and water supply gaps that require women and girls to travel long distances to collect water and firewood, exposing them to harassment and violence. The lack of livelihoods opportunities and the deteriorating economic situation in Sudan also further undermines the safety and well-being of refugee women, girls and boys. Refugee survivors of SGBV have very limited access to justice and legal aid, and there remain significant gaps in medical and other support services. Source Sudan RRP2019 Refugees are similar to their host communities – have little recourse in this economic environment, with limited access to protection, assistance and livelihoods, which will expose the vulnerable people to risk of harassment, exploitation and abuse. Additional to what highlighted in this situation, UNFPA has gathered information from its monitoring and need assessment missions as well as information from partners during 2018 which showing that still most Ethiopians, and Eritreans asylum seekers and refugees, particularly women and girls are vulnerable, under-served with regard to basic services and also lack behind with regard to exercising their reproductive health rights, they do not have sufficient knowledge and means to access available services including information on SRH and GBV and this will continue to remain a challenge with new arrivals.

Activities

UNFPA will strengthen its coordination with UNHCR ensuring complementary of interventions. UNFPA will focus on provision of RH services and prevention of and response to GBV through various mechanisms for multi sectoral services. Specifically, 1. Support Establishment/refreshment of community protection networks including training of members, support functionality and sessions on GBV replicated by the networks members targeting community members.2. Training workshop for (lawyers, legal aid workers, persecutors) on provision of legal aid services including form 8 in regards to SGBV survivors.3. Support community awareness raising campaigns on GBV/HIV/RH during 16 days of activism.4. Support psycho-social support and referral pathway sessions for service providers.5. Training for social workers on SGBV, Case management.6. Support livelihood skills and income-generating activities of most vulnerable trafficked, smuggled and other persons7. Conduction of community awareness in gender equality and root causes of GBV related issues.8. Provide direct assistance such as medical treatment, transportation cost for services of counseling accommodation, and psycho-social support for women and girls at risk of GBV.9. Support Y Peer Educators rolled out awareness activities through campaigns for refugees on SRH, HIV, and GBV dissemination of messages, for community members.10. Procurement and transportation of Basic dignity kit: One kit consist of sanitary pads, tooth paste, tooth brush, body soap, laundry soap and underwear.11. Procurement and transportation of emergency reproductive health and GBV kits for Health facilities, 12. Support and strengthen referral pathways for obstetric emergency cases to high level care,13. Support training on Family planning and safe motherhood protocols.14. Support mobile Clinic services.15. Awareness raising for women, girls, men and boys on RH, pregnancy danger signs, ANC and PNC care , support safe motherhood campaign

Impact on environment/natural resources

Addressing gender equality contributes to environmental sustainability by ensuring the continuity of knowledge and practices related RH health issues affected the environment and population dynamics. In addition the limited access to gender oriented reproductive health services, lack referral mechanisms, skills and knowledge about GBV and health services for women, girls, and GBV survivors, not only impacts on social and economic well-being; it also has an impact on sustaining the resilience. During the project, implementation UNFPA will closely monitoring any mitigation measures that, reduce environmental impacts and do no harm to enhance the environment and Community benefits from the intervention.
