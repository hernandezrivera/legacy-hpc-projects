Project Summary

IOM will implement a range of livelihoods and self-reliance activities for affected Rohingya and host communities including those living inside camps focusing on addressing immediate needs of the vulnerable populations to meet basic necessity and providing sustainable economic development support to ensure strategic approach which aimed to promote social cohesion and peaceful coexistence and accountable for affected populations.
IOM will continue with providing income generating activities and portable skills development for Rohingya in 18 IOM managing camps and Tekanf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar District by linking up with government extension officers and utilizing existing initiatives for on/off farm activities following technical guidance from the Food Security Sector. IOM will diversify vulnerability criteria and extend support including those persons at risks due to protection related concerns, climate change, environmental degradation and COVID-19 through multi-dimensional interventions in coordination with different sector expertise of IOM. It will include the most vulnerable in camps and host communities especially single mothers, female headed households, families with dependent children/persons with disabilities.
Quick impact projects through cash-based interventions will be provided by creating innovative initiatives with youth and vulnerable households to promotes environmental, localization and diversity inclusion.
Study/needs assessment/mapping will be conducted in partnership with organizations and agencies to analyze different needs of post-COVID and coping mechanisms in camps and host communities with sex, age, disability disaggregated data.

1 - Identified Needs

The gains of JRP 2019 made on improving and diversifying livelihoods options of host communities and bolstering coping mechanisms of refugees have been hugely offset by the COVID-19 pandemic, causing a drop in the living conditions. According to the September 2020 ACAPS/WFP vulnerability assessment, there has been a drop in incomes by 45%. The study showed 3 of 7 households resort to intensive coping mechanisms including sale of essential assets, and borrowing to pay for households needs, health care and shelter. With the coordination support of the JRP/FSS, emergency unconditional cash assistance from the humanitarian community benefited close to 35,000 individuals in Ukiya and Teknaf, representing poor households. However, this temporary measure could not adequately address the disconnect of local farmers and retailers from the predominant new food supply chains that has collapsed local and indigenous production. The growing cash economy, focused on supply of essential goods, food and services to meet the needs of the humanitarian response for refugees has resulted in many households unable to create and sustain small businesses or access support to rebuild livelihoods. The sense of unequal access to rights and entitlements within host populations amidst these challenging moments of the Joint response is even higher. Considering Rohingya refugees remain highly vulnerable (REVA 2020, MSNA) and 100% dependent on humanitarian assistance and also more than 370,000 people were reportedly living under the poverty line with an 400,000 poor or vulnerable anticipated to have been impacted as a result of COVID-19 economic repercussions in host community of Cox’s Bazar district, IOM will continue to support local livelihoods opportunities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas and self-reliance assistance in IOM managing camps.

2 - Activities or outputs

-Conduct of a post-pandemic anticipatory needs assessment in camps to design appropriate integrated technical and market oriented skills training that meet needs including market assessment, facilities mapping (women specific institutions) of different refugee category (age, gender, social status, disabilities, etc and beyond-crisis expectations) in 18 IOM managing camps-Provide self-reliance and portable skills assistance to Rohingya through input support and trainings (gardening, handicrafts, upcycling, snack making) to build resilience from the shocks such as pandemic, environmental and climate change, degradation of natual resources as well as to contribute to humaniterian response (WASH, shelter, infra etc.) -Cash for Work for Rohingya youth in camps to restore community infrastructure, cleaning initiative, garbage collection, literacy -Provide market-oriented on-farm and off-farm livelihoods support to vulnerable host communities in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas -Expand market linkages with private sector, existing market system and commercial entities Cash based interventions to restore communal infrastructure through cash for work and unconditional cash assistance to respond to shocks and invest in livelihoods-Production of hygiene materials (face mask, hand sanitizer and face shield)
