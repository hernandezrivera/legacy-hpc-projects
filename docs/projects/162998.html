Project Summary

The joint action for GBV risk reduction, mitigation, response and recovery will focus on the following provinces; Manicaland, Masvingo and Midlands. In Manicaland Trocaire and partners will work in the following districts; Makoni, Mutare urban and rural, Mutasa, Inyanga and Rusape. In Masvingo the intervention will be in the following districts; Chiredzi rural and urban, Chivi, Gutu, Masvingo Rural and urban, Mwenezi and Zaka. In Midlands the intervention will be in two districts that is Kwekwe urban and kwekwe Rural. The programme will target women and young women (18-35) as the primary beneficiaries, it will also target men and young men in preventing GBV through social norms and systems transformation. The intervention will target key duty bearers and service providers to improve GBV service delivery, strengthen referral pathways as well as coordinated multisectoral response to GBV in emergencies.
The project will seek to address the protection needs of women and girls through the provision of safe spaces for women and girls where they can come together for psychosocial support, information sharing and recreation. In addition to addressing the psycho-social needs, the project will also include GBV case management to ensure that women and girls safely access quality, survivor centered, psycho-social support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery. The joint action will also promote Legal and justice actors to support GBV survivors to access safe and survivor-centred legal services that protect their rights and promote their access to justice through mobile legal aid clinics for women to seek legal advice on issues such as protection orders, peace orders, child custody etc. Support for dignity kits will also be provided.
Also key to this emergency response is ensuring the availability of quality GBV services for women by strengthening GBV referral systems and strengthening the relationships between the various GBV actors in the districts such as Victim Friendly Unit, Ministry of Health, Judiciary Service Commission and Social Welfare among others. During Cyclone Idai, Trocaire supported community based information kiosks which served as referral pathway information points as well as spaces for women and men to access GBV services information. These will be also replicated in the project.
The intervention also intends to address the protection needs of women and girls in emergencies by transforming negative social norms which perpetuate GBV and which often worsen in times of emergencies. The intervention will support men and boys and the wider community to challenge and transform systems as well change norms using principles from tried and tested strategies such as Men Engage and SASA! Faith, which Trócaire has been implementing in several of the target areas.
Since multiple inequalities may shape women’s risks and experiences of GBV. Women and girls who face intersecting inequalities include women and girls with disabilities, as such the intervention will ensure through mainstreaming disability the inclusion of women and girls living with disability as project beneficiaries.
In sum, a total 96 606 community members, 10 000 survivors of GBV will benefit from access to safe spaces and provision of dignity kits across the project areas. An additional 14 151 women will receive Psycho-social support over the course of the project. Up to 32 993 women and 28 982 men will be reached with community outreach and awareness-raising to increase timely and safe access to services
and mitigate risks of GBV through information kiosks, IEC materials, radio programmes, flash theatre and roadshows. At least 480 duty bearers and service providers will also be reached by the intervention through multisectoral prevention and mitigation coordination meetings and trainings.

1 - Activities

• Establish or strengthen existing and manage safe spaces for women and girls to provide psycho-social support activities, through the community based shelter model as well as one stop centre model. • Provide individual and group psycho-social support services that are safe and accessible for women and adolescent girls, welcome and integrate women and girls who experience discrimination, and address barriers to access while not exclusively targeting GBV survivors. This will be done through the provision of systemic counselling to survivors of GBV as well as group therapy to both survivors of GBV and any other women who have experienced discrimination.• Provide skills and knowledge-building opportunities for women and girls to improve their psycho-social well-being, e.g., social and emotional learning, financial skills, numeracy and literacy these will be delivered in the safe spaces during the safe spaces sessions. • Identify and promote community-based support, self-help and resilience strategies, including working with women and girls to establish support groups and networks that promote healing and recovery.• Capacity building of local partners and GBV caseworkers to implement the steps of case management, in a survivor-centred way and respecting the GBV Guiding Principles.• Establish a functional and emergency context-appropriate referral pathway that builds on existing GBV services and community-based structures. This will be done through training GBV service providers and response workers on the root causes, consequences and impacts of GBV, survivor-centred principles and skills, and the capacity to support survivors in emergencies through a coordinated multisectoral approach• Provision of survivor-centred legal support services through mobile legal clinics, court support services as well as legal representations for survivors that seek to take their cases to court.• Equip male community activists to act as allies for GBV risk prevention, risk mitigation and recovery programming. This will be done through training and mentoring of community activists in social norm change strategies using principles from men engage. • Develop IEC material to disseminate information and engage the community around the availability and utility of case management services, psycho-social support, referral pathway. Legal aid and systems and norms change through the development of IEC material and the establishment and management of information kiosks in affected communities. • Monitoring and evaluation through process monitoring as well as outcome monitoring to ensure the effective implementation as well as attainment of set outcomes. Monitoring will be done with project beneficiaries and their views will influence project design and decision making.
