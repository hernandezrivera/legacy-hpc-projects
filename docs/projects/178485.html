Project Summary

Through this General Food Distribution (GFD) project, Samaritan’s Purse in North East Syria is meeting the critical food assistance gaps for 1,572 vulnerable conflict affected individuals in Hassakeh (out-of-camp populations) and in 2021 plans to also target Raqqa Governorate (3,692 in Tel Samen camp) via 12 rounds of GFD, which will enable vulnerable populations to meet their basic food needs. The situation in IDP origin locations remains unstable, resulting in prolonged displacement across Raqqa and Hassakeh governorates. SP is responding to identified gaps in current General Food Distribution (GFD) coverage by the NES Forum and Humanitarian Affairs Office (HAO). Securing a partner to provide monthly food baskets has been a key priority for Tel Samen camp management,(camp coordination call, 16 March 2021) and the NES INGO Forum has also flagged the need to secure monthly food distributions to this camp, particularly from Apr to Dec 2021, which is where SP will focus GFD efforts to the full population of 3,692 individuals.
SP will align with the sector recommended package by contributing to the 2021 SO1 sector targets of emergency food rations and food baskets. If feasible S02 targets of agriculture inputs and income generating activities will also be targeted by SP. We are currently implementing and income generating project in Hassakeh via a grants and apprenticeship program and if feasible this will be scaled up in line with sector targets for 2021. Assessments will be completed with our local partners to determine opportunities to link food security programming to further livelihoods opportunities through 2021 in Tel Samen camp in Raqqa, and for out-of-camp populations in Hassakeh. Livelihoods and food security assessments have already been completed by SP and it is expected that future programming will link our existing work to the opportunities identified in the assessments, once the data is analyzed. In particular SP is aiming to align with the 2021 S02 sector targets including agriculture inputs and income generating opportunities, as well as addressing the 2021 sector targets of food baskets and emergency rations.
This response is partly funded and covers the out-of-camp populations which is currently being implemented. Funds will be solicited to cover the response needed in Tel Samen. A minimum of twelve rounds of monthly food assistance has been funded by SP, who are working together with our implementing partner, Syriac Cross, to distribute the food in the current out-of-camp locations. The approach is harmonized with other actors to ensure there is no duplication or crossover and both Samaritan’s Purse and Syriac Cross are in full regular co-ordination with the North and East Syria Forum Working Group and Humanitarian Affairs Office (HAO). SP’s current basket has been built in coordination with the Forum Working Groups, and the kilocalorie count falls in line with food assistance standards. Further, households surveyed preferred in-kind food support (Development of NES NGO Response Priorities 2021, Consultation 1, 20th Jan 2021).
SP previously delivered GFD and RTEs to 28,198 IDPs from Mar to Dec 2020. During this time SP were always able to receive the full order on time and distributions were never cancelled or delayed. Undertaking this distribution alongside partners has ensured a streamlined distribution process. Specifically, SP has held quarterly trainings with Syriac Cross, on warehousing, commodity management, distribution reporting, and assessment and beneficiary data collection to refine and further develop their capacities in GFD program implementation; and continues these capacity building activities in 2021. SP and Syriac Cross adhere to Sphere standards for safe locations of food distribution sites and consult with local populations to create safe strategies for access and equitability and to mitigate risks and protection/PSEA concerns exacerbated by the needs presented.

4 - Needs

Needs and gaps expressed by the Forum and HAO include 59% of IDPs in Hassakeh reporting not being able to afford essential food items - REACH. Based on further REACH data from Tel Samen camp, food needs are still a clear gap in many instances; 21% of households responded that they ate cheaper or poorer quality food for at least three days in the previous week as a coping strategy amongst to mitigate food insecurity in Tel Samen camp (followed by 15% who ate cheaper/poorer quality food out of 4 days in the last week, and 13% who responded that they ate cheaper/poorer quality food out of 5 days in the last week. This is the most prevalent coping strategy in Tel Samen camp. (REACH Camp Profile, Tel Samen Camp, March 2021). Food distributions are the most common source of food amongst respondents (100%), with markets inside the camp at second place (98.9%), followed by markets outside the camp at a large drop off (23.2%). (REACH Camp Profile, Tel Samen Camp, Mar 2021). Needs of HHs Tel Samen camp continue to be met given the expanding waiting list (SSWG November 2020). SP will cover gaps in assistance, especially from Apr-Dec. In late 2020 Tel Samen had a waiting list of 932 HHs (SSWG Nov 2020) and an additional 500-650 tent plots are now planned (Tel Samen Camp Meeting, SSWG Meeting, Feb 2021), illustrating the need for a partner to continue to provide GFD. Monthly food assistance will continue to be a need given the context and trend of IDPs relocating to the camps (Feb 11, 2021 camp coordination demographic updates); with deteriorating socio-economic conditions expected to last through the duration of this program.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities of distributing 790 MT of food in 2021 to 5,264 individuals across Hassakeh and Raqqa governorates over 12 rounds of GFD are implemented through Samaritan’s Purse and Syriac Cross. Key activities:• # Metric tons of food commodities distributed (790 MT total target for 2021).• # People provided with MT of food (1,572 individuals in Hassakeh and 3,962 individuals in Tel Samen camp, Raqqa).• # Trainings conducted on distribution methodology• # COVID messaging displayed at distribution Each registered and verified household will receive a registration card which is stamped at the distribution point. Beneficiates lists are updated and verified prior to each round of distribution. At the distribution point, the household and family size is recorded, and new families are recorded as unique beneficiaries. Samaritan’s Purse counts individuals and the MT of food distributed each month. To avoid double counting, city council registration lists are verified with the previous months’ lists. Targeted GFD in NES neighborhoods distributes monthly food rations to 5,264 individuals living within the Hassakeh host community. This project will build the capacity of local NGOs by providing ongoing training and support in distribution methodology. Samaritan’s Purse has a unique and expansive network of partnerships with local NGOs throughout NES. In coordination with local organizations including the Kurdish Red Crescent (KRC), Syriac Cross, Rojava, and the Al-Hassakeh Organization for Relief and Development, SP ensures effective targeting of beneficiaries in each sector and has conducted programming across the region in difficult to access areas.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks identified for this project include: 1. Beneficiary harassment to and from distribution sites (for example beneficiaries are at risk because they may be in need of transport to/from the distribution sites, which makes them vulnerable to those who may commit coercion or aim to exploit the beneficiary sexually), 2. Access issues at distribution sites (e.g. poor lighting, little room to modify the queuing system if needed), 3. Beneficiaries or staff exploited by staff members (for example, women being targeted or exploited at the distributions). To mitigate these risks and ensure a high level of service delivery as a whole, the quality of activity implementation is monitored by two complaint mechanisms, as well as through SP quality control at distribution sites. Beneficiaries have the opportunity to leave an anonymous complaint at the distribution point via a complaint box, or by making a formal complaint at the HAO office. The HAO office then passes complaints to SP. SP then determines how to improve and built capacity of local partner. A safeguarding focal point is assigned to this program and they support the next steps of the complaint and are trained in the discreet handling of issues arising in the program. SP regularly visits distribution sites to monitor distribution practices. SP receives feedback from beneficiaries via Syriac Cross on commodity and services. Protection is streamlined through this program by ensuring that all Samaritan’s Purse staff involved in the project are trained in managing and/or referring specific gender and protection concerns which may arise in the program and have a good operational awareness of protection risks and issues.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

• Staff or beneficiaries assaulted en route to/from distributions, for example vulnerable beneficiaries are at risk because they may be in need of transport to/from the distribution sites, which makes them vulnerable to those who may commit coercion or aim to exploit the beneficiary sexually.o Transportation services are provided to staff and beneficiaries (for example, vulnerable individuals can utilize community support mechanisms to have their GFD rations delivered to their homes).o This risk is further mitigated by guards and distribution staff on site at distribution points to support beneficiaries, including a safeguarding and protection focal point. Female staff remain on-site so that women have a female point of contact to approach. • Beneficiaries or staff exploited by staff memberso This risk is mitigated by background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed at distribution points and clear reporting system for violations. There is a feedback and complaints processing department to support the mitigation of this risk. Our implementing partner, Syriac Cross (SC), work on the ground to collect and verify anonymous feedback (and if not anonymous, they will anonymize the information), and provide a weekly summary to Samaritan’s Purse. The feedback is discussed and acted on, with SC and Samaritan’s Purse working together with their respective safeguarding and protection focal points to follow up PSEA issues.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Access issues (closure, disruption) might make informal border crossing (Feshkabour-Semalka) inaccessible. o Mitigate through protocols for remote working including remote programming and support to local implementing partner, Syriac Cross. • Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time.o This is mitigated by ensuring strong communication lines with the Forum and KRI to obtain approvals in a timely manner and with with NES staff and partners to ensure there are no gaps in programming. o Implementation will be maintained through local partner, Syriac Cross, providing an added layer of programmatic stability as programs can continue in the event that IFS are not able to be in country.o Samaritan’s Purse partnership with Syriac Cross also mitigates risks in the areas SP is working, providing added security and sustainability. Working with Syriac Cross also enables SP to empower local organizations by providing dignified employment, and protecting local capacity. • Tension leading to conflict between IDP and Host community where GFD is distributed, or between other neighborhoods that are not targeted. o Mitigated by ongoing communication with the HAO and Forum to determine if there are needs within the host community. There will be potential programming for host community built into the program if deemed appropriate/necessary and in consultation with the Forum and HAO regarding gaps and needs. If the host community creates an insecure environment, distributions may be halted until a solution/resolution is found.• Increase in COVID-19 in NESo Reduce contact by combining 2 monthly rations into 1 month. Conduct distribution over multiple days to smaller groups.
