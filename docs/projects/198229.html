Project Summary

Extraordinary rainfall events related to the climate crisis have increased water-related diseases, especially cholera and acute diarrhea. Floods coupled with the population dynamics in IDPs camps, such as the high density of the population and the increase of returnees to their places of origin, have resulted in problems for the operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure, and for the proper management of fecal and solid waste, with a high environmental and public health impact. This is in a context of an increase in the number of people needing WASH assistance, whose figure has risen from around 3 million to 5 million, most concentrated in the northeastern part of the country. In response to the needs, IOM will scale up WASH services targeting internally displaced people and returnees in camp, camp-like settings, and host communities in the BAY States.
For this purpose, IOM will (A) Ensure safe access to water facilities and services through the drilling, installation, maintenance, rehabilitation, and water quality monitoring of boreholes; (B) Ensure safe and equitable access to sanitation facilities (latrines, showers, including laundry spaces), handwashing stations) through the operation/maintenance of existing facilities and construction of additional gender-segregated facilities and wastewater treatment systems; (C) Promote improved water and sanitation outcomes in targeted settings through the systematic promotion of personal and environmental hygiene messages (following national guidelines on water-borne diseases) and including COVID-19 preventative measures; and the support for increased capacity of established gender-balanced WASH committees; (D) Ensure environmental sanitation (solid waste management) in operation area through the establishment of WASH Committees including- male and female community members, procurement and distribution of tools/equipment; (E) Distribute WASH NFI following the national/ cluster standards, and contribute to reinforcing the WASH sector core pipeline strategy to facilitate WASH NFI's access to humanitarian actors implementing actions; (F) Contribute to the strengthening of capacity building activities of local authorities, stakeholders and key partners through the integration of specialists in innovation and research in the areas of management of water, sanitation services and environmental risk mitigation. The project will be implemented in two states: Borno (5 LGAs) and Adamawa (4 LGAs) targeting an estimated 300,000 individuals.
IOM will ensure that the age, gender, as well as disability-specific needs of the project beneficiaries are considered during implementation and specifically addresses women and girls’ needs, such as Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM).
Regarding assessments, monitoring, and evaluation of gender, age, and disability-specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures in these locations during missions and set up communication channels where possible. Using the IOM WASH Monitoring and Evaluation Framework and the Gender Inclusion and gender-based violence (GBV) Risk Mitigation Toolkit for WASH Programming, IOM will ensure that “Do No Harm” principles are adhered to throughout the WASH interventions, particularly in latrine and water point locations, through the use of various mechanisms, such as consultations with vulnerable groups.
IOM will analyze the potential risks due to context-specific environmental degradation and will prioritize actions that mitigate environmental risks. In that regard, IOM will favour the use of solar-powered water supply where possible to reduce the number of fossil fuels generated by diesel pumping. IOM will ensure that groundwater drilling is linked to monitoring initiatives to ensure adequate aquifer recharge. Latrines will be rehabilitated using materials that do not deplete local natural resources, while hygiene items will be of high recycled content

1 - Needs

WASH needs, mainly access to clean, sufficient and safe water for drinking, hygiene, and sanitation, have continued to increase in scale and severity across north-east Nigeria. New dynamics of population displacement and return, together with the effects of the climate crisis (cycles of floods and droughts) have increased the population in need and the challenges in managing water resources and WASH services in general.Access to safe water has been constrained as a result of new and protracted displacement as well as returns (self-organized and government-led), resulting in host communities registering a reduced resilience due to high pressure on the existing and already overburdened water points and sanitation facilities. This, compounded by the climate crisis, has also brought to the table the imperative need to improve the environmental risk management aspects of humanitarian interventions in Nigeria. New displacements and challenging groundwater potential in some areas have resulted in the need for emergency water provision through water trucking and increased costs for in-depth hydrogeological studies and optimization of water points to ensure water resources are used effectively. Access to sanitation continues to be diminished due to wear and tear of emergency-type construction, continuous displacements, and seasonal flooding leading to high open defecation and reduced handwashing practices. Sanitation needs for persons with special needs, women, and children to ensure safe and equitable access are a critical gap in the sector, which requires additional resources as other issues of proximity and design of the facilities are considered. The WASH and Health sectors continue to work together on disease early warning and cholera prevention campaigns coupled with environmental hygiene to prevent high AWD cases and mitigate cholera outbreaks. From 2021, the WASH sector has further rationalized partners’ operations to ensure more accountability to beneficiaries and better coverage of needs with reduced funding, which has improved the quality of the response. IOM, as a WASH Sector partner, continues to respond to the WASH needs in north-east Nigeria. IOM WASH services will not be limited to the planned camps – but also to the new arrival sites, transit and reception centers, spontaneous camps and camp-like settings, and host communities with limited or no WASH services in BAY States.

2 - Activities

IOM will strengthen its humanitarian activities in line with the objectives and guidelines of the Nigerian WASH sector and the global IOM's WASH principles of implementing actions that are immediate and rapid; appropriate, innovative, and compatible with needs; scalable to respond to the changing dynamics of the Nigerian context; and sustainable to respond to environmental issues and the challenges of WASH resource management and environmental risk management.This, IOM will implement activities of 1. Provision of safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for the affected population through the drilling of new boreholes and installation of hand pumps; rehabilitation and upgrading of existing boreholes to pumping systems powered by solar energy; water quality monitoring; capacity building of water system maintenance committees and WASH committees. 2. Delivery of safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities through the construction of new adequate segregated sanitation facilities (latrines, toilets, and showers) at household level and institutional and public places. Likewise, the rehabilitation and upgrading of existing sanitation facilities; operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities; desludging of latrines; construction of wastewater treatment and disposal systems; provision of hand washing stations inclusive of soap at public sanitation facilities and institutions; provision of solar lamps at blocks of latrines and showers; and in consultation with communities and beneficiaries. 3. Provision of a safe and dignified environment through the provision of sustained environmental sanitation services (solid waste management)., including the provision of waste bins and waste disposal pits and sites; and transportation of garbage to disposal sites. 4. Affected communities benefit from community-tailored gender and age-sensitive personal and environmental hygiene messages aiming to promote hygienic behavior and practices through sensitization sessions with community members and stakeholders to minimize the spread of WASH-related and other communicable diseases such as COVID-19. waterborne diseases – including cholera and AWD, and COVID-19. Thus, the establishment of School Hygiene Clubs; training of Hygiene Promoters; cholera preparedness and response activities; distribution of hygi ene, WASH NFIhygiene kits, and menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, and the production and dissemination of contextualized Information, Education, and agreed Communication (IEC) materials. Upon distribution, affected population will be sensitized on its use and disposal. 5. Development of capacity-building activities for key stakeholders, authorities, and partners, including developing research and innovation programs on WASH resource management, environmental risk management, groundwater, flooding, and climate change impact on water resources.6. Activities to strengthen the resilience of conflict-affected people and promote the HDP nexus, early recovery, durable solutions, WASH and peacebuilding, and social cohesion.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The objective of IOM is to target populations and authorities who are encouraged to take ownership of WASH programs, promoting long-term resilience and sustainability while supporting the operationalization of the HDPN.The transition and recovery of IOM WASH Nigeria actions begin in humanitarian settings by applying development principles in such settings. The IOM WASH approach aims to gradually turn the dividends of humanitarian action into sustainable crisis recovery, resilience building, and development opportunities. Durable solutions, self-reliance, and community stabilization are key components of transition and recovery, as they can contribute to peacebuilding by addressing social tensions. These factors, therefore, lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and peace and reduce the likelihood of further displacement. IOM WASH interventions are uniquely placed to deliver on these objectives by providing tangible services as an entry point for delivering basic needs whilst delivering on additional aspects, such as governance, institutional capacity building, job creation, and conflict resolution simultaneously. IOM Nigeria will develop this strategy by supporting WASH beneficiaries, key stakeholders, and local governance to gradually hand over responsibilities for WASH management and finance to beneficiaries and regulatory authorities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM, in seeking to ensure that affected communities needs are addressed, human rights are upheld, and their needs fulfilled, subscribes to the concept of Protection Mainstreaming and Centrality of Protection. Protection Mainstreaming is the inclusion of humanitarian protection principles into the crisis response, ensuring that any response is provided in a way that avoids any unintended negative effects (do-no-harm), is delivered according to needs, prioritizes safety and dignity, is grounded on participation and empowerment of local capacities, and ultimately holds humanitarian actor’s accountable vis–à-vis affected individuals and communities. IOM ensures that it provides humanitarian assistance that impartially prioritizes all civilians and non-combatants without due regard to legal status since all affected populations, irrespective of their status, are entitled to humanitarian assistance and protection. IOM also considers the specific needs of all population groups and conducts activities that lead to equitable coverage of those needs.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the project’s design and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning regarding accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the community-based WASH committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
