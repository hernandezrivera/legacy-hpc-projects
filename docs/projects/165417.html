Project Summary

The Women’s Protection and Empowerment program (WPE) is composed of Partnership and Capacity Building (PCB) component and Protection Provision Services (PPS) component and adheres its activity to women 18-50+ years and adolescent girls 13-19 years old. The PCB component works directly with pre-selected partners inside Syria while also building partners capacity through specialized trainings for future sustainability, proceeding knowledge and skills development, and coaching and mentoring protection awareness and understating for prevention and intervention measures. The PPS includes four WPE mobile teams working directly with women and adolescent girls inside NWS Syria delivering three structured activities: My Safety My Wellbeing session for adolescent girls, New Mothers Support Group for new mothers and mothers to be, Psychosocial Support Group for women and adolescent girls, Problem Management plus for women and adolescent girls and focused psychological services with internally and external safe referral (for women and adolescent girls) at a predetermined (safely selected location, accessible, safe, comfortable and welcomed by the community) in Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) and delivering Psychological First Aid (PFA),. In addition, WPE conducts awareness sessions on its services within the nearby community using the GBV SC awareness raising toolkit including topics prioritized for men and boys. The WPE program focus is to ensure women and adolescent girls are supported emotionally, psychologically and physically while provided further knowledge on GBV, GBV forms, risks and prevention. In addition, the WPE ensures GBV survivors are safely referred and connect to required specialized service for further adherence to meet her protection concerns and needs As the IRC – WPE acknowledges the significant protection concerns women and adolescent girls in Syria face, WPE rounds its functionality in order to directly and indirectly adhere to the prevention and intervention measures required to ensure the increased safety measures are deliberated to women and adolescent girls. The IRC works closely with GBV sub-cluster working groups in Turkey as one of the major protection actors in the humanitarian field. Under the IRC, WPE – PCB component, the program supports sub-cluster raised protection capacity needs, protection knowledge and protection management training material.

4 - Needs

Given the situation in Syria (Northwest Syria) in particular, and as we head into the ninth year of the continued conflict, there are very limited services and deliverables to individuals in need which reflects on the inability to cope with the current situation. GBV continues to disproportionally affect the lives of women and girls in Syria, both inside and outside their homes. New and recurring GBV trends confirm that women, adolescent girls, widowed/divorced women and girls, women and girls with disabilities and older women are exposed to a multitude of GBV risks, exacerbating their vulnerability and resulting in very few places where they feel safe.” HNO 2020. Also women and adolescent girls face “stigmatisation, discrimination, sexual violence, and forced marriages. They face specific challenges in accessing assistance including but not limited to shame, and risks of sexual harassment and exploitation”. HNO 2020. Furthermore, GBV, especially child/forced marriage, sexual harassment and sexual violence (including rape), denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse continue to pervade the lives of women and girls, resulting in very few spaces where they feel safe. With the lack of international laws, humanitarian access and available services, protection response remains most required and critical service for further improvement and development. Empowering women and adolescent girls to combat GBV is a necessity and a requirement from humanitarian actors with protection specialization trained and well-practiced WPE partnership and capacity building teams train local capacities that require further experience, knowledge and enrich of skills in order to address the community needs and respond to protection cases through case management services. 5 - Implementation of activities

Four GBV mobile teams will deliver response and prevention services. Mobile teams’ locations will be determined based on displacement /population density and needs of women and girls and will provide immediate lifesaving services to women and girls newly displaced and in the host communities. Prioritized GBV services include: PSS, safety audits, problem management sessions for individuals, GBV risk mitigation, referrals, coordination, awareness raising/information sessions on topics women and girls request including CSD, mine action and early childhood development. IRC’s teams will continue to run age-appropriate group support structured PSS sessions for women and girls in semi-static locations provided by local councils or rented buildings by IRC. Structured sessions at these locations will include stress management, dealing with emotions, relaxation techniques, and ‘my safety my well-being’ (MSMW) sessions for adolescent girls which will be replaced with an updated curriculum of Girl Shine. PSS structured sessions are designed to build coping skills and resilience among women and girls, support social networks. Women who participate in new mothers support groups and PSS activities will be introduced to early childhood care content to help them provide stimulating activities with their children, while 3-6 years old children will participate in early childhood education activities in the safe spaces. IRC will continue to support up to 50 case workers from 10-15 local Syrian partners in northern Syria to strengthen their GBV case management programming by building capacity of staff knowledge, systems and management structures and aims to graduate 5-7 organizations who will receive all IRC tools to enable them train their own staff in subsequent years.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Security situation of Syria remains challenging while administration of activities in the affected areas. Given the sensitivity and the importance of services be delivered in a safe and dignified manner, IRC works with its safety and security dedicated departments and ensures locations are sensitively determined to ensure the utmost safety measures are taken for beneficiaries and staff, this is run by collaboration between the Jordan remote team and Syria field staff. Management is run through Jordan remote team. Coordination is conducted to ensure service provision and delivery meets the minimum standards and ensures do not cause further harm to beneficiary focus. IRC works with GBV sub-cluster for recommendation to cover areas in need that lack protection services for targeted vulnerable group based on need identified as women, PWD, elderly and adolescent girls. WPE reaches local council within the community inside Syria for further support within the community. Humanitarian Access Unit (HAU), coordinate with program field management to identify risks that may impact IRC to manage, mitigate and prepare contingency plans. IRC follows its Security Management Plan (SMP) and (SOPs) for NWS to support further needed assistance and safety measures to be taken for beneficiaries and operating staff. Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FARM) reinforced by IRC assists beneficiary accountability and credibility. IRC is reinforcing Client Responsiveness (CR) for its programs to support beneficiary involvement to ensure services provided actually address required, raised and necessary needs. Protection risks include: termination of activities security, restricted movement of female clients, transportation challenges, possibility of the community not accepting GBV service provision, and staff turnover.
