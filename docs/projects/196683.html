Project Summary

Resulting in ADRA’s presence in the targeted areas of Homs, Aleppo and Deir Al Zour, severe protection concerns and gaps in the humanitarian response have been identified, particularly regarding people with special needs, and those who were affected physically and mentally by the explosions and violence happened during the years of the crisis. Based on these identified needs, ADRA will implement a variety of protection activities in line with the protection sector objective 1, targeting a total of 25.300 individuals, 5.300 of whom have special needs. ADRA will implement the project in close cooperation with the Ministry of Social Affairs and the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) in the target areas the activities will include rehabilitation of damaged disability centres benefiting 5000 PwD, distribution of 300 disability devises to PwD under indicator 4.3 and provision of street light working on solar power benefiting 20000 individuals under indicator 3.2.

3 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis has had a devastating impact on the civilian population with approximately 15.1 million people needing humanitarian assistance in 2023 (HNO 2023). Prolonged conﬂict and multiple displacements have resulted in high unemployment rates, a loss of livelihoods. The price inflation of basic commodities, shortages of food and fuel and depreciation of local currency are expected to worsen, driving increased vulnerability and leading to heavy reliance on humanitarian assistance and resorting to harmful coping mechanisms, in particular for women and children, as well as sexual exploitation and abuse (HNO 2023). More than 6 million children, including those with disabilities who were affected by UXOs, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services. The ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services continue to significantly affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children and caregivers (HNO 2023). ADRA field staff in the governorates reported the need to support PwD with disability devices as they are not able to precure it due to the high cost. A lack of street lighting was reported to ADRA staff to lead to increased levels of SGBV, particularly among women, boys and girls, as reported to ADRA Syria ﬁeld staff.

4 - Implementation of activities

ADRA Syria plans to undertake a community-based approach to provide quality and integrated protection services (through the rehabilitation of disability centres) and (distribution of assistive devices to persons with speciﬁc protection needs and street lighting in high-risk areas). These activities address the extensive destruction of infrastructure and services and will help reduce the incidences of SGBV. ADRA Syria will target an estimated 5000 with the rehabilitation of three disability centres, planned for Homs, Aleppo, and Deir ez Zour and provide 300 people with disabilities with assistive devices. This will be implemented in close coordination with MoSAL who will be responsible for the management and running the services of the centres following rehabilitation. ADRA Syria will support particularly vulnerable women, boys, girls and the elderly who are often more vulnerable to unemployment status and without the economic means of procuring essential disability devices. ADRA will coordinate the selection of beneﬁciaries with (SARC) and the newly rehabilitated centres which has detailed understanding of individuals protection needs within the targeted community including the inclusion PwD. ADRA will make sure to take into consideration WGSS in the disaggregation the beneficiaries. Streetlights, to be installed in one location in Deir ez Zour will reduce the protection risks, particularly for women, boys and girls, who leave home after daylight hours for 20,000 individuals

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk that community / individual tensions may occur if the demand for assistive devices is larger than the available amount. (Likelihood: Low; Impact: Medium) Mitigation: ADRA will work closely with local stakeholders to ensure a clear selection criterion for the selection of assistive devices and that the most vulnerable will receive the assistance available.There is a risk that beneﬁciaries or staff will be exposed to sexual exploitation or abuse. (Likelihood: Low; Impact: High). Mitigation: All ADRA staff is required to sign the code of conduct, as well as undertake training in protection principles. Staff will go to the ﬁeld in gender balanced teams to ensure accessibility, accountability and prevent any issues arising. In addition, an established complaints mechanism is available and will be provided to beneﬁciaries to report any concerns or issues.Risk: Difficulty in accessing distributions for people with physical disabilities including mental health disorders. Mitigation: ADRA will provide seats for PwD who cannot stand queuing for long periods, and will chose location close to beneficiaries, as well ADRA will consider house-to-house distributions with female staff present or transportation support for people who are unable to leave their homes or have movement limitation


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: The field staff or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, likelihood low, impact high. As a mitigation measure, ADRA Syria will stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct, and will regularly refresh and train its staff and partner staff on the importance of maintaining an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Project staff at all different levels, partner staff, contractors, and suppliers will be encouraged to read and understand the ADRA code of conduct and safeguarding, the protection manual and commit to abide by this principle. ADRA protection officer will ensure to have systems in place for activating the system, gathering information, and conducting an investigation where necessary to make decisions together with ADCOM for taking any necessary actions towards any misconduct reported.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several potential risks have been identiﬁed that may impact the success of the project. One is the changing security situation. To mitigate this risk, ADRA Syria will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. ADRA will also choose locations which have been stable for some time and unlikely be directly impacted by renewed conﬂict. If needed, ADRA will negotiate with the donor to conﬁrm the ﬂexibility to change the project locations as required. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). On the other hand, as the proposed project is mainly community-based level, and targets mostly people with disabilities, it is unlikely to create protection risks of project beneﬁciaries. However, these is a slight risk that by reopening disability centres, individuals will be exposed to protection concerns either on the way to and from the centres. ADRA will select centres in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the beneﬁciaries also ADRA will ensure the sustainability of the centres through official handover to MoSAL and provision of capacity building to MoSal staff to be able to run services. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes). Delays in obtaining the approvals. To mitigate this ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary approvals and discuss any delays with the MoSAL (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes).
