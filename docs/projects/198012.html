Project Summary

The project aim at improving the protection of IDP and affected children from violence, exploitation and abuse. To achieve this we will identify children in need of mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS) and ensure child rights violations are documented to inform additional child protection interventions. This will be carried out by monitoring the situation of affected people in hard to reach areas, the establishment of Psychosocial Support Units (PSSU) for psychosocial support, by identifying and documenting violations against children; undertaking preliminary identification of unaccompanied and separated children for the provision of alternative care /support by “community based” agents; family tracing and reunification, as well as long-term foster care where family tracing is not successful; and the provision of capacity development for “community based” agents; Monitoring and documenting violations and helps in the better protection of children; Providing children at risk of violence, abuse and exploitation during humanitarian situations with child sensitive case management (multisectoral services such as psychosocial support, education and family economic empowerment); Providing support to issue birth certificates to humanitarian situation-affected children; Identifying and implementing measures to prevent risks of violence, abuse and exploitation of children during humanitarian situations (sensitized parents and caregivers and community leaders about key measures to prevent family separations in humanitarian context; informing children and caregivers about risks related to explosive remnants of war; sensitizing caregivers (women, men) on positive parenting;). Project will also build the capacity of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms
These interventions will include direct support provided to child victims or witnesses and/or referrals to specialized services as needed

1 - Besoins / Needs

Specifically, in the Northwest and Southwest regions (NWSW) where a violent crisis broke out in 2016, the conflict has produced some 532,290 IDPs (August 2022 statistics) as well as 417,516 returnees while exacerbated the protection environment. As a result, the protection situation is marked by systematic violence inflicted on civilians by the parts in conflict (State forces and non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and increased gender-based violence, attacks on property, illegal or arbitrary arrests, recruitment of children into armed forces and groups, attacks on life, physical integrity are among the most recurrent violations reported in the regions. Children remain predictably vulnerable to violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect. Many have experienced fear and have been affected by armed conflict firsthand, many have lost or have been separated from their parents and primary caregivers and some have experienced gender-based violence triggered by the conflict. The vast majority has been deprived of a supportive and effective community protection from violence and exploitation. Forced displacement has also resulted to the lost and destruction of personal documentation and civil registries. Significant number of IDPs, particularly children and adults do not possess birth certificates and are the risk of being stateless and deprived of their rightsFrom January to August 2022, 3252 protection incidents were reported through the protection monitoring, while in the same period 7594 GBV incidents were recorded (31% Emotional abuse cases, 22% Denial of resources/opportunities; Physical assault 21%; Sexual assault12%; Rape 8%). More than 50% of these cases involve children.
