Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining WASH assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
MC will improve access to basic water services through the identification of critical gaps and the rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure and solid waste management, in particular in areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees (Ar-Raqqa and DEZ), and areas we anticipate significant inward displacement (Dana, Daret Azza and Atareb in Idleb and north Aleppo). Identification of gaps and needs will be carried out through ongoing coordination with WASH cluster and working group, local councils and other agencies. A special focus will be given to water quality assurance and treatment before the distribution. MC will actively seek solutions for operation and maintenance (O&M), through support to local committees and water authorities directly after the completion of water supply rehabilitation. Water tanks and toilets will complement more comprehensive HH shelter activities, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for displaced and for those who have chosen to return. Where no other option exists, MC will provide access to safe drinking water through trucking for a period up to 3 months until alternative low-cost and sustainable solutions are implemented. Water interventions will be complemented by solid waste management services. Additionally, MC will support sanitation through repairs to community-level sewage networks and desludging of septic tanks in camps and out of camps. Hygiene promotion, using carefully tailored messages, and the distribution of hygiene kits will complement WASH infrastructure rehabilitation, water trucking, and solid waste management interventions. MC will also distribute multi-purpose cash assistance to conflict-affected households based on regional SMEB values, which include hygiene items.

4 - Needs

MC's RNA from October 2019 highlighted that 45% of the IDPs in collective centers in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa governorates do not have access to water, and 49% of communities with water supplies reported that it was insufficient. An unregulated private sector has filled the gap left by the absence of a centralised water system but is inadequate, unaffordable and often low quality. 34% of assessed HHs reported paying money for water from private sources while another 43% depend on water trucking from NGOs, placing immense financial burden on families. Availability of sanitation is another major concern, with 55% of the respondents from the KAP survey reporting they do not have access to adequate sanitation facilities and 74% reporting they do not have access to latrines and go to open areas for defecation. 84% of respondents expressed safety concerns for women and children to access latrines, HHs further reported shortages of hygiene items.MC assessments revealed severe damage to pumps, generators and piped infrastructure impacting water quality, quantity, and availability. With public water systems operating at less than their pre-conflict capacity and unable to ensure the same water quality. High needs of life-saving supplies and services were reported in Aleppo governorate and Idleb governorates. In both formal and informal camps there is a huge need to increase the water supply and manage drinking water safety to ensure the displaced people stay away from potential diarrheal diseases. Similarly, there is an increasing need for desludging, as the septic tanks and pit latrines are flooding causing contamination to nearby water sources.

5 - Implementation of activities

1 Repair and rehabilitation of water systems: MC will identify critical service gaps and needs for rehab of water stations and networks. Technical assessments in consultation with the water directorates and local councils will inform BoQs design. Staff will also facilitate FGDs with men, women, boys, and girls to identify protection risks faced by each in accessing WASH sites, which will further inform the design. 2 Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems: MC will provide capacity development and O&M support for municipal water service team through provision of monthly maintenance costs and necessary materials and equipment, including generators, pumps, fuel tanks, diesel, disinfectants, and spare parts for periodic maintenance. 3 Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems: MC will provide garbage removal services and provide communal bins in informal settlements, camps and urban settings. 4 Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion: MC will conduct hygiene promotion activities to raise awareness of safe hygiene behavior and practices and distribute hygiene kits. MC will conduct a Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey to identify KAP gaps in hygiene, water and sanitation.5 Improved access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services: Where no other option exists, MC will support emergency water trucking services. The duration of water services in emergencies will be for a maximum of three months based on agreed Syria context minimum standards. Full water quality analysis will be conducted with the water supplier in advance. Trucked water will be chlorinated and FRC levels will be checked throughout the entire supply chain.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the Mercy Corps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection risk analysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a corner stone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff and partners will be trained on key principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all.1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. 2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming. 3. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Similarly, where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavour to engage with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. 4. Persons without documents refused assistance: As part of a protection mainstreamed intervention, Mercy Corps does not require beneficiaries of protection or non-protection projects to possess national ID documents.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and cluster analysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2019 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria.The humanitarian situation in NWS deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. According to the HAT’s Dec 2018 Report, “The DMZ Agreement”, there is likely to be a partial or complete breakdown in the political process if key provisions are not fully implemented, leading to a surge in IDP movement. Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signalling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions of operational viability across northern Syria in 2019. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
