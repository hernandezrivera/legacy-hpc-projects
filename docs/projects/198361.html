Project Summary

In the First Phase Response the main objective will be to provide immediate access to education for newly displaced children 6-18 to rapidly restore a sense of safety, calming, and provide lifesaving knowledge during emergencies. This intervention will last no longer than 3-6 months with the aim of reducing the out of school period for affected children and the related protection risks. FPR will involve parents, caregivers and community volunteers in PFA and PSS models along with structured recreation in order to create a foundation for future learning, whilst learning outcomes will be limited to basic literacy and numeracy during this phase. A FPR and preparedness SoP will be developed in close coordination with the Emergency Response team. NRC education team will a focus on transition out of FPR in to longer term learning pathways including formal and non-formal education and improved coordination with other EIE actors, (via the Education Cluster and MoE) for the identification newly displaced children of emerging needs. Preperdenss steps will include DRR and TiCC training along with propositioning of teaching and learning materials such as locally contextualised School-in-Box and hygiene kits. Coordination with the NRC Shelter team will ensure appropriate use of Temporary Learning Spaces for the FPR.
NRC will establish two Non Formal Education distinct programmes for IDPs/ host community children and youth 10-25 years who are out of school. The main goal of this response area is to assist children and youth in re-entering formal education or embarking on a TVET pathway (See Youth Programming below). First: at the national level NRC intends to pilot an Accelerated Education Programme (AEP). This flexible, age-appropriate programme will run in an accelerated timeframe for overage, out of school children who had their education significantly interrupted. NRC will work in partnership with the MoE to develop and provide a primary level re-enrolment pathway (under national DRR policy), utilising a condensed primary curriculum (curriculum prioritization) along with the external exam mechanism. Through this, long term drop outs will be supported to obtain accredited learning within a condensed timeframe. NRC will collaborate with the MoE and Teacher Training Instititue to train teachers on the condensed curriculum and accelerated teaching methodologies and where appropriate utilise EdTech for follow up training of teachers and facilitators. Second: at the Provincial level, NRC will continue to work with other EiE actors to harmonise a non-accredited provincial level Catch Up Programme for short term out drop-outs. This will be specifically designed for children in Cabo Delgado and will utilise the 20% flexibility. It will incorporate outreach elements to families who have struggled to prioritise education during displacement and financial hardship.
Supporting Transition and Retention in Formal Education will target two groups: Newly enrolled (within the previous 2 academic years) IDP and returnee boys and girls 6-18 and IDP/returnee/ host community boys and girls 6-18 at increased risk of drop out due to the emergency (i.e., children with additional needs such as separated, unaccompanied, children with disabilities etc). Support will build on NRC’s existing strong relationship with provincial level education authorities and primarily employ the SIP model in which PTAs are supported technically, financially and materially to design and implement their own SIPs. Interventions will focus on transition and retention weak spots for affected children; such as transition between different education systems (FPR to Formal or NFE to Formal) and at between grades.
Youth Programming will target female and male IDP, host community, returnee and other vulnerable youth aged 15-25. The TVET start-up response will employ a pathways model, following a placement process, youth will be supported in their chosen carer, business or education pathway.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

NRC will conduct assessment of barriers and needs to access education including protection analysis together with communities and authorities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The NRC makes a comprehensive and specific assessment of the needs of the target group. Regarding the response, NRC encourages and advocates hiring female facilitators in order to balance the almost non-existent number of female teachers in the schools where are working.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The assessment is inclusive and involves questions regarding disability. NRC participates in the Disability Working Group to ensure that the implementation strategy and response is aligned with this specific need and ensures that the most vulnerable are included in the response.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

NRC advocates for the preferential use of local and sustainable resources in all activities.
