Project Summary

AVSI's 2020 interventions in the area of Ikwoto, Torit and Magwi, for Eastern Equatoria State (EES) and Cueibet and Rumbek, for Lakes State (LS) will utilize an integrated approach to address the food security needs of the population. The overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce food insecurity of the selected areas in a sustainable way, focusing on the implementation of activities that will provide benefits to the population both in the short and long term and that will bear results also after their completion.
AVSI’s interventions aim to strengthen the productive bases of agricultural communities, specifically targeting farms and production groups to enhance overall production. At the same time, it targets the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more-developed groups and production systems. Support is given through Cash Based Intervention and activities focusing on home gardens to ensure household food security. Single mothers and returnees are the specific target for vulnerable households. This model also uses the ‘model-farmers’ approach, where successful local farmers set the example for other community members.
Activities will target both the local institutions (with a strong component of capacity building and awareness) and the local communities (with direct support, capacity building and access to improved agricultural inputs and services). At the institutional level (county and local authorities), the interventions will focus on creating and strengthening monitoring systems in the fields of livestock (disease early detection and outbreak prevention) and agriculture. This will be achieved through training Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) in livestock health (including vaccinations and treatment campaigns), continue supporting crop monitoring system and reinforcing skills and competencies of county extension officers. Finally, natural resource management is established through the formation and empowerment of NRM committee that will directly lead dialogues at Boma and Payam levels.
At the community level there will be two main groups of beneficiaries: IDPs/returnees and ‘host’ communities. For the former, activities will focus on their integration/interaction through agriculture (cash for work in local communities farms and feeder road rehabilitation) and in their enhanced access to quality and improved agricultural inputs (seeds, tool, drip irrigation kits) and general services (cash for NFI, housing and school fees). For the second group, a wide range of activities will be implemented including capacity building in agriculture using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach.
For both, the diversification of livelihoods activities will be supported (eg. fish farming, bee-keeping, vegetable production, agro-forestry etc), also through the distribution of improved agricultural inputs. Crosscutting interventions will be implemented in the field of water, hygiene, sanitation and health in order to create a healthy environment from which all the other interventions will benefit. Finally, in some areas of intervention, support will be provided in the form of advanced services including training from agronomy experts and trials for seed improvement.

1 - Needs

The food security and nutrition conditions of Ikwoto, Torit and Magwi, in the former Eastern Equatoria state (EES) and in Cueibet and Rumbek Centre, in former Lakes state (LS), continue to be worrisome and, after an improvement in conjunction with the peak of the harvesting season, they are expected to continue to be so in 2020. According to IPC analysis of August 2019, in EES an estimated 560,000 people (52.6% of the state population) were facing a food security phase of Crisis or Emergency (Phases 3 and 4). More specifically, 35% in Torit, 30% in Ikotos and Magwi were in a status of Crisis. According to projections, food security should improve within EES during the typical harvest season, Sep-Dec 2019, but is believed to be a short lived relief for some of the population as figures are likely to return to around 50% again during the post-harvest season of Jan-Apr 2020. Very similar prospects are also shown for LS where in August 2019, an estimated 660,000 people (57.9% of the state population) were facing a food security phase of 3-5. 60% in Cueibet and 55% in Rumbek Centre, were in a status of Crisis and Emergency. As EES, food security should in the short-term improve in LS during the harvest season. For both EES and LS, high market prices due to increased demand as households completely depleted their own food stock was one of the general drivers of food insecurity in August 2019. Climatic shocks such as drought and increased instances of inter-communal violence were also among the more prominent drivers of food insecurity in LS.Additionally, the delayed onset of rain resulted in delayed planting and harvests that extended lean season as well as late development of pasture and surface water for livestock while floods and fall army worm also attributed to poor crops performance. Currently, with the harvest season, staple food availability has largely improved but market dependent households continue to suffer as prices remain high due to deteriorating road conditions and even greater depreciation of local currency. According to AVSI’s June 2019 SMART Survey conducted in Ikwoto, a significant number of households were found to be at risk of food insecurity where only 38.6% of the surveyed households had acceptable food consumption, and 61.1% of sampled households had low diet diversity. 95.8% had a low reduced coping strategy index. The SMART survey result also revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate for the former County of Ikwoto was 9.2% (6.6 – 12.8 95% C.I.), and the severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate was 2.0% (1.0 – 4.0 95% C.I.). GAM indicates a poor nutrition situation while SAM is classified as critical according to WHO classification. Results also showed a significant difference between boys and girls in the prevalence of acute malnutrition estimated by Weight for Height Z-score (p-value = 0.0291). Boys were more malnourished compared to girls with GAM rate at 12.5% for boys and 5.9% for girls. This suggests a higher vulnerability to acute malnutrition by boys compared to girls. 26.9% (22.4-31.9 95% C.I.) of children were found to be underweight while 4.3% (2.7-6.6 95% C.I.) were severely underweight. The rate of chronic malnutrition in Ikwoto is also worrying with a level of stunting considered "serious" at 31.9%. In fact in EES, Ikwoto County in particular, chronic malnutrition is the highest compared to the entire country, highlighting the need to prioritize stunting prevention interventions. In LS, Food consumption score (FCS) according to the FSNMS round 24 data, was poor for 50% in Cueibet and 36% in Cueibet where 57% and 36% of the respective populations exhibited a reduced coping strategy index of less than 19. GAM prevalence has also deteriorated due to the limited access to and availability of food with seasonal increase in morbidity – such as malaria, and lack of adequate access to health services.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

AVSI’s effort in the field of protection and food security are oriented to ensure the respect of human rights to life and education. The combination of such response aims at fostering the conditions for people to know their rights and to have the means to make them.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

One objective of AVSI’s project in EES intends to reinforce the resilience by improving the food security and education of returnees from Uganda. One aspect of the intervention is based on the premise that the economic and livelihood opportunities are improved, strengthening the productive relationship between refugees and host communities with support for access to the market and the land. Moreover, given the relative stability of the South Sudanese regions bordering Uganda, the intervention can contribute to the voluntary return of skilled youth. AVSI has recently conducted two surveys, one with refugees in Uganda and another in South Sudan, to assess the needs of displacement-affected people, including their perspective on repatriation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

AVSI, given its steady presence in EES since 2005, has had the opportunity to establish over the years a deep relationship with local authorities, both at the county and at the state level. AVSI foresees the presence of staff that support local authorities in defining needs, implementing intervention strategies and putting into action planned activities. Ikwoto county itself, and in particular the agriculture departments, acts as a local partner in AVSI’s interventions, supporting throughout the project cycle. The departments, in turn, will benefit from the intervention thanks to the organizational support, the strengthening of their structure and the training of personnel on the financial management and monitoring of food safety services. In Cueibet, AVSI’s interventions will also closely involve relevant local government and state ministry staff, for example through inclusion in trainings, support to visit project sites, and information sharing throughout inception and implementation. This will contribute to increased government and better involvement and ownership of government roles in project activities – laying the groundwork for an eventual greater role by the GoSS in resilience building when political circumstances permit.AVSI’s interventions have the purpose of strengthening the local governance structure, making the project more sustainable and durable over time, both by nurturing and strengthening the capacity for management, organization and monitoring of the situation of food insecurity and malnutrition of the direct beneficiaries and more generically than the population of the counties, both by ensuring that this (a situation of food insecurity and malnutrition) may be present in the future and be reflected in local, state and national policies.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

AVSI’s interventions are in line with the 2018-2020 Country Program Framework, which envisages, among other pillars, i) Supporting and establishing conditions suitable for the development of sustainable agriculture, ii) Increasing sustainable production and productivity to improve food security, iv) Improving market opportunities in rural areas. To Increase resilience in the food security sector implies to foster a gradual transition from emergency to development approaches. For the purposes and activities envisaged, this proposal integrates the objectives and recommendations of the HRP 2020 (Humanitarian Response Plan). The active participation of AVSI in the Food Security Cluster, both at the national and state level, guarantees the alignment of its response with the priorities indicated by the humanitarian system and international cooperation agencies, in line with the country's political developments. Activities are also aligned with the priorities mentioned in the main national planning documents, such as the National Development Strategy 2018–2021, the National Gender Policy 2013 and in particular the South Sudan Comprehensive Agriculture Master Plan 2015–2040.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The conditions of protection are far from stable, let alone safe. Especially in Lake state, abuses are common and need of mental support are high. In Ikwoto county, AVSI survey found that around 15% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physically abuses and harsh punishments, 8% was discriminated against, and 7% sexually abused. In Torit county, around 11% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physical abuse (the double of what was found in Ikwoto County, EES), 2% were sexually abused, and 7% do drugs or engage in other deviant practices. Bur seems to be the place where children are more subject to abuse. In Cueibet county, around 37% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physically abuses (the double of what was found in Ikwoto County, EES), 7% was sexually abused, and 12% does drugs or engages in other deviant practices. Moreover, mental distress is also quite much more common than in EES, with almost half of the population seeking the repeated intervention of some healer (whose qualifications are difficult to assess but presumably inadequate). 40% of Cueibet’s families, moreover, report that women are face specif risks, physical assault, rape and forced marriage being among the most common. In the face of such threats, leveraging on the expertise gained in relation to its education projects, AVSI promotes the development of capacity in the field of protection for all its project officers, actively seeks the inclusion of women in all its interventions, including with local civil associations of women, and implements projects that integrate education components, as a means to provide protection to young children, and FSL projects, to ensure the sustainability of such support.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

AVSI aims to strengthen the link between humanitarian assistance, development and peacebuilding and consequently develop project activities in such a way that they are as durable and sustainable at the financial, institutional, socio-cultural and environmental level. The irrigation/water-related interventions will give the target populations the possibility of using a constant source of water supply, which will allow farming to continue sustained even after the end of the intervention. This will then be further maximized with the formalization of CWMC, which will make the water supply usable even after the end of the project. Agricultural production will not only contribute to improving people’s nutrition contributions in the present, but will also contribute to the improve people ability to undertake work in the future, thus fostering a steady improved nutritional supply during all seasons. In addition, the training of the model farmers will contribute to a constant transfer of knowledge and monitoring activities even after the end of the intervention. AVSI also aims to have an overall positive impact on the environment, the promotion of community NRM and environmental care being a paradigmatic example. Communication and uptake of the linkage between environmental care and the availability of environmental services/increased production and income, will create a self-reinforcing cycle in communities.The project’s efforts to build resilient attitudes, while working with the grain of local beliefs, traditions and knowledge, goes into the direction of supporting both local ownership and communities’ self-directed continuation of activities. In addition to addressing attitudes, project staff will give concrete support to the establishment of plans, processes, responsibilities, and enforcement mechanisms that give these attitudes structure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Firstly, as is often reported from communities, humanitarian assistance is at times channeled to more organized tribes, leaving other tribes (even more vulnerable populations) less assisted. To minimize this, AVSI always attempts to cover large as well as small communities in both central and more peripheral locations. For the second risk, rather than lack of awareness of referral services, there is more a problem of lack of access to psychological support to traumatized children and victims of GBV. AVSI focuses on prevention and mitigation by providing safe spaces to children, promoting female education and gender inclusion in general. Lastly, the delivery of humanitarian assistance may always spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources. To mitigate this risk, AVSI is always transparent in its selection criteria and strives to implement activities that have sustainable and long-lasting benefits for the entire community.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 5 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl. The most recent example of one of AVSI’s feedback mechanism happened in the Lake States in August where AVSI staff validated the finding of two researches on challenges faced by Persons with Disabilities (PwD) and on Gender Based Violence. The 3-day validation meeting was attended by key stakeholders including PwD. AVSI ensured that at least half of the 50 attendees were females in positions of authority at the institutional or community level.
