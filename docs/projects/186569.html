Project Summary

Overall Objective:
Strengthening access to safe, quality, equitable and inclusive basic education for 774,501 conflict-affected girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years and 20,000 youths in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States of Northeast Nigeria.
Specific Objectives:
1. Strengthen access to basic education for 774,501 children (ensuring 54% girls, and 30,000 children from Tsangaya and Islamiyah schools ) through the provision of essential learning materials, dignity kits, support to enrolment drive campaigns (EDC) and Girls for Girls (G4G) programme.
2. Provide access to remote learning opportunities to 67,500 conflict-affected children (50% girls).
3. Provide continuous professional development opportunities to 35,000 teachers.
4. Construction of TLS/Classrooms and gender-sensitive WASH facilities to increase access to safe learning spaces for at least 16,400 conflict-affected children.
5. Increase access to learning, training and skills development for 20,000 youth (50% females; aged 15 - 24)
6. Enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies through establishing or re-activating and training 400 School-based Management Committees (SBMC) in targeted LGAs on effective school management
7. Strengthening the Education Management Information System (EMIS)/Annual School Census (ASC) in providing timely & reliable data.
8. Support the scale-up of EduTrac which is used as a survey tool to collect data from schools using SMS messages and as a governance monitoring tool and improve accountability.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 8.7 million people (most of them women and children) in Nigeria need humanitarian assistance following the 12-year insurgency. There are 1.9 million people internally displaced out of which 1.7 million of them require humanitarian aid for their survival with the majority based in the northeast states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. About 58 per cent of those affected are children with many unaccompanied minors or orphaned by the crisis. People living with disabilities make up 16 per cent of the affected population. At least 1.3 million people (97 per cent children and 2 per cent women) need urgent educational services. The education system in North East is overstretched because of the prolonged multiple crises and population displacements. An estimated 19,000 teachers are displaced, and 645 teachers have been killed since 2009. Approximately 1,200 schools have been damaged or destroyed or are used for military purposes. In the Northeast, education facilities are often explicitly targeted by non-state armed groups in Nigeria. Since 2009 schools and the formal education system have been deliberately targeted with some schools suffering multiple attacks. Borno is the most affected with school closures of up to 60 per cent compared to 14 per cent in Yobe. An estimated 52 per cent of school-aged children have never attended school and the annual dropout rate of children in the northeast is the highest in Nigeria. There are large numbers of out of school children and youth with limited or a lack of access to formal education as a result of being either over-aged or missing too many years of education and the government-owned alternative education opportunities are inadequate to cater for these teaming youths and out of school children. The formal education sector is also struggling to cope with the responsibility of educating IDP and returnee students in addition to students in host communities. Overcrowded classrooms, poorly maintained infrastructure, inadequate teachers with many unqualified and poorly renumerated, inadequate or a lack of WASH facilities including potable drinking water are major barriers to access to education. The COVID-19 pandemic has placed additional pressures and impact on already limited opportunities and services particularly livelihoods and education among others; deepening the vulnerabilities of populations across the northeastern states especially for girls who face cultural barriers to education such as early marriage and an increase in the volume of domestic chores among others. There is a need to provide alternative learning opportunities for children and youth not currently accessing education, support construction/rehabilitation/renovations and infrastructure in schools and equip temporary learning spaces, provide furniture and WASH facilities, train teachers, provide teaching and learning materials as well as student kits and school uniforms.

2 - Activities

In 2022, UNICEF will focus on strengthening access to safe, quality, equitable and inclusive basic education for 774,501 conflict-affected girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years (54% girls) and 20,000 youths in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States of Northeast Nigeria. UNICEF will embark on the following specific activities: ACCESS: 1. Construct/rehabilitate 328 temporary learning spaces and classrooms 2. Construct and rehabilitate 78 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces with connecting water sources. 3. Provide 67,500 conflict-affected children with remote learning. 4 Provide basic learning material (stationery and school bags) to 774,501 school-age children. 5. Provide dignity kits to 30,000 girls. 6. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 20,000 youth (aged 15 - 24).QUALITY: 1. Support 35,000 teachers with continuous professional development and in acquiring the qualification for teaching primary grades. 2. Train 15,382 teachers on psychosocial support (PSS). 3. Provide life skills to 100,000 conflict-affected children (52% girls).Response Capacity of Education Service Providers: 1. Train 2,000 School-Based Management Committee(SBMC) members on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with a focus on hard-to-reach areas. 2. Support the scaling up of EduTrac and strengthening of the Education Information Management System (EMIS) to provide timely and reliable data.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

UNICEF considers the protection of all persons affected and at-risk when planning humanitarian response and protection is central in UNICEF’s preparedness and life-saving activities throughout the project. The population at risk would be identified considering the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual and other minorities. Primary consideration will be given to UNICEF’s accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their protection measures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed action is defined by daily interaction and collaboration with affected communities through our third-party facilitators in targeted LGAs and IDP camps, including support to community-based structures such as the SBMC’s and their participation in public awareness-raising, referral processes and collaboration in identifying and implementing initiatives which contribute to improving the protective environments. Furthermore, UNICEF ensures independent consultations with specific groups, including women, girls and children, to ensure their voices are heard and ideas are incorporated into all activities. As part of the emergency response activities, beneficiaries will be consulted through regular monitoring visits and post-distribution monitoring exercises, during which individuals will be consulted on matters including the quality and relevance of assistance received. UNICEF short text messages (SMS) based U-Report tool will be used to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback or complaints. The U-Report 24x7 Hub receives complaints, reports and feedback from communities, analyses the data and shares with relevant sections in UNICEF and its partners for action. As reports and complaints regarding other services handled by different agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to take action. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
