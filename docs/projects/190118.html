Project Summary

Due to the conflict started in November 2020, currently, about 6.2 million people are in need of emergency relief assistance, and at least, 1.5 million people remain internally displaced, according to findings of a Multi-Agency Seasonal Assessment of Tigray of 16 November – 07 December 2021.
As the conflict has uprooted and deprived millions of people across Tigray off their household assets/items, 4.2 million people in Tigray need Emergency shelter/NFIs assistance (OCHA’s recent report, 3 Feb 2022).
Most IDPs in Tigray reside in overcrowded and sub-standard shelters, communal structures, with relatives, as well as open spaces without blankets. As such, they are highly exposed to further vulnerabilities women and girls do not feel safe. Despie some progress, the shelter response remains concerningly low compared to the needs, across the region.
This project thus, aims to provide 63152 IDPs and returnees with prioritized emergency shelter and NFI needs in Adwa town, Aheferom, and Chila in Central zone, and Erob woreeda in Eastern zone of Tigray

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Alight will pursue a people-centered and rights-based approach that will promote empowerment and protection of sexual and gender minorities, women, and girls. Additionally, regular oversight will be provided, and monitoring visits conducted to observe service delivery. Feedback collection mechanisms will be instituted, and data will be collected and segregated by gender and age. In addition to the regular cluster/donor/government requirements, Alight has its own information and feedback tool known as kuja-kuja, which collects beneficiaries' feedback on monthly basis, and disseminates findings for necessary adjustments of programs/project. Accountability to Affected Persons ALIGHT with actively engage affected populations in all levels of the project cycle, including in the rapid needs assessments, in the design and planning, implementation as well as in monitoring and evaluation. In so doing, Community consultations will be conducted before starting intervention to discuss beneficiary selection, the distribution process, location, timing, and duties and rights, etc. Alight will establish information sharing mechanisms to enhance participation and ensure accountability to beneficiaries; and maintain a strong relationship with all stakeholders in Tigray region. Regular meetings with the IDP/returnee committees and local stakeholders will be held to identify the target people’s preferences/needs, concerns and feedback about the project. Some of the affected population are likely to have suffered gender-based violence (GBV) in the past and remain at risk of GBV. Using the different complaints and feedback mechanisms, beneficiaries can report such cases in person, to Alight staff or anonymously by calling the CBFM Hotline, free of charge.
