Project Summary

Communities most impacted by escalating conflict, displacement and disrupted health service access are at significant risk to life threatening diseases. Based on thorough coordination with NMCP/MISAU/Clusters/partners, MENTOR propose to conduct indoor residual spraying to protect 500,000 people and technical training for 230 APEs.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IRS will be conducted across targeted urban centres of Cabo Delgado to cover 500,000 beneficiaries. MENTOR will, in close collaboration and partnership with the NMCP/MISAU, identify, train, equip, and supervise spray teams to conduct a comprehensive IRS campaign that will provide life-saving protection from malaria and other VBDs. MENTOR will support IRS supervisors to have oversight over the spray teams and to spot check quality of spray technique and coverage of assigned house/shelter areas. This will rapidly achieve significant coverage and provide immediate protection to beneficiaries. IRS has the added advantage of also controlling fleas, mites and other nuisance insects and disease vectors that are resulting in skin disease, and reduced quality of life and dignity. IRS teams will also deliver health messages and IEC to raise awareness of malaria, diarrhoeal disease, other VBDs and Covid-19, and to promote health service seeking behaviour. Technical training will also be delivered to the 130 currently active APEs in the target districts, as well as 100 APEs to be recruited by DPS. The training will focus on differential diagnosis, case management, surveillance and reporting for malaria and vector born disease. Other areas such as IMCI, cholera and Covid-19 will also be incorporated in coordination with DPS.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

IRS Campaign + door-to-door health messaging: IRS will be performed before the rainy season so as to provide enough protection throughout the malaria and other VBD transmission period. The geographical areas to be selected will be based on the following:• High endemicity of malaria and other VBDs• Recent increase in malaria and other VBD cases• High concentration or recent influx of internally displaced people (IDPs)• Areas experiencing increased pressure on health services due to high disease burden or high influx of population• Areas accessible and deemed secure enough for IRS campaign Technical training for APEs: This will be for all MoH-supported APEs of Cabo Delgado with training taking place in Pemba. DPS have explained to MENTOR that APEs are now carrying the burden of diagnosing and treating patients in areas where health facilities have closed due to insecurity. APEs are having to fulfil the majority of primary care delivery, beyond their usual scope of responsibility and lack the technical knowledge to cope with the increased demand. Technical training will include differential diagnosis and treatment of severe cases to increase skill sets of APEs and provide them with the knowledge to deal with a wider range of diseases as demand increases, particularly for hard-to-reach communities. Whilst the aim is to provide training for APEs across Cabo Delgado province, priority will be given to APEs working in areas of increased insecurity to receive technical training first.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

All staff and day-workers recruited by MENTOR will be subject to MENTOR’s Code of Conduct and safeguarding management systems. MENTOR will ensure that all such staff selected provide the programme services to all targeted communities and to all members of those targeted communities, regardless of gender, age and religious belief, and are inclusive of people with additional or special needs, such as disabled or elderly people. Any breach of this will result in MENTOR removing the member of staff, and they will be reported to the local authorities. MENTOR’s training for all household level prevention interventions includes aspects of beneficiary respect and sensitivity, including children, who are particularly susceptible to the psychosocial impacts of crisis and displacement. The programme team will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organisational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that the affected populations can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organisation and its affected populations over information provision. This will help to ensure that the targeted populations are fully informed of what standards are expected and therefore can identify where there are any issues or failures. This information will be corroborated by communication activities including regular community liaison meetings, ensuring visibility in the catchment areas, and therefore, accountability.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

As a core requisite of the programme, MENTOR will actively seek input from the programme beneficiaries and other humanitarian partners. This will have multiple purposes: to improve policy and practice in programming design, to adapt where necessary if there are issues in implementation or where there is an unintended outcome, to address any issues or challenges that may arise, to empower local populations to consider and resolve local challenge, to encourage ownership and involvement in the programme (especially in terms of take-up and support of the services being provided), and to maximise sustainable outcomes beyond the lifespan of the recovery phase response, where possible. The programme will enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes regarding the delivery of emergency disease prevention activities and to ensure that the most marginalised and affected are represented and have influence. There will be regular consultations with community workers, village leaders, and local community groups from the early planning stages of IRS/IEC campaigns and as these will be staffed by community-based day workers, this strategy will ensure a participatory and community-based approach, engaging people of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, and will ensure their representation and influence in these programme consultations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The very core of the community worker approach is its proximity to the target population. MENTOR’s emergency community-based prevention provides a unique access to households regardless of gender, age and religious belief, and is inclusive people with additional or special needs, such as disabled or elderly people. All disease prevention and promotion activities will be delivered at household level across targeted communities, and prevention workers will move door-to-door for IRS/IEC campaigns, in every targeted community. They will be trained to be especially sensitive to those who cannot leave their home due to disability or other issues, and to respond accordingly. They will be trained to refer any sick people found in the homes that they visit, to the nearest supported health facility for care. Where needed, community prevention teams will take additional steps to ensure that less mobile beneficiaries are able to receive the same access to disease prevention tools and IEC. Handling and communication with beneficiaries that have special needs, including the elderly suffering from mental impairment or other disabilities, and this will be incorporated into MENTOR staff training of all prevention workers involved in the delivery of this programme.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

MENTOR will identify areas for IRS based on the environmental risk factors (prone to flooding, poor sanitation infrastructure) and vulnerability of population (overcrowding and high concentration of IDPs). This will be done in close coordination with NMCP, DPS and other health authorities through analysis of historical and present malaria data. As specialists in IRS campaigns, MENTOR aim for the highest standards when implementing activities including ensuring that the insecticide is WHOPES approved. A significant part of the training for IRS daily workers is the protection of themselves, by wearing PPE, and the protection of the environment when handling insecticide. Identifying areas to clean spray pumps and ensuring daily workers follow protocol closely is an imperative part of IRS campaigns by MENTOR. This includes ensuring no insecticide is run off into water sources such as rivers.
