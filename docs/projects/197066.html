Project Summary

RI will aim to reduce nutrition related mortality and morbidity among children under five years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate (DEZ) of Northeastern Syria (NES). This project contributes to the prevention and treatment of acute and chronic malnutrition in DEZ, with particular attention to children aged 0- 23 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) through the implementation of quality community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling programs as well as supplementation of micronutrients. The project aims include:
1. To improve access to and utilization of quality CMAM program for children under five and PLW with acute malnutrition;
2. To improve IYCF and nutrition knowledge and practices among PLW and care takers of children under two;
3. To ensure PLW and children under two are protected from or treated for micronutrient deficiencies;
4. To improve coordination, information sharing and nutrition resource mobilization among nutrition stakeholders in NES
In 2023, RI plans to integrate the above nutrition interventions in 11 Health facilities in DEZ. The facilities targeted include - Jdidat Bakara hospital, Shihel hospital (in collaboration with AFH), Kisreh CPHC, Hejneh PHC, Mhemideh PHC, Basira CPHC, Kobar PHC, Hawayej PHC, Sabha PHC and Kishkiye & Sha’afa PHCs (in collaboration with IPV).

3 - Needs

Malnutrition is a significant public health challenge across NES, as its prevalence has been rising since 2020, and an estimated 5.5 million children under 5 (CU5) and PLW are in need of nutrition support. One in four Syrian children are stunted and at serious risk of irreversible damage to their physical and cognitive development, repeated infections, developmental delays, disabilities and death (HNO 2022). PLW are also facing increases in micronutrient deficiency, with 1 in 3 women affected by iron-deficiency anemia. In May 2022, RI implemented a SMART Nutrition Survey across 4 governorates in NES to inform evidence-based response in the region, in close coordination with WoS nutrition cluster, the NES NWG, and nutrition partners in NES. The survey found that Kisreh sub-district in DEZ has the highest acute malnutrition rate among CU5 (12%) and PLW (20.5%), as well as the highest GAM rate (12%) which is close to the WHO 15% emergency threshold. Based on OCHA’s 2022 HNO, an estimated 3,115 children suffer from acute malnutrition of which an estimated 830 have SAM, and an estimated 1,492 PLW have acute malnutrition. Based on regular reports produced by the RI CHW team on nutrition screenings, Basira sub-district appears to face a similar situation as Kisreh. Without intervention, an increase in morbidity and mortality will occur as a result of the high rate of acute malnutrition coupled with high rate of communicable diseases and lack of adequate access to health services.

4 - Implementation of activities

Direct determinants of malnutrition addressed by treatment of acute malnutrition (AM) & micronutrient deficiencies (PLWs & CU5). IYCF counselling and nutrition education messages for PLW and caretakers of children <2 will address underlying determinants of malnutrition (child-feeding and care practices). Integrated approach with RI’s existing health and protection activities: nutrition activities to be implemented as part of reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health services, CU5 & PLW screened for malnutrition & provided with micronutrient supplements. Health, nutrition, hygiene messages for women and men by 1-1 counselling/group health promotion. At women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), women will be screened for AM, IYCF and other health & nutrition key messages will be provided, thereby facilitating access for PLW to peer groups, and they will benefit from support services (day care, free transportation, joint awareness-raising at the community level).RI will monitor & provide technical support to nutrition activities at supported HFs & community through regular supportive supervision by international health coordinator & nutrition officer. Standard CMAM and IYCF supervision checklists will monitor quality of activities. Nutrition data collected from outpatient treatment center used to calculate nutrition program performance indicators to evaluate quality of the program. Monthly nutrition surveillance, treatment of AM, IYCF counselling reports to donor and NWG.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Lack of quality services and referrals exposes survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, community and family ostracization and further trauma, in addition to the possibility of putting survivors at risk if confidentiality is not secured due to service providers’ lack of capacity on information management. Likelihood and impact are high. However, RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1. Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only; 2. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Due to the high vulnerability and need levels of the target population and the limited availability of humanitarian assistance, there is a risk of SEA cases, which is expected to increase with the worsening of socio-economic situation in the area. RI has a robust Safeguarding framework, which is strongly connected to its feedback and complaint response mechanism (FCRM), to ensure multiple channels through which the population can provide information and report incidents. RI has developed a system of field safeguarding focal points, who have the responsibility of strengthening awareness raising at community level about humanitarian organizations’ responsibilities and how to safely report incidents and access needed response services for survivors, providing capacity building activities to staff about PSEA and safeguarding and duty to report, and they are trained in receiving and safely routing incidents through the appropriate channels. RI is also an active member of the NES PSEA Network, to ensure coordinated prevention and response efforts in the area.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RI Syria has a Risk Register that identifies the key risks within the operational environment and provides tools for mitigating risk, in addition to following enhanced due diligence measures. Key risks and mitigation measures for this project are: 1. Temporary local insecurity (civil disorder, security operations, acceptance issues) and larger shifts (conflict threats from state actors or armed groups). RI has security focal points in all operational areas who are trained to provide timely responses to emerging problems and enact contingency plans if necessary.2. Blockade of border crossings due from either side (Syria or neighboring countries through which RI sources items) could jeopardize transportation of medical and nutrition commodities including pharmaceuticals, and supplies leading to shortage of essential nutrition and medical supplies. RI will ensure availability of adequate buffer stock of medical and nutrition supplies within NES to avoid potential stockouts.3. A disease outbreak (including COVID-19) could stretch the health system, negatively impacting the proposed implementation strategy. Where possible, RI will put in place measures to reduce overcrowding. HFs will have triage space where all visitors will be screened for relevant symptoms and suspected cases will be seen separately. Refresher training on infection prevention and control and personal protective equipment will be provided to the health workers of the target HFs and CHWs.
