Project Summary

There is approximately 2.7 million IDPs in Somalia, Conflict, insecurity, and impact of political divisions continue to drive humanitarian needs, displacement and protection concerns in most regions of Somalia. The situation was made worse by the outbreak of COVID 19 pandemic which led to restriction of movements which heavily affected the country’s economy. The situation has been worse for the minority clans, the elderly and those living with disability.
An estimated 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden) face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213 400 likely to be severely malnourished. Urgent nutrition and health support is required to address their needs. Urgent health and nutrition support is also required for areas with high prevalence of acute maternal malnutrition. Coverage of health and nutrition services (both treatment and prevention), including vitamin A supplementation and measles vaccination should be expanded, especially in areas where coverage is currently low. This will therefore increase the morbidity and mortality rate if immediate health support is not provided to the affected population within the targeted areas.
According to the Post Gu 2021 report Morbidity prevalence was high (≥ 20%) among majority (7 out of 15) of rural population groups. Crude Death Rate (CDR) and Under-Five Death Rate (U5DR) were low across most rural livelihoods. Exceptions are: Shabelle and Bay Agro-pastoral which recorded Serious CDR (0.5-1/10 000/day)). Serious CDR in were also observed. Shabelle Riverine also recorded Serious U5DR (1 to1.9/10 000 /day). Measles vaccination and Vitamin A supplementation and household access to clean water and sanitation remain low in many rural livelihoods.
BADEF therefore in this proposed interventions will work towards all gender groups, marginalized groups and the disabled who are often left out or given unequal treatment.
Direct beneficiaries will include 9240 women with: 200 for post rape clinical management and GBV services,2940 for ANC and delivery,2990 with PNC,4500 for outpatient consultations,4010 with RCC. 16890 Children,80 with PRCM and GBV services,7240 with measles and Penta 3 vaccine, BCG vaccine,and treatment of complicated severe acute malnutrition and 3200 with RCC.
Indirect beneficiaries will include 15600 men with outpatient consultations, 1000 male headed households and 30 men for GBV services and 15600 with RCC.
BADEF will closely work with the local administration so as to have a smooth services delivery especially on the outreach services and community outreaches. We will work closely with the other actors on the ground providing heath services to avoid overlaps and to as well have a good network in cases of referrals. BADEF will ensure that there is monthly and quarterly reporting to the cluster as well as participation in the coordination meetings.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

BADEF will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that project stakeholders understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. We will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups. Gender disparities are visible in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. The project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. Gender mainstreaming has been considered in the design of this project at all levels. All targeted beneficiaries will be registered, profiled and data disaggregated by gender and age as for the following categories: 1. Women and pregnant women 2. Children under 5 years 3. Population under 18 years 4. IDPs resident in IDP camps 5. Access of men to transmissible diseases services. During implementation and monitoring of the services a participatory and gender sensitive approach will be adopted to allow equal opportunity to all. The hygiene and health promotion interventions will involve hospital staff, both health workers and support staff, patients, relatives and visitors with specific sessions addressed to mothers of patients in pediatric ward and women in obstetric department. Gender specific group discussions and interaction are organized. Hospital staff recruitment will respect gender equality ensuring opportunities to all genders on the base of the professional qualification.
