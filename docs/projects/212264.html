Project Summary

The project aims to provide early learning opportunities to children affected by conflict in Syria to promote resilience and wellbeing in children ages 0-8. Exposure to conflict and the experience of displacement has been shown to have lasting effects on children’s brain development and well-being. Caregivers who are themselves experiencing trauma may struggle to provide children with responsive care. Elevated stress and disruptions to care during the critical period of early childhood can harm their social, cognitive, and emotional development with far-reaching consequences. Studies have shown that children who begin primary school with a developmental disadvantage, are more likely to struggle academically, engage in risky behavior, or drop out of school entirely (Grantham-McGregor et al., 2007). Supported with providing psychosocial support services, the goal is to support a generation of children affected by crisis and conflict in Syria by introducing children to a world of nurturing, playing, and learning. This will include all children regardless of their gender, religion, legal status, financial status and/or disability. All activities under Ahlan Simsim can be adapted for the different types of disability to ensure the active participation of children with disabilities and their caregivers. IRC will regularly work on enhancing the skills and capacities of service providers and caregivers across the different sectors in terms of Early Childhood Education and Development child safeguarding and protection (including PSEA) to achieve its desired change in the targeted communities and support the continuity of this knowledge in the existing systems of the implementing and non-implementing partners under this project This falls in line with the needs for ECD/ECE that were highlighted in the Education Strategy for Syria, along with Nurturing Care Framework in the humanitarian context that is led by UNICEF and WHO that is focusing on integrating ECD under the different sectors starting with Education. IRC has flexible and highly adaptable implementation modalities to ensure reaching the most vulnerable groups, M&E tools are designed to measure outcomes and outputs and ensure quality reporting not only for reporting but also to create evidence to support advocacy efforts that demand increasing investments and funds for ECD across the region.
