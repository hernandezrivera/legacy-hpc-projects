Project Summary

This project designed in participatory and inclusive manner intends to support 21643(3000 returnees, 5500 IDPs and 13143 residents) drought affected population of Kapoeta East County in Eastern Equatoria State of South Sudan to deter famine. The intervention is humanitarian, Peace and development linked which is thought to support vulnerable 3600 households whose livelihoods are eroded by drought and conflicts in the state. It targets highly affected households who will be identified by intensive selection criteria with the local communities and authorities in the project locations. This project is believed to prevent famine and improve the livelihood and food security of the households to help them improve their mental, physical and social wellbeing. It targets 8557 male (boys and men) and 12086 female (girls and women). This project is feasible as NSDO has the staff capacity required for the project implementation and NSDO will work with all stakeholders in collaborative manner and contribute to the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster's objectives and its standard indicators to measure progress and report timely and share information to the cluster and other stakeholders to achieve the Humanitarian Response Strategic Objectives. Therefore, NSDO is seeking a funding value of 649300 USD to finance the implementation of the project to achieve its intended impact on the 21643 target beneficiaries most in needs out of over 29076 people in needs in Kapoeta East County.

1. Needs

This project will be implemented in 7 payams of Kapoeta East. Kapoeta East is one of the counties in South Sudan that lies in arid region of the country in Eastern Equatoria State. It is the largest hard to reach counties greater Kapoeta bordering Kenya and Ethiopia. It is a home to 165,518 people majority of whom are Toposa and Jie, occupying 7 payams. The population is a mixture of IDPs, Returnees and resident population groups. Kapoeta East experiences below average sparse rainfall throughout a year characterised by normally long dry spells. It has seasonal rivers running throughout the county. The population practice both farming and cattle rearing as their livelihood activities. 78% of the population practice farming and 85% of the population rear cattle. As part of South Sudan, basic Services are rendered in the backdrop of recurrent climate change extremes and disasters, economic meltdown, food insecurity and loss of livelihoods, continuous sub-national and Inter-community conflicts and low infrastructure development. The county is one of the poorest counties in the South Sudan. Despite the implementation of the Revitalised peace agreement, conflict has been observed in the county with neighboring Kenya as a result of the contested area of Elemu triangle which lies in the county and cattle raiding amongst the community. According to the IPC reports, the county has been under emergency state since 2021, an estimated 27,586 households are under IPC4 emergency state in the county with over 21,643 people in dire need of humanitarian and developmental needs. They lack food, safe water, sanitation and health services which was exacerbated by long dry spells, inter-communal cattle raids and border conflict with the Turkana people of Kenya. 13% of the population is in severe hunger crises. Poor harvests and animal diseases have been the cause of the worsening food insecurity in the county impacting quality of life of the population. Most of the section of the population affected are children, women, people with disability and elderly age groups. NSDO Office in Torit in coordination with its Kapoeta field office conducted several meetings with local community structures including women groups and people with disability and NGOs in Kapoeta East upon the acknowledgement of the IRNA Reports from Jie and Kauto payams early this year. NSDO found that there are needs to prevent famine and restore the livelihood of population of Kapoeta East County driven by long spell of drought in the County prevent migration that causes displacement. Though there are number of partners including NGOs like WFP, OPRD, AFSS and AMREF undertaking interventions to prevent famine and build resilience, only an estimated 5,000 out of 29,060 people received food assistance this year in only two payams. This proposed project therefore seeks fund to contribute to emergency response to avert famine in Kapoeta East for 21,643 beneficiaries (3,000 returnees, 5,500 IDPs and 13,143 residents) who are most affected by the drought in the county through provision of food assistance to most in need, emergency food production inputs provision and supporting resilience building to adapt to the climatic condition in the county through agricultural asset creation for irrigation and agroforestry.

2. Project activities

In order to achieve CO1, of providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5, the following activities will be undertaken a) Distribution of food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers to 10,600 beneficiaries b) Distribution of food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers to 1,600 returnees under Phase one Returnees ResponseTo achieve CO 2, of enhancing and sustaining emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock, NSDO will undertake the following activitiesa) Procurement and distribution of agricultural inputs/vegetable & fishing kits to 21,643 households b) Vaccination/treatment of 35,000 livestock c) Setting up 1,000 kitchen gardens at Nutrition facilities/Schools/HouseholdIn order to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes, CO3, NSDO will undertake capacity building and training, improved livelihood productivity, market development, Anticipatory Actions to 21,643 individuals.To achieve these objectives, NSDO will implement the activities with full involvement of the affected communities in the project location across age group, gender and disability. This will include identification and registration of beneficiaries for food distribution, non food items/agricultural inputs. This will include selection and registration of beneficiaries who are most in need inconsideration of households that host people with disability and elderly in selected Payams.
