Project Summary

About 1.8 million South Sudanese families will face Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues in 2022 - of whom 34% are vulnerable children. HLP is a key contributor to national peacebuilding, resettlement, recovery and stability and in turn it drives conflict and displacement if not addressed.
Duk, Twic East, Bor South and Magwi are among 22 counties facing severe HLP needs in South Sudan moving into 2022. While IDPs and refugees are expected to return to their places of origin due to promising progress of the R-ARCISS implementation, HLP needs are expected to rise as a result of destruction of housing and property by floods or conflicts, illegal occupation, grabbing, lack of implementation of legal provisions, abuse of women HLP rights and lack of effective land documentation practices. The most affected persons of concern are women, children, women-headed households, child-headed households, persons with disabilities, and elderly.
Particular emphasis must be laid on women's HLP needs. Despite the legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, the knowledge, recognition and protection of those rights remains very limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights remain largely conditional, derived through their marital or childbearing status and dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women of their HLP ownership is common. There is tension between competing notions that customary rules and practices should adapt to changing socio-economic circumstances and those who resist change, fearing its impact on tradition and cultural identity, cause a significant gap between the law and practice, particularly in rural areas (South Sudan draft Land Policy, p.4). Moreover, during such emergencies, persons with disabilities face unbearable shocks such as risks of drowning, behind left behind during flights and physical violence, exploitation and abuse. They tend to lack basic needs on top of their physical or mental disability, including land, shelter/NFIs, assistive devices, food, medication, and access to essential protection information.
CINA will therefore target 15,000 HLP families (6,750 women, 5,250 men, 2,400 girls, 2,100 boys) including 2,250 persons with disabilities with equitable access to HLP through the following HLP programming: HLP awareness raising and outreach activities; Community-based dispute resolution; Legal support / counselling/ assistance / case management and HLP needs assessments and monitoring through static and field-base roving/mobile response.
In all its implementation phases, CINA will strictly adhere to national laws and international norms on HLP, Code of Conduct, Community Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, uphold the Do No Harm Principle, promote centrality of protection principles and enhance accountability to affected populations before, during and after project implementation.
CINA has a strong static presence in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria, with qualified personnel, assets, governance and organizational structures in place, where it currently implements general protection, HLP, Child Protection, GBV, and EORE and health programs, and has field offices in Magwi, Bor, Twic East (Panyagor), Duk (Padiet, Poktap). CINA has two motor speedboats, stationed in Twic East, used by CINA and other humanitarian agencies to access Twic East, Duk and Bor South and their surrounding islands to deliver aid and aid workers.
CINA will therefore use this existing protection presence in Jonglei (Bor South, Duk, Twic East) and Eastern Equatoria (Magwi) to reach populations in need of HLP. This project is designed in line with Protection Cluster HRP 2022 objectives and will be coordinated with South Sudan HLP AoR and partners on ground to build synergies and optimize nexus efforts, including with humanitarian, peace building and development actors.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, housing, land and property (HLP) issues are both a consequence and a cause of conflict. The COVID-19 pandemic, flooding and inter-communal violence have exacerbated some of the risks associated with HLP issues in several areas of the country especially with, Jonglei state (Twic East, Bor South) and Eastern Equatoria (Magwi county) in need of HLP assistance (HRP Locations 2022). This is evidenced by widespread destruction and flooding, degradation of housing due to displacement, secondary occupation, forced eviction, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure which are all features of the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. (HLPAoR HRP 2022 Strategy). It also is noted that HLP rights including reclamation, restitution and reconstruction continue to be a crucial issue in South Sudan (South Sudan HLP TWG: HLP Challenges in South Sudan January 2022). In Bor South , Twic East and Magwi, HLP issues constitute a major challenge to creating conditions conducive to pursuing durable solutions for displaced populations and peace among the communities. Spontaneous returns of both IDPs and refugees are expected to increase in 2022 as the government moves forward with the implementation of the R-ARCSS. This will result in a rise of HLP issues in areas of return especially illegal occupation and grabbing of houses and lands, which has potential to contribute to subnational and localized violence along with conflict based on identity lines in some areas (HNO 2022). There is also limited awareness of HLP rights among local authorities and affected populations. Displaced and returnee populations, in particular women, women-headed households, child-headed households, elderly, persons with disabilities, women facing GBV, and other minority groups in the community lack legal assistance to access the limited courts or alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to claim their rights. Worst of all, women, children and people with disabilities disproportionately struggle to assert rights to housing and land. Despite the legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, recognition and protection of those rights remain limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights remain largely conditional. Dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women is common. Moreover, there is increasing number of South Sudanese refugees returning from the neighboring countries such as Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia and Sudan, most of them get settle in Magwi . In a context of increasing land-related disputes, and anticipation of returns, relocations, and local reintegration of IDPs and refugees, HLP issues will be a major priority need to be addressed in South Sudan in 2022. CINA reached 1510 people (298 men, 55 women, 328 Boys, 325 Girls) on HLP activities UNHCR project 2021. Therefore, in line with Protection Cluster HRP objective: Displaced populations, host communities, returnees, including women and persons with disabilities, have increased knowledge of and ability to exercise and enjoy housing, land and property (HLP) rights, a significant investment is required to achieve durable solutions for successful return and integration of returning communities.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1. CINA will conduct HLP Awareness raising to community members on HLP rights. These people include IDPs, returnees and Host communities. The awareness will be centered on the issues affecting the community social cohesion and what can be done to avoid conflict in common places of social concerns. access to land and property is one of the main problems IDPs and returnees face in the area of return. To facilitate HLP awareness raising activities, CINA will establish a HLP desk in the community centre at each targeted location (Jonglei state Twic East, Bor South and Eastern Equatoria (Magwi), manned by volunteers supervised by CINA HLP staff which will conduct awareness sessions on HLP and distribute the Information and Education Communication (IEC) material on HLP issues, as well as identify and respond to HLP cases. Particular focus will be given to women, who often do not have access to justice due to the lack of knowledge of their rights, availability of legal aid and associated payment fees. In addition, very few women are in a possession of land and even fewer who have documents confirming land ownership. Activity 2. . Provide legal Assistance and Counselling to Beneficiaries. Individual legal assistance, pro bono services/case management will be provided for specific HLP cases of IDPs and returnees. CINA will identify and prioritize HLP beneficiaries to receive HLP support. Depending on the severity of the concern, the individual may receive more specialized support which might include additional legal advice or referrals to the authorities, whereas individuals who may not require case management will be referred to the HLP Help Desk. Through this component, beneficiaries will be supported to access both formal and informal justice mechanisms (individual cases follow up with law enforcement, courts). Where beneficiaries require referrals to external services, CINA will conduct direct payments to these services to ensure the beneficiaries are able to access this critical support. Activity 3. Conduct 6 HLP Protection assessments to identify violations of HLP rights and protection risks encountered by internally displaced persons (IDPs), Returnees and other affected populations with an immediate objective of assisting and guiding evidence-based intervention by relevant national and international actors to ensure the full respect of the rights of the affected populations. Activity 4. CINA, will work with the community in IDP locations and areas of return to establish alternative dispute resolution mechanisms/committees representative of IDPs, returnees and host communities. Three ADR mechanism will be set up in each location with the composition made up of local leaders, representatives of minority groups and people who have knowledge of disputes related to HLP after attending the awareness sessions. Due to lack of access to the formal justice system and traditional courts, CINA will use community-based approach to enable communities to work together cooperatively to reach the best and mutually agreed resolution. Activity 5. Conduct Capacity Building sessions to Local GovernanceStructures’ members on HLP rights, peace-building and coexistence mechanisms. Activity 6. Conduct Peace-building activities to through Dialogues, Focus Group Discussionsand training. CINA will foster dialogue between different communities (IDP, returnees and host community) by facilitating the organization of community-led joint events and community information sessions to provide groups with access to information on available services. A total of 6 peace dialogues and information sessions will be delivered by CINA staff, targeting people in each location.. Activity 7. Establish/strengthen 6 Information Assistance/Help Desks in Bor South , Duk, Twic East andMagwi that will be used to identify, respond and report HLP issues. IADs/Help Desks in will comprise 5 members for all the locations. They will be based in Community Centres.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash will be used to support HLP case management, in terms of legal aid, documentation, title deeds, and to build a temporary shelter for the most vulnerable HLP families. From CINA's past experience in cash programming in protection, cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent protection needs offering dignity, flexibility and choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. Assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basicneeds or to assisting them in protecting or reestablishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach and greatly contributes to centrality of protection.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

During CINA integrated protection projects (with HLP component) in Magwi in 2020/2021, it was observed that markets for purchase of local shelter materials and legal support services are available and functional. Further market assessments will be conducted as part of this project to ascertainmarket functionality. Areas like Twic east and Duk do have functional market.However, as the operating context keep on changing, market analysis will be done to examine market functionality predictors such as an increased focus on income and impact measurement, absence of evidence of harming the market, functional and healthy market, increased availability of products in the market, increased quality products in the market, resilience of households, gender, protection and inclusion of vulnerable people, shifts in income and gender perception, decreased disease rate, quality occupancy of housing and increased labour capacity in construction, for it tofeasibly implement cash based assistance.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA has a PSEA Policy and Code of Conduct, and as part of its HLP Programming, staff will ensure strict adherence to these policies and other universal principles enshrined in the international human rights instruments and the Bill of Rights in the Transitional Constitution of the Republic of South Sudan with reference to other related laws of the land, will incorporate protection principles to ensure and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity while implementing this project. In consolidating that, CINA HLP staff will be thoroughly trained on PSEA and sign the Code of Conduct before implementing the activities. The team will then strive to mainstream PSEA throughout theimplementation process by giving awareness to the Community Based Protection Networks and Information Assistance Desks established at the community level. PSEA messages will be printed on banners and disseminated along with HLP awareness messages. The HLP beneficiaries will be empowered to report any information regarding sexual exploitation and abuse using thesestructures. Finally, CINA will analyze and provide appropriate feedback through the PSEA Focal Person or the Project Manager. This will also include attending the inter-agency working groups in Duk, Twic East and Magwi to understand the trending protection concerns such as GBV Related issues alongside HLP issues from other humanitarian actors and consequently also alerting them on safe/unsafe environment. The HLP beneficiaries will be empowered to report any information regarding sexual exploitation and abuse. Finally, CINA will analyze and provide appropriate feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The feedback will inform the organisation programming on how interventionsshould include the information received through community feedback mechanisms and address those concerns raise. . At the beginning and during the implementation of HLP project, the target community will be engaged and sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit feedback or complaints, and information will be clearly displayed in all the community sites targeted. Subsequently this information will be used to establish a referral pathway for complaints and feedback. The Information alternative dispute and HLP help Desk including the Protection Focal Points will be trained on the pathway so the community can handle complaints and feedback after the intervention. Additionally, CINA will provide training and capacity building to members of the focal points to ensure that the community is better able to advocate for its own needs in future and able to deliver their own training for community. CINA is committed to establishing and maintain a system that gives beneficiaries the possibility to formulate comments, feedbacks, suggestions, compliments or complaints on our work, as well as other humanitarian actors.
