Project Summary

ANPPACNSOM is currently responding to the emergency nutrition situation in parts of Waqooyi-Galbeed region with SHF and UNICEF funding providing services in 9 health centers, 1 SC and 4 mobile OTP. ANPPCANSOM seeks to expand and scale up its nutrition support to areas with dire needs. This approach, which is in line with the Somalia Humanitarian Response Plan, will be primarily aimed at providing lifesaving nutrition interventions.
ANPPCANSOM drought response plan will scale up treatment of severe acute malnutrition and moderate acute malnutrition by increasing the number of static and mobile outreach clinics from 9 to 23 subsequently reaching an estimated 2450 children affected by SAM and 1260 children with MAM while maintaining treatment outcomes consistent with Sphere standards.
ANPPCANSOM will build on and sustain the delivery of a holistic basic nutrition services package (BNSP) in priority to internally displaced people and their host communities and other socially marginalized groups in pastoral and hard to reach areas while ensuring the inclusion of treatment, promotion and prevention components in this project.
ANPPCANSOM will use the combined protocol for the management of acute malnutrition in close consultation and coordination with Somali Nutrition Cluster, Somaliland Nutrition Working Group and other nutrition partners including MOH, UNICEF and WFP. Meanwhile, ANPPCANSOM proposed OTP sites (fixed and mobile) will provide a platform for the delivery of integrated services while ensuring full community participation in service delivery.

Needs

Protracted humanitarian crisis aggravated by prolonged dry weather risked escalating Somaliland’s unstable and fragile drought crisis into famine in 2017 and 2018. However, scaling up of humanitarian assistance largely helped avert the looming catastrophe. That said, the humanitarian impact of the severe drought has been devastating with acute malnutrition levels worsening, which caused high death of livestock- the back bone of the local economy. Most of the people who lost their livestock migrated to the nearby villages and towns as destitute in all six regions contributing to a massive increase in internal displacement. According to IOM and UNCHR IDPs assessment reports, over 243,000 displaced people left their permanent settlements and ended up in informal settlements of urban centres with inadequate access to WASH and health infrastructure. This has led to an increase in disease outbreaks, especially AWD and measles, two conditions which exacerbates malnutrition. According to results of the seasonal FSNAU led food security and nutrition situation assessment, the nutrition situation in most districts of Somaliland improved due to the implementation of massive humanitarian intervention during the pre-famine prevention, but the situation is expected to deteriorate due to the high vulnerability to food insecurity of the people as most of the rural population lost about 80% of the livestock which was their main source of income. The latest FSNAU assessment classified the majority of the IDPs and rural population in Somaliland is in IPC3 and IPC4 which required regular food security and nutrition intervention for preventing famine. Considering the prolonged drought situation in most regions of Somaliland, the National Disaster and Food Reserve Agency (NADFOR) declared drought emergency in January this year. In this declaration, the government emphasized the persistency of the drought emergency impact and the increasing depletion coping strategies for the affect

Activities

1. Routinely screen children 6-59 months and PLWs with MUAC tapes to identify acutely malnutrition cases at community and health facility levels.2. Establish 10 mobile OTP and 23 fixed nutrition sites to treat and prevent severe and moderate acute malnutrition in under-five children and pregnant and lactating women. 3. Provide therapeutic treatment to cases presenting with acute malnutrition with lifesaving therapeutic and supplementary interventions. 4. Provide measles vaccine to under-five children through both the fixed sites and mobile teams.5. Provide preventive interventions through supplementary feeding including micro-nutrient supplementation targeting pregnant and lactating women and children.6. Conduct basic training for CHWs on basic tenets of nutrition including identification of malnutrition through MUAC screening, adherence to nutrition therapy, defaulter tracing and balanced meals.7. Establish and ensure the referral mechanism is in place and allows follow up.8. Promote IYCF education and counseling for pregnant women and caretakers of children (0-24 months) through household education visits by CHWs.9. Train health workers on CMAM guidelines (both male and female staff).10. Undertake regular monitoring and verification visits on key performance indicators on a weekly and monthly basis.11. Organize regular meetings with MoH at district, regional and national levels to improve engagement and promote government ownership of the outcomes.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Results, transparency and accountability are vital aspects of ANPPANSOM program values while effective identification and handling of risks is cornerstone to our Risk Management Policy. ANPPCANSOM will ensure that the funding made available by SHF delivers value for money and intended results. Our organizational risk management policy is used as a tool for ensuring quality and good results, in addition to reducing the risk of corruption, mismanagement and other misuse of funds. Our best practice underlines the need for the active management of risk throughout the life of the project, with particular attention to overrunning time and cost and/or failing to deliver the intended results. We will make sure the organizational risk register compiled, reviewed and updated continually throughout the life of the project. We will introduce effective complaint mechanisms to identify and prevent corruption and other malpractice. By providing beneficiaries and local communities with safe channels to report any incidence or suspicion of corruption or other malpractice, complaint mechanisms allow for the identification of problems which might otherwise not surface, and for subsequent corrective action to be taken. Local communities will be adequately mobilized to identify and submit complaints or alert if there are suspicion of mismanagement of project funds including commodity and supply.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

ANPPCANSOM has strategically selected project locations and target beneficiaries, which mostly focus on internally displaced people, pastoral communities, under-served people as well as remote and hard-to-reach populations. The proximity of the selected static and mobile teams to marginalized communities removes barriers to the accessibility of nutrition services. Through the engagement of CHWs, we will be able to identify most vulnerable children and women within communities and provide them with targeted and prioritized BNSP services.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

ANPPCAN uses practical ways in mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence including prior consideration to the needs of all affected people.
