Project Summary

regarding HNO-2021-consultation meeting Report,Key child protection needs –draft
• Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of gender based violence. About one in 10 girls marry before the age of 15, and about 5 in 10 girls before the age of 18. Nearly all (99 per cent) of women 15-49 years have undergone female genital mutilation (FGM), with 71 per cent circumcised between ages five to nine.
• Separation from caregivers is reported in 48 per cent of IDP families and 44 per cent in host communities. Death of caregivers/losing parents, child marriage (mostly adolescent girls) and child recruitment (mostly adolescent boys) were the main cause of family separation. As at July 2020, 10,655 unaccompanied and separated children have been registered, most of whom report experiencing emotional distress. These groups of children are at further heightened risk of child trafficking, abuse and exploitation in the camps and communities.
• Child labour is reported in 55.5 per cent of camp and urban communities with child recruitment and use by parties to the conflict; children are made to beg in extreme heat, shine shoes, sell goods in the market being the most common .
Children with disabilities are especially vulnerable and report a very high level of unmet needs. They are the most vulnerable, marginalized and at-risk group within Somalian society as a result of the numerous attitudinal, environmental, and institutional barriers they face.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of marginalized community’s and minority groups. Minority groups in Somalia are rarely targeted in humanitarian project cycle and are mainly marginalized and invisible. Women and children and persons with special needs among minority groups are even more sidelined and excluded and do not benefit from interventions that accords them some degree of protection. The environment in Somalia as a result of ongoing armed conflict and cyclic often deem minority persons and groups more vulnerable. In addition, at least 15% (10% children)of the population are people with disabilities who face heightened risks and exclusion from services. To mitigate on this risks, SAYS will intentionally target and empower, protect and promote the rights of minority communities, with a focus on women and children who experience multiple-discrimination on account of their gender and minority status. SAYS will follow a selection criterion for beneficiaries that include members of minorities as vulnerable groups that require special attention. Selection of beneficiaries will be through participatory methods in collaboration with trained community representatives. Through protection monitoring, SAYS will identify trends of exclusion and marginalization. Data collected will be used for targeting and prioritization, ensuring that no one is left out. SAYS will consult people with disabilities and/or their representatives during the design and Identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities. Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of women, girls and boys Women, girls and boys remain vulnerable to protection risks and harmful cultural practices, GBV, child recruitment and hazardous child labor. Their ability to access justice and remedies and to enjoy rights is often limited and further weakened in case of displacement. Furthermore, stigma related to abuses often deters survivors from accessing assistance or reporting incidents. To address this, SAYS will include specific needs of women, girls and boys when designing and implementing the project. Using the rights based approach SAYS will ensure that all activities build on protection standards but also leverage national frameworks. will ensure proper coordination, especially on referrals to maximize quality use of already limited resources Mitigation the risk of external stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection There are significant number of displaced communities and people in locations that are not easily accessible to humanitarian assistance, this could be due to insecurity and people living in far to reach areas. In addition, there are instances where external stakeholders demand targeting of particular groups or prevent others from receiving assistance. To mitigate on these challenges,SAYS will use beneficiary selection criteria that considers vulnerability as an entry point to assistance. SAYS will engage in consultation processes adequately gathering relevant information including power dynamics from the communities and other stakeholders and taking these views into account when selecting beneficiaries and implementing the action. SAYS will work with existing or revitalized community committees among the target groups that will be part of the beneficiary selection.SAYS will conduct community consultation and inclusive participation ,provide full project information and responsible of each and encourage community ownership implementation.
