Needs

According to the 2018 HNO in 2019, some 3,000 in GCA and 8-10,000 in NGCA vulnerable families (mainly non-displaced, but also returnee and internally displaced), living along the “contact line”, will need humanitarian shelter assistance for the repair of their homes. As per the REACH trend analysis, shelter needs increased significantly, as 35% of respondents in the GCA 5km zone (up from 26% in 2016) reported living in damaged accommodation. PIN vulnerability analysis shows that shelter remains a priority for residents of the LoC in GCA, as shelter was identified as being the second highest priority with 33% of assessed HH reporting it as a priority and 31% HHs reporting shelter damages of various severity. The percentage of HH reporting limited access to fuel for winter has almost doubled in the GCA 5 km zone (up to 34% from 19%). The coal blockade established with regard to NGCA led to a significant reduction of availability of coal on the market. And, the realization of the EU package of reforms for Ukraine in the heating subsidies sphere have significant impact on the increase in expenditure for some consumers (due to the fact that subsidies were reduced). The heating costs, at USD 560 (Shelter Cluster, 2018), 4 tons of coal heating fuel requirements, representing more than 40% of the annual income. The shelter team plans to help households that do not have a gas heating system. The amount of the subsidy is not more than 70 EUR per household that does not cover the minimum amount needed for the heating season. There is also a problem with limited access, in some places there is no appropriate transport logistics and residents do not have the opportunity to travel for subsidies to local administrations. In NHO 2018 mentioned that the most conflict-affected people, particularly the elderly and vulnerable households, rely heavily on the government’s social protection scheme as their main source of income. Peoples’ savings and reserves are exhausted, and those in most need are being forced to stretch already limited resources or simply go without. The recent analysis of the impact of the conflict in eastern Ukraine by Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Cluster, Socio-Economic Summary Report, 2018 shows a clear link between the conflict and the worsening socio-economic situation. REACH ABA, 2018 indicates that nearly half of rural households and one-third of urban households in GCA of the two conflict-affected regions do not have adequate supplies for the winter. The local residents, mostly elderly people with small incomes, many of whom have various disabilities, and IDPs, are not able to purchase necessary food and non-food items to maintain decent living conditions in their houses that suffered war-related damage. Thus, considering the fact that during winter in Ukraine the temperatures can go regularly below -20°C, with less than 30% of winterization needs covered, by the information of Shelter cluster (Fact Sheet, 2018), life preserving and lifesaving assistance remains urgent and time-sensitive. With regards to vulnerability criteria and beneficiary selection, generally, PIN will follow recommendations of the Shelter Cluster. PIN will continue to prioritize individuals in vulnerable categories which have limited access to income opportunities (list is not exhaustive, only indicative: Households/persons without any income; women headed households with children, households/persons that do not receive appropriate social benefits for different reasons; chronically ill people and people with disabilities; people over 60 years old; IDPs; and single headed households/families.

Activities & outcomes

With the above needs/strategies in mind, PIN proposes the following shelter/NFI activities for 2019. Outcome 1. Satisfied essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through monetised/in-kind assistance and contingency1. Providing solid fuel & heater distribution• PIN Shelter team plans to cover needs for winterization with solid fuel (coal or briquettes) to the residents of the frontline zone. The fuel will be delivered to the houses of the most vulnerable categories of the population. Delivery of 4 tons of coal or briquettes for each HH will be carried out exclusively within the 5 km LoC, on both sides of the conflict. Average cost coal per HH is $5602. Providing winterization cash grant transfers• PIN will provide non-conditional winterization cash grant for Shelter assistance (GCA) to 500 HHs . This intervention will enable beneficiaries to meet their basic winterization needs by purchasing necessary non-food items, such as solid fuel, construction materials, etc. Average grant size is $250 Outcome 2.Provided adequate transitional solutions [monetised or in-kind] related to shelter and NFI needs meeting minimal international and national shelter standards1. Implementation light and medium repairs• Materials and labor for light and medium scale repairs on war damaged private homes mainly consisting of roof patching, full rehabilitation of roofs, light wall repairs, doors, and/or some light interior work. Average value of light repairs set at $400, and $1000 for medium repairs. • Installation of high quality plastic/PVC windows with frames to replace old, conflict damaged windows for private homes and apartments. Average price set at $350 per HH.• Roof repairs for multistory buildings: Materials and labor of repairs of sections of roofs on multi story buildings. Each section covers one stairwell, and is estimated at $3000 each.2. Providing cash for rent or other shelter-linked monetized solutions• Cash grants of $400 for light scale shelter repairs, and $1000 for medium. Amount is meant to cover the cost of materials and labor. The PIN also plans to give cash grants for apartment repairs, since this need remains uncovered.Outcome 3. Provided/upgraded permanent shelter solutions for the most vulnerable conflict affected population1. Implementing structural repairs ("heavy repairs")• Materials and labor for heavy scale repairs and reconstructions (using existing foundations) of private homes consisting of rehabilitation of exterior/interior walls, roofs, windows, flooring, electricity, plumbing, etc. Average value of repairs set at $4000.2. Implementing full reconstruction repairs• Materials and labor for heavy scale reconstructions (using existing foundations) of private homes consisting of rehabilitation of exterior/interior walls, roofs, windows, flooring, electricity, plumbing, etc. Average value of repairs set at $8000---10000.
