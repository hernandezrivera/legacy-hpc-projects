Project Summary

The Rohingya community, compelled to flee Myanmar, grapples with heightened vulnerability due to entrenched social issues like discrimination and violence. This susceptibility disproportionately affects children, adolescents, women, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. The project's core objective is to bolster community mobilization by disseminating information through established, inclusive community centers. These centers will facilitate the formation of diverse groups, categorized by age, gender, vulnerability, and diversity. FDMNs will receive specialized sessions addressing anti-trafficking, emergency response, gender-based violence prevention, and positive parenting. Persons with disabilities will actively participate in a self-help committee, contributing to the development of action plans. Barefoot counselors, serving as community volunteers, will conduct door-to-door visits to identify protection concerns and support needs. Staff and volunteers will be trained to form a Community-Based Protection Committee (CBPC) and develop action plans to address various forms of violence and mental health condition. Installation of solar streetlights at the block level aims to enhance safety and dignity. Inclusive activities, such as play therapy for children and focus group discussions, address diverse needs. The project establishes a community-based complaint mechanism, ensures transparent referral support, and provides basic primary health care by paramedic support. Tree plantation activities contribute to ecological protection, and a contingency plan with project staff involvement in the PERU team addresses emergencies. Counter-trafficking initiatives involve advocacy, screening, and collaboration with stakeholders, aligning with the project's broader goal of impacting community-based protection and supporting potential repatriation for FDMNs to their country of origin.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Caritas Bangladesh is currently engaged in the provision of general protection services, recognizing the imperative need for a community-led approach to address existing gaps in human rights promotion and mitigate various forms of violence risks. A notable concern is the alarming incidence of Anti-Trafficking cases among Rohingya refugees, with 91 reported cases between July and September 2023, and a staggering 98% impacted by forced labor. The dire socio-economic conditions, including limited livelihoods and educational opportunities within the camps, drive refugees to seek alternative prospects, exposing them to the risk of human trafficking. A concerning trend is the lack of awareness among victims regarding their rights and entitlements. Among the refugees, 20% reported individuals known to them leaving the camps, primarily due to factors like insufficient work opportunities, food scarcity, and an overall decline in security conditions. Protection risks weigh heavily on refugee households, with 60% expressing concerns. Worries encompass crime, violence, and potential associations with criminal groups. Notably, 37% of refugee households feel unsafe after dark, and 5% refrain from going out alone during nighttime. In contrast, approximately 22% of refugees feel very safe. Child marriage emerges as a pervasive issue, fueled by cultural practices, educational deficiencies, economic pressures, and protection concerns. This concern is significant for refugee community children and caregivers. Distress signals, as highlighted in the JMSNA report, were reported by about 38% of households in the refugee community. Recognizing these challenges, Caritas Bangladesh is poised to enhance its efforts to address protection risks, promote human rights, and contribute to the well-being of refugee communities. The identified issues will inform tailored interventions, ensuring a comprehensive and community-centered approach to mitigate the multifaceted challenges faced by Rohingya refugees alike.

3.3 - Age & Disability

This project places paramount importance on community engagement, targeting a diverse demographic, including adolescent girls and boys, men, women, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities. The primary goal is to deliver effective remedial and comprehensive services addressing protection risk mitigation and human rights promotion, thereby enhancing overall well-being. The project systematically prioritizes the inclusion of persons with disabilities, considering age and gender diversity, and mainstreams their participation throughout the program. To further this inclusive approach of community-based protection committee will be formed, focusing on the best interests of diverse groups, particularly different age categories of persons with disabilities. Also, there will be disability support group, where the person with disabilities and the caregiver will be the participants, there will be capacity-building training on disability inclusion which will be provided to both staff and the community to ensure proper needs assessment. Project proposals will incorporate indicators for accountability, ensuring robust participation. Service mapping specifically for persons with disabilities will be conducted to streamline and enhance their support mechanisms. This comprehensive strategy aims to create an inclusive and responsive program, addressing the unique needs of various community members, particularly those with disabilities.

The project incorporates a robust Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) mechanism, integrating key principles such as Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and regular stakeholder consultations. 3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) upholds a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy and PSEA policy tailored for of the humanitarian context. This commitment is reinforced by the presence of a dedicated Safeguarding Committee and a designated Safeguarding Focal, PSEA focal in all offices, including the Cox's Bazar office. CB ensures that its staff and volunteers receive annual training on Safeguarding, the Code of Conduct, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) orientation. As part of the onboarding process, staff and volunteers are required to sign a Safeguarding and Code of Conduct agreement, with their references thoroughly checked before formal job offers are extended. Participants are well-informed about the Safeguarding policy through pre-service delivery and distribution orientations. Safeguarding remains a recurrent agenda item in all meetings, training sessions, and workshops. A Bengali version of the Safeguarding policy, along with a concise one-pager, is distributed among staff and host volunteers to ensure accessibility. The commitment to safeguarding extends to all visitors, advisors, and vendors who must read and sign the safeguarding form before accessing the camps, ensuring a unified approach to protection. These stringent measures and obligations are equally applicable to the staff, facilitators, and volunteers operating under the proposed project. CB's steadfast adherence to safeguarding principles underscores its commitment to creating a secure and protective environment for all, emphasizing the importance of continuous awareness, training, and diligence in upholding these essential policies and practices.
