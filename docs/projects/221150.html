Project Summary

In the North-West, South-West regions, IOM plans interventions for 2025 to respond to the most urgent needs of displaced populations through a multisectoral approach focusing on coordination, Shelter and NFI, Mental health support (health), psychosocial support (protection) and WASH interventions. The last Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) exercise conducted in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, undertaken in September 2024, showed needs in most of the sectors, including access to water, food, health, education, etc. The last Mobility Tracking assessment showed that the North-West (NW)-South-West (SW) crisis impact continues also to spill over to the neighbouring regions with the number of IDPs coming from NW-SW increasing from the previous year, while the last MSNA, undertaken at the same time, showed again severe needs in most of the sectors. In view of such needs identified, IOM plans to expand its programmes accordingly and in line with each mandate and capacity, with a multisectoral response aiming at contributing to reinforce life-saving assistance and to improve well-being of displaced and the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis. IOM will mainstream protection and recovery through the development of local capacities, in all its proposed activities to support displaced populations in preparing and achieving durable solutions.
IOM will support the work of the Coordination sector through the collection and production of critical data on displacement situation, alerts as well as Multi-sectoral humanitarian needs following its DTM methodology, that will be provided to the humanitarian community to inform its humanitarian programming. In the frame of the Shelter Sector response, IOM will distribute emergency shelter and NFI kits, both in-kind and using cash-modality solutions when possible (bearing in mind that lack of civil documentation and concerns of diversion of cash to non-licit uses poses very important challenges) and help the construction for most vulnerable people. In the WASH response, IOM will drill and rehabilitate boreholes, construct water points, latrines and other infrastructures and conduct hygiene sensitization and promotion activities combined with distribution of WASH kits. Finally, IOM multisectoral assistance will also include mental health activities including direct MHPSS assistance using PMTs, reinforcing referral mechanism and capacity building of health actors (Health sector), while also directly providing psychosocial support to populations impacted by crisis by reinforcing safety and social network as well as strengthening community capacity to provide psychosocial support to victims of GBVs (Protection sector).
Through activities, direct feedback from beneficiaries and community members will be gained via IPs and also directly by IOM staff during field visits and visits of the M&E/AAP independent team in accordance with the wider IOM AAP emergencies workplan. AAP focal points will be assigned in Buea and Yaounde respectively, with clear SOPs to be in place to capture complaints from beneficiaries and to take any appropriate action. Members of IP will be trained on topics such as the proper handling of disclosures of gender-based violence (GBV) incidents, aspects of confidentiality, and respectful communication in high-stress environments. Targeting of beneficiaries will be done according to a vulnerability criteria including persons living with disabilities assessed using the shelter cluster household level assessments tool. Also, IOM will integrate measures to ensure gender-specific needs when conducting needs assessment and to mitigate risks of GBV in all activities (e.g., including through training of IPs on GBV core concepts, guiding principles as well as on how to respond to disclosures of GBV). IOM will also ensure the effective participation of these communities in decision-making processes and in establishing community-based mechanisms.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Humanitarian needs in Cameroon remain high. In the NW-SW regions, the sociopolitical crisis that erupted in 2017 after peaceful protests by lawyers and teachers against a perceived encroachment of the regions’ cultural practices and autonomy, and which turned into an armed conflict between the government defense forces and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), continues to trigger very dynamic and massive internal displacements and related humanitarian needs. Throughout the year 2024 the landscape of NSAGs continued to be fragmented following a further breakdown between the diaspora and the NSAGs on the ground, blurring command and control structures and making humanitarian access more complex. This and the sustained rise in the use of improvised explosive devices points towards the increasingly protracted nature of the conflict, with unaddressed grievances of local populations in NWSW and low amounts of socioeconomic opportunities for youth who are also vulnerable to exploitation and recruitment by armed groups. In September 2024, IOM’s Mobility Tracking counted 334,098 IDPs and 440,523 internal returnees within the NWSW. In the neighboring regions of West and Littoral, an estimated 159,308 displaced populations are presently displaced dominantly due to the spillover from the NW-SW region conflict into these regions (98%), with 68% of IDP individuals assessed were displaced in 2021 and before (Mobility Tracking September 2023). Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility of the NWSW crisis at an international level. The last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by IOM in the North-West, South-West regions (September 2024) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors especially WASH, Health, Protection, Shelter and NFI. Assessments in the NWSW region, such as previous MSNA findings also recognize the rising number of urban and peri-urban displaced populations andthe identification of cash as the preferred modality of assistance, as these persons have more access to functional markets than in rural areas. For the Shelter/NFI sector for example the largest proportion of the assessed population lives in mudbrick houses for all three population groups (IDPs, returnees and host community), 55 per cent of host community and IDPs and 57 per cent of returnees are living in mudbrick houses. The second majority of sample population (20%) is living in timber wood houses while the third majority (16%) is living in concrete houses. Thirty-four per cent (34%) of shelters are damaged. For civil documentation, thirty-sixper cent (36%) of assessed households needed to obtain civil documentation in the past 6 months, while 62 per cent were not in need of civil documentation for that period. The type of documents needed by the household members included national identity cards (78%), birth certificates (75%), marriage certificates (10%) and death certificates (2%). In terms of education support, sixty-four per cent of households reported needing grants. Sixteen per cent cited the need for school materials and 8 per cent cited the need to ensure teacher and student safety. Finally, 2 per cent cited the need to create temporary spaces for teaching. Regarding nutrition, sixteen per cent of households assessed reported that in the past month, a community health worker visited the household to educate them on breast feeding and how to feed young children. Another 94 per cent of the assessed population did not have any community health worker who visited to educate the household on how to breast feed and feed young children.
