Project Summary

The project aims to create pathways for sustainable self-reliance and resilience among displacement-affected youth aged 18 to 24 in Cox’s Bazar, with a focus on access to equitable, quality, and safe skills development. The project leverages NRC’s extensive experience in working with and for youth to implement an integrated approach combining vocational, life, and soft skills training, along with transition support for income-generating strategies. It addresses economic and social aspects, preparing youth for meaningful engagement in socioeconomic sectors aligned with the skills development objectives.
The project will offer market-driven trades to align with local economic trends, focusing on in-demand skills including Sewing Machine Operations, Handicrafts, Teachers’ Professional Development, Solar System Installation and Maintenance, and Plumbing. This approach ensures that youth acquire relevant skills and, with NRC’s support for transition, these can translate into livelihood opportunities, whether entering the workforce or starting small businesses.
Participants will receive business startup kits and tailored in-kind support to facilitate their entry into the labour market. Paired with coaching and mentoring, these kits help youth navigate income-generating opportunities, boost confidence, and establish strong market connections. NRC will foster linkages across sectors to ensure sustainable access to job placements, internships, and hands-on experience, further enhancing practical skills and economic mobility.
NRC will incorporate its Life Skills training, developed to meet the personal needs of youth. Leveraging NRC’s toolkit for Life Skills, this training focuses on personal development, critical thinking, and cognitive skills enhancement, addressing key themes such as personal leadership, identity, and resilience building. Participants will acquire coping mechanisms for navigating challenges. Accelerated Adult Literacy will offer literacy and numeracy skills, essential for competitive entry into the job market. Digital skills and language courses will further enhance employability and expand opportunities.
NRC’s psychosocial support Better Learning Programme (BLP) will equip youth with coping strategies to address psychosocial distress linked to forced displacement, isolation, and insecurity, ensuring they develop the resilience needed. Learning spaces will be established or upgraded following the Comprehensive School Safety Framework, ensuring inclusive environments with gender-sensitive WASH facilities, especially for young girls. The programme will prioritise youth who are not in employment, education, or training (NEET). Vulnerable groups, including young mothers, persons with disabilities, female-headed households, and other marginalised populations, are central to NRC’s inclusive design. The programme is both disability- and gender-responsive, addressing access barriers and ensuring that female learners, in particular, are supported in gaining equitable access to education and training.
NRC will collaborate with government, vocational institutions, the local market, and other humanitarian actors to create diversified livelihood opportunities and create a holistic education and skills development programme. We will focus on three key objectives: Enhanced Access to Learning: Deliver foundational literacy, numeracy, and life skills training through flexible, youth-centric delivery methods. Vocational and Skills Development: Provide gender-appropriate vocational training aligned with the needs and aspirations of Rohingya and host community youth, emphasising transferable skills and sustainable livelihoods. Youth-Led Community Engagement: Establish youth-led initiatives and leadership training, enabling refugee youth to participate actively in the design, implementation, and monitoring of humanitarian programmes.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Overstretched in resources, Cox's Bazar hosts the world’s largest refugee settlement, leading to overcrowded, resource-limited conditions that exacerbate the challenging living situation. Vulnerable youth in the Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities face severe constraints in achieving self-reliance and resilience. According to UNHCR, young people make up a third of the refugee population and face disproportionate impacts due to prolonged displacement and lack of age-appropriate opportunities. The National Labour Survey reports a national unemployment rate of 8%, with many host community youth engaged in the informal market, where job security and earnings are limited. Limited access to skills training and livelihood opportunities, as highlighted in the Assessment of Economic Opportunities by the Population Council, severely hinders their ability to contribute to family and community economic and social well-being and exposes them to long-term vulnerabilities. This crisis, further intensified by climate-related disasters and reduced humanitarian funding, has created an unstable environment where youth are particularly vulnerable to security risks.An NRC youth-led assessment from 2023 shows that restricted access to education and prohibitions against employment leave over 90% of Rohingya youth without income-generating activities, preventing their productive engagement and increasing dependency on humanitarian aid, which sustains 92% of households, as reported by MSNA 2023. For the few who do find work, they often rely on unstable, informal labour with little job security. This has forced many into adverse coping mechanisms—including early marriage, drug use, unsafe migration, and involvement in criminal activities. Without pathways to economic self-reliance, young people face pressure to meet basic needs for themselves and their families, often with few viable options. Limited access to timely and relevant information also hampers their ability to make informed economic decisions, affecting their potential for market participation and livelihood planning.The Market Demand Analysis conducted by BRAC shows that young women face compounded restrictions, including limited mobility, minimal access to skill-building facilities, and heightened social conservatism in Cox's Bazar. Gender-based and cultural expectations significantly limit their career prospects. Youth with disabilities are also highly marginalised and face significant barriers to economic participation. They are less likely to be employed, as workplaces and learning environments rarely provide the necessary accommodations for accessibility. Discriminatory attitudes compound these barriers, limiting opportunities to gain skills or access dignified employment, further isolating them from the community and deepening economic and social disparities.The NRC youth-led assessment shows that the lack of essential skills—such as literacy, numeracy, problem-solving, leadership, teamwork, and language proficiency—has significantly reduced employability among youth, as noted by local business owners. The assessment also shows that the gap in skills development is accompanied by a high need for psychosocial support, as widespread anxiety, stress, and frustration drive many young people towards negative coping mechanisms.The crisis disrupts youth transitions to adulthood, limiting their education, employment opportunities, and participation in community-based social structures. Youth engagement in shaping and implementing humanitarian programmes remains minimal and often tokenistic, further alienating young people from meaningful roles, according to NRC’s 2022 youth-led assessment. With limited interventions promoting vocational skills and dignified employment, the gaps in developmental pathways present a society-wide danger, contributing to a growing cohort of youth prone to frustration, which can heighten risks related to protection.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

NRC ensures that learners can claim their right to socioeconomic participation regardless of gender, background, or abilities, with physical and emotional safety prioritised so they can learn and thrive. Protecting learners from harm or abuse is a vital part of NRC’s accountability. Physical and emotional safety is recognised as a prerequisite for learning, making it fundamental to NRC’s response in crisis-affected settings. The project aligns with core protection objectives, incorporating preventive strategies to mitigate risks of negative coping mechanisms by promoting meaningful, structured engagement in a safe and inclusive learning environment. NRC is committed to mainstreaming essential protection principles throughout the project lifecycle through the consistent integration of its Safe and Inclusive Programme (SIP)—focusing on safety, dignity, 'do no harm,' equitable access, accountability, and active participation. This includes the integration of inclusive approaches, prioritising individuals with disabilities, female learners, and gender-diverse groups to promote the participation of adolescent girls and other marginalised groups. The Child and Adolescent Friendly Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) ensures that beneficiaries’ perspectives shape interventions and programme delivery. To enhance the protective elements of its programming, NRC places strong emphasis on capacity-building within communities, fostering active participation to create safer learning environments. The initiative also includes robust training for volunteers, particularly on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Gender Policy, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding, underscoring NRC’s commitment to ethical, safe, and transparent operations. This structured approach ensures that young learners access services in secure, inclusive settings, reinforcing NRC’s dedication to advancing protection outcomes in complex humanitarian contexts.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Vulnerable groups face significant barriers to accessing services, with restrictions particularly impacting gender-diverse groups, persons with disabilities (PWD), and over-aged youth not in employment, education, or training (NEET). In alignment with NRC’s commitment to inclusive action, this initiative prioritises a holistic approach to age and disability inclusion, ensuring all learners, regardless of physical abilities or age, have equitable access to safe, quality skills development and support services. Recognising the compounded vulnerabilities faced by PWD, the project is designed to adapt its environment, approaches, and materials to be accessible to all, integrating principles from the Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach. This includes modifications to learning spaces to accommodate those with mobility challenges and the provision of assistive devices to support visual or auditory impairments, ensuring an inclusive environment that respects the dignity and rights of all learners. Training for volunteer instructors on inclusive teaching practices will further enhance access and retention. Through these efforts, NRC aims to mitigate barriers that prevent learners from reaching their full potential, contributing to resilience-building among youth who are often left behind due to age or disability. Additionally, NRC incorporates sex, age, and disability-disaggregated data and uses Washington Group Questions in its activities and reporting, ensuring that programme impact is measured and that services are responsive to the specific needs of all learners.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

NRC prioritises accountability to affected populations across its programmes, ensuring that the voices of refugee and host community adolescents and youth influence programme design, implementation, and evaluation. As a key member, NRC actively participates in the Accountability to Affected Population Working Group (AAP WG), attending coordination meetings regularly. We are also part of a specialised working group focused on establishing minimum standards for CFRM.To operationalise this commitment, NRC conducted two youth-led assessments to ensure that their needs and priorities are integrated into programme design. As a co-lead of the Youth Working Group, NRC supported the inclusion of a dedicated youth assessment in the Inter-Sector Needs Assessment, amplifying young people’s voices and ensuring their perspectives are reflected in broader strategic planning .NRC’s Child and Adolescent-Friendly Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is an inclusive platform that enables the target population to voice their concerns, provide feedback, and influence decision-making. The CFM integrates protection principles by ensuring accessibility, confidentiality, and response timeliness, allowing NRC to respond effectively. Additionally, Community Support Groups (CSG) will engage youth and community leaders in participatory assessments, establishing channels of accountability that reflect local priorities and cultural contexts. A robust Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEL) system enables NRC to assess progress against intended outcomes, maintain high standards of quality, and implement tools that capture feedback. The system will gather valuable insights on effectiveness and identify areas for improvement, ensuring that interventions remain responsive to the evolving needs. By empowering project participants and establishing mechanisms for participation, NRC aims to build trust and enable young people to become active contributors in shaping services.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC is fully committed to integrating the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) across all levels of its programming, with a particular focus on creating safe learning spaces for Rohingya and host community youth. As a member of the PSEA network, NRC actively participates in the Cox’s Bazar-based PSEA network, attending coordination meetings regularly. We are also involved in a specialised working group dedicated to developing child-friendly PSEA. NRC aligns its messaging, uses IEC materials, and submits regular reports to enhance the safety and integrity of all NRC-supported environments. This project implements a comprehensive PSEA strategy, starting with mandatory training for all NRC staff, volunteers, and partners on ethical conduct, the Safeguarding policy, that includes PSEAH, and reporting procedures, in line with NRC’s Code of Conduct. It also includes awareness sessions for adolescents and youth, creating a safe environment where they can learn about their rights, the acceptable and unacceptable behaviour of NRC staff, volunteers, and partners, and the reporting channels available to them. Child-friendly reporting mechanisms are established to ensure accessibility and promote a culture of transparency, particularly within learning spaces. By embedding PSEA principles throughout programme activities, NRC upholds a zero-tolerance policy towards exploitation and abuse, fostering a foundation of trust, accountability, and respect for the rights of learners.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Recognising the increasing impact of climate change, especially on vulnerable populations in Cox’s Bazar, NRC’s programme integrates both climate adaptation and mitigation measures, with a particular focus on raising awareness among youth and equipping them with practical skills for resilience. This includes awareness-raising on environmental responsibility, climate risks, and sustainable practices, designed to promote climate-conscious attitudes and behaviours. NRC is committed to utilising eco-friendly, durable materials in learning facilities and promoting sustainable infrastructure that minimises environmental impact whenever appropriate. As part of its Life Skills programme, community-based projects led by youth will incorporate activities that contribute to the Climate Action strategy. Through these efforts, the programme will equip young people with the skills, knowledge, and attitudes necessary to lead advocacy initiatives and become active agents of environmental protection within their communities.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

NRC is an active member of the Emergency Response and Preparedness Working Group (ERPWG) and remains fully committed to contributing to interagency disaster preparedness and emergency response efforts. NRC’s emergency response team is pivotal in providing capacity-building training for both staff and volunteers, equipping them with enhanced preparedness and response capabilities. The project incorporates Emergency Response and Preparedness (EPR) measures to ensure a rapid and coordinated response. A business continuity plan is in place, outlining organisational readiness to respond to critical disruptions and guiding the response team through essential steps to address the critical needs of people affected by natural and human-made crises. Furthermore, contingency funds are allocated to address the life-saving needs of affected individuals, allowing for immediate access to resources if demands increase. These EPR components collectively enhance the project’s resilience, enabling it to adapt and continue providing aid effectively, even in challenging or rapidly evolving situations.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

NRC's interventions are grounded in the principles of Youth Positive Development and Youth Well-being, designed to adopt a comprehensive approach that caters to the diverse needs of young people. Informed by NRC’s Youth-Wellbeing Framework, the programme aims to create an enabling environment for personal, emotional, and social development through the provision of integrated services. Recognising adolescents and young people as essential agents of change within their communities, NRC is committed to advancing the five key actions outlined under the Compact for Young People. Furthermore, NRC’s emphasis on gender-responsive programming ensures that adolescent girls, who often face heightened restrictions, are actively involved and supported in leadership roles. By fostering a safe, inclusive environment, this initiative encourages youth to take ownership of their learning and development, bridging the gap between educational attainment and social contribution. In doing so, NRC reinforces the importance of youth agency, empowerment, and resilience-building, aligning with the broader humanitarian goal of nurturing future leaders and creating sustainable pathways for young people in displacement settings. Additionally, NRC co-leads a cross-sectoral Youth Working Group (YWG), a platform designed to elevate and address the needs and priorities of youth within humanitarian settings. This YWG is a collaborative effort involving various humanitarian stakeholders, including local and international NGOs, government representatives, youth-led organisations, and community groups, to ensure that youth voices are integral to decision-making processes. By co-leading this group, NRC can advocate for youth-centred approaches across multiple sectors including education, protection, and health—ensuring that young people’s unique challenges and aspirations are systematically reflected in programme designs, policies, and service delivery frameworks.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project unlocks the potential of marginalised youth, providing them with practical skills, knowledge, and tools to enter the workforce. By focusing on both technical and life skills, youth can enter income-generating roles, succeed and grow within them. This support will help youth turn their skills into practical experience—through apprenticeships, internships, and hands-on training—which lays a path towards sustainable livelihoods and self-reliance. Youth will gain a sense of agency, meeting both their personal needs and contributing economically to their families. This contributes to a stronger, community-wide resilience as they begin to see themselves as active contributors rather than dependents. The project prioritises life skills such as communication and problem-solving, critical for personal resilience and professional success. These skills empower them to navigate daily challenges, build relationships, and enhance long-term employability.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

This initiative is designed to address challenges faced by youth who lack access to economic empowerment, contributing to the objectives of the Development and Growth Priorities and JRP. Recognising the lag on developmental indicators in Cox’s Bazar, the project seeks to mobilise resources to equip young people with the knowledge, skills and tools necessary to promote participation in the socioeconomic development. By implementing tailored support, the project will provide assistance with a focus on youth not in employment, education or training. NRC will collaborate closely with the government and other actors to create diversified livelihood opportunities for learners across different sectors. The initiative will also work to enhance the employability of youth by offering relevant skill-building programmes and internships. It aims to strengthen local partnerships, fostering a sustainable ecosystem that empowers youth to become key contributors to the district’s long-term development.
