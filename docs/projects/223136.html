Project Summary

More than 14 years working tenure in Cox’s Bazar humanitarian context, HI has been providing specialized services and support to improve the protection environment and living conditions of the most vulnerable population with increased protection risks among the refugees and host community population by giving specially focus to persons with disabilities, children with disabilities and elderly people. The main objective of this JRP project 2025 is to ensure quality and timely access for people with disabilities and impairments and elderly people through HI’s provision of direct services including Protection and mental Health/Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) while empowering communities and enhancing the community-based protection and psychosocial structures capacities in camps and host communities. This JRP project will be directly implemented by HI and by its two operational partners, the Center for Disability in Development (CDD) and Protibondhi Narider Jatio Parisad (NCDW) as well as funding support from BPRM, DFAT, GFFO, FCDO and ECHO. Disability identification, Inclusive case management (Identification, Assessment & referral-IAR), referral support, community awareness, and capacity building of relevant actors are the major broader activities of this JRP project.
HI will continue to provide individual life-saving protection assistance by safely identifying, assessment & referring individuals being most at-risk or survivors of violence, including women & girls with disabilities. HI will also disseminate essential information on rights- including rights of persons with disabilities, social barriers and stigma related to mental health, key protection messages on topics such as family separation, human trafficking, early marriage & violence against persons with disabilities as well as any other community-identified protection concerns.
Besides, HI will review consortium partners' protection monitoring tools considering the DGA marker to facilitate gender and disability inclusion in humanitarian actions taken by the partners. HI will contribute to the Protection sector Joint protection monitoring reports – including incidents collected by HI.
In addition, HI will organize inclusive sports and recreational sessions in different forms at both camps and host-community levels for the children with and without disabilities to increase their physical and psychological well-being and social cohesion through actively participating in those events with other children. HI will also have focus on training for humanitarian workers of different humanitarian partners on different topics to promote disability inclusive humanitarian actions, those are- Disability Inclusion in Humanitarian Action, Disability, Gender and Age intersectionality, Vulnerability factors for persons with disability to Protection risks, data collection on disability and identification of persons with disabilities (using the Washington Group Questions Set), informed consent, establishment of knowledge platform, Sector analysis on disability Inclusion, IEC Materials development, RAAL LAB Workshop and inclusive communication with persons with disabilities, as well as working with caregivers.
HI will conduct Quarterly perception surveys on peace and security. Develop and implement communication strategies to prevent violence and recruit and support affected refugees and communities through regular activities in community centers, refugee community groups and committees, youth groups, women groups, etc. Response to protection needs through posters with information on where to get protection assistance, including MHPSS. HI will organize sports for peace activities, mainstream peace and security messages in the Sport for Protection curriculum for sports coaches and other recreational activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Over seventh year since their mass displacement from Myanmar, over 1,003,394 Rohingya refugees reside in Cox’s Bazar with 51% female, 52% children, 4% elderly &12% persons with disabilities. Following the Protection Sector-REACH study, 2021, people with disabilities & specific needs in both camps & host communities face the risks disproportionally compared to the rest of the population, given the fact that intersectionality of factors (gender, age, disability, ethnicity, religion etc.) lead to further exclusion, discrimination, hence increase vulnerabilities. Following the ISNA-2024, the most significant protection risk perceived by 61% of refugees was crime and violence, this includes violent & criminal activities led by organized groups which are becoming increasingly common. In 2023, the J-MSNA revealed that 48% of households had listed crime and violence as a protection concern &25% expressed concern about a family member joining criminal groups. This year, the number of households perceiving protection risks increased by over 10%, and those concerned about individuals joining organized groups doubled to 21%. According to Volunteer Risk Mapping report developed by the Sector, participation of beneficiaries in sector activities dropped by 60-70%, particularly amongst boys and young men as response to threats and intimidation by organized groups during May and June. Over the past six months, the most significant security concern reported by refugees (51%) was theft. The presence of organized groups and lack of lighting in some areas of the camp leave shelters and facilities prone to robbery. Besides, the presence of trafficking/smuggling groups was raised by 17% of the households as a security concern. According to the ATWG data, in quarter 3, 86 individuals were identified as victims of trafficking, including 52 men who were trafficked for forced labor and 21 women. In the ISNA Report, approximately one-third (33%) of households reported that at least one member showed symptoms of psychological distress. This trend is consistent with findings from the previous J-MSNA, where over one-third (38%) of households reported similar symptoms of distress among their members. According to ISNA-2024, 12% population is living with disabilities in Rohingya refugee camps &79.21% of Persons with Disabilities experience challenges in accessing humanitarian assistance. A report by the IOM (2021) specifically highlights women, adolescent girls, individuals with disabilities, and caregivers as the most vulnerable demographic groups in such circumstances. Lack of lighting in the community was explicitly identified as a critical concern, with 57% of respondents mentioning it as a major risk that includes accounts of women -including women with disabilities-facing sexual harassment and fearing kidnapping or sexual assault due to the lack of lighting. Women & girls, particularly those living with disabilities & Hijras, are vulnerable to physical, psychological, sexual harassment & abuse. HI will undertake specific measures to meet the unique needs considering the gender and disability sensitivity and transformation, those are- All staff of HI and partners are trained on Gender Equality, Disability Inclusion; Periodic review (internal) of Disability, Gender and Age Marker conducted and derive action points for progressively improve intervention modalities towards more disability inclusive and gender- and age- responsive and transformative when possible; Gender-responsive M&E is in place that includes gender, age and disability disaggregated data of beneficiaries of the services; Percentage of women/girls with disability who participated in the project’s services/activities; and Number of accessible messages/information developed and disseminated to reach persons with disabilities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The program incorporates protection principles throughout all the phases including the design & inception phases. Starting from the design phase HI has carried out protection needs assessment through community consultations analysis of data from ongoing projects & analysis of specific feedback received through accountability mechanisms. Consultation & dialogue with communities including women & girls with & without disabilities have been ongoing in the previous phases of the program & will continue throughout continued implementation to promote active participation of affected populations.Additionally, Staff and volunteers will also be trained on Protection Mainstreaming Principles including data protection, confidentiality, and “Do no Harm” principles. HI Project Staff are in daily contact with HI beneficiaries through routine activities & more importantly at a home-based level. Those activities allow for collecting feedback about services received or any updates regarding the camp situation.This year proposed project also be implemented jointly with the Center for Disability in Development (CDD).

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will ensure the segregation of data per age, gender and diversity in all activities and ensure gender equality through the participation and representation of an adequate number of women and men in all the training, awareness activity and vulnerability capacity assessment exercises. Ensuring all personnel are trained on disability, gender and age inclusion and accountable to HI’s Code of Conduct. This includes staff, partners, and stakeholders including consultants, suppliers, etc. The project will collect and analyze sex, age, and disability disaggregated data for all types of beneficiaries.HI has in-built human resources and technical tools dedicated to identifying the specific needs and capacities of people from different groups and diversities. HI strongly follow the DGA Policy Guidelines and DGA Marker in every project. For disability identification, HI uses WGQ (Washington Group Short Set of Disability Questions) and CFM (Child Functioning Module) tools. HI provides orientations on the tools for all its staff and partners to include people with disabilities in their services and facilities. HI conducts identification exercises house to house in all 33 camps by trained rehabilitation professionals to identify and assess people with disabilities. This data will be shared with UNHCR for updating its progress database to give access and reliable information to all humanitarian actors.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HI places beneficiary participation, feedback, & community empowerment at the core of its interventions, from design to implementation. Meaningful participation is one of the pillars of HI Disability, Gender & Age Institutional policy, as meaningful involvement of boys, girls, women & and men of different age groups, including people with different disabilities, in the design, implementation, monitoring & evaluation of actions or policies affecting them is key in the activity implementation to ensure the relevance of it, & the acceptance by the Rohingyas community which the services are designed for & delivered to.HI maintains a comprehensive process throughout the project to ensure effective engagement & participation of beneficiaries, as well as accountability towards them: the necessary tools, guidance, & delivery of training modules for the project staffs to engage beneficiaries from identification to case closure are developed.The Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM) create communication loops that enable teams to receive feedback from the project participants & to respond in a timely way to their suggestions & concerns. It’s a two-way communications mechanism designed specifically to gather & respond to feedback from the project participants & other community stakeholders. This mechanism includes complaint boxes (in static point) that is accessible, a hotline, an email address, contact of Country PSEA focal point with phone number, all shared to the community to ensure that the access to feedback & complaints mechanism is accessible & inclusive. The diversity of feedback mechanisms is key to ensuring this accessibility to the affected persons, as well as raising awareness about it among the caregivers.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Recognizing the lack of consideration of people with disabilities in existing disaster risk reduction (DRR) planning and implementation in this humanitarian context, HI will deliver specific outputs to sensitize and experience relevant stakeholders to facilitate inclusion of people with disabilities in all disaster response and climate change actions. Technical support will be provided to partners, Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) working group members to give special focus to persons with disabilities, elderly people and other vulnerable people before and during the disaster emergency. HI maintains a preparedness plan and contingency stock to provide emergency support to beneficiaries in need during disaster. For emergency support, HI formed a disaster emergency team at base level to ensure emergency support to vulnerable people in the community. Community awareness sessions on household level IDRR plan will be delivered to people with disabilities and their family members to ensure they are empowered with knowledge of local emergency response plans, evacuation routes, and safe shelters. HI will also conduct community awareness session on environmental protection to sensitize people on 3R. Considering that, field and base officers are gradually being covered by solar energy.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

HI regularly monitors early warning messages from relevant authorities to proactively respond to potential risks. Continuously update the contingency and mitigation plan and take necessary measures accordingly to effectively address risks. Coordinate and collaborate with concerned stakeholders, including the government, to ensure emergency support is provided to persons with disabilities during disasters.Maintain contingency stocks to ensure the availability of essential resources during emergencies. Identify safe and alternative locations in advance to provide services to beneficiaries during disasters, especially in disaster-prone areas. Coordinate and collaborate with relevant organizations and explore more Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Climate Change Adaptation (CCA) funding to better prepare for and mitigate potential risks. Work with different partners, ensure infrastructure accessibility and road safety and adopt inclusive modalities to protect vulnerable populations. Advocate with concerned authorities to make DRR plans inclusive for persons with disabilities and older people to ensure their safety and well-being during emergencies. Adapt project activities based on different scenarios to effectively address risks and mitigate potential impacts.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

HI will reach out to all the beneficiaries irrespective of their age, gender and ability. All the beneficiaries will be well-oriented of this SOP using local languages/audio, pictorials, traditional signs, visual presentations etc. This SOP will be consulted with self-help groups of youth, women, older people and people with disabilities for further modification and changes considering the most appropriateresponse and reporting channels. HI will continue to provide necessary therapeutic interventions, assistive products and reasonable accommodations for the beneficiaries to improve their participation in orientation, consultation and response process. HI will also raise community awareness to minimize the social stigma to facilitate beneficiaries’ participation in feedback mechanism. The caregivers may assist thebeneficiaries during orientation, consultation and response process (especially for people with communication difficulties).

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, our project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees through targeted MHPSS initiatives. We focus on enhancing individual and community coping capacities, fostering emotional resilience, and supporting inclusive activities that empower participants to build supportive networks and manage challenges independently. We recruit community volunteers from the same locality and trained them on different protection topics for their effective engagement with the project. This knowledge will remain in the community as they are from the same locality. Additionally, we also provide technical support and guidance of local actors, Organizations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs), disability inclusion promotion groups (DIPG) and local partners to sustain and localize these efforts.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, HI’s project activities in the host community align with Cox’s Bazar district development priorities by supporting inclusive, sustainable growth. We focus on capacity building of local actors and provide technical support to health sub-sectors, advocating at Cox’s Bazar level on disability inclusion. We promote the inclusion of disability data in the UNHCR progress data sheet, provide rehabilitation services at district and upazila hospitals, and develop partnerships with local and national actors to enhance localization, inclusion, and accessibility.
