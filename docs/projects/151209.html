Project Summary

The aim of the proposed project is to improve the IDP Camps’ management and protection of 21,750 IDPs residing in Mogadishu, Galkaio South, Adado, Dhusamareeb, Hobyo and Abudwak districts and ensure improvement of sites and services together with vocational assistance to IDPs, while focusing on moving toward attaining durable solutions for the IDPs with their full participation and that of the local authorities.
Through improved site management and coordination, the project will strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of the humanitarian assistance in the camps. Site development, care and maintenance would be among the key interventions whose aim is to improve the living conditions of the 21,750 displaced persons. The capacity of the Camp Management and Camp Coordination Committees (CCCMCs) will be strengthened so that the IDPs self-management and access to critical information is improved. One main purpose of the project would be to create, facilitate and support opportunities for the displaced families for them to achieve durable solutions.

Needs

Out of the 4.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, 2.6 million are internally displaced persons who face the highest vulnerability. Galmudug State has many IDPs courtesy of the last drought & people fleeing Al-Shabaab’s forceful children recruitment. Mogadishu is also a home for the majority of IDPs in the country. The majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements in these two target Regions, with living conditions being poor, observed high rates of Gender Based violence, poor basic services delivery as observed by CPD’s Needs Assessment in Mogadishu in June 2018. There are few rights for the IDPs including on the land they settle (lack of land tenure) with forced evictions being a regular feature in the IDP Camps. The IDPs in the two Regional States, especially those in urban centres are faced with the challenges of inadequate site level information on humanitarian services, and protection needs. Moreover, poor beneficiary selection curtails effective service delivery to the IDPs, making them more vulnerable, and increasing protection concerns and risks. The majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Sites are overcrowded and unplanned which increases risks of flood, fire and disease outbreak. These IDP have no access to information about their rights and available services. They not only need access to information but also representation in the community governance structure in order to make an informed decision about how to receive services and potential solutions to displacement. IDPs have limited options for durable solutions to their displacement. The JMCNA findings indicate that the majority of IDPs prefer local integration, however, this has remained a challenge due to lack of land, repeated evictions and minimal livelihoods options.

Activities

Main activities that will be undertaken in order to address the identified above needs include: Strengthening CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterpart Ensuring through CCCM coordination that relevant responders are mobilized towards providing sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable Conducting site planning, verification and maintenance in collaboration with the CCCM committees. Regularly monitoring service delivery at site level to ensure that there are no gaps or duplication of activities and that minimum standards are being met Establish roving site management teams to ensure coordination with community governance structures Identify existing governance structures and focal points for sites  Provide training on site maintenance and site planning to camp committees’ Support community led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees, distribution of tools) Advocate with local authorities for public land for displaced people Conduct safety audits on regular basis in coordination with relevant partners Support relocations to public land when appropriate Mapping of existing community structures Provide training on CCCM principles to community governance structures Provide community spaces are available for management committees to meet Establish/reinforce mechanisms for communication of multi-sector services available per geographical area or in IDP sites in the appropriate language Harmonize feedback mechanisms relevant for sites across all clusters Coordinate/ conduct intention surveys with relevant partners and clusters to ensure the intentions of populations are understood and supported Support communities in IDP sites towards self-reliance in locations where prospects of obtaining land rights is achievable

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Apart from project districts in Banadir region, the protection environment is sufficiently stable to enable durable solutions/resilience in the other districts of Galmudug States where land tenure is not a challenge. In these Galmudug States, the project is linked to the local government's plan to find a durable solution for the displaced communities. The hand-over of the established and trained camp management structures to the camp dwellers is a sustainability component built into the project. The project's proposed cash provisions through cash-for-work to the beneficiaries will be legally recognized as has been before in the target areas/states.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Among the main risks facing the targeted population is their exclusion in terms of targeting and distribution when it comes to humanitarian assistance. The displaced families are often excluded in the targeting and distribution of humanitarian assistance despite them being the most bearers of the brunt of the humanitarian crisis. The other risk they face is exclusion from the decision making process on issues like their eviction and site planning. The project plans to incorporate the camp leadership into the local authorities' decision making organs so that the camp members are consulted whenever decisions that affect their lives are made.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The exclusion risks on gender and age include lack of consultation with the different gender and age groups whenever designing/planing/implementation certain project activities. For instance, modes of cash and tools distribution, who to be registered as the household head, location of distribution, the appropriate time to do the distribution and composition of camp leadership. All these factors will be considered by the project from a protection lens in order to avoid any chance of excluding beneficiaries on the basis of their gender and age.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

There is always the risk of the target beneficiaries to be caught up in armed conflicts and violence as the general security of the country is precarious. Children from these vulnerable groups like IDP families are also at risk of being victims of child soldiering and ideological indoctrination. The mitigation measures that the project will employ is first the choice of distribution locations. The project will ensure that distribution of tools/cash will be done in the safest modes, and locations that are easily accessible to all. Even the distribution time will be one that is in line with the safety of the beneficiaries. The project team in close collaboration with the camp/beneficiary leadership will continuously gather information and monitor any threats of children soldiering and indoctrination in the project areas so that it can be shared with the relevant government authority.
