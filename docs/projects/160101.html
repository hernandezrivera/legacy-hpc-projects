Project Summary

Terre des hommes (Tdh) is the leading Swiss child relief organization and has been operating in Yei River State since 2016, with extension of its education and child protection services to rural Yei (Mugwo and Otogo counties) since 2019. Through its current interventions in education, child protection and WASH, Tdh is aware of the barriers to education that many children face, that range from direct barriers that impact provision of education, such as lack of or non-functional facilities, poorly or untrained teachers, lack of or inappropriate pedagogical materials; and indirect barriers that can impact truancy, drop-outs and educational outcomes, such as household economic vulnerabilities, unsafe WASH facilities increasing exposure to protection risks, lack of school meals, etc.
Tdh has been working both in schools and through community-based programming, such as CFS, since 2014, with the overarching goal of helping children reenter formal education following displacement and conflict. Tdh works to improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable children in an effective and sustainable manner, by providing direct services aiming at preventing and responding to cases of abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence; as well as by strengthening the resilience of children and communities through a holistic and child-centered approach, considering children’s intersectoral needs in education, protection, and WASH. To improve access to quality education and contribute to the improvement of the child protection outcomes, including a focus on improving children’s sense of security and self-confidence, Tdh has embraced a holistic approach to programming through interventions in schools.
Needs assessments in and around Yei town have demonstrated a clear need for the provision of integrated WASH and Education programming in order to address gaps inside schools. In Yei town, as well as in rural Yei (Mugwo and Otogo), Tdh will provide essential support to 16 schools, including 6 schools in rural Yei, through the construction / rehabilitation of 5 temporary learning spaces; as well as the procurement and provision of essential education in emergencies pipeline supplies for children aged 3-17.
In addition, Tdh will implement the “Blue Schools approach” in targeted schools to improve access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services. This approach, developed in consortium with other organizations, will combine the rehabilitation/construction of water supply, sanitation and hygiene infrastructures with longer-term learning objectives on hygiene and nutrition promotion. By focusing on the creation of school gardens inside schools, Tdh will promote environmentally friendly agricultural practices, share technological best practices and support the diversification of teaching methods.
Tdh will ensure that CP is mainstreamed in all activities provided at the targeted schools, including by establishing/maintaining help desks in order to effectively address and refer child protection concerns. In addition, Tdh will provide relevant trainings on CPiE, GBV and PSS/PFA to teachers and teaching volunteers, including for the development of emergency life-saving skills and life-sustaining skills.
Given its longstanding presence in Yei River State, Tdh benefits from favorable acceptance from the communities and has established strong relationships with local authorities, officials and state ministries, in particular the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare and the Ministry of General Education and Instruction. Tdh also collaborates closely with other international and local actors in the targeted areas, including through joint needs assessment missions, to ensure complementary action. In Mugwo and Otogo counties, Tdh is one of the only operational actors delivering Education, Child Protection in Emergency and WASH services to hard-to-reach highly vulnerable children.

1 - Needs

The breakout of violence in South Sudan since 2013 spread to the Equatorial states, including Yei River State, in 2016. Human rights violations have been widely reported, and high numbers of civilians, including children, have been forced to flee their houses and villages as a result of armed conflict. Access to education has been particularly impacted by conflict-related disruption of services. According to UNMISS, several schools were occupied by Government forces from January to April 2019, notably in Karpeto, Kergulu, Morsak and Yappa. Furnitures have been looted. Virtually all teachers in the area fled and only a few returned. In areas where the population has been able to return, communities made provisional arrangements to start schooling, often in improvised temporary structures, with minimum materials and lack of trained teachers. Another major factor constraining education is the reported refusal by the government to provide incentives to teacher in so-called “rebel-held” areas. Also, in these remote areas, communities are said to have moved education institutions away from easier-access roadsides to deeper locations in the countryside in order to escape the indiscriminate violence meted out by armed forces. This limited access to schools for a lot of children as well as for aid actors such as Tdh. In Yei town, the lack of teaching supplies, inconsistency in payment of teachers’ incentives, non-availability of school feeding, lack of appropriate learning structures and the overall insecurity are fueling education needs. It is estimated that only a third of internally displaced children in Yei town attend school. According to a Tdh assessment conducted in June 2019, the proportion of out-of-school children was particularly high in areas with high numbers of recently arrived IDPs, notably Mahad (66%), ECS (54%), Gezira (42%) and Jigomomi (35%). To accommodate IDP and returnee learners, the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) encounters difficulty when planning to expand the construction of classrooms and WASH infrastructures, in particular related to a lack of space in school compounds. According to Tdh assessment carried out in schools within Yei town: 97% of the surveyed schools provided “no service” for safe drinking water; 32% had “limited” or “no sanitation services”; and 27% of the surveyed schools had “limited” or “no service” for hand washing . In rural Yei, following three inter-agency needs assessments in Tore, Ombasi and Wudabi in June, July and August 2019; high needs were identified although the security situation did not allow Tdh safe access to operate in these areas. Tdh conducted another assessment, in Mugwo and Otogo counties, in August 2019. The assessment demonstrated high needs in provision of education materials in emergency, low attendance rates and lack of available and/or trained teachers. Tdh decided to target 6 schools in the area, in coordination with other actors on the ground, to provide comprehensive EiE, CPiE and WASH support. In both locations, only 37% of the adolescents in upper primary are girls. Access to education for girls and adolescent girls in and around Yei town remains a critical issue, heavily restrained by social and cultural norms, including in relation to menstrual hygiene management as well as to the practice of child and forced marriage upon reaching puberty. In addition, family separation and enrollment into armed groups left considerable numbers of young women as head of households, increasing their vulnerability and hindering their access to services, in particular education. Tdh may extend services in 2020 to other locations in Tore, Ombasi and Wudabi, upon improvement of the security situation and humanitarian access.

2 - Activities

In 2020, Tdh will continue to provide integrated CP, Education and WASH programming in order to comprehensively respond to the protection, education, health and hygiene needs of children. Tdh will target 16 schools, including 10 schools inside Yei town and 6 outside Yei. - Rehabilitation/construction of 5 temporary learning spaces and 20 classrooms in line with INEE minimum standards and MoGEI guidelines; including 2 temporary learning spaces inside Yei town and 3 in rural Yei. - Assessment of teaching material needs. Procurement and provision of essential education in emergencies pipeline supplies, including teaching-learning materials and recreational materials for 5,800 (3,200 M; 2,600 F) children aged 6-17 . - Capacity building of 160 (112 M; 48 F) teachers and teaching volunteers on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies. - Training of 160 (112 M; 48 F) teachers and teaching volunteers, and 140 (84 M; 56 F) key community members on CPiE, GBV and referral pathways, including trainings specific to UASC and CAFAAG. - Provision of training on PSS/PFA to 160 (112 M; 48 F) teachers and teaching volunteers, and 140 community members (84 M; 56 F), including on PSS activities related to emergency life-saving skills, life sustaining messages and disease preventing messaging. - Awareness-raising and community mobilization on go back to school campaign, reaching 5,000 community members (2,500F; 2,500M). - Identification of teachers outside Yei to receive monthly payment allowance according to education cluster guidelines. Furthermore, Tdh will ensure equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDP, host community and returnee children through the roll-out of its “Blue School” strategy in targeted schools. - Rehabilitation/construction and/or operation (including water testing) and maintenance of water supply infrastructures in 16 “blue schools” providing safe drinking water for 6,200 children aged 3-17 (3,410 M; 2,790 F). - Construction/rehabilitation and/or operation and maintenance of secured and gender appropriate composting or simple pit sanitation facilities for 6,200 children aged 3-17 (3,410 M; 2,790 F). - Rehabilitation/construction and/or operation and maintenance of 30 group hand-washing facilities, including provision of spare parts and cleaning materials. - Reactivation/establishment of 16 school water management committees (WMC). Training sessions on O&M of the water supply infrastructures, sanitation and hand-washing facilities are provided to 800 individuals (400 M; 400 F), including members of WMC and school administration; ministry representatives, community hand-pump mechanics, etc. - Awareness raising on water management, hygiene and health promotion to 6,200 children (3,410 M; 2,790 F) through the Blue School catalogue of practical exercises. - Provide dignity kits and awareness raising on menstrual hygiene management (MHM) to 800 adolescent girls aged 13-18, including training on how to create reusable sanitary pads. Furthermore, Tdh will promote good practices in regard to water management, food production and solid waste management, through school garden activities, including the provision of seeds and tools, theoretical and practical trainings on sanitation, planting and harvesting. 16 student-led gardeners clubs will be established and will be trained along with school agriculture teachers on efficient management of water, water/soil conservation and farming techniques. Tdh will also ensure that CP is mainstreamed throughout the activities, by training teachers on basic CPiE and referral pathways and setting up or maintaining help desks in schools. Finally, Tdh will continue to engage with all relevant actors, clusters and working groups, and will continue to assess needs in and around Yei town, including through inter-agency assessments.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Focus group discussions are vital for the implementation of protection activities, these are therefore carried out across various stakeholders, as detailed in the section on AAP, in order to ensure that displacement-affected communities, whether host or IDP can access services which they have contributed to develop. Development and improvement occur through regular and ongoing consultation. Tdh has extended its consultation group by extending to un-serviced rural locations and thus integrates broader perspectives into its implementing strategy.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Throughout the planning and implementation of the project, Tdh will coordinate its activities with Yei River State (YRS) Ministry of General Education and Instruction; YRS Ministry of Cooperatives, Rural Water and Sanitation and YRS Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare. Furthermore, it will seek to enhance the capacity of SMCs/PTAs through training on PSS, as well as on school management, monitoring and record keeping. In addition, it will provide support to school water management committees to operate and maintain safe water, sanitation and hand washing infrastructures.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project focuses on Education, CP mainstreaming and provision of WASH in schools. For this matter, it will look at Education, CP and WASH nationals, in particular: - South Sudan General Education Strategic Plan 2017-2022.- South Sudan Child Act No. 10 of 2008; - South Sudan Strategic National Action Plan on the Prevention of Child Marriage 2017-2030; Furthermore, Blue Schools efforts and all activities carried out by Tdh will aim to support the roll out of the National Education Curriculum, and will be conducted further to bilateral consultations with state authorities to ensure alignment with MoEI, MoGSCW and MoWI’s specific objectives. Furthermore, the project is aligned with the National Refugee Return Framework (aware of its redrafting) in view of its recognition of the possibility of supporting returnees as a beneficiary group due to non-discrimination on the community of origin of children. Vulnerability criteria are applied across host and hosted alike, refugees, returnees would be catered to accordingly.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

While there is a constant growth in cautious optimism in the country, Tdh is well aware of the sheer volatility and proximity of conflict, particularly for Yei - geographically speaking. As for Yei, the state is a peculiar and yet literal representation of the humanitarian development nexus. While there is increased stability and access in certain locations, the population remains vigilant due to ongoing clashes outside the town between non-signatories and government forces. Tdh is of the opinion that the programming it rolls out needs to straddle the reality, pave the way for future implementation and yet maintain its capacity to respond to shocks be they brief or small, or protracted and of a larger scale. The outlook changed drastically throughout 2019, particularly around Yei where, as of April 2019, increased movement was a possibility - this has allowed for extended remit of planning and a notably palpable sense of stability in certain locations. The patchwork of the area outside Yei town nevertheless proves to be tedious and open to stark and sudden changes, or slow onset of renewed conflict should all collapse. Tdh has sufficient reason to believe that the Protection environment has the potential to be safer and more stable.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes the handover of rehabilitated/constructed temporary learning spaces, furniture, learning-teaching materials and other items to the school administrations, the community and the authorities. Furthermore, Tdh will promote the sustainability of its activities through the provision of relevant trainings to teachers and teaching volunteers, including on EiE, CPiE, GBV and PSS, including for emergency lifesaving skills and hygiene promotion. In addition, the project will contribute to safe water, sanitation and hand washing sustainable operation and maintenance through the training of children involved in the school water management committee, as well as members of the school administration and relevant community members.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Few specific risks have been selected since Tdh attempts to plan its intervention aiming to minimise the multitude of risks above through varied consultation with multiple actors. Nevertheless, particularly in the context of Yei, matters related to high incidence of GBV, child safeguarding and its impact on the communities in and outside town is extensive (as can be corroborated by a recent PSEA task force risk assessment exercise). Due to a limited presence of actors in town, limited access to services and possibility of redress, prevailing cultural norms and social and anthropological factors the impact of GBV and child safeguarding risks is high for both the population of concern, beneficiaries and national staff of Tdh (and other organisations). This has led the organisation to prioritise Gender and Diversity mainstreamining components across the whole country program thanks to direct support from Headquarters. By putting explicit focus on the matter, Tdh has shifted to dedicating clearly defined time for programme staff to undergo and roll out training around PSEA and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms internally and externally. This has supported in the hand-in-hand roll out of the complaints and feedback mechanisms available to beneficiaries and staff alike and will continue to be an area of regular refresh to counter the turnover of staff and beneficiaries. As for data protection, Tdh seeks to operate within the EU's GDPR regulation and thus increase efforts around encryption of lap tops and extend use of CPIMS and IM tools.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, through: - Surveys and FGD with specific age groups, women, men, students, youth out of school and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Quarterly focus group discussions will be conducted with children and communities to assess child protection knowledge, attitudes and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with participatory assessment. - Tdh will maintain and raise awareness on its Complaints, Feedback, and Response Mechanism. This mechanism will follow Tdh guidelines, ensuring multiple methods for submitting complaints and receiving responses, including CFRM boxes in schools and safe spaces, as well as through static helpdesks. Where applicable, this CFRM will be adapted to fit with existing Community-Based Feedback Mechanisms already used in the location.

This will be considered for future use.
