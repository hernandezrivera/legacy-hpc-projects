Project Summary

This project will address education cluster needs and gaps across three vulnerable and conflict-affected local government areas of Yobe State: Bade, Geidam and Yunusari LGAs. It will build on COCOSOHDI's ongoing ECW funded education project in Bade, and the National Home-Grown School Feeding Programme in Yunusari and Geidam LGAs. COCOSOHDI has a long-term established presence in the targeted LGAs. COCOSOHDI is an experienced organization with an proven expertise in implementing educational interventions. We are currently the focal point of the Education in Emergency sub-sector working group for Gujba LGA, Yobe state; coordinating activities of education partners including the local government education authority and other national and international non-governmental organizations.
The proposed project will support in providing teaching materials to teachers and learners will be provided with scholastic materials. Capacity building support will be provided to teachers/volunteers to provide quality foundational literacy, numeracy and SEL/PSS skills to learners using the Accelerated Basic Education Programme Curriculum (ABEP Curriculum). To continue to ensure the provision of safe, protective, and inclusive learning environment for the target beneficiaries, there will be a construction of Temporary Learning Spaces, boys and girls will also be provided with dignity kits. The proposed project will support in providing access to safe and inclusive quality learning opportunities for vulnerable and conflict affected children between the ages of 3 – 17 years in Bade, Yunusari and Geidam LGAs of Yobe state. The project will support Out-of-school (OOSC) children through re-enrolment into formal school, non-formal education intervention for Children who are Out-of-school for a long time and literacy and numeracy skills will be provided. The project will support Accelerated Learning Programs (ALPs) to ensure continuity of education and create transition pathways from non-formal to formal education for vulnerable girls and boys.
Additionally, COCOSOHDI will establish/strengthen and train community structures such as the Community Education Committees (CECs)/Center Based Management Committees (CBMCs) for ALPs and School Based Management Committees/Parent Teachers Association (PTA) for formal schools on the development and management of child-focused School Improvement Plan, Resource Mobilization and Management skills and Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Safeguarding and Service Referral Pathways. The community mechanism (CECs/CBMCs and SBMCs/PTA) will be supported to design and implement child-centered School Improvement Plans in formal schools (SIPs) and Community Action Plans (CAPs) at the community level. To ensure accountability to the affected populations, COCOSOHDI would establish/strengthen Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms in both schools and communities of implementations and build the capacities of the community on how to make use of it.
During the 12 months proposed project implementation period, the project has a target of 1,978 direct beneficiaries; this includes 1,100 children and adolescents, 27 formal school teachers including teachers from Integrated Qur'anic Schools, 6 volunteer teachers for ALP centers, 45 SBMCs/PTA, and CBMCs/CECs. 800 community members will be directly reached through awareness raising and enrollment drive. 60% of target children, adolescents and community will be girls and women and 10% of the target beneficiaries will be people living with disabilities; the Washington disability questions will be utilized to ensure children and adolescents living with disability at all levels are included in the intervention. The project will also ensure the prioritization of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and 50% host community members and returnees.

1 - Needs

In 2022, an estimated 1.3 million people in NE Nigeria are in need of education services, 1 million of whom are targeted in the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The risks children face is high, and include school drop-out, SGBV, child marriage, child labour, recruitment and exploitation. 30% of schools were unable to enroll some children in the last school year 2020-2021. Of those enrolled only 21% attend school every day and around 25% come just around a quarter of the time. Reasons related to poverty (such as afford learning materials, school fees, and work-related reasons) and educational value to parents are frequently linked to the missing school or drop-out. According to United Nations Children Education Fund (UNICEF) estimates, Nigeria has over 10.5 million out of school children aged 5-14 years, majority of which are in the North – East with Yobe State having an estimated 658,770 children out of school. Data from Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) (2017) shows that 42.2 % of children of primary school age in Yobe State are out of school. The situation has not significantly changed as of June, 2022 (2022 Yobe Educational Situational Analysis Report). COCOSOHDI is a member of the 2021 Joint Education Need Assessment Working Group (AWG-JENA, 2021) and findings from the report (unpublished) shows that almost half of the schools are still closed because of the 2012 emergency - In North east, 339 working schools against 305 closed and 3 in an unknown situation. Girls marginally outnumber boys at the early childhood school level through to JSS (54% of total enrolment), but, this pattern reverses at SSS Level for Yobe. Additionally, one in four schools were sharing infrastructure, typically with schools from conflict-affected communities which had moved to safer or more accessible locations (for example, those in Yunusari are moving to Geidam and Bade). This situation increases the number of students per classroom and per teacher, causing a decrease in the quality of education. The average number of students per classroom in BAY states is 124, very high, considering also that just 60% of teachers have some form of qualification and 14% are volunteers. Around 90% of schools reported that not all their students have sufficient learning materials. Overall, some and half of their students have learning materials. Finally, in September 2021, COCOSOHDI has conducted an education-specific needs assessments in Bade and Geidam LGAs covering 10 communities. These assessments included the communities targeted for this project, and sought to identify specific gaps and needs impacting child wellbeing. The assessments included Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) with stakeholders, community surveys, community mapping and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with boys, girls and parents. In all the communities assessed, high numbers of children in need of education and protection support were identified. The assessments also highlighted high numbers of children being out of school, mainly due to financial barriers and pressure on children to help support their families to generate income. In some communities, girls were more likely than boys to be out of schools and exposed to protection risks due to traditional gender roles and attitudes.

2 - Activities

On the proposed project, COCOSOHDI will implement the following key activities throughout the project life time; Activity 1.1.1 Identify and engage community volunteer teachers for ALP Centers Activity 1.1.2 Provide 6 Community Volunteer Teachers (CVTs) with training on ABEP curriculum, including PSS, identification and referral of vulnerable children Activity 1.1.3 Provide CVTs with Instructional materials and learners with Scholastic materials Activity 1.1.4 Conduct All Inclusive Monitoring of Centers by COCOSOHDI and Local Government Education Authorities Activity 1.1.5 Support CVTs to hold end of program graduation/placement examinations in NFLCs Activity 1.1.6 Provide Learners with transition/mainstreaming scholastic materials Activity 1.1.7 Mainstream/place learners into nearest formal school Activity 2.2.1 Procure and distribute scholastic materials including dignity kits for girls and boys in formal school. Activity 2.3.1 Carry out facilities assessment in targeted schools, including infrastructure needs and safety considerations. Activity 2.3.2 Construct or rehabilitate 2 classrooms and 2 TLS in the 2 selected locations Activity 2.3.3 Equipped classrooms with furniture in formal schools Activity 3.1.1 Strengthen/Establish and train school-based management committees on the roles of committees, safety and protection in schools, and development of child-friendly school Improvement Plans Activity 3.1.2 Support schools’ improvement, safety and resilience plans Activity 3.2.1 Organize training of 45 SBMC/CEC members on resource mobilization, management and safety and security of schools Activity 3.2.2 Monthly review meetings with SBMCs and CECs Activity 3.3.1 Consult with girls and boys to establish safe school and community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms. Activity 3.3.2 Organize awareness raising in schools and communities on the mechanism as well as COCOSOHDI's Safeguarding Policy. Activity 3.3.3 Collect regular feedback and complaints in schools and communities and address them. Activity 4.1.2 Organize peer to peer enrolment and drama activities with girls and boys’ school clubs Activity 4.1.3 Hold awareness raising in communities of implementations, particularly on the importance of education, school safety/security and school attendance retention.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

COCOSOHDI will ensure protection are mainstreamed throughout all our activities to ensure the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries and community members associated with the project. Any unintended consequences of our work will be closely monitored to be able to avoid or prevent harm to the beneficiaries or communities we work. A comprehensive risk assessment will be carried out by COCOSOHDI field M&E officer to identify possible risks, and to develop necessary precautions for successful project management and implementation. This will include specific considerations to vulnerable groups, including people with disabilities, and will include specific consideration of the varied needs of and risks facing different groups of children and other community members. In Northeast Nigeria, women and youth (both boys and girls) are generally missing from the decision-making structures, both at the community level and in high-level institutionalized decision-making processes. There are limited prevention mechanisms and response services to address gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual and reproductive health for women and girls, including psychosocial support. GBV prevention and mitigation strategies are already incorporated into COCOSOHDI's child protection policy and standards, and prevention of GBV will be integrated into the different stages of project implementation. This will include the inclusion of GBV risk reduction activities in community engagement activities and trainings. Safe, confidential referral pathways for reporting GBV and SEA will also be established through the sensitizations of COCOSOHDI's Safeguarding Policies and procedures in all implementation sites. COCOSOHDI will establish a free toll hotline and ensure is accessible to communities to report any incident (with proper visual communication and information). On a monthly basis, a report desk will be put in place during community consultations to allow the reporting for those not familiar using phone calls and feedback shared. COCOSOHDI will provide training on complaints and feedback at the outset for the beneficiaries and the help desk will provide follow up support as needed. All staff involved on this project will go through regular training on safeguarding, Protection on Sexual Exploitations and Abuse (PSEA) and understand the code of conduct to work with children and with accountability towards affected populations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The capacity of the SBMCs and CECs will be built to understand project deliverables and timeline. Monthly coordination and review meetings of the SBMCs/CECs will be attended by COCOSOHDI's Community Mobilization and Complaint Response staff to collect feedback and share progress on satisfaction with the project implementation. Men and women will be equally represented in the SBMCs/CECs. A focal person for CRM for this project will be identified for each committee, and will collect and share feedback and complaints from community members. Focus group discussions will also be held at baseline and endline with girls, boys, men and women, and will provide an opportunity for these groups to share their feedback on the project activities and implementation. Anonymous complaints boxes will also be placed at each project LGAs and communities, and will be checked for feedback bi-weekly by COCOSOHDI CRM staff. This feedback received will then be fed into management decisions and review sessions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate. In addition to programmatic feedback, COCOSOHDI will set up and sensitize communities and schools on clear reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries and community members to report safeguarding or exploitation incidents or concerns. This includes a toll-free line which communities can call to report incidents and safeguarding focal persons in each community.
