Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at ensuring food security for the conflict-affected Syrian populations in NWS with the focus on the IDPs as being among the most vulnerable groups. Within the targeted population, the interventions largely target those that have recently been displaced to areas near the Syrian- Turkish borders due to the latest GoS offensive on Maaret Al Numan and Saraqeb cities in early 2020.
The referred targeted groups were also affected by the floods that happened in January 2021 as well as the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic that largely restricted their ability to find/ sustain livelihoods required to meet their basic live needs. Furthermore, the enormous inflation to the local currency among other conflict- related circumstances also added to the sufferings of the targeted populations.
The proposed intervention aims at addressing around 35,000 IDPs with food baskets that will be designed according to the FSL cluster guidelines. Each of the planned food basket will contain 48 kilograms of uncooked/ canned food items (rice, groats, sugar, chickpeas, date, flour, lentil, peas, canned tuna and mortadella) that is sufficient to reach the nutritional requirements for a family of 5 individuals (Including those for women and children) for a duration of 1 month.

Additionally, Human Appeal Turkey/Syria (HAT) aims at supporting 10,000 beneficiaries as a direct donation from HA, the people most in need with uncooked meat rations that can help addressing their protein nutritional needs through the implementation of the seasonal Qurbani activity.
The humanitarian situation in Syria remains a serious concern, with around 13.4 million people in need of humanitarian aid. According to the World Food Programm, 12.4 million people – nearly 60% of the population – are facing food shortages. The increasing vulnerability of families is further exacerbated by the coronavirus pandemic, which has pushed another 1.4 million people into food insecurity. The crisis is characterised by a lack of protection and continued violations of International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights.

4 - Needs

Ten years into the Syria crisis, humanitarian needs are deepening. Ongoing insecurity and the compounded impact of displacement, combined with worsening socio-economic conditions characterized by sharp currency depreciation, record-level price increases and cuts in subsidized goods have prompted an almost 20 per cent increase in the number of people in need compared to early 2020 - with a disproportionate impact on women and children. As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. https://reliefweb.int/report/syrian-arab-republic/syrian-arab-republic-2021-needs-and-response-summary-february-2021- Widows and orphans always need their feed more than the others because no one lead their family and provide all life needs.- Disabilities, they have needs more than the other because they can't reach their life needs without help.- Floods and storms, it came in January 2021, hundreds of tents destroyed by the rains and floods, so the water destroyed every things in the tent specifically foods and furniture.- Poor people, which they don't have jobs especially with Covid19 epidemic, many people lost their jobs.
