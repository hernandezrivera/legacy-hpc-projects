Project Summary

This action is proposed to support affected children including out of school, those at risk of dropping out and koranic school (children, teachers, parent, school infrastructure, etc.). Main focus will be made on creating and maintaining the protective learning environment for children and teachers, as well as strengthening their knowledge and skills cope with threats and attacks; special focus will also be put on developing and expanding relevant non-formal and informal education opportunities for children and adolescents. Through this action’s support, parents will be at the centre of awareness sessions on the importance of education which will also be applied to support parents and communities in enrolling and retaining their children in school and learning centers.
This action will target children, girls and boys (3–17 years) affected by the protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria, especially in Borno (Askira/Uba, Monguno, and Konduga LGAs), Adamawa (Hong and Michika LGAs). Plan international has strong presence in these locations. And this action will leverage on previous and existing interventions.
Also, this action is designed in line with Northeast EiE Working Group objectives to provide access to inclusive quality basic and vocational skills opportunities to conflict-affected children and adolescents.
Equitable access to safe and protective learning environments shall be facilitated through the Construction of (classrooms, latrines and water points), school facilities built and used by all students, girls and boys, including children living with disabilities. Access to quality alternative education adapted to the needs of girls and boys (3-17 years old) who are out of school because of the crisis shall be reinforced through learning or training activities. Rights of girls and boys shall be protected from immediate and future threats through access to conflict-sensitive education, disaster and conflict risk reduction, strengthening psychosocial coping and protecting schools from attack.

1 - Needs

According to UNOCHA, the number of people in need of urgent assistance in north-east Nigeria rose from 7.9 million at the beginning of 2020 to 10.6 million since the onset of COVID-19 (Humanitarian Needs Overview, UNOCHA, 2020). Of this number, more than 4 million children across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states do not have access to education since the beginning of the pandemic (Nigeria Situation Report, Education Sector Status, UNOCHA, 2nd August, 2020). Also, an estimated 4.3 million people have also become food insecure, up from pre-COVID-19 figures of 3.7 million (Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview, UNOCHA, 2020). The Nigerian Centre for Disease Control reported that 71,344 COVID – 19 cases and 1,190 deaths were recorded in Nigeria as of 11th of December, 2020. Of this number, 1,153 COVID – 19 cases and 63 deaths were recorded in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe state as at 11th of December, 2020 – according to Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC; https://ncdc.gov.ng/). The lockdown has increased women and girls chores at home because men, traditionally are responsible to fend for the family, no longer have access to their main sources of income such as teaching, petty trading in the market, herding, farming, brick laying, carpentry, water trucking, tailoring and commercial driving. This has exerted pressure on many families which has resulted in an increase in the rate of domestic violence and abuse against women and girls as well as forced marriage and early pregnancy and discrimination in some families. The security context in the northeast, particularly in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states has remained volatile since the beginning of 2020. Members of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) have carried out a number of aggressive attacks directed at military personnel, civilians, including children, as well as, increasingly, against humanitarian actors throughout the project period. Other obstacles to safe and effective humanitarian operations caused by the activities of the NSAGs include creation of illegal check points on major supply routes around Borno state, suicide bombings carried out in strategic locations, abductions of humanitarian workers and arbitrary killings of prisoners and local community members. Many women and children including girls have been abducted during the insurgency raids across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State since the beginning of 2020. The implication of the COVID-19 Pandemic and the protracted crisis on children particularly girls are that the situation exposed them to the risk of school dropout, risks of sexual abuse, rape, forced marriage, early pregnancy, physical injuries, sexually transmitted disease such as HIV and gynaecological problems and mental health problems during and after the period of abduction.While important interventions in the current education sector strategy, about 1.3 million school aged children still need humanitarian assistance in education. Actually it’s critical that these vulnerable children benefit from learning opportunities in safer environment in an innovative approach for a wider reach.

2 - Activities

This action is inspired by the learnings made on previous interventions implemented by Plan International Nigeria in Borno and Adamawa states with funding from NHF in 2019 – 2020. The action has been designed in line with the key strategies and priorities the multi-year Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019- 2021, the Nigeria Education Sector strategic objectives and Plan International’s education in emergencies strategy, 2019 – 2021.Plan International is targeting the most vulnerable children and youth in need of emergency life-saving assistance in Borno and Adamawa states, focusing on its commitment education in emergencies interventions to respond to the critical needs of those affected by the insurgency. With an effort to further align the proposed action with the Education Sector objectives and priorities, the project is contributing to the following Education Sector response strategies: i) Provide teaching and learning kits to teachers and students; ii) Provide temporary incentives to support teachers in hard-to-reach areas; iii) Establish disability friendly and protective semi-permanent learning spaces, classrooms gender segregated latrines with adequate basic WaSH facilities for both formal and non-formal education, targeting … children; iv) Provide recreational/PSS kits to school going children and support peace/sports clubs in schools; v) Provide early childhood development materials to preprimary school-aged children. Through these response strategies, the action is aimed, particularly, at increasing innovative access to quality and inclusive formal, non-formal and early childhood development education in safe and protective environment … conflict affected children, including those who are living disabilities in IDP camps and host communities in Borno and Adamawa state. Plan International Nigeria will work in close coordination with relevant education stakeholders such state ministry of education (MoE), State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), Local Government Education Authority, School Based Management Committees in order to successfully implement this action in public/government owned schools. Also, Plan International will work very closely with State Agency for Mass Education (SAME), Ministry for Religious Affairs (MoRA), Community Education Committees (CECs) to implement this action in non-formal ALP and IQE centres in order to facilitate the enrolment and retention of school children in school. Plan International Nigeria employ a community and right based approach during the implementation of this action to ensure its continuity – local community structures such as parenting groups and other operational partners will be identified and supported to ensure the sustainability of this action.
