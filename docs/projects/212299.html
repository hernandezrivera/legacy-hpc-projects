Project Summary

The initiative aims to fulfil its objectives by providing both multi-round and one-off emergency cash support to the most vulnerable households (HHs) in North Central Syria (NCS) and Northwest Syria (NWS). This approach is designed to assist displaced households and vulnerable communities, allowing them to address their immediate basic needs for food and non-food with dignity and flexibility. This support is critical in contributing to the overarching goal of the intervention, which focuses on saving lives by meeting urgent needs.
Syria Relief (SR) will assist 30,000 displaced individuals (5,000 HHs) across NWS and NCS by offering them a regular MPCA of $100 for eight rounds. Additionally, 60,000 individuals (or 10,000 HHs) will receive a one-off emergency MPCA of $150. This dual approach of regular and emergency MPCA grants beneficiaries the freedom to purchase necessary items from local markets, thereby addressing their basic needs for food and other essentials in a dignified manner. The allocation of funds for both types of assistance is based on the latest recommendations from the CWG, with the amounts and program design subject to adjustments according to monthly updates and price monitoring reports from REACH.
To ensure assistance reaches the most vulnerable HHs in NWS, the distribution process will incorporate registration and verification steps, utilizing a scoring system to identify and prioritize those in greatest need. The selection criteria will be set according to the harmonized vulnerability and eligibility criteria issued by the CWG.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to HNO 2024, The multifaceted crisis in Syria creates continuous cycles of vulnerability. Syrians are getting poorer as the economy continues its freefall. Since February 2023, the Syrian pound (SYP) has lost about half of its value against the US dollar (US$), averaging, in November 2023, around SYP14,200/US$1 in the parallel foreign exchange market. From February to September 2023, inflation, as proxied by the World Food Programme (WFP) minimum food basket price index, rose by 88 per cent, driven by reduced access to goods, disrupted supply chains, and heightened logistics costs, all of which exerted significant upward pressure on prices. SR aims to help people in need in Northwest and North-central Syria. This plan will support 20,000 people (4,000 families) with a regular MPCA of $100 for eight rounds and 15,000 people (3,000 families) with a three-round emergency MPCA of $150 in 2024. Additionally, 15,000 people (3,000 families) will get $100 monthly for eight rounds, and 10,000 (2,000 families) will receive a three-round emergency MPCA of $150 in 2025. This helps them buy necessary food and non-food items, respecting their choices and dignity. The cash harmonised transfer values are based on the CWG recommendations, with regular reviews to adjust the plan or cash amounts as needed. We focus on helping the neediest families, using a careful check-in process to make sure the aid goes to those who really need it. SR will use its SoPs during this project. Our team will check and register families based on set criteria to ensure the aid is given out fairly. Our MEAL team will check on the project's progress, from start to finish, to make sure it's working well and helping those in need as planned. a set of activities such as registration monitoring, verification, DM, PDM, etc. will be conducted by SR MEAL team to make sure that our response is going based on the SoPs. We're also taking steps to make sure everyone, including women, disabled people, and other vulnerable groups, can safely and easily get the help they need. We're hiring female staff for some roles, offering home delivery for those who have physical disabilities, and setting up a CRM system for reporting any problems, or abuse or providing suggestions and feedback. We've also planned to avoid risks like sexual abuse or not being able to reach help by training our staff and making sure messages about these risks are clear to everyone involved. SR is aware of risks like market functionality or changes in money value affecting prices. We'll keep an eye on prices and make sure our aid doesn't hurt local markets out of the do-no-harm principle. We're also ready for safety risks, planning to use vouchers to reduce theft risks and following safety rules to protect against larger threats. In short, SR is dedicated to providing thoughtful and effective aid to those in need in Syria, ensuring safety, fairness, and dignity in our support.
