Project Summary

This project is proposed in response to the Shelter Sector Objective 2: Strengthen the resilience and cohesion of vulnerable communities by improving housing and related community or public infrastructure. Hence; this project is designed to meet the dire need of returnees’ population in Jebel Samaan where the severity is 3 PiN 988,105.80 per HNAP 2021. Rescate had finished 390 rehabilitation of damaged houses in 2019 in Al Shaar neighborhood. Rescate is currently working in the area SHF project for 159 damaged houses rehabilitation, and found that there is a need to have wider coverage in the same location Noting that the area is eventually underserved in Aleppo per the outcome of the workshop between the acting parties/sector and the governorate because it constitutes of ~5000 houses, yet interventions were limited to rehabilitation of Rescate 390 houses, ADRA 13 and DRC 16 [from Rescate].
The objective of the project is to rehabilitate damaged houses for vulnerable returnees 75% and people who stayed in their houses under the fighting 25%. Rescate will build its intervention on the remained houses from the previous projects. Rescate finished 390 houses and is now working on 159 additional houses, but need is massive in the zones I, 2 and 3 awaiting without been rehabilitated. There are 8000 households’ returnees to these zones living in dire conditions and are in need for assistance. Rescate is submitting the proposal to rehabilitate additional 150 houses. Rescate already has field assessment and detailed BOQs including market studies with updated estimation for the average costs. In coordination with the municipality, a desk will be set at the Mayor office to review beneficiaries’ HLP dox. Rescate will submit the intact files for MOLAE final approval. Shelter sector standards will be applied to the work as well as the humanitarian residents and social conditions, returnees and displaced persons, and consideration will be given to achieving privacy as much as possible in line with the number of rooms allowed under SWG intervention criteria through the registration mechanism for future beneficiaries at the targeted neighborhood.
Due to current unstable currency and market prices, Rescate will encourage contractors to submit proposals by giving the awarded contractors (two for risk mitigation) a down-payment of 30% on condition they immediately purchase the materials as per sample written approvals. Rescate team will inspect the materials at warehouses.
Subject to the COVID- approvals, contractors will be asked to carry out the works with space segregation for Corona measures: All persons at site will be strictly using PPEs at all working-hours.
1) Works will be carried out in Lots of 20 each time
2) Each type of works will be carried out by a sub-contractor at each time, such as painting, sanitation, doors & windows, wood, electrical works in one Lot at any one time, then 2nd Lot starts.
It is worth to state that Rescate engineering team are ready to start working with the gained experience from the previous projects in Aleppo and the same area of intervention. Rescate will obtain the structure safety licenses from the Syndicate for the green buildings under this intervention before works initiation. Rescate will cover the buildings’ common areas. Rescate will reflect any savings from the fund by adding houses, with consideration to the vulnerability score (FHHs, CHHs, elderly, families with PWDs) in addition to those who lost their jobs to COVID-19

4 - Needs

Based on the explicit Needs Assessment which was conducted for the project in March 2021 and the review to design response action for this HRP based on the population’s dire needs in Al Shaar district, coupled with OCHA HNAP and returnees’ reports, Rescate is submitting this proposal.A qualitative and quantitative needs assessment was conducted by Rescate team had undertaken additional assessments and community consultations in the targeted location, the outputs were integrated in the needs’ analysis. Rescate also reached to information about beneficiaries in consultations with the stakeholders but final selection was based on vulnerability scoring. The intervention is designed to meet the operational plan being developed by the inter-sector coordination priorities: Al Shaar is one of the areas under focus by the Governorate and the Shelter Sector due to increased number of returnees who live in dire needs taking into consideration that most of Al Shaar residents are low-income being workers on man/day jobs. Around 75% of the population are returnees while 25% have remained in their houses during the events being unable to move for lack of resources and/or physical inability to move. Detailed BOQs for 150 houses and field assessment finished, Based on actual information from the Mayor in the three targeted zones; there are 8000 returned families in Shaar district, but due to limitation of funding, the project will add 150 direct families to 450 previous projects. Al-Shaar community are of low income and do not have sufficient financial capacity to rehabilitate their houses. The Mayor confirmed that residents visit his office on daily basis asking about new projects. M&E focusing on the vulnerability scoring.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will contribute to the strategic cluster objectives by supporting vulnerable returnees to live in dignity in their homes. The solutions are rather durable to assist inhabitants to live in decent life sustaining standard. The project has direct contribution to the Shelter/NFI sector by improving the housing conditions to the most vulnerable people in the selected and approved areas in Aleppo. This will be done through rehabilitation of the houses & upgrading quality standards by including WASH component in each building. Activities are:- Coordination with MOLAE & Governorate.- Procurement procedures, two contractors awarded to carry out the rehabilitation works as per the standard procurement and evaluation policies. - Site supervision & monitoring on materials and works, M&E, Field visits, beneficiaries’ feedback on assistance provided- Data collection and beneficiaries registration in collaboration with governorate committee - Final receipt of the works from contractors at each house is concluded with the governorate committee- Post evaluation for 150 rehabilitated houses, beneficiary satisfaction surveys conducted upon receipt of each lot of houses, after warranty period- Final closing reports financial and narrative submitted to Donor and sector 4Ws.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Local inequalities – communal tensions between IDPs, host communities and/or returnees. Likelihood: medium, Mitigation: Rescate has a transparent approach for the applicants & beneficiaries’ selection. Monitoring would be followed by analysis, effectively disseminated for the use of all actors.Safety and dignity will be enforced and transversal during all the activities of the project, as strict Covid-19 prevention measures will be taken into consideration at all times and communicated with all stakeholders including beneficiaries. The principles of Do No Harm are considered in this project as Rescate will have a conflict sensitivity, first understanding the context where we will be intervening and identifying the relations between different groups that we will be targeting. Rescate will act upon understanding these interactions in order to avoid negative impacts. An example, Rescate will ensure during implementation of the project, elderly or people with other illness. Efforts will be done in order to ensure that these groups also get benefited and their needs are foreseen, such as direct outreach to them by Rescate volunteers and additional equipment (handrails, and toilet seats) are installed. The project enables equal and impartial access since assistance will be provided on the basis of assessed vulnerability/need and NOT on the beneficiary’s application only (will avoid discrimination based on gender, age, IDP-host community). In order to support self-protection, Rescate will adapt to SOPs during the intervention. The objective of this is to ensure that people have the life-saving information and PPEs to protect themselves and others (from the virus and to reduce its impact on health and social life).

As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues. All PSEA and/or GBV issues will be addressed first by the Project coordinator, who will share the concern with the Head of Mission based in Damascus, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA/GBV survivor. When needed, the Head of Mission will scale the issue to ONG Rescate Headquarters in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.
