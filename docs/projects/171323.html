Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2021 HRP speciﬁc objective 2 of WASH Sector: “Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank have access to essential services” and will focus on 2 sectors:
- WASH and Protection support
Based on recent WASH Cluster Vulnerability Map, CESVI and ACTED assessment and consultation with key stakeholders (WASH Cluster, PWA, CMWU), the project will target 160 vulnerable households (828 individuals) in Middle Area governorate. ACTED and CESVI have successfully covered the needs that emerged in 2019 for Wadi al Salqa community through an ongoing project funded by AICS (Italian Agency for Cooperation). Based on new assessed needs, ACTED, CESVI and Youth Empowerment Centre (YEC) are now proposing a similar intervention targeting vulnerable households not covered in Wadi al Salqa and in the surrounding communities. The aim of this project is to restore access to domestic and drinking water for the most vulnerable households of Middle Area, by ensuring their access to drinking water supply, increasing water storage capacity and improving their sanitation facilities. This project will also raise awareness and build capacity on good hygiene practices, considering Covid-19 needs. By improving access to drinking water, basic sanitation and hygiene awareness, this project will also prevent further deterioration of the households’ living conditions, reduce the protection risks related to inadequate WASH facilities such as exposure to public health and nutrition risks, limit the adoption of negative coping mechanisms (e.g. purchase of water from unreliable vendors or using open or semi-open spaces as toilets). The project will be implemented by CESVI in partnership with ACTED and YEC. CESVI has developed strong expertise in WASH (South Hebron Hills and ongoing project in Gaza in partnership with ACTED – adequate access to water in terms of both quantity and quality). YEC has proven experience in implementing WASH program such as improving access to WASH services for fragile families, strengthening human and protection assistance, distributing hygiene kits, and raising awareness in WASH issues to achieve a positive impact on public health issues. The project design has been coordinated with relevant stakeholders, such as the WASH Cluster, Palestinian Water Authority, Coastal Municipal Water Utility, and the Ministry of Social Development. The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA) during the targeting selection (individual vulnerability-based assessment), the implementation methodology (support to reparations to HH’s WASH facilities unable to overcome the workload), the monitoring (respect of shelter and WaSH protection principles and minimum standards) and the accountability in the action (identiﬁcation of arising diﬃculties through the establishment of a Complaint Response Mechanism). Gender principles will be mainstreamed during the action and Committees will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus given to women and children issues such as those related to GBV. Through this intervention, ACTED, CESVI and YEC have proposed a project design that will simultaneously address the urgent WASH needs of vulnerable populations while empowering members of the community to become knowledgeable in WASH good practices.
-Support for Solid Waste Management (SWM) service provider – JSC
Based on the WASH Cluster Vulnerability Map and CESVI SWM assessment, in collaboration with service providers in the Middle and South area, the project will support the Joint Service Council of Khan Younis, Rafah and Middle Area (JSC KRM). The support will provide disinfectant material and tools (PPE) to the 75 workers for improving the safety of their work with particular attention to Covid-19 health-related risks.

1 - Needs

It is estimated that over 1.6 million Palestinians across the oPt are suffering from insufficient access to WASH services and will be in need of related humanitarian assistance in 2021. (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021)Due to decades of the blockade, Israeli restrictions, succession of wars and acute escalation, Covid-19 pandemic, funding gaps, existing WASH facilities have been severely affected and are non or partially operational. Due to both the shortage and inﬂating price of fuel, households are reliant on private water trucking to access domestic and drinking water; desalination and wastewater treatment plants cannot complete treatment cycles, thus resulting in the delivery of non-potable water and the release of partially-treated waste in the sea. Moreover, the solid waste management sector is not capable of providing adequate services, due to the additional workload incurred by the operational cost related to Covid-19 safety measures. As a consequence, over 96% of water extracted from Gaza aquifers is unﬁt for human consumption (according to the World Health Organization standards), and the water supply does not meet the minimum standard for potable water in Gaza, pushing households to purchase drinking water from private vendors. Almost 20,200 families cannot afford such costs. (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2021). In addition to WASH issues linked to access to safe water for domestic use, the pandemic is exacerbating the dire economic situation in Gaza. The containers and tanks bought by those households in Gaza are 10 to 30 times more expensive than piped water, further burdening heavily indebted families. In order to identify and select vulnerable households eligible for the proposed intervention, CESVI and its partners followed different processes and relied on several sources.The WASH Vulnerability Map 2020 carried out by WASH Cluster partners, surveyed 207 communities in the Gaza Strip; multi-sectoral assessment, in 2019 and update in 2020, to address WASH, Shelter and Protection need in the Middle Area. The outcomes of the assessment conﬁrmed the global trends in terms of WASH needs.In the target area, 100% of the household relies on water trucking for drinking water (price up to 50 NIS per cubic meter); none is connected to the sewer system and the poor sanitation facilities are connected to septic tanks or cesspit. The cost for vacuuming those pits is above 75 NIS in two communities. This situation affects around 18,000 WASH vulnerable people in eight target communities.Through these assessments, and in coordination with the Ministry of Social Development, partners will update the available data and verify the needs in order to select the most vulnerable households in need to be targeted by this intervention.Finally, through the ongoing AICS funded project, the water quality analysis shows that only 15% of households receive chlorinated water and that 70% of vulnerable families consider their basic WASH needs are unmet.The majority of people rely on poor and unsafe cesspit tanks that cause untreated wastewater to overspill, particularly during flooding, which highly impacts the health of children and other vulnerable people such as pregnant and lactating women, or elders. The poor water quality delivered to HHs members is at high risk of waterborne diseases. Based on the WASH Vulnerability Map, in 24 communities in the Middle Area and Khan Younis Governorate, there are families not reached by the solid waste service and are suffering from waste accumulation. This lack of service is due to the weak structural system and the lack of consumables, especially in terms of disinfectants material and personal tools for the Joint Service Council workers.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 - Establishment and capacity-building of WASH committees. 3 Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPC) will be established. They will be involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at the community level and to guarantee better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups. CBWPCs will provide support in the distribution of hygiene kits, upgrade of basic water and sanitation facilities. ACTED will provide capacity-building to the committee members on WASH, SPHERE and protection standards. . Activity 2 - Provision of drinking water and installation of Household Water Treatment Storage. ACTED and CESVI will provide targeted households with Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTS) unit, according to PWA indications and approval, to minimize the risk of cross-contamination before consumption. The design and selection on the HWTS are based on previous experience of CESVI in the West Bank and Gaza. It will be implemented also in close coordination with the water quality working group and the hygiene promotion working group (under the umbrella of WASH cluster). The technical performance and user acceptance evaluation methodology were assessed during 2018, 2019 and 2020 in three projects, funded by HIF, UNICEF and AICS respectively. Dedicated practical training will be carried out in the 160 identiﬁed HHs on the operation, correct use and maintenance of the module. The project team along with the CBWPCs' members will test the quality of water at the supplying point, treated water and at the point of use (at the cup). ACTED and YEC will distribute 200L of potable water tanks to 200 HHs in order to ensure that every HH has enough storage capacity. Activity 3- Rehabilitation and upgrade of sanitation facilities at households and neighbourhoods’ level 160 HHs will receive conditional cash assistance to upgrade sanitation facilities (upgrade of kitchen and bathroom/toilets, the connection of drinking water tanks to a sink equipped with water-saving supply, and connection of their domestic water tank to the water network inside the house). Work implementation will be monitored through visits conducted by ACTED to each household to review progress made by beneﬁciaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel instalments. Monitoring visits will also be used to identify any technical issues beneﬁciaries could be facing and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance. ACTED will provide drainage to 160 HHs in critical need of sewage drainage and in 2 communities, the sewer HH connections to pits septic tanks will be improved. In the meantime, proper pit drainage will be carried twice a month for 9 months. Activity 4 – Promotion of hygiene practices and protection principles. At the beginning, of the project, a KAP survey will be implemented by CESVI, ACTED and YEC to assess WASH knowledge and practices within the 160 targeted households. The KAP’s outcomes will be used to adjust WASH promotion materials and topics of the awareness-raising sessions. YEC will then implement awareness-raising sessions. In total, 4 members from each of the 160 HHs targeted by Activities 2 and 3 will beneﬁt from this campaign. At the end of the training, YEC will implement a post-training test to measure progress in terms of WASH and Covid-19 prevention knowledge. Speciﬁc sessions on hygiene promotion will target mothers, children and supported by the distribution of 160 hygienic kits, incl. both long-term and consumable items. The kits will be designed and distributed according to hygiene promotion working group and WASH cluster guidance and requirements. The sessions will be adapted to the Covid-19 situation and related restrictions, aiming at reducing the health risk for beneficiaries. Furthermore, they should be conducted according to COVID-19 protective measures and COVID-19 task force guidance also considering using specific communication mechanisms via social media. Activity 6 – JSC KMR support. CESVI will provide cleaning materials and tools for the 75 JSC KMR sanitation workers: chemicals disinfectants for containers, vehicle, floors, clothes, hand sanitiser and PPE (gloves, masks and disposable clothes). The material will support the workers need for 6 months, even in case of protracted crisis from the pandemic.

3 - Indicators

Indicators are selected based on clusters' indicators. In addition to those, CESVI and its partners will measure the following additional indicators: # of Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPCs) reinforced through a capacity-building training to scale-up their ability to provide WaSH services to their communities (Target: 3; Means of Veriﬁcation: Terms of Reference (ToRs), Training report); % of female members of Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (Target: 40%; Means of Veriﬁcation: CBWPCs ToR and list of members, Training attendance sheet); % of CBWPCs members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the capacity-building training (Target: 48: Means of Veriﬁcation: Pre-Training Assessment (PTA), Post Training Evaluation (PTE)); # of potable water tanks (HWTS) distributed (Target: 160; Means of Veriﬁcation: Activity report, PDM); # m3 of sewage drained/pumped to the closed sewage network (Target: (2000 6000); Means of Veriﬁcation: (technical tool to be developed); # HWTS distributed (Target: (160); Means of Veriﬁcation: (technical report, handover certificate); # water sample without contamination after HWTS (Target: (80%); Means of Veriﬁcation: (technical report,water analysis); # sanitation workers received PPE (Target: (70); Means of Verification: handover certificate). Moreover, the target of the indicator CLWSH/CA21 "The provision of sanitation facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and tools" is inteded as # of Joint Service Council, as per agreement with WASH Cluster coordination Team. Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The CESVI Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) officer and ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. MEAL and AME activities will enable the gathering and reporting of detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneﬁciaries and to measure the project outcomes of different target groups. Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the MEAL officer and AMEU, will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP and CHS standards and SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBWPC members, Protection Oﬃcer and independent monitoring staff from YEC, to ensure that feedback is received from beneﬁciaries, and, when preferred, conﬁdentiality is ensured and respected. During the project, the Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPC) will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the Protection Oﬃcer. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to CESVI as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, the Protection Oﬃcer, with the support of the CBWPC, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. Were any issues to be identiﬁed, the Protection oﬃcer will offer guidance and complimentary training to beneﬁciaries.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ensured through the project Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM), implemented in all CESVI projects. It will be adapted to ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and considers the harmonisation with PSEA complaints and whistle-blower. All beneﬁciaries of the action will be explained their right to provide feedback and complaints in conﬁdentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance and will provide with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CFM and CRM rely on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, ﬁeld paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CFM and CRM system will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms feature several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by staff to ensure that feedback is received from beneﬁciaries, and, when preferred, conﬁdentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is a key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of projects. Beneﬁciaries' satisfaction is continuously monitored through regular visits and interviews to beneﬁciaries regarding ongoing activities. The MEAL officer and AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. ACTED has been implementing projects in the targeted governorates since 2010 and thus benefits from a high level of acceptance in local communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with CESVI and ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

A community-based protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at the community level. 3 Community-based Protection Committees will be strengthened through training in Protection and WASH. Their role throughout the whole project will be that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households, households with people with disabilities). Do No Harm: members of CBWPCs will receive refreshing training on protection and all beneﬁciaries will be informed on the correct usage of the hygiene kit received, in particular on items which could expose beneﬁciaries to risks if misused. All team members involved in the project implementation will undergo extensive training in PSEA, Protection and WASH Sphere standards to ensure they engage with the beneﬁciaries and take into account the speciﬁc needs of all household members. The Protection Oﬃcer, together with CBWPCs’ members, will conduct additional visits to ensure that the responses to speciﬁc needs, regarding gender, age and disability, are integrated and the needs of female members are prioritized in the rehabilitation plan. Inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanisms such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Beneﬁciaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance (for example items to be included in the kits), and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. A self-help approach will be adopted to implement rehabilitation and improvement of sanitation facilities at HH level has recognized to have several added values as it assigns an active role to the beneﬁciary in carrying on rehabilitation works of their own house, thus increasing ownership and personal satisfaction, and tends to support economic recovery in the Gaza Strip by involving the local market contractors which is more cost-eﬃcient. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneﬁciaries will be informed and sensitized by CBPWC members on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practices. CBWPCs will be responsible to refer to the project team any acutely vulnerable households that could have been neglected, or who would present signiﬁcant vulnerabilities despite not ﬁtting the criteria mentioned before for further investigation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have different needs. CBWPCs will receive training on the speciﬁc needs of various groups, in order to ensure they factor them in throughout project implementation and that assistance can be adapted to beneﬁciaries’ needs. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, using and maintaining WASH facilities at the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanisms such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Community-based WASH Protection Committees and ACTED Protection Oﬃcer will ensure that the rehabilitation work of the WASH facilities will include adequate lighting, internal lock and privacy fencing in order to ensure women safety, security and privacy inside their house. Women will be highly encouraged to be part of the Committees, follow all the implementation and join partners team during the ﬁeld visits to the households. During the awareness sessions ACTED, CESVI and YEC teams will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as “women issues” In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actively engage in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between females and males of proper hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. Speciﬁc sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers and children. In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected population, partners are engaging households in the intervention design. Prior to the procurement of assistance, each household will be informed on the planned assistance in order to determine if needs have signiﬁcantly changed since the original needs assessment. If outstanding needs are identiﬁed through this process, the material assistance will be adapted to meet those needs, as long as it ﬁts within the budgeted amount for the location. Community-based WASH Protection Committees will represent communities’ interests throughout the implementation process. Indeed, they will ensure that the repair to be undertaken by the household is decided in collaboration with the household members (women and male) and is in line with their priorities and needs. They will also ensure that all protection and privacy concerns faced by the household due to the substandard WASH facilities (toilets and kitchens) are addressed in priority.
