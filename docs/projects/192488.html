Project Summary

JoiNER is a common appeal by 13 WaSH NGOs, constituting the majority of non-UN active sector partners. Modalities are adapted from crisis-sensitive components of the HawkaMaa-EU development project consortium. Coordination at the sector level was led/facilitated by the coordination team, which encouraged collective action, and which is also invited to support the implementation of the JoiNER.
The ERP acknowledges that public services are “overstretched and underdeveloped due to years of under- investment” and adopts a “system approach” to maximise reach and cost effectiveness while minimizing inter/intra-communitytensions and adverse effects of parallel services on public utilities. JoiNER reinforces this approach by tackling the two main components of water service provision, production and distribution, across three categories of localities/communities; those served by Water Establishments (WE), others by municipalities, and those unserved by public systems. It assumes that UNICEF and/or WFP, as sector leads (Water and Logistics respectively), will provide the necessary consumables and maintenance required for continued operation of priority pumping stations.
Recognizing that water production does not guarantee its effective and equitable delivery, JoiNER will primarily tackle distribution problems to ensure the minimum standard of safe water (35 l/c/d) is received by the most vulnerable communities and key outbreak-prone institutions. This entails addressing urgent maintenance requirements of WE and municipal networks. Unserved communities/households will be served through temporary water trucking or private pumping systems. It is notable that under the Lebanon Humanitarian Fund First Standard, $1.4 million has been allocated to the WASH sector to respond to the emergency needs through implementing activities (water points & renewable energy) to ensure the minimum standard of safe water is received by the most vulnerable.
Against referrals from the Health and Food Security sectors, JoiNER will implement a rolling Hygiene Kit distribution activity. Also, it’s relevant to mention that in line with the LHF strategy, ACF, GVC and DPNA provided 5722 WASH kits to mitigate hygiene risks in Akkar, South, and Bekaa. These activities are made more urgent by the recent Viral Hepatitis A outbreak.
Participation will be subject to partners’ proven access, capacity, and readiness to implement timely interventions. This will be enhanced by inviting them to replicate or jointly tender framework contracts with infrastructure contractors and suppliers based on HawkaMaa-EU’s experience, with guidance from WW-GVC. Similarly, LebRelief will lead a joint national tender for hygiene items, as well as use Hawkamaa-EU (which has dedicated funding) to provide (with ACTED and LEWAP) capacity building for local NGOs.
National coverage will be ensured through a geo-split akin to that employed within the refugee (IS) response, adopting a wider unit of WE branch/section jurisdiction, except for Hygiene Kit distribution which is divided by District/Caza. JoiNER will coordinate nationally to (re)allocate funding dynamically as the understanding of vulnerability evolves with incoming analysis.
Off-grid renewable energy for pumping stations will be exceptionally considered where feasible to help break WEs’ perpetual ‘energy crisis mode’. JoiNER will prioritize service recovery/improvement in collaboration with public entities by providing the necessary resources while maintaining direct oversight, reinforcing community trust in the public sector. This is critical to support the wider drive to reinforce public institutions, but JoiNER will stop short of providing them with direct support, conscious that the “ERP is strictly of humanitarian nature”. This has become more pressing with the protracted energy crisis and delays in unlocking the World Bank energy loan, which promises to install some USD 30m of solar generators for water facilities.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

1.1.1 Installation/maintenance of renewable energy for pumping stations; assessing pumping stations relying exclusively on EDL without on-site backup generators nor access to alternative energy sources and determining the feasibility of installing RE systems. Install or maintain RE systems where they. 1.1.2 Transmission and distribution network urgent maintenance responding to referred or directly identified distribution system failures severely impacting services, providing maintenance to repair bursts, blockages, and faulty components, as well as connecting unserved communities/households (permanently if subscribed, temporarily if not) to networks where more cost effective than providing emergency/temporary supply. This may be done by providing resources (supplies, rented equipment, etc.) to WEs/municipalities or hiring contractors to implement directly, with direct oversight from partners in all cases. 1.1.3 Water point installation, water trucking, and private borehole activation; providing temporary supply to those not served by public systems through water trucking and/or temporary activation (repair, etc.) of private boreholes to supply households directly or central communal water points. 2.1.1 Provision of Hygiene Kits and Protective Personal Equipment; procurement of hygiene and COVID protective materials in line with sector guidelines for kits and provision through a rolling distribution exercise to most vulnerable through referral from health and food sectors.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Partners adopt a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual/child exploitation, abuse, or violence, enforced via policies in the organizations and in the program and all individuals involved with it. Staff will be trained on protection (Safe identification and referrals, safe distribution, etc.), PSEA, and proper handling of complaints. Partners will ensure relevant gender/age groups are involved in FGDs, assessments, etc. These will include gender focused questions on WASH knowledge, roles, and needs. Community consultations will also be held for women only groups, helping adapt implementation and M&E as needed. Partners will also focus on the participation of head of HHs of both genders. Additionally, partners will implement Hygiene Kit distribution focused on Menstrual Hygiene Management and SRHR awareness. PSEA will also be mainstreamed in the distributions/services provided, e.g., ensuring distributions/water trucking in daylight and public water points in central locations.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

artners assigned to serve a geographic unit will declare to the WE local office, municipalities and their unions, and other CSOs and community representatives the scope, scale, and priorities of their intervention. For the wider community, reporting urgent needs through the WE hotlines or the aforementioned representatives will be encouraged through appropriate media broadcasts, allowing for multiple referral pathways. Interventions will be published through partner, WE, and municipal media channels just ahead of commencement and within one week of completion. Partners will also conduct dedicated Third-Party Monitoring activities of each other’s interventions within the bounds of logistical feasibility. The JNER’s focus on tackling the distribution aspect of water services naturally leads to partners actively seeking the least served, most peripheral/marginalized localities, communities, and households. It forces partners to address the challenges faced by those excluded from public.
