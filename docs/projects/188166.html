Project Summary

The Project will improve children feeding practices to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality among infants and young children in NWS & NCS by ensuring access to safe, adequate and appropriate nutrition services in 24 months.
Anchored on the first 1000 days’ window of opportunity, different project models to target specific development period of a child will be implemented. And in recognition that malnutrition is not solely a health problem, WVSR will push for mainstreaming of nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions. One example of which is creating Income Generating Activities (IGA) opportunities for families with malnourished children and promotion of nutrition-sensitive agriculture such as homestead gardening for micronutrient rich crops, promotion of animal source foods, bio fortification. There will be provision of safe space (Mother-Baby Areas) and Specialized Support at EPI center/camp building on and contextualizing integrated nutrition programming model, the Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies component of the project will implement integrated IYCF-E and PSS activities in the camps and EPI sites. IYCF-E staff will be trained in PSS skills to support mothers who are in distress or unable to engage with their infants, and to recognize when a mother needs further, specialized mental health support and also other red flags such as signs of gender-based violence. If Child Protection needs are identified during the assessment, or following, families will be referred to the CP CM program. The intensive counselling and education will be supported by information, education, and communication (IEC) materials that have been developed by or in line with the Nutrition, Protection and FSL clusters.
WVSR will be identifying the right partners who has both nutrition sensitive and nutrition specific interventional capacity to support the intervention as local partner. The national NGO as partner is to be confirmed as soon as the negotiation stage is over with the donor. The activities will be implemented in the North West & North Central Syria in Aleppo, Idleb, Al Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa. The project will be targeting the households having under five children, PLW as well as adolescent girls (13-18 years) to prevent maternal malnutrition. IYCF approach will target mother and caregivers of children under five on recommended nutrition and feeding practices. It covers promotion of appropriate breastfeeding, quality complementary feeding and feeding during difficult circumstances. In addition, the intervention will target caregivers, Community Health Workers and Community Support Groups for interventional, institutional and behavioral sustainability of the interventions. The project will ensure the intentional target of Women or children with disabilities to support with assistive devices, mainstreaming interventions with access to services and resources in Health Care facilities.
In addition to nutrition activities, active case finding, referral & treatment of acutely malnourished children under 5 and PLW Community-level screening and referral for treatment of acute malnutrition will be conducted. Children with Moderate Acute Malnutrition and Pregnant and lactating women with a MUAC of less than 23.0cm will be referred to available supplementary treatment programs. Severe acutely malnourished children with complications will be referred for inpatient care at the existing stabilization centers. The project will provide training in nutrition screening and referral services. The project will also ensure that HFs /EPI ceneters are equipped with minimum supplies needed for nutrition screening activities, (e.g. MUAC tapes, registration and reporting format, referral forms, guidelines etc.), promoting and supporting community mobilization and sensitization activities towards nutrition screening program. The PHC and Health Facilities will be equipped with SAM/MAM cases to be treated

4 - Needs

HNO 2021 indicates children under two years of age and under-five children of IDP households are disproportionally affected by malnutrition, with highest severity levels observed in the sub-districts of north-western Idleb, northern Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor, Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh Governorates. Malnutrition and micro-nutrient deficiency also affect an estimated 1,1 million pregnant and lactating women (PLW), with rates particularly high amongst IDPs in north-west Syria where 11 per cent of mothers were found to be acutely malnourished. At least 72.4 per cent of assessed families have no option but to consume less nutrient-rich and cheaper foods to meet their basic food needs. Hidden hunger has increased, as one in eight children are reported to be stunted in Syria, with PLW also showing higher rates of food insecurity as they struggle to meet the micro-nutritional needs. Dramatic increases in food insecurity (including COVID-19 stress over markets and overall economy) can worsen already-elevated levels of anemia in Women of Reproductive Age (WRA), posing a risk to adverse health outcomes for both mother and child, and, when coupled with diarrheal disease, can exacerbate the risk of poor malnutrition outcomes in children under five years. Chronic malnutrition has intergenerational consequences and can only be reversed in the first 1,000 days of life. Contributing factors include poor IYCF practices, lack of psychosocial stimulation, repeated illnesses; and low birth weight.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project adopts CMAM with interventions to address malnutrition that are both preventive and curative. The preventative approach will address the causes of malnutrition and identify mitigation measures through community sensitizing on malnutrition among children under 5 and PLW. The project will educate PLW and caregivers on infant and young feeding practices to promote optimal infant feeding practices, including exclusive breast feeding. The curative approach will screen, identify and manage the already malnourished cases through OTP and TSFP based on the criteria of admission based on CMAM guideline. The project will engage local NGO partners and their Community Nutrition/Health Volunteers who will provide pre-distribution nutrition education as well as, post distribution monitoring. This project will establish, train, and support Mother to Mother Support Groups for the successful implementation of Maternal Infant & Young Child Nutrition. The project will also sensitize community will some nutrition sensitive interventions like homestead gardens, vegetables production as appropriate. A baseline will be conducted at the inception. Project monitoring will take place at all levels, including community, local council, and governorate levels. The project will submit monthly updates and formal reports every 6 months to disseminate activity progress and challenges with validation and triangulated with the clusters (4W), partners. An accountability mechanism will also be there.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.The likelihood of Safety and security risks is high as there is always a geopolitical, religious crisis and economic downturn sometimes. WV will continue monitoring the situation and adjust the programs based on the context change. 2.Unequal access to services for women, girls and other at-risk groups like children with disabilities. A high likelihood of low decision making capacity in the family leads to child marriage and adolescent pregnancy leading to malnutrition. WV will emphasize gender mainstreaming in the programs and engagement of men for some interventions.3.Increased risk of GBV due to lack of global and national sector standards related to protection, rights and GBV risk. Although a medium likelihood WV can build awareness on GBV with supportive linkages to GVB case management. 4.Lack of participation by vulnerable group in project in age, gender & culture appropriate way. This is low likelihood as WV plans to engage community at all stage of project cycle management. WV will initiate program accountability to listen to the community.5.The need to advocate for and support the deployment of GBV specialists within the nutrition sector. WV will keep special eyes on the equity of feeding practices, complementary feeding, adolescent (girls) nutrition, vaccination, etc. to ensure the wellbeing of women & girls. Another focus can be given on sensitizing education sector on equity for nutritional aspects.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WV adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards Sexual Exploitations & Abuse. The support will be provided for the PSEA mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners. The ToT training will be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WV field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WV field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle. Furthermore, WV has its own safeguarding incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WV uses a standard online platform named Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response. WV will ensure this hotline information portal is available to every location to ensure quick complains and will be responded by a third party investigator to remain unbiased, fair management of the complains.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Prices remain generally stable or fluctuate within the buffer allowed for inflation. The indicators can be high inflation rates, significant increases in the cost of implementation. Project budget has taken into account the possibility of price fluctuation and will closely monitor monthly spending reports and market prices. The budget will be revised as necessary.Covid-19 Pandemic may create the uncertain situation if cases increase and impose lockdowns. The indicators can be high cases of C-19 reported increase positivity rate daily, high death rate due to C-19. WV will continue interventions with online modalities using phones or other available devices maintaining health measures. WV will support the beneficiaries with adaptive interventions by giving in kind supports as needed for survival.No logistical constraints beyond WV’s control. The indicators can be mon renewal of cross border resolution, flooded or impassable roads due to rain/snow, challenges with road safety at primary roads. WV will closely monitor the supply and price of goods available through networking with other organizations and multiple suppliers. Additionally, during the heavy rain season, WV will coordinate with partners and identify alternative solutions to transportation challenges and ensure enough lead time for deliveries.Security condition significantly disrupt project implementation like threats, attacks, violence. WV will monitor the context on the ground and adjust activities accordingly.
