Project Summary

Based on evidence-base and lessons learnt collected during two and half years of implementation, SARD intends to continue to support a number of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and children at risk of separation through continuation and extension of its specialized and unspecialized child protection services in two locations: A’zaz and Idleb. The focus will be in particular on teenagers (e.g. people aged 10-19), the project falls under NWS PC Strategic Objectives 1 & 2.
First of all, SARD wants to consolidate and scale up its Child Protection in Humanitarian action response by continuing to engage in in case management, strengthening the resilience of children through PSS support, and improving the protective environment of children by working with parents and caregivers. The preventative part of the programme is specifically ensured through case management of children at risk of separation, and by resuming the work with vulnerable families, through a holistic case management service, and community-based child protection committees, building on pre-existing committees or support new groups. Adequate capacity building/coaching will be provided to community members to strengthen their contribute to the prevention part, while starting thinking of the response part as well in the near future.
Participation as a way to empower people will be fostered through ensuring consultations and FGDs are conducted with community members and children, in strategic moments of the project cycle.
Although there are no specific indicators on Unaccompanied and Separated Children, this is at the heart of SARD work in CP, so it is paramount to outline it in this summary. The focus on the work with UASC through the Interim Alternative Care (IACC) will remain, enhancing their mental and physical well-being, supporting re-enrollment in education opportunities, and of course working towards a sustainable reunification with primary caregivers or members of extended families. In the lack of an enforced legal framework on foster care and alternative care, the organisation is committed to engage in the legal support through the services of a lawyer, and through networking with other NGOs specialising in legal advice.
PSS support will be provided through two modalities. The mobile approach, certainly more praised by the CP community of practitioners, will have an important place and will ensure we can include the hardest to reach children. On the other hand, the Child Friendly Space opened in Azaz and Idleb will continue to be operational: even though this modality has some well-known limitations, the CFS are now well trusted points of reference on Child Protection for community members, and at present, it is beneficial to keep operating them.

Implementing Partners

The latest HNO reports a significant increase in humanitarian needs in Syria, with 16.7 million individuals requiring assistance, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Among them, 5.5 million are displaced, with over 2 million in last resort sites, notably in Aleppo (3,505,148) and Idleb (2,596,397). Additionally, Syria faced further challenges with earthquakes in February 2023, causing casualties and displacements.
In 2023, SARD conducted a Yearly Needs Assessment, it revealed prevalent child protection issues: child labour, marriage, and family separation. Challenges include poverty, abuse, child marriage, and violence, affecting vulnerable groups disproportionately. Child labour is widespread due to poverty and societal pressure. Child separation stems from various factors like death, divorce, conflict, and economic challenges. Violence against children occurs due to economic stress.
Based on evidence-base and lessons learnt collected during two and half years of implementation, SARD intends to continue to support a number of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and children at risk of separation through continuation and extension of its specialized and unspecialized child protection services in two locations: A’zaz and Idleb. The focus will be in particular on teenagers (e.g. people aged 10-19).
First of all, SARD wants to consolidate and scale up its Child Protection in Humanitarian action response by continuing to engage in in case management, strengthening the resilience of children through PSS support, and improving the protective environment of children by working with parents and caregivers. The adopted household approach also encompasses strengthening the sustainability of the intervention through cash assistance support and constant referrals of pre-identified households to other SARD departments (or other stakeholders), to cover multi-faceted needs. The preventative part or the programme is specifically ensured through case management of children at risk of separation, and by resuming the work with vulnerable families, through a holistic case management service, and community-based child protection committees (building on pre-existing committees of support new groups). The focus on the work with UASC through the Interim Alternative Care (IACC) will remain, enhancing their mental and physical well-being, supporting re-enrollment in education opportunities, providing opportunities to engage in recreational activities, and of course working towards a sustainable reunification with primary caregivers or members of extended families. Once more, for the sake of sustainability, SARD will pilot activities to ensure vocational training is provided to UASC who, due to age or other reasons, can’t be re-enrolled in the formal education pathway.
In the course of 2024-2025, SARD would also like to go beyond this, and adding a knowledge building component to our CP programme, start filling the gap of knowledge building and analysis, exploring the issue of UASC, a particularly vulnerable group of children that often, lack visibility. According to a brief developed by the CP Area of Responsibility in June 2023 and sent to Brussels for advocacy purposes, “UASC in NWS face numerous legal and protection issues in the absence of the rule of law and with lack of harmonized Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) among child protection actors specifically designed for UASC and agreed common advocacy messages among coordination actors. Counting on the CP AoR and Protection Cluster guidance, SARD will seek legal support within a network of specialized actors in the area whenever possible.
Finally, under a two-year implementation perspective, SARD would like to increase targeting young children, who have been neglected for years, also for the focus and efforts put on primary school-age children.
To monitor and evaluate the project, SARD has established multiple approaches to pin-down this matter. First, internally, program team organize weekly meeting to monitor the progress of the activities and provide technical support for the team. The weekly and monthly reports are as well used to monitor the progress. Second, M&E team has its own monitoring and evaluation tools to track the progress and quality of the CP activities. They carry out random field visits to monitor the activities at the IACC. As for monitoring the CM work, the M&E conduct random phone calls to check the clients' satisfaction. Program team utilize the results from the M&E team and internal report for learning purposes.
Reporting, in addition to the above-mentioned reporting tools such as weekly and monthly reports, also includes filling in the 5Ws and CM reporting tool of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility.
This new project cycle is based on the gaps and needs observed at field level and listening to children and their families in our daily work. SARD is one of the (local) NGOs that have the “privilege”, but also the heavy task, to implement CP activities in an area that poses a high security risk, and not accessible for big international organizations. In addition, the knowledge of the reality comes without cultural bias, all our CP staff members, including the whole of the CP team based in Turkey (4 people), being originally from Syria and very familiar with the context.
A child-rights based approach will be adopted in all phases of the project cycle: design, implementation, and M&E. Children aren’t seen as mere recipients of humanitarian aid in general, but through this project, their participation is fostered, starting from an open dialogue and the formal consent from the child to open the case. We aim to empower children and adolescents temporarily hosted at the center to become “ambassadors of child rights” within their communities during events such as the International Child’s Day on 20th of November.
SARD serves cases through various channels: SARD's CM team, the Protection cluster, local authorities, other humanitarian actors, and self-referrals. Following referral/case identification, SARD's CM Team studies each case and sets a tailored plan. The PSS team, and the Interim Alternative Care Center unit, work to provide harmonised CP services.
