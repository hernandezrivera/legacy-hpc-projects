Project Summary

This project will improve food security situation for 5000 most vulnerable individuals (2000 households) along the contact line in both Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts in GCA.
E-voucher modality will grant households access to food products in nearby shops located all along the contact line, within a 5-kilometre area. By providing three consecutive rounds of 3-month electronic vouchers, this project will enhance food security of the most vulnerable households by addressing their immediate food needs as well as support local economy and small-scale businesses through indirect impact.

Needs

During 2018 TGH has been collecting data of 14000 vulnerable households in 11 rayons along contact line. Among others, data on Food Consumption Score and income were collected. TGH has thus an overall and comprehensive overview of the food security situation in this area.If food insecurity levels have decreased in GCA compared to 2017, with 9% of overall population still considered to be moderately food insecure and 1% severely food insecure, the situation in areas close to “contact line” still requires actions to be taken. The share of the population with moderate or severe food insecurity remains higher than in the rest of the country with 15% of the population affected. For the others, their resilience has been strongly impacted by the conflict and 57% within the 5km buffer zone and 59% per cent overall are found to be marginally food insecure. Their resistance to any external shocks such as escalation of the conflict or economic shocks is then limited. Localities close to contact-line and under constant shelling such as Pisky 2, Mayorsk or Zolote 4.are of particular concerns in this aspect as TGH has been able to witness it during its identification process. Furthermore food security situation in Ukraine and especially near the contact line is linked with socio-economic features of households. The biggest barrier for households to reach an acceptable food security level remains the economic access to market. A market study held in August 2018 by the cash working group showed that 93% of interviewed men and women stated the high prices as the main problem in accessing markets and commodities. High prices of food items have an important impact on households’ capacity to access a satisfying level of food security near contact line. According to TGH data, the average monthly income of households is as low as 1207 UAH per person, far below the official minimum subsistence level in Ukraine 3,300 UAH. Average monthly income of families with children under 5 years old and of women-headed households are even lower – respectively 957 UAH per person for the first group and 921 UAH per person for the second one.According to Food Security and Socio-Economic Trend Analysis conducted by the Food Security and Livelihoods cluster in March 2018 the share of households with poor and borderline levels of food consumption has increased to reached 20.9% in February 2018 and is currently being around 16% in areas near the contact line. The food insecurity level is even higher among households with vulnerable members such as persons with disabilities, chronic diseases, older persons, single-headed households, or families with 3 or more children. Unemployed people between 40-60 years old have been found as the most vulnerable group by different reports (REACH assessment, HNO) confirming the trend linking unemployment and food insecurity. Furthermore, market studies conducted by the cash working group since August 2017 show an increase by 9% in food products prices. This increase strongly affects localities near contact line which are already heavily hindered by the protracted consequences of the ongoing conflict.Rural areas, which are predominant near the contact line, are particularly vulnerable as, according to the TGH database, 24% of their population are older persons (more than 60 years old) while this number decreases to 18% in urban areas. According to the recent study conducted by Help Age International older persons spend 55.8% of their incomes on medicines, with 26.2% spent on food (October 2018). To meet their needs most of older persons interviewed by Help Age International opted for negative coping mechanisms. Ranking as the first negative coping mechanism, 78.6% of older persons decrease their food intakes hence severely harming their food security level. Among households surveyed by TGH, 21% spend more than 50% of their incomes for food it leaves a tight scope for households to face unforeseen expenses.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Access to food for vulnerable population in the buffer zone is ensured through distributions of e-vouchers.TGH has been using this e-voucher modality since 2016 to enable up to 3600HH purchasing of food products. It is cost-effective, saves transportation costs for vulnerable people and allows reaching out the most remote areas and people with low mobility. Preferences of this approach were expressed by TGH beneficiaries. Importantly, it enhances local economy and strengthens solidarity mechanisms inside communities.Activity 1. Individual identification of beneficiariesThe identification of most vulnerable households will be based on the FSL cluster criteria.To ensure a transparent process and accountability towards vulnerable populations’ identification of beneficiaries will be done in close contact with local authorities with criteria and selection process clearly defined.TGH will select the 5,000 most vulnerable beneficiaries through a comprehensive questionnaire focusing on individual’s characteristics (men/women, children/adults/elderly), food consumption score, share of expenditures for food, shelter, health and hygiene, income per person, as well as other vulnerabilities. Activity 2. Activation of partner shops network, identification of new shops and deployment of e-vouchers system.A network of partner shops will be activated across 11 rayons alongside the contact line. New shops will be identified in the locations if needed. Training sessions will be conducted for the shops on the use of the interface as well as fixing of prices for the priority food items.Among the 58 TGH partner shops, 65% are run by women. TGH intends to mainstream gender approach and keep this ratio. The-voucher modality employed by TGH will ensure adherence to the ‘do no harm’ approach and support local economy. Activity 3. Programing and distribution of e-vouchers.Once beneficiaries are selected they will be assigned to the nearest partner shop. An e-vouchers programme distribution plan will be developed by TGH to ensure smooth e-vouchers distribution. Each selected person will receive an 825 UAH e-voucher on his mobile phone and will have three months to redeem it in once or several times in partner shops. The value of the voucher has been harmonised through FSL cluster. This system allows close monitoring of the redeeming of vouchers but also to analyse consumptions trends by beneficiaries. TGH will implement 3 distributions for these households. Each distribution will provide vouchers for a three-month period. The support provided will allow addressing the food security needs of the most vulnerable persons along the contact line to, regain economic access to market and to secure their food consumption and reduce their suffering.Outcome 2. Monitoring and accountability of the project are ensuredActivity 1. Implementation of a mechanism for feedbacks and complaints.TGH will, from the start of identification process, open a hotline for the affected population. This will enable a close monitoring of the project from the start and enhance TGH accountability towards beneficiaries and donors. Feedback and complaints mechanism will also be put in place and will allows fast and efficient response to feedback and complains and decision-making. Activity 2. Monitoring of project implementation, coordination with key actors.Baseline information will be collected at the project start and will be used as a reference all along the project to measure outcomes. PDM will be ensured after the end of each distribution round. TGH will closely monitor the use of vouchers but also the impact of its project on the food consumption score and on the coping strategy index. PowerBi, an interactive data visualization tool, will be employed to monitor purchases in real time and ensure fast and informed decision-makingTGH will coordinate with relevant key actors at each stage of the project to avoid overlaps and to ensure better coverage.
