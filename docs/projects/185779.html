Project Summary

DRC will work with community groups of diverse genders, ages and abilities to assess, proritize, implement and maintain site development activities ranging from construction and plantings to environmental protection communications. Focus will be on interventions that reduce disaster risks such as pre-monsoon drainage cleaning, engaging households in sessions about the proper disposal of waste, and plantings implemented and maintained by small household clusters.
Construction or repair of durable and non-durable access (pathways, stairs, bridges), retaining walls, drainage, and shared structures (water point sheds, silt traps, soak pits) will be evaluated by community groups to ensure that they meet their needs and handed over to block-level groups for long-term monitoring and maintenance. DRC's drainage cleaning activities will include engagement with nearby households to understand causes of blockages and encourage safe waste disposal. DRC will prioritize individuals from vulnerable households identified through SMS camp surveys for cash-for-work recruitment, assigning tasks that are culturally appropriate and suitable to their abilities.
DRC will increase vegetative ground covering in the camps to reduce soil erosion and flash flooding, using native plants and involving surrounding households in the planning and implementation. DRC will support small household clusters near tree-planting plots to care for seedlings, and selected households will be provided materials for homestead gardening to support vulnerable individuals who have difficulty leaving their shelters due to illness, disability or cultural restrictions. DRC will conduct regular joint monitoring visits with community groups and households to encourage appropriate use of materials and proper care of plants.
Community groups will be engaged in gender- and age-segregated, participatory workshops on DRR principles, environmental protection and fire safety. They will develop communications tailored to their peers and be provided interpersonal communication training to disseminate information to their community.

1 - Identified Needs

Most Cox's Bazar camps are self-settled and unplanned, resulting in overcrowded sites that lack basic infrastructure and are prone to landslides, soil erosion, flooding and fire. Environmental risks in the camps are further exacerbated by steep slopes, exposure to monsoon rains and two cyclone seasons. The impact of inadequate infrastructure falls disproportionately on women, children and people with disabilities: 31% of women and 30% of children in camps reported having difficulty moving around the camps, while 47% of women with disabilities and 44% of men with disabilities reported challenges (J-MSNA, October 2021). Pathways are blocked, slippery, damaged or too steep, posing serious barriers to accessing essential services and emergency evacuations (ACAPS/IOM, "Our Thoughts: Rohingya share their experiences and recommendations", 2021). Impermeable surfaces and reduced vegetative cover coupled with insufficient drainage lead to soil erosion and flash floods during the monsoon. 1,492 households were affected by flooding, 39 households by slope failure, and 5 individuals died from landslides in the Cox's Bazar camps in July 2021, leading to widespread displacement (SMSD Situation Report, July 2021). Restrictions on use of durable materials means that the majority of the infrastructure that humanitarian actors have installed needs to be constantly repaired and rebuilt. Insufficient community engagement in the planning, design and maintenance of built works further exposes structures to material theft and misuse. Drainage in the camps is regularly blocked due to gaps in solid waste management and lack of community responsibility for maintaining shared spaces. 70% of women living in the camps do not feel included in decision-making and camp inhabitants feel that they are not included in discussions about solutions (ACAPS/IOM, "Our Thoughts: Rohingya share their experiences and recommendations", 2021). Infrastructure is not sustainable without community participation, and the disconnect between humanitarian actors and communities runs serious risks of resources being allocated ineffectively.

2 - Activities or outputs

4,157 meters of construction or repair of durable and non-durable access including pedestrian pathways, stairways and water crossings. Interventions will respond to needs referred by SMS or communicated directly from the community to DRC Site Development and will be prioritized through consultations with Protection community groups of diverse ages, genders and abilities. 5,750 square meters of construction and rehabilitation of slope protection including soil stabilization, retaining walls and vegetated solutions as appropriate. Interventions will respond to needs referred by SMS or communicated directly from the community to DRC Site Development. 1,800 meters of construction and rehabilitation of secondary and tertiary drainage after assessment of site topography, catchment area and peak flow during monsoon. Interventions will respond to needs referred by SMS or communicated directly from the community to DRC Site Development. 5,000 meters of drainage cleaning and desilting before and during the monsoon. Sessions will also collect recommendations from households about how to reduce drainage blockages that can be implemented through this or future projects. Construction of 35 shared structures such as sheds over water points, micro soak pits for household greywater, silt traps, as well as strengthening community centres like mosques and learning centres. Interventions will respond to needs referred by other camp actors, community leaders or directly to DRC Site Development, and will be prioritized through consultations with Protection community groups of diverse ages, genders and abilities. Designs will strive to be as accessible as possible for children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Increase (by 10% from baseline) vegetative ground covering in the camps through plantings on unprotected slopes and tree-plantings led by small household clusters. DRC will organize household clusters near potential tree-planting plots and provide them with materials to plant and care for trees. Household clusters and DRC will conduct joint monitoring visits to ensure that trees are maintained. DRC will also provide selected vulnerable households with members who have difficulty/cannot leave their shelter due to disability, illness or cultural restrictions with materials for homestead gardening. Conduct 600 camp-wide assessment of existing infrastructure, prioritized in consultation with Protection community groups of various ages, genders and abilities. Assessments will include data about who is affected by the proposed works, the urgency of needs, and the GPS coordinates. Engagement of individuals from vulnerable households (20% - female-headed households, households of people with disabilities, and households that have a person with chronic illness, households hosting unaccompanied or separated children) in cash-for-work activities that are culturally appropriate and tailored to their abilities. Accommodations such as allowing individuals to work from home and providing them with seating and shade will be provided where possible to ensure that activities are accessible. Conduct regular meetings with block-level community groups (300 individuals) of diverse genders, ages and abilities to encourage community-led infrastructure maintenance. They will be responsible for checking the condition of all completed works in their care on a weekly basis and raise any deterioration or safety issues to DRC. They will also convey community feedback on completed works, ongoing site development activities, concerns and gaps to DRC. Conduct participatory workshops and engage 3,000 on DRR principles, environmental protection and fire safety with Protection community groups through gender- and age-segregated sessions. Groups will work with DRC to develop communications tailored to their peers and be given interpersonal communication training to disseminate the information to their community.
