Project Summary

Pathfinder intervention aims to improve the lives and livelihoods of adolescent girls and young women by reducing child, early, and forced marriage, and other types of GBV and enhancing their rights and knowledge, in Palma , Ancuabe, and Pemba districts as well to improve health outcomes in the communities in Palma and Pemba with the following key interventions:
• Work with communities and girls caregivers, to shift social norms and behaviors that perpetuate child, early, and forced marriage and other norms that limit girls agency.
• Develop adolescent girls’ and young women’s agency and intention to avoid early marriage, complete school, earn an income, and access health and social services.
• Support the health facilities to improve the provision of gender- and youth-responsive health services including post-GBV care as well as to expand social, and protection services.
• Work with institutions and systems to ensure laws and policies are enforced that discourage and prohibit child, early, and forced marriage, and to advance policies that enhance the rights of girls and young women.
• Provide social, educational, and economic opportunities to adolescent girls and young women to reduce early marriage

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Pathfinder will prioritize adolescents and youth (10-24), with particular focus on females. In addition, it will work with adolescents’ caregivers and influential people in the communities (community and religious leaders, madrinhas, TBAs, APEs etc.) and civil servants from key institutions and services (health providers, teachers, police, judges and attorneys)

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Core activities will be:Ancuabe & Pemba districts: GBV case management; health care for GBV survivors; psychosocial support; justice and legal aid; women and girls empowerment; GBV awareness raising activities. In Pemba also women and girls friendly spaces. Palma district: health care for GBV survivors; psychosocial support; GBV awareness raising activities among adolescent boys and girls in schools.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project staff and community network of activists are trained on PSEA, so that they are prepared to identify potentials risk cases. Also staff is sensitized to detect potential cases of discrimination against any groups (ex.IDPs). Trainings will be reinforced. Activities as the AGYW groups and household visits permit us to identify risks and refer them to services. The project raises awareness of the hotlines for reporting abuse and the existing services to assist cases of GBV. In the work with the health facilities to improve the health providers capacity and skills to manage GBV cases, the project promotes a no-harm and survivor-center approach

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project has a strong focus on empowerment of adolescent girls and young women (AGYW), as well as on participation of youth, particularly AGYW in decision making spaces, to have a meaningful engagement on issues related to their rights and SRH needs. Different interventions will be put in place to encourage very young adolescents, older adolescents and young women to learn about their rights and speak out about their needs. The project does not work with elderly people as direct beneficiaries (despite engagement of older influential people in the communities in project activities for social and gender norms discussions).

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project does not have specific actions to support people with disabilities, although promotes their inclusion in all project activities. When conditions permit, the project prioritizes spaces that people with mobile disabilities can easy access. During training sessions with health providers and school staff the project also raises awareness on the particular vulnerability of AGYW with disabilities to GBV. In the case of the vocational trainings, a specific quota will be reserved for young women with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project does not have serious effects on environment (in addition to car and plane travel carbon footprint). The project includes environment assessments in the health facilities to monitor correct waste management of clinical products with focus on the SRH and GBV areas, in order to reduce environmental impact.
