Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to Improving living condition, promotion of rights and physical safety to promote dignity and protection of returnees, displaced people and hosts communities in Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga and Chari divisions in Far North region of Cameroon. Indeed, since 2014, these populations have been victims of the Boko Haram crisis, which has a direct impact on the satisfaction of basic needs, including NFI and Shelter as presented by several surveys and assessments carried out both by the Cameroonian government and the international agencies and organizations (IOM, UNICEF, NRC,..). the project will be implemented both in the community and in schools.
The project will provide NFIs and emergency shelter kits for the construction of transitional shelters. The Shelter kit will be mainly composed on tarpaulin and ropes while bucket, soap, mosquito nets, mats, etc…). a total of 3,000 households (with 51% of women head of households) will benefit from the assistance. The distribution will be carried out in synergy with WASH hygiene kit distributions in order to provide a comprehensive WASH/shelter/NFI kit to beneficiaries. NRC will also support with construction materials the rehabilitation of permanent shelters when displaced populations return to their places of origin or integrate into host communities. A total of 300 shelters are planned, each shelter will also have benefited for one family latrine.
NRC will provide CVA for NFI in Logone and Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga. The aim will be to allow beneficiaries to purchase relevant items needed for their households near of their location. Before CVA implementation, a survey will be done to design the qualifying process, the modality and utilization by beneficiaries.
In the event of a sudden and massive arrival of the displaced, the project will also distribute the NFI and shelter kits
In schools, the project will rehabilitate classrooms and make gender-sensitive benches.
Ultimately, the project aims to reach about 23,100 beneficiaries (i.e. 51% women). IDPs will account for 50% of the target while the host community and returns will be 20% each and out of camp refugees 10%. Approximately 14,322‬ children under the age of 18 will benefit from the project's actions, compared to 692 elderlies (more than 59 years old).

Besoins / Needs

Access to shelter : The Far North is the poorest region of Cameroon. The Far North has been hard hit by the armed conflict with the Boko Haram (BH) group because of its proximity with northern Nigeria. According to IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), the Far North region is home to about 71,263 Households and 270,870 Internally Displaced Persons. The most recent data on displacement patterns as depicted in Displaced Tracking Matrix (DTM) August 2019 report indicated that the number of out of IDP increased. The most vulnerable population is elderly persons without family support, single women heads of households, persons with physical or mental handicaps, minors in charge of minors.The same report show that the majority of refugee live in hosts families. According to the multisectorial assesment done by NRC in january 2019, 34% of shelters in returned areas are destroyed and others ares in poor living conditions. Very few humanitarian actors intervene in the construction/rehabilitation of shelters for return areas in the Far North region.Access to NFI : In the NFI/Shelter sector, the lack of shelter and NFI is one of the main needs since the beginning of the conflict facing out of camp refugees.In addition, the Lake Chad Basin crisis in the Far North region has led to the destruction, abandonment and deterioration of schools in many villagesNRC's response will be in line with those of the NFI/Shelter cluster in order to meet the above needs mentioned for out of camp refugees in the departments of the Logone and Chari, the Mayo Sava and the Mayo Tsanaga. populations. It will also provide children of the target populations with the appropriate scholar infrastructures.
