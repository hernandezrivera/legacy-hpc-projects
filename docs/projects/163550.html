Project Summary

9 months of conflict has led to multiple displacements, loss of livelihood opportunities, and destruction of social support systems and disruption of services. Though men and boys have been affected, women and girls are particularly facing multiple risks of GBV due to the ongoing conflict. An estimated 166,227 people displaces and at risk to protection issues. Due to the erosion of the protective structures both formal and informal vulnerability to violence, exploitation and abuse has become ever more acute. According to UNFPA implementing partners reports indicates incidents of GBV have increased as a result of the ongoing conflict in Tripoli and other cities; incidents ranging from sexual violence including rape, physical violence, denial of resources, opportunities and services, intimate partner’s violence/domestic violence and child marriage. However, the numbers don’t reflect the actual prevalence rates, due to fear of retaliation and social stigma, lack of specialized services including lack of trust in existing health services and providers as a barrier for help; survivors fear that they will not receive the care they need due to the attitude, lack of supplies and capacity of service providers. Displaced women and girls, women-child headed household, separated and unaccompanied girls and boys, orphans, women and girls living with disabilities and from marginalized groups at greater risk. As a result the primary objective of the proposal is to “Support the provision case management, medical, psychosocial support and safe spaces for GBV survivors”. Interventions at aims at scaling up UNFPA’s current response; to address the following forms of violence that are prevalent according to partners data; sexual violence including rape, domestic violence/intimate partner violence, forced/early child marriage, psychological abuse, denial of resources , opportunities and services ; as the primary focus for intervention. Due to the sensitivity nature of GBV and the low level of capacity on the ground; UNFPA will support health including sexual reproductive health services and safe spaces as an entry point for GBV services most notably clinical management of rape. Support the expansion of medical, psychosocial care, and by using health facilities as an integrated service hubs with facilitated referral to other services chosen by survivors of rape, child marriage, domestic violence/intimate partner violence, and psychological abuse among others to services such as legal, police and shelter. UNFPA through the reproductive health working group will work to expand the number of facilities that are providing GBV services through partnership and provision of drugs and equipment and through CMR trainings strengthen the health providers capacity to respond to survivors needs. These clinical and psychological services will be complemented with facilitated referral to other needed services chosen by the GBV survivor, such as legal, police, shelter, etc. The GBV case management services will be provided at all levels by the health workers and targeted service providers trained in GBV /case management including GBV referral; in GBV psychosocial counseling and in GBV data management. Develop cities specific GBV referral maps/pathways with clear identified and trained focal points. support safe spaces; as a safe gateway that enable them to access information, reproductive health and GBV services in a non-stigmatized manner thus ensuring safety and dignity. communication materials will be developed to raise awareness on the availability of GBV health services as an entry point to GBV Services. GBV hotline will be another entry point for GBV survivors to reach out to the needed services of psychosocail and legal counseling.

1 - Needs

This project is unique by way of providing a comprehensive response to survivors of GBV. The approach encompasses coordination among key humanitarian sectors on provision of GBV services from coordination and referral to reintegration of survivors to the community, such that a holistic approach in responding to the current crisis is adopted. On the other hand, a survivor centered approach is adopted by way of saving and protecting the lives of survivors and at risk women and girls through the establishment of comprehensive, multi-sectoral GBV services. The bulk of health services currently provided across Libya facilities are limited in the provision of medical care to GBV survivors, with a majority of health providers focusing on trauma. Medical care in health facilities is almost non-existent, with less than 0ne percent of these facilities providing clinical management of rape. UNFPA through the Inter-Agency Working Group on Reproductive Health aims to expand the number of health facilities that are providing GBV services through partnerships and provision of drugs, equipment and trainings on clinical management of rape. UNFPA aims to ensure a comprehensive provision of services to GBV survivors who require clinical care and emotional support that result from their experience of GBV, which includes, fear, resentment, blame and hopelessness. These clinical and psychological services will be complemented with facilitated referral to other needed services chosen by the GBV survivor, such as legal, police, shelter and other required services The GBV case management services will be provided at all levels by the health workers and targeted service providers trained in GBV case management, including GBV referral, GBV psychosocial counseling and GBV data management. The proposed project will also support the distribution of UNFPA post rape treatment kits (Kit3) in both static and mobile health facilities, ensuring health facilities have adequate clinical supplies for provision of GBV services (Through UNFPA reproductive health intervention). As GBV survivors have multiple needs, the proposed project will support the development of functioning GBV referral systems from communities to health providers including psychosocial care, shelters, legal and security. Thus, the proposed project aims to develop cities specific GBV referral pathways with clearly identified and trained focal points to facilitate the functioning of the referral system supported by a standard operating procedure. In order to track functionality of the GBV referral system and provide peer counseling support. Issues from GBV case management meeting which require structural or policy support will be referred to the needed services. It is critical that women and girls especially in the current humanitarian context access safe spaces that enable them to obtain information, support and services. A safe space is a formal or informal place where women and girls feel physically and emotionally safe. The term ‘safe,’ in the present context, refers to the absence of trauma, excessive stress, violence (or fear of violence), or abuse. It is a space where women and girls, being the intended beneficiaries, feel comfortable and enjoy the freedom to express themselves without the fear of judgment or harm. Safe spaces are an integral part of ensuring survivors access comprehensive GBV services without stigma and fear of retaliation from their families and communities.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 Provide medical, psychological services and legal assistance through tailored case management approach Activity 2 Provide Training to health providers to deliver quality care to survivors including CMR, GBV case screening, identification male survivors and to provide services that are responsive to specific needs of women, girls, men and boys from clinical and psychological perspective. Activity 3 Conduct case management training as a means for expanding survivors access to comprehensive services. Activity 4 Develop functioning GBV referral system for each targeted cities across Libya. Activity 5 Establish four Women and Girls Safe Centers.Activity 6 Conduct community dialogue, awareness raising on GBV prevention and response, as well as dissemination of GBV services to the community to promote self-referral. Activity 7 Procure and distribute dignity kits to the most vulnerable women and girls affected by the conflict through the four Women and Girls Safe Space. On another hand, and through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) project, UNFPA will target 12,977 new displaced women and girls with dignity kits in addition to 1038 pregnant women with mama kits and baby kits. Through RRM, UNFPA will also run rapid assessments and provide logistic and distribution services working jointly with UNICEF, IOM and WFP.Activity 8 support in running the GBV hotline 1417 to provide PSS services

Ensure staffs complies with safety and security controls; Continuous update and monitoring of local actors’, mapping and strong networking with local communities for ownership and support to enable stronger and smoother communication channels with both state and non-state actors. 6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNFPA Libya country programme stakeholders and in particularly beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regards to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of UNFPA’s activities. This is done through conducting focus group discussion with the GBV survivors in the women and girls safe space. UNFPA will use beneficiary feedback mechanism which includes; for health facilities and providers-client feedback forms and key informant interviews will be conducted. UNFPA will also roll out the client feedback forms in the safe spaces and other service points and conduct focus group discussions and key informant interview with survivors and case workers. The submissions and report will be used to produce quarterly analysis. The programme will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV. At the outset, the programme will set-up a beneficiary feedback mechanism through which affected populations will be able to inform of the challenges that the programme may cause or will be face and evaluate the adequacy of programme interventions. This information will be utilized to guide work planning and implementation. Implementer's will be made accountable to address beneficiaries’ concerns and complaints within a limited time-frame and report on (or to the broader public, if complaints are anonymous) on how they have addressed. Beneficiaries’ feedback is a key indicators to measure performance under this programme.
