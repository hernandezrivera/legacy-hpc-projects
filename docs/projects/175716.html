Project Summary

SPEDP proposes 12months humanitarian response plan for the year 2021 to continue and expand its integrated Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services to the vulnerable population in Yei and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria; Old Fangak and Pigi Canal Counties of Jonglei State and Rubkona County of Unity State. The project will provide timely equitable access to safe clean water, adequate sanitation, and improved hygiene behaviors through an integrated approach with WASH-nutrition in Yei and Pigi Canal; WASH-health in Yei; WASH-GBV in Yei and Old Fangak, and provision of access to basic WASH services in Rubkona and lainya Counties. SPEDP will conduct its response in a holistic manner through coordination and collaboration with nutrition, health and GBV partners in the respective project locations. This will contribute to mitigation and reduction of high levels of malnutrition, WASH related diseases and Covid-19, gender based violence (GBV) issues as well as lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices .
This response plan is an accompaniment to SPEDP’s ongoing health project in Yei and lainya of Central Equatoria State; ongoing health and WASH projects in Rubkona of unity State and WASH project in Old Fangak and Canal Pigi of Jonglei State that ended on March 2020. The proposed intervention will reach a target of 23,436 vulnerable people (5437men, 5752women, 6013boys and 6234 girls) among internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities including 586 disabled people and people with special needs. The project interventions will target 794 individuals for WASH and Nutrition; 4,276 individuals for WASH and Health; 10,937 individuals for WASH and GBV and 23,436 individuals with access to basic WASH services. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected people ensuring that services are delivered in a gender sensitive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, people living with disabilities, etc. Regular meetings will be conducted with older people and people with disabilities within the community to monitor safety and access issues to inform the design of WASH facilities.
The activities will include:- Construction of new water points after GBV risk analysis; rehabilitation of water points in health facilities and community level after GBV risk analysis; construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in health facilities and community level after GBV risk analysis; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing in order to reduce water and sanitation related diseases and Covid-19; hygiene promotion education sessions and use of IEC materials on hand washing with soap or ash at critical times; domestic hygiene (food handling); safe excreta/solid disposal; water protection and environmental hygiene through door to door visits, use of mega phones and media; distribution of standard hygiene kits to mothers or care takers of children admitted for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) treatment at outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/SC and sessions on menstrual hygiene management and provision of mentrual hygiene kits to schools girls and young women.

1 - Needs

The five years of conflict, economic crisis and recurrent flooding in South Sudan has negatively impacted on the delivery of WASH services hence WASH continues to be a great challenge in the Country. It is anticipated that the living standard and well-being of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. Due to the 2016 conflict, most people in the defunct Yei River State fled their homes to neighboring Uganda and Democratic Republic of Congo meanwhile others remained as internally displaced persons (IDPs). Although people started returning voluntarily in small numbers due to the signing and slow implementation of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (RTGoNU), the returnees and the host communities face challenge of access to adequate water due to breakdown of the water facilities hence leading to tension and conflict over the scarce resources. The Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation (MWRI) states that 30-50 percent of water facilities are non-functional at any point in time due to the lack of spare part supply chains, weak maintenance capacity, poor management and/or inappropriate choice of technology. Forty-one per cent of South Sudanese households access their water from an unimproved water sources. For those using an improved water source, 34 percent travel less than 30 minutes to and from the source while the remaining travel more than 30 minutes. The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are in Jonglei and Central Equatoria. In Old Fangak of Jonglei State, the (Inter-Agency 29th to 30th January 2021 assessment report) indicated that most of the population draw their water from the river, swamps and ponds and few get their water from boreholes which are contaminated by the riverine flood. While Pigi Canal has no any single access to borehole. The population in Pigi Canal relies on surface water sources. In spite of the fact that the overwhelming majority draw their water from sources which are susceptible to contamination, water treatment is one of the most unpopular practices in Fangak and Pigi Canal. Furthermore, only 13 percent of the population has access to adequate sanitation facilities, random open defecation is the order of the day in Fangak and Pigi Canal with feces along the roads, and water sources. Beyond limited access to improved water and sanitation there is low knowledge and practice of good hygiene behaviors. The lack of access to improved water and sanitation and poor hygiene behaviors is a principal cause of water-related diseases such as acute watery diarrhea, ARI, cholera etc. and contributes to malnutrition. In addition to above dilemmas, an estimated 6.35 of the population of South Sudan are facing severe acute food insecurity (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification [IPC] Phase 3 or above) with a projected 7.2 million people (60%), to face severe acute food insecurity between May and July 2021, of whom over 2.5 million are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Additionally, 20 per cent of IDPs and 28 per cent of returnees live in settlements where people feel unsafe when they go to collect water, highlighting the protection risks especially for women and girls who experience harassment, assault and sexual violence while collecting water far from their homes. Armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 restrictions and flooding have exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV. It is against this background that SPEDP proposes to remedy this situation through integrated approach to reduce and mitigate high levels of malnutrition, WASH related diseases and Covid-19, gender based violence (GBV) issues as well as lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices among the IDPs, returnees and host communities in the targeted project locations.

2 - Project activities

The key interventions SPEDP will be addressing in the targeted Counties of Central Equatoria, Jonglei and Unity are as follows:– a) Water Supply: 1-SPEDP will conduct drilling of 5 boreholes in health facilities and community level in order to provide vulnerable populations in Yei, Rubkona and Lainya with access to safe clean water; rehabilitation or repair of 35 boreholes (hand pumps) at community level and health facilities in the targeted project locations. rehabilitation of 4 water yards and protection of 3 shallow wells. Women, girls, elderly and people with disabilities will be consulted for the allocation and design of the water points constructed or rehabilitated. Each water point will be located at 500 meter walking distance serving 500 people with safe water, a total of 23,436 individuals will be served with access to safe clean water for drinking, hygiene and domestic purposes. All the water sources will be chlorinated and water quality test done for the microbiological quality to mitigate against malnutrition disorders. Steps and ramps etc for the boreholes will be provided for easy access for children, elderly and disabled people.2. Training of 47 water management committees (7members per water source), a total of 329 members (214 females and 115 males) to provide ongoing maintenance of the water point, and fencing for effective and efficient functioning of the water sources. Covid-19 SOPs will be strictly maintained during the training.3. Training and refresher training for 94 individual(66 male and 28female) new or existing pump mechanics to equip them with knowledge on operation and maintenance of water sources to ensure effective and efficient functioning of the water sources when the project phases out. Covid-19 SOPs will be strictly maintained during the training. b) Sanitation:1. SPDEP will construct 65 shared household latrines in densely populated settlements and rehabilitate 20 blocks of 4 or 3 stances of institutional latrines in all the project locations based on the assessed needs. Women, girls, elderly and people with disabilities will be consulted for the allocation and design of the latrines constructed or rehabilitated. Steps, ramps hand rails etc for the latrines will be provided for easy access for the children, elderly disabled people. Both shared household and institutional latrines will be lockable from inside for the safety and dignity of the users to mitigate GBV risks. 2. SPEDP will conduct sanitation clean up campaigns (garbage collection) in markets, community and household level in all the project locations to remove existing debris to minimize public health hazards. The community will be provided with sanitation digging kits for excavation of rubbish pits at household levelfor safe disposal of household waste. c) Hygiene:1. SPEDP will conduct hygiene promotion in the target areas. Training and messaging will address water treatment, hand washing, food handling, proper latrine use and maintenance, waste management, human excreta disposal and Covid-19 all of which are in dire state due to the increased IDP and returnee populations. SPEDP will train 46 Community hygiene promoters (26male and 20female) to disseminate key hygiene and Covid-19 messages in a participatory manner through outreach activities i.e. mass education, use mega phones, drama, door-to-door awareness visits by hygiene promoters and inclusion of hygiene messages in poems in schools. Formation and training 15 school hygiene clubs with 40 members per club (20boys and 20girls) to promote hygiene in schools and children to be messengers of change at home. SPEDP will provide 2,500 adolescent girls and women of child bearing age with menstrual hygiene kits (MHK) and accompanied with informative sessions on menstruation management. Families of children admitted for SAM and MAM treatment will be provided with WASH NFI kits and hygiene promotion and access to safe water to facilities in Yei and Pigi Canal.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The gender focal person for SPEDP will be dedicated to discus with women and girls on the GBV issues affecting them once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of females. The project will target both sexes (male and female) with gender and age disaggregation. Our commitment to beneficiary protection (including protection of sexual exploitation and abuse) and communities will be continuously consulted on the process and contents of interventions conducted; local support, including males and females, will be recruited on a temporary basis to support the project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation and ensure that the intervention is locally appropriate, sensitive and relevant. SPEDP takes into account women’s dignity and security during WASH service provision to reduce possible risks of GBV and fear of not using the facilities. Also protection of females against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) during access to WASH service will be closely monitored. The project will provide trainings and orientations to project staff on Programme, Participation, Protection, and Policy (P4) and PSEA policy. It will focus on the five working principles: 1) rights of all, 2) participation and access to decision-making, 3) non-discrimination and equal access, 4) accountability and access to rule of law and 5) transparency and access to information. The other factors such as cultural, gender norms, values and beliefs will take into account while developing behavioral change communication messages.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will be set up and made functional and accessible to the targeted community. The CFRM will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. The project will measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls). Access barriers could be at following level i.e. within IDP, refugee returnees and host communities and age and sex. SPEDP has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to report concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of services, whereby the hygiene promoters and water point attendants will gather information daily and report to the Project Manager. Additionally, SPEDP will gather complaints and feedback through the weekly FGD. The SPEDP gender focal person will be dedicated to conduct this activity once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of females. Through its activities, SPEDP will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the project managers will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project in order sort remedial action.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.2. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - SPEDP has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy which includes PSEA. Ongoing orientation for new and existing staff and affiliates will be completed. SPEDP adheres to a zero tolerance policy of SEA. 8.8. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources, SPEDP will remedy this risk through through consultative and inclusive approach with both primary and secondary stakeholders. 8.9. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area - In order to mitigate local disputes around the land ownership following clearance operations, SPEDP will liaise with local authorities, traditional and religious leaders even before the teams start operating. A handover certiﬁcate will be issued to local leaders after the land being cleared and a community meeting will be called to ensure transparency and accountability. Additionally, SPEDP will closely monitor the socio-economic impact of its work by utilizing its own methodologies and dedicated M&E mechanism to ensure that targeted local communities remain at their locations and use the cleared land for agricultural or hunting purposes in an eﬃcient way. This will help building their resilience and pave the way for self-suﬃciency at community and household levels. It will also reduce the level of vulnerability towards humanitarian aid. 8.10. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area - Risk will be mitigated by prioritizing accreditation of EORE Teams in a timely manner (refresher trainings and ongoing capacity development). EORE Teams are equipped with mobile phones of key focal points at UNMAS and NMSS should immediate assistance be required. Further, EORE Teams carry identifying materials for themselves and communities to be able to mark any UXOs discovered.

Children admitted for severe acute malnutrition will also be provided with standard WASH hygiene kits to improve on their living conditions.
