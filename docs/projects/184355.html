Project Summary

Increased fragility due to constant climatic shocks including drought, floods and locust invasion, conflict and insufficient governance systems continue to perpetuate complex humanitarian situation that has persistent for almost four decades now. As a result, FSNAU estimates nearly 3.5 million to be facing food shortages putting them in crisis (IPC 3) with 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. This puts more demand on an already insufficient health system that is barely coping with the increased demand created by COVID-19 and increasing non-communicable disease burden. The health system is poorly funded, and the government lacks the capacity to effectively coordinate the actions of numerous NGOs and donors who are all trying to play a role but most of the times duplicate efforts as they concentrate services in urban towns and areas accessible to their staff. These leaves large population in remote villages under the control of armed groups without access to basic health services. Vulnerable populations such as women, boys, girls, persons with disability, urban migrants, refugees, and minority clans are worst affected by the crisis.
The proposed project plans to expand the availability of basic health services for 417,733 vulnerable population with limited or no access to basic health services in Hiran(3 districts), Banaadir(6 districts) and Lower Shabelle(2 districts). Among the target population are ; 103,436 urban migrants and 314,297 host community. Children under five (77,506), persons with disability (38,794) and women of childbearing age (78,804) will be access services mirrored on the Essential Package of health services (EPHS 2020) focusing mainly on maternal and child health services and treatment of noncommunicable diseases. Outreach services (8 mobile units) will be deployed to reach marginalized populations.
Strong human resource capacity will be built by recruiting and training additional nurses, doctors, and midwives to enable them to provide quality health services. Essential supplies including drugs and other non-pharmaceuticals will be supplied and appropriately stored. Referral will be established using well equipped ambulances (3) that will transfer complicated pregnancies, children with danger signs and cases of trauma to hospitals and other levels of health care to avail specialised care.
Strong community mobilisation and awareness on behaviour change will be prioritised in the project. Community health workers will be trained and deployed to sensitise the community on sexual and gender-based violence, early marriage and FGM. SGBV (Sexual and Gender-based violence) survivors will access confidential Clinical management of rape, counselling, and referral for severe cases. Similarly, services will be friendly and accessible to persons with disability. WARDI will ensure are oriented on the special needs of people with disability and how to offer quality services, dedicated staff will be trained to handle persons with disability. Health messaging will be designed in such away that persons with varying disabilities can easily access information.
Cognizant of the COVID-19 pandemic situation, WARDI will ensure health facilities offer services in protective environment that does not endanger the health of staff and patients. Well-equipped triages will be established in the entrance of all health facilities and other COVID-19 protocols enforced. Staff will be trained on COVID-19 and continuously supervised to ensure compliance.

1. - Projects activities

Objective: Improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis affected population to 103,436 IDPs and 314,297 non-displaced populationThe project activities will include the following:1) Provide curative and preventive health services including management of childhood illnesses and outpatient consultations at the outpatient department in 13 health facilities and 8 mobile health units.2) Provide outreach services to expand basic services to 125,320 marginalized populations through 8 mobile health units.3) Provide quality and safe Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care Services (CEmONC) for 78,804 women of childbearing age.4) Expand the availability of routine immunization for 16,709 children under the age of one year to reduce morbidity and mortality related to vaccine preventable childhood illnesses.5) Screen 46,233 children under 5(23,597 girls and 22,654 boys) will be screened for acute malnutrition and identified cases managed or referred for appropriate care.6) Provide skilled delivery for 11,697 pregnant women.7) Provision of confidential care and services for 11,070 sexual and gender-based violence survivors (SGBV).8) Provide micronutrition supplementation for 36,255 pregnant and lactating women.9) Training of 869(36 male and 50 female) frontline health workers on Integrated management of childhood illnesses, BEmONC, integrated management of acute malnutrition, clinical management of rape.10) Ensure reporting on key national health indicators using the District Health Information System(DHIS 2) and surveillance of disease through EWARN.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

WARDI will closely engage communities and governments to advocate for protection for young boys and girls to exposure. Community level sensitization on the need for protection for children and risk of recruiting children to armed groups will be conducted through community level health workers. Further services will be taken closer to populations to eliminate the risk associated with travelling long distance specially for women and children. Facilities and mobile units sites will be well lit to ensure safety of beneficiaries.
