Project Summary

This project will provide Shelter and NFIs to support 29,863 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities seeking refuge in Twic , Cueibet aand Nyirol due to internal subnational conflicts, flooding, conflict in Sudan and food aid suspension in neighboring refugee settlements in Ethiopia, Uganda and Kenya. The returnees need ES/NFI and resilience support, and are still either settling within the host communities in these locations, while finding it hard to sustain their families, or sheltering in public places like schools, churches and other government institutions. NH has recently implemented and Emergency GBV project in Nyirol and transitional justice projects in Cuiebet and Twic and according to the latest IPC reports released on 6th November 2023, estimated 35,000 people are classified in IPC phase 5, including Nyirol with 3000 and Duk respectively , S/NFI remain a lifesaving support needed in Nyirol where this project will be implemented . NH will provide lifesaving shelter and NFIs in-kind support focusing on the following cluster objectives: the percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition (new and protracted displacements); enhance community dignity and engagement in the provision of S/NFI services; the percentage of beneficiaries who have access to safe, child and gender sensitive complaint, feedback and reporting channels in humanitarian response sites for reporting sensitive issues and concerns in aid delivery and misconduct by aid workers disaggregated by age, sex and diversity; and community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures. Narrative Hub will put much consideration to people with special needs, concerns and prioritize supports to women and girls mostly affected by the crisis, such as persons with disability, pregnant and lactating women, elderly, child headed families and other vulnerabilities with the aim of incorporating protection principles during the project implementation. As a short-term intervention, the impact on the population and on the contextual dynamics is sensitive and therefore delicate balances are at play, and for this reason the team will ensure that all accountability processes to the affected population are carefully followed. The intervention will be clearly designed and protection concerns taken into consideration so that distributions respect community dynamics, community engagement and participation in a dignified and meaningful way. Contextual feedback mechanisms will be established in line with the distribution method and the location dynamics. Beneficiaries will be identified using approved South Sudan S/NFIs cluster criteria of selection. In addition, Narrative Hub will endeavor to give priority to child-headed households, women, persons with disability, the elderly and other vulnerable people in the community who will benefit from Emergency Shelter and NFI distribution. Narrative Hub will aspire to conduct joint missions including joint assessments and responses when needed in order to better respond to the complex needs of the beneficiaries, pull resources together and minimize overlap. The project activities will include:
• Needs analysis of Shelter and NFIs needs
• Beneficiary selection based on the most critical needs
• Distribution of emergency shelter/NFI to population in need
• Rapid monitoring and Post distribution monitoring survey
• Training to local partners on ES/NFI cluster response methodology, CVA programming, and HLP
• Awareness creation on disaster risk reduction and climate change including setting up committees and plans

1. Needs

According Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FAWS NET) report of September 2023, in the northern counties bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. Nyirol and Cuiebet counties remain of high concern, where some households are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) according to IPC report released on the 6th November 2023, most especially in Nyirol. Due to multiple years of asset and livelihood erosion exacerbated by the arrival of South Sudanese returnees and conflict-related disruption to trade flow with Sudan. The acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying according to the latest IPC report, with the latest data showing that 5.83 million people (46.3 percentage of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). An estimated 35,000 people are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in Nyirol (3,000) county of Jonglei State, while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61.3 percent), Upper Nile State (56.1 percent) and Lakes State (52.8 percent). The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023, attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, and cattle raids continued to displace people. Cattle raids were characterized by multiple forms of violence against civilians, including killing, injury, rape, abduction, looting and destruction of housing and property.The Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 16 as of 20th October 2023 by UNOCHA noted that South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families. As of 16 October, 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5 per cent of all arrivals. New arrivals continue to report harassment, intimidation, torture and extortion of money during their journey to South Sudan, including by security personnel along the route. Life-saving assistance is needed for returnees, including shelter and non-food items, food, water, sanitation facilities and protection services. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has had a significant impact on trade between Sudan and South Sudan. The reduced imports from Sudan have led to a rise in the cost of the food basket. According to market monitoring conducted by humanitarian partners, the cost of the food basket has increased significantly since the start of the Sudan crisis. An IRNA assessments conducted in Twic, Nyirol and Cuiebet counties between June and December 2022 to determine the effect of flooding and subnational conflicts on the population reveals an estimated 95,462 has been affected. This population, particularly is in urgent need of shelter, nonfood items and protection services. A number of protection concerns were noted in the settlement areas. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls.

2. Project activities

This project will focus on the following cluster objectives: the percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition (new and protracted displacements); enhance community dignity and engagement in the provision of S/NFI services; the percentage of beneficiaries who have access to safe, child and gender sensitive complaint, feedback and reporting channels in humanitarian response sites for reporting sensitive issues and concerns in aid delivery and misconduct by aid workers disaggregated by age, sex and diversity; and community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures. The following activities will be implemented: • Conduct a needs analysis of Shelter and NFIs needs• Conduct a beneficiary selection based on the most critical needs • Conduct distribution of in-kind, cash and voucher emergency shelter/NFI to beneficiaries in need • Construct shelter to beneficiaries with people with special needs and critical condition.• Rapid monitoring and Post distribution monitoring assessment using cluster recommended tools.• Conduct training sessions for partners on CVA programming• Conduct training sessions for partners on cluster response methodology• Conduct awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction• Develop Disaster Management Committees and Climate Change Adaptation Plans• Conduct training sessions on HLP (Due diligence, and access rights and challenges)
