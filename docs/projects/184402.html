Project Summary

Recent Post Gu FSNAU nutrition analysis (2021 post-Gu) indicates that at the national level the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained at a serious level of 11.1%. Based on this analysis and the latest population figures, it is estimated that in 2022 around 1.19 million children, under the age of five years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently including 213,400 will be severely acute malnourished children (SAM) and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM) children. In addition to this, around 179,899 Pregnant & Lactating Women (PLWs), and 629,567 children between 6-23 months will be at risk of acute malnutrition and hence need both curative and preventive nutrition services.
The predisposing factors for acute malnutrition in Somalia remain food insecurity. Based on the latest FSNAU projection survey report between October and December 2021 shows food insecurity will further deteriorate among the poor rural, urban, and displaced population due to anticipated, below-average Deyr rainfall, continued insecurity. Poor access to clean and safe drinking water and sanitation facilities leads to acute watery diarrhea (AWD) which predisposes children and leads to acute malnutrition.
The total number of IDPs population that are in urgent need of nutrition services is 0.3 million including women and children. IDPs population in Somalia faces serious and numerous challenges in accessing nutrition services and based recently FSNAU survey shows that IDPs has a high GAM rate of over 15%(IPC4). The covid-19 pandemic has resulted in challenges related to the accessibility of children into the nutrition services as well as supply due to logistical challenges posed by the global lockdowns.
The above challenging factors in combination with the already existing weak health services delivery system, outbreaks, and high prevalence of AWD, food insecurity due to lack of purchasing power will predispose Somalia children between the age of 6-59 months to all forms of acute malnutrition.
To address the predisposing factors and ensure malnourished children from 6-59 months access nutrition services, IOM proposes the following interventions,
- Integrate nutrition services to 22 static and 13 mobile team across Somalia (Puntland- Sanaag Region, SWS- Baidoa district, Jubbaland- Afmadow, Lower Juba, Gedo region (Badhere, and Dolow), Somaliland-Sanaag region( Ceel Afweyn).
- Train and capacity building the local staff on the management of acute malnutrition and IYCF.
- Conduct health education and promotion to prevent AWD outbreaks.
- Screen children from acute malnutrition and admit them at the health facility if diagnosed for severe acute malnutrition and refer children with moderate acute malnutrition to the nearest MAM providing services.
- Screen PLW for acute malnutrition and refer to the nearest TSFP providing services.
- Ensure IYCF influencers are capacitated with the right information on IYCF practice and its importance to the prevention of malnutrition.
- Provide children between 6-59 months with vitamin A supplementation.
- Implement Mother Led MUAC project to empower mothers and caregivers to screen their children and refer to the nearest health center for further treatment
- Conduct a SMART and KAP survey to strengthen the surveillance system in the country.
IOM will partner with MOH to implement the proposed activities and continue to work with relevant stakeholders, clusters, both federal and state Ministry of health, and local authorities to avoid disruption and duplication of services and resources.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1: Integrate 22 health and 13 mobile sites in Jubaland, Southwest State, Somaliland, and Puntland. Activity 1.2: screen children between the age of 6-59 months in both the community and the health center with a target number of 105,600(52,800 boys and 52,800 girls). Activity 1.3: Identify and treat 3,500 children aged between 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition(SAM). Activity 1.4: Provide 40,000 pregnant and lactating women with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling and training, combined with and linkage to immunization services especially measles vaccination. Activity 1.5: provide vitamin A supplementation to children admitted to the program. Activity 2.1: Preposition and distribute ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) and medicines to 22 static and 13 mobile teams. Activity 2.2: Establish referral mechanisms from community level to OTP sites, and from OTP sites to/from other nutrition programs Activity 3.1: Provide training and on-the-job mentorship for 200 clinical health and nutrition staff (100 women, 100 men) across all OTP sites on IMAM and IYCF guidelines. Activity 3.2: Train 300 community outreach nutrition volunteers (150 women, 150 Men) via on-job training who will provide MUAC screening, referral to OTPs, and counseling in catchment communities of the OTPs. Activity 4.1 Conduct SMART surveys and IYCF surveys to strengthen monitoring and surveillance, analysis, and utilization of early warning information. Activity 5.1: Implement Mother lead MUAC project so that mother can identify and refer children with malnutrition to the nearest health centers.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

2. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse to access to humanitarian assistance.The project will hire female staff members at project locations to ensure the beneficiaries (women and girls at the sites) feel more comfortable talking to them as will prevent sexual exploitation and abuses at the workplaces. The project will also employ females as community health workers to educate women about the dangers of sexual violence and possible of preventing and reporting to the nearest GBV centers. Girls and boys will actively be engaged in children's nutrition, health, and hygiene promotion groups/campaigns. In the event there is a GBV survivor at the project site, the individual will be linked to the relevant protection body and will be provided with a referral system. 4. Risk of exposure of violence because of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. Outpatients therapeutic service locations across Somalia, will be situated in central, visible places within the settlements and not more than 500 meters away from settlements. This will help to protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing the services. Each integrated OTP site will have separate toilet facilities within internal protection(locks) in place for women and men in OTPs with pictorial signs to ensure they are gender-specific. During the setup of the toilets, threats are anticipated and will be considered. To avoid people coming late hours in the clinics and prevent harm to the women, the OTP sites will be operating during the day. IOM will ensure nutrition services are secure with perimeter wall and minimum and security guard on duty to protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing services. Facilities will also be situated in a secure environment. Coordination will be made with shelter/NFI partners on the ground for solar installation.
