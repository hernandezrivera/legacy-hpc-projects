Project Summary

This project plan is designed to respond to the persistent Health Insecurities in six (6) Counties that have continued to show acute malnutrition and high prevalence of diseases. The Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Oct 2022 indicated, the main drivers of these disasters are multiple phenomena including; climatic shocks, conflicts and economic down streaming. climatic shocks (Floods and dry spells) have submerged Health infrastructures, destroyed supplies impeded access to Health care services and above all affected productivity of crop and livestock yieds1
Conflict and Insecurity equally continued to disrupt livelihoods and caused mass displacements and affected functionality of social services including health care service delivery. There is an increasing vulnerability caused by the prolonged crises the Counties of; Canal Pigi, Akobo, Ayod, Tonj North, Mayendit and Kapoeta East. The South Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Dec 2022-March 20203 projection indicated acute food insecurity, high levels of malnutrition and persistent trend of prevalence of diseases such as Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), Respiratory Tract Infections (RTIs), measles and other vaccines preventable diseases2 These Counties host most of the IPC 4 & IPC 5 Populations facing the worst levels of crises and catastrophe.
Health Action Africa (HAA) works in the above Counties since 2017 and therefore edited the 2022 response plan to 2023 response plan to support mobilization of resource for continuation of Humanitarian Assistance to the Populations described facing crises and Catastrophe in these Counties.
This 2023 Project Response plan proposed to support thirty (30) Mobile clinics to address the acute Health Insecurities these Counties. HAA planned to continue supporting 5 Mobile clinics in each County.
Key Activities: The Key activities are adopted from the Health cluster emergence clinical Package. These key activities includes;
Support health facilities with essential supplies and drugs, deployment of mobile teams, conduct outpatient and In-patient Consultation and treatment (OPD &IPD) at health facilities(Mobile Clinics) for effective case management of either Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea(AWD), Respiratory Tract Infection(RTIs) and other endemic diseases, vaccination against measles and vaccine preventable diseases, training of health care workers in clinical management of rape (CMR),Health Promotion, disease prevention and risk communication, Conduct reproductive Health services including The Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEMONC), carry out disease surveillance /EWARN and other activities the Health Cluster may delegate to be implemented.
The above modality is carefully aligned to all the three Health cluster Objectives. The activities above meets the CLHEA/CO1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations;
CLHEA/CO2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and
CLHEA/CO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms
The 2023 response plan is designed to reach 52,983 beneficiaries.

2 - Project activities

The project activities are adopted from the Health cluster emergence clinical package. The Key activities that will be implemented are; Support health facilities with essential supplies and drugs, conduct deployment of mobile teams to priority locations, conduct outpatient and In-patient Consultation and treatment (OPD &IPD) at health facilities(Mobile Clinics) for effective case management of either Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea(AWD), Respiratory Tract Infection(RTIs) and other endemic diseases, Conduct vaccination against measles and vaccine preventable diseases. This will be complemented with Vitamin A supplementation. Facilitate training of health care workers in clinical management of rape (CMR), Conduct Health Promotion, disease prevention and risk communication at both facility and community level, Conduct reproductive Health services including The Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEMONC); ante-post natal care, screening for vaccination needs, MUAC screening for malnutrition needs, skilled deliveries, family planning and safe motherhood. Carry out disease surveillance; early warning alert Response and the Integrated Disease surveillance reporting EWARN/IDSR Provide training on COVID 19 and Key messages, conduct awareness raising campaigns on COVID 19 Mental Health services; provide first line management of mental health services, trauma management, psycho-social support therapy and subsequent referrals Conduct effective coordination with the relevant stakeholders; SMoH at state level, CHD at county level, local authorities, health cluster and other humanitarian agencies

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The participants will be constituted from the local stakeholders comprising the local authorities such as the office of the County commissioners representatives, The County Relief and Rehabilitative Commissions representatives (RRC), County Health Department (CHDS), community representatives, youth groups (males and females), representatives of persons with special needs, and humanitarian partners. This forum/workshop allows women, men, boys, and girls from various backgrounds to participate in meaningful programming on common issues affecting the vulnerable persons facing persistent health insecurities. Communication, Information sharing and transparency: The project will ensure all persons of concern, women, men, boys, and girls of diverse backgrounds, have access to timely, accurate, and relevant information on (i) their rights and entitlements and (ii) the project activities. HAA will also communicate and share information in languages, formats, and culturally appropriate media for and accessible to all community groups through health promotions and campaigns. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA (Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), Do No Harm Policy: Health Action Africa (HAA) has adopted the PSEA policy from the UN (United Nations) core humanitarian Standards. The HAA management will ensure project staff receive a PSEA refresher training to ensure compliance with the protection of anyone, whether direct or indirect beneficiaries, while protecting project staff from all forms of inappropriate sexual conduct. The HAA Human Resource officer will conduct this refresher training. The summarized PSEA code of conduct will be displayed on stand banners in all offices or project locations and during project implementations, refresher trainings will be complemented by creating awareness every month to ensure all staff, representatives, or third parties connected with HAA are aware of the lofty standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect adults or children from any form of sexual abuse and exploitation Dissemination of PSEA Key zero-tolerance messages to project staff: The key messages include: exchanging money, goods, services, or employment for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading, or exploitative behavior use of child or adult to procure sex for others. Any actual or attempted abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, trust, or dependency for sexual or sexualized purposes. These include offering or promising monetary and social benefits as an incentive or form of coercion. HAA will be strict on discouraging the cultivation of emotional relationships with those in positions of vulnerability or inequitable power to manipulate these relationships into sexualized dynamics in the future Prevention: through awareness and good practice, ensuring that all staff minimize the risks of any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. PSEA Reporting: All project staff will be familiarized with the reporting mechanism for PSEA to ensure that they are clear on the steps to take where suspicions or concerns arise regarding allegations of Sexual Exploitation or abuse of adults in vulnerable populations. HAA will take immediate action /response to identify and address reports of sexual exploitation and abuse and ensure the safety and well-being of the sexually exploited or abused.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

This intervention is only designed to take health care interventions to populations facing Health insecurities affected by either floods or any other disaster.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Safe access to Health care services: The planned response clinical package under this project is mobile response modality. The Mobile clinics will be established within the communities to provide a comprehensive continuum of health care; Curative Services, Health promotion and prevention services and protected referrals. A trained Health Care workforce will be deployed to the mobile sites and essential health care commodities; Drugs and vaccines will be mobilized from the core pipe line managers; WHO, UNFPA, UNICEF and the National Malaria Control Program(NMCP-MoH). At the mobile clinics, there will be effective GBV Case management: The project will directly be involved in dispensing effective case management through the clinical management of rap e (CMR) services, clinical counselling for rape survivors and referrals of acute cases. GBV information dissemination: The project will obtain materials from the core pipe line managers (UNFPA) and disseminate GBV preventive messages through the risk communication and community engagement campaigns across the target populations. GBV Prevention: The project will establish protection corners in the mobile clinics to enable the various social groups’ access tailored GBV preventive packages. Mental Health: The project will dispense Psycho-social support services (PSS), manage mental health cases and subsequent referrals for more specialized care. The project will also dispense services for trauma injuries through wound care, treatment of referral. The above design directly facilitate safe access to health care services, mitigate GBV risks and prevention and above all improved quality of Health care services.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project will contribute by addressing the Health insecurities in the IPC4& 5 Populations through establishment of Mobile clinics. These populations will access critical health care services. Mobile outpatient consultations and treatment of epidemic prone diseases;Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea(AWD), Respiratory Tract Infections(RTIs) among others. Reproductive Health services; ante natal and post natal care, family planning, clinical management of rape, mental health and trauma Induced injuries services will be dispensed across the mobile clinics.
