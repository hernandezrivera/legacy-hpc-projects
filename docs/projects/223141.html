Project Summary

ADA plans to implement a multi-Purpose cash intervention, in Fangak County of Jonglei State, Malakal and Renk county of Upper Nile State. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024, 9.3 million people are in needs including 5 million children, 2.2 million women and 1.3 million people with disabilities will require humanitarian assistance. This increase in the need is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict in Sudan, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services greatly contributed to the increase of needs. Those in need of help are 24% women, 53% children and fifteen percent persons with disability. According to the snapshot of November 2024, it mentioned that over 32,000 flood-affected people in Renk were in needs of humanitarian assistant,. The snapshot also mentioned that Renk and Malakal counties reported were reported by the partners that both counties were badly hit by the rise in cholera cases due to inflows from Sudan, limited access to clean water and sanitation and ongoing flooding. According to the inter-sectoral severity of needs analysis conducted in September 2024, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Fangak county of Jonglei State. A continuation forms of crises in Sudan including floods reported in the HNO 2024 will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2025. Unprecedented continuous flooding impacted children and their caregivers` physical well-being, living standard, and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2025, as a result households’ populations have increased to more than 6 people, creating congestion and overcrowding, exacerbating the affected population. Most affected Households lack basic household kits.
ADA through this plan, will target to reach 35,400 individuals’ beneficiaries with cash assistance to meet their urgent basic needs and priorities in the 3 counties.
24,400 individuals newly displacement 70% 0f the affected population ; 5,310 individuals returnees-15% and 5,310 individuals 15% residents and others. MPCA target per location: 2,000 HHs in Fangak 1,800 HHs in Malakal 2,100 HHs in Renk. The project will target elderly persons 5% (1,770 individuals) ADA will mainstream its responses through implementing AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations), HLP assessments, and safety audits activities during the four (stages) of MPCA response methodology, i.e. Market assessment, Registration and verification of needs; Distribution of supplies and post distribution monitoring. In Fangak, Malakal and Renk counties, unexploded ordinance (UXOS) and landmines issues or presences will be highlighted through awareness sessions especially during community sensitization and mobilizations meetings. An inter-sectoral referral system will be established to strengthen the MPCA synergies with others sectors like NFI, WASH, Child Protection.

1. Needs

South Sudan situation remained unchanged for the last 4 years, peace implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people including refugees and the returnees. Hence, South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2025 and lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2025, Fangak, Malakal and Renk, were hit hard by the triple shock of a third consecutive year of major flooding, rapid influx of the returnees due to Sudan crises, cattle raiding and sub-national inter-communal violence. All are compounding factors, hence worsening the needs of vulnerable persons. Some 9.3 million people are in need of humanitarian assistant, some of them remained internally displaced, over 2 million remained in the neighboring countries as refugees, countries like Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Egypt, Central Africa Republic, DR Congo among others. Insecurity, lack of basic services such as shelters, non-food items -plastic sheeting, mosquito’s nets, cooking utensils, blankets, community dignity, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers (HNO Jan 2024). Overall food insecurity worsened, communities including the following counties Fangak, Malakal and Renk are facing emergency IPC (phase 4) needs. Affected people’s coping mechanisms are weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks and lack of access to resources, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour, sexual exploitation and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards due to the dollar rate and lack of harvest. (Access to essential services, like ES/NFI health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, are already limited and much of the service infrastructure like shelters were destroyed either by inter-communal violence or floods. So for that very reason ADA is proposing a plan response of Multi-purpose Cash assistant to 35,400 affected HHs in Renk, Malakal and Fangak county of Upper Nile and Jonglei State.

2. Project activities

Community mobilization, baseline survey, market assessment, registration and verification, distribution of cash and PDM will be conducted in Fangak, Malakal and Renk Counties: 1) ADA will mobilize and sensitize the affected community. Local authorities including women, church leaders, chiefs and persons with disability will be engaged & consulted on the needs survey, and be used as focal and entry points for the project activities. 2. ADA will conduct baseline survey to understand the needs and the way of survival before the Cash response. 3) Market Assessment will be conducted: ADA with support of partners & authorities will conduct a Market assessment understand the market functionality. Data will be collected by trained enumerators, analyzed and recommendations presented for intervention, to ensure ADA clearly understands the community needs. The project will employ various data collection techniques like Market assessment questionnaire, FGD, KII and observation. 4) Registration of most vulnerable 5900 HHs through consultation and coordination with local authorities, community committees, enumerators and static partners. The project will target 60% of newly IDPs; 15% of returnees and 25% of vulnerable host community, this targeting aims at promoting strong community cohesion for peaceful coexistence. Of these, 60% will be female and 40% male. The project will target 5% with awareness sessions on disaster/flood risk reduction. ADA will use the CWG selection criteria of household to household verification of needs and registration of the most vulnerable affected beneficiaries to receive the assistance, and priority will be given to the most vulnerable communities especially the elderly, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), female and child-headed households, and persons with disability. Tokens will be issued to the registered beneficiaries to facilitate quick and easy identification in the distribution sites. 4) Distribution of Cash to the beneficiaries. ADA will identify distribution points at all project areas and consult its protection team and partners to conduct safety audit, to ensure affected persons’ dignity and rights are promoted and protected. Trained community mobilizers will be tasked to move from community to community to sensitize the beneficiaries about the distribution dates, venue and time. Prior to the distribution, subsequent consultation will be conducted with local community to ensure participation; distribution will take place at an appropriate agreed points where the entire beneficiary will come to receive the Cash. The distribution points will be selected not more than 30 minutes’ walk to mitigate protection incidences during return home. Financial service provider will be hired by ADA to deliver and distribute the cash 5) ADA will administer a PDM tool after two weeks of distribution to evaluate the project impact and collect feedback from the beneficiaries on their level of satisfaction with the project received. Local enumerators, will be identified with support of authorities, trained and deployed to the field for data collection. Different data collection techniques will be employed including FGD, KII, household questionnaire and observation to ensure rich information is gathered for analysis. A random sampling method will be used to pick respondents for the interview to ensure each beneficiary has an equal chance of being selected to provide their opinions.
