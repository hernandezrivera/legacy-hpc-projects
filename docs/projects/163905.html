Project Summary

Start in 2019, this project aims to improve access for Nigerian refugees to social services namely quality primary education for boys and girls, vocational training and economic integration for youth and young women through improved governance of councils and local institutions in areas affected by the Lake Chad crisis in the Far North region. NRC Cameroon will strongly collaborate with UNHCR within this cross-border project to ensure that youth refugees registered for voluntary return are taken into consideration in the vocational training and supported/monitored by NRC Nigeria for self employment opportunities once returned back to Nigeria.
2019 activities and results :
Protection : Main activities were focused on sensitization about access to birth certificate registration. Main target was men, women, boys and girls from IDP, Host communities households.
Education : Education provided NFI to 3500 children, within 1674 girls. 200 teachers were trained on PSEA and PSS.
Food security : 114 individuals received a cash for food assistance.
2020 Activities and Targets
Protection : Project will continue sensitization about birth registration. Legal assiatance will be provided to facilitate birth registration for 1860 boys and girls. 4800 men, women, boys and girls will also benefit to sensitization on birth registration.
Education : Education will continue with NFI distribution and teacher training
Early recovery : The main activities will capacity building on conflict management, mainly linked to House, Land and Property. 150 men and women will be targeted by the project
Food Security : NRC will support IDP, Host Communities and Refugees Households through CVA for food needs, 248 Households with average 1984 men, women, boys and girls will be assisted.

Besoins / Needs

NeedsAccess to civil identification The conflict has affected access to civil and identity documentation. Many civil registration centres are not functioning and the system is not designed to cope with the particular demands of displacement. In areas that are already sorely lacking in functional institutions and civil servants, insecurity and the destruction of some infrastructure have only made access to basic services more difficult.Land tenure: NRC's assessments in Far North of Cameroon show the significant challenges faced by displaced populations in accessing housing rights, land and property (HLP). out of a sample study of 292 people, only 7% were able to produce a document that justifies the occupation of the land they use. 93% of them exploit or use parcels on the basis of unwritten agreement or spontaneous occupation. Nigerians who have taken refuge in host communities have no written or formal agreement to secure their land title, which in a context of high pressure and competition over income-generating opportunities, ended up marginalising IDP's empowerment initiatives. Reduced livelihoods: Livelihoods are decimated and the populations are unable to gain and secure their resources because of the interruption to commercial and market dynamics. This is also the case with verylimited agricultural production, difficult access to land and limited income-generating opportunities. In particular, the conflict has disrupted smallholder irrigation systems initiated to promote agricultural productivity and resilience. The conflict has also destroyed the community cooperatives that used to operate such irrigation systems. Many households to this day have few income-generating opportunities and remain highly dependent on international assistance over the next several years. A fragile school system: The destruction of school infrastructure has prevented hundreds of thousands ofpeople from accessing basic services. Pre-conflict school enrolment rates were already among the lowest in the region. The Far North region of Cameroon was ranked last in terms of access to schooling and parity of access to education. In the three most affected departments (Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava, and Mayo Tsanaga), the evaluation conducted in June 2018 by NRC in collaboration with MINEDUB (Ministry of BasicEducation) showed that 36.5% of children and young people (35% girls and 38% boys) stopped learning because of displacement. In return areas, 23,997 children and young people do not have access to school due to the closure of 92 schools, of which 57 are in Mayo Sava, representing 67% of the total number of closed schools in the region.Children are exposed the risk of recruitment into armed groups, association with gangs, drug use and early marriage. The pupil-to-classroom ratio is 116 learners / room and thisis due to the lack of appropriate rooms and equipment. At the household level, 80% of the families consulted consider themselves unable to purchase small teaching materials and the payment of parents' teachers. This situation discriminates against girls whose education is sacrificed as parents prioritise the education of boys. The assessments also revealed that 65% of children in primary schools do not have birth certificates, yet they are essential for primary school exit exams and entry into to the secondary level.
