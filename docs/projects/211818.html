Project Summary

This proposal aims to implement vocational training initiatives targeting 90 beneficiaries in Azaz and 90 beneficiaries in Dana, within North West Syria (NWS). The primary purpose is to address the urgent need for skill development and economic empowerment among vulnerable populations affected by conflict.
Purpose and Target:
The project aims to equip beneficiaries with vocational skills and entrepreneurial training to enhance their employability and facilitate the establishment of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). By providing targeted support, the project seeks to alleviate poverty, promote self-sufficiency, and contribute to the socio-economic stability of the communities in Azaz and Dana.
Needs:
According to HNO 2024, more than 2.5 milion individuals in Aleppo and more than 2.2 milion people in Idleb are in need for early recovery services, this includes vocational training. The most common barriers to accessing livelihoods were lack of money (does not cover costs of living) in 87% of communities assessed, followed by lack of job opportunities (79%), and lack of job opportunities that match people's skills (44%).
A thorough needs assessment revealed significant gaps in vocational training opportunities and economic resources in the target areas. Many individuals, especially women and youth, lack access to formal education and face barriers to employment due to limited skill sets. Additionally, the ongoing conflict has disrupted local economies, exacerbating unemployment and poverty rates.
Activities:
Vocational Training: Tailored vocational training programs will be conducted, focusing on in-demand skills such as carpentry, tailoring, and food processing.
SME Training: Beneficiaries will receive training in business management, financial literacy, marketing, and other essential skills necessary for entrepreneurship.
SME Toolkit: Following completion of training, eligible participants will receive toolkits to establish or expand their SMEs, providing end of training toolkit (approximaltely 950 USD in kind). To be continued in Project Summary (extended).
PRA:
GAN support equal and safe access to life-saving assistance without discrimination of any kind. pay particular attention to not exclude women, girls, people with disabilities, and other people belonging to disadvantaged or marginalized groups.
The project acknowledges potential protection risks faced by beneficiaries, including gender-based violence, exploitation, and discrimination. To mitigate these risks, strict safeguarding measures will be implemented throughout the project cycle. This includes conducting gender-sensitive assessments, providing psychosocial support, and establishing safe reporting mechanisms for incidents of abuse or exploitation. Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the training centers.
PSEAH:
Beneficiaries may be exposed to exploitation by project stakeholders, especially vulnerable women. GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct including acts of SEA and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy.
GAN is a member of the PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN has developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, staff members will sign the policy and received systematized and refreshed training on PSEA. The Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level. GAN will provide awareness sessions about the organization’s policies and procedures. GAN’s staff and targeted communities will have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

GAN is planning to conduct the same activities in Dana and Azaz subdistricts targeting the same number of beneficiaries (90 individuals males & females). Starting by conducting a rapid market survey through KIIs interviews and FGDs to identify the key gaps in the market, followed by selecting beneficiaries (former detainees, SGBV and other atrocities survivors) and verification process, conducting surveys with beneficiaries and crosschecking with market survey to select three VTs, renting a training center, hiring qualified trainers, conduct VT and SME, distributing SME toolkits (in kind). After providing SME training and the toolkits, GAN’s field teams will accompany the beneficiaries during opening (or expanding) their small projects to provide any possible support for them.This will be followed by a two month “mentoring & coaching” intervention which will be led by GAN’s teams in coordination and collaboration with the trainers. This activity will provide support and supervision for the beneficiaries in their own places (on site). The primary objective of this project is to support individuals who have endured human rights violations. Recent surveys have revealed that a notable percentage, ranging from 7% to 12%, of these survivors are coping with physical or mental disabilities. In the initial planning stages, GAN's teams will actively integrate a fair representation of interviews with Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) to ensure their perspectives and needs are adequately considered. As the project progresses into implementation, GAN will prioritize accessibility considerations. This includes selecting a training center that is easily accessible for individuals with disabilities, equipped with features such as ramps and elevators to facilitate smooth navigation and participation. By addressing these aspects comprehensively, the project endeavors to create an inclusive and supportive environment for all participants, fostering equitable opportunities for those affected by human rights violations
