Project Summary

Budi county continues to experience high levels of malnutrition due to the deteriorating situation necessitating continued provision of nutrition services through existing OTP and TSFP sites and scaling up services through outreaches. As a result, treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) for children below five years and PLWs continue to be one of the key need for saving lives of most at risk children and PLWs. In order to have high impact response, SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children. This proposal seeks to address nutritional needs of children <5yrs and PLWs affected by acute malnutrition in Budi County in Eastern Equatoria State through both Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program(TSFP). This is as a result of the inaccessibility of the area considering its mountainous topography which has led to a decline in the nutrition situation affecting consumption, access to services and severely disrupting life-saving activities pushing communities deeper into a nutrition emergency. GREDO will be using both static and mobile/outreach response approaches in Budi County to reach all malnourished children and PLWs especially in the mountainous hard to reach areas of Ngauro, Loudo and Nagishot Payams. In 2019 GREDO plans to provide equitable lifesaving nutrition interventions to women and men, boys and girls at existing sites while increasing and continuing program reach to underserved locations through outreaches and mobile clinics. This will include integration of cluster priorities such as - Integrating malaria testing, NFI distribution (e.g mosquito nets), WASH supplies into CMAM activities and extending services to hard- to –reach locations through outreaches and static nutrition sites. GREDO through this approach, targets to provide quality nutrition services to 600 children with SAM, 998 children with MAM and 836 PLWs.

Needs

Eastern Equatoria has experienced chronic food insecurity, a lack of basic services, and few economic opportunities. Cattle rustling, armed robbery especially on the roads (the road between Kapoeta and Budi), and banditry are endemic. With little or no official security presence in many areas of the state, protracted cycles of revenge attacks over natural resources, particularly land and cattle (cattle rustling), are common. Thousands of people in South Sudan’s Eastern equatoria state are likely to face severe hunger due to lack of rainfall and unexpected crop failure according to situation on the ground in Greater Kapeota consisting of Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Budi counties as the first phase of harvest has gone without food crop yields. The population of Budi County is standing at 99,199 based on 2008 census and 2019 population projection stands at 123,198. In Budi County, the 2017 state level FSNMS report round 20 show that Budi County has high malnutrition rates with a GAM rate of 20.9% and SAM rate of 5.9% which is above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. Furthermore, the IPC report for Jan-July 2018 show that Budi is in IPC Phase 4 and the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate significantly as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services due to the heavy rains. During this time Budi county is expected to reach Critical levels of acute malnutrition. Budi county continues to experience high levels of malnutrition due to the deteriorating situation necessitating continued provision of nutrition services through existing OTP and TSFP sites and scaling up services through outreaches. Based on the recent (September 2018) South Sudan national IPC analysis, it is expected that in Budi County the nutrition situation will have deteriorated to critical levels of acute malnutrition in this case being classified as phase 4 (Emergency). The areas of Ngauro, Loudo and Nagishot Payams in Budi County can be considered hard to reach as a result of the inaccessibility of the area considering its mountainous topography which has led to a decline in the nutrition situation affecting consumption, access to services and severely disrupting life-saving activities pushing communities deeper into a nutrition emergency. As a result, treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) continue to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk children < 5 years and PLWs. In order to have high impact response, SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children.

Activities

To address the aforementioned needs, GREDO will carry out several activities including but not limited to the following:• Active case finding (screening for acute malnutrition) and referral of malnourished children (both SAM and MAM) and PLWs into appropriate nutrition programs for appropriate care. • Active social mobilization to encourage communities to send malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites. • Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months • Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months • Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among PLWs • Train health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition • Provision of quality MIYCN services in nutrition sites and community level • Test and treatment of malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months • Increase access to Infant and Young Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E) to mothers and caregivers through social mobilization, individual counselling and group discussion by CNV’s and PLW’s groups at community level and Nutrition workers at community and health facility level respectively.• Establish/rehabilitate 10 nutrition sites (Nahochod, Kadumakuch, Ngauro, Tulugi, Kathac, Hibongorok, Haliba, Ngatuba, Kapeta and Ngathilani) and ensure quality service delivery is provided through strengthening referral system and linkage through other nutrition services and sectors. Implementation:GREDO will be implementing minimum package of Integrating Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM and MAM children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Mother’s (PLW’s). In addition, GREDO will work towards integrating Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting the existing 10 OTP/TSFP sites and 2 Outreaches. GREDO will provide these life saving nutrition services in collaboration with MoH, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralization of TSFP sites, increasing awareness through community mobilization. GREDO will in essence: -• Carry out door-to-door screening of children and PLWs in the community for malnutrition using both MUAC and Weight-For-Height assessment criteria once every month at the community level and routine screening at the health facilities.• Advocate for IYCF/MIYCN Practices for prevention of Malnutrition. HRP funding will enable GREDO to provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a MAM caseload of up to 998 for under 5 children and PLW’s 836, and to a SAM case load of 583 for the year 2019. GREDO will train CNVs and PLW’s groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM/MAM and MIYCN so as to provide quality nutrition services. Existing health facility staff will be capacitated for integration of Acute Malnutrition management into health programs especially on malaria screening and referral of malnourished children under 5 and PLWs to the OTP/TSFP inteventions. GREDO will take lead on identifying, admission and treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition Under 5 and PLWs, the admission and distribution process will take place in the established TSFP sites and follow up will be done through our CNVs every week to the community. GREDO Project focal persons will undergo the mandatory IASC Gender Marker online assessment to ensure clear understanding of best practices of gender in the humanitarian context. GREDO health and nutrition staff including clinicians, nutrition assistants, nurses and midwives will be shared in this programme to increase cost effectiveness and strengthen output results.
