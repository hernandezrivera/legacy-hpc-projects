Project Summary

This project will ensure provision of Effective, Sustainable and lifesaving health and nutrition services
targeting 95,437 (20,834 women, 14,478 men, 35,474 girls,24,651 boys) in 4 administrative units (Ailleit, Haskanita, Futaha, and Abu Sufyan) in Ailleit locality in North Darfur
through an area based multi-sector approach to ensure comprehensive response to the needs of the
most vulnerable population towards more resilient communities

1 - Needs

Health:Health indicators remain firmly in the emergency classification in North Darfur due to both protracted and ongoing displacement;compounded by a largely anemic and dysfunctional health system.Improvements are needed at the levels of infrastructure, staff availability and capacity and supplies. Prevalence of communicable diseasesare relatively high, rate of diarrhea among children for example is 29%, acute respiratory illness 24.6%, and fever about 24% as well.Regarding ANC, about 24.4% of pregnant women did not attend ANC, and only 39% attended four or more ANC visits. Health educationneed to be improved, only 42% had received health and nutrition messages, the preferred media channel is through Community OutreachWorkers 59%. Use of water and soap for hand washing is not a common practice at critical times, identified 5 key hand-washing times isless than 1% ( S3M 2019)Nutrition:Relative to the rest of the country, North Darfur has even higher rates of GAM (27.9%) with children and PLWs most affected. In addition tolimited access to health care, poor feeding practices and limited access to nutritionally-diverse food sources are contributing factors tomalnutrition (UNICEF Sudan humanitarian situation report – May 2018). Rates of SAM and GAM are high, rates by using MUAC are 1.93%and 11.24% respectively, SAM and GAM rates by using weight for height are11.73% and 33.77% respectively; IYCF coverage is 23.42%,

2 - Activities / Outputs

Health: Activity 1: The service will be delivered through support of health facilities in collaboration with SMoH ). consultations will include general curative services, reproductive health and antenatal care, maternal and child health and immunization services; support and strengthen the referral of complicated cases to a secondary health care. Activity 2: A number of trainings and refresher trainings will be conducted for health staff (women and men). The training varies between HIS, IMCI, EMoC and RH, HIV/AIDS and STI national protocols, child growth and vaccine management, Universal Precautions and medical waste management, ANC and PNC. Activity 3: Conduct awareness / orientation sessions at the health facility for the community: Public health education sessions will focus on critical health areas such as communicable and non-communicable disease prevention, reproductive health and nutrition. (IEC) materials will be printed to support information dissemination. Activity 4: Support or conduct public health alert investigation, verification and response, including outbreaks. Community Health Workers will support community surveillance of outbreak-prone diseases and report deaths to health facilities. The reported cases of communicable diseases will be referred and recorded at health facility level for compiling the weekly surveillance. Activity 5: Expand and maintain disease surveillance system with early warning component: CDO in coordination with SMOH will start investigation and establish a response and intervention plan whenever surveillance data or community reports highlight suspicious cases or high number of communicable diseases. Activity 6: Strengthening Early Warning, Early detection and reporting of AWD (EWARS) through training of CHWs, VHCs and VTMs in the targeted locations Nutrition:Activity 1: Provision of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases of U5 children and PLW (incl. refurbishment of OTP/TSFP, provision of RUTF/SUTF, MUAC screening, referral service for SAM with complication case. For successful implementation of nutrition services. Activity 2: Conduct training for nutrition workers, community volunteer on CMAM, IYCF, and MtoMSG. ve adequate support to enhance their skills on the CMAM protocols to ensure quality of services. Various trainings will target health cadre staff, nutrition staff, community outreach teams and community members on implementation of CMAM protocols. The training package will be tailored and presented as per specific needs identified in each group participating in the management of malnutrition. Weekly supportive supervision by nutrition senior staff and monthly monitoring visits will inform the kind of support needed for each health and nutrition cadre staff. Training on IYCF and MtoMSG will be targeting CNWs and lead mothers to enhance raising awareness of mother’s and caregiver’s knowledge and practice on breastfeeding and infant and young child feeding. Activity 3: Conduct community awareness campaign on CMAM, IYCF etc. This project will use multiple but complementary methods to influence behavior change for optimal IYCF practices. IYCF promotion activities will include group discussions at health facilities, public places and also using local leaders and religious leaders in providing promotional messages on appropriate feeding practices for young children. Information given will be gender and culturally sensitive and will not only be directed to mothers and mother-in-laws, but also to men. Activity 4: Establish mother support group for promotion of IYCF: IYCF practices will be improved also through Community IYCF corners.Activity 5: Provision of micro-nutrient supplementation: micro-nutrient supplementation (Vitamino) will be supplemented to children (6-24 months)

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The methodology and implementation approach for the proposed intervention is based on protection strategy. Protection is at the core of CDO interventions: through a protection-informed approach CDO will ensure full respect of the rights of all persons in conditions of equality, regardless of their age, gender, religion, ethnic or social origin or political orientation.All activities will be conducted in accordance with the principles of CDO’s Protection Policies, including through a rights-based, community-based and age-gender-diversity sensitive approach.Protection will also be mainstreamed at every stage of the project cycle.Within this protection framework, this project will work to ensure impartial and equal access to the project benefits and services.Target communities will have a lead role in all activities implementation including decision making in locating the construction sites, active monitoring during the activity implementation, evaluation of the action as well as reporting any complaint through the established complaints handling mechanism.CDO will ensure mainstreaming protection in the Health and Nutrition proposed activities. Promoting equitable access to health services is important in ensuring that the project is able to reach the most vulnerable members of the targeted communities. Nearly all health indicators are gender-dis-aggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to current and emerging mothers through reproductive health services, IYCF counseling for mothers. With the project’s new approach of integrating hygiene messaging into existing health and nutrition programming, CDO will look for opportunities for more gender mainstreaming, such as incorporating menstrual hygiene management as awareness session topics. Men will also be encouraged and included in IYCF awarenesssessions. For people with disabilities and the elderly, CDO will seek to prioritize them in beneficiary selection criteria and provide assistance to reach services if needed. CDO will also ensure that all clinics are accessible to the disabled and elderly. As part of its global Accountability to Communities Framework, CDO will make information available at health facilities on how beneficiaries can provide feedback or report serious incidents.This project will include psycho-social stimulation activities for infants and young children admitted to the OTPs. MtoMSGs will also be provided with psycho-social support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to health and nutrition outreach and awareness-rising.All CDO staff have been trained and signed to the principles of Do No Harm among others in the humanitarian work, and have been required to sign an ethical standards document as an integrated part of their contract.
