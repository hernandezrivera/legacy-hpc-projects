Project Summary

The WASH project aims to improve access to sufficient safe water and enable adequate sanitation and hygiene practices for the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in conflict-affected areas and put at greater risks in the face of COVID-19. Through enhanced targeting of particularly underserved communities and enhanced community-based approach, the project covers both actions of immediate relief and longer-term resilience and self-sufficiency, and will invest special attention and efforts to support both transition in GCA and responding to enduring humanitarian needs in NGCA.
The project first aims to improve access to sufficient safe water to the most vulnerable left with chronically unmet water needs in conflict-affected areas, through a dual approach. The project will first focus on improving the water system infrastructure (including repair/maintain centralized, decentralized and private water supply in public infrastructure and social institutions and/or organizing new access to sustainable/alternative water source). It will do so through the facilitation of partnerships among communities, authorities and water utilities for an inclusive and accountable implementation. The project will then keep an agile approach to respond to the life-saving needs for safe drinking and technical water of the most underserved and vulnerable communities and individuals through water trucking, installation of water tanks, and in case of shocks and emergencies, distributions of water bottles. To help transitioning the response to ensuring the sustainable provision of access to safe water, the project will include to foster community-based and multi-stakeholder engagement on water governance, as well as to amplify advocacy with local, regional, national and international stakeholders on water quality.
The project also aims to improve access to adequate sanitation by initiating support for community-driven waste management. This will include to facilitate and support partnerships between communities, CSOs, the local administrations and other local stakeholders on improving solid waste management in selected communities. This will also include to test community-based waste management activities in the most vulnerable, underserved locations where over-accumulated solid waste is presenting a serious public health threat.
The project finally aims to enable adequate hygiene awareness and practices, especially in the face of COVID-19 risks, to the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men. It will do so by keeping here as well an agile approach enabling different modalities of assistance, through the provision of hygiene kits, vouchers and promotion through risk communication and community engagement actions.
Throughout these actions, PIN will maintain a robust Community Feedback and Accountability Mechanism though its community-based approach and engagement with women, girls, boys, men, elderly and people with disabilities to raise their voices; enhanced consultations and testing of participation and social accountability mechanisms; feedback and complaints hotline, response, referrals and analysis for further adjustment of the assistance.

1 - Needs

In 2020 the conflict crisis in Ukraine has been aggravated by Covid-19, leaving the most vulnerable people even more at risk to lose access to livelihoods and basic services incl. health, water and hygiene supplies. In light of C-19, the access to WASH services is urgent, as maintaining appropriate hygiene levels mitigates the risk of contamination and virus spread. Armed clashes continue causing damage in power lines, water supply systems and pumping stations, despite a slight decrease in number of incidents with critical WASH facilities, compare - Jan-July 2020 with 57 armed incidents to 65 at the same period in 2019. Furthermore, the water systems need substantial repairs due to aging and bad maintenance. PIN's assessment (Jan 2020) noted limited local budget allocations ase a key barrier for addressing acute WASH needs in the affected area. AS a result, the number of people in need of assistance in accessing water, is estimated at about 1.7 mil. people (WASH Cluster, 2020). As a result of hostilities, worn infrastructure, overpricing of essential items and C-19 restrictions (e.g. transport restrictions, therefore isolation of hard-to-reach areas) access hygiene items itself remains a major concern. Thus, as for ACCESS Rapid Market Assessment (Apr 2020) for GCA, prices of hygiene items rose by nearly 7% from Feb to April. Reportedly, hygiene items were not fully available locally for 25% of people (worse in “peripheral locations”) in April, compared to 7% in Feb. It is particularly concerning, as population of the affected locations is, to a great extent, comprised of older people with disabilities/ chronic issues (97.85%, at least 1 significant functional impairment, HAI, 2020). WASH Cluster stated that HHs in both GCA (51%) and NGCA (47%) require WASH support, providing that 31% have problems with water, 26% with sanitation and 14% with hygiene, and this situation disproportionately affects different demographic groups. The Snapshot of Older People’s Needs (HAI, 2020) indicates that in GCA along the LoC, 82% of the older people (incl. 85% of women) need basic hygiene items (urological pads, diapers). Due to its complex impact, C-19 pandemic will likely exacerbate existing economical vulnerability of older women, incl. when it comes to hygiene items purchase capacity. 71% of people of older age (67% women) reported to have limited (90%) or no (10%) access to safe water. HHs with children become affected by bad-quality drinking water and irregular supply. Such deteriorations directly affect children’s health (for instance: in the area around Kostiantynivka (DGCA) in 2019, when after a drop of water properties there were reported cases of gastroenteritis (79 children and adolescents out of 99 residents). Such deteriorations also worsen gender inequality, be it for effects on reproductive health of girls and women, or by adding to the daily stressors, time poverty and exhaustion of mostly female caregivers. PIN's most recent analysis confirmed issues of dried-up wells, outdated water infrastructure in multi-storied building, water pipes destroyed in conflict, low quality of water, need for water tanks and supply of drinking water etc. The analysis’ 96 FGDs and KIIs, 609 respondents, were conducted in 18 locations within 0-5km from the LoC, considering needs of men, women, boys, girls, men, women 60+, people with disabilities (AGD approach).Another major concern is that, healthcare facilities in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, already limited due to the conflict, would be overwhelmed by COVID-19 patients. Insufficient testing capacities, lack of PPE and other sanitation items in medical facilities will affect the availability and quality of medical help especially for people in rural areas. Even C-19 designated hospitals (10%) do not have sufficient equipment for testing (Revised HRP 2020). Medical institutions also face a lack of water for cleaning (in 29% cases) and have no means for infectious waste disposal (aprox. 55%).

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Improved access to sufficient safe water• PIN will support partnerships between communities, CSOs, the local administrations and utility companies to repair/maintain centralized, decentralized and private water supply in public infrastructure and social institutions.• If necessary and feasible, in hard-to-reach locations where re-establishment of the existing water supply system is not possible and where water trucking is a constant need due to damages of the main water supply lines, PIN will facilitate cooperation between communities and local stakeholders to organize access to a sustainable water source, e.g a new borehole or pipeline connection.Based on updated, localised context analysis to target the most vulnerable communities with chronically unmet immediate water needs: • PIN will provide drinking and technical water through water trucking. PIN will strive to provide people residing in areas of PIN water trucking interventions with the amount of water recommended for the total basic WASH needs (Sphere standards). • PIN will also install smaller water tanks in social or in private HH for water storage to ensure impact of water trucking activities. These tanks will be also used for contingency water storage.• In the areas with no other possibility and especially in the face of new shocks, PIN will procure and distribute bottled water on very short term (contingency) basis to the most vulnerable individuals and households . • In parallel, through community-based and multi-stakeholder engagement, PIN will support good governance in water management to ensure that underserved communities gain greater access to public water system coverage and emergency assistance in case of shocks.• PIN will do evidence-based advocacy for water quality to benefit the most vulnerable communities.Outcome 3: Improved access to adequate sanitation • PIN will support partnerships between communities, CSOs, the local administrations and utility companies on improving solid waste management.• PIN will support community-based waste management activities in most vulnerable, underserved locations where over-accumulated solid waste is presenting a serious public health threat. Outcome 4: provide critical WASH related supplies and/or awarenessBased on updated, localised needs assessments to target the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men:• PIN will organize the distribution of hygiene kits that take into account the gender and age specific needs of the most vulnerable (targeting sick and disabled, single mothers/fathers with children, etc.) – material donation, voucher and cash assistance. A certain quantity of hygiene kits and disinfectants will be kept as contingency stock.• Hygiene promotion targeting the most vulnerable (chronically ill, elderly with disability, groups with cumulative vulnerabilities incl. children, pregnant & lactating women..) on the topics of appropriate hygiene practices (personal hygiene, waste management, water consumption) to prevent COVID-19 related diseases will be conducted by PIN.• PIN will provide hygiene vouchers as one of the most efficient and appropriate modalities to provide life preserving subsistence assistance.For life-saving assistance PIN will identify the most vulnerable women, girls, boys, men based on context analysis of the most underserved communities and AGD-sensitive analysis. PIN will develop a plan together with community members in coordination with local authorities, to determine the most appropriate way to reach people with special needs; identify possibilities of joint actions with relevant humanitarian agencies e.g. Help Age or MdM in such cases. PIN will also aim to improve water governance. Through a community engagement approach, PIN will seek to facilitate sustainable solutions at local level. PIN will support local capacity to create emergency response plans. PIN plans to build the capacity of local NGOs partners by implementing the project together.
