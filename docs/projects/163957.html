Project Summary

SARD is committed to assist affected people most in need in northwest Syria, despite the unpredictability and the risks of working in areas, under security threats in the recent offensives.
The action with a budget of 5 000 000 € will address the needs of about 50 000 Individuals people over 12 months, addressing life-saving and life-sustaining food and resilience assistance.
The multi-sectoral assessment conducted in 2019/2020 cuts across the Food, Non-Food Items, shelter and livelihood sectors.
Findings show that food and NFI were ranked by 87% of households as their main priorities for the next 3 months and that 73% were unable or barely able to meet their current food and/or NFI needs. In addition, 72% reported that access to income generation or livelihood opportunities was close to impossible, leading to some of the 4th top priority of people in need of assistance.
Multi-sector assistance will range from emergency food support to life-sustaining regular food support and restoring the source of livelihood. It will also include the provision of necessary non-food items, shelter intervention (described in the SNFI document).
SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have improved access to life-saving assistance through an effective and efficient response mechanism, implemented with a protection lens, the action will also include gender-sensitive activities as well as ensure that people with disabilities have safe and secure access to the aid.
- Emergency food support is provided to up to 35 000 individuals in the form of cooked meals or on-off cash the modality will be according to the situation f the families as well as the market and the security environment.
- Monthly food assistance targeting 20 000 food insecure beneficiaries through in-kind, voucher or cash assistance depending on the best-adapted option for up to 8 months. The action provides up to 8-months multi-sector assistance with food for selected most
vulnerable persons including IDPs and host communities. Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism.
Identification is based on a set of eligibility criteria such as household vulnerability, shelter conditions, possession of productive assets and food security level (livelihood coping strategies).

4 - Needs

SARD conducted several needs assessments throughout the 2019 year to better understand – and respond to, targeted populations’ needs. Reports confirm pressing multi-sectoral needs cutting across first line emergency assistance, including relief (food and NFI). In addition and as mentioned in some HNO 2020 extract, the local context analyses indicate that poor economic conditions, physical destruction of infrastructure, and large-scale displacement have led to competition over scarce livelihood opportunities, exposing the affected population to use negative coping mechanism to address their primary basic needs.With about 45,000 newly displaced people move to areas in northern Aleppo Governorate, seeking safety and to reach services, including Afrin and A’zaz (OCHA, 2020). SARD rapid assessment to 1,562 newly displaced families affected by the recent hostilities shows that food remains households’ top five priority needs, with almost all households reporting food (99%) and NFI (98%) assistance as a priority for the next 3 months. Financial constraints, high prices and inability to access local markets were cited as the main challenges faced by households to access food and NFI items, as well as the loss of assets and livelihood opportunities.This latest wave of people displaced, compounds an already dire humanitarian situation,(OCHA issue 08) corroborates finding of the SARD assessment in 2019: 1,530 households (incl. new and protracted IDPs, returnees and host households) were interviewed. Findings show that food and NFI were ranked by 87% of household as their main priorities for the next 3 months and that 73% were unable or barely able to meet their current food and/or NFI needs. In addition, 72% reported that access to income generation or livelihood opportunities were close to impossible, leading to some of the 4th top priority of people in needs of assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will respond to the food needs to cover 55000 people in total as follows:- Short-term food assistance through cooked meals, or food basket or cash support depending on the best-adapted option, targeting 35000 IDPs- Monthly food assistance targeting food insecure beneficiaries through an in-kind, voucher or cash assistance depending on the best-adapted option for up to 8 months, targeting 20000 people.- Income-generating eligible families, SARD team will then establish a Bill of Quantities for a tailor-made package for each case, making sure to address small scale income-generating opportunities, with an emphasis on small business, or vegetable garden, chickens and other light livestock – assistance through cash grant, those families will be the same families targeted with monthly food assistance. To address the multi-sectoral needs described above, SARD is committed to ensuring that the critical, multi-sectoral needs of the most vulnerable households are met and their resilience strengthened through both life-saving and life-sustaining multi-purpose assistance ranging from emergency food support to restoring livelihood (described in the present document). It will also include the provision of necessary non-food items, shelter intervention (described in the SNFI document). SARD will ensure that most vulnerable households have improved access to life-saving assistance through an effective and efficient response mechanism.To implement such output the following activities will take place:• Identification and targeting of newly arrived IDPs • Registration and distributions in close coordination with stakeholders • Assessment on the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable to determine the modality of the response (cash, voucher or in-kind).• Multi-purpose assistance is provided according to the identified needs of the family. • Monitoring and post monitoring of emergency support.• Close coordination with relevant clusters.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Relief activities are monitored by SARD’s M&E team on a regular basis. A strong complaint response mechanism (CRM) is advertised to local communities and offers multiple channels to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike. Community tensions/crowd management: SARD has built strong relationships with local authorities over the years to ensure the buy-in of activities with local communities. SARD –whenever feasible, offers door-to-door distribution or distribution on sites chosen in coordination with local authorities and offering gender-disaggregated lines. Distribution of relief is staggered to facilitate crowd management. To avoid the risk of inter-community tensions due to varied assistance packages, SARD follows the FSL Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content and ensures that distribution of kits is based on needs and not status. Bias or exclusion errors in selection: Selection criteria are transparent, and communities are informed of selection criteria through sensitisation sessions. Community members are encouraged to report any complaints – including on selection, to SARD’s CRM. Criteria are clearly explained to local authorities who are actively engaged in identifying and preparing beneficiaries list. PSEA: Staff were trained on protection principles, including on PSEA, child protection and GBV.. All staff have been required to read and sign the new policy along with SARD’s Code of Conduct. PSEA focal points were also appointed. Extortion/Price Increase: SARD favours a voucher modality, whenever markets are accessible and functioning. This modality, in addition to give beneficiaries the dignity of choice, provides low visibility thus reducing risks associated with in-kind assistance. Shopkeepers are thoroughly trained on SARD’s processes and shops are monitored during the redemption period. Should SARD projects negatively impact prices on the local market, the team is ready to adjust activities and modalities accordingly.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Locations of project activities are secure and accessible.- Supplies, contractors, commodities and materials are accessible.- No interfering from armed groups and no divert aid.- Women are equally included in the decision-making process.- IDPs population are not excluded and/or neglected.- Protection is mainstreamed throughout the response by promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity with "do not harm" as a key principle. Risks and Mitigating measures • Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries.• Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in target areas affecting the SARD ability to operate and access.- Ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access.- Staff and volunteers from the local community are hired.- Staff trained on remote management.- Re-direction of programming into other accessible governorates.- Security of staff remains of utmost importance and work will be suspended in case of serious threats endangering the staff and the program.- Local Security Plan (LSP) for operations inside Syria is established and shared among all staff. LSP is a document summarizes all Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) as well as contingency plans. It gives field staff and SARD management team guidelines how to mitigate risks during the field operations inside Syria, and how to behave in a certain incident situation.-Risk assessments, risk matrix, situation scenarios, and mitigation measures are being reviewed quarterly a year and on ad-hoc bases.-The field team releases a monthly security report. It includes security & safety recommendations for the staff.-Security map produced monthly; with layers of locations of residence of staff, areas of operation, conflict areas and air strikes.-All routes of transportation vehicles of field staff are mapped, and the routes are modified in accordance with security reports.-SARD coordinates with OCHA Access WG.
