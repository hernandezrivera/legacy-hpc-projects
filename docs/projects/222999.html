Project Summary

The proposed project objective is to address the Rohingya crisis by providing support to both the vulnerable Rohingya community and the host communities. The primary focus will be on ensuring food security, promoting sustainable livelihoods, and building resilience. Recognizing the various challenges these communities face—such as economic difficulties, rising prices, and existing natural hazards—World Vision International (WVI) will implement food and cash-based interventions. These initiatives are designed to improve livelihoods, reduce vulnerability, and enhance the overall quality of life. Additionally, the project will incorporate considerations related to environmental and climate change when relevant. Overall, it will employ effective and practical strategies to foster self-reliance among Rohingya refugees and marginalized host communities.
The project will deliberately assist the FDMNs with food assistance support under the first objective to ensure and sustain the timely provision of lifesaving, nutrition-sensitive food assistance for Rohingya refugees/FDMNs through WFP’s General Food Assistance (GFA) Programme. Through the intervention, the project will reach approximately 189,262 beneficiaries each month to distribute WFP’s food assistance along with high-energy biscuits (HEB) and hot meals at the time of any emergency or any unwanted situation as per SOP.
For the Rohingya refugee population (Food Security Obj_02), the project will implement several key initiatives like fresh food corners under the GFA program, homestead gardening, tree plantation, integrated aquaculture, and capacity-building training on DRR. The project will initiate subsidiary hands-on climate-smart agricultural activities like mini-nursery for vegetable and fruit saplings to ensure dietary diversity and decrease malnutrition in the camp community. The total coverage is about 8,000 refugees. The project will also provide necessary support to the community with adequate training and awareness sessions on food habits, cooking demonstrations for improved nutritional practices, disaster risk management and mitigation, benefits of climate-smart agriculture, as well as necessary inputs for agricultural activities among the project beneficiaries.
The project will implement interventions aimed at enhancing food security in host communities under the objective 03. A multifaceted awareness-raising initiative will reach out to 10,000 individuals from these communities to strengthen household food security, nutrition, and resilience of host communities through the climate-smart livelihoods of food production, food management, and market-linkage activities. Alongside this, support for crop agriculture will be extended to 10,000 individuals.
Social cohesion is a vital element of this project, which aims to include and mobilize youth and women into groups to promote social unity. The initiative will incorporate principles of Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and gender equality, and will also consciously aim to involve individuals with disabilities. Importantly, the project intends for 60% of its beneficiaries to be women. Overall, the project plans to reach a total of 207,262 individuals across 10 camps, specifically targeting the camps labeled 8E, 8W, 9, 11, 15, 18, 19, 20, 20xt, and 24. Furthermore, the project will engage 6,000 individuals in selected host areas within Ukhiya Upazila.
To ensure the best planning and implementation WVB will communicate and coordinate with the Food Security sector and other concerned stakeholders regularly focusing on regular participation in the FS sector meetings, providing timely quality reports and updates. Additionally, WVB will communicate proactively with FSS for the assistance request on implementation guidance and necessary consultations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Access to fresh and nutritious food remains a significant challenge for the refugee population living in the Cox’s Bazar camps and surrounding host communities due to the ongoing Rohingya crisis. Many refugees lack formal income opportunities and the technical skills needed to adapt quickly to changes in their circumstances. The total number of Rohingya in Bangladesh has increased from 767,324 individuals (154,264 households) following the August 2017 influx to 1,003,394 individuals (203,572 families) and among the total number 35,629 refugees are residing in Bhasan Char (UNHCR population factsheet data, September 2024).Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-7) Report, July 2024, reflects an alarming trend of increasing vulnerability across both Rohingya and Bangladeshi communities. The report indicates that 9% of households were found to have moderate to high vulnerability, significantly higher than the 69% observed in the Bangladeshi community. This vulnerability is attributed to persistent economic pressures, lack of livelihood opportunities, and inadequate access to essential services. For the first time since 2017, 10% of Rohingya households were classified as having poor diets, while 70% were found to be in poor to borderline conditions. Additionally, 24% of these households were unable to afford the minimum food basket, remaining below the Food Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) despite the current levels of humanitarian assistance. Simulations estimate that if this assistance were to be completely withdrawn, 83% of Rohingya households would fall below the MEB line, highlighting their significant dependence on humanitarian aid for survival. The shift was most likely due to the high price hikes and inadequate livelihood opportunities, which negatively benefitted the Rohingya. In some worse situations, many households withdrew children from school and engaged in emergency measures such as begging.According to the Joint Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment Report J-MSNA) in 2024, The median income for households headed by females was 0 BDT, while those led by males reported an income of 3,500 BDT (32 USD). On average, households had monthly median expenses of 7,480 BDT (69 USD). This indicates that their regular expenses exceeded the humanitarian assistance they received, necessitating the search for additional sources of income or cash. This can be also explained from REVA-7 as in 2023, economic vulnerability among Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar increased by 3% compared to 2022. Many families in the camps relied on less preferred or cheaper food, with significant food insecurity leading to borrowed food and reliance on support from friends and relatives. A substantial number of households reported having to restrict adult food consumption to ensure children received at least one meal a day. Key crises, such as a funding shortfall, ration cuts, a major fire, and severe flooding, exacerbated the struggles of these communities. About 74% of households faced ongoing challenges related to ration cuts and a lack of livelihood opportunities, while only 56% reported some form of income—a 10-point decline from the previous year. From 2022 to 2023, Rohingya households continued to sell food assistance to meet their monetary needs. Among those households that sold food, 77 percent in Cox's Bazar and 81 percent in Bhasan Char did so to buy other food items. Many of these households also opted for less preferred or cheaper food options.Around 19% reported safety concerns affecting their movement and access to food and services, with theft and murders being the most common security issues. Despite improvements over the years, multidimensional deprivation among Rohingya households remained high, indicating ongoing vulnerability and limited capacity to respond to shocks.

This inclusive strategy aims to effectively address community needs and minimize gaps in support. Additionally, a multi-sectoral approach will be employed to integrate efforts across various sectors to ensure a comprehensive and holistic approach to supporting beneficiaries and enhancing overall community well-being.

WVB has established a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which includes a Hotline, help desk, and complaint boxes, for receiving feedback and complaints from the targeted populations. Protection assessments and the CFRM process protection issues in the community by collecting community feedback and responses in an organized manner. WV will do capacity development training for Staff to enhance their understanding on protection and AAP. Our intent is to contribute to changes that result in the improved well-being of children and communities. WV works with the most vulnerable to identify critical needs and seeks to ensure their right to receive assistance and protection based on their informed consent. We recognize the importance of community involvement and participation in all activities and plans that affect the lives of communities. We reaffirm our commitment to receive and respect their opinions and ensure timeliness and appropriateness of our interventions. World Vision’s preferred role is to work alongside children and communities and in partnership with governments, churches, civil society organizations and donors. WV affirms our role as a citizen of the humanitarian and development community and respects international laws and treaties as well as national laws.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

WVB upholds a zero-tolerance policy towards Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment, extending this commitment across organizational levels and stakeholders. The agency prioritizes SEA prevention and provides immediate assistance to victims, collaborating with partners to foster a safe workplace. WVB has been an active member of the ISCG PSEA network in Cox's Bazar. Annual ‘Our Voice’ survey is conducted aiming to gather PSEA-related concerns from staff & following assessment findings comprehensive action plan is formulated.PSEA training is mandatory for all WVB personnel and volunteers, emphasizing reporting wrongdoings and survivor-centered support. TWVB facilitates reporting of misconduct through an online platform and hotline accessible to all. The PSEA focal point receives complaints and ensures rapid reporting to the concerned body and the Response Director. The organization has a retaliation policy to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers from any form of retaliation. WVB is dedicated to enhancing staff capacity on SEA issues & empowering partners, service providers, and volunteers to raise awareness within Rohingya and host communities. Community-based awareness programs utilize diverse resources to inform communities about policy. Regular monitoring ensures compliance with PSEA policies across all supported facilities, complemented by quarterly meetings among WVB and Partners to address challenges and strategize. Periodic capacity assessments are conducted to support partners in upholding necessary PSEA protocols. To align with global standards in SEAH investigation, WVB partners participate in specialized training organized by the Network. An Investigation Team is responsible for handling all types of SEA investigations. WVB contributes to technical groups within the PSEA Network, including PSEA Communication with the Community Task Team, Technical Review Team for SEAH Investigation ToR, and Task Team for Drafting the Sexual Harassment Law.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project is designed to significantly enhance environmental protection and strengthen disaster risk reduction (DRR) efforts in targeted areas that are particularly vulnerable to climate change and natural disasters. By implementing a variety of climate-resilient interventions, the project aims to create a safer, healthier environment for both refugee and host communities.One key component of the project involves improving communication facilities to ensure that essential information can be disseminated quickly and effectively. This is crucial in climate-vulnerable and disaster-prone areas, where timely communication can save lives and enhance community preparedness.To foster environmental sustainability, the project will promote climate-resilient homestead gardening techniques. These techniques will not only improve food security but also increase the resilience of local agricultural practices to changing climatic conditions. Furthermore, cash-for-work initiatives, such as waste management and tree plantation programs, will provide employment opportunities while simultaneously contributing to community well-being and environmental health.In addition, the project will establish landslide and early warning systems tailored specifically for both Rohingya and host communities. These systems are vital for developing effective contingency plans that can be activated in the event of a disaster. The project will focus on preparing communities in advance by establishing disaster risk reduction preparedness plans and enhancing disaster response capabilities. Community awareness is essential to this initiative. The project will engage local communities through educational campaigns and outreach programs, fostering collaboration among government agencies, civil society organizations, and development partners to improve disaster preparedness and promote sustainable practices.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

For each rapid response, BRCR follows its Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) as well as reports from ISCG and RRRC or other nexus assessment findings. WV BRCR collaborates with the government and various organizations to facilitate a quick recovery in both host and camp communities. Our strategies align with the Rohingya response initiatives of like-minded development partners, such as the 2022 Humanitarian Implementation Plan (HIP). The Rapid Response strategy prioritizes life-saving needs based on the severity of the disaster and available resources. Field-level implementation focuses on the most urgent needs, guided by insights from RNA and RRRC, ensuring that our responses are manageable. In accordance with the WV BRCR strategy and recent emergency experiences, we have developed a Rapid Response Plan that is periodically reviewed to ensure continuity of operations. An Emergency Rapid Response Team (ERRT) has been established, led by a Senior Manager of PQGE.Each year, WV BRCR implements emergency programs that include the distribution of High Energy Biscuits and Hot Meals. We operate a multipurpose center featuring a mega kitchen with 65 burners, capable of preparing a significant number of hot meals each day. Annually, we coordinate with the World Food Programme (WFP) to provide and distribute hot meals.In the host community, we deliver dry food rations to the most affected populations following disasters each year. Additionally, WV BRCR provides non-food item (NFI) support for emergency responses and undertakes repairs and renovations of certain structures in the camp locations.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project will be actively implementing a range of initiatives aimed at fostering self-reliance among the Rohingya people. These initiatives include cooking lessons, kitchen gardening, and skill development activities that empower individuals and enhance their livelihoods.We have established 20 Community Cooking and Learning Centers (CCLC) as vital hubs for community members to learn and share knowledge about self-reliance. Each center is managed by a local committee that addresses learning challenges, promotes educational activities, and fosters personal and communal growth.Our project focuses on promoting sustainable gardening, specifically vertical and climate-smart practices suited to the camps' unique conditions. By encouraging beneficiaries to grow fresh vegetables, we aim to improve their diets with nutritious food and help families save money on grocery costs.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project focuses on the production and distribution of Moringa saplings among the local population. It also aims to commercialize various packaged products made from Moringa plants. The project will also establish integrated aquaculture, or fish farming, in groups across three unions. Furthermore, the project will promote homestead gardening and sapling production through nurseries.For disaster-affected individuals, the project will provide dry food and hot meal packages, along with non-food item (NFI) distributions for those most impacted. WVB Community Resilience owns three cyclone shelters located in Cox's Bazar, which are always prepared to offer emergency shelter to the most vulnerable individuals.Moreover, WV BRCR maintains an emergency support stock of non-food items for rapid response. To enhance the well-being of the host community, the project will also conduct awareness and training sessions aimed at promoting better agricultural practices and dietary habits.
