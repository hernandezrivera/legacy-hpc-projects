Project Summary

This project integrates preventive and treatment of malnutrition activities, responding to the needs of the most vulnerable communities residing in Lebanon, with a focus on pregnant, lactating women (PLW) and children under five. The proposed activities fully align with the nutrition sector priorities and objectives, building on existing capacities and complementing ongoing projects under UNICEF. The project activities will be implemented in close coordination with the nutrition sector, Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) National Committee, UNICEF and other nutrition implementing partners.
Through this project, IOCC will improve the nutrition status of some of the most vulnerable in Lebanon by enhancing their quality of life and decreasing risks of morbidity and mortality. The proposed interventions are to:
- Provide skilled IYCF counseling and support to 13,500 PLW and caregivers of children under 24 months old, promote the timely initiation of breastfeeding and exclusive breastfeeding, and introduce safe, age-appropriate, and nutrition-diversified complimentary food at the community and primary healthcare center (PHC) levels
- Provide micro-nutrient supplements to 7,400 PLWs and children aged 6-59 months and HEB to 2,000 PLWs and children aged 6 – 59 months to improve their nutrition and quality of diet
- Conduct outreach to mobilize the community and link it to the PHCs, and provide awareness sessions focusing on maternal nutrition and optimum Infant and Young Child Feeding and healthy nutrition for care givers and adolescent girls
- Establish 16 mother care groups to encourage positive behavior change in IYCF practices and scale up the family Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) approach at the community level, strengthening referrals to PHCs for screening and treatment of malnutrition
- Support the malnutrition treatment services in Lebanon by subsidizing the PHC consultations and transportations fees for cases identified with acute malnutrition.
- Monitor, follow up on, and address violations of the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes, and enforce law 47/2008
- Provide capacity building and coaching to PHCs on screening and treatment of acute malnutrition, when needed, and on linking the community and PHCs to each other and referring suspected acute malnutrition cases to the nearest PHCs
- Provide capacity building sessions, trainings and workshops on IYCF, malnutrition and the IYCF SOP to healthcare providers and frontline workers when needed
All proposed activities will be conducted respecting COVID-19 safety measures.
Protection will be mainstreamed across all interventions; Awareness sessions will be disseminated to introduce the complaint and feedback mechanism to the community, and inform them of their ability to direct complaints to IOCC’s safe line stressing the confidentiality of the complaint mechanisms

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

IOCC will scale up nutrition and IYCF interventions in all governorates to meet existing and emerging unmet needs. IOCC will scale up nutrition screening at the community level and strengthen the referral system to the PHCs for prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition amongst children under five and PLW. At the PHC level, in coordination with MoPH and UNICEF, IOCC will provide on the job and refresher trainings whenever needed on screening and treatment of acute malnutrition, coach the PHCs, encourage them to follow up on cases, and ensure timely referral and follow-up from the community to the PHCs and hospitals if needed. IOCC will support the malnutrition treatment services at PHC level by subsidizing the consultations and transportation fees for acute malnutrition cases. IOCC will conduct awareness sessions on IYCF and hygiene, provide individual counseling to caregivers of children and PLWs, empower the community by the establishment of mother care groups and link the community to the national IYCF hotline. IOCC will report any violations to the Code or Law 47/2008 to the national IYCF hotline. This activity will complement the work done with UNICEF. IOCC will build the capacity of organizations/parties on safe provision of artificial feeding support and on the national IYCF SOP. IOCC will also provide necessary awareness and support on IYCF to PLWs and caregivers of children under two.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IOCC has worked on incorporating PSEA into its work plans on both Prevention and Response level to be able to identify, prevent, and combat sexual exploitation and abuse. IOCC has assigned a PSEA focal point to coordinate the development and implementation of IOCC PSEA policy and practices. IOCC’s CRM was updated with a focus on sensitive allegations, and CRM policy and procedures, and communication materials were developed. Mandatory trainings on PSEA were rolled out to all IOCC staff and regular sensitization sessions are being carried out. The sessions tackle PSEA core principles, zero tolerance policy to SEA, prevention and response, commitments and personnel obligations. A code of conduct that includes PSEA commitments is signed by all IOCC staff, new and existing where the new staff attend an orientation on the PSEA and protection mainstreaming. Through outreach activities, IOCC disseminate PSEA educational material produced by the PSEA network to the reached beneficiaries.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

FGDs, satisfactory questionnaires and observational monitoring visits will be developed to ensure that beneficiaries are not at any risk as a result of the intervention and to share feedback and address concerns and complaints. IOCC set up a safe line so beneficiaries can reach IOCC in case of any complaint regarding the assistance provided. Awareness sessions are disseminated to inform beneficiaries of their ability to direct complaints to the safe line stressing the confidentiality of the CRM. Participation includes the collection of data for surveys that will be discussed through FGDs which include beneficiaries from the host and migrant communities who will together agree on the best ways to address hygiene among their counterparts mentioning the topics that might interest them most. IOCC will develop participatory activities that apply various communication techniques and tools adapted to both genders and different age groups in order to encourage them to participate.
