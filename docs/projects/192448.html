Project Summary

Since October 2019, Lebanon has been assailed by compounded crises: the economic crisis, followed by COVID-19 Pandemic and lately the 4th of August Port explosion of Beirut. This has affected 400,000 children being left out of school as a result of poverty and other factors
The economic crisis is forcing particularly the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant households to adapting livelihood crisis coping strategies, to reduce the economic burden on the families. Meanwhile, the country’s education sector is struggling to retain teachers. The situation is further exacerbated by the anticipated increase of 100,000-120,000 students transferring from private to public schools, seriously compromising the capacity of the public-school system to ensure basic conditions are in place, including adequate human resources, to provide relevant, inclusive, and quality education
The Appeal will enable MS response to the most immediate educational needs and time-critical early recovery needs arising from the intersectionality crises in Lebanon
The ERP for Education has a two-pronged strategy to address the above challenges:
Strategic Objective One: Direct community support to support the most vulnerable children to ensure continued learning
The main aim is to ensure that the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children, including children with disabilities, are reached and supported to access and continue learning despite the challenges faced due to the deteriorating economic situation. This is achieved by providing support to both children and parents ensuring their wellbeing and accessibility through the below interventions:
1. Conducting Needs Assessment to identify out of education Lebanese and migrant children
2. Outreach, awareness raising and referrals on Out of Education Children to basic services and to bring them back to education
3. Provision of assistive technologies for children with disabilities
4. Cash for Education targeting the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant families to enable retention in education
5. Remote learning support:
5.1. Home-schooling support including transferrable skills, responsive caregiving and basic and digital literacy to caregivers.
5.2. PSS-SEL programs integrated in the remote learning support for the most vulnerable students, parents/caregivers
5.3. Promoting and applying SGBV risk reduction in adapted learning programmes through raising awareness amongst children and caregivers
5.4. Supporting with low-cost solutions for inclusive remote learning
6. Procurement of food packages for children and youth enrolled in learning support programmes
To achieve the above result MS will use a combination of modalities which range from joint assessments, community-based support through outreach and awareness, and direct beneficiary level support through cash, remote learning, and wellbeing and food assistance. The sector has identified very specific vulnerability criteria to be able to have a targeted approach, including but not limited to:
• Most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant students who are at risk of dropping out of schools
• Students who have suffered a high level of learning loss in the last 2 years
• Children and youth who are Out of Education and at risk of negative coping mechanism
• Students who have moved from private schools to public schools
Strategic Objective Two: Improving Formal Education of most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children through schools
The main aim is to ensure that students affected by the multi-crisis are provided relevant, inclusive and quality education adhering to national and international standards on education in emergencies and child protection. This is achieved by focusing on the procurement of learning materials (stationary, books, light equipment) as direct support to the most vulnerable children
To achieve the above objective, MS will use a mixed modality of direct procurement of the equipment and learning materials to be provided to students

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

MS has been mainstreaming its inclusive approach into its overall programming including Education, to work with and include people that are most vulnerable, in a non-discriminatory fashion, i.e. Equity in terms of nationalities of beneficiaries, and gender sensitive approach, i.e. Cater for the specific needs of vulnerable groups such as children with disabilities and groups of girls travelling long distances and in unsafe places will be given priority in the provision of transportation. MS’ grass-root approach and acceptance by the community increases the likelihood of targeting most vulnerable Lebanese populations, boys, girls, men and women MS will ensure that all staff involved in this program are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases. Moreover, MS will work on enhancing its internal PSEA minimum standards through participation in trainings, coaching

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

MS accountability system will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have access to information packages including information about Partners, the community-response mechanism, and the project (incl. selection criteria, planned activities). Information will be disseminated through, helplines or through sessions/meetings planned under project’s activities. Several channels will be made available for beneficiaries to request information, provide feedback and complaints, including a separate hotline. Complaints related to the project will be handled by MS, and other ones will be referred to the relevant agency. Information on the situation of beneficiaries will be collected through monitoring and evaluation systems where perspectives on the implementation will be captured and addressed on a regular basis. The feedback gathered will strengthen project accountability to those it intends to reach and provide the opportunity to make project design adjustments
