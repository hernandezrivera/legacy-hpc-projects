Project Summary

The protracted conflict in Cameroon’s North-West and South-West regions has caused significant disruptions to the education sector, leaving many children out of school and exacerbating existing inequalities, with 20% of children not accessing education (MSNA, 2024). Children are particularly vulnerable, with high dropout rates driven by insecurity, economic hardship, and limited access to safe, inclusive learning environments. A survey of 14,812 households revealed that only 6% of children participated in distance learning (MSNA, 2024), highlighting the limited reach of alternative education systems like Radio Learning.
The Mobile Neighbourhood Learning Corners (MNLCs) initiative, implemented by Street Child of Cameroon (SCOC), has demonstrated a transformative impact in addressing these challenges. To date, 10,558 children have benefited from foundational literacy and numeracy instruction, structured support, and access to critical learning materials. This initiative has also equipped teachers with effective methodologies, including the Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) approach and Radio Learning programs.
However, key gaps remain. Many out-of-school children are unable to access these resources due to economic barriers or disabilities. Furthermore, insecurity and poor infrastructure continue to hinder progress in underserved communities. Building on the successes of the MNLC project, this proposal seeks to expand access to education, address barriers to retention, and foster long-term resilience within Cameroon’s education system, in alignment with the Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) Minimum Standards. The MSNA 2024 analysis reveals that 64% are in dare need of educational assistance, and that divisions like Ndian have 25% of children without access to education.
Objectives
1. Expand Access: Provide quality, inclusive education to 5,500 additional out-of-school children, prioritizing underserved and marginalized groups, including children with disabilities.
2. Strengthen Teacher Capacity: Enhance 550 teacher effectiveness through targeted training in TaRL and Radio Learning approaches, with a focus on delivering education in emergency contexts, to fill the gaps in the lack of teachers revealed by findings from MSNA Round 10 (2024).
3. Mitigate Barriers to Retention: Address economic and social barriers by providing financial support to families and ensuring inclusive, safe learning environments.
4. Foster Community Ownership: Increase community engagement to support education initiatives, enhance accountability, and build resilience in conflict-affected areas.
Challenges and Mitigation Measures
1. Challenge: Security concerns and mobility constraints in conflict zones
Mitigation: Expand remote learning programs and deploy mobile learning stations to reach children in inaccessible areas.
2. Challenge: Economic pressures leading to high dropout rates.
Mitigation: Provide financial aid to families and prioritize enrolment for the most vulnerable children.
3. Challenge: Limited inclusivity for children with disabilities.
Mitigation: Develop and distribute adaptive learning tools and train teachers in inclusive practices.
4. Challenge: Insufficient teacher retention.
Mitigation: Introduce professional development incentives and psychosocial support programs to improve teacher well-being.
Conclusion
This proposal seeks to scale up the successes of the MNLC initiative to ensure that vulnerable children in Cameroon’s conflict-affected regions have access to inclusive, quality education. By addressing key barriers and fostering resilience in the education system, this project aligns with INEE Minimum Standards and aims to empower children, educators, and communities to thrive in challenging circumstances.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

This project focuses on addressing critical barriers to education for children in underserved and crisis-affected regions. The assessed needs include: 1. Limited Access to Quality Learning Opportunities: Many children, particularly in underserved areas, lack access to foundational literacy and numeracy education due to insufficient educational facilities and materials. Additionally, children with disabilities face heightened barriers due to the lack of inclusive tools and resources. 2. Inadequate Teacher Capacity: The crisis has exacerbated the shortage of trained educators. Many existing teachers lack the skills to implement effective teaching methodologies, such as Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) and Radio Learning, or to address the needs of diverse learners, including children with disabilities and those requiring psychosocial support. 3. Economic and Social Barriers to Education: Poverty and stigma are significant barriers preventing children, particularly those with disabilities, from accessing and staying in school. Vulnerable families struggle with the financial burden of education, while social stigma further limits enrolment and participation, especially for marginalized groups. 4. Unsafe and Inadequate Learning Environments: Poor infrastructure and safety concerns in schools discourage attendance and retention. Schools often lack basic amenities such as gender-segregated toilets, accessibility ramps, and secure environments conducive to learning. 5. Weak Community Engagement and Support: Low awareness of the importance of education, especially in emergencies, undermines community involvement in supporting educational initiatives. Without active engagement, monitoring, and support from the community, child retention and enrolment remain low. 6. Insufficient Monitoring and Evaluation Systems: Weak systems for tracking educational outcomes and ensuring accountability hinder the ability to identify gaps, measure progress, and adjust strategies in line with the INEE Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies. Proposed Activities include:1. Expanding Access to Learning Opportunities• Establish 215 additional Mobile Neighbourhood Learning Corners in underserved locations to provide foundational literacy and numeracy education.• Distribute inclusive learning materials, including adaptive tools for children with disabilities. 2. Teacher Training and Support• Train 300 new teachers in effective methodologies, including TaRL and Radio Learning, and provide refresher training for 250 previously trained teachers.• Conduct training workshops to build teacher capacity in inclusive education practices and psychosocial support. 3. Addressing Economic and Social Barriers• Implement financial aid programs to reduce the economic burden on vulnerable families, ensuring children remain in school.• Collaborate with community leaders to develop inclusive strategies that address stigma and increase enrolment for children with disabilities. 4. Enhancing Safety and Infrastructure• Develop and implement school risk mitigation plans to improve the safety and security of learning environments.• Upgrade school infrastructure to include gender-segregated toilets, ramps for accessibility, and shaded learning areas. 5. Strengthening Community Engagement• Organize awareness campaigns to promote the importance of education and community support for learning initiatives.• Form and strengthen community monitoring committees to oversee school operations and support child retention efforts. 6. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning• Establish a robust Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework to track progress, assess impact, and identify areas for improvement.• Conduct quarterly reviews with stakeholders, including educators, parents, and community leaders, to ensure alignment with INEE Minimum Standards.
