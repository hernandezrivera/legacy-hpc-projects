Project Summary

This proposal is line with the objectives and the framework the OPTI country HRP strategy as follows:

Strategic Objective 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL.

Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.

Further, this project is also aligned with the Shelter Cluster HRP objectives as follows:

To addresses the needs of IDPs through providing them safe shelters, affording them enhanced protection and to meet their basic humanitarian needs during crisis. Activity: Carry out inter-cluster and multi-sectoral initiatives to enhance emergency response Indicator: Complete the work started in 2017 to upgrade (14 government) schools as designated emergency shelters

Specific cluster objectives :

1- Displaced populations are provided support that mitigates immediate harm and ensures adequate and safe temporary shelter solutions. 2- Vulnerable HHs exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity. 3- Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased.

Needs

During the 2014 conflict, nearly 485,000 people were internally displaced, whereby 285,000 IDPs were hosted in 93 UNRWA schools and 48,000 in 24 PA schools (excluding informal shelters). However, Gaza lacks well-equipped and safe formal shelters. According to a qualitative gender focused study conducted by MA’AN in 2014, several constraints affected women in DES such as restricted mobility compared to men with freedom to move inside or outside shelters. Women reported that toilets were inaccessible and showering was non-existent. Moreover, during FGDs conducted by Oxfam in July 2018 for the Pre-Crisis Market Analysis (PCMA), participants experiencing displacement and sheltered in DES stated that they would not go to a DES if displaced, due to protection, safety and restricted movement issues including close proximity of men and boys and far toilets reporting refrain from using toilets after dark, causing health issues. Overcrowded shelters resulted in disputes between males and females due to fear of harassment and GBV. Based on the 2014 review of the Emergency Operations Center and discussions with Gaza’s inter-cluster coordination group (ICCG) and all clusters, alternative shelter solutions were explored. Conclusions reached, in coordination with MoEHE and MoSD, is that schools are most feasible options for IDPs: Hard component: Identify and upgrade schools as DES. Soft component: Develop sector-specific, gendersensitive Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) and management tools for DES operation, integrate GBV prevention and protection and train shelter managers. Engagement with IDPs (men and women) in shelter management and decisionmaking to ensure equal access to humanitarian assistance, registration and access to information. Oxfam is currently implementing most of the soft components funded by GAC and ECHO. The proposed project will upgrade 14 governmental schools to serve as DES; targeting people fleeing homes due to security concerns or lack of services in current residence. Post emergency phase, schools will be converted into Collective Centers if needed. The 2018 HNO addresses IDP needs as a priority, including safe shelters with enhanced protection during crisis and meeting basic humanitarian needs. Led by OCHA/IDP working group in cooperation with UNRWA, the MoSD and MoEHE and relevant clusters, a selection criteria was agreed including a list of 65 schools. 32 schools were selected, based on five additional criteria: location, capacity, access, existing facilities/infrastructure and security/safety. A multi-sectorial technical taskforce (TTF): Oxfam, ACF, NRC, PUI, UNICEF, GVC, UNDP and others, in cooperation with relevant authorities assessed each school in April-May 2016. Considering UNRWA shelter standards, the TTF developed two tools: A technical assessment tool to assess the security, capacity and suitability of nominated schools; and SoPs in consultation with clusters, mainstreaming protection, gender and GBV in line with IASC guidelines. The inclusion of specific considerations like assigning separate showers and toilet units, lighting, safe spaces for women and children and cooking spaces, privacy and cultural concerns were considered (i.e. prefabricated partitions inside shelter rooms). The needs of persons with disabilities were considered through accessibility measures (ramps). The assessment analysis by TTF ensures all DES requirements are met. The nominated schools were prioritized as per the criteria, including hosting capacity, budget and geographical distribution. The final outputs (scope of work, budget, design, project time frame) were presented to the ICCG and shared with stakeholders, including the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), Coastal Municipality Water Utility (CMWU), MoSD and MoEHE. The total number of beneficiaries is estimated at 22,500 IDPs in 32 schools ( 703 IDPs per school).

Activities

In close coordination with partners (ACF, Oxfam, UNICEF, PUI, NRC,GVC and UNDP), the proposed intervention will cover WASH, Shelter, food security, and energy components for 14 schools to meet needs of OCHA/IDP working group assessment, clusters and humanitarian agencies (Handicap international, Islamic Relief, GVC, NRC, Oxfam, ACF and PUI) in cooperation with UN agencies (UNRWA, UNICEF, UN-Women, UNDP and WFP) who provided technical advice ensuring that DES design guarantees safety of displaced IDPs including gender and age. WASH components are designed to ensure access to clean, safe water, sanitation and solid waste facilities per DES including rehabilitation and installation of existing sanitation and hygiene facilities, (construction of water wells and brackish water desalination plants with a capacity of 10 to 20 m3/day; procuring and deploying standby generators, domestic and drinking water storage tanks, solid waste containers, and laundry facilities). Toilets and showers will be installed in sufficient numbers for both females and males. The DES components will include maintenance works in existing buildings, lightning, external ramps, extinguishers, installation of laminated film on windows for safety, and fabricated temporary partitioning to ensure privacy for women and girls. The Food security sector intervention will include the provision of stoves and protected gas cylinders to allow preparation of light meals for babies and children. DES key equipment and hardware, including backup generators, should be protected by security fencing for child safety and against unauthorized access. Climate conditions summer vs winter will be addressed during the upgrade where applicable. Oxfam proposes to rehabilitate 4 out of 14 unfunded schools (Asad Al Saftawe, Salah Khalaf, Mostafa Hafez and Ahmed Shawqe) benefiting approximately 2,800 IDPs in the four schools with an average of 700 IDPs per school. Currently 18 schools have been upgraded by UNICEF, NRC, Oxfam, GVC and PUI through ECHO, DIFID and the OCHA Pool fund while KFW has agreed to add the DES shelter components to an ongoing school building project. Partners (UNICEF, Oxfam, NRC, PUI, GVC and UNDP) are seeking funds to upgrade the 14 remaining schools and to complete outstanding components in ongoing projects. The total construction and rehabilitation works (32 schools) is estimated at 7.664 USD million while the available budget for the completed and ongoing works in the 18 schools is 3.34 USD million (excluding admin/operation costs). The estimated gap for the construction/rehabilitation costs of the 14 schools is 4.324 USD million. It is worth mentioning that targeted school students and teachers will benefit from the project as follows: signs, access improvements to people with disabilities, ramps, backup generator, additional stairs, rails for stairs, essential maintenance, fire extinguishers. It is estimated that 34,885 students and teachers (19,396 girls and 15,489 boys) and 825 female and 644 male teachers will benefit. PEF, the implementing partner, has diverse expertise in WASH including community mobilisation, hygiene promotion and mass media and has established trust with vulnerable communities. PEF will implement community mobilization and will announce tenders in local newspapers in compliance with procurement regulations. The rehabilitation works will be undertaken by private contractors with similar experience (solar systems). Oxfam and PEF will supervise and monitor implementation on a daily basis in coordination with the IDPs working group and the MoEHE technical engineers who will monitor progress, quality of work, inspection of materials and approval.

Indicators

At the beginning of the project, monitoring and evaluation plans will be developed to ensure efficient monitoring and management of the assessment process and reporting progress against project targets. This will be carried out in coordination with the ICCG, IDPs working group and clusters. The plan will describe the project’s entire M&E system and will include indicators, roles and responsibilities, what forms and tools will be used, and how the data will flow through the organization. Regular field monitoring visits will be conducted by Oxfam’s M&E officers on a weekly basis to follow up on the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan. The M&E officers will share field monitoring reports summarizing main findings after each monitoring visit. Interim and final reports including updates on activities and progress of indicators will be submitted by Oxfam to the donor on agreed dates. To ensure accountability, implementing organizations will maintain ongoing discussions with all concerned stakeholders, including but not limited to potential beneficiary households, MoSD), the Palestinian Civil Defense (PCD), and MoEHE, as well as the relevant water authorities, to ensure that the proposed activities take into consideration the real needs of the affected population. Consultations will also be held with the management teams of the DES. Furthermore, each organization will implement its own feedback and complaint mechanisms to collect any complaints or suggestions regarding the implemented activities. All implementing partners are considering communication with community elements in the upgrading process. Partners met with the affected people as a result of the recent conflict on different occasions and continued to update them through official meetings with their representatives who attended different bilateral meetings including IDP WG meetings lead by OCHA. Communication with communities adjacent to the DES will be consulted to address their concerns during the design and upgrading of the DES. In addition, partner’s agencies received communication with communities (CwC) training carried out by BBC Media Action in coordination with OCHA. The training will help implementing agencies to develop/improve communication strategies and tools during the project phase.
