Project Summary

This project aims to provide life-sustaining and life-saving WASH services to 350,000 beneficiaries in conflict-affected, newly-accessible or Hard-To-Reach (HTR) areas in the governorates of Idlib, Aleppo, Homs, Deir-Ez-Zor, Rural Damascus, Damascus, Dara’a and Quneitra. ADRA will provide life-sustaining assistance to 300,000 men, women, boys and girls through the rehabilitation of water networks, sewerage system rehabilitation, the distribution of household and community water tanks and garbage bins as well as debris removal in support of WASH Objective 1. ADRA will provide life-saving assistance through the rehabilitation of WASH facilities in last resort sites including camps, collective centers, transit centers and informal settlements for newly-displaced populations as well as the distribution of hygiene kits benefitting 50,000 men, women, boys and girls in support of WASH Objective 2.
ADRA will target highly vulnerable communities with WASH severity scale of level 4 or above in 7 governorates including Aleppo (Jebel Saman), Deir-Ez-Zor (eastern rural DeZ - Al Mayadeen, Al Boukamal, Al Shaarah, Mo Hasan, Mehkan, Ak Koureyah Sbekhan, and western rural DeZ including Ayash, Al Shmeteya, Al Treef, Al Tebni, Al Khraytah), Rural Damascus ( Kafr Batna, Darayya, Babella, Maliha, Duma, Qatana, Kiswah), Quneitra (Beir Ajam, Breiq), Dara’a (Kherbet Ghazala and Jizeh) and Idlib (Idlib). The target areas for WASH intervention will be either HTR, newly-accessible or conflict-affected and will include newly-displaced populations, self-organized IDP returnees, over-burdened host communities, People with Disabilities (PwDs), female-headed households (FHH) or IDPs residing in last resort sites.
The project aims to increase the resilience of the Syrian population through the provision of WASH services and the mitigation of protection risks to men, women, boys and girls as well as highly vulnerable groups such as older adults and People with Disabilities. In IDP camps and settlements, there is increased risk of GVB for women, young children, children (boys and girls), adolescent and young adult females who must travel distances to WASH facilities often without protective lighting. The lack of household-level access to drinking water places a financial and physical burden upon male and female middle-aged and older adults as well as People with Disabilities to procure safe drinking water. In addition, inadequate needs assessments may result in hygiene services neglecting the needs of young children, children, older adults, and people with disabilities.
SWASH Project will be regularly monitored and evaluated by gender-balanced project staff including technical, social, education and MEAL staff and volunteers to ensure comprehensive and neutral monitoring is achieved. ADRA staff will solicit feedback on project design and implementation from young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults to ensure the best coverage of needs and safety of all beneficiaries, particularly for hygiene kits, the location of WASH facilities and garbage bins. Beneficiary feedback and ADRA staff monitoring will guide project modifications in all activities.

Needs

As the crisis in Syria has evolved in 2018, with many HTR and besieged areas becoming newly-accessible, the depth of its impact upon the lives of the 15.6 million Syrian individuals in need of WASH assistance, including 6.2 million with acute WASH needs, has become increasingly evident (HNO Draft, 2019). Greater access to previously HTR areas has resulted in an increase in men, women, boys and girls moving back to their place of origin with limited access to basic services and infrastructural damage to water and sewerage networks, creating significant access barriers to safe, clean and sufficient drinking water. This has resulted in many communities resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as the adoption of poor hygiene and sanitation practices particularly women, boys and girls who require greater volumes of water per day. In addition, men, women, boys and girls rely upon informal and unsafe sources of drinking water (ADRA Syria Needs Assessment, October 2018). Due to extensive damage to water and sewerage networks, priority needs across Syria were identified for safe and clean drinking water. While across Syria as a whole, up to 55% of households rely on unsafe water sources to meet WASH needs due to partially-functioning water networks, in Dara’a, Quneitra, Idleb and (DeZ), 35-45% of the population depend on water trucking as their sole source of drinking water (HNO Draft, 2019). Damage to sewerage systems leaves 70% of sewage untreated across Syria significantly increasing health risks (HNO 2019) particularly for boys and girls. In addition, 10% of garbage is being left in public areas with higher percentages (35%) recorded in areas with recent returnees increasing pressure on local solid waste management resources and contributing to the increase in leishmaniosis cases over the past year amongst men and women (HNO 2019). An inadequate sanitation environment poses significant health risks for vulnerable groups such as women, boys, girls, the elderly and PwDs while the newly displaced are often the most severely impacted. In IDP camps and settlements, IDPs spend up to 50% of their income on water and WASH needs were identified as a top priority for 30% of IDPs (HNO 2019) due to restricted movement and limited access to humanitarian assistance. Based on multi-governorate ADRA field visits, water network rehabilitation is required in many areas dependent upon water trucking from partners or unsupervised private sources such as Bir Ajam and Bareka in Quneitra, Moadamieh (RD) and Ghasm (Dara’a). Other areas require water and sewerage network rehabilitation due to drinking water from contaminated water sources and sewage leaking. Shaba’s (RD) sole borehole has sewerage leaking while in recent water quality tests in the Kafr Batna area (RD), the main source of available water is dug wells, which tested with coliforms at a level of 10,000/100 ml (where 0 is acceptable) and NO3 levels tested at 120 (where <50 is acceptable). IDPs in Hajin, DeZ, collect untreated water directly from the river and dig their own pits to serve as latrines outside the settlement while in southern rural Aleppo, water is unpotable in the wells due to pollutants infecting the water. The need for the distribution of sanitary storage containers such as household water tanks as well as hygiene promotion and garbage bins to create a sanitary environment is priority, particularly in recently accessible and rural areas as well as those with a high IDP population such as Tell Rafat in Aleppo and DeZ both eastern rural DeZ and western rural DeZ. Based on a recent widescale ADRA Syria WASH needs assessment in Kafr Batna (RD), communities rely heavily upon unsanitary storage containers for water collection from contaminated shallow dug wells, increasing the likelihood of infection and placing a burden on vulnerable individuals including women, girls, PwDs and the elderly for water collection.

Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Repair and rehabilitation of water systems and sewerage systems This activity responds to the WASH sector plan for HRP 2019. In close coordination with the Ministry of Water Resources and SARC, ADRA will select areas of priority needs for water or sewerage system rehabilitation, conduct social and technical assessments to identify priority WASH needs in the area, collect feedback from beneficiaries including men, women, boys, and girls, tailoring activities to expressed needs. Rehabilitation work will include repair, rehabilitation and maintenance of existing water and sanitation networks including boreholes and the installation of submersible pumps, main network line repair, rehabilitation of operation rooms and elevated tanks as well as the provision of sanitary water storage containers (household and community water tanks). Areas will be selected according to the severity of WASH needs (access to drinking water and the sanitary environment), population density, presence of highly vulnerable groups such as FHHs, PwDs, women, children, the elderly and with high populations of self-organized returnees or in last resort sites such as IDP camps and settlements. All activities will alleviate the burden of water collection by children, adolescents, young adults, male and female middle-aged adults, older adults as well as PwDs who hold varying degrees of responsibility for water collection. Following identification of priority areas, the ADRA team will conduct technical assessments and prepare the Bill of Quantity (BoQ). Based on the BoQ, the tender committee will take place to identify successful contractors. Experienced engineers will participate in the tender committee, assessing each bidder for quality of submitted samples, previous experience in similar projects, timeframe and price. Once the bidder is chosen and implementation begun, the ADRA engineers will monitor the progress to ensure the adherence of the contractor to the guidelines and contracted agreement. Activity 2: Provision and distribution of WASH supplies such as hygiene kits for infants and adults, including promotion of hygiene to the most vulnerable. An assessment will be carried out to identify the specific needs of each community including young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged men and women, PwDs, and older adults. NFI and hygiene kits will be provided in selected areas complementing multi-sectoral intervention or in areas with priority sanitation needs. ADRA will follow the cluster and SARC recommendations for components of hygiene kits which will have special consideration for vulnerable groups such as sanitary napkins for women and lice shampoo for infants, young children and adolescents. Hygiene promotion will complement the distribution of hygiene kits ensuring that items are used correctly, hygiene practices are promoted and raising awareness on topics of concern for male and female young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults. Activity 3: Provision and distribution of garbage containers and debris removal Following close coordination with the relevant governorate to identify the priority areas for installation of garbage bins, ADRA will request the necessary approvals, conduct social and technical assessments in the target areas to ensure an equal distribution of services amongst the local population which are equally accessible to men, women, boys, girls, PwDs, FHHs, and the elderly. ADRA will distribute the garbage bins in areas with a high IDP population and population density or newly accessible areas with a high returnee population. When considering areas for distribution, ADRA will coordinate with municipalities to ensure that the collection and disposal system is in place and appropriate trucks and disposal sites are available. The size of bins will be agreed upon with the local municipalities and procurement will take place according to ADRA’s procurement policy.

Protection Risk Analysis

A number of risks may arise during the implementation of WASH activities. There is a medium risk of unequal distribution of services with high impact upon men, women, boys and girls when selecting works and locations for water / sewerage network rehabilitation. Without a careful understanding of community dynamics, the works may be perceived as empowering the position of armed groups. To mitigate this risk, ADRA will establish strong links with authorities and local community leaders to ensure a ‘do no harm’ approach is maintained. The inclusion of community members in planning, needs assessments, and project design is essential to gain a comprehensive understanding of how the distribution of services will be perceived and avoid privileging one group over another. In addition, there is a low risk with a high impact of theft of WASH rehabilitation or construction materials. To mitigate this risk, ADRA will promote community ownership of the works through the promotion of cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance.The rehabilitation of emergency WASH facilities poses a high risk of GBV with a high impact on the community, especially for the elderly, PwDs, women, boys and girls. This risk will be mitigated by ensuring SPHERE standards are respected during implementation as well as consulting women and girls during the needs assessments, project design, implementation and evaluation stage. ADRA has an active complaints mechanism matrix to receive feedback from beneficiaries and incorporate their comments into project adaptation and lessons learned. Finally, the risk of unequal distribution of hygiene kits is high with a high impact upon the community, in addition to the risk of gender insensitive contents and the distribution of expired goods in the event of delays in distribution. There is a high risk of creating tension which could lead to violence among community members as a result. In addition, the needs of women, young children, boys and girls may not be met as a result of gender-insensitive contents. This risk will be mitigated through the use of standardized kits contents and will involve community members including men, women, boys and girls in the planning and distribution phase to ensure no single group is prioritized to the neglect of another and that the most vulnerable families are selected in a neutral and informed selection process.. Additionally, ADRA will perform extensive coordination with authorities to obtain approvals and distribution plans in a timely manner.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions • Required approvals are granted in a timely manner for assessments and implementation of activities and ADRA maintains strong relationships with the governorates during the life cycle of the project. • The security situation remains stable and allows access to beneficiaries during the lifecycle of the project including during needs assessments and implementation to ensure the most appropriate and needs-based services are provided. • Skilled workforce, qualified suppliers and contractors are available and willing to work on the proposed project and are granted access to project sites during implementation. • Relevant ministries have the capacity to continue the regular maintenance and running costs of rehabilitated water systems.Risks• Permissions are not obtained in a timely manner, making ADRA unable to access the target locations.• The security situation becomes unstable and hinders or delays the implementation of some/all activities.• Qualified staff are not available leading to challenges in implementing planned activities.• Risk of spread of water borne diseases due to deteriorating hygiene situation and limited access to safe water.• Pressure on ADRA to target locations or individuals outside the selection criteria.• Qualified contractors are not available to work on the proposed project.• Planned activities pose protection or security risks to beneficiaries.Contingency Measures• ADRA will maintain its relationship with all stakeholders and undertake advocacy efforts with high level officials to obtain approvals from relevant government authorities if approvals are delayed.• If the security situation prevents access to target areas, ADRA will utilize volunteers from the local area to monitor the situation. In case of a prolonged unstable security situation or inaccessibility, ADRA will design alternative plans to move the project to accessible areas.• ADRA will identify potential reliable staff and build their capacity where necessary to implement the project activities.• Coordinate with the MoWR and SARC to integrate the project with water treatment from the source and expand awareness in the community to enhance the hygiene situation and access to safe water.• ADRA will ensure full transparency with all stakeholders about the planned activities and associated selection criteria. If any undue pressure is placed on ADRA to divert resources away from the most vulnerable, this will immediately be taken up with the relevant authorities and UN representatives. • Identify potential reliable suppliers and contractors during the initial stages of the project through connections with UN and other INGOs.• All activities will be planned and monitored closely in coordination with the local community to ensure no harm comes to beneficiaries. If this occurs, the activity will immediately be halted and the impact assessed and an evaluation made of the possibility to continue, alter or cancel the activity.
