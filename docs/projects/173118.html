Project Summary

The project’s objective aligns with Libya Protection Sector Strategy Objectives for 2020-2021 by enhancing the protection environment and provide integrated and specialized protection services for the highly vulnerable people and population at risks. The project includes activities which aim to address the protection needs of the communities affected by the conflict by the provision of integrated protection assistance to reduce vulnerabilities at individual level, sensitization and awareness on protection issues and concerns faced by the community and provision of protection information management reports. The access to services will be ensured in synergy with other ongoing PUI’s projects implementing in the area and through an integrated response including protection, psychosocial support, health and socio economical support to the most vulnerable persons and persons at highest risks. The project aims also to reinforce community’s capacities by ensuring sensitization on key protection issues and participatory approach during the whole project implementation .
The project targets both Libyans and non-Libyans populations, IDPs, returnees, Refugees, migrants (including highly vulnerable persons, conflict affected persons, persons at risks and population exposed to protection risks due to the COVID-19 pandemic…)
PUI’s analyses of the context and needs aims to be Age, Gender, Diversity sensitive and will ensure to implement the interventions in a way to address specific needs identified and ensure equal access to services. Humanitarian principles will guide the implementation of this project to promote safety, dignity and rights of affected people, respect age / gender diversity and protection mainstreaming, and mitigate exposure to additional risks through conflict sensitive and do not harm approaches.

1 - Needs

In 2020, Libya continued to face protracted humanitarian and protection crisis, resulting from several years of armed conflicts, crises governance, and breakdown of the rule of law and socio economic challenges. Population living in Libyaare particularly affected by the conflict, leading to displacements and exposure to violence, human rights violations and abuses, contamination from explosive hazards and critical access to essential services.. The recent COVID 19 pandemic exacerbated pre-existing needs and vulnerabilities in a context of decrease of livelihood opportunities, rise of prices, deterioration of living conditions and restrictions of movements. . The breakdown of the rule of law, lack of consistency of national legal framework with IHRL and IHL, lack of enforcement of protective law included in national legal framework contributes to further exacerbate the protection risks and impunity of perpetrators committing grave human rights violations. The degradation of the security in the entire country and the collapse of governmental institutions had a particular impact on the region of Al Kufrah. The Al Kufrah Mantika is suffering from the consequences of decades of local conflicts that further exacerbated insecurity and humanitarian situation. Conflict related protection risks continue to occur in the area, impacting physical and psychological well-being of the population affected by presence of protection threats; violence, coercion, deprivation, including community tensions, presence of explosive hazards and infrastructures damaged.The overall population living in this mantika are suffering from lack of access to basic services and are exposed to extreme levels of risks and forced to engage in perilous and exploitative coping or survival strategies. Al Kufra mantika is considered as one of the mantika with the highest severity of needs and part of the prioritized areas identified by the Protection Sector for 2021 humanitarian response (people in protection need is estimated in Al Kufra area to 9092 persons and includes 2,742 IDPs, 474 returnees, 997 Non displaced, 4868 migrants and 11 Refugees who are having specific protection risks).IDPs living in Al Kufrah and surrounding areas, are particularly vulnerable to protection threat and issues and face challenges in accessing basic services, due to the loss or lack of civil documentations and reduced access to adequate shelter and economic opportunities. Migrants and refugees are considered as the one of the most vulnerable groups in Libya and faced additional protection issues due to their status and are at further risk of grave human rights violations including torture, inhuman treatment and human trafficking, violence and sexual abuse, arbitrary arrest and detention and exposure to exploitation. exploitation. Their vulnerabilities are increased by various factors such unemployment, gender, nationality, duration of stay that have direct impact on migrant’s humanitarian needs (Health, NFI and Food). Tebu Community living in the area, face specific protection issues and are exposed to violence, discrimination, lack of civil documentation and limited freedom of movements. Due to the threats they are exposed to and the risk of violence they face, this community face particular challenges in accessing basic services such as food, health, water and education.In addition to the populations groups at risk described above, person lacking legal documentation, children, women, persons with disabilities and older persons are facing particular protection threat, discrimination and additional barriers to access services. Moreover, in the current context of COVID 19 pandemic and its impacts on socio economic situation, all populations groups with preexisting vulnerabilities and poor socio economic standing are at further protection risks and will need to receive adequate and appropriate integrated protection assistance.

2 - Activities

A 1. Protection monitoring and protection needs assessments, identification and referrals. PUI Protection team will conduct regular protection monitoring in the most affected areas in Al Kufrain order to monitor the protection environment and key protection issues ( freedom of movement, barriers to access services, safety and security concerns, psychological well-being of the affected population, social cohesion and issues related to COVID 19’s impacts Regular protection monitoring reports will be produced and shared to provide evidence based information and address protection issues identified in the area. A2. Provision of integrated protection services with a focus on psychosocial assistance, and targeted support to persons with specific protection needsPUI’s team will identify people at risk and in need of protection response and will provide integrated protection assistance through information sharing of available services, provision of PSS and PFA, referral to specialized services – including MHPSS and Health services provided by PUI’s team and referrals to external specialized actors. Identified people at heightened risks will be also provided by socio economic support aiming at reducing negative coping mechanisms.. A 3. Capacity building activities targeting humanitarian actors and national / local authorities and informal leaders to improve quality provision of protection services and enhance the centrality of protection in non-protection responsesPUI will provide capacity building activities to key community leaders on key protection risks identified in the area. Targeting humanitarian actors and PUI’s staff will be provided with capacity building to ensure the centrality of protection through the provision of humanitarian assistance and ensure safe, dignified, participative and meaningful access for the population. A 4. Targeted awareness raising on key protection issues through a participatory approach, PUI will implement awareness campaigns.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

PUI commits to uphold fundamental human rights, and is continuously seeking to increase the input of Affected Population (AP) in shaping the assistance through incorporating systematic engagement of the AP in different project’s phases-Need assessment: Active participation/engagement of the AP is the main pillar in designing and implementing qualitative/quantitative data collection processes. Capturing diverse perspectives regarding risks, and protection concerns faced by the AGD groups, in addition to involvement of different stakeholders and key actors to ensure proper coordination of response complying with do-no harm principles.-Design, implementation and follow-up: emerging from the need assessments’ results, activities and methodologies of service delivery are defined and Feedback Processing Mechanisms (FPM) are adapted accordingly, to capture contextual determinants and priorities of the AP. Prior to the implementation, AP and relevant stakeholders are informed of the activities and FPM designed, informed of their rights to provide feedback-Collecting/receiving feedbacks: FPM is incorporated in project cycle and linked to the monitoring & evaluation (M&E) activities, promoting transparency and trust, to introduce corrections/improvements actions in timely-manner. Different key steps are identified to ensure collecting AP’s feedback, including but not limited to:1.Surveys,either fully dedicated or including questions to collect feedback on appropriateness and quality of activities 2.M&E activities: steps to receive/collect AP’s perspective are incorporated into standard M&E surveys and templates. 3.Feedback Channels: different feedback channels are dedicated solely to receive feedback from the AP (ie, hotline, secured email, boxes….etc) appropriate channels are identified according to local context to ensure safe and easy access by the AP
