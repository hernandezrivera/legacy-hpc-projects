Project Summary

Since November 2017, the socio-political crisis in the North West and the South West regions turned into a situation of violence with increasing reports of human rights violations and abuses, including extra-judicial killings, arbitrary arrests and destruction of property, and rising humanitarian needs. These needs range from immediate lifesaving to protracted recovery. Almost 680,000 Cameroonians are now internally displaced due to this crisis in the NW and SW regions, but also in West and Littoral. The displaced communities have acute needs for nutritious food, protection, shelter/NFI, water and sanitation as well as access to health and education.
Prior to the crisis, the NW region presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with a high rate of stunting of 36%. The current crisis compounded the situation and it is estimated that more than 188,000 people are in need of nutrition services in the NWSW regions. Up to 8.000 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop severe acute malnutrition in the 2 regions. In the NW region, reports have indicated a precarious GAM situation of 5.1% and analysis from Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners between January and September 2020, also showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 4.8% in NW. Populations living in remote areas, internally displaced persons (IDPs) in urban/peri urban and households/ communities hosting IDPs are vulnerable to malnutrition since they are exposed to aggravating factors (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions, sanitation and hygiene services, and morbidity due to overstretching of basic social services). The nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate in 2020 since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations.
Emergency Food Security Assessment done by WFP in the NW have revealed a rapid deterioration of the nutrition situation with SAM prevalence increasing by more than five times in North West (from 0.4% and 0.5% in 2014 to 1.5% and 2.8% in 2019). An estimated 16,335 SAM and 20,175 MAM children are in need for immediate treatment. Based on the EFSA findings and expected incidence of both SAM and MAM, an estimated 76,580 children will be affected by acute malnutrition if no appropriate response is provided on time. Intervention packages should comprise case management of moderately malnourished children and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) while ensuring continuum prevention-treatment- prevention among the most affected population to prevent further deterioration of nutritional situation putting the children in high risks of morbidity and death.
The purpose of this intervention is to improve the nutrition and the health status of vulnerable IDPs and host communities and prevent all forms of malnutrition in children aged 6–59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The intervention will target at least 15,000 beneficiaries from Bali, Batibo, Mbengwi, Njikwa and Bafut health districts. It is expected that access to diets, practices and services that prevent stunting, underweight and micronutrient deficiencies amongst for boys and girls aged under five years is increased. Such practices and services should also lead to early detection, and treatment of all forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition (including severe acute malnutrition) for boys and girls aged under five years through expansion of nutrition services and enhanced community screening and referral. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition amongst vulnerable groups will be enhanced mainly through the Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programs (BSFP) with the provision of specialized nutritious food and the promotion of optimal infant and young child feeding in emergencies.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian agencies such as OCHA, UNICEF, WFP have since 2019 reported deteriorating nutrition conditions in the North West Region as a result of the ongoing conflict that has caused multiple displacements. Women and children counting 48% and 44% of the total displaced population respectively, remain the most vulnerable categories among the affected population by the violence, including persons living with disability. Needs range from immediate lifesaving to protracted recovery. Poor indicators on Infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5% of exclusive breast-feeding rates for NW region. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) was reported to be low at 31.8%. The major drivers of malnutrition (food insecurity, morbidity, poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacement) accelerated in 2021 and likely to remain the same in 2023. The nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations. Within the nutrition sector, needs range from immediate implementation of BSFP through the provision of specialized nutritious food (super cereal plus), supporting Community engagement for behavior and social change, identification of SAM and MAM cases within target communities for treatment, integrated management of SAM and MAM cases and the implementation of activities to promote infant and young child feeding practices in emergencies. Promotion of Infant and Young Child Feeding Practices: This activity will target mainly PLW and caretakers of children aged 0-23 months as key strategy to support and protect these demographic groups. IYCF-E community outreach activities such as sensitization on appropriate feeding practices, counseling will be conducted by CHW at both distribution sites and community levels for all PLW-Gs and caretakers and through support /action groups. Sensitizations will focus on the promotion of exclusive breastfeeding up to six months of age and complementary breastfeeding up to 24 months; counseling on optimal complementary feeding; appropriate feeding practices for sick children; and, hygiene practices related to food preparation and handling, including hand-washing and proper storage of food and water. It is expected that IYCF practices would lead to behavior change and acquisition of knowledge. COMINSUD in close coordination with WFP will provide initial training to all project staff (CHWs, community mobilisers, team leaders etc.) on blanket supplementary feeding, promotion of IYCF and MUAC and edema screening. Implementation of BSFP: Under this activity, children 6-23 months and PLW will be enrolled into the nutrition program and receive food rations (Super cereal Plus) in order to prevent malnutrition. Each beneficiary will receive approximately 2 parquets of 1.5kg each of super cereal + each month. In the case of treatment, this package could be multiplied by four each month. Community door-to-door screening and referral: This activity will target all children aged 6-59 months. It is expected that at least 50000 children are screened in each quarter of the year to identify existing SAM and MAM cases within the communities and engage referrals and treatment. CHW and community mobilisers will conduct door-to-door screenings once every quarter. All SAM cases identified during screening will be referred to Therapeutic Supplementary Feeding Program Centers for proper treatment. Integrated management of SAM and MAM: In the absence of MAM treatment services, the Caretakers of the MAM cases will be sensitized and counselled on appropriate complementary feeding and use of locally available nutritious food for their children. During the treatment, children will be provided a double ration (200g/child/day) compared to the children 6 to 23 months.
