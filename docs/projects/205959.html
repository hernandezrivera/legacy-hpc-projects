Project Summary

More than 259,451 individuals have fled from Sudan into neighboring South Sudan, with 49% being women and girls, 46% below the age of 18, and 4% over the age of 60. An alarming 91% of arrivals are South Sudanese. Interviews with families and children reveal experiences of insecurity, harassment, and violence during their journey to South Sudan (IOM report 2023). There are concerns about gender-based violence (GBV), psychological trauma, children separated from their families and increased cases of early marriages. Majority of those IDPs, returnees and refugees are children, women and elderly person.
The recent clashes in Upper Nile and Warrap State continue to affect the population and may increase in the lead-up to the General Elections in December 2024. In Equatoria Region, the presence of small arms, light weapons, mine contamination and explosive remnants of war contribute to the level of violence. South Sudanese women and girls continues to face the direct effect of violence and sexual gender-based violence.
Majority of the women and girls affected by the conflict in Sudan and flooding in Upper Nile and Jonglei States are unemployed, some girls and women were subjected to rape and sexual abuses. This makes it easy for the youths to be easy targets for recruitment into the army or local militia groups. Which are linked to increasing poverty, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and psychological abuses.
Panyikang County is affected by flood & Armed Conflicts causing massive displacement of host community and IDPs (men, women, and children). The recent armed conflict in Tonga town, has displaced a significant number of people who had returned to their homes. Fashoda County was recently affected by floods and Armed Conflicts compounding the situation for the vulnerable communities, impacting livelihoods and subsequently exposing women and children to exploitation, abuse and negligence. Since 2022, Melut hosted thousands of IDPs from Fashoda and Manyo County. The project will provide sustainable solutions to the protection concerns of the host communities, IDPs, returnees and refugees. WV is currently implementing multisectoral projects in Greater Bahr al Gazal, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria, for both emergence response and resilience, supporting communities. World Vision proposes to reach 83,957 beneficiaries (50,374.20 Females (30,225 girls) and 33583 Males (20149 boys) including 907 girls and 605 boys with disabilities.

1. Needs

The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview identifies 15.8 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan, 8.9 millions of whom are women and girls, 2.5 million IDPs, 0.9 million returnees, and 0.9 million refugees and asylum seekers from South Sudan, Central African Republic, Ethiopia and Eritrea. According to UNHCR 155 cases (132 adults, 23 minors) were reported in Apr - Jun 2023 among the newly arriving populations. Reported cases 95% were reported by women and girls and 5% were reported by men and boys. A high number of physical assault cases were reported at 56% followed by men and woman who is physical/emotional abuse and rape at 34% and 20% respectively. 79 reported cases received psychosocial support, 53 were referred for medical services, 21 were provided with material assistance while other cases were provided with security and legal support. WV has been supporting multisectoral programs including GBV and Child protection in Greater Bahr al Ghazal, Upper Nile Counties. WV plans is Protection of Vulnerable Children by dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and survivors of the gender-based violence (Women, men, girls and boys, people with disabilities and older persons) facing life threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, injury, and severe distress have access to specialized life-saving health care, including MHPSS, child protection and GBV services. To achieve the objectives, WV will work with Women, men, girls, boys, persons with disabilities and older persons to ensured they are supported to meet their need through; a) Provision of Case management services. b) Unaccompanied and Separated children who have received family tracing and reunification services. c) Community based mental health and psychosocial support to distressed community members. d) Strengthen GBV referral pathways through conducting GBV service mapping. e) GBV awareness raising and outreaches.f) Train non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response. g) Provision of GBV services to people before they reach their intended destinations. WV programming in Renk, Melut and Twic, The GBV/Protection sector reached 500 individuals with (1) 40 members trained and 2 groups of protection committees trained to enhance case reporting and management (2) 350 individuals reached with awareness raising on child protection concerns, GBV concepts and SEA (3) 50 individuals reached with training to health care workers, social worker and community volunteers to respond to GBV and CP case management and, 60 supported with individuals counselling to help them cope with from shocks. WV will establish Child Friendly Spaces for recreational activities like games, sports, singing, drama for children whose education has been disrupted by crisis and helping them to interact. Referral, treatment and material support to SGBV survivors, Family Tracking and Reunion (FTR) services and alternatives for children on the move/ unaccompanied children will be provided in collaboration with the Ministry of Child, Gender and Social Welfare and Red cross Social. Prevention of SEA as well as GBV services to community. Health care workers, social workers and community volunteers shall be trained to respond to GBV and CP case management. Project will provide individual counselling Interpersonal Therapy Groups (IPTGs) sessions to support survivors to cope after traumatic experiences. The sessions will cover dissemination of MHPSS messages to create awareness on mental health and importance of seeking support early. GBV survivors will be given some cash for their needs and will also receive training on basic income generation activities such kitchen gardening among others.
