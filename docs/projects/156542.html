Project Summary

In consultation with the Education Directorates (EDs) in Idleb, Ar-Raqqa and Aleppo governorates, and after signing Memorandums of Understanding (MoUs) with them, 36 Temporary Leaning Spaces (TLSs) - 25 schools and 11 camps - will be established to accommodate 4,500 out-of-school (OOSC) and dropped out children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 15 years old – 10% children with disability). These children will be targeted with Self Learning Program (SLP), Accelerated Learning Program (ALP), Remedial Classes and Catch-up Classes with embedded school-based Psycho-social support (PSS), Life Skills and citizenship activities. 203 education and PSS personnel (females, males, IDPs and Host Community) will be hired and trained to run the 36 TLSs and will be provided with incentives. The overall Objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to non-formal education for crisis-affected children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 15 years old – 10% children with disability) which is in line with the education sector objectives and HRP 2019 outcome statement. The project aims at increasing safe an equitable access to non-formal education for crisis affected children in line with the education sector objective and HRP 2019 Objective-1, through Back To Learning (BTL) campaigns, Open day activity, awareness raising, SLP, ALP, Remedial Classes, Catch-up Classes, light rehab of classrooms and schools as to be disability friendly, the provision of furniture, school bags, stationary, recreation kits, Self-Learning Materials (SLM) including textbooks, transportation and supplementary materials. The project also aims at enhancing the quality of non-formal education for out-of-school/dropped out children ( girls and boys aged between 8 and 15 years old – 10% children with disability) within a protective environment in line with the education sector objective and HRP 2019 Objective-2, through training teachers and education personnel on SLP, ALP, Remedial Classes, Catch-up Classes, child-centred protective pedagogy, Education in Emergency (EiE), active learning, classroom management ...etc, and training PSS animators and supervisors on PSS theory, skills and methods, Life Skills and citizenship. Parents and Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be formed in each TLS. Teachers Learning Cycles (TLCs) will be formed in each TLS. A referral path way will be established to integrate referrals to specialist service providers. A feedback mechanism will be established as well in the TLSs. Meanwhile, the project aims at enhancing resilience through building positive and social norms and generate a sense of connectedness to teachers, peers, parents and caregivers as to cope and thrive in the face of negative events, challenges or adversity, taking into consideration that the community leaders, the local education authorities, the parents and caregivers are involved from the very beginning of the project to the end. Meanwhile, indicators will be set to measure whether the needs are met and the activities are delivered.

Needs

As per WoS Education Severity Needs Analysis, 2018, the protracted crisis in Syria, now in its 8th year, has forced an estimated 2.1 million, 1/3 of children, OOSC and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (853,292 in Idleb, 1,304,138 in Aleppo and 235,508 in Ar-Raqqa) and over 120,000 teachers are in need of education assistance, 61 per cent of whom are in acute and immediate need. Hostilities have resulted in over 1 in 3 schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes according to WoS Education Needs Assessment, 2018. Over 140,000 teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts as per EMIS data, Ministry of Education, 2017-18 academic year, which limits the quality and provision of education. OOSC face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. In some areas, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over 2/3 require specialized health services which are not available in their area according to Physical Rehabilitation Working Group, 2018. COOW interviewed KIs in 6 sub-districts in Idleb 9 sub-districts in Aleppo and 1 sub-district in Ar-Raqqa (ranging 4 and 5 as per sub-district Severity Scale HNO 2019). The results showed: On average, 45% of students were IDPs; Overcrowding is a key issue with 50% of schools. All KIs confirmed the presence of OOSC/dropped out children around the schools, because of lack of awareness, educational failure, lack of curriculum or lack of qualified teachers. The majority of KIs reported that overcrowded classrooms and the lack of textbooks were significant challenges facing teachers. Only 40% of them see that schools met the Sphere minimum standards for the provision of toilet facilities. Furthermore, no schools were found to be disability-friendly. The most commonly reported teacher training needs were Classroom management, EiE, and active learning; 83%, 73%, and 70% of KIs respectively; Teachers were facing aggressive and demotivated students, 43% and 35% of schools respectively; representing a clear need for PSS training among both teachers and education personnel. According to the above findings and the huge number of OOSC, COOW proposed project will enhance capacity building and opportunities for the future of children in line with the ‘No Lost Generation’ initiative. COOW objectives for education are designed in the line with the Education Sector Cluster strategy for Syria and HRP 2019. COOW will increase access to education to crisis affected school-aged children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 15 years old – 10% children with disability) with a specific focus on the most vulnerable, through non-formal education activities through scaling up of self-learning activities, and small-scale rehabilitation of classrooms and gender sensitive WASH facilities as to be disability-friendly as well. In addition, the project aims at improving the quality of non-formal education (NFE) for OOSC and dropped out school children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 15 years old – 10% children with disability) by providing teachers, animators and other school staff with professional development skills in NFE, active learning, PSS, management of overcrowded classrooms and procuring and distributing stationery kits, teaching and learning materials including textbooks, recreational kits and school equipment. COOW will significantly reduce the risk of increased rates of drop out, repetition, and academic failure when formal education resumes, ensure access to safe and quality education for OOSC and dropped out children, raise the awareness and build the resilience of the affected communities, and ensure to target the most vulnerable children.

Implementation of activities

COOW will implement SLP, ALP, Remedial and Catch-up Classes with embedded PSS, Life skills and citizenship activities for OOSC and dropped out children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 15 years old/10% children with disability). This can be measured by the number of OOSC/dropped out children and the percentage of children with physical disabilities enrolled in grades 1-6. Community Leaders, EDs, parents and caregivers (females, males, IDPs and Host Community) will be introduced to the project, the objectives, and the outcomes, be consulted on how to better implement the activities and target the most vulnerable. COOW jointly with EDs identified vulnerability criteria to prioritize children, including those who are separated from, or have lost one or both of their parents, parents who can't read and write, and IDP children as well as children with disabilities. This will be measured by a satisfaction survey about the selection process. The physical condition of 50 classrooms will be improved and 25 schools will be disability-friendly. Gender-sensitive WASH facilities will be constructed in the 11 IDP camps. This will be measured by the number of schools rehabilitated to be disability-friendly and the number of classrooms rehabilitated. 203 project staff will be trained, in consultation with EDs, on pedagogy methods in complex teaching environments, Classroom Management, the humanitarian principles, basic standards of CP, EiE, dealing with children with disabilities, PSS and NFE. This can be measured by the number of the project staff who attend the training.COOW will provide 72 teachers, 4 educational coordinators, 18 educational supervisors, 36 animators, 4 PSS supervisors, 18 cleaners, 36 school-bus assistants, 11 night guards and 4 logistic workers with incentives. This will be measured by the number of teachers and education personnel receiving incentives.32 BTL campaigns and open days will be conducted to raise awareness of parents, OOSC and community leaders to the importance of bringing children back to school. This can be measured by the number of campaigns conducted.All enrolled children will be subject to a placement test developed by COOW and EDs. Then, the child will be enrolled in the suitable level. Children will be provided with SLM of UNICEF, covering 4 core subjects-Arabic, math, science and English-for grades 1 to 6, school bags, stationary, recreation kits, supplementary materials and transportation. This can be measured by the number of children receiving these materials.36 PTAs will meet regularly to discuss challenges, identify solutions, and support children’s enrollment and learning.36 TLCs (Teachers are introduced to new material. They work to plan how to apply it in classroom. Then, they evaluate the success of the implementation, assess progress towards strategic goals, and identify areas in need of improvement or training).A referral system between schools/camps with existing child friendly spaces and health facilities offering child protection, PSS and health services to at risk children.A complaint mechanism with a joint committee of M&E team of COOW and members of EDs to follow the feedback and the complaints in order to address them. COOW will enhance child safeguarding procedures in schools/camps, enhance teacher’s resilience and well being through PSS activities and Duty Of Care policy. COOW will ensure building the resilience of the affected populations through providing an important context for restoring familiar roles and routines following the crisis. EDs, parents and caregivers to continue supporting the children after the closure of the project in order not lose the previous investment.All classrooms and TLSs will be handed to EDs to continue serving the children. COOW will make efforts to find resources to cover the costs needed to keep the classrooms functional. The investment in the teacher and education personnel capacity will contribute to a more skilled teacher pool.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk (PR) of supporting NFE is community acceptance or disapprove of activities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium/high. Mitigation measure (MA): disseminate information about projects, organize community events, and beneficiaries participated in the assessments feeding the design. Monitoring: Liaison with LCs and MOU signing. Resources needed: Field staff coordination with all relevant stakeholders. PR of SLP include risk of children dropping out of formal education to attend SLP centres. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. MA: identify specific criteria for accepting children into the program. Monitoring: Ensuring that only OOSC children register and attend this program throughout the duration of the project. Resources required: Field staff coordination with all relevant stakeholders. PR of providing students and teachers with teaching and learning materials & school bags: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across education sector members. Likelihood: medium. Impact. medium. MA: agreeing to standardized assistance package across sector (minimum standard). Monitoring: Strengthen monitoring of supplies packages and composition, better logistical planning. Resources needed: Field staff coordination with all relevant stakeholders, human Resources to analyze data on variations of standard packages (minimum standards). PR of constructing new classrooms; providing schools with furniture, and providing maintenance and running cost in learning centers: attacks on schools with high likelihood and high impact. MA: Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff. Monitoring: Coordination and information sharing among members and local authorities, and tracking attacks on schools and related advocacy. Resources required: Engineers, HR and time. PR of training teachers and education personnel is limited number of beneficiaries in the program, which might cause inter-community tension and pressure on implementing staff. Likelihood: medium. Impact: low. MA is setting clear-cut selection criteria and inform the community of limited resources and the selection criteria. Monitoring: Regular reports and field monitoring throughout the duration of the Project, and coordination and on-going communication between local authorities and schools. Resources required: HRfor training and mapping services as well as 3PM. PR of training teachers and education personnel on psychosocial support is increased risk of adding harm to children if knowledge is not applied appropriately. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. MA: ensure that teachers are trained properly with pre and post assessment for validation. Monitoring: Regular reports and field monitoring throughout the duration of the project. Resources required: HR for training and mapping services as well as 3PM. PR of providing teachers and education personnel with incentives: Increased risk of teachers/education personnel leaving formal schools to work in non-formal education: likelihood: high. Impact: high. MA: setting clear selection criteria for unemployed teachers/education personnel and offering salaries that are in line and comparable with public school teacher salaries. Monitoring: Sector/cluster monitoring use of common scale, coordination and on-going communication between local education authorities and schools. Resources required: HR, time and funding. PR of providing textbooks for children: Risk of not being able to access HTR areas: Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. PR2: some locations not accepting textbooks with MoE logos. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. MAs are printing books with and without logos depending on the location served and working with partners who are able to access HTR areas. Monitoring: Regular distribution list to be provided during every distribution of the materials, regular feedback by reporting and ensuring that all beneficiaries benefit from the textbooks. Resources required: HR and Funding.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Escalation of violence and fighting within civilian areas in Syria is an external risk caused by military actors with medium likelihood and medium impact. The mitigation measures are raising awareness and guidance among children and parents to deal with emergency situations, and increasing coordination and continuous communication with local authorities.2- Direct targeting of educational centers is an external risk caused by Syrian regime and pro-forces with low likelihood and high impact. The mitigation measures are raising awareness and guidance among children and parents to deal with emergency situations, develop evacuation plans and train those concerned, and providing all centers with first aid kits.3- Lack of responsiveness by parents and lack of awareness about the importance of sending their children back to school is an external risk caused by parents and caregivers with low likelihood and medium impact. The mitigation measures are implementing awareness and guidance campaigns explaining the importance of early learning, and reducing financial burdens by providing free of charge services.4- Inability to deliver the required curricula and equipment to Syria is an external risk caused by Administrative and governmental Authorities with low likelihood and medium impact. The mitigation measures are searching for suppliers inside Syria, and reliance on early purchase and storage in warehouses.5- Sudden economic changes influencing currency prices is an external risk caused by Economy and Markets with low likelihood and medium impact. The mitigation measures are rational management of available resources and adoption of early storage, and rationalize the process of converting currencies from foreign to local.6- Hindering the Project by the military authorities is an external risk caused by Military Factions with low likelihood and medium impact. The mitigation measures are increasing coordination and continuous communication with local authorities, and conducting awareness campaigns for the host community about the importance of the project and its objectives.
