Project Summary

WHO in South Sudan has intensified efforts and operations, catalyzed technical and financial resources to support improved inpatient therapeutic programs (ITP) for SAM/MC since the beginning of the humanitarian crisis in 2013. Through this project WHO proposes to reduce the health impact of the nutrition crisis through integrated health and nutrition interventions, focusing on quality live-saving services, in areas with high burden of acute malnutrition, in South Sudan. WHO will implement the following; a) Providing treatment for severe acute malnutrition and psychosocial support on MIYCN to children and care givers in collaboration with mobile medical teams(MMTs), MoH and health partners b) Conduct nutrition surveillance for timely detection and prompt action for severe acute malnutrition cases in the stabilization centers c) Conduct monitoring and supervisory visits to improve on the quality of care for children with severe acute malnutrition and verify the accuracy of data for analysis, reporting and prompt action d) Provide refresher training on case management of SAM/MC, psychosocial support and MIYCN for 50 nutrition stabilization center staff at national level e) Conduct on the job mentorship training on quality management of acute malnutrition (SAM) and IPC (WASH) in stabilization centers f) Provision of learning material and Guidelines for facility-based trainings on Inpatient Management of SAM/MC g) Raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on client’s rights and entitlement in the SC.G) Provision of Vitamin A supplementation to children aged 6-59 months. In the effort to close child survival gaps, the project directly targets 26,804.5 children with SAM including those with medical complications (10 % of the SAM/MC annual targeted caseload in the all counties.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in the country remains critical due to a combination of dry spells, poor harvest, and past conflicts. The findings of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis for January-July 2019 informs that food security is expected to worsen, with a projection that about 7.1 million people (63 percent of the total population) will face acute food insecurity or worse in the projection period of February to April 2019. This will be an increase of 6 % from the 6.33 million people (57 percent of the population) from the same period in 2018. In projection period of May to July 2019, and in the total absence of humanitarian food assistance, an estimated 7.68 million people (67% of the population) will face crisis acute food insecurity or worse, an increase of 8% from the 7.08 million people (63 percent of the population) from the same period in 2018. About 45 percent of the 51 SMART surveys conducted in 2018 reported critical nutrition situations with global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 15 per cent and above, the World Health Organization emergency threshold. IPC for Acute Malnutrition projection of January through April 2019 classified 12 counties in ‘Critical’ (IPC AMN Phase 4) and 30 in ‘Serious’ (IPC AMN Phase 3) situation is projected to worsen in the period May through July where a total of 36 counties are classified as in ‘Critical’ (IPC AMN Phase 4) and 22 counties in classified as in ‘Serious’ (IPC AMN Phase 3). The programme data reporting in the Nutrition Information System (NIS) indicate a significant increase in admissions in the period from January to April 2019 compared to the same period in 2018. New admissions of children under five with severe acute malnutrition have increased by 17% (83,018 in 2019 and 70,898 in 2018) while admissions of children under five with moderate acute malnutrition have increased by 35% (180,023 in 2019 and 133,048 in 2018). The drivers of the nutrition situation (food insecurity, sub optional child care and feeding practices, morbidity, lack of safe water and sanitation, internal displacement and conflict) continued to worsen in 2019. Food security is still likely to deteriorate based on current outcomes and past trends. The high level of food insecurity and shocks are also reflected in the severity of household coping mechanisms reported in the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) round 23 (2018/19).

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

WHO will engage the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholder workshops will be organized in the location identified for implementation of the response. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, community leaders, women, youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries. This will be the medium through which communities will be encouraged to express their concerns, views and provide regular feedback to the implementing partner in a regular structured modality. Other reasonable modalities for feedback that is useful to the communities/beneficiaries will also be considered. This feedback will form part of the project performance reporting to the Health Cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience.
