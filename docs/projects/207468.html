Project Summary

CARE proposes an integrated approach to gender-based violence, integrated sexual and reproductive health and MHPSS services that will reach 39628 people living in Camps 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 and Jaliapalong Host Community in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The project design is tailored to the findings of the AHP III gender assessment, focusing on program areas and interventions that empower women and girls to live free from violence. CARE’s current rapid gender analysis and operational expertise is adapted to meet the differing needs of men, women, adolescent boys, adolescent girls, person with disabilities, and gender diverse populations. CARE is committed to providing high-quality protection services aimed at preventing and responding to gender-based violence against women and girls, through the enhancement of GBV services.The GBV component (response, prevention, and risk mitigation) of the project will deliver services and information through WGSS, multipurpose center, and outreach activities. GBV activities will begin with the upgrading of the current WGSS to ensure added safety, privacy, and comfort. Women and girls will receive GBV case management, MHPSS services and participate in recreational activities that include resilience building and empowerment programs within the WGSS. Case workers will be trained in the full package of Case Management procedures and proper confidential documentation and adhere to the GBV minimum standard in the program. The MSP service package, as piloted and recommended by the ISCG, will be integrated into the program. This initiative aims to help individuals to manage stress and encourage them to adopt healthy coping strategies, positively impacting their health, well-being, safety, and social relationships and staff well-being. CARE will also implement ‘Girls Shine’, and EMAP an initiative for adolescent girls focusing on life skills and transforming beliefs and attitudes towards gender and violence. Protection activities will use community outreach to improve access to information and service-seeking behavior and will be delivered at the multipurpose community centers to ensure equitable access, with special focus on including people with disabilities. This project will include (MHM) services, which involve education and provision of appropriate menstrual products like sanitary towels/napkins. Additionally, project will offer specialized case management services for survivors of child sexual abuse (CCS) and will also collaborate with child protection actors to support and promote child-centered good practices. CARE will offer Women Lead in Emergencies (WLiE) approach focuses on supporting women’s empowerment by identifying barriers to their participation and creating opportunities for them to become leaders and decision-makers.
CARE plans to offer integrated GBV and SRH services. This strategy will create opportunities for survivors to access comprehensive services, where both GBV and SRH can serve as entry points. Through the multipurpose center, men and gender diverse populations will have access to GBV services including GBV case management.
Disability Inclusion is critical to this project. CARE’s activities themselves will be tailored to meet the needs of the vulnerable, including people living with disabilities. This project will ensure accessibility of service, reasonable accommodation of self- help/DSC group and assistance device of person with disability people. CARE will ensured to use the Washington Group Set of questions for disability disaggregated data collection for proper identification and needs.
CARE will maintain robust coordination with key entities like the GBV sub sector (GBV SS), GBV information management system, case management working group, Gender in Humanitarian Action (GiHA) working group, PSEA, Age and disability WG and other relevant teams to ensure harmonized, quality, and collaborative GBV services.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya crisis, predominantly cantered in the Cox’s Bazar district of Bangladesh, continues to be one of the most significant and challenging humanitarian crises in the Asia region. Currently, it involves approximately 978,000 Rohingya refugees distributed across 33 congested camps and the island of Bhasan Char. (Population fact sheet, UNHCR, Oct-2023) Most Rohingya fled to Bangladesh following violence in neighboring Myanmar in August 2017. Their presence continues to place enormous pressure on the local host communities in Cox’s Bazar, affecting 538,000 people. Over the last five years, the needs of both the Rohingya refugees and host communities have transitioned from immediate emergency relief to more long-term, protracted needs. However, the complexities and challenges in ensuring the availability, accessibility, and acceptability of the GBV services remain significant. In bboth the host communities and the Rohingya refugee camps, there is a rising concern regarding GBV and protection issues, which are further exacerbated by hazardous events. According to the recent REACH findings (2023) a minimum of 15% Rohingya refugees in the camps and 8% host communities are people with disabilities. In the refugee camps of Cox’s Bazar, GBV remains a significant concern, with one in four Rohingya women and girls having experienced it. This trend of GBV is a persistent issue in the context of this crisis. Data from the inter-agency Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBV IMS) reveals that intimate partner violence (IPV) is the most prevalent form of GBV in Cox's Bazar.In 2023, IPV incidents constituted 80% of all reported GBV cases. Notably, the majority of these incidents, about 92%, occurred in survivors' own residences. This high percentage of IPV incidents occurring in survivors’ residences is alarming. It is compounded by the fact that many women and girls in these camps have limited access to information. This, coupled with inadequate service provision and institutional barriers, often leads them to not seek help when faced with GBV or other forms of abuse. Research finding highlights that, “while all adolescent Rohingya girls are marginalized from fulfilling their educational and employment trajectories, married girls face additional GBV risks, particularly in the form of IPV. Strong evidence suggesting poorer mental health outcomes and increased distress in the lives of married girls underlines the importance of targeted interventions”. It suggests that involving these girls in community activities and vocational training, tailored to align with local market needs, can be a powerful means of empowerment. By providing married girls with opportunities for engagement and skill development, they can gain a sense of agency and potential pathways towards economic independence, which are crucial factors in improving their overall well-being and mental health. According to the 2022 GESI study, revealed a concerning finding regarding the Rohingya community: people with disabilities, sex workers, and member of the LGBTQ community face significant barriers in accessing gender-based violence related services. Men have reported a lack of PSS services for their demographic (CARE’s Rapid Gender analysis, 2022). Additionally, persons with disabilities and gender diverse populations are still marginalized. Restricted access to most services and stigmatization leaves gender diverse population more open to abuse and exploitation (FGD with transgender group). Limited-service provisions combined with lack of diversity in these services, contributed to the underutilization of support by GBV survivors, further normalizing gender-based violence. However, empowerment and economic development programs for women and girls, excluding men, have led to backlash, creating tensions and violence. This is particularly acute in a context where men are unable to fulfil their traditional roles as breadwinners.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

CARE will follow a systematic and harmonized approach that will be needed in to ensure that no one is left behind in service provision. Currently, CARE is following JRP rationalization process and principle to meet the humanitarian needs with standard package of services to all through seven WGSS in the Rohingya refugee camps (11,12,14,15,16) and in the host community of Jaliapalong, implemented by the local partner Mukti Cox's Bazar, in accordance with the 2023 JRP rationalization. All WGSS are providing one to one GBV case management support including prevention approach with prioritizing GBV minimum standards and service package. In line with the GBV SS's rationalization strategy, CARE plans to transform two WGSS in camp 16 into a multipurpose center to broaden the access of men and the LGBTIQ population to GBV services. Furthermore, in collaboration with the SRH team, CARE will provide selected SRH services through its WGSS and will seek funding for integrating SRH services into these facilities. CARE will offer integrated service with others sectoral needs and avoid duplication of services. In relation to MHM/dignity kits, CARE will coordinate with the WASH team to ensure availability for GBV survivors. As Rationalization and standardization practices CARE will update regular service activity through 5W in service location. CARE will ensure implementing partner is the part of the current JRP process and will provide support and assistance to relevant approvals by the Government and will engage and enable to active members of relevant coordination groups. A partnership approach will enable this, engaging partners through regular planning, monitoring, and review meetings to ensure that the voices of local partners are reflected in the project design and partners are included and engaged in decisions across all areas of programming. For predictability and continuity, CARE has secured funding until December 2024 for the six WGSS operated under the local partner.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project is tailored to the specific protection needs of the most vulnerable among the Rohingya refugees and the host community. Throughout the project cycle, women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, and the LGBTIQ population will be consulted through baseline and endline assessments, reguler (FGD), and community sessions. Additionally, CARE will conduct capacity building training for (PERU) members and protection partners. Regular protection/GBV risk assessments will be conducted to identify emerging protection concerns, and the referral pathway will be updated accordingly. The design of WGSS and the multipurpose center follows a survivor-centric approach. Regular feedback and complaints will be collected, with necessary actions taken to ensure safe and dignified service delivery. Men, women, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, and the LGBTIQ will participate in various awareness and capacity-building sessions. In coordination with the DRR, livelihood, WASH, and SRH teams, participants will be linked to broader services and livelihood opportunities. WLiE and EMAP will be utilized to engage men and women, promoting active participation and leadership. At the GBV sub-sector level, CARE, along with other agencies, will continue to work with camp authorities and actors to ensure adherence to GBV referral pathway procedures and standards. Community engagement and service information dissemination activities will be organized in spaces easily accessible to women, proactively creating opportunities for them to directly receive information and contribute to needs assessments. Acknowledging the risks faced by adolescent girls face due to increased child marriage and trafficking, groups for adolescent girls and young mothers will be convened to provide SRH and family planning information, skill building training, and emotional support. The ‘Do No Harm’ principle will be applied throughout the project cycle.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Preliminary results from a comprehensive survey of camps and host communities by REACH show that a minimum of 15% of people in the Rohingya camps and 8% host communities are people with disabilities with 42% of households having at least one or more persons with a disability. This project places a strong emphasis on ensuring that persons with disabilities and the gender diverse population are better represented, more actively involved, and have meaningful and equal access to services in GBV programming. Self-engagement of persons with disabilities and LQBTIQ population has been limited as participants of humanitarian services. However, it is necessary to consult with these groups to ensure their perspectives on GBV cases are heard and included in finding appropriate solutions. The WGSS will be upgraded to ensure accessibility for persons with disabilities and a WGSS will be transformed to multipurpose center to improve accessibility of men and the LGBTIQ community. This approach aims to improve equal opportunities and ensure adequate participation in decision-making processes for accessible and inclusive services to targeting 10 to 59 age with all diverse groups, thereby fostering a more inclusive and equitable environment for all participants. People with different age groups of will be part of self-help groups, Community outreach groups will be formed with person with disability that to build the SHG or promote them in socially connection to others, strong capacity they should in build among themselves raise their strong voice for support and networking (OPD). However, this project will focus more on engagement with Camp actors and relevant organization to make them capable for social support, awareness raising and advocacy. CARE will emphasis regular monitoring, need assessment considering of protection aspects, identified the barriers of needs and assistive device support, capacity building, opportunity for equal participation in sectoral approaches.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) in all interventions, CARE Bangladesh has established and incorporated a robust Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) in all of its humanitarian projects in Cox’s Bazar. This system consists of various solicited and unsolicited channels to receive information, recommendations, feedback, opinions, questions, complaints, or requests from participants, stakeholders, staff, volunteers, government agencies, other organizations. CARE has ensured that all affected community, regardless of sex, age, disability, race, caste, or color, has equal access to this safe and responsive CFRM approach. The CFRM team responds to almost all problems received at the earliest possible time based on feedback type and priority. The Accountability Focal person regularly shares reports with various donors and projects, following schedules like weekly, monthly, or quarterly intervals. CARE has its own Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) for CFRM and utilizes different platforms and tools for data collection, including KOBO, ODK, Activity Info, CFP, and MODA.This project will continue using feedback hubs, household visits, suggestion boxes, and hotline numbers to collect community complaints and feedback. The project will review and update the (SOP) for CFRM that includes timelines for resolution and escalation procedures. CARE will ensure to train staff, community-based volunteers (CBVs), and partner NGOs on CFRM procedures and inform them of the importance of timely and respectful feedback resolution. Regular reviews and analyses of CFRM data will be conducted to identify trends and areas for improvement, including enhancing accessibility for people with disabilities. Project participants will have access to the phone numbers, and focal points will be trained in receiving feedback over the phone. MEAL team will conduct PDM following appropriate methodology and provide feedback.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

CARE’s Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy aligns with international rules, regulations, guidelines, standards, and policies, demonstrating a strong commitment to addressing safeguarding issues. CARE maintains a zero-tolerance stance towards sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse, adheres to Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action, and the Inter-Agency Guidelines for Case Management. All CARE and partner staff, including volunteers, receive orientation on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH) and Child Safeguarding policies. Training on PSEA is mandatory for all newly joined staff, and CARE routinely offers refresher courses to ensure ongoing awareness and compliance. All staff are required to sign the PSEAH and Child Protection policy and adhere to its principles.PSEA messages will be mainstreamed throughout the project. This project will uphold PSEAH and child safeguarding policies by being sensitive to the local culture, gender, and age power dynamics. The responsibility of staff to report abuses will be emphasized strongly, ensuring adherence to the safeguarding policy in all activities throughout the project implementation. CARE is a regular and active member of the PSEA networking group and has a dedicated safeguarding focal for the Bangladesh program. In line with CARE’s localization and partnership approach in program implementation, and as part of agreements with partners, there will be a strong focus on addressing child protection-related risks. This includes reviewing and strengthening the organizational policies of partners regarding child protection.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project is committed to supporting search and rescue/DRR volunteers, GBV/Protection workers in the camps, as well as individuals and communities affected by sudden hazardous events and disasters, with a focus on their mental health and well-being. Recognizing the diverse mental health and psychosocial impacts of emergencies and climate change, the project acknowledges that individuals may experience a range of responses, from transient and minor stress reactions to significant distress and psychosocial impairment. Social stigma, often associated with these experiences, can exacerbate stress, thus awareness-raising sessions will include messages related to DRR and climate change. A key aspect of the project involves capacity building of CARE and partner staff, focusing on minimum first aid requirements and waste management in coordination with the WASH and DRR teams. Additionally, in alignment with the Government’s campaign to reduce plastic bag usage, CARE will advocate for the use of cloth bags in this project interventions.
