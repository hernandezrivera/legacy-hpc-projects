Project Summary

In order to continue to address the burden of the leishmaniasis epidemic across northern
Syria, The MENTOR Initiative will continue to partner with stakeholders and communities
to ensure that the most vulnerable receive:
1. Prevention of leishmaniasis transmission within their homes. MENTOR will provide
protection to 658,260 households through an indoor residual spraying campaign (IRS)
northern Syria of Idleb, Aleppo, Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates.
2. Information about leishmaniasis and its transmission. MENTOR will run combined
information, education and behavioral change campaign (IEC/BCC) to ensure
households are empowered to prevent leishmaniasis transmission within their
household. This will be done by various media and delivery strategies, to maximize
uptake and correct utilization of leishmaniasis prevention and case management
services.

4 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in Syria has resulted in poor living and sanitary conditions, impaired nutrition, economic hardship and the proliferation of infectious diseases, of which leishmaniasis is one of the most prominent ones. Made worse by two years of COVID-19, local authorities/governments across North-East (NES) and North-West Syria (NWS) are still struggling to mount an effective response. According to the 2021 W52 EWARN Epidemiological Bulletin, there were 58,745 and 34,900 cases diagnosed in NES and NWS, respectively, each of which needs on average more than eight consultations. Of those 93,645 cases, 79,107 (84.4%) were diagnosed and treated in MENTOR-supported health facilities and mobile clinics, accumulating 671,852 consultations. An increase in Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL), a fatal disease if left untreated, is particularly concerning, with 87 cases diagnosed by MENTOR in 2021, an increase of 64% when compared to 2020. MENTOR’s project directly addresses this need by implementing prevention intervention in Idleb, Aleppo, Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor (DeZ) governorates. MENTOR bases needs assessment on a network of MENTOR-supported HFs and MCs across NWS and NES, which collect and send comprehensive data to the MENTOR M&E department every week. All activity locations and programs are based on the most recent incidence and population movements and after taking into consideration the needs laid out in the 2021 Syria HRP and the presence of partners.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) campaign will be strategically conducted prior to the disease transmission season, which typically lasts from March to October, with peak sandfly numbers in September. As IRS lasts 6-9 months, depending on the substrate sprayed, correct timing ensures maximum benefit. The residents perceive the benefit of IRS because of the reduction of sand flies as well as more conspicuous nuisance pests such as cockroaches, bed bugs, flies and mosquitoes, which is also an added health benefit. MENTOR will use ICON & FICAM as the insecticides in 2022. A minimum coverage of 85% of households will be achieved following WHO guidelines to ensure community level protection. Community resistance to IRS in a country where suspicion of chemical reagents is high can understandably be a problem. MENTOR has worked to overcome this issue through effective awareness campaigns, the employment of local workers, the recruitment of female workers to facilitate access to homes when the male head of household is absent, and IEC/BCC materials accompanied by verbal explanations. The main resilience-building component is the capacity building within the communities. People are chosen directly from the community to be part of the workforce and trained in theory and practice. The knowledge and skills can be used in future IRS campaigns, which hopefully will emerge as part of national authorities carrying out similar activities and make them more cost- and time-efficient.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The possible risks for sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) are1. SEA as an exchange for treatment2. SEA as an exchange for promises of hiring to work at the NGO3. SEA as an exchange for money All MENTOR staff sign a Code of Conduct, Safeguarding, Sexual Harassment and Bullying, Whistleblowing and Child Protection policy. They outline the principles under which MENTOR operates, and incorporates the Inter-Agency Standing Committee principles on Prevention of Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA). MENTOR enrols all staff on PSEA training and emphasizes refreshing the PSEA training regularly. Through the Complaint Response Mechanism, MENTOR makes available channels to facilitate feedback and complaints via a WhatsApp hotline that is advertised on IEC/BCC materials, complaint boxes, and a feedback tool employed during monitoring activities. MENTOR notifies all communities in which we work that our staff operate under a strict CoC, the standard of behaviour that should be expected from our staff, that all treatment is free of charge and the confidential reporting mechanisms available to communities and beneficiaries. MENTOR staff are trained to identify sexual violence and report these immediately through their line management to the Country Director for (CD) for further action. In case of any PSEA reporting, MENTOR applies a strict follow-up method to address and solve the issue. Recruitment is made in coordination with local health authorities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Volatile security: Before IRS implementation, MENTOR conducts a security risk assessment (SRA) to avoid any risks to beneficiaries or staff and regularly updates the SRA in the operational area. If the security situation interferes with MENTOR direct access, MENTOR switches to a remote management strategy. Necessary approvals for services are not secured: MENTOR maintains high coordination levels with the authorities to obtain/renew the permission of implementing the activities. MENTOR signed various MoUs with local authorities across northern Syria. Fraud, graft and corruption occur at any level i.e. support offices or field: MENTOR asks all staff sign policies and refer to manuals such as Logistics Manual, HR Manual, Financial Manual and the Field Operational Manual where fraud and corruption are explained in detail. MENTOR regularly checks the financial, HR and logistic files from the field to headquarter level.The IDP/Refugee and host communities do not support the projects: MENTOR ensures accountability to beneficiaries through participation in design and implementation of activities, communication on project activities and objectives, transparency efforts, feedback and community response mechanisms.Procurement and supply chains interrupted: In this case MENTOR seek donations from partners and manipulate essential stocks from other locations and explore alternative transfer mechanisms. All supplies are always procured well in advance of the project start.
