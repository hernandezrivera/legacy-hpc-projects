Project Summary

NCA plans a project to rehabilitate and improve of the water supply networks in nine towns/villages located in Telkaif district, in the north-west of Ninewa governorate, about 30 kilometres from Mosul namely Telkaif, Tallskuf, Rashidiyah, Qawsiat, Bawizah, Sada, Tall Yabis, Najmok and Derke . Telkaif district is part of the so-called Nineveh plain, historically the homeland of most of Iraq’s minority groups, such as Christians (Assyrians and Chaldeans), Yezidis, Turkmen, Sunni Arabs and Shabaks (Shia Kurds) and is known for many archaeological sites e.g. Lalesh, the holy site for Yazidis.
NCA aims to ensure sustainable access to adequate and safe drinking water, as well as enabling people to protect their health and prevent spread of waterborne diseases through hygiene promotion interventions, especially targeting at-risk groups like returnees. In this sense, the project is compatible with the priorities of the UN and of the Government of Iraq. Specifically, this project aims to ensure sustainable supply of drinking water to 9 towns/villages in Telkaif district, through the rehabilitation and improvement of sustainable and permanent water distribution networks. With much of the water distribution systems in disrepair in these places, the connection with Telkaif will not serve any benefit to local communities unless a complete overhaul of existing network is undertaken in the target areas.

Needs

According to UNOCHA , there are estimated 8.7 million people requiring urgent humanitarian assistance, despite transitioning out of the “first emergency phase”. Around 80% are concentrated in three governorates: Ninewa, Kirkuk and Anbar. Ninewa remains the epicentre of the crisis, with 46% of the people in need living in this governorate. OCHA’s prognosis shows the need to deliver urgent life-saving WASH assistance to 2 million returnees and host communities in North Iraq. Ensuing water depletion and deteriorating economic situation can add fuel to the fire by igniting further conflict along religious or ethnic fault lines. Telkaif, as chosen area for humanitarian WASH intervention, is already experiencing tension over water resources, exacerbating the precarious cohabitation of multi-ethnic/multi-religious groups, already undermined by ISIL’s recent brutal occupation. In addition, scarcity of water and lack of access, obliges the local populations to either rely on water trucking with increased costs or on unreliable water sources, increasing risk of waterborne diseases. This situation poses a risk to human health, especially of babies, children, elderly people and returnees that are struggling to make ends meet.NCA conducted a quick assessment of nine locations in TelKaif District, Ninewa namely Telkaif, Telskuf, Rashidiyah, Qawsiat, Bawizah, Sada, Tall Yabis, Najmok, Derke. According to the findings of the assessment, a large majority of inhabitants of these villages were relying of unsafe water sources with limited capacity of storage and transport linked issues related to water scarcity as well. Issues related to quality and quantity of water are potential factors for public health risks and disease outbreaks Assessment also revealed access to insufficient quantity of water for drinking, cooking, domestic and hygiene purposes. (less than 15 liters of water per person per day - SPHERE). It was also noted that some of the residents also buy water from the vendors. Furthermore, treating water at HH level especially that is collected from open sources and surface water with risks of biological contamination is not treated at HH level. Issues related proper handling of water at HH level and community level was also found which can lead to both contamination of water and its wastage.

Activities

The overall objective of the project is "To ensure access to adequate safe drinking water and promote water conservation; and to reduce the risk of waterborne diseases." Following activities will be carried out to achieve the aforementioned objective;1. Carry out a Pre-KAP survey and establish baseline of the nine villages selected.2. Rehabilitation/Up gradation of identified water systems/networks and facilities in 9 identified villages 3. Strengthening of operations and maintenance (O&M) mechanisms of the rehabilitated infrastructure in close coordination with communities and government departments4. Community awareness campaigns on water safety and conservation5. Hygiene promotion targeting at personal, domestic and environmental hygiene through specific approaches of Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) and Child Hygiene And Sanitation Training (CHAST) I project will develop a comprehensive strategy to cater the needs of all gender and age groups through participation and continuous feedback.

Protection Risk Analysis

The beneficiaries may be exposed to risk of GBV and sexual abuse due to limited access to water at present. Women and children traveling long distances to fetch water may be exposed to such risks. Furthermore, lack of clean drinking water in terms of quality and quantity can also be a major factor for conflicts between the community to have control of resources and use it for benefit. Specific challenges could be posed for female headed households and disabled members of the communities that are more vulnerable and have difficulties access unless specific arrangements are made.

Mitigation Measures

The project is designed to protect people basic right to safe water and improved health. The activities will ensure beneficiaries dignity and will include consensus and involvement of the targeted beneficiaries at all stages of the project. Similarly, project will equally benefit girls, boys, women and men in terms of access to safe drinking water. The intervention will also reduce fetching of water from far-flung areas by women, adolescent girls and young children which are the major contribution in GBV and protection issues. This will result in curtailing of GBV and protection issues in the areas of return. The project will also address issues of vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly, female headed households and disabled through involving community groups and elders so suitable arrangement could be made to ensure equitable access to all project beneficiaries. The project will also develop strategies to ensure inclusion of community including all vulnerable groups to ensure feedback through regular consultations and improving community participation from design to completion of the project.

How is your project accountable to affected population

In order to ensure accountability to the target group, the project will be implemented with close coordination and participation of the target group/benefiaries. NCA is a HAP certified organisations and prioritizes principles of accountability and transparency. This delegates ownerships in the community and they feel responsible for the outcomes of the project which will help NCA in the successful completion of the project. NCA’s experience shows that participation of the community leads toward sustainability and effectiveness. For this very purpose, the project will be implemented with full participation of the affected population and the beneficiaries. Community mobilization will be an integral part of the project for participation of the local communities (affected population and the beneficiaries) which is essential for success and sustainability of the project. Project will involve community structures of the respective villages for their role and involvement of the project planning, implementation and monitoring. All information will be shared with the affected population and the beneficiaries at large. The project will involve community and recruit workers from the community for the cash for work component that directly contributes to improving access to safe drinking water. Using Common Humanitarian Standards and HAP as benchmark, NCA also has a Complaint Response Mechanism in place that is strictly followed as an integral part of the program. As part of the SOPs on CRM, the project beneficiaries (men, women, boys, girls, PWD, elderly persons) will be oriented on the complaint mechanism and on their right to share their feedback regarding the quality of the project and their satisfaction. Each complaint received by the NCA team will be recorded and an acknowledgement will be given to the complainant with a unique complain number. Once the complaint is registered, it will be classified based on high-low importance level and will be assigned response time accordingly between 1 week and 24 hours. Different channels will be identified for based on the sensitivity of the complaint so proper action could be taken. If the complainant is not satisfied with the outcome, a appeal process will also be introduced that will go directly to the management of NCA to respond to. Further Pana flexes and Banners will be displayed at the main sites in the field having information about the contacts of complaint focal person from NCAs Management. NCA will also ensure that beneficiaries must know about the project activities as well as the selection criteria. Complaint Boxes (locked) will be installed in each targeted areas and hygiene sessions sites and the community to lodge their complaint (if any). The complaints log and action taken will be shared with the donor team accordingly. All complaints will be recorded with action taken and feedback provided to the complainant. The complaints could be lodged either through email, in writing, telephone or in person. NCA will ensure the information sharing with all relevant stakeholders including government, other CSOs/NGOs working in that areas and the beneficiaries to avoid any confusion, conflict and duplication/overlapping and ensure transparency through meetings, data, plan and reports sharing.
