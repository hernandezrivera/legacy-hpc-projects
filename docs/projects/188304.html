Project Summary

World Vision (WV) is proposing a livelihoods-focused intervention in response to the needs of both IDPs and members of host communities in Northern Aleppo. WV want to increase access of affected communities in Azaz, Suran and Mare’ sub-districts to decent jobs and (self-) employment opportunities through market-driven TVET, OTJ placements and start-up support. WV will leverage the learning acquired from their previous and currently implemented projects in the A’zaz sub-district. WV will extend and replicate the services to fill the gaps and reach the underserved communities in A’zaz, Suran and Mare’ sub-districts. Specifically, as per the labor market assessment and in alignment with the integrated ERL priorities. WV will ensure safety, accessibility, and equal access of the most vulnerable groups, especially young women and persons with disabilities by providing integrated protection support and abiding by “Twin-Track- Approach” principles for mainstreaming inclusiveness.
The discussion on the incentive payed for the benefices and the best modality to deliver the incentive and start-up kits support will be discussed at area level with the humanitarian actors with ensuring the involvement of the local community.
Within this operational environment, and when cash modality identified as the best modality to deliver the incentive/start-up kits support. WV identifies the need of using the hardest currency available in our distributions so that it preserves its purchasing power for beneficiaries. However, following area-based programming, a joint decision between the humanitarian actors on currency to be made based on cost-effectiveness and cost-efficiency and involving the local community at the area level in developing the targeting/prioritization to avoid local tension.
Where cash applicable, WV will rely on its digital platform Last Mile Mobile Solution (LMMS) and LMMS E-cards to ensure accurate management of beneficiaries data and will contract a trusted financial service provider (hawala) for the hard-cash provision in distribution points accessible and safe for both recipients and staff.
WV MEAL department will monitor local markets pre and post-project implementation to ensure the functionality of local markets, proximity to beneficiaries, capacity to absorb increased demand for goods and services, and potential impact on markets and non-cash recipients accessing the same markets. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be in place and will allow WV to promptly address recipient’s feedback.

4 - Needs

The conflict inside Syria has continued for over a decade, forcing more than 6.6 million people into internal displacement (HNAP, 2021), and a further 5.6 million to flee the country and seek refuge abroad (UNHCR, 2021). High levels of civilian casualties, restricted humanitarian access and limited access to basic services has resulted in a dire humanitarian crisis across the country (UNOCHA, 2021). The livelihoods of Syrians have been affected by the ongoing Syrian crisis due to severe economic shocks and its knock-on effects like loss of assets, income, land, and productive livestock. Economic activity declined by more than half compared to 2011 - the result of fragmentation, trade autarky and the depreciation of the Syrian Pound (Chokr, 2021). This has led to an increase in the number of food insecure people and exacerbated living conditions for families and individuals with pre-existing humanitarian needs. Based on the Livelihoods Analysis Report, jobs were reported as increasingly scarce and wages insufficient. Only 42% of individuals aged 18 and above worked over the past 3 months with 91% of in-work individuals belonging to extremely poor households (HHs). Reasons for gaps in employment opportunities vary between men and women. However, chronic gaps in employment availability, market-demand skills, lack of access to finance and experience are identified as some of the primary reasons keeping both genders out of work.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Activity 2.1: WV will target households willing to start or expand their MSE and support them through providing specialized business development and management training. At the end of the training, the beneficiaries will be entitled to receive start up fund/kit. WV will dedicate business expert coaches to provide tailored and customized business support for the beneficiaries to overcome business challenges up to the end of the cycle.2- Activity 2.2: WV will leverage the learning acquired from the previous implemented projects in the A’zaz sub-district. The intervention under this activity aims to fill the gaps and reach the underserved communities in A’zaz, Suran and Mare’ sub-districts. The development of the VT training materials will lean on a deep market assessment to insure market oriented technical training provided. Further, robust, regular market monitoring will be in place to assess price, availability, and market system capacity to absorb increased demands of goods, and programming will be adjusted accordingly. 3- Activity 2.3: WV will support short-term work opportunities through supporting the on the job placement scheme (internship). Beneficiaries selected to the OTJ scheme will be linked to one privet sector employer based on their preference and employer requirements. The beneficiaries will receive on the job business couching services and wage payment equal to the average wage in the targeted community.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WV adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA. And it is committed responsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men – are treated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WV field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which is led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WV field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle. Furthermore, WV has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WV uses a standard online platform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks of market distortion and market volatility with the creation of start-up businesses not adapted to market needs and with the closure of key markets and borders leading to a fluctuation of commodities/services prices and affecting general market. As mitigation to the market distortion, a market assessment will be conducted to understand market behavior and trends to align with local market needs. As for market volatility, robust, regular market monitoring will be in place to assess price and availability, and programming will be adjusted. In addition, an area-based coordination with other aid actors and community members of targeted areas will take place to engage them in the intervention design and avoid duplication in the services. The risks of interference of authorities and of aid diversion are monitored for these targeted areas. In addition to WV adopting a participatory approach through its area-based programming, allowing community to be involved in developing the targeting/prioritization to avoid local tension and authorities are informed about the intervention for transparency to reduce interference. As for the risk of aid diversion, WV due diligence include a screening on project stakeholders and MEAL mechanisms for the traceability and reporting on the provided assistance. Furthermore, adequate coordination is maintained with authorities and formalized through agreements to also reduce the risk of interference and address it efficiently if it occurs.
