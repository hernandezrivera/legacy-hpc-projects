Project Summary

YOPCODI intends to provide access and countinuity to protective basic education for 5000 Children comprising of (3000-60% girls and 2000 -40% boys) aged 5-17) including those living with disabilities in schools, camps, and host communities affected by emergencies as a result of protracted conflict in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States - Northeast, Nigeria.The adult target will be 360 (60% women and 40% men) teachers who will be trained on improved teaching and learning approaches including PSS & conflict sensitive education. Also, 60 SBMC members (22 men, 22 women, 3 boys & 3 girls) will be trained on safe schools, conflict-sensitive education and support to learners. A target of 5% of the overall individuals to be reached will be persons living with a disability. 500 out of the total learner’s target will be Out of School Children (OOSCs) who will be identified in consultation with the community people and enrolled on the age-appropriate Accelerated Education Programme (AEP). The 5000 learners targeted will be supported with age and class-appropriate learning materials; the 300 teachers targeted will be supported with class-appropriate teaching materials, while the 500 out-of-school children will be supported with Back-to-school enrolment kits.
Moreso, caregivers of targeted learners and OOSCs will be reached with positive parenting sessions for
those who are able to come to the schools for the session while those who are unable to attend the
sessions will be visited by the CVTs' (door-2-door). Targeted adolescent girls in school will be
reached with Menstrual hygiene management life-skills sessions after which Menstrual Hygiene
Management Kits will be distributed. The Pads in the kit will be reusable sanitary pads.
YOPCODI will work with child protection colleagues to identify OOSCs using the
community-based PSS as an entry point and in collaboration with the Community Based Child
Protection Committees (CBCPCs) and Local Education Authorities (LEAs) in Bama, Konduga,
Bursari, Yunusari, and Gujba LGAs to identify and profile Out of School Children (OOSCs) for enrolment during the termly back-to-school campaigns.

1 - Needs

The over decade-long crises in North East Nigeria had rendered about 4.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. The crisis, which is fundamentally a protection of civilians’ crisis, has largely been triggered by an ongoing regionalized armed conflict, characterized by massive and widespread abuse against civilians including killings, rape and other sexual violence, abduction, child recruitment, burning of homes, pillaging, forced displacement, arbitrary detention, and the use of explosive hazards, including in deliberate attacks on civilian targets.The impact of the crisis on the education system has also been severe, leaving generations of children without opportunities to learn and even more vulnerable. Since the conflict erupted in 2009, 2,295 teachers have been killed leading about 55 percent shortage of teachers in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, 1400 schools damaged or destroyed and more than 802 schools forced to close. As a result, an estimated 630,000 children (327,600 girls) have lost access to learning while 42 percent of children in camps do not attend school. 60 percent of girls of primary school age are out of school in Borno State - the highest percentage in the country(1) .Of those who do attend, 72 per cent are unable to read upon completion of primary six, while Borno State has the lowest literacy rates at only 35 percent of female and 46 per cent of male adolescents. The Nigeria Education in Emergencies Working Group (EiEWG) has over the years worked tirelessly to ensure school age conflict affected children have continued equal access to quality basic education through the provision of Education in Emergencies across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The Project will mainstream child protection and GBV prevention activities into all thematic activities, leveraging from the dedicated child protection component. Safeguarding and protection risk assessments will be developed jointly by consortium members and regularly reviewed and updated, in order to provide a best possible protective environment within the communities of implementation. Suchassessments will, for instance, consider the varied protection concerns of women, men, boys, girls, elderly people, people with disabilities, survivors of protection breaches or GBV, former CAAG, and other diverse or intersectional populations with additional protection needs.Community Volunteer Teachers (CVT) and community Based Protection Committees (CBCPCs) will be included within implementation structures, in order to build linkages between the community protection mechanisms and non-formal education.All project partners hold comprehensive and strictly adhered organisational policies on child safeguarding, GBV, and other codes of conduct which are signed and mandated for all staff and vendors. GBV risk reduction activities will be carried out in all project spaces, with referral mapping activities also extending to mapping of GBV referral pathways. This will include both clinical and legal services, and YOPCODI will leverage its network of NGO and government partners with specialization in GBV response. Community sensitisation will explain that AAP and CRM utilities are also available to community members who wish to report concerns of safeguarding and GBV not directly related to the project implementation. YOPCODI's MHPSS specialist will also be available to provide crisis psychosocial first aid and support to survivors of GBV in addition to external referral. Beneficiary selection will be based on clear, standardised, and targeted vulnerability assessments and criteria that ensure selection based only on need and household vulnerability. Communities will be sensitised on the need and use of these selection tools and will be supported to develop contextually appropriate criteria, with support of consortium partners. IDP and host community members will be provided with support in order to promote social cohesion, while reintegration activities will prioritise one CAAG child to every two non-CAAG children in order to prevent association of "rewards" for former CAAG, in line with international best practice.Finally, the project will ensure to mainstream the inclusion and participation of children and adults with disabilities as far as possible throughout the project. The additional needs of CwD will be addressed through the development of targeted sections to the positive parenting manual, and activities involving caregiver and child peer groups will ensure to include groups focused on CwD. Wherever possibleYOPCODI will seek to employ staff with visible disabilities at the community level, for instance as CFS or life skill sessionanimators/facilitators, in order to build visibility and acceptance of disability within communities.YOPCODI will continue to use the approved Harmonized Child Protection Information Management System (HCPIMS++) and Gender Based Violence InformationManagement System (GBVIMS+) for case management to protect the data, privacy and security of at-risk women children. SC has receivedtraining on CPIMS+ via UNICEF and will step down this training togovernment partners and field staff as necessary. More generally, YOPCODI haspolicies in place to safeguard the confidentiality of beneficiaries and protect them from potential protection risks such as discrimination orstigma.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AAP and CRM design will leverage heavily on lessons learned in the previous NHF consortium allocation, and center participation of affected people in the development, implementation, and review of contextually appropriate and adaptive accountability mechanisms.Diverse community members and leaders including women, men, girls, boys, and people with disabilities will be engaged from start-up to close-out through comprehensive sensitisation activities that ensure their understanding and empowerment of project activities, and will be supported to propose mechanisms that reflect their own needs. For instance, communities with strong mobile network signal and access to mobile phones may prefer to primarily use toll-free lines to register complaints; other communities with poor coverage might prefer either written or pictorial IEC and safeguarding materials in different local languages including Hausa, Kanuri, and Fulfulde. YOPCOD will use contextualised IEC developed in previous consortium projects, with support of Translators Without Borders. For child centered activities partners will mainstream child-focused mechanisms, including focus group discussions and key informant interviews, that facilitate childrens' involvement in project design and activity adjustment to best suit their diverse needs on a community-by-community basis. Regular community meetings will refresh understanding and collect feedback on the feedback mechanisms.A focus on strengthening of community mechanisms will also contribute to overall accountability and participation of community members.Protection structures such as CBCPC's will receive additional sensitization on project design and activities, with bi-weekly meetings between the project team and these structures to gather feedback provided by the community.
