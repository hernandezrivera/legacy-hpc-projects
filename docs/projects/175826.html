Project Summary

The food security and livelihood sub-component of the program is aimed at improving the overall food security and livelihoods of 12,000 affected households including 6,200 IDPs. This is achieved through layering and sequencing of interventions across the two interlinked and mutually supportive sectors, i.e. agriculture/food security and economic recovery and market strengthening.

1 - Needs

South Sudan and its people continue to reel from the impacts of civil war, intercommunal violence and limited development investment. A large section of its population remains displaced in internally displaced person (IDP) camps and more than two million live outside of the country. Many areas are still suffering from intra and inter-communal violence enabled by small-arms proliferation and weak rule of law. This is often driven by resource scarcity in areas that have seen years of severe food insecurity. Across South Sudan, 5.29 million people (45.2% of the population) are estimated to have faced crisis (Integrated Phase Classification [IPC] Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Another 1.11 million people have faced emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. About 40,000 people were estimated to be in catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) classification, Aweil South is one of areas targeted by this project. This situation is projected to get worse if no urgent measures are taken to contain it. The IPC projections indicate that the situation could deteriorate with over 6.48 million people (55% of the total population) facing severe acute food insecurity (Phase 3+) and in need of assistance, although this is 5% lower than what was projected for 2019. Meanwhile, at least 1.3 million children under five years old (U5) are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and require treatment in 2020. In addition, the January 2020 IPC analysis reports that at least 292,300 severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases, 1,008,700 moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases and 352,000 acutely malnourished pregnant or lactating women (PLWs) will be in urgent need of treatment in 2020. Twenty-eight counties across South Sudan, including Northern Bahr El Ghazal and other are classified as facing a serious global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of between 10 – 14.9%. Nonetheless, an assessment conducted, does offer a ray of hope. This assessment indicated that, on average, 57% of the households (HHs) benefiting from the current Food security project have an acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS), and almost half are borderline. However, 50% of the HHs interviewed said they never had enough food to meet their nutrition needs during the year, meaning almost all these HHs will suffer from food shortages during the lean months of April – August. therefore unable to access land to farm. In 2019, South Sudan received an unusually heavy rainfall and flooding which affected an estimated 908,000 people. To date, 620,000 people are still in need of recovery support to help them rebuild their livelihoods. Coinciding with the harvest season, over 73 metric tons (MT) of cereals were lost and 42 nutrition centers across the country were suspended. Besides, flooding triggered population movement and displacement in different parts of South Sudan. With homes and shelters destroyed by floods, men, women, and children were forced into unsafe living conditions either in makeshift shelters or in communal shelters. The risk of waterborne and communicable diseases also increased, forcing many to seek medical care in already stressed health centers. Meanwhile, the areas affected by flooding, such as Aweil North and Aweil West remained completely cut off for more than three months. Priority needs in these areas include food and health care support to the most affected HHs, livelihood assistance in the form of seeds and tools, and restored WASH services. The protracted economic crisis, characterized by high food prices and continued currency depreciation, continues to reduce the purchasing power of the affected HHs who are reliant on market purchases for their food and other basic needs. Ongoing economic crisis, lack of agricultural produce to sell, depleted HH assets and limited income-generation opportunities leave many vulnerable

2 - Project activities

The agriculture/food security’s sector’s aim is to improve HH agricultural production through promotion of improved agricultural production techniques (including pests and disease management and adoption of improved post-harvest handling techniques), small-scale irrigation, seed system strengthening, as well as adoption of sustainable fishing practices. To improve HH agricultural production, CRH-SS plans to train farming HHs in improved crop, management and post-harvest handling techniques in Aweil South/Aweil North and Aweil Centre The trained HHs will be also provided with agricultural inputs, both tools (malodas, matoks, picks, knives, etc.), and assorted staple crop seeds. In addition, HHs will be supported to engage in profitable vegetable production through the provision of vegetable seeds, training in vegetable production and nutrition practices. To achieve this, agricultural training groups will be formed in Aweil South/Aweil North and Aweil Centre. All groups will be trained in organic pest management practices. Topics will include identification of local pests and effects on plants/crops, production of botanical herbicides (medicinal tree leaves, burning cow dung and cow urine), and crop husbandry practices of pest management, such as intercropping, crop rotation, and live fencing. Meanwhile, the HHs will be supported to access micro-irrigation equipment (watering cans and treadle pumps) to water their vegetables during dry seasons. Beneficiaries will also be trained to repair and maintain irrigation equipment. Finally, to encourage adoption of sustainable fishing practices, HHs will be provided with fishing kits (twins and hooks) with aim of improving fish harvesting and consumption. These HHs will be trained in sustainable fish harvesting, processing and marketing. The activities planned under Economic Recovery sector are designed to improve the social economic status of the host community and IDPs living in Aweil South, Aweil North and Aweil Centre counties. This is based on the understanding that improving economic statuses will directly contribute to the improvement in overall food security and living standards of the affected HHs. To achieve this, CRH-SS will carry out carefully planned interventions across the three mutually supportive sub-sectors of market system strengthening, financial services/inclusion and new livelihoods development. Market system strengthening activities are aimed at enhancing opportunities for income generation among Host, IDP, returnees, and vulnerable resident populations through strengthened engagement with local markets. To this end, a series of activities have been planned which include conducting a market assessment to help understand the dynamics and entry points for vulnerable HHs; creation of business groups; establishment of savings and lending groups; and improving post-harvest handling and processing in order to broaden the benefits arising from agricultural production. The financial services have the main aim of increasing access by vulnerable/non-bankable HHs to financial services through the village, savings and loan association (VSLA) model. This year, CRH-SS has planned to mobilize VSLA groups in Aweil South/Aweil North and Aweil Centre. These groups will go through a nine-month animated VSLA training using training manuals developed by VSLA associates. Finally, to support vulnerable targeted HHs to venture into new livelihoods, a livelihood opportunity mapping exercise will be conducted to with aim of helping interested HHs to make informed choices regarding which livelihood options to pursue. The key outcome of this mapping exercise will be the livelihood inventory, which lists all potential livelihood and investment opportunities in each area, and will form a basis for beneficiaries to choose their new income generating activity (IGA) investment.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CRH-SS has put in place a number of mechanisms through which feedback and complaints from all project participants, including vulnerable members, can be collected. These include suggestion boxes installed at project meeting venues/distribution points, complaints desk manned by a trained officer at each input distribution sites, and community project committees, through which beneficiaries will be able to submit their complaints or feedback about the services provided by CRH-SS. Vulnerable members such as persons with disabilities, those who are not literate, women, and girls will be educated on how best they can share feedback using any of the above channels without fear of their information being disclosed.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Christian Restore Hope South Sudan will seek feedback from affected populations in order to address any problems that may arise during program implementation and to improve how activities are designed and carried out. Beneficiaries will be informed about how they can register complaints or provide feedback in a variety of ways. For example, during distributions, a helpdesk will be set up where beneficiaries can provide feedback or register complaints with a trained staff member. Beneficiaries are then notified about availability of such services. The staff member at the help desk will be a member of the monitoring and evaluation (M&E) team, rather than the distribution team, in order to ensure adequate separation of duties. The helpdesk staff member will have a standard form for receiving and recording feedback/complaints. At each of the distribution sites, there will also be a complaint box where community members can submit written complaints if they prefer. In addition, project steering committees will be established in each community to receive and submit to Christian Restore Hope South Sudan any complaints/feedback community members bring them. Beneficiaries will be informed about the availability of these complaint and feedback channels on a regular basis (i.e. during community meetings, training sessions, before and during distribution exercises) so that they are well informed.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The feedback submitted in boxes will be regularly collected on and combined with the feedback received by the committees during distributions. Non-sensitive feedback will be summarized and shared with beneficiaries and staff at the field level. The information generated via feedback and complaints system will be used to continually improve program implementation. In particular, sector managers will meet at least quarterly to discuss general program feedback received and to agree on action plans to address any concerns raised. Program teams will then provide feedback to beneficiaries about what will be done to address their concerns, and will then carry out the agreed action steps. Program staff will provide feedback to beneficiaries at the start of program activities to inform them about steps taken to address their concerns (e.g. through pre-distribution messaging for distribution activities, at the start of trainings for training activities, etc.). Where applicable, sector leaders will also inform the relevant local authorities about plans to address general concerns beneficiaries have raised. Non-sensitive complaints that do not involve general program feedback, but which are specific to an individual or individuals, will be reviewed by the Complaints Manager, presented to the Area Coordinator, and shared with relevant sector leaders as part of the Program Manager’s monthly reporting. Where such feedback necessitates a response, Program Managers, in consultation with the Area Coordinator, will agree on action steps and the individual(s) who raised the complaint will be notified about what will be done to address their concerns. Feedback involving critical or sensitive protection issues, however, will be reported to senior management team at the field office level. The field office will also notify CRH-SS Country office. In such cases, an action plan will then be created to address the issues raised and a team unconnected with the complaint will investigate

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Before program activities commence, beneficiaries are informed of their right to file complaints, including reports of SEA and sexual harassment, through established reporting mechanisms such as the CRH-SS hotline. Christian Restore Hope South Sudan highly safeguards safety, confidentiality, and dignity of the reporting beneficiary and handles the matter with utmost respect and in a non-discriminatory manner. Program-specific complaints are directed to the PSEA focal point in the country office. Any report of SEA or sexual harassment is reported immediately to the Executive Director, per the Whistleblower Policy, for an appropriate response and/or investigation. Per the Whistleblower Policy, the Compliance Officer will enlist support from various individuals to ensure a prompt, diligent, and confidential investigation appropriate to the complaint. For allegations of SEA, the Compliance Officer will involve the Executive Director of Safeguarding to coordinate the investigation efforts with appropriate personnel in the field office, including the focal point(s).

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To minimize the risk of exposure to violence during registration and distribution as a result of long distances travelled by beneficiaries to access humanitarian assistance, efforts will be taken to ensure that distribution sites/locations will be identified in close consultation with community leaders as well as beneficiary representatives (women, girls, and older persons). Project staff will ensure that such sites are located in areas that are accessible and safe for women and girls. Registration of beneficiaries will be conducted by CRH-SS staff in close partnership with members of the project steering committee. This reduces the risk of people moving to far places for registration. Christian Restore Hope South Sudan upholds a code of conduct that is consistent with the six core principles of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Beneficiaries will be informed of their right to file complaints, including reports of SEA and sexual harassment, through established reporting mechanisms such as the Christian Restore Hope South Sudan hotline. Christian Restore Hope South Sudan highly safeguards safety, confidentiality and dignity of the reporting beneficiary and handles the matter with utmost respect and in a non-discriminatory manner. Program-specific complaints are directed to the PSEA focal point in the country office. Any report of SEA or sexual harassment is reported immediately to the Executive Director, per the whistleblower policy, for an appropriate response and/or investigation. In this way, the risk of women, girls and boys without or with disabilities being exposed to violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse, will be minimized. There is a likelihood that external stakeholders may try to influence the criteria developed to guide the selection of beneficiaries. To avert this, Christian Restore Hope South Sudan will continue to collaborate with all agencies and government institutions involved in the project to solicit their input in the selection criteria. Any disagreements arising from selection will then be addressed before commencing the selection and registration of beneficiaries. There is a likelihood of increased tension and conflict between refugee, returnees, and resident communities during the process of allocating land for demonstration purposes. To avert this, Christian Restore Hope South Sudan will continue to collaborate with all agencies and government institutions involved in the promotion of peaceful coexistence between refugee and resident communities. Any issues arising will then be handled before they escalate into larger problems

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The program will provide emergency assistance to beneficiaries with a particular focus on those in IPC 3, 5 and 5 living in Northern Bahr El Ghazal state (Aweil South, Aweil North and Aweil center). The assistance will ensure that critical inputs are available, accessible, and utilized in a manner that reduces the suffering and vulnerability of those affected by conflict and natural flooding. They will be provided with emergency food production assistance including main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution and fishing, vegetable, and crop kits. With this type of support, affected HHs will be empowered to produce sufficient food to last them through the lean months when food is normally scarce. Dry season response kits, fishing kits and vegetable kits, which will be provided just before the onset of the dry season, will supplement this. Women and youth will be targeted as they have a key role in ensuring food security in the household.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

yes

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project adopts an integrated multispectral approach with interventions occurring in other sectors (WASH, Health, agriculture/food security, economic recovery, etc.) that directly contribute towards the reduction of global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates among targeted HHs. For example, by encouraging beneficiaries (women) to join VSLAs, the project will offer a platform for additional group-based activities and information sharing on topics such as nutrition and health. Field supervisors and village agents will be trained to layer these topics into VSLA group meetings. Christian Restore Hope South Sudan plans to conduct cooking demonstrations with a focus on the vegetables promoted by the project. The purpose of conducting the cooking demonstrations will be to enable the mothers/caregivers to gain new food preparation skills, develop their confidence in preparing new dishes, and create awareness of underutilized locally available vegetable/staple crops. All HHs participating in dry season vegetable production activities will be instructed on how to prepare nutritious meals in addition to other topics (e.g. washing or cutting vegetables, food hygiene, meal frequency, etc.). In addition, through the provision of fishing kits and training on fish harvesting and fish processing, the targeted beneficiaries will further be provided with another layer of support through which they will be able to improve household food security and nutrition.
