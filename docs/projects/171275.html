Project Summary

The project is to respond to the Health Cluster Objective 1 “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities across oPt” and Objective 2 “Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building”.
This project aims to provide urgent response to immediate health needs by ensuring access to health and nutrition services for the most vulnerable patients (pregnant, lactating women, neonates and children under the age of ﬁve, both boys and girls) through the provision of medical supplies, malnutrition treatment and targeted outreach postnatal home visiting services. Additionally, the intervention will provide essential COVID-19 medical supplies (laboratory tests, infection prevention and control items and PPEs) to build the capacity of the health system to timely and effectively respond to the outbreaks of new and re-emerging infectious diseases.
The project will respond to two key components of the Health and Nutrition cluster response plan: a) Health Cluster partners will scale-up the COVID-19 response through technical support to the health authorities as well as procurement of supplies, laboratory testing, infection prevention and control, surveillance and contact tracing, case management; b) Health Cluster partners will provide primary and secondary healthcare, including preventive health services, through enhanced monitoring of shortages of medicines and other essential supplies; capacity-building for enhanced quality of life-saving healthcare; and support to preventive programs and diagnostic services. This will include procurement of essential medical supplies to increase the availability of drugs, disposables, and medical equipment to ensure accurate diagnostics and treatment for high risk pregnant and lactating women, including victims of or at risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV), newborns and children under the age of ﬁve (boys and girls) across the ﬁve governorates in the Gaza Strip. Furthermore, access to quality newborn and child health care will be improved through further enhancement of health workers’ (doctors and nurses, both females and males) capacity on lifesaving health interventions, including essential newborn care, and provision of early intervention services to children with developmental delays and disabilities and strengthening communities’ preparedness, response and resilience, especially during crises. The project will be implemented in close coordination with Health Cluster partners including WHO, UNFPA, international and local NGOs to ensure complementarity of the interventions for the improvement of maternal, newborn and child health care.
The total number of women and children that will beneﬁt from the HRP funding will be 78,450 across the ﬁve Gaza governorates over a twelve-month period. It is estimated that 47,000 high-risk women and caregivers and 31,000 children under the age of ﬁve (15,810 boys and 15,190 girls) and 450 health professionals (doctors and nurses; female 60 per cent and 40 per cent males) will beneﬁt from urgently needed lifesaving health interventions.

1 - Needs

More than 1.45 million Palestinians, across the oPt have been identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2021, two-thirds of them in the Gaza Strip. Since March 2020, the already challenged healthcare system in the oPt is experiencing added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak. The reallocation of already scarce resources to respond to the pandemic, along with the contagion of healthcare workers, has undermined people’s access to health services. Direct mortality from the virus and indirect mortality from preventable diseases is of major concern. Overall, the pandemic has rendered an estimated 115,000 people in need of health humanitarian assistance. In Gaza, the rapid spread of the virus in the community since late August has posed a heavy burden on a healthcare system, already over-stretched due to 13 years of Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian divide and violence. In the first half of 2020, 43 per cent of essential medicines at Gaza’s Central Drugs Store were completely out of stock. Efforts to contain the outbreak have undermined the delivery of other essential healthcare services. Only 19 out of 51 primary healthcare centers remain operational. Healthcare centres have been converted into COVID-19 quarantine centres, while some 400 doctors, nurses and administrative staff were mobilized to support their operations. Pregnant women and new mothers are at increased risk of not being able to access proper healthcare, especially with the current lockdown measures and the severe reduction in medical referrals. Redirection of resources from provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, obstetric and neonatal care and emergency nutrition services affected an additional 80,000 vulnerable women and 210,000 children, including 25,000 neonates. In Gaza, there was an increase in maternal mortality from 5.9 in 2017 to 19.5 in 2019 deaths per 100,000 live births, with 63% of mortalities ante-partum suggesting signiﬁcant gaps at primary healthcare level (MoH, 2019). Adolescent girls are particularly at high risk, as 28.6% of women are married before the age of 18 and 25.1% aged 20-24 had a child before the age of 18 (PCBS, 2014). Maternity and neonatal wards in Gaza are overstretched which leads to the early discharge of mothers and babies within 2-3 hours after birth with no access to post-natal services, which reduces opportunities for detection of potential medical complications and provision of lifesaving interventions. Findings of the external evaluation of the PNHV (2018) program proved the effectiveness of the UNICEF supported PNHV interventions for high risk pregnant lactating women. The report’s ﬁndings argued for the relevance and eﬃciency of the PNHV targeted interventions particularly for the most vulnerable and hard to reach pregnant and lactating women and new-borns in Gaza. In addition, a mapping study of the Maternal, Newborn, Child Heath (MNCH) health system conducted by UNICEF showed that the inconsistent supply of medical supplies negatively affects the quality of MNCH services. Access to quality maternal and newborn health care has further deteriorated due to decreased electricity supplies, undermining basic health care services already compromised by the blockade and restrictions on the import of medical supplies. Hence, the life of 25,000 newborns which require neonatal health care interventions could be severely jeopardized with intermittent and insuﬃcient electricity supplies. The fragile situation of the health system in the Gaza Strip will negatively impact the response to high-risk pregnant and lactating women, where medical services, equipment, consumables and drugs are insufficient and do not cover their need. This project is designed to contribute signiﬁcantly to the gender equality and gender-sensitive programming focusing on the needs of gender-speciﬁc vulnerabilities faced by high risk pregnant and lactating women and their new-borns and boys and girls under the age of ﬁve.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will partner with relevant stakeholders, NGOs and Health and Nutrition Cluster (HNC) agencies to address identiﬁed needs and improve access to adequate lifesaving maternal and child health interventions through the implementation of a comprehensive package of services in the selected eight maternity and neonatal hospitals, clinics and communities in five Gaza governorates. In addition, UNICEF will address the need to strengthen the healthcare system capacity to respond to the COVID-19 outbreak, focusing on maternal and child health services. Furthermore, the project will ensure complementarity with the WHO’s, UNFPA’s and an international NGO partner’s HRP projects and will jointly contribute to the implementation of the Gaza Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022. In order to reach the 2021 HRP objectives 1, “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities across oPt” and Objective 2, “Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises, including health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building” the following activities deﬁned under the HNC will be implemented: - Activity 1.4. Provide access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring that clean and safe delivery, essential newborn care and emergency obstetric and newborn care services are available at all times through provision of essential supplies for neonates, children under ﬁve and high risk pregnant and lactating women, training, community awareness and establishing a clear referral mechanism for obstetric and newborn emergencies by working at the community, primary healthcare and hospital level, focusing on the most vulnerable people, which will be implemented by local NGOs; and, - Activity 2.1 Provide essential medical supplies: laboratory, hospital and community-based case management, infection prevention and control items to build the capacity of health system to timely and effectively respond to the outbreaks of new and re-emerging infectious diseases. This will be fully coordinated with WHO, UNFPA and an international NGO complementing their intervention in maternal, newborn and child health. UNICEF will oversee project monitoring and will ensure timely reporting. Programme oﬃcers will conduct regular ﬁeld visits to the project sites and review progress reports from implementing partners. Quality checklists will be used to ensure quality of the interventions. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported. The programmatic visits will also assess barriers and challenges and lessons learned. Data will be collected, analyzed, and disaggregated by gender and by age group. Gender will also be mainstreamed by working with other clusters to ensure that clinical management of GBV is improved. Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Target beneﬁciaries have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussion and will be continually involved in its implementation. Many mothers who have been consulted during the focus group discussion have reiterated the needs for targeted PNHV services in hard to reach areas of the Gaza Strip, awareness sessions on nurturing care (e.g. health, protection, responsive feeding, early learning), and intervention services for children with disabilities. During the project implementation, channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction survey and recording goods of services delivered to the end user, so that assistance is delivered in accordance to the needs and in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.
