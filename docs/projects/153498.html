Project Summary

As a result of the ongoing complex emergency facing Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut and Longechuk Counties; majority of people in the region are in need of assistance. While the operational environment in the targeted areas remains challenging, life-saving support in key sectors remains critical in internally-displaced persons (IDP), returnees and the areas hosting them.
RI’s proposed program, “Emergency Food Security Assistance For Conflict Affected Populations”, aims to address the critical food security needs. The program will use a voucher approach that will provide unconditional food assistance to vulnerable households to not only assist needy households to be able to meet immediate basic food needs but also mitigate the prevalence of negative coping mechanisms and prevents malnutrition and further depletion of productive assets. Majority of the support will target households with children under the age of five with a focus on those with severe and moderate acute malnutrition, pregnant and nursing women, and female-headed households, which are more likely to be food insecure than male-headed households but also seeks to support other vulnerable populations directly or indirectly affected by the conflict, including IDPs and returnees.
The program also provides agricultural inputs to the target beneficiaries to be able to produce in the 2019 cropping season and hence it restores production capacity of households dependent on food aid.
RI recognizes the roles played by men and women during this difficult time; the program will therefore ensure equitable involvement of both men and women during implementation of the project. Selection of beneficiaries will give equal opportunities to men and women, boys and girls and registration of beneficiaries will take the priority needs of both men and women into consideration.
All program activities are closely in line with Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster priorities, and include gender and protection mainstreaming where appropriate.
RI has an outstanding reputation and has gained the trust of the beneficiary communities. Its ongoing programs have also enlisted the support of local authorities and generated considerable interest and participation from the community. RI will use its existing track record to successfully implement this emergency food security program.

Needs

Through various reports and assessments, including RI’s food security assessments, SMART surveys, observations and consultations, both with men and women, RI has identified widespread needs regarding access to food, among the host communities, IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community members. RI’s rapid Food security assessment in Longechuk (June 2018) indicated that majority of the population, including the IDPs, have lost their livelihoods and have limited access to cash/income sources. This, together with distorted markets and increased prices, renders access to food very difficult as indicated by the survey respondents. These findings only support the findings by IPC survey that led to place Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk counties under food insecurity emergency (IPC Phase 4). The IDPs are particularly vulnerable and many of them reported having switched to negative coping strategies which include both selling their assets and decreasing the number of meals per day per person. The latter is directly linked to the high malnutrition cases reported among the children under five years old in the five counties. In Maiwut, the most recent SMART survey conducted early August 2018 by RI has revealed a GAM rate of 10% (classified as serious) with the poor health and food security an aggravating factor in the county. The SAM rate in Maiwut is 2.4%. The same SMART survey has shown a coverage of measles vaccination among children 9 to 59 months of age very poor at only 7.2%, vitamin A supplementation coverage was 5.7%, and deworming tablet supplementation was only 3.4 percent – resulting in high morbidity rate in a county where malaria is endemic. The GAM rate is expected to increase if these contributing factors are not addressed.In Nasir the GAM rate is 17.2% and SAM rate is 3.3% - also classified as emergency according WHO. The prevalence of childhood diseases in the county (is also known as one of the county with high prevalence of acute bloody diarrhoea in the county) is directly linked to the malnutrition situation. According to the latest IPC analysis in September 2018, this situation is compounded by high food prices in the rare markets and very limited access to land for cultivation. The presence of Fall Army Worm (FAW) in the county and a rainfall shortage reported in 2018 will likely result in a poor harvest. In Ulang the GAM rate is 14.5% and SAM is at 6.1% - highly above the WHO threshold of 2.5%. The IPC classification is projecting 30% of the population of Nasir to be in Phase 4, and access to markets may remain limited due to unpredictable insecurity.In Longechuk the GAM rate is 15.8% and SAM rate is 3.2%, both above WHO emergency threshold. The IPC analysis released in September 2018 is projecting 30% of population in Longechuk to fall under Phase 4. The analysis has identified key shocks including the dry spell, increasing food prices, and the fact that 57% of households do not own livestock. Food insecurity remains one of the immediate causes of malnutrition in the county.In Maban the nutrition situation remains also serious with a GAM rate of 10.2% as per the SMART survey conducted in June 2018 and SAM at 2.8% (above WHO emergency threshold). The SMART survey has also revealed an exclusive breastfeeding rate of only 31.9% in Maban host community - a direct link to malnutrition.Although the SMART Survey and IPC shows a slight improvement of overall acute malnutrition rates compared to the same period last year, the current rates are still above WHO thresholds. With the conflict still ongoing, factors driving food insecurity, including high food prices, restrictions on safe movements, worsening hygiene and access to water, and below-average agricultural sector labor opportunities are all likely to continue to deteriorate food security outcomes. Poor households will likely face further food consumption gaps as they exhaust coping strategies and as disruptions to livelihoods continue.

Activities

1. Food Voucher distributions targeting children/care givers of malnourished children and households with pregnant/lactating mothers and children under 5. The food vouchers will consist of staple food products including sorghum. Beneficiary households will receive vouchers that can be exchanged or to purchase with food commodities every 6 weeks for 6 months. 2. In-kind provision of agricultural kits (vegetable seeds and tools) for dry season vegetable production and fishing kits will be distributed to vulnerable households in Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang and Maban Counties. 3. Seeds and tools vouchers. This will be through seed fairs organized to bring traders and farmers together to transact agricultural inputs. RI will support access quality seeds for main season cultivation. 4. Provision of training to farmers on good agronomic practices and post-harvest handling. Training will be provided to groups of farmers through demonstration gardens to improve on agricultural skills for better yields. RI expect to support farmers with training on new technology, agricultural techniques, crop and seed storage to be able to increase production. 5. Establishment/strengthening of Village Savings and Loans groups (VSLAs) to promote the culture of savings and investment among vulnerable population. VSLAs have to a large extent supported vulnerable families in absorbing shocks due to unplanned emergencies including crop failure and it’s seen as a kind of insurance mechanism. RI will support formation of 30 VSLA groups comprising approximately 20 members each with priority given to women. 6. Provide support to 60 entrepreneurs and micro businesses through trainings, in-kind grants and apprenticeships programing. The proposed project will enable RI to expand its emergency food security activities and deliver as an integrated emergency assistance to the vulnerable population. RI through support from WFP is implementing General Food Distributions in the targeted areas as such the proposed program will complement and bridge gaps in the GFD. As both programs will be implemented in the same locations and have the same objective with similar activities, the two programs together will not only allow RI to reach more beneficiaries but also to support each other and create synergies between the activities where needed. Additionally, RI is implementing Health, Nutrition and WASH programs funded by OFDA as well as UNICEF Nutrition funded programs. The proposed program will therefore support RI to reach more vulnerable populations and complement the current programs.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Information will be provided to beneficiaries through community leaders (both formal and informal), community committees (including project management committees, VSLAs), as well as at Institutions (schools and healthcare facilities). Additionally, mass messaging will take place through community mobilizers, and where possible, radio campaigns.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be provided in both verbal form as well as through visual aids. Mass messaging for all groups will be provided in a generic format to blanket reach beneficiaries. To reach the most vulnerable including women, girls, the elderly, and disabled, specific messaging including house-to-house visits and messaging by female staff will be provided.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PSEA is already mainstreamed throughout all of RI's policies and programming. Populations will be informed of reporting mechanisms, anonymous and non anonymous through AAP messaging. SEA is unacceptable in RI, is considered gross misconduct and is grounds for immediate termination of staff.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Local leaders - informal and informal have been consulted on a regular basis throughout 2018 for feedback on 2019 programming through formal and informal meetings. Consultation with beneficiaries also has occurred through meetings with farmer groups and project Management committees, at distribution sites for livelihoods kits, and through training. Beneficiaries of RI's 2018 programming are consulted on a regular basis for feedback on programming and quality of service to improve and enhance programming in 2019. Additionally, coordination meetings with national and international NGOs take place in Juba and in the field to review ongoing programming and plan for 2019.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Feedback and complaint mechanisms are established through multiple mechanisms including: - compliant desks at distribution sites - anonymous and non-anonymous reporting mechanisms to a reporting focal point in each of RI's field offices - post distribution monitoring (PDM) - Ad-hoc supervisory and beneficiary feedback meetings conducted by senior monitoring staff.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Relief International will use community-driven participatory approaches to guide the community through a process of self-identification to select the most vulnerable HHs. Where possible, RI will work with community committees. In case of committee selection, women will be included to ensure there is a gender-balanced approach. Selection criteria and distribution method will be adequately communicated at payam/boma and community levels through meetings and awareness sessions. RI will also use public information channels where possible to ensure that selection criteria are widely disseminated in order to reduce tensions among those receiving support and those who are excluded.Given the fact that RI staff members are living in close proximity to the beneficiaries, RI has direct insight in all target locations. RI will liaise with committees to ensure that all vulnerable groups are identified, including the elderly, people with disabilities and female-headed households. Furthermore, post distribution monitoring (PDM) will be used to track the suitability and end use of relief items, while providing greater accountability to beneficiaries.Verification will be conducted through household visits to registered participants to check that all beneficiaries meet the selection criteria. RI aims to randomly verify 50 percent of the participants. As part of the awareness creation activities, communities will be informed of the establishment of a complaint and grievance mechanism. This mechanism will include setting up feedback boxes in communities, hotlines with contact numbers (where there is phone network) posted at public places, forms left with community leaders, and post-distribution monitoring.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers specific protection needs of all population groups in the following ways: - At all distribution sites a safe space for PLWs and mothers/caretakers of children will be established for breastfeeding, childcare, and socialization (promoting sharing of experiences). - Rights awareness messaging is disseminated prior to all distributions and through PMCs -Referral mechanisms to health, nutrition, and SGBV care are established and will be utilized for cases of illness/diseases, malnutrition, and SGBV – identified cases of illness and malnutrition will be immediately referred to the nearest health/nutrition facility. Self-reported cases of SBGV are referred to the main protection partner (ADRA, CMD) for care and case management. – Vulnerable groups including elderly, households with malnutrition, PLWs, and the disabled are prioritized for programming.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Barriers to access include conflict, and swampy, blocked roads due to rain and wet roads. To address barriers to access, RI utilized community mobilizers as well as mobile teams to reach the most vulnerable where RI may not be able to reach. Additionally, RI regularly engages in RRMs to reach IDPs and returnees - where static services may be inaccessible. Moreover, in all operational field sites RI maintains means of movement including boats, motorbikes, donkey carts, and vehicles to reach all targeted beneficiaries.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Inequalities are addressed mainly through the prioritization of women in beneficiary selection as well as in decision-making processes. Women are prioritized for hiring, and are consulted on all decision-making procedures to ensure their voice is heard and considered. The threat of coercion and community tension is mitigated through involvement in programming by localleaders and beneficiaries, to ensure transparent and accountable mechanisms.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

While ensuring that all community members have equal access to the program, RI designs all its activities to be gender-sensitive in South Sudan cultural context and refrains from challenging gender norms to a degree that might create protection issues. That concerns both the selection and distribution of vouchers in the communities. RI will go directly to the communities to deliver the vouchers and where necessary, female staff will deliver vouchers and provide community sensitization and outreach to female beneficiaries.Community leaders will be involved in each step so they encourage women to participate.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

To minimize the risks to beneficiaries, RI will use the existing markets in the area to provide food, whereby vendors will be responsible for transporting food along their regular routes, thus significantly reducing organizational presence and visibility. Additionally, the voucher mechanism allows beneficiaries to receive food items close to their houses so they wouldn’t need to undertake unsafe travel or go to large distribution points. This protects women and girls in particular, as culturally they are not allowed to travel long distances or interact with men.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

A security lens will be applied to targeting of communities and households in order to mitigate any potential conflict related to program exclusion. While ensuring vulnerable households with women and children are the primary beneficiaries, the program is designed with sensitivity to the South Sudan cultural context.Additionally; RI is committed to promoting a productive, safe, and secure work environment in order to protect its staff, beneficiaries and assets. As the current conflict continues to evolve in South Sudan, RI will undertake several measures to reduce security threats to the lowest level possible. RI works in some of the most difficult and dangerous places in the world, including Afghanistan, Somalia, and Iraq and has a global reach in security training and guidance through established agency-wide security protocols. Every RI office has an appointed security focal point that is responsible for advising, developing, and updating local security procedures for the protection of staff and beneficiaries. Also, RI receive information regarding the security situation through the UN Department of Safety and Security and the International NGO Security Forum. RI will use these resources to keep its staff, beneficiaries and assets safe.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Community based protection is an essential component of RI’s human security work. Human security recognizes that vulnerable people are exposed to a constant series of threats and risks and focuses on need to retain a minimum quality of life defined as freedom from fear, freedom from want and freedom to take action on one’s own behalf. RI acknowledges the importance of this rights as such; all its programs are designed to empower vulnerable people to claim their rights and emphasize the need to protect, respect and fulfil people’s rights and assist the most vulnerable by increasing their safety with dignity by developing local prevention and response strategies and organizing their resources to reduce exposure to harm.For example; RI will go directly to the communities to deliver the vouchers and where necessary, female staff will deliver vouchers and provide community sensitization and outreach to female beneficiaries moreover community leaders will be involved in each step so they encourage women to participate openly and air their views in case of dissatisfaction.
