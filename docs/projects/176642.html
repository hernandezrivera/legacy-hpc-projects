Project Summary

Pathways to Resilience is a multi-year, conflict-sensitive, humanitarian/development response with an integrated WASH approach aimed at strengthening physical and social assets and increasing agricultural
production, food security, and access to input and output markets, to will provide pathways to resilience for families who currently depend on food assistance.

1 - Needs

To stabilize vulnerable communities in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria States with a range of emergency and resilience-building activities and respond to the deteriorating food security situation in communities in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria as a result of flooding during the 2020 rainy season. All counties targeted by P2R have a projected IPC Acute Food Security Situation Phase of 4 (Emergency) as demonstrated from the most recent IPC publication projecting the phase from April 2021 to July 2021. Each County is listed below.-Duk (Phase 4 Emergency)-Akobo (Phase 4 Emergency)-Kapoeta North (Phase 4 Emergency)-Budi (Phase 4 Emergency)

2 - Project activities

-Expanding access to physical, livelihood and social assets and markets -Supporting advancement across agriculture, livestock, alternative livelihoods, nutrition, WASH and social cohesion -Meeting emerging diversity of needs including youth and women -Reducing dependency on humanitarian assistance and gradual transition from humanitarian caseload.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will comply with CRS Protection and Safeguarding policy

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Among feedback and complaints channels, CRS will ensure that there is at least one channel that gives community members an opportunity to share sensitive complaints related to safeguarding and fraud issues in a confidential manner. CRS projects also actively seeks feedback from beneficiaries through informal discussions with all the projects beneficiaries who include the most vulnerable members.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CRS consults beneficiaries on their preferred feedback channels and engages key stakeholders at different levels at the appropriate targeting of individuals. CRS disseminates knowledge on services provided through IEC, mass messaging, and community meetings. Projects also conduct education during pre-registration and distribution, which highlights the existence of a response and feedback mechanism and how the mechanism can be accessed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project has a database for feedback. CRS also conducts regular review of feedback received and consider any changes to the project. All feedback will be responded to individually or in groups, as appropriate. CRS policy requires feedback is responded to or referred within 90 days from when it was received, and that feedback is analyzed to inform project decisions. Feedback will be managed by dedicated gender and protection staff in collaboration with the MEAL team to ensure communities are aware of how to submit feedback and expectations regarding responses.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CRS will not operate in locations of active political violence over boundary setting and will re-allocate program resources to more stable payams. Projects will explain this policy as part of its engagement with communities and stakeholders. All sensitive feedback (feedback regarding suspicions of fraud, protection violations, or exploitation, abuse, and harassment) will be handled by the Country Representatives of the respective organizations or CRS’ online reporting mechanism. Focal points assigned to each field location will train staff on confidential reporting using CRS’ online FRM. A fair and impartial FRM will be set up in collaboration with the communities. Throughout the project cycle, CRS and partners will create awareness on the project’s feedback and complaint channels (two channels in each target community). These may include channels such as suggestion boxes, focus group discussions, and in-person reporting to trusted focal points. Initial project targeting will be completed at the start of each implementation cycle. Returnees will be identified – via continuous engagement with county and state RRCs, OCHA, and clusters (e.g. food security and livelihoods, WASH, nutrition, and protection) in addition to monitoring REACH and International Organization for Migration (IOM) displacement and return data – and subsequently registered and sensitized to project activities. Due to the high number of IDPs in the project area, the project will continually engage with local structures to ensure tensions aren't aggravated between IDPs and host community. The project will also implement several social cohesion interventions to reduce the likelihood of conflict and insecurity being aggravated in target areas.
