Project Summary

The project addresses critical protection concerns of 271 families including IDPs families and other families who are at risk of displacement due to not being in a position to afford their rent. Severe socioeconomic vulnerabilities associated with poor access to safe and adequate shelter had resulted in a critical humanitarian situation for 353 women (18-59), 322 men (18-59), 417 girls (under 18), 408 boys (under 18), and 14 elderlies (above 59)). The project target includes 29 women headed households, 16 disabled headed households, and 8 elderly headed households.
This action is in line with the assigned objectives of the Shelter Cluster; objective 1: “Displaced populations are provided support that mitigates immediate harm and ensures adequate and safe temporary shelter solutions”, and objective 2: “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”.
The project aims at:
1. Providing for the protection of vulnerable internally displaced families who are living in inadequate shelters through the provision of safe temporary shelter solutions, and adequate NFIs.
2. Providing for the protection of hardship cases, who are at risk of losing tenancy due to not being in a position to afford their rent, through provision of temporary shelter assistance, and adequate NFIs.
The Interventions include:
1. Provision of temporary shelter assistance to IDPs from previous conflicts and rely on the TSCA to pay the rental accommodation. (Target: 165 families of IDPs including 196 women (18-59), 192 men (18-59), 244 girls (under 18), 239 boys (under 18), 10 elderlies (above 59)).
2. Provision of shelter assistance to households at risk of displacements due to economic deterioration and salary cuts. (Target: 106 families including 157 women (18-59), 130 men (18-59), 173 girls (under 18), 169 boys (under 18), and 4 elderlies (above 59)).
3. To support the targeted families to be more prepared for the winter weather conditions through the distribution of winterized NFI kits. (Target: 271 families, including 353 women (18-59), 322 men (18-59), 417 girls (under 18), 408 boys (under 18), and 14 elderlies (above 59)).

Needs

The deteriorating socio-economic situation in the Gaza Strip and salary cuts has deepened the severe humanitarian situation for Palestinian families. A situation left hundreds of families at risk of displacements due to not being in a position to afford their rent. In coordination with the Ministry of Public Works and Housing, a list of 106 families, who are living in rented houses and at high risk of displacement as they cannot afford the rent, had been prepared for assessment. The assessment included home visits to the 106 families with particular focus on addressing the needs of women, persons with disabilities and elderly. Team of social workers, including 9 women, were responsible for the data collection. GBV specialist and child protection advisor were consulted throughout the planning, design, analysis and interpretation of the assessment. The assessed 106 families include 157 women (18-59), 130 men (18-59), 173 girls (under 18), 169 boys (under 18), and 4 elderlies (above 59). 99.1% of assessed families are abject poor with an average monthly income of 187.0 NIS per adult equivalent and other 0.9% of the families were classified as poor with an average monthly income of 593.6 NIS per adult equivalent. 38.7% of families reported no income and a completed dependency on aid. There are 17 women headed households, 10 disabled headed households, and 2 elderly headed households. The assessment revealed alarming vulnerabilities exacerbating the protection concerns that are related to the shelter access. They reported accumulated accrued rent with an average of 1195.7$ per family. The 106 families reported that there is no available alternative of adequate shelter in case of displacement. Urgent need for winterized NFIs had been reported. The assessment included analysis for women related needs and roles. Besides the adverse effect on psychological, social, and physical well-being of women, 14% of the consulted women reported that the current situation of poor access to shelter enforced women and girls to accept improper work conditions (unsafe, and unhealthy) in order to support for accessing temporary shelter solutions.Since June 2018, MA’AN has been working with 165 families of IDPs (Non Refugees) supporting their access to adequate shelter through the provision of safe temporary shelter solutions. The disbursed rental subsidies cover the needs till the end of December 2018. The families include 196 women (18-59), 192 men (18-59), 244 girls (under 18), 239 boys (under 18), 10 elderlies (above 59), 17 people with disabilities, and 20 pregnant or lactating mother. The 165 families have significant socioeconomic vulnerabilities. 40 families have no access to any source of income and totally dependent on aid. 93.91% are abject poor with an average monthly income of 188.2 NIS per adult equivalent and other 2.4% of the families were classified as poor with an average monthly income of 607.4 NIS per adult equivalent. The living conditions of displaced girls and women, accommodated with host families or in rented apartments, raise a range of protection concerns, including lack of privacy and increasing exposure to gender-based violence. 98% of the interviewed women reported that their poor access to adequate and safe shelter have reflections on the social relations and affects adversely their psychological well-being and increases the risks women face in the form of violence. According to recent update from the Ministry of Housing, there are interventions planned to start within few months to provide for permanent shelter solutions for the IDPs families. However, it is expected to have a gab till the IDPs received the allocated assistance. The Ministry of Housing appealed to provide for Transitional Shelter Cash Assistance for three to six months starting from January 2018 to address the related protection concerns till the disbursement for the payments of reconstruction.

Activities

The elaboration of the project activities had considered the conducted needs assessment, the coordination with the clusters, and further consultations with the target beneficiaries regarding their priorities and the proper response mechanisms with particular focus on women and children. The project activities prioritized female headed households, people with disabilities, the elderly, GBV survivors, and other highly vulnerable individuals and groups who are more likely to resort to negative coping strategies. The project includes: Activity 1: Transitional Shelter Cash Assistance (TSCA):This includes to provide rental subsidies to (1) 165 families of IDPs who are renting a shelter and being at risk of losing tenancy due to not being in a position to afford their rent; and other IDPs families who have been living with hosting families with exposure to high protection related concerns, and (2) 106 families of hardship cases, who are at risk of losing tenancy due to not being in a position to afford their rent. The value of the rental subsidies will be calculated on a case by case basis during the appraisal phase, and will amount to an estimated 214 USD per month per family. The cost of one month rent is estimated with considering to the family size according to the agreed categories with MOPWH (1 to 5 family members 200 USD; 6 to 9 members 225 USD; 10 and above 250 USD). For IDPs and according to the recommendation of the Ministry of Public Works and Housing, four months’ rental subsidies will be granted into two instalments. Regarding the 106 families of hardship cases, six months’ rental subsidies will be granted into three instalments. The transfer of each installment will be conditioned by the results of the validation visits that will be conducted by MA’AN team of field monitors to verify the status of the targeted families. According to the assessment, several cases reported that they are living in housing units in substandard conditions and being at high risk during severe winter weather. In such cases, the paid TSCA will be considered to adapt the shelter conditions to be more protective and this will be considered in negotiation with the shelter owners. In case the shelter owner rejected such a model of payments disbursement, the target family will be guided to rent another house which is safe and adequate. Activity 2: Emergency Winterization AssistanceThis should target 106 families of hardship cases. According to the needs assessment and through direct consultations with families, an urgent need for winterized NFIs was recognized. The priorities and preferences had been checked with particular focus on the priorities and needs of women, children, elderlies and people with disabilities. This had considered the kits that had been distributed to the supported 165 families of IDPs (summer kits). The needed Items estimated at 300 USD per family and include mainly mattresses, heating appliances, warm blankets, safe and emergency lighting system (LED and battery). The kits will be procured by MA’AN to be distributed for beneficiaries “in kind”. The vendors will be identified based on the best prices with considering to the essential technical and legal criteria. Activity 3: Awareness Sessions on GBV and Child ProtectionThe project beneficiaries (including women, men, girls and boys) will participate in awareness sessions (two sessions) about child protection, and GBV. In these sessions the beneficiaries will be oriented about protection services providers and the child protection and GBV referral pathways. MA’AN will ensure coordination on protection referral, including standardization of tools for assessments and monitoring in order to address the serious protection gaps with a priority on referral for GBV and child protection. This will be addressed through the family centers and other social services centers.

Indicators

A monitoring and evaluation mechanism will be established to follow up the progress of the process, to ensure the matching of the identified indicators and the expected outcomes of the project. The M&E plan identifies tasks, quality of outputs, means of verification and persons in charge in respect to the project timeframe and budget. It will compile also:a. Full coordination and involvement of relevant stakeholders during all project phases, supported by MA’AN M&E teamsb. Ensuring the gender mainstreaming in all project’s phases and outputs Accountability to involved stakeholders is encouraged and assured through a complaint mechanism which involves a direct communication flow among beneficiaries, family centers and MA’AN, to include review of activities and outcomes, if needed. MA'AN M&E officer will be in charge of monitoring the conduction of the winterization and the TSCA activities in accordance with the humanitarian standards. The main M&E tools MA'AN will employ are questionnaires, field visits forms, baseline and endline surveys, and beneficiary satisfaction assessments. All monitoring data collected will be fully disaggregated by age, sex, disability status and governorate. Data collection and monitoring process will be done with the engagement of local committees (Community Based Child Protection Committees), beneficiaries and also field staff (men and women), in realization with the cultural and social challenges specific to the targeted communities with the emphasis of doing no harm approach. Specific focus will be on providing appropriate opportunities to amplify the voices of groups with protection needs including female headed households and disabled persons, not only to articulate their own needs but to give them space to contribute to project decision making and evaluation.
