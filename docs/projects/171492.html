Project Summary

Based on the analysis of the current flooding situation in Beletweyne, HIWA intends to extend its protection program to contribute towards enhancing protection, increasing safety and promoting the dignity of flood-affected women, men, girls, and boys, in Beletweyne and Bulo Burte districts, The overarching objective is to contribute towards enhancing protection response capacity, through the deployment of mobile teams composed of Case Workers, Case Manager, and field Monitors, for increased identification and service delivery. This assistance will be provided where there is currently very limited access to GBV services and to provide tailored and effective assistance where it is needed most,
To ensure the most vulnerable are reached, HIWA will target 26,500 newly displaced women, men, girls and boys from both IDPs and host communities, it will also give priorities to the people living with disabilities who are in need of urgent humanitarian assistance. This project will fill gaps in service provision where government and humanitarian support is currently insufficient. Provision of services in general and child protection services, in particular, have been significantly challenged by the limited access.
HIWA is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving GBV services to vulnerable populations in Hiraan Region;
1. Conduct Rapid Protection Assessments to identify the most urgent needs after new displacements. 2. Provide specialized GBV case management to at-risk women and girls. The guiding strategy will be twofold: combining collection, analysis and dissemination of relevant information through community outreaches; and direct support of identified cases through comprehensive case management (including case management for boy and girl survivors of GBV; medical and psychosocial support and referral to other specialized care and ensuring appropriate follow up and care where necessary. 3. Distribution of emergency material support through the distribution of dignity kits to women and girls and distribution of solar lantern and engagement with communities to build community-driven protection mechanisms 4. Psychosocial support through temporary women and girls friendly spaces providing psychosocial support, recreational activities, and non-formal education activities. 5. In close collaboration with the GBV sub-cluster in Beletweyne review and strengthening of local level referral pathways. 6. Site Safety audits will be conducted to assess the flood impact and examine aspects pertaining to protection and safeguarding, to ensure the safety of men, women, boys, and girls, including people with disabilities. 7. Community outreach and engagement activities to disseminate key information on GBV, including information on the availability of services, and promote positive social norms to improve the safety and well-being of women and girls The project aims at reaching as many beneficiaries as possible offering them multilayer assistance and different service provided. The core of the proposal is the mobilization of caseworkers who will access different areas and reach newly displaced, as well as receiving community members. The teams will provide different services, from general protection (case management, psycho-social support services, provision of medical (CMR) services for survivors of rape, monitoring of protection issues and documentation of GBV cases management and referral). Target beneficiaries will include: women, men, girls and boys among displaced, including newly displaced people and/or newly accessible people; receiving community members. The guiding strategy will be twofold: combining collection, analysis, and dissemination of relevant information; and direct support of identified cases through comprehensive case management (including internal/external referral) and distribution of dignity kits.

Project Activities

1.GBV Case Management. Provision of GBV response support to boys, girls, and women which including the provision of GBV service package (essential medical services including CMR/PEP within 72 hours, psychosocial support, to identified survivors). 2. Provision of material support for the most vulnerable beneficiaries through the distribution of 500 solar lanterns as an emergency response targeting women at risk of GBV. The criteria for distribution will be determined by HIWA after consultation with community leaders, in order to ensure fair distribution, with particular emphasis on the most vulnerable community members, e.g. female-headed households, GBV survivors, disabled people and unaccompanied adolescent girls.3.Conduct community outreach and engagement activities to disseminate key information on GBV, including information on the availability of services, and promote positive social norms to improve the safety and well-being of women and girls. Awareness sessions for IDPs & the host community will be conducted by HIWA community caseworkers. Through the community outreach, HIWA will distribute information and education and communication (“IEC”) materials harmonized by the GBV sub-clusters. The awareness-raising activities will target members of the communities; social groups, local authorities, and policymakers to put emphasis on and change the negative attitudes and behaviors of the communities towards GBV and harmful practices. The activities use the most effective methods to convey messages to the most number of people targeted. 4. Provision of material support for the most vulnerable beneficiaries through the distribution of 2000 dignity kits as an emergency response targeting women and girls at risk of GBV. Dignity kits consist of Somali dress (dirac), shawl (gabasaar),sanitary pads regular, women underwear, pieces of bar soap, packets of powder Soap, petticoats (gorgorat), head scarf (shaash). The dignity kits will be distributed by HIWA in the safe spaces and will target GBV survivors, women and girls in female-headed households, adolescent girls and women with special needs. Due to the vulnerability of women and girls, especially those that experience rape, HIWA will prioritize meeting their menstrual needs as well as restoring their dignity after an incident of sexual violence. 5.Psychosocial support through temporary women and girls friendly spaces providing psychosocial support, recreational activities, and non-formal education activities. Through counselors and community, health workers provide community-based psycho-social support targeting 200 GBV survivors and women at risk. 6.In collaboration with Beletweyne GBV sub-cluster lead update and strengthen local level referral pathway and conduct safety audits. GBV safety audits in different IDP settlements. Implement GBV safety audits, through Outreach Workers, and share the findings with relevant clusters for action. HIWA Outreach Workers will coordinate to implement safety audits that examine aspects pertaining to protection and safeguarding, to ensure the safety of men, women, boys, and girls, including people with disabilities. Safety audits will be implemented in locations where newly displaced IDPs settle, where they have not yet been implemented and/or where they have not been implemented since the onset of flooding.
