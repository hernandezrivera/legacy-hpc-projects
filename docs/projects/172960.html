Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the resilience and coping capacities of vulnerable mobile populations, including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees and their host community to enhance food and livelihood security as well as to mitigate the risk of malnutrition and poverty. This will be achieved through delivering agricultural, livestock interventions vocational trainings and alternative income generating activities. Despite the growing humanitarian needs, the planned activities will promote the synergy between increased agricultural production and the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN).
The interventions will target the most vulnerable small-scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and local communities with special attention to youths and women. IOM will apply a right based, community centered approach to promote community initiatives and sow the seeds for durable solutions and ensure that planned interventions are met, based on needs and the rights of the vulnerable migrants and community, to access improved economic opportunities.
The project will focus on ten localities and this includes: El Fasher locality (North Darfur), Geneina and Krenik localities (West Darfur), Al Radoum ,Rahad El Berdi and Um Dafug localities (South Darfur), Ghubaysh localitiy (West Kordofan State), Wad Al Mahi and Ar Rusayris localities, (Blue Nile State) and Dourdeb locality (Red Sea State). Returnees, IDPs and local communities in these localities are vulnerable to food insecurity and limited livelihood opportunities due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high staple food prices. This contributes towards food insecurity alongside chronic and severe malnutrition. The identified localities suffered from the high prevalence in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 (Crisis) and/or phase 4 (Emergency) further highlighting the need for timely and adequate livelihood interventions to address food insecurity, strengthen community resilience to protect lives, and move towards economic self-sufficiency.
Under HRP 2021, this project aligns with Objectives 2 and 3. The planned interventions will include: (1) Community needs assessments and capacity building for local production cooperatives and inputs on agriculture employing climate adaptation techniques whilst promoting synergies with natural resource management and climate change mitigation, alleviating protection concerns and increasing the resilience of communities in coping with disasters. (2) support sustainable livestock husbandry practice and veterinary services (3) small business support and vocational trainings for youth and women on agriculture and other livelihood skills such as food processing activities to enhance community resilience and reinvigorate local economies through the creation of small businesses. IOM has already established a good access to the most of target localities including (Dourdeb, Krenik, Al Radoum, Geneina, Um Dafug, El Fasher), and seeks to continue implementing community stabilization and durable solution initiative activities under UNDAF outcomes. The planned interventions will be delivered in a way to complement past and on-going activities implemented, thereby strengthening links between the HRP Outcomes and UNDAF Outcomes.

1 - Needs

The continued increase of COVID-19 cases, coupled with the economic crisis, natural shocks such as floods and civil unrest has exacerbated the livelihood situation of vulnerable communities in Sudan. The high price of agricultural inputs and food has undermined food security and livelihood situations. The economic crisis has led to a drop in people’s purchasing power and reduced their ability to purchase enough food and essential basic services. This is particularly relevant for small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by human displacement. Furthermore, a lack of livelihood opportunities, food insecurity, and lack of knowledge on nutrition that results in poor feeding practices drive acute and chronic malnutrition among the most vulnerable groups of the population especially pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and their children. Growing constraints on access to sustainable income generating activities have added to the high level of vulnerability in the targeted localities. The influx of returnee and movements of IDPs and seasonal migrants is adding further pressure on the usage of local natural resources of host communities. The 2021 HNO identifies 7.2 million people in need of food and livelihoods assistance, an increase from 6.2 million people in 2020. Since 2016, IOM began working in return and displacement areas of Al Radoum, El Fasher, Geneina and Krenik localities and other affected areas such as Dourdeb and Um Dafug localities. IOM’s need assessments informed that community members in these areas highlighted that most of the communities rely heavily on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income while many of them are selling local agricultural and livestock products for a short-term income to acquire enough foods.These locations also align with the IPC projection of priority locations for 2020 that currently have a high percentage of crisis and emergency levels.Despite its' self-identified importance, local production of crops and livestock remains a low and limited source of self-subsistence due to fluctuating weather patterns, deteriorating grazing lands and poor farming and livestock practices that do not meet the local market demands. Post-harvest losses are also a major constraint on farmers’ profitability. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access for the vulnerable population. Many of female headed households in the target localities still rely on unstable income sources such as firewood collection and charcoal making which have a potential of environmental damage, with limited ownership of common and productive assets. They also adapt food based negative coping strategy- such as selling the last remaining female animals to acquire enough food, which is leading to irreversible loss of livelihoods. The project will respond to the needs by delivering immediate agricultural assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through resilience-based agricultural and livestock interventions. To enhance skills and knowledge for better agricultural production and nutrition sensitive, climate adaptive techniques, agricultural training, vocational training, and inputs will be implemented by having technical assistance of Sudanese agricultural experts in Sudan and from abroad. IOM will seek to strengthen the existing partnership with the Sudanese diaspora to improve traditional agricultural activities and to better absorb shocks to climate change, whilst at the same time carry out trainings for the local authorities to increase their capacity. IOM will mainstream gender sensitivity with an overall target of 49% men and 51% women. By age IOM will attend to 70% adults, 20 % children and 10% Elderly and Disabilities of the total selected beneficiaries’ number 25,000.

2 - Activities/Outputs

All proposed activities will be coordinated with the relevant government authorities and the sector leading agencies and other livelihood partners working in the same areas. IOM will support each locality utilizing a needs-based approach. In addition, in South Darfur, IOM will attempt to reach the sector target of Um Dafug locality (a total 7,295) through the planned livelihood activities while a greater number of beneficiaries in Rahad El Berdi locality will be supported. Due to geographic location, the residents in both localities are interacting for trading. Hence, there will be a potential that communities in two localities could share the existing resources and the project inputs for a mutual benefit. Activities I. Community Needs Assessments, Agricultural and Livestock Support: 1. Conduct ten community needs assessments per locality building on and updating prior community workshops conducted in the selected areas. 2. Set-up new and train existing local agricultural cooperatives for each locality (ten committees: 10-20 members for each committee). 3. Organize and conduct Training of Trainers for the established committee members (targeting a total of 160 trainees) and this will be done by the Sudanese agricultural expert. 4. Distribute improved seeds and farming tools for a total of 20,000 beneficiaries 400- 500 households (HHs) (approximately 2,000-2,500 individuals from each locality) and other agricultural inputs such as small water pumps for irrigation farm, motorcycles (tuktuk) to support transportation of products to local markets, as needed. 5. Conduct agricultural training (5-7 days) for the selected beneficiaries by the trained cooperatives members. 6. Establish an irrigation system for three selected farms (target localities may include: Krenik, EL Fasher and Um Dafug) through having the technical support of a Sudanese agricultural expert: The irrigation systems will be established, having a potential to develop an integrated farm that will allow target communities to nutrition sensitive vegetable, livestock and fruits. The interventions will include the distribution of vegetable seeds, fruit seeds, farming tools. Motorcycles (tuktuk) for each farm will be provided to support transportation of products to local markets. The project inputs will be shared between target migrants and local communities. 7. Provide upgraded veterinary care equipment and refresher training targeting a total 60 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (5-10 CAHWs from each locality). 8) Provide vaccinated healthy local livestock (goats) with training on better husbandry practice and livestock health for female headed households (HHs)- A total of 800 HHs (at least 80--90 households per locality). II. Vocational Training and Small business and Income Generating Supports: 1. Mapping of production cooperatives, youth committees and women’s groups (composition, organisations, mandate) in the target localities and conduct needs assessment study. 2. Provide small business support and support the youth in establishing agricultural cooperatives and link them with CSOs and/or private sector companies operating in the areas. Provision of assets could include will be determined based on community consultation process. Training on machine maintenance and business trainings will further be provided. 3. Provide food processing and conservation training based on local agricultural productions and addressing key components of nutritional care for women (PLWs) and children: The interventions will target a total of 800 female headed HHs with small children (80-100 HHs from each locality). 4. Provide vocational trainings, income generating activities and build capacities of youth and women in sustainable agricultural production, micro-processing, and marketing on household livestock products.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Planned agricultural and vocational trainings will be organized and addressed by technical Sudanese experts in the field. Interventions mainly targeted towards supporting agricultural inputs, seeds and tools to farmers seek to mitigate the impact of COVID-19 containment measures on supply chains, and support livelihood diversification and home-based food production. Local agricultural cooperatives will also be capacitated to be able to face shocks in supply value chains in the face of the ongoing economic crisis and COVID-19 lockdown measures and assist in the provision of animal treatments, feed and supplements, specifically targeting herders whose movements are restricted, and support livelihood diversification to vulnerable communities.

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will employ a right based approach to promote the protection and human rights of migrants. The project will pay special attention to returnees, IDPs in vulnerable situations including, youths and women as well as local/hosting communities who do not have adequate access to basic services and vulnerable to food and livelihood security. The project will empower these groups by involving them in the decision-making processes through a people-centered approach. The project will focus on community consultation process during the project design and implementation and this will ensure that the proposed/delivered livelihood activities are met with the needs of target beneficiaries. A broad range of mobility issues will be discussed during the community consultative process, ranging from protection threats faced by vulnerable returnees, IDPs, women and youths, for example what causes their displacement and vulnerabilities and economic disparities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase -immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. The proposed project is built around a community-centred and participatory approach to ensure that the communities themselves are enablers and drivers of their own resilience, recovery and development. Accountability will also be promoted through capacity development activities for relevant government stakeholders, community leaders and leaders from women and youth groups, etc. As part of capacity development, the project will establish the community management committees or re-activate the existing committees and the key responsibility of committee will be to coordinate and monitor overall project implementation as well as obtain feedback from the project beneficiaries. Women’s representation will be assured within the committees, and female committee members will be encouraged to report to IOM’s focal point if protection threats such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse are reported by target beneficiaries. If needed, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling and medical assistance to such victims and this will be done in coordination with other partners in protection sector.
