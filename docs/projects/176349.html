Project Summary

AVSI's 2021 intervention utilizes an integrated approach to address the food security and livelihoods needs of the population in Eastern Equatoria State (EES) (Ikwoto, Torit, Magwi, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South) and Lakes State (LS) (Cueibet, Yirol and Rumbek North). The overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce food insecurity of the selected areas sustainably, having as main targets food insecure communities classified in IPC 3,4 and 5, single mothers, returnees and vulnerable households. FSL service delivery will integrate emergency to reliance interventions, aiming to strengthen the productive bases of agricultural communities to enhance overall production. At the same time, targeting the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more-developed groups and production systems. This model also uses the ‘model-farmers’ approach, where successful local farmers set the example for other community members. Activities will target both the local institutions (with a strong component of capacity building and awareness) and the local communities (with direct support, capacity building and access to improved agricultural inputs and services). At the institutional level (county and local authorities), the interventions will focus on creating and strengthening monitoring systems in the fields of livestock (disease early detection and outbreak prevention) and agriculture. IDPs/returnees and ‘host’ communities are both considered in order to avoid conflict. For the former, activities will focus on their integration/interaction through agriculture activities, enhanced access to quality and improved agricultural inputs (seeds and tool kits) and general services (health services, accessibility to water, school fees). For the second group, a wide range of activities will be implemented including capacity building in agriculture using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach. For both, the diversification of livelihoods activities will be supported (eg. fish farming, bee-keeping, vegetable production, agro-forestry etc). With the lessons learnt from compounding effect of COVID-19 pandemic to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines and approaches shall be adhered and implemented in 2021 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services in the context of COVID -19. The project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programing with other relevant sectors such as nutrition, WASH, health, protection and food security through effectives coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to mult-sectoral basic service which will further reduce food insecurity and increase resilience. Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that programing of FSL services approach is community/people centered in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs. Consideration gender and age variation of the vulnerable groups will be prioritized as well for efficiency of the response.

1 - Needs

The food security and nutrition conditions targeted areas in Eastern Equatoria (EES) and Lakes (LS) States continue to be characterized by fragile food and livelihoods conditions in 2021.Data from the latest IPC report1 shows Lakes State with 55.6% of its population is facing Crisis (P3) or worse with the counties of Cueibet, Rumbek Center, Rumbek East and Yirol East facing Emergency (Phase 4). The data of the projection for the first half of 2021 indicate a worsening situation with up to 57.4% of Lakes State population facing Crises or worse at the end of the second quarter. In Eastern Equatoria State the 41.6% of its population is facing Crisis (P3) or worse with Kapoeta North County facing Emergency (P4) and the projection for 2021 foresees a deterioration indicating that the percentage will increase until 46.2% in the second quarter.As underlined in the HNO of SSD 20212, the main reasons behind the deterioration condition in Lakes include: the currency depreciation related to the macro-economic crisis of the country and the related increase of commodity prices has eroded the purchasing power of already vulnerable households who rely on markets as a source of food; floods of July/October 2020 lead to scarcity of food and intercommunal fights and cattle raids reduced safe access to farming and grazing lands; the poor livestock health and productivity has a negative impact on the coping mechanisms of pastoral communities. Concerning Eastern Equatoria State, as reported in both IPC report1 and in the HNO of SSD 20212, the main drivers of food insecurity are high food prices, unusually heavy rainfall resulting in flooding that caused livestock deaths and destroyed crops, the incidences of fall armyworm (FAW) and desert locusts (DL) that further reduced crop yields. In both areas, the direct and indirect effects of Covid-19 pandemic had severe consequences: on the importation of goods and food from neighboring countries, linked to the increasing cereal deficit of the country; high market prices, as well as increased demand as households depleted their own food stock due to flooding and pests (as DL and FAW).Considering the data reported in the last FSNMS (round 25)3, in Cueibet County Food Consumption Score (FCS) was poor for 36%, while the 81% of the total exhibited a Moderate Household Hunger Score with 22% of the population consuming 0 to 2 food groups and 35% consuming 2 to 4 food groups; 70% of the population adopt Emergency copying strategies. Yirol West county faced the same problems with part of the population displaced in schools and churches. FCS in Yirol West County was poor for 52% of the population, while the 88% of the total exhibited a Moderate Household Hunger Score and almost one third consumed maximum 2 groups of food while 35% consume 2 to 4 food groups and 68% of the population adopt Emergency copying strategies. In Rumbek North FCS was poor for 62%, while the 61% and the 14% of the total exhibited respectively a Moderate and a Severe Household Hunger Score. 73% of Rumbek North residents were consuming not more than two groups of foods (crops and vegetables) and up to 45% of the total admitted to have put in place Emergency copying strategies. In Eastern Equatoria State, the last FSNMS (round 25)3 reported Ikotos and Torit with population with a Poor Food Consumption Score (FCS) of 48% and 30%; the situation of Kapoeta North County is extremely concerning since 35% of the population reported a Household Hunger Score of 4 (Severe) with 88% of the population adopting Emergency Coping Strategies to face the difficult situation. Kapoeta South and East confirm the trend on coping strategies with 87% and 81% of their population adopting Emergency coping strategies as well. On the Households Dietary Diversity (HDDS), 48% consume 3 to 4 groups while in Kapoeta East data show that 54% of the population consume maximum 2 food groups while 37% consume 3 to 4 groups.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The numerous permanent bases that AVSI has throughout the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes; protection help desk points and suggestion boxes for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complain. Moreover, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates, distribution points and community).On the other hand, safety audits are conducted and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Strengthening community, staff and stakeholder’s capacity through PSEA trainings.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

On regularly basis, AVSI conducts community engagement meetings such as awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that allows open participation/discussion over the quality of progress of the activity conducted, several actions for instance field farmers schools and AAPs have built-in system of community monitoring. AVSI embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field. AVSI reports to the FSL state cluster the result of its activities. AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analyzed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind, a follow up interview of key informants, field personnel is conducted to review precisely the barrier that imped a more equitable targeting the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers. This has led to cite some example, at timing the interventions of FSL more in line with the seasonality followed by farmers, and focusing educational support to nurseries and low primary classes.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Yearly, AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and payam officials and traditional leaders (Boma and sub Boma Chiefs). During these events, AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. Last March, for instance, in Ikwoto, along with the results of the 2018 Great Ikwoto Multi Sector Household survey, the need to move toward more development-oriented interventions and thus to apply more demanding criteria for the selection of beneficiaries was discussed with the communities. In that occasion, AVSI received positive feedbacks from the community as well as suggestions regarding operative mechanism to employ for the implementation of projects. AVSI extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, establish a case specific dialog where AVSIs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges/ intervention failure chance. Beside the surveys, AVSI’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings which provided platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported thus increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design. Allow information sharing at village level through creation of local key players as AAP committee members and suggestion boxes and protection help desks service points in the communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beside the annual surveys, AVSI has a tested system of detailed post-distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community at level. During each field visit, AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure initial involvement of women and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and in planning activities implementation; during project implementation will be provided information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women and girls about their needs/safety concerns; Where community workers are going door to door especially during the covid -19 pandemic, the project will ensure CNVs are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathway. Beneficiaries access to information in form communicating complaints and receiving feedback will be enhance through formation of AAP committees at Boma level with representatives from each villages who will be in touch with beneficiaries to receive compliant and feedback. AVSI will meet with the AAP committee to discuss on the complaints and feedback from time to time (Monthly); On quarterly basis there will be a help desks at field sites attended by one personnel from AVSI at a higher position to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Protection sensitive services will as well be mainstream in the project, the activities will include; facilitation of child stimulation and ECD in all nutrition sites, GBV/PSEA awareness to nutrition workers and beneficiaries, referral of GBV cases to access service at the management sites. Likewise, it will be making sure that trainings will target at least an equal number of female and male, that the monitoring will include sex and age disaggregated data, that the project personnel is trained on gender/protection topics including GBV identification, handling and referral.Equal participation will be ensured through the involvement of women and men, equally in decision making right up from induction meetings, and routine focus groups discussions. For instance, Inclusive selection process with set % for specific population components typically victims of inequalities (min 60% of females in project implementation, inclusion of elderly and people with special needs, and returnees). Finally, AVSI operates through numerous permanent bases distributed across the targeted communities and serves an adequate number of service sites equally distributed though out the counties as a means to avoid crowding of beneficiaries per sites, reducing distance to access services, and thus diminish the gap for remote, inaccessible communities.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

In Lakes State where 55.6% of its population is facing Crisis (P3) or worse with the counties of Cueibet, Rumbek North, Rumbek East and Yirol East facing Emergency (Phase 4). The data of the projection for the first half of 2021 indicate a worsening situation with up to 57.4% of Lakes State population facing Crises or worse at the end of the second quarter. Slightly different is the situation in Eastern Equatoria State where 41.6% of its population is facing Crisis (P3) or worse with Kapoeta North County facing Emergency (P4) and the projection for 2021 foresees a deterioration indicating that the percentage will increase until 46.2% in the second quarter. To improve on production and utilization of nutritious locally available food at household for optimal MIYC feeding the project will integrate nutrition to food security component, establishing sustainable solutions as demonstration kitchen gardens at household level, as well as at nutrition sites, schools, institutions (as County, Payam and Boma-based offices). AVSI will continue engaging in cereals large-scale production through progressive farmers’ groups and enhancing the mechanization of cereal production in the targeted areas with common/group farms of above 20 feddan of dimension and supporting in production management and post-harvest methods. AVSI will also facilitate cooking demonstration to enlighten the mothers, fathers and caregivers on how to prepare nutritious foods harvested from the kitchen gardens and encouraged to replicate the same at home. Mothers and caretakers of children with acute malnutrition will be linked to community general food assistant to improve on house hold food security. People with chronic illnesses and mothers of children admitted in SC for management of severe acute malnutrition will be link to institution feeding program to receive food assistance.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Given the fact drivers of malnutrition and food insecurity cuts across multi sectors the project will ensure operational linkages exist with other sectors such as health, WASH and protection through improve coordination and convergence programing. The following activities will be implemented such that the vulnerable targeted groups (food insecure households, malnourished women, children boys and girls) have access to multi sector basic services; Joint health and nutrition education/promotion shall be conducted at all nutrition sites; provision of LLITNS shall be provided to children in OTP programs as a preventive measure of malaria, the project will ensure all children admitted in OTP are tested and treated for malaria, all children identified for medical complication shall be referred for medical care. Malnourished Pregnant mothers accessing treatment at nutrition shall be referred to ANC services. WASH services of enabled access to safe water sources are integrated to the intervention, and kits shall be provided to children admitted in OTP program and all nutrition sites will be equip with hygiene equipment such as hand washing facilities and the project will ensure sanitation facilities exist in all nutrition sites, together with schools and targeted communities.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The following approach shall be used; vulnerable groups PLW and children under will be provided with specialized food supplement through blanket supplementary Programs (BSFP) to prevent malnutrition; the project will emphasis maternal infant and young child nutrition counselling among pregnant, and lactating mothers/caregivers to children under five. Awareness on promoting health/ nutrition especially breastfeeding, hygiene shall be conducted. To optimize feeding practice among PLW and children the project activities will include conducting cooking demonstration to mothers and caregivers accessing services at nutrition site. Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies is very vital in reducing GAM prevalence the project will facilitate vitamin A supplementation campaigns and will refer children with identified deficiency for immediate treatment. The project will ensure access to early screening and timely quality treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition. Other key component that will contribute to reduced GAM prevalence is to ensure effective and efficient programing through strengthened program monitoring, ensuring community full participation and commitment and improve coordination and nutrition information management and utilization.
