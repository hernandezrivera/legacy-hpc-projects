Project Summary

The crisis in north-east Nigeria has severely affected access to basic and social services and livelihoods, including trading and tannery activities. There are currently an estimated 5.5 million people in urgent need of early recovery and livelihoods assistance in 2019. Considerable efforts by the Nigerian Government, humanitarian and development actors have yet to close the huge remaining gaps in humanitarian and recovery needs. For many households, the conflict and subsequent displacement has led to a loss of housing, livelihoods, productive assets, and access to markets. About 86 per cent of households spend more than they earn, and 30 per cent are economically inactive.
According to a Household Survey conducted by the UN Development Programme (UNDP) to assess changes in livelihoods and economic recovery, 7 out of 10 people in the states of Yobe, Borno, Adamawa and Gombe were living below the World Bank extreme-poverty line of less than $1.90 a day, and economic opportunities were severely limited. The situation has only worsened since then. For the past three years, fields have gone unplanted and once-vibrant market places are near empty, with prices of goods skyrocketing to almost 150 per cent higher than in 2016. Insecurity has affected access to basic services, farmlands, markets, and other sources of livelihoods leading to high unemployment rates and low economic participation. The main coping strategies adopted by households include child labor, begging, debt contraction, and reduction in the number of meals per day and sale of assets.
In addition, the current conflict has worsened the pre-existing issues of exclusion and, in some cases, ruptured the bonds and relationships between and within groups and communities. Intra-communal structures and processes that traditionally regulated violence and resolved conflicts weakened. There are signs of social fragmentation as tensions based on ethno-religious, social and other divisions including between IDPs and host communities, are emerging. Borno State has the highest number of IDPs (1.4 million).
This project seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience-building of conflict-affected populations through a series of community support interventions. As people continue return to places of origin, there is an urgent and significant need to restore infrastructure, promote early recovery of economic means of livelihood, and facilitate a robust resilience building process. The proposed livelihood activities aim at strengthening the existing IOM emergency livelihood response. Livelihood Programme interventions will include Cash for Work (CFW), and promotion of livelihoods skills acquisition. Vocational skills training will be organized as per labor market assessments to create human capital, as necessary for the early recovery phase, with a special focus on youth. Each targeted beneficiary will also receive business start-up capital to establish small scale business. CFW and skilled labor schemes will support activities such as comprehensive neighborhood upgrades and light community infrastructure rehabilitation.
Cash based interventions are planned for the project, for accessible areas where this is modality is feasible. These will aim to empower affected populations, promote peaceful co-existence and benefit local economies.
While immediate early recovery needs must be addressed, enhanced coordination and complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization and development partners, through a people-centered approach, is also essential. The provision of technical and logistical support to community peace, and security committees’ operations as well as building the capacity of law enforcement bodies, including traditional/religious leaders, to prevent and respond to GBV incidents in compliance with human rights standards and through a survivor-centered approach, would be part of this endeavor. Additionally, IOM plans to guarantee the participation of women and men as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and age group, and specifically address women and girls specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access concerns. All activities related to this project will equally benefit women and men, girls and boys and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. In regard to assessments, monitoring and evaluation of gender specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible. All activities will be centered on adherence to Do No Harm principles.

Needs

In 2018, one million IDPs remain in host communities while 1.6 million have returned to their Local Government Areas (LGAs) of origin, primarily to areas no longer directly affected by the conflict but also to severely affected LGAs in Borno (Damboa, Konduga, Monguno) Adamawa (Madagali, Mubi North, Hong) and Yobe (Gujba, Potiskum, Fika) states. 1.9 million IDPs as well as returning IDPs continue to place a heavy strain on already vulnerable communities with pre-existing inadequate levels of access to basic infrastructure and services. Data from recent multi-sector needs assessments and Livelihoods and Economic Recovery Assessments present inadequate levels of access to basic services and public infrastructure relative to the size of the population, limited income and livelihood opportunities, and increased resource demands. In many cases, these dynamics lead to societal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities. Of the 5.5 million people in need, 1 million are IDPs in host communities, 1.5 million IDP [and refugee] returnees, and 2.9 million people in host communities . Several LGAs have been prioritized based on the levels of damages and service delivery burden associated with high numbers of returnees and IDPs in host communities: Damboa, Konduga, Monguno (Borno State); Madagali, Mubi North, Hong (Adamawa State); and Gujba, Potiskum and Fika (Yobe State).The severe damages to key public infrastructure and service provision, including LGA government facilities (i.e. LGA offices, schools, clinics, markets) is adding to the long history of marginalization and chronic under-development in the north-east. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, and the elderly, against future vulnerabilities and shocks. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs (whether they choose to return, locally integrate in their place of displacement, or relocate elsewhere in the country) and restoring social cohesion. Without those pre-conditions, a premature returns could lead to secondary displacement, deepen IDPs’ vulnerability, and exacerbate tension and social polarization between IDPs, host communities and returnees. The restoration of key basic infrastructures (especially civil authority including police, the judiciary, education, health, water, sanitation) and housing is critical to laying the foundations for durable solutions. Assessments reveal that 77 per cent of IDPs residing within host communities do not intend to return to their places of origin in the near future: the main reason cited was damage to their houses. Specific measures to restore livelihoods may include short-term employment creation, providing market-based skills, including vocational training, and micro and small enterprise recovery, targeting the recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women, derived from community consultation. This is essential to address negative coping strategies many households have already resorted to, including: borrowing food, buying food on credit, selling of productive assets, borrowing cash from others and spending savings, and protection concerns including early marriage, child recruitment, child labor, trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse. 52 per cent of households reported having no income and resorted to debt contraction while some 30 per cent of households in Mubi North (Borno State) reported begging for money. In addition, areas of return and host communities must be conducive to receive returnees in terms of security (including freedom of movement; safe routes; removal of UXOs/IEDs including from housing, agricultural land and market/trading routes) and the re-establishment of civil governance including the presence of police and judiciary.

Activities

The convergence of the deep social, security, humanitarian and developmental concerns in north-east Nigeria requires actors to adapt and seek more innovative ways of working. Although the scale of humanitarian needs, especially in Borno state, demand relief support in many areas, actors must also recognize and build on opportunities for recovery and reconstruction where they exist. In line with this, the proposed project will actively contribute mainly to Strategic Objectives of HRP 2019:SO 1: Improve Access to Infrastructure and Basic Social Services through Rehabilitation and Reconstruction of Public Service Facilities Activities: • Rehabilitation/Reconstruction of Health Care facilities at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.• Rehabilitation/Reconstruction of Schools at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.• Rehabilitation/Reconstruction of community Centers at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.• Advocacy of communities to health care, education access and services and sustainability of services at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. Above mentioned activities will be carried out through Cash for Work (CFW) approach and as well as through sub contracts. SO 2: Support Livelihood Stabilization and Income RecoveryActivities: • Establishment of Small Medium Enterprises (SMEs) for women’s and youths • Start- Up cash grants for business developments at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.• Income Generation Activities (IGAs) particularly for women’s at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.• Youth engagement in rehabilitation of community infrastructures through cash for work incentives at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.• Conduct vocational skill trainings for marketable skills/jobs at Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. SO 3: Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in Host Communities, Returnees, and Host communities. Activities: • Greater social cohesion between IDPs, returnees and host communities• Peacebuilding initiative through workshops and capacity building• Advocacy workshops on gender rights for women’s and youths SO 4: Support Restoration of Local Governance Administration and Services Activities: • Conduct capacity development workshops for Local Governments for effective coordination and monitoring • Rehabilitation of Vocational Training Centers• Provision of tools and equipment’s to rehabilitated vocational centers• Strengthen local governance capacity to resume the delivery of basic public services;• Rehabilitation/Reconstruction of Police stations Activities will be implemented in Borno state (Damboa and Konduga LGAs), Adamawa state (Mubi North and Madagali LGAs) and Yobe state (Gujba and Potiskum LGAs). Importantly, the proposed activities under this project will complement ongoing activities under IOM’s humanitarian portfolio, which are supporting the provision of shelter, WASH and NFI solutions, psycho-social support, capacity building as well as cash-based support in displacement sites, to reduce vulnerabilities. The project will fill critical gaps by supporting the establishment sustainable solutions for communities affected by the crisis in the north-east.
