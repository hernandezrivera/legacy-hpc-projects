Project Summary

IYD seeks to save lives, uphold rights, and promote access to key institutions and services through providing WASH services in particular. So, IYD in the Turkey office is remotely managing the delivery of integrated WASH assistance with muti humanitarian sectors and advocacy in Idleb. Basic WASH assistance is delivered to the targeted community by taking into account the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls, in addition, taking into consideration the special needs of disabled people in all the project activities, especially during the infrastructure designs.
Therefore, IYD aims to reach 285,000 beneficiaries, 45,000 households (HHs) living in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates in 5 sub-district and 30 camps with basic WASH services including Expansion of water distribution networks and sewerage systems, solid waste removal, Rehabilitation/reactivation of WASH facilities, create a solar system for the water networks, Construction of latrines, showers, and regular emptying of septic tanks, de-sludging latrine septic tanks, solid waste management, garbage bins distribution, an extension of Sanitation network in communities, in addition to solid waste management in 9 communities
The proposed project is targetting communities and IDPs camps as follow:
- five communities are located in (Dana, Atareb, Azaz, Jarablus, and Idleb sub districts ) in the prioritized locations based on the 2022 HRP.
- 30 camps are located in three main sub-district (Idleb, Marrat masrin, and Dana ), IYD has proposed Dana and Jarablus sub-districts to target five camps in significant need of to water and sanitation network, in addition to a renewable system to operate the water station inside the camp.
The project design was done according to SPHERE standards and WASH guidance in line with current HRP priorities.
The planned intervention will mainstream protection principles especially (Do no harm) throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, and older adults. The provision of sanitation services (latrines, washing facilities, etc.) will be consulted with the targeted camps and communities, and take into account the specific needs of each group.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Risk of inter-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served/cannot access or if the information offered is not consistent between communities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly.Mitigation:• Identifying the target community as an accessible area where the needs of the affected population are the highest.• IYD will use existing mapping and coordinate at the local level with other early recovery actors to ensure balanced coverage responding to needs; avoid overlapping or concentration of activities.Risk 2: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff.Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly.Mitigation:• Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish these criteria. • Follow Guidelines adopted by the CFW actors and ensure that they are properly applied in the project location. • Avoid the exclusion from the assistance of persons with no identity documents by adopting alternative mechanisms for identification.Risk 3: The backlash from family or community on women participating in project activities and preventing them from traveling to other areas and accessing services.Likelihood: low. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct•Complete PSEA Checklists: The organizational checklist was be completed by the management and the HR unit in communication with the CEO. Also, the Field Observation Checklist for PSEA Mainstreaming will be completed by IYD's program managers who will utilize these tools in all our interventions during the implementation and monitoring stages to identify gaps and risks related to PSEA


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the project leading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. However, IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge • Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up the service providers day by day, to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them. • Social risks: From a social standpoint, this project presents no risk to the IYD and local communities. Although women are still underrepresented in decision making bodies, their concerns have over time been taken into account, and this project will seek to ensure their better presence and representation. •Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas, and deterioration of the security situation. IYD has a strong relationship with neutral communities’ leaders and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities or IYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety •
