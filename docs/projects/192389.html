Project Summary

The intervention will focus on strengthening the protection environment for GBV survivors and individuals at risk in Beirut and North Lebanon, through GBV prevention activities and response services as well as the provision of capacity-building to national partners and actors, currently implementing multi-sectoral humanitarian and development programs, across the country.
CARE will work jointly with Abaad and the Lebanese Democratic Women Gathering (RDFL) as best placed national actors with a track record of implementing GBV and PSEA activities. Those two actors were selected thank to a thorough selection process, with three different CARE staff + the Partnership Manager, according to a matrix document.
This project will target women and girls, survivors of GBV and/or at high risk, as well as individuals from the most marginalized communities, including vulnerable Lebanese and refugee populations, in addition to migrant communities, who remain a severely underserved group extensively exposed of violence and exploitation. Particular attention will be given to People with Disabilities, ensuring unhindered, accessible and quality services.
The intervention will build on the existing work conducted by CARE and ABAAD and supported by OCHA in 2020 and 2021 through the first LHF standard allocation, in coordination with the PSEA network, as well as the work conducted by CARE under the Empowered Aid project, in partnership with George Washington University’s Global Women Institute and URDA in Lebanon. Coordination with the PSEA network, along with humanitarian actors in the country will be sustained and further enhanced to ensure unified messaging and proper mainstreaming of PSEA across sectors.
Requests and need for capacity-building and system strengthening are still high and relevant, and this intervention will contribute to fill the gap in a comprehensive and sustainable approach.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

In addition to the PSEA component of this project (funded by LHF), project personnel will be trained on PSEA & SEA key messages & reporting mechanisms mainstreamed across activities. Personnel will be trained on gender and protection mainstreaming. Additional G&P and social inclusion considerations include: Inclusion and active participation of persons with disabilities across all activities; “do no harm” approach and strong commitment to protection mainstreaming to mitigate unintended negative outcomes and ensure meaningful, dignified and safe access to aid; community-centered participatory approaches to ensure equal access to aid; RGA findings to inform activity design and implementation; CARE’s gender marker used for any new project to assess the level of gender responsiveness and measures the integration of gender into programming.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

CARE International in Lebanon and partners recognize individuals’ right to voice their opinion, raise complaints, and be involved in shaping our interventions. In humanitarian crises, vulnerable people often find themselves unable to influence decisions that affect their lives. Our commitment to the universally recognized Core Humanitarian Standards includes a commitment to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints. Furthermore, an effective Feedback Accountability Mechanism is central to CARE’s duty of care to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). The system is already in place to safeguard such accountability, while the project will ensure consultation at the community level at all phases. The feedback and complaint mechanism system will be operational in each phase of the project disseminating the channels to beneficiaries, to be able to reach CARE confidentially to ensure confidentiality and appropriate treatment given the high sensitivity often involved.
