Project Summary

This project aims to provide emergency livelihoods support to the most vulnerable people in Sinjar, Ninawa, focusing on youth returnees, the project will achieve its target by the following activities
1) Information sharing on financial literacy and management of micro businesses.
2) asset grants to restore productive assets to support establishment and expansion of micro businesses.
The Sinjar economy has been devastated by the war. The mass displacement of civilians, combined with a shortage of basic services, has created additional aspects of the conflict as resources are drained. This is particularly true Sinjar District, which hosts tens of hundreds of Returnees with vulnerable host community. Thus, Sinjar-D and it suburbs are suffering from livelihoods pressure, lack of schools and social tension.
POINT and its local partner (Humanity organization) developed this project according to the 2017 Minimum Economic Recovery Standards. The aims are to alleviate those pressures, and tensions also supporting the most vulnerable people by providing emergency livelihoods support though restoration of productive assets and employment opportunities.
POINT and its implementing partner Humanity will conduct a needs-assessment to identify the needs of the most vulnerable people in need in Sinjar. We will also hire a business coach to support beneficiaries with assets recovery, with career counseling and help them engage with the private sector.
Information sharing on financial literacy and management of micro businesses.
POINT will organize 15 information sharing workshops for 300 participants. Each workshop will last for 1 week. The workshops will be on different topics to cover the skills for managing micro businesses.
Assets Mini Grants
POINT will identify beneficiaries to receive assets grants to restore livelihood related productive assets, that will contribute to their income. 300 beneficiaries will be awarded an asset mini-grant; each beneficiary be selected based on a selection criterion that will support the most vulnerable people. Assets grants will be awarded on a quarterly basis, for a total of 300 grants.
POINT designed this project to support the recovery of lives and livelihoods in Sinjar-D as a safety net and immediate income for the most vulnerable males and females in Sinjar. with our local partner Humanity that has a wide experience in implanting projects in Sinjar since 2015.

Needs

Point and Humanity have conducted a multi-sector needs assessment in November, 2018in Sinjar district, this needs assessment included Key Informant Interviews in three type offorms, one of them with community leaders to identify the livelihoods, Agriculture andlivestock needs,in addition to general information of the assessed area, the second type ofkey informants was education related key informants like school managers, the third typewas local/community based organization employees to identify weakness points in thecapacity of local Communit Based Organization CBOs and areas of improvement. The data collection was conducted in 5locations, Sinjar, Borek, Dogre, Sinunni and Serdisti Camp, and it showed that thepercentage of returnees is around 50% of total population in average in all five locations. theassessment showed that there is a high damage rate in buildings and infrastructure, thepercentage of damage is as follows: Sinjar (81%) and Dogre (75%) comparing to otherlocations. Main income resources were identified to be activities related to Agriculture andlivestock breeding/herding, however, most assets were stolen by ISIS so farmers andlivestock herders are now unable to secure their livelihoods or sustain any incomegenerating activity anymore. so most people who were relying on such activities are livingnow with no income resource and are relying on humanitarian assistance, however, thereare some that still try to start small business like barbering shops and small markets. Theassessment showed that the average expenditure of households is around 275,000 IraqiDinar (230 USD), but this figure decreases to half in camps. Vocational training and smallbusiness training, the data analyses revealed that there is no vocational training beingconducted in any of the assessed locations except Dogre, the most demanded werebarbering, sewing, carpentry, small businesses like mini market (trading), as for women,they reported the most appropriate vocations were sewing, agriculture, handcrafts, womenbarbering and pastry, for men the assessment showed that there is a need for training onfood processing, and food services, men barbering, carpentry, phone maintenance, sewingand building crafts. The assessment also showed that the percentage of farmers is about50% on average, and the most challenges facing farmers is securing water for irrigation,the absence of Agricultural machinery, and the lack and high prices of agriculture inputs ingeneral, like seeds and fertilizers, in addition to difficulties in products marketing andhaving diseases affecting corps, lack of agriculture pharmacies (inputs shops) in allassessed locations, and one agriculture association were found in Dogre. People ingeneral ask for fertilizers and agriculture extension services. 30 to 40 percent of peopledepend on livestock as a source of income, and almost all kind of livestock are availablein the Sinjar and other surrounding villages, cows, sheep and poultry. The main challengesfacing livestock breeders and herders are the availability of fodder as most of them usefodder and compared to relying on grazing, the other is diseases and pests, finally theavailability of vaccination, All locations have a veterinary center and at least there are twoveterinarians in each location.In Sinjar Center there is a small market that try to cover the daily needs of the people such as fruits, Vegetables or Clothes..etc but the number of shops are not enough to cover the needs, also there is lack of small workshops or small business to cover the daily occupation in the market such as maintaining cars, agriculture support, generators repaires and desserts, While in the Sinjar’s villages such as Sinunni, the market is weak and not exciting the basic needs of people so they forced to travel to Sinjar center around 16KM to can purchase the needs from the market.Most of the organization that are working in Sinjar working in protection, health and child protection but there are lack of funding for project support the livelihoods and markets.

Protection Risk Analysis

main Protection risk to the project are:1- Selection criteria, vulnerability standers: there might some compliance from the beneficiaries and community leaders’ side, and conflict between beneficiaries. 2- Limited number of beneficiaries: a large number of beneficiaries might match the selection criteria but due to the specific number there might be some conflict. 3- Beneficiaries might drop out in the middle of the project or miss use the assets which will be distributed in later stages of the project: due to some personal reasons or lack of their commitment.5- Schedule of implementation of activities for the beneficiaries: beneficiaries might have other important commitments and schedules that will be at the same time of the implementation activities. 6- Women’s access to the project services: because of traditional customs or not having support enough, to attend. And some of the women might be households. 7- Volatility of the security situation, conflict between groups of beneficiaries: especially in a conflict zone as Sinjar that’s been controlled by different political groups, and there might not be similarity in the ideology between the beneficiaries groups.8- Transportation accidents: due to requirement of some of project activities there will be transportation from a place to another place.9- Some of the most vulnerable might be less educated than others: so they may understand in different ways or need help in comprehension some of the subjects.10- Lack of availability of communication in the area: internet connection in some of location might be weak.11- Market capacity in procuring the assets: the assets required may not be available in the area so, they may be needed to be brought from other areas.

Mitigation Measures

POINT and Humanity have experience in successfully delivering various kinds of project despite ongoing conflict. Additionally, we have designed security plans for the team and participants according to different scenarios. Our primary priority is the safety and security of the beneficiaries; therefore, we will conduct safety and security assessments prior to implementation and update the safety plans accordingly. And there will be coordination with local authorities in the communityClear selection criteria and vulnerability criteria standards will be explained to communities and beneficiaries. And accessible accountability mechanism will be used in the project, known communication lines as well. Awareness session will be provided to the beneficiaries, Do no harm Standards will be applied at all stages of implementation and the project life cycle. In order to do that, POINT and Humanity will conduct baseline assessments to ensure that none of the project activities will harm the community in any way, such as by disrespecting the local culture, ethics, customs, and traditions. For example, we will organize the training workshops with particular sensitivity in order to accommodate the needs of both men and women, and to make sure both groups are comfortable. Additionally, POINT and Humanity will conduct security risk assessments and safety and security reports in order to ensure the safety and security of project beneficiaries. We will not risk the safety of the people. When there is a dangerous risk in the community or when we are particularly concerned about the well-being of beneficiaries, activities will be postponed, modified, or canceled accordingly.Furthermore, POINT and Humanity will open offices in the area of project implementation and the offices will be open to the community, and our field staff will follow-up with project participants on a weekly basis in order to mentor them and provide career counseling and coaching, as well as listen to any concerns they might have. And facilitator will be provided during the training Moreover,POINT and humanity will conduct market assessment in begging of the project in order to assess the capacity of market to securing the assets for beneficiaries.

How is your project accountable to affected population

This project was according to the needs of community in Sinjar, who have been adamant in their desire to work and engage in the community. So POINT and Humanity assessed community needs before designing project by asking youth, women, community leader and men for their needs. POINT will involve the target groups (women, men, youth, returnees, and other vulnerable segments of the population) through community engagement efforts, employer mobilization, and we will confirm the projects for CFW and restoring of productive assets.Before implementation, we will announce the project information in the community, and share our selection criteria for the training, mini grant program, and cash-for-work opportunities. We will develop a media campaign on the internet and distribute flyers in the town.POINT and humanity has already has developed a feedback mechanism. We have feedback boxes in our office in Sinjar, as well as a public WhatsApp account in which residents may communicate with at their convenience. These communications will be directly sent to the MEAL team, which will follow-up with them and respond to the sender within two working days. Sinjar residents who do not have access to a smart phone or other internet-based communications devices can submit confidential complaints and feedback at office.For information-sharing and community participation, POINT and Humanity will share project information with employers and mobilize efforts through its cash-for-work program.During the project implementation the MEAL team will conduct PDM after each activity in order to ensure the activity delivered to all the target people and to got the satisfactions from project benef based on gender and age.
