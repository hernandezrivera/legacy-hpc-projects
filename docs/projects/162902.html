Project Summary

Despite the recent positive developments in the establishment of a transitional gov., it is expected that Sudan will continue to face severe macro-economic political challenges. Overall, the situation remains fluid and humanitarian needs will further increase as underlying challenges are not expected to change in the immediate future. The humanitarian situation in Sudan will remain a complex mix of IDPs and IDP returnees. The sustainable solutions for the issues of displaced persons ” are prioritized among the “essential issues for peace negotiations” agreed upon the Constitutional Charter for the 2019 Transitional Period, signed in 17 Aug.19 agreeing on transitional arrangements for the forthcoming 39 months. The aim of the Constitutional Declaration is to “end the war by addressing the roots of the Sudanese problem and handling its effects”.
It envisages the completion of a fair and comprehensive peace in the Sudan no later than 6 months from its signing. As of Nov.19, peace negotiations between the Gov. of Sudan armed groups are ongoing with yet to be determined outcomes. Areas of origin in Sudan have been affected by years of conflict, marginalization and neglect. They often lack basic community infrastructure essential social services, while rural livelihoods were seriously disrupted.
Competition for, and pressures on, natural resources within Darfur, partly due to a history of drought and famine, compound the legacy of the conflict. Communities usually lack resources, structures governance capabilities allowing for different groups, youth in particular, to engage collectively in peacebuilding, dialogue, public service, economic activities. UNHCR will work closely with the gov. on IDP protection and ES/NFI assistance, on safe and dignified vol. return to Sudan, on durable solutions for IDP populations including related peacebuilding initiatives. The main areas of origin include all 5 Darfur, SKS and BNS. Although Sudan has not formally committed to the CRRF yet, the country has de-factor applied several components of the CRRF.
Operational priorities in 2020 are:
1. IDP protection

1 - Needs

Due to the current armed clash in the Jebel Mara area, Central Darfur will continue to witness new displacement. In fact, UNAMID strategic review team from New York has decided to make UNAMID’s Darfur Head Quarters in Zalengie, Central Darfur. Although some locations of UNAMID have been closing and will continue to go through rapid withdrawal, following the extension on the Mandate to one year. It is expected that UNAMID team sites and the two Sector, West Darfur and East Darfur are expected to be closed in mid-2020. As observed in 2018, the conflict in Central Darfur has further drives new displaced population into neighboring states of South Darfur and North Darfur. Even though no specific contingency planning figures are indicated within Sudan’s HNO, in 2018 IOM verified and registered 24,000 newly displaced population. Further to this, there will be locations where humanitarian agencies will newly access for emergency response. As per Sudan’s Emergency Response Framework, newly accessed IDPs will be treated as newly displaced population when designing a response plan. Given new displaced population and newly accessed IDPs are in dire need of Protection and ES-NFIs response, UNHCR will engage in addressing the protection and ES-NFIs unmet needs identified by Area HCT. With Security and land tenure usage situation improving and since the government’s top political priority is closing IDP chapter in Sudan through solutions, large numbers of IDP returnees have been verified and reported in 2018 and will continued in 2019. While 2018 HNO figures indicated that 386,000 IDP individuals have returned home, the authorities in Darfur are reporting over 900,000 returns (not confirmed yet though if these are seasonal or permanent returns). In any event, RRR Sector in support of UNHCR protection Sector, guidance note was endorsed on how return should be verified, registered and essential needs identified. In this part, whenever return is reported, the first initiative is for UNHCR to conduct voluntariness of return verification mission which also consist of assessing safety conditions and if indeed return is permanent. Based on such verification report document, IOM will conduct registration. In an agreement with RRR members and relevant authorities, an inter-agency assessment mission will be carried out to identify protection and essential basic service’s needs. UNHCR will participate in such inter-agency assessment missions to identify protection and Shelter/NFI needs. From the missions conducted by UNHCR in 2018-19 clearly informs that essential services and infrastructures are not in place in majority of the places of return. Specifically, for safety and security issues, there is a strong intervention needed to support rule of law (law enforcement authorities and administration of justice). Further to the needs in strengthening essential services, provision of durable shelters and NFIs needs to be provided for the vulnerable IDP returnees. UNHCR will further work closely with community-based structures and identify projects which compliment in the empowerment and mobilization of such structures to mitigate social protection issues. There are also plans by the government to integrate IDP camp attached to El Fasher and Nyala to be integrated as per the urbanization plan. The IDP camps attached to both cities comprise of an IDP population which is around 200,000 individuals. As indicated in the above section where prioritization is identified, given the return movement and urbanization plan will continue in 2020, UNHCR will engage through the existing inter-agency coordination forum to identify and address unmet needs associated with protection and ES-NFIs. Response to the urbanization planning will primarily constitute in what is agreed with HCT and relevant authorities.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Through this project, UNHCR is seeking to ensure: 1. Conditions created for durable solutions for displaced and returnee population through peacebuilding initiatives, involving close coordination and collaboration with the Peace Commission to be established by the Transitional Government of Sudan; 2. The Durable Solutions Working Group is co-chaired with UNDP and extends necessary support to the field; 3. Necessary contribution is made in leading the Protection, ES/NFI sectors (clusters) within the IASC framework led by the RC/HC in Sudan; 4. The HCT protection strategy is implemented according to the action plan; 5. Promotion of inclusion and participation of communities of concern, particularly women, youth and minorities in all aspects of humanitarian response programming;6. Protection needs assessments and monitoring by protection practitioners to better identify needs and inform a strategic response. This will further be strengthened by improving systematic information collection and updating regularly the Protection Information Management System; 7. Strengthen conflict resolution and peace building skills of youth and women in Darfur, Kordofan and Blue Nile areas to contribute in decisions and peaceful coexistence of their communities; 8. Advocacy initiatives within the humanitarian community and with other stakeholders, notably government authorities and other entities to draw attention to protection issues, inform the response and enhance the protective environment.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Rights and community-based approaches will be at the core of the project activities. Population of concern will be involved during the entire humanitarian program cycle through participatory assessment. During this process, primary consideration will be given to the principle of accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Different segments of affected people will be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The Office will build from its work in 2018 and 2019 to establish a robust PSEA response across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability across the operation through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partners and contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices; and collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to refugees in all locations with an active refugee response. The Office will continue to lead the Protection Sector activities, and will maintain its active participation in- and contributions to other inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network.The project will ensure that activities will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. The project will support humanitarian actors to mainstream protection throughout all activities and to coordinate specialized protection services for affected populations.
