Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen inclusive protection mechanisms for vulnerable groups in conflict-affected districts of Cabo Delgado, Mozambique, including Pemba, Ancuabe, and Montepuez. Targeting people with disabilities, older adults, survivors of torture, detainees, and victims of explosive hazards, the initiative seeks to create an enabling and protective environment by promoting access to information, basic services, and reporting mechanisms.
Guided by protection mainstreaming principles—do no harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and dignity—the project will provide tailored and accessible case management, assess, identify and refer individuals to specialized MHPSS services, including psychological first aid and group counseling, and facilitate accessible referrals. Participatory mechanisms and awareness campaigns on protection risks will empower communities, while barriers to accessing services and information will be identified and addressed.
The action emphasizes disability inclusion, training persons with disabilities as community officials to deliver services, raise awareness, and manage cases. By ensuring accessibility and inclusion, the project advocates for universal access to services and promotes the implementation of inclusive approaches by stakeholders.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Develop, Integrate, or Strengthen Disability-Specific Referral PathwaysThe project will establish and enhance disability-specific referral pathways to ensure that individuals with disabilities, particularly those facing heightened protection risks such as GBV survivors, survivors of torture, and those affected by explosive hazards, can access appropriate services without barriers. Mapping Existing Services: Conduct a comprehensive mapping of available protection, health, legal, and psychosocial services to identify gaps and ensure alignment with the needs of people with disabilities.Creating Disability-Sensitive Protocols: Develop standardized referral protocols tailored to the needs of individuals with disabilities, ensuring that information is provided in accessible formats (e.g., braille, sign language, or easy-to-read materials).Capacity Building for Service Providers: Train service providers on inclusive practices, equipping them to effectively support and communicate with individuals with diverse disabilities and heightened risks.Monitoring and Evaluation of Pathways: Implement mechanisms to continuously monitor and evaluate the efficiency, accessibility, and inclusivity of referral pathways, incorporating feedback from beneficiaries to improve systems.2. Establish Inclusive and Accessible Case Management Sites Located case management sites will be set up to provide holistic support to individuals in vulnerable conditions, with particular attention to people with disabilities. These sites will serve as safe, inclusive, and accessible hubs for service delivery. Physical Accessibility: Ensure that the infrastructure of case management sites meets international accessibility standards, including ramps, handrails, appropriate signage, and accessible toilets.Inclusive Service Provision: Train case managers in inclusive practices, emphasizing the importance of confidentiality, dignity, and cultural sensitivity in addressing the unique needs of vulnerable populations.Integrated Support Services: Offer a range of services, including initial assessments, referrals to specialized services (e.g., MHPSS, health care, legal aid), and follow-up support to ensure individuals receive comprehensive care.Mobile Case Management Units: Deploy mobile teams to reach remote or underserved areas, ensuring that people with disabilities in hard-to-reach communities can access support.3. Conduct Accessible Awareness-Raising Sessions on Protection RisksCommunity-based awareness campaigns will be designed to educate populations about protection risks and promote the rights and protection of people with disabilities, fostering a culture of inclusion and support. Community Engagement Strategies: Use participatory approaches to involve people with disabilities, their families, and local leaders in designing and delivering awareness-raising sessions. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will focus on strengthening community and family support. The objective will be to help people maintain their psychosocial well-being by enabling them to access community and family support, with special attention to people with disabilities and older people. Tailored Messaging: Develop and disseminate protection messages in accessible formats (e.g., sign language, braille, audio, and visual aids) to ensure inclusivity for diverse audiences.Focus on Reporting Mechanisms: Promote understanding and utilization of accessible referral and reporting mechanisms, ensuring that community members know how to report protection risks and seek support.Inclusive Outreach Channels: Use diverse communication platforms, including radio, community theater, and digital media, to reach wide and varied audiences, including marginalized groups.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project is grounded in protection mainstreaming principles, ensuring that interventions prioritize the safety, dignity, and rights of all individuals while avoiding unintended harm. The following considerations and actions will drive the overall intervention: Comprehensive Needs Assessments: The project conducts participatory assessments in collaboration with affected communities to identify protection risks, barriers to accessing services, and vulnerabilities, particularly among people with disabilities, older adults, and survivors of violence.Contextual Analysis: Continuous monitoring of the evolving conflict and displacement dynamics in Cabo Delgado ensures that emerging risks, such as security threats or changes in community power structures, are promptly identified.Inclusion of Marginalized Groups: The project engages vulnerable groups, including women, people with disabilities, and older adults, in identifying risks specific to their contexts, ensuring that overlooked issues—like GBV, social exclusion, or accessibility challenges—are addressed.Do No Harm Approach: The project carefully designs interventions to prevent exacerbating existing vulnerabilities or creating new risks. For example, efforts to involve persons with disabilities in community service delivery are implemented with sensitivity to local cultural dynamics to avoid stigmatization or backlash.Ensuring Meaningful Access: All project activities are tailored to address barriers to participation and service delivery, including physical, informational, and attitudinal barriers. Accessible referral systems and case management ensure that no one is excluded due to disability, gender, or other vulnerabilities. Dignity and Confidentiality: In providing case management and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), the project adheres to strict confidentiality protocols and survivor-centered approaches, particularly for survivors of GBV and trauma. This ensures that individuals are protected from stigma, retaliation, or further harm. Coordination with Local Stakeholders: The project collaborates with local authorities, humanitarian actors, and community groups to align interventions with broader protection strategies, reducing risks of duplication or conflicting actions.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities (PWDs) are at risk of being most excluded from protection mechanisms because of multiple barriers: physical/architectural, attitudinal/socio-cultural, economic, communication/information, organizational and others. Despite the existence of specific disability laws and policies, line Ministries – at central, provincial and district level – are not fully aware of PWDs rights and they don’t have the tools to apply policies. This affects program design which are not inclusive, such as protection services (e.g. CP, GBV prevention and assistance interventions among others).The project is designed specifically to guarantee the access to GBV information and services by men and women with disabilities on equal basis with others. PwDs will be identify with the collaboration of local DPOs. AIFO has a longstanding experience in the field of disability in Mozambique, especially in Cabo Delgado. During 2021, AIFO has been leading numerous capacity building activities in Cabo Delgado, involving local government (district and provincial level), civil society actors and community structures on inclusion of PWDs and Inclusive Planning in different sectors.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will be implemented in the respect of AIFO Environmental and Sustainability Policy, included in AIFO Manual of Operational Policies- Code of Ethics and Conduct 2020. According to the essential points of AIFO Environmental Policy, an environmental assessment will take place and it will consider also gender aspects: women living in rural areas are often more vulnerable to environmental degradation due to inequality in access to land.The project will tackle community preparedness to natural disasters, as the geographic area is exposed to catastrophic natural events, as part of the Disaster Risk Reduction framework. Within this framework, environment assessment elaborated within the catchment area will be taken into consideration.
