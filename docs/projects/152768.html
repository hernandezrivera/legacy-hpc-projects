Project Summary

Since December 2013, former Unity State and in particular southern part has been hit hard by the violence. The records of random killings, rape, displacement, sexual violence and disappearance of girls and women, recruitment of boys into the armed forces and allied militias are key pressing issues. As a result, most of human rights violations took place in this region. People who were exposed and actually witnessed these atrocities being committed are traumatized and that is evidenced in the way they demonstrate violent behaviors and emotional vulnerability. It is against this background that Coalition for Humanity (CH) is seeking to intervene in enhancing preventions and preparedness program to mitigate risks associated with this trauma in the view of strengthening and building resilience of the affected communities in Southern Leer and with emphasis to Panyijiar County of former Unity State where service providers are limited both in capacity and scope. Coalition for Humanity (CH) aims to work in social aspects such as enhancing Psychosocial Support (PSS) tailored toward counseling those members of the communities who are identified to be severely affected and making referral pathways where appropriate that can be achieved in partnership with other partners including our own protection project and GIZ conflict transformation projects having the same or similar mandates. CH has strong presence in these hotspots and has already established Youth and community Friendly spaces in some strategic points in the two locations but plans are underway to reach more difficult-to-reach areas especially in Islands South of Leer and Payinjiar County where people are returning as result of relative peace. Hence there is need for robust presence and capacity to cope with this overwhelming humanitarian situation in the areas. The project shall target total population of 8,000 out of total of 198,784, which is the overall population for the two counties (Leer 119,954 and Payinjiar at 78,830. Source: IPC 2018). Out of the target population of 8,000 beneficiaries, of these 5,000 are children boys and girls aged 10-18 and 3000 are adults aged between 18-59. This break down is general but specific focus shall be placed on youth, adolescents and adults affected by the conflict. This will include the host community, IDPs and returnees. Local authorities and government officials will also benefit from these activities including local law enforcement agents, traditional leaders who aids in legal and other referral pathways. Based on the indicators we have on the ground, members of these groups are severely exposed to actual fight especially the youth group who at times physically participated in the act of violence. CH will engage these youth in a view to mitigating these harmful impacts of the conflict and change their mind-sets since peace is gradually returning to the areas. CH as an indigenous non-governmental organization is well placed to address, together with our partners, most of these humanitarian challenges envisaged herein if funding is availed. CH has a strong presence in most of the potential returning areas of IDPs and returnees in particular Islands South of Leer and Payinjiar being the areas highly affected and anticipated for returns. Through local coordination mechanisms in the field as well as in the cluster levels both in state and national, efforts will be directed toward harmonization and collaboration to avoid duplication of work among partners. CH through the support of other partners shall activate or re-energize local coordination mechanisms in Southern Leer and in Payinjiar County where these have lagged. CH is working in collaboration with MEDAIR and planned to take over Behavioral change communication (BCC) project in key islands of Kok,Rubchai, Tochriah.This shall be integrated with this protection project together with our health, WASH and livelihood projects covering Payinjiar and Southern Leer.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had negative impacts on the entire population with particular adverse impact experienced by community and children. Since the beginning of the current conflict in 2013, millions have been displaced internally with hundreds of thousands (majority of them women and children) crossing the border as refugees. Leer County has been the epicenter of conflict that forced most of population to seek safety and protection in many islands South of Leer town with big number reaching Panyijiar County as IDPs. Payinjiar County has been on the receiving end with huge displaced persons still residing there. This has weakened the community coping mechanisms when it comes to protection issues. Much focus was placed on other hotspots where fighting continued without attention to relatively safe locations where IDPS have sought safety. On several occasions, different locations in the central unity especially in Leer have changed control between government and the opposition forces causing huge displacement and suffering to vulnerable communities especially community, girls and children. This huge population displacement spilled to Payinijiar County with little service providers to handle the increasing needs. With the declaration of famine in Southern Unity in 2017 and with pockets of areas still under famine or catastrophe phase 5 according to IPC 2018,the situation need intervention. The prolonged fighting which led to displacement of communities to islands that has put all the communities in psychological trauma has resulted in so many causalities with massive displacements leaving most community and children in highest despair and need of protection. Leer, and Payinjiar have a combined estimated population of over 198,784(IPC 2018). Over 60% of that population of all ages is in dire situation and urgent needs of psychosocial support. The on-going conflict has caused insecurity and has impacted immensely and therefore many families became vulnerable and are unable to access basic services. In former Unity state Coalition for Humanity (CH) in partnership with Care and DRC IPMT members has conducted series of assessments and intervention in hard to reach areas including islands targeting population of over (5000 PSN) found to be in critical need of protection and general humanitarian assistance. The Protection Cluster’s mapping results had also agreed with the situation findings and identified critical gaps of needs. Among the related gaps issues are the capacities among the affected communities and the coping mechanism to ensure strong resilience of vulnerable people to access to sustainable services. The recruitment of child labour has also led to abuse and exploitation where girls are suffering from violence, sexual harassment and early or forced marriages leading to the teenage pregnancy, unsafe abortion or/and even death. The absence of life-skills (lack of awareness and knowledge about sexual reproductive health) among boys and girls has contributed to the above-indicated challenges. In Leer and Panyiiar Counties are the most ranked prioritized areas in need of humanitarian support in former Unity State in 2019. Coalition for Humanity (CH) is working in collaboration with MEDAIR and planned to take over Behavioral change communication (BCC) project in key islands of Kok,Rubchai, Tochriah. This shall be integrated with this protection project together with our health, WASH and Food Security and livelihood projects covering Payinjiar and Southern LeerThrough local coordination mechanisms in the field as well as in the cluster levels both in state and national, efforts will be directed toward harmonization and collaboration to avoid duplication of work among partners. Coalition for Humanity (CH) through the support of other partners shall activate or re-energize local coordination mechanisms in Southern Leer and in Payinjiar County where these have lagged.

Activities

Coalition for Humanity (CH) as a National Non-governmental Organization that has strong presence in Southern Unity will undertake the following activities: • Establish multi-purpose youth and community friendly spaces in the following areas; in Payinjiar County (Pacienjok, Tayar port, Pachak Tiab, Kol and Kanynhial). In Southern Leer priority shall be placed in (Meer, Adok, Rubchai, Kok, Pilieny and Yang). Areas earmarked in Payinjiar and Southern Leer are those with IDPs population as well as the host community who fled violence in Leer and Mayendit Counties with plan to target other potential areas of return. The purpose of these recreational centres would be to provide on- locations counseling and rehabilitation support to the most affected members of the communities in the above-mentioned locations. • Build the capacity of 40 psychosocial support facilitators (20 per each county) in nine centers across two counties in Southern Leer and Panyijiar, two (2) PSS Facilitators per each youth and women friendly spaces. Coalition for Humanity (CH) has already established service mapping in Payinjiar and will work in partnership with IRC and ICRC which are the main humanitarian partners with protection mandate in the area to avoid duplication of service. Coalition for Humanity (CH) leveraging on its national character (NNGO) and its membership at Call to Action on Protection from Gender-Based Violence in Emergencies shall work as frontline service provider who will engage the communities on day-to-day basis and create referral pathways for onward support by other partners for maximum benefits to beneficiaries especially in Payinjiar and Southern Leer. • Through the help of these PSS Facilitators, CH will create awareness and advocacy on protection risks and peace building messages building on its conflict transformation project funded by GIZ targeting similar areas. Counseling and referrals will be undertaken within these facilities as well. Coalition for Humanity (CH) shall carry out advocacy and awareness creation on harmful cultural and biased practices against women and girls such as early and forced marriages, intimate partners (IPV) violence and other forms of domestic violence. The aim is to impact positively on social mind-set of the community for the better. • Offer Psychosocial services to victims affected by the conflict and make referral pathway where appropriate. Training of community leaders and local government officials that include community, girls, boys in a view to give them orientation about protection issues and how best they can prevent them through their local law enforcement structure mechanism available to them. • Integrate and mainstreaming protection into community traditional justice system. • Build capacity of the vulnerable community members in order to help cope up with the shocks and enhance resilience. • Establish and implement internal institutional policies, standards, and practices that drive action and accountability on protection and gender equality in humanitarian service delivery as linked to our commitment to Call to Action on protection in emergency. • Incorporating community’s cultural norms to eliminate issues fueling domestic violence issues. • Involve community in decision-making in Alternative Community Dispute Resolution, conflict mitigation, mediation & peace building • Advocate for the rights of IDPs, returnees and the host communities on the rights to land acquisition and land ownership in line with prevailing legal framework (HLP) Coalition for Humanity believes that fair distribution of resources will help in mitigating conflicts and in achieving social justice for communities.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

CH’s one of its core values is accountability. Thus CH looks forward to being accountable to its beneficiaries, children boys and girls, men and community who are vulnerable as a result of the on-going conflict and aims at providing lifesaving-services and CH places the responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. CH is actively committed as an organization to use power responsibly and being held to account by the people it serves. CH designs and set feedback mechanisms in its program design, delivery and lesson learning. The project will increase the participation of communities giving equal opportunities to boys, girls, community and men in Leer, Mayendit and Koch counties to shape their own recovery. CH will endeavour to deliver against its commitments to stakeholders, including the people it serves. CH will ensure effective and accessible information sharing and communication channels timely. This will put communities in a position to understand and shape decisions that impact their lives. CH will use focus group discussion (FGD) as a method of participation that gives a voice to beneficiaries to air their concerns and brainstorm on the effective way to approach in problem solving. Through community structures monthly meeting, feedback from the community will be received and comments put in consideration for the betterment of the affected groups, boys, girls, community and men.
