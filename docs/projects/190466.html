Project Summary

Purpose of the intervention is to support the communities in rebuilding 100 primary schools. This makes to restore access to basic education services via constructing classrooms, libraries, laboratories and pedagogical centers & to benefit for 242,839 students of 100 primary schools. The intervention is to rehabilitate the quality of teaching and learning process, provide safe learning environment of schools. The project ultimately impacted to increase 30 % of gross enrollment rate (GER) of students via providing access of primary school education as well as reduce 25% dropout and repetition rate of students at 100 primary schools in 2023 academic year.
According to the official outcome of the damage assessments conducted by Regional Bureau of Education, 575 schools are completely destroyed and more than 50% of the total schools partially damaged, of which 100 primary schools at 10 Woreda are among the worst affected ones with up to 95% of their academic buildings, administration block and pedagogical center reduced to rubble. The report also stated that; 80% of all classrooms in primary schools are either destroyed or unsafe to learn. This means that over 3,900 classrooms need to be rebuilt. Assessment findings on the percentage of school damaged or destroyed range from 5% to 100%.
The recent assessment report of the regional education bureau shows that 575 schools with 3,796 (100%) of their classrooms, 577 schools with 716 (100%) of their administration blocks, 479 schools with 764(100%) of their pedagogical centers, 449 schools with 835 (100%) of their library blocks and 379 schools with 526 (100%) of their laboratory blocks are categorized as unsafe. 100 primary schools (24%) out of 575 schools are identified as priorities for intervention, which directly affected around 390,000 children, who have still become out of school.
Following the existence of relative peace in majority areas of Tigrai, schools have been reopened in the safest and securest parts of Tigrai with regards to war where students are supposed to learn in non-classroom environment-open and hazardous areas sitting on stones under trees.
TDA now seeks funding to rebuild the 100 damaged primary schools in order to further extend the positive impact of these schools. The buildings will have all necessary facilities such as furniture, latrine, fence, water, playground, library and pedagogical center that can facilitate favorable and stable learning conditions.
The main target group is the most affected students by the crisis, hunger and poverty in the project areas. Initially, the project is targeting a total of 244,689 with the possibility to increase the number of students in the future. Once the structures are fully in place and funds are available, the project will also include girl-child. Direct target are totally 244,689; 242,839 pupils, 1,350 teachers and 300 office staffs). Indirect target 489,378 community members in 10 Woredas and 100 school community members. To be started on 2022, and is expected to be finalized on 2023.

1. - Projects activities

Selection of Contractor for Construction Work: TDA will select potential contractors through open bidding advertisements. Recruit Skilled Manpower and Volunteers: TDA will recruit project staffs and skilled manpower through advertisement. Formation of Project Implementation Committee: to have sufficient involvement & participation of local community stakeholders and government officials to increase participation in project implementation. Provide Learning Resources for Students: through local bidding procurement unit will make a list of materials and procure all materials at a time. Provide; library books, materials and equipment for co-curricular activities and strengthen student reading clubs. Constructing School’s Physical Facilities: Construction works will be begun based on the design presented by engineer. In each school, new academic block (classrooms), new administrative block (staff resource center, offices and stores), new pedagogical block (library and laboratory and science lab) and new latrines will be built. TDA will select 100 schools recommended by REB with a proportionate in Southern (12 schools), Eastern (24 schools), central (32 schools) South Eastern( 9 schools) and NW (23 schools) zones. Provision of Furniture and Supplies: In addition to rebuilding, each school will need furniture and supplies. Needs could be varied from school to school depending on the magnitude of the damage within the school. 100 primary schools will be furnished with basic school supplies to academic block, administrative, library, girls’ club and pedagogical centers.Improving Quality of Teaching: A package of training modules on areas of best teaching practices on method of teaching; classroom follow up and one-on-one support; strengthening teachers’ professional learning communities will be developed. Manuals will be developed and printed for each training sessions. All teachers will attend training at school level by trained teachers from the same school. Provide Training to School Management Committees: to strengthen the capacities of School Management Committee (SMC) representatives for planning and strategies development with a view to encourage them to raise their level of commitments towards quality education. Handover to Community: TDA will have a plan to organize a handover program for the completed projects to community and Woreda administration. The local stakeholders, school management committee members, Donor representative, political leaders and Woreda representatives will be involved in the handover program.Prepare and Submit Report as soon as the Project is completed: TDA is responsible to develop progress and accomplishment report with its output and to timely submit them to the partner organization.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The day to day monitoring of its progress will be closely carried out by the school committee through the project coordinator in consultation with Education Bureau and the beneficiary community members to ensure the attainment of the project goal. At every level progress report will be sent to all concerned bodies in line with agreements for transparency and accountability.The TDA construction directorate will also assess the progress of the project through periodic review meetings with the project staff. Besides, the directorate will conduct periodic evaluation of the various parameters assessing the progress of the project in terms of various qualitative as well as quantitative indicators. A team of engineers from TDA based in the targeted governorates will conduct regular visits to the project sites and sign off payment requests from contractors against work progress. Besides this, Woreda construction and construction committee team will also hold site visits to verify progress and ensure quality of implementation before payments are made. A team of engineers from the TDA as well as project experts, regional education bureau expert and procurement officer will inspect furniture items prior to delivery. Engineers’ monitoring reports will have to indicate that all rehabilitation work be done properly as per the BoQs with added quality in the schools implemented in partnership with local committee and government bodies. At the culmination of each teacher training workshop, teachers will be required to set action plans and fix timelines for employing the new instructional approaches in their classrooms. TDA’s experts and the school’s principals and supervisor will use the action plans for monitoring and implementation of the new approaches by trained teachers and provide tailored support to those struggling to implement the new instructional strategies.
