Project Summary

According to FSNAU-FEWSNET Sept release Somalia remains one of the most complex and long-standing humanitarian crises in the world. 2018 has seen some improvements in the food security outlook, mainly due to the above-average Gu rainfall and sustained humanitarian response. However, such gains are fragile, serious protection concerns persist and humanitarian needs in Somalia remain high. Climatic shocks, armed conflict and violence are key drivers of humanitarian needs and human rights violations. One third of the total population, or 4.2 million people, require humanitarian assistance and protection. Along with humanitarian action, substantial investment in resilience-building and development solutions will be critical to ultimately reduce humanitarian needs in Somalia
OCHA Somalia predicts an estimated 2.2 million people are expected to experience Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes through September and are urgently in need of humanitarian food assistance and interventions to prevent high levels of acute malnutrition. This number could increase as the dry season progresses. In north-central pastoral areas, where most households are still recovering from the extended impact of the 2016/17 severe drought, food security outcomes are driven by stagnated or declining herd sizes. In areas of greatest concern, poor households have maximized debt and credit and are unwilling or unable to engage in extreme depletion of productive livestock assets. As a result, they are expected to have large food deficits reflected by heightened acute malnutrition and excess mortality risk through October, when current livestock conceptions lead to herd size increases. Climatic shocks have also led to the increased severity of needs in areas affected by post-Gu flooding and extended periods of drought within Mudug and Nugaal regions.
The aim of the project is to help deal with climate-related shocks and stresses, threaten people and communities compounding existing poverty and vulnerability and undermining long-term development, encourage communities that have previously relied upon one or two rainfed harvests of staple food and to diversify with a variety of crops with more frequent harvests thru solar panel irrigation system horticulture production. Diversification activities along with training and awareness raising activities on early warning mechanisms, water retention, reducing soil erosion, improving soil fertility and planting trees that can lower temperatures, to allow other crops to be grown nearby and of course some varieties that produce fruits to improve HH nutrition.
The project will be anchored on a social protection concept (SP) to help beneficiaries anticipate and cope with climate-related shocks and stresses, such as droughts the outcome being to gain long term resilience and sustainable outcomes. KDO has paid attention to gender roles and relations between women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities in particular, how they affect intra-household decision making and bargaining, time poverty, and women’s reproductive unpaid care work. KDO is informed by the fact that HH are not homogenous units, but are sites of ‘bargaining’, in which women and men have different abilities to decide and control how the transfers are pooled back into the household.

Project Activities

• Improve 250HH’ immediate access to food for 4 months through provision of conditional cash transfers @100 USD per month (1500 individuals) through severity and vulnerability analysis of target HH, gender, age, disability and willingness to participate in livelihood diversification for food and income, natural resource management, sinking of 15 shallow wells for solar irrigation and 3 community dams.• Target and provide 250HH with unconditional transfers @ 100 USD for 3 months. The CT will be dispensed on a MEB basis and the targeting criteria will include gender, disability, age, and vulnerability analysis of target HH• Establish, restore community-driven afforestation nursery, natural resource management, disaster risk management, and preparedness and training• Provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs (seeds and tools) through Cash vouchers and technical support to 18,500 host communities and 15,650 IDPs target beneficiaries(5,691 HH)Markets in the project target areas are functional, through structured dialogue with the community beneficiaries they indicated that they would prefer Cash transfers, both conditional, unconditional and voucher system modalities as this helps them purchase what they need most and through this transfers, local economy growth is enhanced since the money is spent locally within their village markets. they also cited mobile money transfers as efficient timely and easy to monitor since the transfer is always accompanied by a deliver message which has helped minimize malpractices and other logistical issues associated with relief, in summary, they reported high efficiency in the mobile-based cash transfers.KDO will coordinate and collaborate with the CWG for guidance during implementation of the cash-based interventions.

Mitigation Measures

Community-based approach – The participation of the displaced and the host community will be ensured by the targeting criteria which will include gender, disability, age, and vulnerability analysis of target HH who will be drawn from HH in IPC phase 3 and 4, essential to create a sense of ownership and ensure that activities are adapted to the local cultural and social context, Local community resources will be identified, mobilized and used to implement the project. The community will participate in the design, implementation and monitoring of activities and form part of the decision-making process at all stages. KDO will liaise with local authorities local leaders, national and international aid agencies to have access to displaced populations and obtain first-hand information on the overall conditions and most pressing protection gaps to avoid duplication of activities and risks of exclusion based on displacement. Sensitization that the cash transfers/vouchers provided is for the family groups and not only for the person whose name is being registered for distribution. At a minimum, registration will be based on family groups and will include the names of all male and female adults in the family. All data will be disaggregated by age, sex, place of origin, and background. Please provide further explanation

KDO was established in the year 1994 with the aim of improving Food Security and Social development in Nugal region. KDO has experience in the implementation of livelihood projects in PL state. The NGO is well known in Nugaal region as an active partner of WFP in the implementation of food security and livelihood projects and has high reputation. Since 2009 KDO has supported 68,086 households in difference phases of relief intervention our on-the-ground presence gives us a base in places of high priority in terms of need our ability to facilitate the various stakeholders to ensure greatest impact on the ground. This facilitation is a key component in how we operate as an organization working in partnership with the target communities to plan projects that have long term durable solutions to enhance HH resilience to future shocks.KDO will in this particular project ensure to provide general guidance on the process and conditions necessary for achieving a durable solution; and assist in determining to what extent a durable solution has been achieved through the creation and restoration of sustainable community and HH assets, by opening up of HH farms that will cultivate drought tolerant early maturing crops, community afforestation and fruit tree programmes for food and income and combat climate change and desertification, training and awareness raising activities on early warning mechanisms, water retention, reducing soil erosion, improving soil fertility providing cash conditional and unconditional cash transfers community works employment during lean season’, as an alternative to poor households having to sell their productive assets or take their children out of school to buy food, Communities group coping mechanisms and solidarity networks to mitigate the impact of food shortages encouraged and thrive enhancing durable solutions and resilience.
