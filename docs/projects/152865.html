Project Summary

With the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in September 2018 and the celebration of Peace in the capital of South Sudan in October 2018, many South Sudanese are hopeful that this peace will hold and several others who have been displaced by the conflict are expected to return. However it was acknowledged by the president that there will still be pockets of individuals/groups who may continue armed conflict of whom the government reported, will reach out to include in the peace implementation. These groups may cause more displacements in those areas.
Though calm is expected to return to most parts of South Sudan, the devastating effects of the 5 year conflict will still linger. Homes, property and livelihoods destroyed, lives lost, people displaced. The impact of the conflict has resulted in country-wide economic and humanitarian crisis which has exacerbated living conditions of those most affected. There is prevalence of deaths due to hunger and curable/preventable diseases, increased crimes and insecurity (looting, stealing, robbing, road ambushes), cattle stealing and raiding has become more frequent because of increased poverty resulting in deaths and displacements. Protection risks and negative copying mechanisms are rising; prostitution and survival sex, early girl-child marriages, bride price increment that results into increased promiscuity among girls and young men, exposure to STDs and sexual and gender-based violence, exploitation and abuse.
South Sudan IPC Analysis released September 2018 puts areas Jonglei and Lakes at crisis or emergency level (Phase 3-4) just one level below famine with prediction that it will get worse especially in Pibor, Twic East and Awerial between January and March 2019. Whenever there is high food insecurity as predicted by IPC, in order to survive, conflicted affect persons will sell their belongings to cope with food shortage. People selling their belonging have been seen in these areas thus greatly increasing shelter and NFI needs.

HDC plans to action S/NFI Cluster strategies and objectives (improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement, improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs and support most vulnerable returnees, host communities and IDPs in secondary displacement rebuild lives through coordinated, integrated and targeted shelter and NFIs solutions.) by; providing access to emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to most vulnerable persons in the newly and secondary internally displaced populations in Pibor and Uror; upgrade shelters and replenish essential NFIs and emergency shelters for Protracted IDPs, returnees, HC and people in secondary displacement in Awerial, Bor, Twic East, Duk and Juba; monitor quality of service provided and mainstream protection (especially GBV), HLP and conflict sensitivity approaches in all stages of response.
These objectives can be achieved since HDC is a well established National NGO static in 8 out of 10 former states with current and previous S/NFI projects in Pibor, Bor, Duk and Uror and livelihood and/or protection projects in those other target locations for HDC in 2019. HDC is currently a sub-national co-coordinator for Protection Cluster in Jonglei and focal point for S/NFI Cluster in Awerial – Minkaman. Coordination is key in any response especially in S/NFI and HDC values this and continues to seek for ways and opportunities to improve.
Over the years we have worked well with various international and national NGOs like INTERSOS, NRC, PAH, NCA, SSRC, Concern Worldwide, World Concern, Medair, ADA, TOCH in S/NFI interventions and are now well acquainted with cluster strategies and methodologies of response. As outcome of this, we have been part of the tasks teams to review cluster tools to incorporate protection and AAP aspects a thing that has subsequently built on and strengthened our capacity to deliver S/NFI projects.
HDC’s wide coverage and strong staff helps cushion certain risks – financial, human resource, logistics, etc that can easily derail normal functioning of an organisation to effectively and efficiently deliver to the communities they serve.
In Uror, HDC is exploring an initiative to build on unity and peaceful co-existence by working with its fellow NNGOs. Our believe is that when these conflict affected communities see national staffs working together despite their different ethnities, it will influence them to embrace unity and build peaceful co-existence.

Needs

HDC through analysis of reports from other humanitarian organisations (South Sudan IPC Analysis Sept 2018, South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot July 2018, Unicef South Sudan Humanitarian Situation April 2018), use of its local knowledge as a national NGO and with its static presence in Central Equatoria (Juba – Mangateen newly displaced IDP site, Mahad IDP site, Don Bosco IDP site), Lakes (Awerial - Minkaman) and Jonglei (Pibor, Duk, Bor, Twic East, Uror) has found newly and secondary displaced, protracted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities who are the most conflict affect groups, in need of the following; ShelterProtracted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community live in poor shelters covered with old plastic sheets and rags that; leak during rainy seasons exposing them to cold and diseases like pneumonia, theft and protection risks as they are not protective enough. The limited number of shelters for each family has made some family members especially young teenage girls and boys seek accommodation from neighbours exposing them to early pregnancies. Parents lose control over their young boys as they get exposed to wrong peers, alcoholism, drugs, violence as they get perception of false maturity.The poor shelter structures, normally small in size and to average child’s height, reduces the dignity and self esteemed depriving them of their privacy as well. During the day they become hot and cannot protect the family form the scorching sun.The newly or secondary displaced with lack of shelter sleep under trees exposed to all the protection and health risks. Non-food itemsThe lack of adequate blankets and mosquito nets expose some family members to the cold and diseases such as malaria.Women and girls in particular of concern lack proper dignified clothing which reduces their dignity and exposes them to sexual exploitation and abuseLack of torches to use during the night has exposed women and girls to protection risks like SGBVWomen wait for their neighbours to first finish cooking in order to then cook for their family if they have any to cook. This negative mechanism of borrow has got a negative impact on the health and dignity of the family members – ulcers, disrespected.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

At the onset of the project consultations and introduction of the project will be done with the community through meetings and focused group discussions. During the meetings, the community will be asked to select, 3 gender represented community focal persons CFPs – should consist of a woman, man and a youth. They will then thereafter be actively and directly engaged with project staff acting as a link between the community and project staff. Providing complaints and giving feedback whenever appropriate to the community.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages will be context specific and also tailored to individual needs. The most vulnerable will directly be informed and assisted on how they can access provisions. Focused group discussions will be segregated according to age and gender with same gender staff handling each group to ensure confidentiality. At extreme levels of GBV individual interviews and referrals will be made to experts, protection cluster, who can handle the issue.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The assessment tools and individuals used during the project implementation have GBV at the centre. Protection staff will be used to assess and address GBV cases. Staff members will be advised to refrain from any forms of sexual exploitation and abuse and also be ready to report such cases through the established whistle blowing mechanism as HDC. Beneficiaries will be informed to report such cases through Field Officer or Officer phone that will be shared with the beneficiaries should there by mobile network.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Focused group discussions will be to the community while leaders will be considered during key informant interviews that may be done with other vulnerable individuals like the disabled. CFPs will also ensure that consultations reach far beyond the leaders and any other barriers that may exist within the community.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Rapid monitoring will be conducted during and after distributions to capture, report and give feedback on community complaints and concerns. The CFPs will be considered the primary source of receiving complaints and giving feedback as they are part of the community. The M&E Officer will also be available to receive and give feedback and train field staff.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

During the project cycle, HDC will work together with at least 3 gender-represented community focal persons during the project cycle. This group will also assist the community outreach volunteers in mobilization and quick dissemination of information and help clarify any misunderstandings that may arise because of the information gap. Once every major activity is completed, a meeting with the target community will be held with good representation from target beneficiaries to get their feedback and complaints on how the exercise. This will help the field team to quickly make any necessary adjustments and also be able to erase any misunderstandings as the process and the principles involved are reiterated. This initiative complements the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism desk that will be established to mainstream protection and gender and also monitor and evaluate the project progress.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Before the start of the intervention, protection cluster will be consulted through S/NFI cluster coordinators to analyse and advice on feasibility of response.Once endorsed, HDC together with other partners, will lead its intervention through a protection experienced Field and M&E Officers, one or more static protection staff and S/NFI field assistants to firstly conduct safety audits to analyse further any protection risks. During this audit different ages and genders are consulted separately (by same gender staff whenever possible) including persons living with disabilities and minority groups through focused group discussions, household and key informant interviews. The team will use cluster provided tools on protection to mainstream it throughout the project cycle; GBV and protection mainstreaming guide, safety audit questionnaire, GBV S/NFI checklist, HLP and conflict sensitivity assessment,

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

During the assessments and analysis that can be done in collaboration with other partners, issues of meaningful access are highly pronounced to guarantee all groups are assisted equitably. To ensure that any barriers to access are mitigated or eliminated, the team will consult with local authorities as a first measure to mitigate risks and build rapport to facilitate smooth implementation. Individual groups of women, girls, boys, men and disabled will be consulted collectively and individually on how they can access assistance including information and their personal safety in correlation to receiving support.Our use of local staff knowledge as well as CFPs will be of an advantage in the process as they already understand the context especially on how best to pass information, how they assist the most vulnerable access assistance.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The use of CFPs that is gender representative (women, men, youth) supported by volunteers will facilitate the process of including all groups that are in need. Focused group discussions and Key informant interviews with leaders as well as other individuals; persons with specific needs is typically instilled to overcome issues of inequality.Selection of CFPs and other workers in the group will follow set criteria in consultation with the community. Active participation of the community at all levels; women and girls can collect water, grass, boys and men are involved in construction, etc.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The selection of beneficiaries will be done through set criteria and verified by the project committee that includes CFPs, and field staff. The use of CFPs who gender representative helps to ensure all groups are included. During assessment, analysis on protection threats and risks will be done like appropriate distribution points. The active community engage throughout the project cycle; FGDs, KIIs and use of CFMs to consult with the community and also address issues helps address issues of inequality and protection threats.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Project monitoring will be done in two forms; during activities and after activities with both measuring meaningful access of assistance by beneficiaries. During distributions for example, protection staff with CFPs will be assigned to ensure there are 3 lines – for most vulnerable, females and males. They should also ensure that the distribution site is located close to the beneficiaries and that no risks are involved in trying to access the location especially to women and the disabled and including children. During PDMs particular questions on how safe the beneficiary was able to access the items will be asked. Were they able to access the assistance, did they have any barriers that could not allow them access assistance or distribution site?

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

During monitoring key factors to consider in measuring safety will be safety for beneficiaries to travel on foot to receive items, presence of physical violence like fighting, any fire hazards, any hotspots that may be present around distribution site or presence of armed actors. During rapid monitoring, beneficiaries will randomly be chosen and asked such relevant questions while the team observes any other incidences and use CFPs and local authorities to ensure safety of beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The key issues/questions to consider will be how the staff treated the beneficiaries. Such related questions will asked by ME Officer during rapid monitoring and PDM to ensure that the beneficiaries are not harassed in any way by staff and also by other community members in position of authority.
