Project Summary

Proposed action aims at targeting 180,000 people (30,000 households) comprised of 30000 men, 30000 women, 60000 girls and 60000 boys is in Unity, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Upper Nile States and Pibor County.
Proposed actions are in line with the FSLC Medium Term Strategy (2024-2026); with proposed actions supporting vulnerable agro-pastoral households including returnees and refugees-including people living with disabilities. Through joint prioritization of acute needs and via the triple nexus approach that responds to acute needs while promoting resilience and development in a peaceful environment.
Proposed actions will guided by a food systems approach by promoting agriculture, livestock and fishing value chains including private sector actors linkages that will foster sustainability. Through private sector led where feasible including start-up and scale up of vegetable, crop, fishing and livestock sector including marketing access. These will be tailored with capacity building initiatives across different value chains to enhance extension services provision, production, sustainability and market access.
Proposed action will ensure quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1. Needs

Climatic extremes, which have in the recent past been characterized by unprecedented flooding that has affected at least 1.4 million people in 43 counties, coupled with escalating Sudan conflict with increasing influx of refugees, returnees and asylum seekers crossing into South Sudan and deteriorating local currency and high food prices have continued to further increase food insecurity across Unity, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Upper Nile States and Pibor County. At least 7.69 million people are projected to face IPC Phase 3 and above including 63,000 projected famine. Current flooding extremes have have had consequent implications on multiple displacements of people, limited grazing land and pastures with prolonged congregation of livestock in marsh lands with consequent impacts on increased livestock disease outbreaks/ mortalities. Households have been resorting to distressed sale of livestock for fear of disease outbreaks/decimation. The 2024 VSF livestock flood and livestock disease assessments have indicated increasing livestock disease outbreaks more so in flood prone areas like Aweil East, Rubkona, Panyijiar among others with livestock disease incidences like Rabies, Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia, Anthrax, haemorrhagic septicaemia among others (VSF Reports 2024). Continued influx of displaced people due to the ongoing conflict in Sudan will likely exacerbate already needs, coupled with disrupted cross border trade with Sudan; has had significant impacts on livelihoods/ incomes in addition to increasing food/ commodity costs (WFP/VAM Market Bulletins 2024). Proposed actions will guided by a food systems approach by promoting agriculture, livestock and fishing value chains including private sector actors linkages that will foster sustainability. Through private sector led where feasible including start-up and scale up of vegetable, crop, fishing and livestock sector including marketing access. These will be tailored with capacity building initiatives across different value chains to enhance extension services provision, production, sustainability and market access. Proposed action will ensure inter-cluster integration, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

2. Project activities

Start-up, scale up and private sector and market inclusive provision of crop, vegetable and fishing livelihoods kits including training on climate smart agronomic practices, post harvest handling and value chain promotion targeting 30,000 households. Establishment of kitchen/ back yard gardens and demonstration plots that will enhance peer to peer farmer learning and sustainable production Capacity building of 500 community animal health workers, stock persons and animal health auxiliaries on cost based animal health service delivery that will promote privatization Support the Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries on livestock disease surveillance, response to disease outbreaks and solarized cold chain management. Through cost based animal health service delivery model, provide treatment and vaccination of 600,000 heads of livestock supporting 30,000 livestock dependent households. Support flood affected households including returness through livestock restocking that will rebuild herds including fodder production to enhance resilience.
