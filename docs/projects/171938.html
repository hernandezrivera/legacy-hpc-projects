Project Summary

SCI will meet the most vulnerable families’ basic needs in GCA and NGCA by distributing food vouchers, building capacity on entrepreneurship and supporting promising businesses with micro-grants, based on the families most critical needs. Lifesaving activities are prioritised in areas affected by the conflict and COVID-19, especially in times of difficult economic situation aggravated by COVID-19 pandemic. Check Points remain limited for crossing for more than 6 months, and in addition to internal COVID-19 related restrictions people have no opportunity to cross the LoC to get access to their pensions and social benefits. The situation in GCA with zoning system (green, yellow, orange and red) remains tense and numbers are increasing day by day, posing an increased pressure on population, especially the ones living across the contact line with limited movement opportunities in case lockdown is activated, causing difficulties to get livelihoods or means for survival. The intended aid will alleviate burden for vulnerable HHs with children.
SCI has been implementing similar activities for years with great success and at high levels of quality. 1 - Needs

7 years into the conflict that erupted in eastern Ukraine in 2014, vulnerable families have exhausted their coping strategies and require assistance to meet their basic needs. In both GCA and NGCA along the contact line, the COVID-19 pandemic and related disruptions to employment, school, childcare and health systems have further pushed families into conflict- and pandemic- induced poverty. The quarantine imposed by COVID-19 has had a disproportionate impact on women and female-headed households, due to a rise in GBV, higher exposure to the virus in the health sector, lack of access to education and childcare preventing work, as well as higher unemployment in sectors where women make up the largest workforce (OCHA, 2020). The persisting closure of the contact line during the pandemic has further limited movements and increased economic exclusion, in addition to elderly who are not able to receive their pensions in GCA and are often their only source of income. It is estimated that among the most vulnerable households are households with 3 or more children, single parents (in particular female-headed households) with children, and households with children below 3 years of age. The draft 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 3.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, including 1.53M in GCA and 1.62M in NGCA. According to the HNO’s overall severity scale, areas along the contact line of Donetsk NGCA are the most in need with overlapping vulnerabilities. The Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster estimates that 1.52M people are in need of assistance, including 70% women and 26% children. The main needs are related to access to food and livelihoods assistance. According to REACH’s Multi Sector Needs Assessment (2020) covering NGCA, unemployment and high prices are among the key concerns expressed by surveyed households. Save the Children’s assessment “Listen To Us: Girls’ and boys’ gendered experiences of the conflict in eastern Ukraine” (2020) documents reports of girls having to resort to survival sex to support their families in contact line areas of GCA. According to Save the Children’s assessment ”COVID-19's impact on children in Eastern Ukraine” (draft, 2020), 72% of children surveyed know that COVID-19 is dangerous and only 19% are not worried about it (children with disabilities were less likely to report being worried, pointing to a possible lack of information). Adults, and in particular adults with disabilities, reported difficulties finding and affording food and hygiene items during the quarantine. PPE items in particular were highly requested.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

SCI will implement life-saving activities to enable the most vulnerable families of GCA (0-20km) and Donetsk NGCA (in line with cluster recommendations) to meet their food and livelihoods needs. These activities are particularly critical in a pandemic that restricts movements, pushes conflict-affected families deeper into poverty and threatens their lives. Food vouchers will indeed be critical before winter when vulnerable families lack the resources to meet their calorific and nutritional needs when temperatures drop. SCI has been implementing such activities in previous years from its main office in Sloviansk GCA as well as its office in Donetsk NGCA. In Donetsk NGCA, SCI has 4 permanent staff that work alongside locally accredited organizations to deliver humanitarian assistance. SCI will deliver activities in line with best practice and FSL Cluster technical recommendations, such as the FSL Cluster recommended amount for food vouchers. All the activities will nevertheless be tailored to the age and gender groups being targeted. Outcome 1: 3,450 (1,150 HHs) conflict affected boys, girls and their families have increased capacity to cover their food needs through provision of food vouchers in Donetsk NGCA Activities: Registration and verification of beneficiaries; Procurement procedure to select supplier; Distribution of food vouchers; Post distribution and endlinemonitoring; Lessons learned session Outcome 2: 215 conflict affected and displaced families have increased capacity to cover their basic needs through capacity building and micro-grants for income generating activities Activities: Registration and verification of beneficiaries; training on entrepreneurship, transversal skills and gender equality; development of business plans; provision of micro-grants for the best business plans (in several tranches); monitoring of knowledge acquired and businesses Women will be encouraged to apply for participation in trainings and micro-grants competition. Vulnerability criteria: HH with children and members with disabilities, female headed and elderly headed households with children, HH with young children, etc. SCI has been conducting similar activities for the past years, and started with food vouchers in NGCA in 2020. All these activities will be used as opportunities to raise awareness with beneficiaries on COVID-19 prevention measures. Protection and gender mainstreaming: in line with SCI policies (Child Safeguarding, Adult Safeguarding, Anti-Harassment and PSEA), SCI will ensure that protection risks and gender equality concerns are duly taken into account while implementing activities, and partner’s capacity will be built on these topics. M&E: PDM will be conducted to assess the outcome of the activities and monitor proper utilization of assistance provided. In NGCA, monitoring will be done jointly with local partners. Accountability: Conflict affected people (male, female, boys, girls & elderly) will have access to mechanisms (social media, hotline) to give feedback and will be consulted during the project cycle. Information will be shared regularly in an accessible way. Coordination: SCI will engage with the FSL Cluster at all levels. SCI works bilaterally with agencies engaged in similar activities to ensure geographical coordination and avoidance of overlapping, while ensuring referral mechanisms across organisations. SCI coordinates with the Donetsk NGCA de facto authorities and informs them beforehand about program activities, which will then be approved, demonstrating transparency and accountability. Partnerships: SCI will implement through its existing local partner organizations in GCA and NGCA. These local partners have been trained and have experience in such activities. SCI selected only qualified and accredited partner organizations, to ensure that implementing the project is done by partners with sufficient capacity, knowledge andexpertise.
