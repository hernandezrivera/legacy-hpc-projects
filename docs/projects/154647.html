Project Summary


UNFPA aims to continue providing IDPs, host communities and returnees with quality reproductive health services at all levels – primary and referral.
The demand for reproductive health services still in high demand due to the existing IDP camps as well as returns to the areas of origin, where basic social services are yet to be fully functional. Given this situation, lack of services including health is one of the top three concerns of both IDPs and returnees in Anbar (60% among returnees and 41% among IDPs), while it is the top concern among returnees in Kirkuk (100% among returnees), and Ninewa (72% among returnees and 51% among IDPs), and 81% among Salah al-din returnees. Preliminary findings from reproductive health service mapping reveal that while many partners have started to provide reproductive health services, such as, antenatal care, family planning and sexually-transmitted infections syndromic treatment, none of the partners provide support to emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC), both basic or comprehensive.
Through implementation of this project UNFPA plans to intervene in selected governorates and districts on the basis of numbers of IDPs, residents and returnees in the governorates of Ninewa, Salah al-din, Kirkuk, Anbar, Diyala, Duhok, Erbil and Sulaymaniyah. UNFPA will ensure uninterrupted RH services in all 52 IDP cams, four delivery rooms in IDP camps, six delivery rooms in hosting communities and six maternity hospitals in selected governorates. UNFPA during the implementation of the project will provide support to the service providers, procure and distribute RH kits and essential medical equipment to strengthen the technical capacity of health faculties. Through the implementation of this project, UNFPA will conduct series of training for service providers to strengthen their technical capacity.

Needs

In 2019, a total of 2.4 million people will be targeted by the health cluster. This translates into 624,000 women in reproductive age and 96,000 women who will be pregnant or in labor. These include populations in camps who have restricted movement and where, in many occasions, referrals are compromised. They also include out camp IDPs, most vulnerable returnees and those who do not fall under the government umbrella of social protection, hence requiring humanitarian assistance. For each of these target groups, UNFPA has developed a mode of engagement. Over the last years, UNFPA has been able to support the delivery of reproductive health services, both through supporting service delivery, and providing RH kits and training to different health actors. A mode of engagement developed to target different populations in an appropriate manner.The delivery of health care services in both conflict affected areas and IDPs hosting areas has been severely impacted and curtailed. Approximately 25 hospitals and over 220 health facilities have been damaged or destroyed and are either partially or completely non-functional. The demand for health services have increased due to the ongoing displacement and an increased risk of morbidity and mortality (health cluster). How this affects communities is demonstrated in the location assessment of the DTM. A package of RH services for most vulnerable IDPs women based on the RH MISP will be provided in a timely manner. This partnership will also allow standardizing RH services to meet the minimum package required in humanitarian settings and to ensure a Human based approach that ensures quality, acceptability affordability and accessibility.The project builds on current work implemented by UNFPA through its partners on providing RH services in Humanitarian context throughout Iraq. UNFPA has supported to establish and expand the RH services at primary and secondary level health facilities through National and International partners in KRI and South and Central Iraq. All services, both static and mobile, are in full coordination with the health cluster partners, where partners provides the primary health package except RH, while UNFPA provides the RH services package. Both partners and UNFPA have agreed that reproductive health interventions will be set up in coordination between different actors, to ensure a full health package provided.

Protection Risk Analysis

Mitigation Measures

UNFPA's long-standing relationship with different actors, mainly key governmental and non-governmental actors, will facilitate the presence in different areas, as demonstrated during 2018. UNFPA is closely coordinating with medical and engineering team of the stabilization department of UNDP, which is responsible for reconstruction of hospital structures affected by war and providing medical equipment to avoid overlapping and duplications in project-supported areas. In close collaboration with UNDP and local and central authorities, UNFPA is preparing exit strategy for each supported areas. Supporting different entry points for GBV survivors is seen by UNFPA to be the most effective approach to create a conducive environment for survivors of sexual violence to seek services. This includes the support to women community centers, a safe space where women can speak out, as well as health facilities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

UNFPA and its partners will ensure that all women in fertile age and girls will receive timely and high quality RH services. Communities, women and youth leaders will be involved for outreach activities. Ensuring high quality of health service will be done through monitoring visits, utilizing findings from RNA, information from IDP Call Center and CwC.
