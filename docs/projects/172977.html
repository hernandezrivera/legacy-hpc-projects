8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The project was designed after conducting a Needs Assessment in our Local Rights Programme Areas (LRPs), which involved the communities and improved the targeting of assistance and influence the nature of interventions supported and the location of services provided. AAZ also undertakes continuous awareness raising on available complaints and feedback mechanisms in place from community to the level of the organisation and partners. This is done to ensure that people who have grievances can safely lodge a complaint and organisations engage a mechanism to redress wrongdoing. The organisation uses suggestion boxes and tollgates. In addition, we are consistently mapping existing partners and engaging in different coordination meetings and clusters. This will culminate the promotion of community participation and better information-sharing strategies and platforms. Coordination compliance to the CHS for accountability and quality assurance is key in humanitarian programming. We also realize the importance of acting in a timely manner, and adapt our actions to the capacities and mandate of the organisation, as well as the phase and circumstances of the crisis we are in. Communities and people affected by crisis will know that they will get assistance that is best suited to their needs.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will ensure that it increases food production and increase environmental quality at the same time through climate resilient strategies, and as ActionAid Zimbabwe, our chose approach is agroecology. As an institution, we are implanting food security and resilience projects across our Local Rights Programme Areas where we are scaling up and scaling out agroecology as a mitigation and adaptation measure. The measures will involve focusing on sustainable farming practical systems which optimize and stabilize crops yields, while promoting a multi-functional role agriculture and strengthening economic viability. Approaches which reduce greenhouse gas emissions for mitigating climate will also be adopted by empowering communities through community-based adaptation. Use of organic agriculture will be promoted, we will also promote diversification of both flora and fauna to achieve sustainability, renewable energy, livelihoods diversification and local seed and food security. Some of the actions are outlined below • Promote soil conservation practices (e.g. soil fertility trenching, solid & liquid organic manuring, soil compositing, diverse cropping patterns like rotation and companion cropping. • Introduction of soil reclamation techniques and restoration of degraded landscapes (e.g. gulley reclamation, soil testing kits, watershed/catchment area management, etc.,)• Introduction of water conservation practices like contour ridging, dead-level contours and infiltration pits.
