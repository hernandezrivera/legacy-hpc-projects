Project Summary

The combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. These findings reflect the outcome of the 2020 post-Gu seasonal food security and nutrition assessment conducted across Somalia during July and August 2020. The assessment and the subsequent analyses were jointly led by the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU, a project managed by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations) and the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET, a project funded by USAID) with the active participation of Government institutions, UN and NGO partners. 2020 Gu cereal production in southern Somalia is estimated at 74 000 tons. This is 40 percent lower than the long-term average for 1995-2019. The main factors for below-average 2020 Gu cereal production include: recurrent and severe flooding, erratic rainfall and a prolonged dry spell, insecurity and conflict. The Desert Locust infestation has been mostly confined to northern and central Somalia, causing damage to crops, fruit trees and pasture. In the northwest, 2020 Gu/Karan cereal harvest expected in November is estimated at 22 500 tons. this is 45 percent below the 2010-2019 average, mainly due to erratic rainfall and Desert Locust. Internally displaced persons (IDPs), a majority of whom are poor and live in urban areas in desperate conditions, have limited livelihood assets and coping options and rely more heavily on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, a significant proportion of IDPs continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. Some of the urban poor across Somalia who struggle to make ends meet also continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. For both groups (i.e. IDPs and urban poor), the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 was a factor, associated with a decline in remittances, increased food prices, and a decline in employment and other income-earning opportunities in urban areas.This project will provide outpatient treatment to children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition in the following districts Afgooye, Marka in Lower Shabelle, Balaad in Middle Shabelle, Kahda and Deynile districts under Banadir region, Gadon and Mareergur under Dhusamareeb, Janaale town and Marka town of Marka district.
The project will provide Outpatient Therapeutic Program and supplementary feeding program to children under 5 (Target is 69,800 in 6 districts), and Pregnant and lactating women (Target is 31,410 in 6 districts) with acute malnutrition.

Project Activities

1. Conduct individual and group IYCF-E counselling among Pregnant and Lactating Women and caregivers of children aged 0 to 23 months. Target is 56,194 in 3 districts (Afgooye, Marka, Dhusamareeb)2. Mitigation Measures

1c. Children and mothers with disabilities and or with limited mobility, nutrition staff in the mobile teams will make home visits to provide treatment and IYCF counselling. Those needing transport for specialised treatment will be transported using STS ambulances and referral vehicles. • STS will ensure there is adequate integration and linkages with Health/WASH/Protection services in the targeted areas to minimise distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. 2a. • Beneficiary selection will be systematic to ensure vulnerable persons, such as marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Mothers/caregivers/PLWs with disabilites are not excluded. • Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for distribution points. • During treatment, distribution and mobile outreach, beneficiaries with disabilities & other special needs, who cannot stand in line for long periods, will be identified and given priority. • Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from different sociodemographic, economic and cultural groups know about nutrition services, distributions times/sites. • Community Nutrition Workers and Mobile Nutrition teams will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, socio-demographic groups, disability). 2b. • Mother to Mother support groups will be formed within different vulnerable socio-demographic groups within the target communities. • STS will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. • Malnutrition and IYCF awareness messaging will involve women and men and will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. • All beneficiaries seeking treatment will be served without discrimination strictly following protocols and guidelines for treatment of acute malnutrition as outlined in the new Somali IMAM guidelines. 3a. • Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. The integration of STS Protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. • STS will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect nutrition practices, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build caregiver self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • STS will train all nutrition staff involved in care and treatment of acutely malnourished children on psychosocial support (PSS) including identification and support for disabled beneficiaries. 3b. • STS Nutrition staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. •STS Nutrition staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). 3c. • STS will locate distribution points and OTPs in locations that are accessible by vulnerable population groups from the catchment population. • Mobile health and nutrition clinics will be located close to beneficiaries as possible and will open at times that allow mothers to walk to and from the services safely. • BSFP distributions to take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. • Mother to Mother support group sessions will be held in a central place that mothers choose and are located near caregivers’ homes to ensure short safe distances travelled to and from the meetings. 4b. STS will maintain a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained.
