Project Summary

The proposed project will provide protection services to vulnerable communities in northwest Syria, who are subject to, or at risk of, protection concerns including child protection issues, gender-based violence and/or sexual violence, PWDs, through mobile outreach teams and structured preventive as well as responsive and remedial services. In addition, the project will provide those who are subject to or at risk of the heavy bombardment and shelling through conducting voluntarily reallocation to safe areas. Moreover, Violet will provide referral services to the most vulnerable and also raise their awareness on available services and rights. Awareness-raising activities will be conducted through direct contact with the affected community, with either individuals or in-group sessions. The sessions activities will include provision of information sharing to the services available via distributing leaflets , small information cards, posters and drawings to ensure participation and meaningful access , special cases will be handled by the Case workers who will conduct the necessary risk mitigation issues , refer and follow-up
Those will be socially and culturally appropriate and aim at increasing their knowledge of protection issues and how to address those individual or with community support. Outreach visits also will identify individual cases for case management and proper reporting and follow up per the SOPs. To focus on sustainability, despite the deteriorating situation, Violet seeks to establish community protection committees, who will become the community-component of the proposed project and will play a key role in community resilience towards protection risks.
A GBV technical specialist will be responsible for providing support for the GBV caseworkers on the field, in order to support their activities and provide guidance. The GBV (case) workers will screen and identify cases, from the community and the recent displacements IDPs, conduct risk assessment and refer the identified cases both internally to benefit from the PSS activities and externally to other static service providers in the area using the service mapping tool.
The PSEA focal point in Syr will be reporting directly to the focal point in Turkey in regards to investigations issue, whereby will report to the project manager in matters related to case identification, technical issues and follow-up. In addition, Violet will invest in its trained volunteers to provide the voluntary relocation support and services through transportation and associated protective package for people to move safely and in dignity away from hostilities to safe destinations; taking into consideration family unity and attention to the needs of most vulnerable groups, including female-headed households as well as PWD. Guaranteeing effective referral at the earliest possible opportunity for medical emergencies and unaccompanied children. The latter will be referred to child protection actors on the ground for an appropriate course of action inconsistency with the best interest of the child;
For all activities, GBV SOPs, PSEA guidance, and case management policies for the Syrian crisis will be used. PSS targeting adults, youth and children will enable them to identify positive coping mechanisms besides their current situation. Also, the project will roll out training and capacity building on protection key elements and protection mainstreaming to ensure that all Violet’s non-protection staff and volunteers are in a position to mainstream protection in all project cycles and especially emergency response projects.
Violet will provide PFA support, based on displacement and needs of newly arrived IDPs, Violet will respond in areas of new arrivals. Furthermore Violet will provide referrals pathway services including CP and GBV centers, for 1.500 cases

4 - Needs

Violet seeks to address the most urgent protection needs to mitigate potential risks. The needs are based on Violet’s protection needs assessment, but also on the constant need’s updates from the protection ﬁeld team on the ground. The increased bombardment has forced hundreds of thousands of people to the north, which leaves them without covering basic needs. Due to the lack of basic services, safe reallocation, shelter, NFI, and food, IDPs are left in a vulnerable situation that pushes them to utilize negative coping mechanisms and increases their risks towards protection related issues. A high vulnerability is present for women with regards to SEA and GBV risks, as well as children and CP related issuesAccording to HNO/voices 2020, The Syria crisis is one of the most severe and protracted humanitarian crises in the world today and has impacted women, men, girls and boys differently. Around 7% of IDP households and 9% of total households are FHH, with higher percentages for Aleppo and Homs, at 12% and 10% respectively.18. The crisis has permanently altered the fabric of society in the nation, with far-reaching consequences for its future resilience.Women and girls have had a unique experience of the crisis and are often exposed to different risks and vulnerabilities. Traditional gender roles have changed due to large-scale displacements, family separation, and economic crisis (unemployment). This, in turn, has driven high levels of family separation, with women becoming heads of households and thus the primary breadwinners. In other households, male members are present, however, they are unable to secure employment or other financial sources to maintain their families. Women and girls are therefore forced into work. Consequently, women and girls face increased risks of GBV such as sexual exploitation and abuse by employers, denial of resources, opportunities or services, such as education or male family members denying them their earnings from employment.

5 - Implementation of activities

GBV mobile teams will provide life-saving services, especially during the reallocation and accommodation including case management and PSS services, GBV risk mitigation, coordination and advocacy. These are adapted to the context based on the availability of services, needs, and risks and patterns of violence, as determined from the RNA, Violet will build the capacity of 60 team members by provision of online training package developed by GBV SC, in addition to training sessions on PSEAH, SRH, SIR, CM, CP in emergencies, CPSG and Protection mainstreaming.The team will be composed of the following:- Project Coordinator: to Oversee the project activities- Team leader (outreach): Oversee case management and provision of PSS activities, Lead mobile team, GBV coordination, referrals- GBV Caseworker: Conducting GBV case management, Facilitate PSS sessions for women and girls, Hold regular information sessions, Facilitate safe referrals to other service providers and aware community FBs on GBV core concepts and safe referrals- Community Workers: Conducting FGDs with community members to assess risks, protection concerns, and other issuesbased on updated service mapping, Violet will Update the emergency referral pathway about available services and focal persons for other referral services. This can be in the form of posters or small information cards. Furthermore Violet will Continually update initial rapid service mapping with key servicesViolet will provide safe reallocation for vulnerable groups aiming to save their lives and protect them against attacksA key component in the proposed project is the sufficient and appropriate set-up of Violet’s BNF complaint mechanism.Preventing GBV, to stop it from happening in the first place, is a key priority. Given that GBV is based on gender norms and gender-based power inequalities, GBV prevention strategies set by Violet are intrinsically linked to efforts to increase gender equality more generally

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project at hand aims to target those exposed to protection risks, however, there are key protection risks for both beneficiaries and humanitarian workers to include: 1. A key risk is a gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse through humanitarian workers. As Violet’s staff will interact with the most vulnerable groups of the community, the power dynamics between staff and beneficiary can be exploited. Violet trains all staff on PSEA, raises awareness of beneficiaries and informs on available complaints mechanism. The M&E team will include post-monitoring and PSEA topics in supervision sessions to identify any misconduct. Any complaints, allegations or rumors will be elevated on the country director level and investigated immediately.2. For community and PSS activities, there are child protection risks and GBV risks for participants e.g. due to the time of activities or their location. Violet’s team will mitigate those by assessing appropriate times for PSS activities and choosing a location that allows safe travel. Distances will be minimal and locations will provide sufficient shelter. s3. During evacuations, all community members are exposed to protection risks due to their dependency on those conducting the evacuation. Violet will ensure both awareness of staff on code of conduct and PSEA, but also include monitoring questions for PSEA and satisfaction with the evacuation. 4. During the evacuation, people may be exposed to intensive shilling while they are fleeing. Thuse, Violet will ensure continuous coordination with OCHA to de-conflict the roads and areas utilized to allocate people to safe areas.
