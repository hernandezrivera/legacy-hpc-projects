Project Summary

IOM, as Cluster Lead Agency, has been successfully managing the Shelter and NFI (S-NFI) Core Pipeline since 2011 and has the capacity to continue providing critical services to Cluster Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP)partners. Based on its experience and on-the-ground presence, IOM will ensure swift delivery of life saving assistance to partners and beneficiaries through effective supply chain management. Under the HRP 2020, S-NFI Cluster Pipeline plans to reach 823,251 (80% of total target) beneficiaries across South Sudan. SNFI Pipeline is responsible for procuring, transporting and storing S-NFI materials. Upon S/NFI Cluster endorsement, the Pipeline will receive and process partners’ requests for distribution and prepositioning. The Pipeline team will coordinate with the Logistics Cluster, to ensure cargo movement for planned responses and for stock prepositioning. Further, the Pipeline team will ensure transport and storage from Juba to the main logistics hubs; ensure support to distributing partners on cargo movement by road; and coordinate with the Logistics Cluster for cargo shipment by air. The Shelter-NFI Pipeline provides partners with rapid access to critical materials to meet the emergency needs of most vulnerable populations. In order to allow partners timely access to S-NFI supplies, items are procured centrally by one organization and prepositioned in strategic locations, mainly Bentiu, Bor, Wau, Malakal and Rumbek, but also Torit, Mundri, Yambio, in order to expedite the process by which items are moved to the final distribution sites in field location. The Pipeline system optimizes economies of scale, reduction of cost, harmonized quality of items, non-duplication and supply chain efficiencies. The Pipeline will conduct a survey with partners to gather feedback on efficiency, appropriateness and coverage of the pipeline, the findings of which will be utilized to adjust programming as appropriate. It will also collect beneficiary’s feedback through Post Distribution Monitoring and Rapid Monitoring conducted by its partners on quality, appropriateness and usage of supplies.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the country’s protracted crisis has left 7.5 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance. An estimated 4 million people remain displaced, by the humanitarian crisis: 1.8 million internally and approximately more than 2 million fled to neighboring countries. The Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) sector in South Sudan estimates approximately 1. 9 million individuals will require S/NFI assistance in 2020. Out of the estimated number of people in need of S/NFI assistance, the Cluster will target 1,015,331 people for shelter and NFI assistances in 2020. This figure includes the internally displaced person (IDPs) which includes IDPs that have been subjected to multiple displacements, returnees and the Host Communities (HC). In both protracted and new displacements situations, people are often forced to leave behind basic household items and shelter materials. Those displaced have often left behind vital household items like cooking tools, blankets, mats and mosquito nets, and there often exists scarce community infrastructure and services, which further jeopardizes the livelihoods of affected populations. Women and children remain especially vulnerable both due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. South Sudan has less than 100 kilometers of paved road and one of the least developed road networks. Markets have collapsed in most conflict areas such as Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei States hence, affected population in these locations cannot access basic needs. Seasonal precipitation turns much of the country to swamps and some 70 per cent of the country’s road network is inaccessible during the rainy season from July to December. Airstrips in general are of poor quality, with less than 10 airstrips that can be used continuously in the wet and dry season. South Sudan indisputably is one of the most complex humanitarian operating environments in the world. The deteriorating infrastructure, lack of maintenance, cumulative effects of long and heavy rainy seasons, have left the humanitarian community with limited options to deliver relief items. During the dry season, insecurity along the main supply routes is a major challenge restricting movement and increasing transportation costs due to a high risk operational environment for transporters. The use of pre-positioning relief items in the field by road where/when possible, remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. Preposition of supplies in key-hubs locations across the country is important as part of Cluster’s effort to crucial support to ensure swift access to emergency relief items in a coordinated fashion. This effort reacts to the need to strengthen the humanitarian planning and response among the large number of aid agencies providing assistance in the country. IOM has managed the core pipeline in South Sudan since 2014 and successfully supported S/NFI Cluster to meet objectives. Many partners rely on IOM as pipeline manager for the provision of materials from the Cluster’s common pipeline. Maintaining a steady pipeline is essential to meet needs in a timely manner as they are identified. IOM’s pipeline manager works closely with the Cluster’s coordination team and State Focal Points to ensure prioritized and coordinated coverage across the country, minimizing gaps and overlap of services. IOM also works closely with other Clusters such as the WASH , Health and the Logistics to ensure more vulnerable and underserved populations have access to core pipeline supplies to maintain their basic hygiene standard. In 2020 IOM will continue its commitment to support the S/NFI Cluster by aiming to procure and manage core pipeline supplies to benefit 823,251 people in need.

2 - Activities

The activities are in line with Strategic Objective 1 and Strategic Objective 2 of HRP 2020, as well as the Shelter and NFI Cluster Objectives 1 and 2, namely to 1) Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities, and 2) Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities/affected non-displaced, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs. Activities: 1. Procurement of emergency shelter materials and NFI supplies that contribute to a wider stock able to serve 823,251 individuals2. Appropriate storage and management of the shelter and NFI emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu;3. Preposition of the shelter and NFI emergency supplies to Malakal, Bentiu, Wau, Bor and Rumbek4. Administer and process Pipeline partners’ requests for stock and distribution.5. Transportation of supplies to distribution locations6. Regular monitoring of shelter and NFI core pipeline supply delivery, supplies quality control, storage management and supplies utilization.7. Supports S/NFI Cluster in strengthening the accountability of pipeline program in South Sudan, through regular reporting of pipeline status, collecting utilization reports from partners and post distribution monitoring of supplies distributed by partners8. Conduct pipeline survey with partners for the efficiency, appropriateness and coverage of the pipeline service. 8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

IOM Core Pipeline commits to Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) framework and encourages distribution partners to ensure participation of all groups especially those with heightened risks in any emergency response. S/NFI Need Assessment toolkit and Pipeline Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) categorically incorporates questions on inclusion, participation, and protection in order to measure effectiveness of the response in addressing the needs of the affected population. Findings and recommendations are given to ensure that issues are raised and addressed. IOM has in place policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IOM staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IOM Contractors. Extensive training and workshop on protection against SEA has been rolled out and all IOM Core Pipeline staff has undergone at least one training in the past year. IOM continues this commitment with provision of refresher training, annual signing of commitment to this policy as well as development of visibility materials in all the project locations on protection against SEA messaging with clear information of reporting procedures and contact information for survivors. During the planning of any response, the partner agencies conduct an inclusive consultation process with the community leaders as well as the representatives of all groups within the community. This is when the location of distributions centres and areas of assistance and services are discussed and agreed upon. Access and security of beneficiaries are the most important factors that are considered. Community members also take ownership of the process and tasks such as off-loading, guarding during the distribution, enumeration, and such are given to qualified members of the community. During actual response, security is also monitored by the response team. PDM surveys have questions that are directly related to safety of beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Pipeline distributing partners inform beneficiaries before distribution on items distributing and their usage. Partners have already set standards for information sharing with beneficiaries, which include community meetings, and information disseminated through radio and local chief/community elders. With regards to PSEA, IOM team have all been trained on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse which makes them aware of all important and relevant concerns related to project design and implementation prior to rolling out of any intervention. Pipeline team also works closely with the IOM Protection Officer to ensure protection is mainstreamed in all activities. Pipeline partners have well established procedures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse Post-Distribution monitoring conducted by Pipeline partners collect feedback on material appropriateness and quality, which is used by the Pipeline team to inform items to be procured. In addition, Pipeline conducts partner satisfaction surveys on management of Pipeline. The quality of S-NFI items are assessed by partners through post-distribution monitoring and community feedback mechanisms. Pipeline partners are required to include the community in monitoring and evaluation of the quality and utility of supplies through post-distribution monitoring and community feedback. The pipeline team is then informed of this feedback through PDM reports available on the S-NFI Cluster website
