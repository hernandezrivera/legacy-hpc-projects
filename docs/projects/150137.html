Project Summary

Project objective: To enhance the livelihood and food security of vulnerable Palestinian framers mainly women living in area C, north-west of Jerusalem district
The project is designed to address the urgent needs of Palestinian farmers mainly women farmers living in rural areas in Area C and whose agricultural land has been affected by the separation wall, settlers’ attacks and confiscations. This objective was designed in response to the developed activities in the cluster response plan that was developed in coordination with the food security partners and reflected by the needs and priorities of this sector. RWDS will be focusing on the following cluster activities namely, 1 Land rehabilitation activities in areas at risk of land confiscation, and 2. Provision of agricultural assets for improved productivity/sustaining livelihood.
The planned interventions also correspond directly to the below two country strategic objectives.
Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
Strategic Objective 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Eventually enhancing the livelihood and food security of the targeted farmers mainly women farmers in the 2 targeted communities will contribute to the realization of the first objective of the HRP namely, Strategic Objective 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL

Needs

Due to the protracted occupation, repeated shocks, continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and lack of economic opportunities, the food insecurity in Palestine is affecting 31% of the Palestinian population. Area C which consists over 60 percent of the West Bank, mostly agricultural land, is affected the most from food insecurity reaching to 24% compared to 17% in the remainder of the West Bank. Area C also holds virtually all of the Israeli settler population of the West Bank - around 350,000. OCHA,HNO 2017. In response to the food insecurity in area C, RWDS planned its humanitarian interventions by starting with carrying out a needs assessment to identify the areas to be targeted. Accordingly, Al Jib, and Beit A’nan, north-west of the Jerusalem district were selected. Below are information about each area and its needs. Al Jib is classified as area C with approximately 8,228 dunums, 92.5% of the total village area and the separation wall extends 6km over its lands which is mostly agricultural land and isolates 4,683 dunums of the village, which comprises 52.6% of the village’s total area. Beit ‘Anan is classified as area “C’ with approximately 8,797 dunums, 87.1% of the total town area and the wall in built on 4 km land isolating 1,009 dunums of land (comprising 10% of the town’s total area) from the southwest side of the town. The agricultural sector absorbs 65% of the workforce and faces the following obstacles, lack of capital, lack of support for land rehabilitation and inputs, lack of workers and lack of economic feasibility. The second stage of the needs assessment consisted of carrying out focus group meetings with the beneficiaries to identify their specific needs focusing more on women given that they contribute to more than 60 per cent to labour requirements in agriculture. Adding to that, households headed by women face higher levels of food insecurity than men reaching to 36% among women and 21% among men in the West Bank. (OCHA.HNO, 2017)Accordingly, four focus group meetings were carried out during the following dates, September 9th, at Al Jib women club (attended by 10 women, 5 men, head of the village council (male) and 3 RWDS female staff), on September 16th, at Beit ‘Anan women club (attended by 20 women, 6 men, 1 female member from the local village and 2 RWDS staff), followed by another meeting with the attended women only to identify some specific issues that they felt more comfortable discussing without men. The below needs were identified by the women that participated in the focus group meetings.1. The majority of the agricultural land is located in area C and need urgent rehabilitation and plantation to avoid confiscation by the Israeli Authorities and settler attacks2. Women lack ownership to land due to the inheritance patriarchal traditions. Only 7% of women own land in Palestine. 3. 2 women in the targeted areas own land4. Around 7 women in the targeted areas are house-headed 5. Women don’t have access to resources mainly financial resources.6. Gender biases and inequality between the roles of men and women in the agricultural sector 7. Increased financial expenses and limited access to markets8. Agricultural work is more focused on the older generation and the younger generation is not taking part in this sector9. Lack of agricultural guidance and coaching in organic and free chemical agricultural inputs and plantation, pesticides control, etcThe needs were verified again with the targeted group and are still valid and in high need for 2019.

Activities

Stage one: Establishing community mechanisms and ownership to the project in the three targeted areas Activity 1.1. Establish a project steering committee consisting of 7 members, 2 local village representatives, one from each of the two targeted areas, 4 women, two from each of RWDS women clubs in two targeted areas, and RWDS project Coordinator (80% will be females). Activity 1.2 Present the project to the public in the two targeted areas and select the 40 farmers (25 women and 15 men) 20 from each area Stage 2: Land rehabilitation works Activity 2.1 Construct 200m stone terraces for each of the 20 farmers (15 women and 5 men) Activity 2.2 Construct 300m fence long and 1.5 m height for each of the 20 farmers (15 women and 5 men) Activity 2.3 Clean the agricultural land from derbies, old plantation, rocks, etc. for 40 farmers Activity 2.4 Distribute agricultural tool packages, non-chemical pesticides, seeds and seedlings to 40 farmers Activity 2.5 provide tree trimming works to 40 farmers This project will be replicated in the second year in 3 identified vulnerable areas within the Salfit District namely KufulHaris, Masha and Salfit town. RWDS agreed to follow this approach to allow for the learning and testing of what worked best in the first year and what needed adjustment to offer the targeted communities in the second year work approaches and modalities that are based on best practices and successful implementation techniques

The project's activities and indicators will be the base for the M&E and a detailed action plan will be prepared with a time frame. Pre and post questionnaires, meetings and interviews with the beneficiaries to measure the level of benefit and change before and after the project including frequent field visits will be used as well. A baseline will be generated to help in the monitoring process and the M&E Advisor will follow up on this process and prepare all periodic reports in coordination with the project coordinator The uniqueness of RWDS in M&E is the involvement of the community in this process through the women clubs who will monitor and continue to support the beneficiaries after the end of the project and report to RWDS on the longer term change realized. RWDS will coordinate with the MOA on all project planning and implementation.
