Project Summary

Component-1: Burmese language acquisition for Rohingya Refugee Teachers
Supporting the implementation of the Multi-Year Strategy (MYRP) of the Education Sector in Cox’s Bazar, the project works to ensure that children, adolescents, and youth from the Rohingya refugee and from the Host community in Cox’s Bazar have equitable access to relevant quality learning.
Building the capacity of 200 educators (50% female) and mentors in the use of the Myanmar Curriculum to ensure quality of teaching through acquisition of Burmese language course. The Burmese language acquisition course is for three months of which 45 days are in-person and 45 days are online. This is based on “learning by doing” method i.e., First 10 days for in-person training, then teachers practice in the Learning Centre for next 5 days. Again 10 days online training and next 5 days practice in the LCs. The whole course follows the similar methodology to ensure effective learning and practicing of Burmese language. The practice sessions at LCs are strictly monitored, guided and feedback recorded systematically for further improvement that ultimately contributes to quality learning for Rohingya Refugee children.
UNESCO engages the language training institute in Myanmar and International Language Institute of the University of Dhaka for conducting the Burmese language acquisition course. Both institutes jointly develop the course (online and offline) through a consultative process as per basic competencies of Burmese language. The language acquisition course also includes a short chapter on teachers’ self-wellbeing that ensures the diverse of learning.
The Burmese language acquisition course gives particular emphasis to improve the language skills of Rohingya female teachers because the course module includes gender related issues that helps to multiply the impacts of Burmese language acquisition course.
The project equips 200 teachers (50% female) with durable tablet devices with pre-loaded learning applications and content. Tablet devices are to be rotated among learner households and can be used by children outside learning sessions. Train teachers to be able to use and maintain devices and robot teachers.
Component-2: Piloting of Robotic Teacher (AI teacher -online/office) for 200 crisis-affected Rohingya refugees (50% female) children
The project provides equitable basic learning classes for primary and secondary class-aged children following the Myanmar curriculum to 200 crisis-affected Rohingya refugees (50% female) aged 15-24 in the Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar District using ALP and for grade 5 and 6 using Myanmar Curriculum for Camp Community.
Responding to the significant levels of stress and trauma experienced by marginalized youth and learners from the camp and host community caused by poverty and distress that children, adolescent and youth live in, UNESCO includes online and offline courses on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), GBV, PSEA its education and life skills education classes to provide learners with the help that they need and deserve.
The project builds the capacity of teachers to leverage ICT for distance and blended education that includes in-service training on blended learning approaches and methodologies to all teachers and instructors engaged in the project from the camp and host community.
The Burmese language acquisition for teacher ensures multilingual education, active learning, summative/formative assessments), to enhance teaching quality and to support that parents send their kids to Learning Centers rather than to private tutoring.
Teacher training is carried out in partnership with Language training institute in Myanmar, Dhaka University, UNESCO Myanmar Office and UNESCO Institute for Lifelong Learning (UIL).
Total beneficiary is 4,900 that includes teachers, learners, parents/caregivers for community engagement and stakeholders and total budget is US$ 950,000. Cost per beneficiary is US$ 194.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Teacher is the key actor: Transforming education begins with the teachers. If teachers are not prepared, no other means are enough to ensure quality education for children. It is more important for the case of crisis affected children particularly the Rohingya Refugee Children. Myanmar Curriculum is used in the Learning Centres (LCs) Burmese is not the mother tongue of Rohingya Refugee children and teachers. Educational attendance of adolescent girls remains extremely limited in both the camps and host communities. In the camps, among those aged 11 to 14, girls are less likely than boys to attend education facilities, with this trend increasing with age (Assessment of the Education Sector Response to the Rohingya Crisis, January 2021; p. 25), resulting in the highest proportion of children not in school being Rohingya adolescent girls. Similar challenges are faced by adolescent girls in host communities. This trend is exacerbated by social and religious barriers that restrict mobility of girls in public once they reach adolescence, as well as the prevalence of early marriage. This contributes to social isolation and exclusion, low levels of foundational literacy, psychological distress, exploitation, and abuse. It has, however, been found that parents are more likely to send their girls to education facilities if there are female teachers and sex-segregated classrooms (Review – Rohingya Influx Since 1978, ACAPS NPM Analysis Hub, Thematic Report, December 2017; p. 7). In the camps there has been some success at recruiting a high percentage of female teachers, with 56% of teachers being female (According to the 2021 Education Sector 5W figures there were 4733 female and 3758 male teachers in the camps.). However, the majority of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi (31% (2,639) of teachers in the camps are females from the host communities, compared to 8% (641) being males from the host communities. Sourced from 2021 Education Sector 5W figures.), with female Rohingya teachers only comprising 25% of the total (This represents 41% (2,134) of the total number of female Rohingya teachers, compared to 59% (3,117) of male Rohingya teachers. Sourced from: 2021 Education Sector 5W figures.). This presents a challenge in the roll out of the Myanmar curriculum, and so more female teachers with sufficient Myanmar language skills must be recruited and trained.The difficult living conditions in the camp have also reinforced the practice of families keeping women, particularly adolescent girls, at home. Women and girls in the host community have been experiencing additional access barriers to scarce and overstretched services and income generating opportunities, and an increase in tension in their households and community, including a reported increase in gender-based violence. Families are resorting to unhealthy coping mechanisms: The situation and coping mechanisms that local populations resort to, point to the urgent need for interventions (a) to addressing the most pressing humanitarian needs of the refugee and host communities, including by increasing access to formal and non-formal learning opportunities, (b) to help communities to develop healthier ways of coping with the situation including through community engagement, as well as (c) to strengthen the resilience of the education system by reinforcing the capacity of education stakeholders.AI Robot Teachers: Responding to the lack of qualified and Burmese-fluent teachers, mentors, and supervisors, the project employs an innovative approach by deploying multilingual (Burmese, English) AI Robot Teachers to assist teachers in the multi-grade and multi-subject classroom delivery of the curriculum. Deploying AI Robot Teachers allows to use subject-based lessons developed and recorded by official Burmese speaking teachers in Myanmar, ensuring the quality, efficiency, and relevance of teaching.
