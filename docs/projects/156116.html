Project Summary

This project aims to provide lifesaving Services through integration between Nutrition and health services in health facility which provide secondary health services at the community and health facility levels to IDPs and host population (children U5 and PLWs) Nutrition services will provide by the Health staff (community health workers, Nutrition Technicians, doctors, and Nurses).
preventive services include IYCF practices awareness raising sessions. CHWs and Nutrition technicians will provide IYCF practices including breastfeeding. counseling to all caregiver of children (0 _ 59) months and PLWs mothers in order to raise the awareness of beneficiaries in IYCF Practices.
and Nutrition supplies (plumpy doz-micronutrients- high energy biscuits -Iron folic tab) will be distributes at community and facility levels through health workers.
CHW Teams who will do MUAC screening at the community level and Nutrition technician at health facility level to all children (boys and girls )6_ 59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs).
curative services include referring malnutrition cases from community to health facilities to be treated by applying CMAM (community management of acute malnutrition )program in integration with health sector . RUTF (ready to use therapeutic food) will be use in treatment (plumpy Nut- plumpy sub).
Coordination between Nutrition technician and community health workers will be to do follow up visits under treatment cases at community level.

Needs

According to HNO 2019 4,734,890 Million girls and boys under the age of five years and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of under nutrition and in need of comprehensive curative and preventive nutrition services to continue during 2019. Around 91,811 children under the age of five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition during 2019, out of which 30,821 girls and boys are at risk of death due to severe malnutrition if not treated appropriately. Without appropriate care, acute malnourished children are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. 865,295 girls and boys under the age of five years suffer from micro nutrient deficiencies. It is estimated that 1,596,413 Million mothers will require maternal nutrition services and skilled support for optimal infant feeding and caring practices. Global Acute Malnutrition among boys and girls under the age of 5 years remains within acceptable international benchmarks in most of the assessed areas during 2018. The severity scale ranking for nutrition shifted from areas that were deemed mostly ‘critical’ and ‘severe’ in 2018 to mostly ‘severe’ and ‘major problem’ for 2019. Nutrition needs have increased in 2019, and therefore the response warrants an increase in coverage of nutrition services, in addition to flexible life-saving service modalities that address both immediate emergency nutrition needs of displaced populations and the medium to longer term needs of the community, returnees, and people living in newly accessible areas. SMART surveys and nutrition surveillance in sub-districts throughout Syria in 2018 generally show that global acute malnutrition (GAM) is mostly at acceptable levels. However, in Eastern Ghouta in Rural Damascus, the GAM rate did reach emergency thresholds at 11.9% following besiegement. In areas with newly displaced populations such as in Idlib, acute malnutrition of children 6-59 months of age rapidly increased in a space of 6 months (from January 1.5% to June 4%), and hard to reach areas such as Ar-Raqqa also nearly tripled (4.6% to 11.9%) in 2018. Pregnant and lactating mothers are particularly vulnerable due to their specific nutritional needs and health risks. Reporting data on nutrition screening in 2018 confirmed that moderate acute malnutrition rates of PLWs increased by 134 percent. That is, 10,064 pregnant and lactating women were malnourished in July 2018 compared to 4,297 in July 2017. Lack of access to health services and negative coping mechanisms such as meal reduction are some of the causes linked to deteriorating maternal nutrition. In addition to data that indicates ‘High’ stunting levels in Lajat, Dar’a in 2017 (27.5%) and in the sub-district of Jarablous, Aleppo in 2018 (26.7%) , stunting rates confirmed to be ‘Very High’ in Tel Abiyad (32.4%) and Eastern Ghouta (36%) in 2018, as indicated by SMART surveys and using the 2018 WHO-UNICEF stunting thresholds. The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micro nutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life, and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency.UOSSM is on of UNICEF and Nutrition Cluster partners so In order to respond to the current Nutritional situation, UOSSM Team will provide comprehensive curative and preventive Nutrition services to targeted beneficiaries at Community and facility levels in coordination with health sector.

Implementation of activities

Comprehensive curative and preventive Nutrition Services will be providing to beneficiaries through health staff (doctors-Nurses- Community health workers- Nutrition technicians) at Community and health facility levels.•Strengthening life-saving and preventive Nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on safe and appropriate IYCF practices in emergency contexts and beyond, Nutrition supplies (plumpy doz-micronutrients- high energy biscuits -Iron folic tab) interventions, and optimal maternal nutrition. Infant and young child feeding interventions will be provided in the community, health facilities, and local health system structures in close collaboration and coordination with the health sector and reproductive health sub-cluster, food security and child protection sectors. •Improve equitable access to high quality, life-saving, curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases for boys and girls under five and PLW. The response modality will be informed according to context and will be adjustable and flexible to serve the needs of the target groups. Provision of management of acute malnutrition will be ensured at the health facility and community level and in integration with infant and young child feeding services and primary health care services.The Nutrition sector priority groups are children under five and PLW, regardless of their status or geographic location due to their general vulnerability to Nutrition deficiencies and their immature or compromised immunity. However, children under five and PLW living in harsh conditions such as those in hard-to-reach areas, IDP sites, overburdened communities, areas affected by a high intensity of hostilities, and women and children “on the move” are particularly vulnerable. These groups will be further prioritized by the Nutrition sector with the aim of providing minimum life-saving Nutrition services jointly as part of, where possible, an inter-linked and complementary multi-sector response.The sector aims to improve life-saving WHO recommended infant and young child feeding practices. A specific focus will be the scale-up of, integrated IYCF interventions, especially primary health care, reproductive health, and key entry points at the community level. The capacity of stakeholders such as health practitioners, nutrition staff, and community workers will be strengthened to facilitate critical one-to-one IYCF counseling support, group sessions, and community outreach activities. Preventive nutrition actions will focus on promoting child growth and development in the first 1000 days which will include maternal nutritional support during pregnancy and lactation. Micro nutrient deficiency prevention and control initiatives will be promoted through service platforms such as micro nutrients supplementation within health facilities, during accelerated campaigns, and improving vitamin A coverage for children 6-59 months using immunization campaigns and routine distribution whenever feasible. The Nutrition sector will also prioritize the provision of specialized nutritious food to children 6-59 months among the populations in hard-to-reach areas, IDP last resort sites, overburdened communities or areas affected by a high intensity of hostilities as well as recently displaced IDP children or returnees. Quality, life-saving, curative activities will focus on community- and facility-based screening for acute malnutrition among girls and boys under five and PLW, followed by referral for treatment of acutely malnourished cases with a focus on program quality performance indicators.Treatment of acute malnutrition will be integrated with the infant and young child feeding through using mothers of acutely malnourished children to boost early identification and referral of children to receive appropriate treatment and also mothers of acutely malnourished children will be counselled on optimal feeding practices

Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:1.Prepare separated reception utilities for men and women2.Availability of women in the medical staff (Nutrition technicians &community health workers) to provide privacy while the provision of service3.Separated rooms for men and women while staying in the health facility for recovery and monitoring Risk: Increase risks of Sexual Exploitation from staff while receiving nutrition services. Likelihood: low. Impact: High. Mitigating Measures:1.Providing a simple and clear compliant mechanism2.All staff commits to sign a code of conduct3.Providing PSEA training for all staff Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to providing nutrition services to specific members of the community. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium.Mitigating Measures:1.Providing service to all people in need regardless of any other criteria2- community raise awareness about nutrition services standers especially formula distribution. Risk: Difficulty of reaching health facilities/services for PWDs. Likelihood: low. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:1.Providing facilitation utilities that make it easier for PWDs to reach out for the e services2.Providing comfortable chairs and latrines in place of healthcare service for PWDs3.Implementing all the global standards for dealing with PWDsRisk: Possible inconsistency with local traditions and culture. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:Availability of women in project staff at all levels where contact with beneficiaries are presented.
