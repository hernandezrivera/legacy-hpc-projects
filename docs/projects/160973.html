Project Summary

As South Sudan prepares for the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement and reaping associated dividends, the effects of prolonged exposure to conflict is a reality to deal with given thousands of children facing numerous protection concerns; displaced families; vulnerability to climate shocks e.g. flooding and expected influx of returnees. CMMB would like to propose implementation of comprehensive child protection activities that will ensure that children at risk of, or exposed to violence, exploitation, and abuse access timely, efficient and quality life-saving assistance while prevention and response interventions are strengthened in emergencies. Focused on enhancing well-being of boys and girls in selected payams in Yambio county working with relevant stakeholders, comprehensive child protection case management services including referrals to specialized services for 350 vulnerable children will be a key pillar of the project. To ensure quality service delivery, a total of 20 social workers and community volunteers will be trained on child protection case management, mentored and supervised to effectively support children. To enhance protection of released/vulnerable children,20 foster/kinship caregivers will be identiﬁed, trained and supported to take care of children who need care as their families/caregivers are traced. Family tracing and reuniﬁcation of unaccompanied and separated children will be strengthened through working with volunteers in the local communities, Save the Children, UNICEF and other partners to identify, follow up and refer UASC/released children who will also be linked with appropriate services and reunified. In addition to case management,40 extremely vulnerable children will receive cash based protection assistance as per their protection concerns while another 30 will receive vocational training based on their individual interests.
To reinforce psychosocial well-being of children, both mobile and community based psychosocial support will be delivered through establishment of 9 CFS sites and strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to identify, refer, and support children with protection issues.1350 girls and 1350 boys will be targeted under this intervention. To create a protective environment for children at home and community level,400 caregivers will be trained on basic parenting skills. Awareness raising on key child protection issues that will be aimed at addressing prevailing protection issues and prevent/reduce risk of recruitment of children by armed groups and reduce incidents of family separation will be emphasized targeting 10,000 community members in Yambio and Ezo counties. Child Protection referral pathways will be developed with other key stakeholders both CP and non CP actors to link children and communities with various life-saving services. Provision of life skills will enhance resilience of vulnerable boys and girls hence will be incorporated in child friendly spaces programs and adolescent and youth clubs.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan humanitarian context has remained highly volatile despite numerous efforts by different stakeholders to bring peace. As highlighted by a number of national and regional assessments, protection needs have largely remained unmet. Even with the advent of the revitalized peace agreement in late 2018 and the promise of better times, the cumulative effects of the conflict have translated to sustained poverty and persistent humanitarian and protection needs for more than seven million people in South Sudan as highlighted by the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO).Once regarded as the bread basket of the nation, Western Equatoria slid into conflict in 2015, a situation that has only slowly improved in some parts. Protection related issues like gender-based violence, child labour,abduction of children,use of children by armed groups,adolescents, and young adults, exploitation and abuse have been widely reported. Women, girls, elderly, the sick, and people with disabilities are the most affected with women carrying the burden of supporting their families in the absence of the men. Boys and men also suffered as most of them were abducted and recruited into armed groups. Even though some of these abducted children have been released, it is suspected that many remain in captivity (Gbudue State IRNA Report, 5-14 March 2019). With all these atrocities and conflict many children remain unreached with psychosocial support services, case management etc. In Yambio, access is confined within and around the town with areas that bore the highest burden of conflict not reached with critical life-saving services. Ezo has remained largely unsupported after the conflict.Lirangu was severely affected by fighting during the conflict, leading to damage of properties and displacement. Although some people had returned following the peace agreement, they are experiencing severe hardship in getting key services for survival, impacting the lives of children families in the area (NP Lirangu Protection monitoring, 25-26 June 2019). During psychosocial support activities, children often share how the fears of abductions are still fresh in their minds and how they are tormented by the thought of losing parents or other family members to conflict. The high prevalence of mental health issues and limited access to professional services in South Sudan is a threat to children’s well-being. Displacements have fragmented family and community support mechanisms. As per CPIMS data over 8,000 children need family tracing and reunification services,615 are in Western Equatoria region with CMMB supporting an active caseload of 329 UASCs. These figures are expected to rise once the peace agreement is implemented and counties like Ezo which have remained largely undersupported may see more UASCs among other vulnerable children.Despite all these ﬁndings the level of response is very low, exposing children, especially girls, to early marriages, sexual exploitation and early pregnancies. Adolescent girls remain targets of multiple forms of violence including being taken as wives of the rebel members and forced to have sex. Negative cultural norms continue to reinforce violence and abuse of children, a threat to their future well-being. Lack of adequate CP actors and resources after prolonged conﬂict has compounded suffering and increased the vulnerability for children and their communities. With the hope and expectation of improved peace and calm in the coming days the increased numbers of returnees will further strain current support structures; something which demands an increased response to cater for the influx.

2 - Activities

To appropriately deliver comprehensive child protection services meeting identified needs for vulnerable children, CMMB will work closely with the children and their families/communities to offer comprehensive case management services, community-based psychosocial support and other related services; parenting skills sessions; family tracing and reunification services for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC); strengthening of community-based Child Protection mechanisms to identify, refer, and support children with protection concerns and awareness raising to address prevailing protection issues and prevent/reduce risk of recruitment of children by armed groups and family separation. Child Protection in emergencies training will be conducted to build capacity of staff and community structures while extremely vulnerable children will receive targeted cash-based protection assistance. To ensure that UASC girls and boys receive quality family tracing and reunification services, the project team will work with communities and other stakeholders to identify, document, trace and reunify 150 UASCs (75 girls, 75 boys) with their families/caregivers and provide other case management services. For children identified with other protection concerns including those formerly associated with armed conﬂict/released, comprehensive case management and referral to specialized services will be supported based on individual need and vulnerability through working with the children and their families. Reintegration services will be offered in line with standard practice and guidelines for reintegration of CAAFAG in South Sudan. 40 extremely vulnerable children (20 boys, 20 girls) will be supported with cash or vouchers to meet identified critical protection needs. Case management services will be provided to 100 boys and 100 girls. A further 30 children (15 boys, 15 girls) will be empowered through vocational training. To enhance protection of released/vulnerable children, 20 foster/kinship caregivers (10 women, 10 men) will be identiﬁed, trained and equipped to provide alternative care within communities of origin of vulnerable children. To ensure quality service delivery, 10 social workers and community volunteers (5 women, 5 men) will be trained on case management, mentored and supervised to support children. 30 Child Protection and non-CP staff (15women, 15 men) will be trained on preventing and responding to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children. Provision of both mobile and static community-based psychosocial support will be enhanced through establishment of 9 CFS sites and delivery of structured age and sex appropriate psychosocial support activities that will target 1,350 boys and 1,350 girls. Parenting skills/PSS sessions will be provided to 400 caregivers/other community members (300 women, 100 men) to strengthen a safe environment for children. 10 CFS animators and volunteers (5 men, 5 women) will be equipped with relevant PSS knowledge and skills through training. Reinforcing community-based child protection mechanisms to identify, refer and support children with protection concerns will be done through awareness raising on prevailing Child Protection issues, response and how to prevent/reduce risk of recruitment, and family separation. 10,000 adults and children (5,000 women, 3000 men, 1,000 girls, 1,000 boys) will be targeted for sensitization through campaigns, public events etc. CP referral pathways will be developed/revived and disseminated to link children and communities with life-saving services. Adolescent peer-to-peer support groups will strengthen their coping skills towards life issues and build strong social networks reaching 350 youth (200 girls,150 boys). CMMB, the current co-chair of the State Child Protection Working Group will continue working to avoid duplication of services and strengthen coordination. 20 staff (10 men, 10 women) and community representatives will be trained on PSEA.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

At risk populations have been identiﬁed, how and why they are at risk considering their speciﬁc vulnerabilities,including those experienced by girls and boys and groups such as internally displaced persons,persons with disabilities etc.Minority groups,children living with disabilities,girls and boys will be consulted in every activity planned and carried out within the project.Their views will inform review and redesign of activities to suit their specific interests through activity evaluations at the end of CFS sessions and conducting monthly meetings that will involve both children and caregivers.Potential risky areas within the community will be jointly assessed and mapped out to ensure children are safe and recommended protective actions agreed upon.Community feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through working with volunteers,meetings with children and community,focus group discussions,and ensuring availability of suggestion boxes in strategic locations at the CFS.Limited access to project sites by children living with disabilities will be addressed through engaging community volunteers who might identify and refer such children. Adolescents will be supported to organize activities with children. Empowered community Child Protection Committees will enhance protection of affected and at-risk children.Referral pathways will be developed with relevant stakeholders to ensure needy children access services.CFS will be located within safe and central locations to ensure children do not walk long distances nor get exposed to unsafe environments.PSEA Focal Points will be identified and trained then conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls,boys,and men in communities of concern ensuring they are aware of their rights and entitlements through posters,community meetings,focus group discussions etc with complaints reported to Program Director.Approved information sharing protocols will be used e.g.CPIMS tools that minimize exposure of sensitive information.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Regular community engagement and provision of feedback to beneficiaries throughout project implementation will ensure maximum participation through quarterly review meetings, monthly CFS meetings, activity evaluations with children and joint monitoring with the ministry of education,gender and social welfare. Working with caregivers, community leaders and specific interest groups eg adolscent girls and boys and children living with disabilities will ensure maximum representation taking account of their specific concerns while appropriate response measures are taken. Open dialogue with the various beneficiary groups will keep track of any positive and/or negative impacts and ensure that corrective measures are taken early enough.Confidential mechanisms e.g. use of suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations at the child friendly spaces or community centers, will allow beneficiaries freely share concerns/complaints as well as ideas on how the interventions would be improved.Use of approaches eg smiling faces for children will allow them evaluate specific activities.Available feedback options will be shared, including contacts where the beneﬁciaries can directly report their concerns without involving project teams. Regular monthly meetings at the CFS will encourage children to share their concerns and jointly ﬁnd appropriate solutions. The location of CFS sites will be agreed with beneﬁciaries, including children, to reduce conﬂict and cater for their individual needs while ensuring their safety and easy access.
