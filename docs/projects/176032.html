1 - Needs

The protracted conflict situation in South Sudan has resulted in wide humanitarian gaps that might have dire outcomes on millions of people who remain displaced in and out of the country. Many children do not have access to education. An out of school study conducted in 2018, estimated 2.2 mln children were not attending school in South Sudan (UNESCO & MoGEI,2018). The education cluster estimates that the number of children in need of education increased exponentially to 3.4 million due to Covid19. IPC assessment of Nov 2020 indicates that nearly 7.4 mln are food insecure. Conflict and inter-clan violence has led to destruction of school facilities. According to EMIS data of 2018, some schools either partially or completely damaged, were forced to close down due to insecurity in 2019. These schools had not yet reopened when the government closed schools in 2020 due to Covid. Among the 20% of schools that were non-functional, insecurity is the main cause for closure. Despite calls by the UN to limit hostilities during Covid crisis, insecurity continued to escalate. Apart from insecurity and COVID, an estimated 11,400 refugees returned to South Sudan in June 2020, bringing cumulative number of South Sudanese who have voluntarily returned to the country since 2017, to more than 301,000. (USAID Crisis fact sheet #9, July 2020). NRC assessment reveal displacements continue in parts of the country due to insecurity and at the same time returnees and IDPs require education services. According to the assessment, an estimated 19,362 individuals (3,227 HHs) returnees/IDPs came to Lainya County and the surrounding Payams and villages such as Lainya Centre and Limbe (NRC, June 2019). In Greater Kajokeji, all the 88 pre-primary schools (nursery) were non-functional. Only eight (8) primary schools out of 104 were operational and all the schools in Nyepo County were non-functional (NRC, June 2019). More than 400 schools in 9 states were occupied by flood IDPs (HNO 2021). This further damaged the education infrastructure, which results in further delay of reopening. The conflict also increased the likelihood of girls being married early. South Sudan is ranked the fifth worst country in the world for child marriage, which is viewed as a way to escape poverty and a method to protect girls, but which causes girls to drop out of school. For instance, in 2017, Jonglei had the lowest level of girl’s enrolment in the whole country at 31%. Additional barriers to girls’ attendance and performance in school include: economic and social demands such as household chores and caring for younger siblings; strong cultural norms that favour boys’ education when a family has limited resources; insufficient number of female teachers to act as role models; and inadequate school sanitation facilities. The essential inputs required for provision of equitable, quality education such as qualified teachers, female teachers, text books and learning materials, learning spaces for IDPs; returnees, and water and sanitation facilities in schools remain in extremely limited supply. In addition, young people drop out of school due to long distances to schools, incomplete schools, insecurity and displacement, inability of parents to purchase education materials for children due to poverty, and little to no support services in schools for children with disabilities. Despite improvement in school enrolment, education completion among young people is very low and estimated that 86% of students fail to complete primary education. Besides, only 17% of teachers currently teaching in primary schools are trained. NRC further conducted protection needs assessment in several locations targeted by the project, both those intended to be served by static and mobile activities. Data collected during the assessments informed a protection risk analysis that particularly considered the needs of women, recently displaced persons and older persons with disabilities.

2 - Project activities

NRC will implement multiple interconnected EiE activities to protect crisis affected girls, boys (aged 6 – 14 years); youth-male & female (aged 15 – 24); adult women & men and children/people with disabilities aimed at equitable access to quality education for communities forced to flee due to conflict and natural hazards. NRC will target vulnerable children and youth from IDP, host communities, and returnees providing education services to out-of-school children and youth. This will restore a sense of normalcy and routine to crisis affected children who potentially face psychological distress as a result of the emergency. The project, with first phase emergency response, youth education, formal & non formal education, will build back better efforts and prepare a strong base for recovery. Potential areas for the first phase response are conflict prone areas of Jonglei, Unity, Greator Equatoria, (Eastern/Central/Western), Warrap and Western & Northern Bahr el Ghazal. NRC will implement formal and non-formal education activities in the same states where the need for education is dire. Youth from targeted counties will access opportunities for continued education while learning essential life-skills for social engagement. Specific project objectives are; 1. Children and youth affected by conflict, displacement and Covid-19 in South Sudan continuously access quality learning and training opportunities that are relevant to their psychosocial, emotional and cognitive development in a safe, inclusive and protected environment. 2. Children and youth who missed years of school due to violence, displacement and Covid-19 are able to access their right to education and through access to quality Accelerated Education programs 3. Capacity of community and professional education structures strengthened to support school supervision, inspection, teacher training and mentoring on child centered approaches promoting socio-emotional learning. NRC will implement the following activities; • Community mobilization sessions/Back to Learning campaigns to promote enrolment of girls, boys and youth including children with disabilities (CWD) in primary schools and ALP classes • Enrolment of 28,167 (12,675 girls/15,492 boys) children and youth in schools and temporary learning spaces, including 790 CWD (362 girls/428 boys) in primary and ALP • Establish 16 TLS and rehabilitation of 12 TLS • Provide teaching & learning materials to 28,167 (12,675 girls/15,492 boys) of which 1790 CWD (362 girls/428 boys) are CWD in primary and ALP centres • Disability assessments & provision of assistive devices to CWD • Teacher training for 280 (90f/190m including 13f/15m PWD) teachers on learner centered EiE methodologies, basic pedagogy, psycho-social support, PSEA, code of conduct • Train 300 PTA members (150f/150m including 6f/9m PWD) on school management, DRR, drafting of school safety plans; instituting school plans. • Train 655 (263f /392m) inclusive of 17 PWD (8f/9m) teachers, education personnel and community leaders on referral pathways protection, SRGBV and Covid-19 prevention protocols. • Pay teachers incentives to 160 (70 f/ 90 m) including 17 (8f/9m) PWD teachers in IDP camps • Establish gender segregated WASH facilities (latrines, comfier corners for menstruating girls/female teachers/youth, handwashing stations) accessible to CWD in 34 learning centres • Provide dignity/hygiene kits to 1,316 girls (22 CWD incl. 5 PWD female teachers). • Print, distribute IEC materials & conduct awareness sessions on hygiene, prevention of transmission of viral diseases eg Covid/Ebola & diarrheal diseases; eg cholera/typhoid • Support children’s activities-school clubs and peacebuilding training • Provide school feeding in IPC 4 &5 areas • Train staff on safe programming, child safeguarding and inclusive education • Provide material support to county, payam education officials facilitating school supervision and teacher mentoring.

3 - Project indicators

Project Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) will be undertaken in line with NRC's guidelines that emphasize participatory approaches, relevance, accountability and timeliness of information and data. The focus will be on results monitoring which will measure the contribution of this assistance in changing or improving the lives of beneficiaries. NRC will also use gender analysis to inform the development of monitoring, reporting and evaluation frameworks for quality programming to ensure outputs deliver for the needs of women and men. The M&E system will consist of following key tools: a) An M&E Work plan/matrix outlining plans for data collection, analysis, and use and reporting premised on the log frame indicators. The plan will be a living document that will be reviewed periodically based on emerging needs. NRC will use KOBO mobile data collection tools to conduct survey including multi-sector rapid assessments, PDM and baseline/end line surveys for measuring outcome indicators. b) An Indicator Definition and Analysis will provide a detailed description of each indicator, sources of data, the measurement parameters and beneficiary disaggregation by sex, age, displacement and ability status. c) An Intervention Tracking Tool will track the progress against a target on monthly basis and measure if a result is achieved based on the prescribed activities/outputs in the log frame. The tool will be shared during quarterly reviews of the project. d) A Detailed Activity Implementation Plan will outline key activities and tasks to be undertaken to achieve the planned results. This tool will track activity implementation in order to ensure planned activities are implemented on schedule and where appropriate adjustments are made. e) A simple beneficiary compliant/feedback response mechanism will be implemented to enhance accountability and transparency in the delivery of the project activities. This will also consider child-friendly feedback and consultation methods. NRC will carry out an integrated Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) that cover all distributions to assess beneficiary satisfaction and usage of the facilities provided. In addition, bi-weekly monitoring system used for tracking project outputs will be used. NRC will continue to provide monthly updates to the cluster. Focus Group Discussions to integrate qualitative information in the monitoring system and internal Project Reviews will also be carried out. Results of the monitoring processes will be shared with stakeholders, MoGEI, local leadership, PTAs, school administrators and other relevant parties for accountabilities purposes.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Under this project, NRC will coordinate with protection cluster partners and conduct training, awareness sessions on PSEA. The training will target a variety of stakeholders who include local chiefs, law enforcement agencies, child protection committees, PTAs, school communities and education authorities. The training will also ensure that participants' capacity to identify, prevent and respond to sexual exploitation ad abuse is build. Referral pathways within and outside of the project locations will be made available to the beneficiaries. NRC will print and distribute IEC materials on PSEA. Furthermore the PTA, and teachers training will also have a session dedicated to teachers code of conduct which largely addresses PSEA issues. Under teacher, PTA training, NRC will ensure that a session on PSEA is included. Additional mechanisms to provide information about PSEA include community sensitization and awareness sessions, use of IEC materials, training of program volunteers, providing accessible information for girls, boys, men and women of different abilities, utilization of school mothers and child-led child protection committees

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NRC will ensure that monitoring and evaluation activities are inclusive and accessible. Whether Utilizing IPA case closure and evaluation forms, PDM tools, or more general community evaluation FGDs and KIIs, NRC teams will seek to engage a range of community members from both sexes, all relevant ages, and across a range of diversity characteristics. In assessment stages and throughout implementation, NRC teams will discuss with communities the ideal mechanisms for monitoring and evaluation to ensure inclusive approaches. In hard to reach areas, or those with emergency responses that might mitigate the kinds of monitoring and evaluation approaches possible in more static locations, NRC will take particular care to discuss with communities safe, realistic options for monitoring and evaluation and means of improving accountability.NRC also has policy and standards to enable all external stakeholders, especially the people we serve, to provide feedback and issue complaints on NRC’s organizational performance, and for NRC to address such feedback and complaints in a safe and responsive manner. NRC is committed to accountability and transparency in all activities. This project is framed within that commitment and provides compliance to the Core Humanitarian Standard. The project will work with the communities and people affected by crisis to ensure complaints are welcomed and addressed.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability lies at the core of this project. Prior to activity implementation, the protection team will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders are involved in all stages of project development and informed of the feedback mechanisms available to them. Beneficiaries will be able to make confidential suggestions for improving the project, lodge complaints where they disagree with implementation modalities, or seek feedback. After implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation teams will collect data on the quality of information received by beneficiaries and stakeholders about the feedback mechanism and their satisfaction with the mechanism. The modalities of the mechanism will be based as much as possible on traditional.NRC has policy and standards to enable all external stakeholders, especially the people we serve, to provide feedback and issue complaints on NRC’s organizational performance, and for NRC to address such feedback and complaints in a safe and responsive manner. NRC is committed to accountability and transparency in all activities. This Policy is framed within that commitment and provides broad guidance on complying to the Core Humanitarian Standard. The project will work with the communities and people affected by crisis to ensure complaints are raised, received and addressed timely. NRC will ensure that the project systematically engages with all beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, including the most marginalized, to deliver information about assistance to be provided in such a way as to maximize meaningful participation and consultation. Community dialogues, sensitization meetings are some of the platforms that the project will use to relay information to beneficiaries. Information shared will include the scope of the proposed interventions, entitlements of those involved; clarity on targeting of vulnerable groups, and information essential to accessing complementary services, as well as other relevant information. NRC will consult with communities about the optimal way to share information, and provide information in forms accessible to those with limited literacy and in local languages, to the extent that it is possible. In 2021 NRC will roll out a digital community hub accountability platform where program beneficiaries can access information via bulk messaging, sms, whatsapp, a call centre and and other channels deemed appropriate in the local context. Program beneficiaries will raise their complains through the same media channel and also receive feedback in similar ways. NRC will establish a dedicated complaint response and feedback mechanism which will be used throughout the intervention period from assessment to post distribution or intervention monitoring. Before the establishment of the complaint and feedback mechanism NRC will raise awareness so that affected community get to know where and how to address their complaints, give feedbacks and receive feedbacks as well. The Complaint mechanism will be put in a way that encourages beneficiaries to freely address their concerns and get feedbacks. Specifically, NRC will use the following ways to receive complaints and feedback;1. Help desk will be established and managed by NRC staff and a representative from the affected community will be included in the process of receiving complaints and feedbacks as well as responding to concerns raised.2. Before, during and after interventions, NRC will organize meetings involving the community leaders from the affected population, teachers, education authorities, PTA/SMC members, religious leaders, representatives of persons with disabilities, women representatives, youth representative and others to sensitize them on the project.3. NRC will conduct a focus group discussion with specific group from the affected population including children with disabilities to get their views on the activities carried out by NRC. In this complaints, feedbacks and recommendation will be taken into consideration.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

NRC will use Feedback from beneficiaries' as the main driver for CFM usage in project adaptive programming, learning, improvement and decision Making. Taking precautions to make the project as transparent and inclusive as possible, NRC will engage with the affected populations and ensure that the service provided is relevant and continues to address the priority needs of the participating community. The school communities will be involved in the project activities right from inception and throughout the project cycle. The participation of affected communities with provide them an opportunity to continuously shape the interventions through iterative process. To maintain intervention relevance, NRC will review feedback received from the participants on a a rolling basis and continually hold community dialogue sessions seeking suggestions on specific program adaptations. Beneficiary participation at this level will ensure that the program more inclusive and creates an environment the fosters a sense of local ownership, as well as mitigate possible apathy to ensure sustainability of the project. Inbuilt broad community participation in all the program stages from needs identification, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation ensures that no one is left behind. The ongoing community dialogue and stakeholder consultation sessions will inform NRC on whether the interventions are still addressing the needs of the population. As the project will be implemented in close collaboration with relevant education stakeholders at national and state level these stakeholders will have access to project information, and challenges faced and will have an opportunity to put across their suggestion for strengthening the program. Upon receipt of the feedback, NRC will review, revise and update the interventions based on the suggestions made and in consideration of the broad program objectives. In cases where changes have happened, NRC will revise implementation strategy taking into account community inputs. Changes made may include introducing new activities, partnering with agencies that have a comparative advantage in sectors that the community will be prioritizing through their feedback, or changing the scope of the program as deemed necessary. In all these processes NRC will ensure that the donors are informed and that their approval is sought prior to implementing the adapted interventions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To address the risk of lack of access to information on referral pathways; NRC will work with protection partners in the project locations, conduct trainings, hold sensitization sessions for community leaders, girls, boys, PTAs, teachers and other education professionals. Information will be provided through community meetings, electronic media - radio programs, IEC materials (posters, flyers, banners and leaflets). Further information will also be relayed through child led activities that will promote peer-to-peer learning among girls and boys from affected communities. Within NRC, the education interventions will be integrated with the Information, Counselling, and Legal Assistance (ICLA) activities to ensure that survivors of protection risks access requisite assistance timely. To minimize the risk of low levels/ non participation in feedback processes, NRC will use multiple channels to allow boys, girls, women, men and youth of different ages, abilities and displacement status to participate in the feedback processes. The channels to be used include suggestion boxes, the digital hub which encompasses a call center where beneficiaries can send feedback via sms, whatapp or voice calls. Moreover, NRC will conduct community meetings targeted at specific groups such as children with disabilities and provide them with an opportunity to participate in a safe environment. This is in addition to child feedback and consultation meetings that NRC will conduct in participating communities using child friendly approaches - drawings, art, games, and other artistic tools. To protect beneficiaries data the following mitigation measures will be taken:• Beneficiaries information is only mentioned in registration sheets, which are archived in the NRC archiving system for which the access is limited to few key staff for reporting and auditing purposes only.• Beneficiary including children's pictures are only taken after having consent and the concerned individuals are well informed on how NRC will utilize the information collected.• Project Reports are only shared with the clusters, key partners and donors (the reports do not include any personal information).• NRC staff's use tablets for collection of data during assessment and PDMs phases without any personal individual information.
