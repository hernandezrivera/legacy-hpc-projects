Project Summary

The FAV project was designed to respond to urgent food security needs in three targeted governorates, Rural Damascus, Homs and Deir Al Zour. These areas have high levels of food insecurity due to the presence of conflict, limited humanitarian assistance, high levels of unemployment and poverty.
The overall objective is to improve the food security of vulnerable groups. This will be addressed through two outputs; to improve the food security status of 2,000 assessed food insecure households through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance and support self-reliance of 750 affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives in the targeted areas.
This is will accomplished through the provision of food vouchers to 2,000 vulnerable households, designed to reduce food insecurity in the target areas. Vouchers will enable beneficiaries to access a minimum level of nutritious and a variety of food items for all members of the household. In addition, 750 households will be provided with a small-scale food production kit which will enable a greater amount of food to be produced at the household level, improving food security for the vulnerable household.

Needs

The scale of the humanitarian crisis in Syria has surpassed all expectations. According to recent estimates, 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. The main triggers of food insecurity remain prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. Insecurity and the ongoing hostilities in many parts of Syria has led to the displacement of millions, who, in addition to losing their homes, loose jobs and access to their normal resources and are then unable to secure basic services and necessities due to the increased economic hardship. Agriculture has also been negatively impacted, especially in rural areas where the worst drought in 30 years (2017/2018 winter season in Syria) affected cereal production. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with the lowest wheat production at 1.2 million metric tonnes since 1989. There is a shortfall of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes as well as unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies.Food insecurity impacts on community members in different ways. Negative coping mechanisms employed include skipping meals, giving priority to children to eat (limiting food for adult males and females, as well as elderly men and women and people with disabilities), selling of productive assets, purchasing food on credit and eating lower quality meals. Other negative effects include early marriage (especially for young / adolescent girls), child labour and withdrawing children from school (both young boys and girls, as well as adolescent boys and girls), increased risk of sexual or gender based and family violence, especially for married adolescent girls and adult women, and increased tensions in the household impacting on all family members including infant boys and girls, young children, adolescents, male and female adults and elderly family members. Accessing sufficient food is further challenging for female headed households and widows due to customs and traditions and potential harassment at distribution points. Women and children traditionally take on the role of food preparation in the household, with all family members contributing to farm work as needed. In coordination with the Food and Agriculture Sector, ADRA Syria will focus on extremely vulnerable groups suffering from food insecurity through a food voucher program, mainly in urban areas, as well as agriculture production in rural areas, to reinvigorate the agricultural livelihood of Syria, helping with alleviate food insecurity. ADRA Syria will target four locations: Rural Damascus (Buqassem, and Qutaifeh), Homs and Deir ez Zor. ADRA has identified over 400 households in Rural Damascus and Homs with high vulnerabilities (people with disabilities) and are severely food insecure. There is an absence of stable livelihood sources and high prices of commodities. With the ongoing conflict in Deir Ez Zor, there has been massive displacements, household reliance on high risk and unsustainable livelihood coping strategies and a disruption of the livelihood sources. Therefore, ADRA Syria will target these three governorates to assist those in need through monthly food assistance for the most vulnerable and food insecure, while striving to rebuild agriculture in targeted communities. This combined approach will help build the resiliency of the target population.

Implementation of activities

ADRA Syria will focus on two main activities:1. Monthly food assistance for assessed food insecure people through food vouchers:ADRA will coordinate with local stakeholders in identified vulnerable areas to select the most vulnerable households for potential inclusion in the project. Priority will be given to female headed households, households with a disabled / elderly family member, households with infant and young children and large households. ADRA will identify at least 3 vendors, capable of regularly stocking the variety and quantity of food approved to be redeemed through the vouchers, and are known to be honest and respected in the community and are located within close proximity to target population. Distributions of the vouchers will take place on a monthly basis with special provisions to ensure safe access for women and people with a disability (separate lines, gender balanced staff, selection of appropriate distribution locations, and the ability to nominate another family member to collect). Vouchers will be redeemable for specific food groups that have been selected in consultation with male and female community members, and take into consideration the nutritional needs of men, women, boys and girls. The amount of the voucher will follow FAS recommendations. ADRA staff and volunteers will discuss relevant topics related to nutrition, resiliency and planned impact of the project with beneficiaries on a monthly basis during the voucher distributions to help ensure the longer-term impact of the assistance provided. 2. Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and related training.In rural areas where there is agricultural land available, ADRA will work with local stakeholders to provide agricultural inputs to vulnerable households, with priority to female headed households, households with a disabled / elderly family member, households with infant and young children and large households. Specific items will be based on the needs of each family member, taking into consideration the requirements and abilities of the men and women in the household to tend to provided inputs. The project has been designed through extensive consultations with current beneficiaries of ADRA’s food security and livelihood projects, including men and women and people with disabilities to design activities around actual needs. The FAV project and will be implemented and monitored by ADRA staff and stakeholders, taking into consideration the needs and capacities of targeted households and undertaking modifications as necessary based on the feedback received. Feedback could be received directly to male and female staff and volunteers who then pass the information to project management, or through the established anonymous complaints mechanism and hotline.

Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA Syria will ensure that the FAV project works closely with all stakeholders, following a ‘do no harm’ approach however, due to the nature of conflict, it is possible that some risks may occur. There is a risk that the food/assistance will not be shared equally among family members, or that beneficiary households will be put under pressure to share the food redeemed through the vouchers or in-kind assistance provided with other families. There is also a risk of SEA from project staff or stakeholders of the project for beneficiaries to gain access to the assistance, or that beneficiaries will face violence or discrimination during the distribution or for being a project beneficiary. There is also the possibility that project staff will face undue pressure to add households to the beneficiary list that do not meet the required selection criteria. In all cases outlined above, ADRA will maintain strong community connections with multiple stakeholders, clearly outlining the aim, purpose and selection criteria of the project to all concerned. Tools will be developed to ensure the impartial selection of beneficiaries and awareness raising will take place among the stakeholders, including beneficiaries themselves about their rights and ways in which to report potential abuse or discrimination. ADRA staff will be trained in protection mitigation measures and humanitarian principals and extensive monitoring will take place to identify and mitigate any potential risks.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several risks and constraints could impact on the success of the project. Key risks are summarized below. Contextual risk: Although the security situation in Rural Damascus, Deir Al Zour and Rural Homs is relatively stable, it is possible that conflict could reignite. ADRA will monitor the security situation of each area closely throughout the project lifecycle and be prepared to provide timely assistance based on the context and where possible. ADRA will also provide basic services in many newly-accessible and highly vulnerable areas which may place staff, beneficiaries or stakeholders at risk. ADRA will monitor the situation on the ground carefully though UNDSS, local staff and local stakeholders to minimize the risk of harm to ADRA staff and beneficiaries. ADRA staff will be trained on explosive remnants of war and unexploded ordinances to help prevent incidents. Unequal distribution of services: Due to the unpredictable and often high numbers of spontaneous self-organised returns to newly-accessible and previously hard-to-reach areas, it is possible that ADRA Syria will not have access to accurate records of the most vulnerable communities. ADRA will work closely with local communities and other stakeholders in order to obtain the most accurate information possible. Risk of fraud or misuse of project funds: ADRA Syria has board approved financial management, Anti-Fraud and procurement policies in place, that comply with donor regulations and also include whistle-blowing, conflict of interest and anti-corruption policies. These policies are strictly adhered to and will be verified during regular visits by management and donors where possible to monitor project process. Project risks: The delay or difficulty in obtaining the necessary approvals needed to implement the project activities is a key project risk identified based on previous experience. ADRA will maintain regular contact with the relevant ministries and will encourage active involvement by all stakeholders in the implementation of each stage of the project which includes multiple locations, meaning that if one approval is delayed, it is possible to move ahead in another area if needed. ADRA will have a beneficiary feedback mechanism in place under this programme to ensure appropriate accountability and prevent harm to beneficiaries. Due to the targeting of vulnerable and HTR areas, it is possible that contractors are unavailable or unwilling to implement activities in these areas, or that there is an insufficiency of appropriate materials for the project. To mitigate this risk, ADRA already has an established presence in Homs and Rural Damascus and plans to start in Deir Al Zour with existing projects with reliable contractors available to undertake activities.
