Project Summary

WVI is anticipating to improve the Livelihoods status of the hosts and vulnerable Rohingya Refugees through Unified Skills Development Initiatives. The project will reach out 1500 vulnerable host communities and 500 Rohingya refugees HHs aged 18-45 years with capacity building initiatives of young entrepreneurs to manage e-commerce, improve the quality of agro-fishery products, nursery development, moringa farmer’s associations, tailoring, auto rickshaw & solar panel maintenance, stove maintenance, masonry, bamboo carpentry, mobile maintenance, conditional cash grants, inputs support aiming to address the livelihoods dimensions. The project will establish learning enters inside and outside of the camps. The benefits and progress will be tracked through regular monitoring and data will be collected through program reports, studies and evaluation. Considering the resilience, the HHs will be brought under orientation DRR basics as well as climate smart agriculture. Social cohesion is one of the components and youth & women group will be targeted in the processes where they will be brought under groups and mobilize in social cohesion. The project will incorporate PSEA, gender and purposively will work person with disability inclusion. The project will include women 60% of the total beneficiaries.
The project will cover Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-district and 10 camps (8E, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 19, 20, 20xt, and 24) and will cover 9200 beneficiaries.
In the implementation of the project, WV Bangladesh will coordinate with CiC offices, RRRC office, site management, and other relevant stakeholders like skills related training institutes, service providers etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The vulnerability of the Refugees in Cox’s Bazar camps are gradually increasing due to lack of regular income opportunities and skills that would allow them to change their situation. According to the fifth round of the Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 5) published in June 2022, 95% of all Rohingya households are highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance with a gradual increase from 2020 (96%), 2019 (80%), 2018 (94%). These data imply the slow economy of Refugees with neither income sources nor livelihood opportunity. Therefore, the promotion of self-reliance and livelihoods opportunities through skill development in improved agriculture and creating income opportunities are crucial for camps. Furthermore, the vulnerability in the host communities adjacent to the camps has shown an upgrade trend due to the Rohingya camps and occupation of farming lands by refugees since 2017. The REVA5 assessment revealed that 18% of the host community for potential labour force are not engaged in income generating activities and the employment rate has decreased remarkable during the COVID-19 pandemic. This post pandemic economic shock has placed a sustainable stress on life, economy and resources on these communities. So, the proposed project will introduce market-oriented projects to reduce vulnerability of the persons of concern in the areas, improve resilience and support camp and host communities. Scaling up vocational and skills training, and socio-economic empowerment and self-reliance activities with special attention to uneducated and unskilled persons, youth, and women in the camps and host communities, is critical to enhancing access to economic opportunitiesThe vulnerability of the Refugees in Cox’s Bazar camps are gradually increasing due to lack of regular income opportunities and skills that would allow them to change their situation.
