Project Summary

The project seeks to conduct integrated and harmonized activities in the humanitarian field in the countryside of Aleppo governorate which is considered an area with high needs.
This intervention will contribute significantly to enhancing productivity and economic recovery by supporting the wheat value chain, supporting food security and livelihood assets. This will be achieved through the implementation of harmonious and integrated activities. The Project will be at the community level including three main activities:
- Supporting 500 wheat farmers who cultivated their lands with agricultural inputs,where each Benf. Will receive 150 kgs of Urea Fertilizer N 46%, 100$ supplementary irrigation twice and 50 $ harvesting cost as well as three online technical training sessions about GAP.
- Purchase of 360 tons of wheat seeds with supported price from the communities (current beneficiaries and other farmers) based on specific criteria for ensuring the pureness and quality of the wheat. The purchased wheat will be divided and used as two parts:
A.The first part 240 tons will be milled to produce 192 tons of wheat flour and 48 tons of wheat bran. MASRRAT will support four bakeries by wheat flour to produce bread to be distributed for free to 1500 families in/out of camps and returnees for four consecutive months. MASRRAT will contract with four bakeries to increase community participation and reduce work pressure so as not to exceed daily production capacity.
B.The another part of the purchased wheat seeds 120 tons will be storied, sifted, and sterilized (we will have 114 tons of pure wheat after screening,5% impurities). The wheat seeds will be distributed to 456 new wheat farmers. Every beneficiary will receive 250 kg of wheat seeds and 150 kg of N:P: K (15:15:15) compound fertilizers as well as three online technical training sessions about GAP.
The beneficiaries of wheat activity will be from the local community and IDPs who will meet the selection criteria. The wages of the mill, sterilization, and storage of the 360 ton will be paid from the resulting bran 48 ton, and the rest will be replaced with pure flour and added to the amount of flour that will be distributed to the bakeries as a total of 200 ton to produce 220 ton of free bread.
This project will support 16,600 beneficiaries affected by the Syrian crisis (3,860 men, 3,659 women, 4,428 boys, and 4,653 girls including 144 PWDs). All the people in the targeted villages and markets will benefit directly/indirectly from the project outcomes. The project is designed with consideration to the FSL cluster and WHO recommendation on prevention and mitigation measures related to COVID-19 especially during the distribution of support and conducting training. In addition, protection cluster recommendations were taken into consideration, wherein all the population in the targeted locations will benefit from MASRRAT protection Centers in Aleppo, in the event of any GBV referral cases.
The project will be cover two agricultural sessions, 2022 and 2023. wherein 2023 we will target the same intervention and same number of beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

The overall humanitarian situation remains dire for the people living in NW Syria. According to HNO 2021, an estimated of 13.4 million people are still in need of humanitarian assistance with increase comparing to 2020 (11 million) and the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in NW Syria has increased from 2.8 to 3.4 million people. 2.7 million are IDPs, many of whom are living in over-crowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone). In November 2021, MASRRAT conducted a rapid need assessment to determine the best practices and most suitable methodologies to support the food security and livelihoods of families in Atareb. The result of the assessment identified: More than 61% of the population of Atareb sub-district own lands suitable for cultivation wheat. Most of the population in the Atareb area works in agriculture. The number of farmers who depend on rain-fed agriculture is estimated at 7000 while the number of farmers who depend on irrigated agriculture is estimated at 2900.In the same context, MASARRAT participated in the survey related to bakeries, mills and silos conducted by HNAP 2021 in Atareb, wherein the data shows there are 11 bakeries in operation, 8 bakeries closed and four bakeries need to maintenance.Number of Camps in Atareb area is 36 camps with 10248 families.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MASRRAT will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. MASRRAT understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about MASRRAT position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children. MASRRAT mobile teams, will encourage the beneficiaries to claim their rights and will follow up the issues with the project management in Turkey. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination. People with disabilities, women, and elders have been accessed during the needs assessment process and involved in the discussions in the community of intervention,
