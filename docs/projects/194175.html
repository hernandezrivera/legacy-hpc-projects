Project Summary

The intervention comprises of three core elements – (1) Non-Technical Survey (NTS) in Kobane, Ar Raqqa and Deir-er-Zor utilizing an established local partner, RMCO, (2) Community Liaison (CL) in Deir-er-Zor conducting NTS and emergency Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) utilizing DCA’s or RMCO CL teams with EORE being delivered as a secondary intervention to those returnees and communities where needs are greatest, targeting vulnerable women, children, adolescents, youth and their caregivers including person with disabilities, and (3) Explosive Hazard (EH) clearance in Ar Raqqa and Deir-er-Zor. Each element aims to increase the safety of Internally Displaced People (IDPs), voluntary returnees and host communities, enabling safe and sustainable returns and thus saving lives. This is in line with the HRP Protection objective: “Reduce the impact of explosive hazards [related to strategic objectives 1, 2 and 3] and will contribute to addressing the high levels of explosive hazards contamination across Northeast Syria (NES).

3 - Needs

Explosive ordnance (EO) contamination continues to be a widespread protection concern in Syria. Based on MSNA 2022, one in three communities in Syria is estimated to be contaminated. In terms of perceptions, men and boys are significantly more exposed to EO accidents, comprising more than 80 per cent of victims of reported EO accidents in the past six years. Therefore, it is essential that all groups in communities including children affected by EO, must receive awareness about the risks and how to avoid them. To date, the actual scale and scope of the EO contamination in Syria is unknown as comprehensive and country-wide survey has not yet taken place. Survey is essential for the effective prioritization of mine action, while clearance is the permanent solution to remove the explosive ordnance threat. Yet, survey and clearance activities continue to be geographically limited and underfunded. In the absence of a comprehensive survey, mapping and clearance activities across all governorates, explosive ordnance remains in affected areas and causes injury and loss of life within nearby communities, limiting safe access, land use and infrastructure rehabilitation.

4 - Implementation of activities

Non-Technical Survey (NTS) & emergency Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE): DCA, in partnership with RMCO, will provide NTS in Kobane, Ar Raqqa and Deir-er-Zor Governates to gather information about the level and categorization of explosive contamination in residential dwellings, stabilization facilities, critical infrastructure and other areas based on the EH contamination. These surveys will include information about the impact of the contamination and will help to prioritize the project clearance activities strictly based on humanitarian needs. Further to NTS, age and gender tailored emergency EORE sessions will be delivered to at risk-communities in Deir-er-Zor governate, prioritizing those already living in highly contaminated areas where humanitarian interventions have previously been somewhat impeded. Emergency EORE sessions will provide targeted populations (children, adolescents and youths, women, men, elderly and people with disabilities) with knowledge and skills to adopt safe behavior with regards to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) including Improvise Explosive Devices (IED). Explosive Hazard (EH) Clearance: DCA EH Clearance teams, enhanced with armoured mechanical assets, will focus their activities in Ar Raqqa and Deir-er-Zor targeting areas such as; residential dwellings, stabilization facilities and critical infrastructure based on the data gathered during NTS. Monitoring & Evaluation with combine internal QA/QC processes & interim third party monitoring.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Community threshold of acceptable risk increases as a result of living in contaminated areas for a longer period of time, thus leading to more accidents. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: DCA/RMCO Project staff will ensure that emergency EORE sessions include topics on potential effects and consequences of explosive hazard accidents. DCA/RMCO Project staff with community members will discuss safer alternatives and encourage behaviour change. Protection Risk: Once marking of contaminated areas begins, communities mistakenly assume any area not marked must be safe. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: During NTS, Community Liaison (CL) DCA/RMCO project staff will inform community representatives and members that unmarked areas may be contaminated with explosive hazards and will instruct or remind them how to recognise warning signs and dangerous areas. Emergency EORE sessions will also include the message about confirmed and suspected hazardous areas. Protection Risk: During the clearance of houses, land, and other properties there is the risk of an unintended detonation. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Coordination with local authorities, local tribal leaders, Mine Action Office (MAO), Self- Administration (SA) and other security officials will be made before technical survey and clearance is conducted to ensure residents are aware of activities and not within the corresponding danger areas.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of 'Do No Harm across its project activities'.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threaten the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place.Risk: Closure of border Semalka and Peshkabur Border Crossing Point by KRI authorities and SA. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: DCA Syria programme has well established office in Erbil with national and international staff. DCA will coordinate with border crossing officials through liaison focal point and continuously monitored the situation for access.
