Project Summary

Under this project, NPA’s interventions alongside our local partner Fundação Wiwanana in close coordination with INGD at both national and regional level in Cabo Delgado focus on reinforcing local and national preparedness capacity. NPA therefore through this intervention strengthens Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) at the national and local levels, with focus on developing a national capacity to implement CPP and contribute to the preparedness and protection of people residing in high-risk areas or likely to return to such areas. The action will strengthen national and local conflict and disaster preparedness. At the national level, NPA will partner with both the national and regional INGD delegations to develop conflict hazard DRR/DP protocols, activities, and mechanisms and utilize its CPP framework to provide training, technical, and material assistance to enhance INGD’s capacity. The aim of this component is to create baseline capacity within INGD to coordinate, monitor, and support CPP activities and enable different actors (local authorities, humanitarian actors, first responders such as the fire brigades, community safety councils, peace clubs, etc.) at various levels to implement and benefit from these actions. At local level, NPA will partner with Fundação Wiwanana for implementation in communities to pilot the approaches developed with INGD. NPA and Wiwanana, in coordination and partnership with INGD, will deliver CPP safety and EORE trainings in target at-risk communities. The overall aim is to develop and pilot CPP activities with INGD and equip civilians with the concrete knowledge, skills, and tools they need to better prepare themselves and their communities for eventual attacks and conflict-associated risks and increase their ability to cope with crisis situations. Training modules (tailored to address the different needs and participation requirements of girls, boys, women, and men) will be designed and delivered for at-risk communities and IDP populations with high return rates as well as community focal points and partner staff. Civil servants and humanitarian staff working in high-risk areas will receive pre-deployment CPP training.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Priority activities for this proposed project will include :Activity 1: Conduct a baseline CPP assessment on local conflict-related protection needs, safety measures, and coping mechanismsUnder the first phase of the project and in coordination with INGD, NPA will conduct a comprehensive baseline CPP survey in targetcommunities and IDP resettlement sites accessible to NPA and its partners at the outset of the project, . The CPP survey will consistof a series of interview questions designed to better understand the gender- and age-specific conflict protection needs of civilians innorthern Mozambique, coping mechanisms practiced by different gender-/age-groups as well as the scale of informal return of IDPs totheir home communities despite ongoing attacks. It will also include a conflict assessment which will support NPA to develop andapply a conflict-sensitive, do-no-harm approach. Once the survey is complete, NPA will analyze the collected data and draft a briefassessment report based on the findings which will serve to inform the design of NPA’s CPP and EORE safety message content.Activity 2: Develop national and local capacities to conduct conflict hazard DRR/DP through the generation of protocols, tools, safety messages, and training plans in partnership with INGD, and the provision of ToT for INGD and local actors.Activity 3: Facilitate CPP training and awareness raising sessions in at-risk areas and communities, including the evaluation of action’s impactActivity 4: Develop and disseminate graphical, video, and audio CPP safety messages and content targeting at-risk and conflict-affectedActivity 5: Develop and pilot Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) messages and awareness raising approachesActivity 6: Preposition NFI kits containing items that address gender- and age-specific needs and can be reused, recycled or safely disposed for distribution by all ECHO partners The proposed project will also entail an Explosives Ordinance Risk Educations (EORE) component with the following expected outcomes and activities: Outcome 1: Relevant EORE safety messages for adults and children are identified and standardized through an inclusive consultation and workshopping process involving relevant stakeholders. Output 1.1: At least two consultation and development workshops are held with participation of Protection actors and other key stakeholders, in addition to follow-up meetings, consultations, and remote coordination. Output 1.2: EORE safety messages are determined and agreed upon by relevant stakeholders. Outcome 2: A standardized package of high-quality EORE information materials has been designed, produced, tested, and disseminated to relevant Protection actors. Output 2.1: Specific types of EORE materials (pamphlets, posters, etc.) to be developed are determined and agreed upon by relevant stakeholders. Output 2.2: Draft EORE materials designs are produced and reviewed/approved by key stakeholders. Output 2.3: EORE materials are field tested, evaluated, and further refined into a finalized package. Output 2.4: The standardized EORE materials package is published and distributed to relevant Protection actors. Outcome 3: 9000 direct beneficiaries have received EORE training, are aware of explosive ordnance risks, and are better able to mitigate these risks through safer behaviour. Output 3.1: The capacity of three local partner organizations to implement EORE activities is developed through the provision of ToT, equipment, information management, and coordination support. Output 3.2: EORE training sessions are conducted for 9000 direct beneficiaries (2000 women, 2000 men, 2500 girls, 2500 boys) in high-risk areas of Macomia, Mocimboa da Praia, and Muidumbe.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

NPA and our partners are fully committed to promoting gender equality in crisis preparedness and response in line with our respective policies, in addition to addressing gender- and age-specific needs and protection concernsThrough long-standing presence in the country, close interaction with communities, civil society, local leaders, and institutions, NPA and our partners on this project have developed a thorough understanding of the sensitivities around gender in Mozambique. The Action therefore takes an informed approach to design and implement gender-sensitive and gender-responsive activities, while mindful of mitigating risks and preventing harm for project participants and other community members.NPA has also put in place solid mechanisms to enhance accountability to affected populations within our interventions. These include an NPA code of conduct and safeguarding policy that all staff, partners , consultants and anyone that gets involeved in our programs must adhere to. All staff, volunteers, partners, suppliers, consultants and program visotors must sign this code of conduct and adhere to it at all times.NPA also has a zero tolence policy on corruption , and therefore all staff, volunteers, partners, consultants are fully sensitized and must adhere to this policy at all times as a condition for their continued involvement into NPA programs.NPA operations are also supported by a comprehensive security management plan that has been developped based on a comprehensive analysis of potential risks that might affect our staff, programs and beneficiaries in the target communities. The security management plans enables NPA to anticipate risks and implement mitigation measures in time to mimimise any potential harm on our programs and beneficiaries.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

NPA and our partners on this project have established systems and practices to ensure that girls, boys, women, and men in affected communities are fully informed of the project design, involved where possible, and provide feedback on activity progress and quality.NPA jointly with our local partner Wiwanana are currently working on a comprehensive baseline survey and assessment that will seek to understand the protection issues and risks that our target communities are faced with in order to work with these communities to develop an appropriate and robust protection response for IDPs, host communities and returnees. To ensure a common understanding of the context and to avoid assessment fatigue, NPA and partners on this project will coordinate and/or combine assessments as appropriate. Throughout all consultations, partners will aim to gauge community perceptions related to the Action’s environmental impact as well as ideas to mitigate harm and create positive effects. Building on current practices.NPA will partner with INGD to build their capacity and jointly develop conflict hazard DRR/DP protocols and safety messages throughsupport on CPP, establishing a sustainable foundation for future CPP outreach. A technical support and training plan will bedeveloped with INGD to bolster their role as a coordinating body and leverage that role to encourage adoption and implementation ofCPP at multiple levels for the benefit of communities and response actors. Affected populations will be engaged in the planning andimplementation of CPP capacity development, ensuring an inclusive approach that enables meaningful participation of girls, boys,women, and men in the design of activities, and use participants’ feedback to inform approaches and messaging.NPA and INGD will engage with key government stakeholders like MINT, UNAPROC, police, other relevant GoM ministries and agencies, and provincial/district/local level authorities to discuss project plans and approaches. NPA and Wiwanana will also obtain inputs from local CBOs to ensure relevance and adaptation of interventions to various geographies, cultural contexts, gender and age needs, and languages. Finally, NPA will engage project partners and key stakeholders in a participatory review process.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

To support equitable participation of and access to critical resources for all, NPA and partners will ensure that women and representatives of marginalized population groups, e.g. persons with disabilities, are actively consulted.To ensure adequate access to people with disabilities, NPA will closely coordinate with organizations like humanity and inclusion whose mandate specifically and directly addresses needs of people with disabilities. Through this coordination with organizations like humnanity and inclusion, NPA will gain direct access to useful targetting information that enables us directly reach people living with disabilities.In addition, NPA will ensure people with disabilities representation in the various community based committees that facilitate the implementation of this action including reprentation on Disaster Risk Reducation committees, protection committees, etc... Finally our beneficiaries registration data will constantly seek to disagregate data on gender, age and also disability.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

To mitigate negative impact on the environment , any items to be procured and distributed to the target communities as part of this project will have to reused, recycled, or safely disposed of (in order of priority).In addition, NPA will also integrate environmental risks education messages to the package that will be disseminated to the various audiences. Indeed, environmental degradition is a key risk that often leads to natural disasters such as drought and therefore any related messaging is an important aspect of the proposed intervention, seen from the more longer term perspective of this project.
