Project Summary

The proposed HRP 2021 intervention will target locations with IPC 4 and 5 with emergency food security and livelihoods assistance for the most vulnerable populations affected by shocks including floods and conflict, which has contributed to the elevation of food security outcomes to include Famine Likely. The convergence of shocks including floods, conflict and hyperinflation of prices for basic commodities compounded with the social and economic impacts of COVID-19 have increased the population's vulnerability deepening the humanitarian situation. The proposed interventions are aligned to cluster objectives 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3 and 2), and 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support. The delivery of the assistance will be via two modalities (IRRM and static operations) in the proposed locations: Jonglei (Akobo, Nyirol) and Greater Pibor Administrative areas (Pibor).
With the impact of interwoven shocks, the population of South Sudan is in dire support of humanitarian assistance. Years of conflict have ravaged the country and the population's resilience capacity to withstand shocks without humanitarian assistance has been depleted. As a consequence of the conflict, there are multi-dimensional outcomes which have aggravated the suffering of the most vulnerable households and communities, who have been exposed to recurrent inter-communal violence due to a security vacuum, limited access to food due to reduced farming capacity as a result of displacement and depleted households farming inputs (seeds). The prices of commodities have skyrocketed due to devaluation of the local currency and limited flow of supplies due to insecurity as well as physical inaccessibility resulting from poor road networks. The proposed HRP project is designed to ensure the most vulnerable households have access to immediate food and livelihood assistance to avert the worsening food security outcomes in IPC phase 4 and 5. The proposed interventions will focus on livelihoods assistance and access to food. Livelihood assistance will be implemented for the main season and dry season to ensure continuous production of food at household level, to enable vulnerable households and communities meet their dietary needs. The proposed activities are designed to achieve the objectives of the HRP 2021. The interventions will integrate safe programming principles of Do No Harm, COVID-19 SOPs, and ensure Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP).
The project will be implemented by Oxfam directly but in close coordination with key Clusters, including FSL cluster, TWGs AAP and partners operational in targeted project locations. At field level, the coordination will be with county line departments of agriculture and fisheries and other stakeholders. This coordination will be of essential importance to ensure that the project delivery consistent with AAP principles, avoidance of duplication of exclusion of vulnerable communities. The project will pay attentions to other cross cutting issues in delivery of the assistance such as safe programming with Do No Harm lenses, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, inclusion of other groups such as disability and conflict sensitive programming.

1 - Needs

Nominal prices of commodities have continued to spike amidst the deteriorating value of local currency South Sudanese Pounds (SSP) against the United States dollar (USD). According to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) factsheet of January 2021, the cost of the food basket per household per month stood at USD 69 depicting a 9% increase from the cost of the food basket in December 2020. This means household purchasing power is being further depleted, triggering adoption of negative coping strategies including livelihood asset depletion (JMMI January 2021 Factsheet) IPC analysis released on 18 December 2020 projects that between the period December 2020-March 2021, a total of 11,000 people will be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in Pibor, 1785000 people will be in Emergency Phase 4 and 4024000 will be in Crisis phase 3. The situation is projected to deteriorate in the period April-July 2021 in which 31,000 people will be in Catastrophe Phase 5, 2467000 people will be in Emergency Phase 4 and 4743000 will be Crisis Phase 3 (IPC January 2021) An estimated 1,042,000 people have been affected by flooding across South Sudan. Jonglei state and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area are the worst affected areas, with a total of 495,000 people affected. Flood-affected people’s urgent needs include food and livelihood support, emergency shelter and non-food items (ES/NFI), and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), health and protection services. Multisectoral assessments conducted in Pibor in December 2020 revealed that based on their Food Consumption Score (FCS), 65% of the population falls in the Poor category, 31% are categorized as Borderline and only 4% fall within the Acceptable category - with 41.1% falling within low coping strategies, 58.9% in high coping strategies and only 2.0% applying medium coping strategies (Oxfam December 2020). As of 17th February 2021, South Sudan has a total of 5922 recorded COVID-19 cases (212 new cases recorded on 17th February alone), with 3876 recoveries and a total of 79 deaths. The government had declared lockdown measures to curtail the spread of COVID-19, but little has been seen in public in terms of observing Ministry of Health and COVID-19 taskforce measures and the situation remains unpredictable. The prospect that COVID-19 would cease by 2021 as anticipated has instead been overturned by the resurgence of new variants. Despite national vaccine programmes, Oxfam has warned of a long road ahead to reaching equitable access to COVID-19 vaccines in Africa with developed nations representing only 13% of the world’s population and more than half (51%) of the doses of several leading vaccine suppliers. It is clear that the continent is in danger of being left behind, with the world risking a ‘moral catastrophe’ if COVID-19 vaccines are delayed reaching Africa. Continued prevention and response measures to curb the spread of the virus therefore remain essential.

2 - Project activities

Cluster objective 1 activities:Activity 1: Food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds. Through integrated RRM (IRRM), Oxfam will provide fishing and quick maturing vegetable seeds to the most vulnerable households. Oxfam is Cooperating Partner (CP) to WFP implementing the Field Level Agreement (FLA) for 2021 and thus provision of seeds and fishing kits will be a complementary activity to ensure vulnerable households are able to produce their own food to diversify their food sources to meet their dietary needs. Activity 2: Provision of food through GFD blanket/ seasonal/ targeted and GFD plus. Oxfam has signed a Field Level Agreement with WFP to implement this activity in Jonglei Final Distribution Points. Activity 3: Conditional cash transfers. Oxfam will undertake conditional cash transfer activities to promote social skills and safety nets aimed at improving social assets and contributing skills amongst the population to be able to meet their livelihood aspirations.Activity 4: Provision of conditional food assistance. Oxfam has already signed an FLA with WFP to implement conditional food assistance in Akobo, Pochalla and Pibor. Standard food basket rations will be provided in accordance with WFP standards to meet the agreed feeding days. Cluster objective 2 activities:Main Season activity 1: Provision of emergency fishing kits will be an essential response activity in the HRP 2021. Households will be provided with standard fishing kits in accordance with FAO standards of providing emergency fishing kits which comply with inland marine fishing guidelines and policies and regulation of fishing activities in South Sudan. Main Season activity 2: Provision of crop and vegetable kits and tools. Vegetable and crops seeds and assorted tools will be provided as standard kits. 5 types of preferred vegetable seeds selected by the beneficiaries will be provided and a total of 8 kgs for crop seeds will be provided.Dry season activity 1: Distribution of fishing kits and assorted vegetable seeds as a standard kit. Oxfam will distribute fishing and vegetable kits to the most vulnerable households to meet their livelihood assistance during the dry season.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Oxfam has ongoing market-based programming in the proposed locations. It has supported local traders and local market actors to participate in market-based programming. Considerable investments have been made by Oxfam to support the market supply side of these markets including capacity building, support to local storage capacity including for prepositioning etc. Markets are functional in the proposed locations and within reasonable walking distance based on market access Sphere standards.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Markets are functional in the proposed project locations. Oxfam has been working with local markets in the delivery of humanitarian assistance and it is same approach of market based interventions that cash assistance will be implemented in locations which have a functional market and are responsive to increase in demand. Use of cash based assistance has many benefits both social and economic; it gives choice and dignity to the affected population, is easy to support large scale interventions rapidly compared to conventional in-kind assistance, cash revitalizes the economy and has multiplier effects impacts on livelihoods and economic well being of communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Oxfam has a zero tolerance policy on PSEA and will ensure all staff and volunteers are trained on PSEA and sign up to the relevant policy. Staff will sensitize beneficiaries on the PSEA policy and the existing mechanisms for reporting and addressing PSEA concerns safely.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Oxfam will ensure all emergency livelihood activities/assistances are non-discriminatory and impartial ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance. A protection approach to food assistance and livelihood activities will be consistent with humanitarian principles and human rights-based programming that will encompasses a variety of internationally recognized human-rights, and not only the right to food, the right to be free from hunger and those rights directly related to them. The project will avoid, minimize or reduce any unintended negative consequences or impact of their assistance or interventions are committed to a ‘do no harm’ approach to programming. The project team in consultation with community leaders will engage in regular discussions with the most vulnerable groups to receive their feedback on all aspects of the project. Oxfam will ensure equal access of the most vulnerable including persons with disability older people to tangible assistance and implementation, all the implementing staff member will be aware of their specific needs, sex and age sensitivities within the targeted community. The project locations, to and from where the project is implemented will be safe and accessible to everybody. The provision of assistance will not put the beneficiaries at any kind of risk but reduce the threats (Do No Harm) and will ensure vulnerable and those who cannot go through usual procedures will be given equal chances. The project will further ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally without any discrimination and service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against girls, boys, women or men. The feedback complaint mechanisms will be easy to use and impartially accessible by all including the marginalized and those who are illiterate.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Inception meetings will be conducted for community leaders and beneficiaries to provide information about the project and explain the existing Oxfam accountability and feedback mechanisms to ensure that men, women, boys and girls have access to information feedback and complaint mechanisms. Oxfam is using and perfecting suggestion and feedback boxes, help desks, community-based accountability focal persons and recorded audio messages to reach and be reached by the communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries will be reviewed regularly by the project team and used to inform project assistance provided and for future programming.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

- Activities such as inceptions, registrations and distributions will be conducted closer to the population to avoid too long-distance walk- Locations/sites for registrations will be selected in consultation with local communities and authorities- Oxfam will conduct safeguarding for existing and new staff to increase their knowledge on its zero tolerance policy, ways to detect and report concerns and remain with a duty of care in dispensing their duties - All staff must sign Oxfam's code of conduct, safeguarding and associated policies- Locally feasible mechanisms for reporting complaints and feedback will be strengthened including toll free line, word of mouth, written suggestion boxes etc - through staff, community structures and CFM desks- Quarterly CFM dashboard will be produced and shared with key stakeholders, programme staff and senior management to follow up and attend to issues raised- The programming will be rooted in engagement with local communities, structures and local authorities to ensure that they understand the project activities, objectives and entitlements and implementation approach from the outset and engagement will continue throughout- Beneficiary selection will be community-based and led by representatives from population segments who undertake the targeting in line with agreed criteria. This composition will provide self-balance checks which are later verified in another layer to ensure exclusions and inclusions errors are corrected. The CFM desks will also contribute to ensuring that those who feel aggrieved are able to express their dissatisfaction and actions taken accordingly if any.
