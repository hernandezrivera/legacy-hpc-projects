1 - Needs

Early isolation of all cases is perhaps one of the most important public health measure in COVID-19 control. Following the surge of COVID-19 cases June 2020, the Palestinian Ministry of Health MoH issued a new policy promoting home-isolation of people tested positive to COVID with no or mild symptoms. Only who does not have the capacity to properly home-isolate due to specific living conditions (e.g. large family, small housing unit, etc.) or special reasons, may request to be admitted to a dedicated facility endorsed by the authority. Many families in the 19 refugee camps in the West Bank, living with extended family in small homes, found themselves unable to properly self- isolate at home. In response to requests from the community and after an assessment conducted by the ICCG, UNRWA seeks to support community led isolation centres, set up by the communities in Palestine refugee camps, with the provision of non-food items and hygiene materials. Isolation centres have been established within or close to refugee camps in the West Bank to serve all persons in need. UNRWA staff are in daily communication with Camp Services Committees. The majority of the centers have between 10 to 20 beds to host those who cannot self-isolate at home due to a lack of space. The centers have separate rooms or buildings for male and female patients. Currently, community led isolation centers have been set up in Amari, Kalandia, Deir Ammar, Balata, Old Askar, New Askar, Tulkarem, Jenin, Fawwar, and Arroub, refugee camps. Confirmed patients who have mild disease or do not require hospitalization and cannot self-isolate at home could be admitted to temporary isolation facilities managed properly with all IPC measures in place. These persons may need come symptomatic treatment and constant monitoring; hence medical staff should be available at the isolation centers. In case of worsening of symptoms, a referral pathway previously identified with the MoH and local health authorities should be followed. Strict infection prevention and control measures need to be followed. The proposed intervention is in line with the Shelter Cluster Objective 2 (Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity) directly links with Strategic Objective 2 (Basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL). This intervention aims to address the need for adequate isolation space for Palestine refugees living in crowded shelters at higher risk of Covid-19; and to provide adequate and timely standard or customized NFIs to community established isolation facilities. All the locations have been selected by the communities.The project is also in line with the Health Cluster Objective 2 (Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises) and the Health Cluster’s response of scaling-up the COVID-19 response through technical support to the MoH and health authorities. All the centers have been visited and endorsed by the MoH. Finally, the proposed activity targets the most vulnerable who do not have adequate shelters to home isolate in line with the WASH cluster objectives, The proposed response will target the most vulnerable communities through upgrading the WASH facilities in community COVID-19 isolation centers if needed as well provision of individual hygiene kits. Health workers to be deployed in the centers will receive training by the Protection cluster as part of 2020 ICCG response.

2 - Activities

Providing isolation facilities with non-food items and inclusive hygiene material will ensure that conditions of persons in need, especially Palestine refugees, are dignified, in line with humanitarian standards and basic protection principles. Distribution of inclusive hygiene kits that include hygiene items for females and the elderly will ensure that the needs of women and the elderly are taken into consideration in the selection of items in the hygiene kits. The provision of female hygiene supplies has also been identified as an essential part of the COVID-19 response in guidance provided by the Interagency Standing Committee (March 2020). As the Agency has limited capacities to provide support, UNRWA might engage with partners to provide complementary items if needed. As social patterns of infected persons keep changing preparations have been made to accommodate for persons in need according to age and gender. The project estimates a 50% capacity of the centers with persons in needs staying between 10-14 days. That is around 2,080 individuals over a period of 12 months. If in full capacity, up to 6,300 individuals could be accommodated. An effort has been made by Camp Services Committees to identify locations accessible to persons with disabilities, whenever possible. The use of these locations as isolation centers is expected to be temporary while fight against the pandemic continues.UNRWA’s sanitation laborers will provide removal of medical waste from the isolation centers. Monitoring and Evaluation will be based on surveys of the persons in need who stayed in the centers, and collection of information on the number of hygiene kits distributed and the amount of waste removed. This project is based on a previous intervention that has been set up by the Inter Cluster Coordination Group in late 2020. The Protection Cluster is planned to provide training for the staff working in the isolation centers including on stress management and referrals for GBV cases. WASH cluster partners provided inclusive hygiene kits and dignity kits and training on COVID-19 solid waste management for UNRWA's sanitation laborers. The Shelter and Health clusters have provided respectively NFIs and medical kits. This project seeks continuation of these services in 2021 as the pandemic continues in West Bank.UNRWA health services and Camp Services officers will follow up the workflow of the isolation centers after their establishment to ensure an efficient and effective workflow process. Rehabilitation work, if needed, will be review by UNRWA camp infrastructure services. As access to health care is often a primary entry point for GBV survivors, the offering of isolation services will offer at risk groups and survivors a vital means to obtain basic health services, information on available UNRWA GBV hotlines and support, and obtain appropriate referral services.

3 - Indicators

-The success of the centers will be measured by the number of persons in need using the isolation centers and the provision of NFIs and hygiene kits to vulnerable persons in need.-Surveys will be conducted at the end of infected individuals' stay in the isolation centers.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWAs Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneﬁciaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneﬁciaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWAs Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritises the "do-no-harm"-principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its programme service delivery in education, health, relief and social services, microﬁnance, and infrastructure and camp improvement. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of speciﬁc rights such as the socioeconomic rights to food, water, health and education. By addressing such concerns through ‘do-no-harm’ and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneﬁciaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneﬁciaries. Protection needs are addressed in all aspects of regular UNRWA programme and project design, policies, protocols and procedures, as well as in staff training. In all contexts, UNRWA will work to ensure that its assistance and service delivery is carried out in accordance with internationally agreed standards and its own minimum protection standards. UNRWA will regularly measure its level of compliance with these minimum standards and take necessary corrective action.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA as a service provider is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery, and takes it responsibility in this regard very seriously. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. In planning UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, including protection committees, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding numerous focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in communities that we are working with. In the context of the COVID19 response, additional steps taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise the safety and dignity.
