Project Summary

Between September and November 2024, a staggering 6.3 million individuals, accounting for 47% of the assessed population, are classified in IPC Phase 3 or higher, indicating they are experiencing a Crisis or worse. Of this alarming total, 1.71 million people are enduring acute food insecurity at critical levels, marked as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). At the same time, an additional 41,000 individuals confront catastrophic food insecurity, categorised as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The most vulnerable among this group include 10,000 residents of Malakal County in Upper Nile State and around 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled the turmoil in Sudan. This situation represents an increase of roughly 500,000 people categorised in Phase 3 or above compared to the previous year.
Despite notable achievements in reducing malnutrition rates across South Sudan—such as a significant drop in stunting among children under five from 31.1% to an encouraging 15.6% and a decline in acute malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months from 22.7% to 16.2%, down from 11.3% in 2021—the need for ongoing efforts to solidify these gains remains critical. We are called to tackle deep-rooted disparities among various counties and avert further deterioration caused by persistent challenges, including frequent flooding, the aftermath of COVID-19, and widespread food shortages. Alarmingly, about 5.5 million people are at heightened risk of acute malnutrition, encompassing 302,078 children—148,018 boys and 154,060 girls—according to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS).
The Community Resilience Emergency and Support Aid (CRESA) project aspires to bolster nutrition programs, working alongside partners to confront Gender-Based Violence (GBV) in regions like Canal/Pigi, GPAA, Fangak, and Ayod County. Women and girls—particularly those who are internally displaced, residing near cantonment zones, or living with disabilities—continue to be disproportionately affected by GBV amidst the ongoing distressing circumstances related to flooding and armed conflict.
Presently, a staggering 84.7% of the population is facing acute food insecurity, classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 4-5) or worse. This encompasses a vulnerable group of 876,000 individuals, including 302,078 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) (comprised of 148,018 boys and 154,060 girls) and 1,039,214 children (509,215 boys and 529,999 girls) enduring Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Additionally, 675,548 pregnant and lactating women are grappling with acute malnutrition. Among a total of 328,610 refugees residing in South Sudan, 50,638 (or 15.4%) will require urgent treatment for acute malnutrition, which includes 8,388 children with SAM and 26,092 children with MAM, alongside 16,158 pregnant and lactating women facing similar challenges.
To address these dire needs, the project will implement Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP), Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs (TSFP), and Stabilization Centers (SC) in the mobile sites of Pakur, Manajang, Mayiek, and Paak. Community Health Workers (CHWs) will actively engage in community education initiatives and promote best practices in Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF). Essential nutritional supplies, such as Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) and Ready-to-Use Supplementary Food (RUSF), will be distributed to health facilities and mobile outreach programs. As conditions worsen, the entire state is anticipated to experience an Emergency classification (IPC Phase 4) regarding acute food insecurity.
