Project Summary

Since September 2021, arche noVa implements a multisector food security project in rural Ar-Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor governorate. Low rainfall and drought like events during the last years are major reasons for reduced availability of water at households as well as in agriculture sector. Lack of clean drinking water represents the greatest and most comprehensive threat to the population. Restoring the function of water networks and pumping stations is essential for building structural resilience. Agriculture and thus the resilience of the majority of the inhabitants in NES are also significantly dependent on the water and irrigation infrastructure. The restoration of the irrigation facilities is therefore planned for the beginning of the project. The availability of arable irrigated land is a precondition to improve food security in the frame of the project activities. The project will facilitate landless IDP and vulnerable host communities to access land, seeds, livestock and knowledge. Higher crop yields also mean better availability of food in the market and higher incomes and purchasing power. Without increased purchasing power, the economy cannot develop either and young people remain unemployed. Many of the young adults have had little opportunity to attend school in recent years. The establishment of vocational training centres in rural areas will provide opportunity to create a solid basis for self-employment in relevant sectors. Ideally, this will also have a direct impact on the agricultural sector,
The project will benefit from synergies of overlapping location and beneficiaries as well as complementary activities of education and the multisector humanitarian aid project under the HDP NEXUS approach.
Overall objective is it to strengthening the resilience of vulnerable IDP households and host communities through increased food security, income generation activities in relevant sectors, and improved and safe access to safe drinking water and sanitation.
(1) Food security in 8 communities in the project region has increased due to improved access to land, agricultural inputs, crop diversification and improved practices.
(2) The incomes of vulnerable households in the project region have increased as a result of vocational training and self-employment.
(3) Inhabitants of the communities in the project region have improved access to clean water and sanitation and the number of waterborne diseases is reduced.
The project summary illustrates the targets of the whole 4-year's project, while the submission is isolating the results remaining in budget year three of the the project. As such, each sectorial activity for 2024 is part of the whole framework. For 2024, FSL activities focus on cleaning of sub-irrigation channels (Cash for Work), fodder support to existing livestock keepers, food processing trainings and provision of equipment, seed and input support for growing vegetables.
ERL activities focus on two main activties: vocational training of youths in two centers on four vocations per training round (sewing, beautification, house electrics and solar panel installation/maintenance, plumbing, AC and cooling equipment repair and maintenance). The youth will receive a start-up kit after graduation. Additionally, existing small businesses in rural communities are supported by business management trainings and in-kind support to enhance their businesses.
WASH activties are only focusing with minimal remaining budget on rehabilitation of one water stations and water networks, depending on regional priorities, while other activties had been completed in the previous years.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

AAP: Beneficiaries will be involved – if possible – at all stages of the project cycle. FGDs and area needs assessments were held before site selection. The AN MEAL team in NES are monitoring regularly if the target groups are satisfied with the provided services. This will be done by interviews as well as in writing (anonymously) via complaint box, designated E-mail or whatsapp messages. The feedback of the beneficiaries will be considered and included in further project implementation - as much as possible and feasible. AN employees have experience and knowledge of the the environment and the region. They are aware of which activities can trigger conflicts and tensions and have knowledge and experience of conflict resolution mechanisms:- Interaction and information of the population/beneficiaries: Through intensive interaction with beneficiaries and community representatives before each activity, potential conflicts can be identified, avoided or mediated. Regular internal review meetings as well as Focus Group Discussions with local stakeholders will strengthen the accountability. - Make distributions transparent and participatory to minimize risk of community conflict- Sensitive communication to and respectful of local and cultural norms of IDPs and (host) communities. Specific Protection and PSEA Risks:Economic Dependence: Women and men may rely on male relatives or business partners, making them vulnerable to exploitation. Risk of depencdency on new and imported productsMitigation: Involve community feedback in activity selection, prioritize locally accepted materials, AN conducted several FGDs, both mixed and separated to discuss entry points for acceptable support for women in agriculture value chains and about desired vocational training courses in the communities. Harassment and Exploitation: Women in agriculture or small businesses may face harassment and discrimination. Mitigation: Engage local leaders, establish gender-separated distribution lines, use mixed teams for assessments, Regularly monitor and evaluate the impact of interventions on GBV aspects and improving the safety and well-being. Use data and feedback from beneficiaries to refine and improve programming. Limited Access to Resources: Women's limited access to land and credit increases their vulnerability to GBV. Mitigation: Use mixed programming, prioritize Cash for Work as means to include higher percentage of women compared to land-based acitvities, and focus on women-led households and accepted parts of value chains. Social Stigma: Women in entrepreneurial roles face social stigma, risking GBV. Mitigation: in some communities, separation of sexes is still desired – even though the danger of gender segregation exists if maintaining very strict separation, the risk proved higher to expose girls in vocational training centers if any reputational damages were to happen due to lacking communal perception of propriety of conduct in the centers.
