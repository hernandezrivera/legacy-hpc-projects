Project Summary

According to the HNO 2022 draft issued by the Office for the Coordination ofHumanitarian Affairs (OCHA), there are 13.4 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian assistance - an increase of 21% compared to 2021 - with needs increasingly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy.
Statistics indicate an increased exposure to ongoing hostilities, particularly in northwest and northeast Syria. Limited access to services; Displacement, which often separates children from family members, including primary caregivers, disrupting education. 2.45million children between 5 and 17 years of age are out of school, which increases the risks of reported protection, cases of torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, denial, and lack of access to humanitarian assistance due to lack of civil documentation, among other factors. Wars and the obstacles arising from them
. The project aims to reduce the risks of the remnants of war and enhance community awareness of dealing with them by expanding the services provided at the Al-HaramainFoundation Center for Soldiers by increasing the following protection activities interventions: 1. Educating women, men, and children. With regard to the remnants of war through the mobile teams. 2. Implement case management services that address the vulnerabilities of the injured and primary care providers associated with immediate risks. These services will be provided by workers specialized in case of management through Al-Haramain Foundation 3. Executing surveys and identifying places of waste contamination.
4- Building the capacity of workers in the field of case management. Protection activities will be implemented in the Al-Junudiya area of Jisr Al-Shughour and the surrounding area, and the project will target host communities, IDPs, returnees, community centers, and camps. The center will serve the most vulnerable people. All activities align with the 2022Humanitarian Response Plan as well as the Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives and funds Allocation Sheet. Services will be provided for one year. The project will coordinate with affected populations and stakeholders and is designed to promote gender balance. The project aims to include children with special needs, and all activities will be implemented according to the recommendations of the Protection Cluster and the WorldHealth Organization in order to deal with Covid-19 and the necessary preventive measures to protect the beneficiaries and humanitarian workers. The project will be monitored by the theM&E team, in order to monitor the progress of the project and ensure that the activities are implemented in accordance with the agreed work plan and indicators. 4 - Needs

According to the preliminary estimates, 14.2 million people are in need of protection humanitarian assistance, including over 4 million PLWD and over 6.2 children،and According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, Syria continues to be exposed to the explosive ordnance (EO) contamination issue. Based on the information collected during the needs analysis one in three communities in Syria is potentially contaminated by EO. As pointed out in the Humanitarian Situation Report of UNICEF (2021) humanitarian needs in north-west Syria were among the worst in the country with 3.4 out of 4.2 million people in need of assistance. According to the UN during the last few years over 3,5000 explosive ordnance accidents occurred in northwestern Syria. Exposure to explosive ordnance issue spread across all governorates to varying degrees. The exact scale of EO contamination in Syria has not been identified as a comprehensive country-wide survey has not been carried out. The efforts directed at addressing the presence of EO contamination by collecting information as accurately and reliably as possible, as well as identifying the socio-economic and threat factors, are of utmost importance for scaling the clearance of contamination. As to the assessments pointed out in Humanitarian Needs Overview 61 % of key informants surveyed have indicated that they know someone who has been injured and killed by explosive ordnance

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Low level of obligations related to sexual exploitation and abuse, women disproportionately exposure to misconduct by humanitarian workers at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, and low level of safe reporting of complaints of SEA.Mitigation:1 - Impose mandatory training on protection from PSEAon all employees, including management, field staff, day laborers, volunteers, contractors and vendors, in each area where there are active processes to ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and to equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to both. 2 - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from SEA at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.3 - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.4 - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and societyAll project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA
