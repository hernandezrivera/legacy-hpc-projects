Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to a safe and quality education system with a focus on girls. The action will facilitate access to education for out of school children and adolescents to enable them to obtain the bases for integration into the formal education system. The component will integrate gender issues and sensitization to gender-based violence, through teacher training, awareness campaigns and the promotion of equal and safe education for girls, boys, IDPs or host communities. To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The projects result will be achieved through activity packages aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents. To ensure a consistent and comprehensive response, the project will be developed through the prisms of gender and conflict mitigation, which are two concomitant perspectives to the dissemination of the principles of protection in the project.

Besoins / Needs

Access to education: In 2019, the Far North region remain the poorest of Cameroon with the lowest education rate. The primary completion rate is estimated at only 46 %. The Region has been classified by the Government of Cameroon (GoC) as a priority education zone. The International Organization of Migration’s August 2019 Displacement Tracking Monitoring estimates that nearly 428,289 people have been displaced due to conflict in the Far North Including 270, 870 Internally Displaced Persons, 46 845 Out-of-camp Refugees and 110, 574 Returnees. 62% of these population are children with several & critical education needs. Needs of school infrastructure and equipment: Due to the ongoing crisis, classrooms have been destroyed due to vandalism and obsolescence of functional schools are overcrowded, which are suboptimal for teaching and learning. Following data received from Regional office of MoE in 2019, 58 schools remain closed due to the security context. In many of such schools, lack of infrastructure and teachers are reasons for some children to leave school. Needs of teachers: There are currently 281 schools with just 01 Teacher. This translates to a pupil ratio of 1 Teacher /162 Children. This situation has led to a number of children and youth being out-of-school for several years. As these children grow up, their chance and ability to resume school decreases. Communities used to hire local teachers to complement the insufficiency of trained teachers. However, these locally hired teachers lack the technical background and skills to deliver quality teaching for primary school pupils and are regularly underpaid by the community. Need for alternative education: There are few flexible, alternative education opportunities that accommodate those who do not fit into the formal system. There is no data reflecting the extent to which protection risks, security issues, and gender-based violence (GBV) affects education; however according to the education assessment conduct by NRC, cultural practices in the Far North area exacerbate child marriage, especially girls.
