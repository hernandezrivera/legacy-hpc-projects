Project Summary

Project background and purpose
Somalia’s population is currently experiencing multiple shocks including desert locust upsurge that started in late 2019 in many parts of the country; riverine and flash floods that affect the food security, livelihoods and safety of farmers and people living in flood-prone, populated areas and the novel coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic that is having severe health and socio-economic impacts. Based on the cumulative, combined impacts of multiple shocks, FSNAU and FEWS NET projected in May 2020 that around 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least September 2020. An additional 3 million Somalis were anticipated to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. Analysis of household food security survey data collected in the July/August 2020 post-Gu assessment resulted in reduced estimates through December 2020. However, anticipated La Niña conditions are associated with an elevated likelihood of consecutive below-average rainfall seasons, and Somalia faces a high risk that drought conditions will develop by early 2021 while desert locusts could exacerbate crop and pasture losses. As a result, a high proportion of the population that is projected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2) in October-December 2020 could deteriorate to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in early 2021. The Food Security Cluster projects an average of 3.5 million Somalis to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through 2021.
Against the above background, the purpose of the proposed project is that the food security situation of vulnerable and affected households in Somaliland is stabilized and households are more resilient towards drought, desert locusts scourge and other shocks. Specifically, the proposed project will contribute to two objectives including; 1: Improving access to food through conditional and unconditional transfers and 2: Provide seasonal livelihood inputs support (agriculture and livestock). These will be achieved through conditional and unconditional cash transfers, rehabilitation of communal assets and agricultural inputs support.
Target beneficiaries
The project will target at risk and affected populations in rural areas including farmers, agro pastoralists, pastoralists and rural IDPs and host communities to whom humanitarian assistance is key to prevent them from further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation. In total the project will target 1,450 households (10,150 individuals) in the districts of Owdweyne in Togdheer region and Talex and Xudun in Sool regions of Eastern Somaliland and Zeylac district in Awdal region of Western Somaliland. Priority will be given to households headed by disabled persons or elderly persons without support, poor female-headed households, poor households with malnourished children under 5 years and poor pregnant and lactating women, who are at increased risk of malnutrition. The project will be implemented between January to December 2021.
The criteria for selection of the beneficiaries will be guided by food insecurity levels (IPC 3 and 4, based on latest FEWSNET and FSNAU analysis), Structural vulnerability or social status including minority groups, IDPs, women or child headed households, elderly or persons with disability, and families with malnourished children under 5 years

Project Activities

Component 1: IDPs and host community families receive and utilize unconditional and conditional cash transfers in order to improve their access to food and to stabilize their family’s food security. As part of component 1, 1250 households (HHs) will receive cash transfers with 550 households receiving conditional cash transfers and 700 households receiving unconditional cash transfers. The cash transfers will empower beneficiaries with choice to address their essential food needs in local markets, while also helping to boost these markets. Vulnerable households will be empowered to decide about their lives, make choices that improve their food security and wellbeing sustainably. The food cash values to be transferred on a monthly basis are provided by the Cash Working Group (CWG) Somalia. The latest food cash transfer values for families in Awdal, Sool and Sanag are USD 90, USD 70 and USD 60 respectively ( April – June 2020 dash board Produced on 11 July 2020). The target group for the UCT will mainly be vulnerable IDP and host community families facing high levels of food insecurity. The timeframe for cash transfers will be at least 3 rounds to be disbursed to stabilize food security levels in the lean seasons of 2021. The major activities for cash transfers will start with sharing selection criteria in a transparent manner with village leaders and communities. Component 2: IDPs and host community families have access to rehabilitated livelihood and communal assets to enhance their recovery and resilience on individual and community level sustainably. In terms of conditional cash transfers relating to Cash for Work (CfW) activities, the project will target 550 households (Also mentioned in component 1). As part of Cash for Work, communal assets such as rangelands, water pans/ balis will be rehabilitated to provide for sustainable communal livelihood assets. Against the background of the slow onset drought, desert locusts upsurge, rehabilitated livelihood assets represent a crucial tool to enhance collective resilience. In close consultations with affected communities and village leaders, the Cash for Work activities will support a sense of community ownership within this emergency by engaging community members to prepare for the rains in order to have more pasture for the animals and collect more water. The target beneficiaries selection will be based on their physical capacity and vulnerability of their families, such as families with more than three children, or families who lost major parts of their livelihoods among others. The families with men and women eligible for Cash for Work might not be as vulnerable as the families receiving the UCT, however they have a risk of sliding into crisis mode. Beneficiaries shall be paid at a rate of not less than 7 USD each based on a standard piece of work. Candlelight’s Natural Resource Management and water engineer will ensure technical input in order to ensure technical quality and sustainability. Site supervisors will be selected for each of the sites to monitor and supervise the work being done. Component 3: Agricultural inputs support.Difficulties in accessing farm inputs by the majority of agricultural and agro-pastoral households during the main planting seasons and subsequent cropping seasons as a result of increased costs and loss of livelihoods are likely to pose a major challenge to achieving household food security in the target communities. Candlelight therefore proposes to provide agricultural input support to at least 200 households. These households will be supported with assorted and appropriate seeds (cereals, vegetables etc.) and farming tools and equipment. Furthermore, the 200 agro pastoralists and pastoralists will be trained in Good Agricultural Practices (Agriculturalists/ Agro pastoralists) and Improved fodder production and preservation (Pastoralist).

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project design is informed by best practices in cash transfer programming and previous experience by Candlelight. Cash especially unconditional is an ideal intervention because of the freedom and dignity it gives to the beneficiaries. Its has limited administrative and facilitation requirements and reduced risks. Cash transfers especially conditional support to achieve a range of objectives and will result in increased access to food, short term employment opportunities and re-building of communal infrastructure. Cash transfers might directly impact food consumption in various ways such as households using the cash provided to improve the quantity, quality and diversity of food that they consume. Cash transfers might prevent or mitigate negative responses to food insecurity, such as skipping meals. Further, cash transfers can also increase dietary diversity when compared to in-kind food rations because cash can be used to purchase any type of food available. However, in order to minimise the risks related to cash transfers such as using it to buy other household items without prioritising food, beneficiaries will be oriented by the project staff at registration. These will be encouraged to consider buying food items as the number one priority.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Previous market assessments by Candlelight in some of other members in the Food security and Cash Working Group reveal that the majority of the target beneficiaries can easily access food items in the nearby small shops, vegetable markets. It’s also noted that the markets in the target areas can easily cope with the relatively small scale volumes of cash transfers which are also done after close to 30 days. It has also been observed in the previous similar interventions that when traders learn about an upcoming cash transfer activity in their locations, they stock enough food items. Also, small shops/ vegetable markets especially owned by women will open up in the target communities.

Mitigation Measures

To overcome the above risks, Candlelight will undertake the following measures; a) Make Cash transfers through mobile money (ZAAD) which eases access, and promotes dignity and protection of the benefiting households. b) Consider the frequency and rates of transfers and other distributions to avoid any eventual protection risks – Cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash working group of Somalia. c) Provide information on all activities of the project through different methods such as community meetings, group discussions to ensure communication reaches all beneficiaries. d) Candlelight has a child safeguarding and PSEA policy. This will be communicated to all the project staff and mechanisms of reporting abuse will be communicated to all the target communities.e) Ensure that all staff involved in the project have received training in prevention and response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and other protection issues.f) Develop a beneficiary vulnerability criteria that will ensure that beneficiary registration targets the most vulnerable groups in the community including persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, also considering the power relations, specific needs of any group according to age/gender, disability. g) Ensure that the situation of all displaced populations, including those who are not in camps or broader settlements, as well as those dispersed in smaller groups and living with host families is reflected in humanitarian assessments and targeting. h) Adopt and implement protocols to ensure that vulnerable populations, such as women, children, members of minority clans as well as persons with disabilities access humanitarian assistance. i) Promote toll free number which is part of a complaints mechanism to address individual concerns and ensure accountability. Complaints/ feedback will be analyzed regularly and action taken. j) Promote use of alternative complaint and feedback mechanisms such as community meetings, PDMsk) Ensure that all beneficiary data and lists will be firmly restricted to concerned actors and stored safe from unintended use, due to the sensitivity of the information. l) Develop and widely communicate a beneficiary selection criteria which is based on the vulnerability and needs of the target communities

Please provide further explanation

The entitlement of beneficiaries to services/utilities provided will be legally recognized. Candlelight will sign a legally binding contract with the donor agency to provide the stated and approved set of services to a specified number of target beneficiaries. Also as a requirement by the somaliland government, Candlelight will further sign a letter of agreement with the government line ministry/ agency clearly specifying the roles/ responsibilities of the different parties. At community level during the community inception meeting, Candlelight will inform the communities about the kind of services and how much they are entitled to. Lastly but not least, Candlelight will sign a contract with telesom, a cash transfer agency to transfer to specified beneficiaries a stipulated amount of cash on set dates. However for conditional cash transfers, target beneficiaries will be required to first complete an agreed upon amount of work before they are paid.
