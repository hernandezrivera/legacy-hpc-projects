Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen the humanitarian assistance in Rohingya camps and adjacent host communities. This will be achieved through the coordination of services, promoting community participation, reducing the risks and environmental degradation, and increasing access to essential services such as shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) interventions, including Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG). Key project components include service monitoring, Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM), community representation and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), access and safety, capacity building of stakeholders, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) Shelter/NFIs and LPG. Emphasis is placed on community participation, ensuring community leadership in all phases to achieve sustainable outcomes. This will be fostered through camp committees and groups, through capacity enhancement training, and the establishment of platforms for meaningful dialogues with stakeholders, reinforcing community ownership in maintaining camp infrastructure and managing disasters. Furthermore, collaboration will be done with relevant stakeholders to synergize the efforts of serving the beneficiaries harmoniously.
To mitigate multi-hazard risks, the project will implement cash-based Site Development (SD) and DRR interventions. This includes infrastructure maintenance, environmental restoration through reforestation, landslide risk mitigation through slope stabilization, rehabilitation of drainage network for flood prevention, construction and repair of access roads, watershed management, and installation and maintenance of solar energy solutions. The project will strengthen community-based disaster response volunteer capacity and coordination structures and mechanisms.
The Needs and Population Movement (NPM) will support with informed humanitarian and development decision-making through the identification of needs, conditions, and service provision for the refugee and host communities, enabling strong evidence-based response planning by administering thematic assessments, inter-sectoral needs assessment, and assisting ISCG through daily incident reporting, facility mapping, and joint needs assessment framework.
The Shelter/NFI project will enhance DRR, food security, and safety through provision of Shelter, NFIs, LPG to the refugees and host communities. The project will focus on providing clean cooking fuels through new LPG packages and refills, preventing health and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and environmental risks. The project will repair and replace cooking stoves, distribute pressure cookers, and train the community in stove repairs including training on safe cooking fuel use and efficient methods. The Shelter project component includes Shelter Upgrade and Maintenance (SUM) program, construction and upgrade with new Lime Stabilized Soil Shelters (LSS), repair of Mid Term Shelters (MTS), and distribution of NFI vouchers and solar lamps. Post-disaster, emergency shelter and NFI kits will be distributed, either directly or through the pipeline partners. The emergency distributions in Camps 13 and 19 via Caritas and Habitat for Humanity, in Camps 14, 15, and 16 via HEKS, and in Camps 8E, 8W, and 10 via DRC (partially) will be done after Rapid Damage Verification (RDV). IOM will cover the remaining caseload directly. The project will support host community members, prioritizing their safety and well-being through emergency NFI and solar lanterns distribution. IOM will remain the Shelter Focal Point, conduct RDV and multi-hazard preparedness campaigns, preposition emergency S/NFI kits in strategic locations to ensure readiness for disasters.
IOM, through this project in colloboration with IPs/pipeline partners, aims to improve the provision of humanitarian assistance, ensure equitable and safe access, protection, and promote dignified living conditions, through coordinated efforts, inclusive gender and disability practices, and DRR strategies.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Since the Rohingya influx began in 2017, emergency responses have primarily addressed immediate life-saving needs. The crisis still lacks lasting solutions despite the protracted crises. Funding has declined, with the JRP covering only 42% in 2024, limiting service improvements. Increased protection risks and safety concerns have emerged in camps, exacerbated by tensions over resources with host communities. The new arrivals heighten the need for basics amid strained resources. Fluctuating funding and socio-political factors, including changes in leadership, have led to shifts in services and policies, creating a complex humanitarian environment. High turnover among local authorities and a lack of adherence to humanitarian principles calls for continuation of the Capacity Sharing Initiatives. The ISNA-2024 reported that 54.53% of refugee households (HHs) did not use formal complaint channels, and 43.32% of those who made complaints did not receive timely responses. Improving the CFM is essential under the broader AAP framework. The 2023 Women’s Participation Project (WPP) report indicates that government practices in the camps shape decision-making dynamics, with Camp in Charges selecting representatives without ensuring gender parity. Groups of women, men, PwDs, third gender, youth and imams rarely hold influential roles, highlighting the need for an inclusive selection process to foster diverse leadership. High congestion with poor infrastructures makes the communities vulnerable to hazards. Several small fires and major fires in May and June 2024 affected over 235 HHs, and the monsoon season saw 465 landslides impacting 2605 HHs, resulting in injuries and fatalities. The hilly topography and lowlands, comprised of sand and silt, increases their vulnerability to landslides, and flash floods, with recent assessments identifying 9694 high-risk areas within IOM AoR. The effects of natural hazards are compounded by low socio-economic indicators and limited coping mechanisms in both refugee and host communities. Access to services and risk mitigation will drive SD and cash-based interventions. The frequent weather hazards and changes in facilities and services necessitate NPM to support data and gaps need analysis. Addressing challenges posed by disasters requires a robust emergency preparedness and response framework to mitigate impacts on affected populations. Furthermore, ISNA 2024 indicated that 63% of refugees prioritized shelter materials, emphasizing the need for ongoing shelter interventions. Provision of shelter and NFIs remain crucial, accounting for 53% of the top priority needs. Replacement of essential shelter materials such as bamboo poles, tarpaulins, and ropes, emergency shelter response, SUM, repairs, and MTS construction are necessary as it contributes to their protection, security, privacy, and health refugee populations. The SCCCM Sector assessment (2023) found an average of 0.35 portable solar lamps per HHs, below the minimum standard (2 per HH). Providing solar lamps and maintenance activities ensures safety at night within and outside shelters. Critical NFI needs remain unmet for many, even seven years post-influx. LPG refills remain crucial to reduce firewood use. This prevents deforestation and respiratory illness, improves mental health, and reduces inter-group and domestic violence. Consistent access to LPG is essential to meet basic needs and protect restored landscapes, especially given refugees' limited income to afford LPG. Research into alternative energy sources and training in efficient cooking techniques are required. Considering vulnerable host communities' needs, flexible and needs-based humanitarian response to fostering peaceful coexistence is needed. IOM's Bamboo Treatment Facility provides durable materials and enhances host community skills. Continued LPG refills help mitigate conflicts over resources between refugees and host communities, promoting social cohesion.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM places protection at the core of its response. IOM is committed to mainstreaming protection across all its programming incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people IOM seeks to assist. SMS will continuously participate different gender and diverse groups, monitor and report safety risks and incidents of violence through service monitoring, safety audits, and community feedback. It will also coordinate with protection actors and other related stakeholders for emergency response, relocation, complaints, and information campaigns. In disaster situations, SMS will prioritize vulnerable households in camps, ensuring they receive timely assistance.Collaboration with protection actors will be done for addressing safety, security, social cohesion, and other community concerns, and engaged with camp administrator and security agencies as when needed to secure land use agreements, thereby reducing the risk of eviction, conflicts, and high rental costs. Periodic orientations will be conducted with Protection actors on the process for verification and shelter support. All Extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs) will receive porter support to carry shelter materials and LPG from the distribution point to their shelters, and construction support will be provided accordingly. The project includes installing, maintaining, and repairing Solar Street Lighting systems (SSLs) for lighting pathways and open spaces, which will improve access to services and reduce protection risks, especially for people with reduced mobility. Capacity building for IOM staff and partners will focus on monitoring, reporting, and responding to protection concerns, emphasizing the principles of protection and preventing sexual abuse and exploitation (SEA).

2.3 - Age & Disability

Persons with disabilities are among the most marginalized in crisis-affected communities, facing significant challenges during conflicts and emergencies. IOM aims to reduce barriers and enhance the resilience, safety, and protection of these individuals. Committed to their inclusion in humanitarian response, IOM plans to disseminate timely information about specialized services while collaborating with the Age and Disability Working Group and the Child Protection sub-sector to establish standards that address specific needs effectively. During the implementation of SMSD and Shelter/NFI activities, engagement with the youth and elderly will be ensured. This will be done through training to the youth to enable them to support the shelter upgrade and maintenance activities within their communities, training and empowering age-targeted community groups. At the time of data collection, IOM ensures that age and sex-disaggregated data are collected so that different needs, depending on age, are captured and responded to accordingly. IOM will also ensure that there are works suitable for older persons and persons with disabilities to participate in to ensure that they are involved and engaged in the cash-for-work activities to support the SMSD and Shelter/NFI interventions. To avoid the situation where children are carrying the relief items, community meetings will be conducted before distribution to identify the vulnerable households in need of porter support.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Information-sharing on humanitarian programs is ensured through the design, translation, testing, and dissemination of messages for awareness-raising and sensitization—empowering affected populations. Multimedia resources and IEC materials are contextualized for better understanding. Two-way communication is maintained through regular, participatory community engagement and outreach initiatives. A standardized, multi-sectoral CFM is available in all IOM camps for beneficiaries while safeguarding data protection and privacy and complementing referral pathways for sensitive complaints. This activates appropriate processes and procedures for internal and external referral and reply in a timely manner to foster trust. CFM reports are published for transparency. IOM contributes to various forums through collaboration with peers and its leadership position and active membership in the AAP TWG for technical support, principles, and commitments to collective inter-agency responsibility. Aid effectiveness is prioritized through rights-based, people-centered, and community-led programming to uphold dignity and respect. Consultative evidence-generation allows for the tracking of rumors and timely issues of importance to raise voices and contribute to advocacy. A dedicated team supports all IOM programs for a harmonized AAP strategy. During the project’s design stage, consultation with beneficiaries through focus group discussion (FGD) and key informants’ interviews (KII) will be conducted. Orientation and training will be provided to beneficiaries so that eligibility, selection criteria, and processes are clarified, and materials are utilized appropriately. The strong communication channels with protection and site management for referrals continue to be ensured. Post Distribution Monitoring surveys, FGD, and KII will be conducted to capture the beneficiaries’ feedback and the effectiveness of the intervention and reflect the required change in future implementation.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM has Zero Tolerance Policy toward SEA which binds staff members, implementing partners, third party contractors, vendors and service providers. SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution. IOM has a dedicated PSEA Officer, who will continue to roll out PSEA training packages and offer Training of Trainers for PSEA Champions in the coming year to ensure that IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting system. In the initial phase of the project implementation, SEA project related risks will be screened, and mitigation measures identified to enhance safer programming for the affected population. IEC materials will be developed to raise awareness among affected populations about their rights to free assistance, the availability of different reporting mechanisms as well as available referral and assistance services. The PSEA officer, in collaboration with the program teams, will conduct regular consultations and focus group discussions with beneficiaries to enhance their awareness of PSEA and address any concerns they may have. The field teams in all the camps will have the skills and capacity to report SEA cases adhering to PSEA principles. An annual PSEA and Code of Conduct refresher training will be conducted with all staff, including the Rohingya volunteers. All staff and volunteers working in the camps understand, sign, and adhere to a Standards of Conduct. Daytime distributions with security presence prioritize vulnerable groups, such as households with disabled members, and women-headed and child-headed households. Regular community conulstaion on SEA risks and prefered communication channels are conducted. Separate distribution lines and breastfeeding corners are available, and porter support assists those unable to carry S/NFIs and LPG cylinders.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM is committed to aligning its response with relevant disaster risk reduction frameworks and climate change adaptation efforts. These include slope stabilization measures to prevent landslides and soil erosion, improvement works to enhance accessibility to essential services, and drainage network construction and maintenance to reduce flooding risks. Plantation support environmental restoration, while watershed management enhances soil and water quality with a focus on nature-based solutions (NbS). In addition to utilizing NbS for landslide mitigation and watershed management aimed at supporting the local ecosystem and promoting sustainability, IOM is exploring alternative construction materials, including lime, for DRR and Shelter works to enhance environmental sustainability. The installation and maintenance of solar streetlights, along with the solar-powered facilities, serve as practical and effective solutions for reducing carbon emissions and promoting sustainability. IOM ensures that all bamboo used in shelter interventions are treated to prevent the poles from being infested by insects and rotting due to moisture/humidity, to prolong the lifespan and decrease the frequency of their replacement. Strict quality control measures are applied when procuring bamboo to avoid suppliers harvesting premature bamboo unsuitable for shelter program usage. The provision of LPG prevents deforestation by switching the energy source away from firewood. Annually, LPG utilization prevents the extraction of 330600 tons of firewood from 6000 hectares of forested area around the camps and avoids the emission of 407000 tons of carbon dioxide. IOM also avoids use of plastic packing materials, promoting biodegradable materials that can be used continuously after distribution. NPM will support with timely reports, capturing data on weather- and human-related damages in the camps, along with trend analysis to monitor natural events during cyclone and monsoon seasons.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The disaster-prone nature of the camps, which are susceptible to perennial disasters such as fires, cyclones, and monsoons, means that emergency preparedness and response (EPR) remain critical and vital programming aspects. IOM intends to further strengthen its EPR mechanisms, such as a multi-hazard response and preparedness plan that is regularly updated in collaboration with relevant stakeholders. Additionally, a critical business continuity plan will be revised to address perceived potential scenarios, ensuring continuity during times of disaster.IOM is a member of the central core team of the EPR working group, providing technical support and contributing to collective efforts alongside other partners. IOM will further enhance community response capacity through community structures, such as disaster management unit volunteers, by ensuring relevant training and preparedness activities such as simulations and drills . This effort will be further strengthened by equipping DMU buildings, which will build community resilience and enable them to be effective first responders. IOM will preposition emergency shelter kits and NFI at strategic locations across all 17 managed camps. This will allow IOM to promptly respond to sudden onset disasters, including cyclones, monsoons, and fire outbreaks. These items will be stored at strategically chosen sites within IOM’s AoR, with allocations covering up to 30% of Ukhiya camps and 100% of Teknaf camps. All distributions of emergency shelter and NFI distribution will be aligned with the RDV.IOM will maintain ongoing cyclone and monsoon preparedness within its AoR. NPM will continue to support humanitarian actors by providing timely information through daily and weekly incident reports and datasets. These reports capture key data on damages from weather and human-related incidents in the camps and offer trend analysis to monitor the impact of natural events throughout the cyclone and monsoon seasons.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project will continue to engage youth groups and leaders from the community who have benefited from various capacity-building opportunities. The goal is to involve these youth in identifying solutions and implementing small-scale projects that address specific problems in their community while enhancing their ability to carry out these initiatives. Other initiatives, such as community monitoring and environmental restoration efforts, will also target youth groups and include a capacity-building component. In collaboration with other agencies, skills development will be offered to members of the youth groups. Additionally, through the Community-Led Project, the SMS team will ensure that youth participate in efforts that contribute to community resilience and reduce criminal activities within the camps. This approach aims to keep young people engaged and active, preventing idleness. Furthermore, youth are involved in cash-for-work activities, which require participants to be 18 years or older. These activities provide opportunities for skill development and knowledge sharing while also contributing to livelihood improvements as well as supporting SMSD and Shelter/NFI activities. Additionally, IOM provides carpenter training for young people, helping them develop construction skills. During the program inception phase, the youth community also participates in FGD, ensuring their perspectives and insights are incorporated into future initiatives.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project aims to build the capacity of refugees through training, simulations, and on the job engagement. IOM’s skills and knowledge-sharing initiatives, designed for community volunteers and leaders including disaster management units focus on community-led projects and skills development training. These efforts enhance community resilience to natural disasters and social challenges. Moreover, some of these community-led projects include income-generating components that can foster self-reliance. The IOM Shelter/NFI program emphasizes skill and knowledge transfer through capacity-building initiatives, empowering refugee populations to construct and maintain their own shelters. The cash-for-work approach provides refugees with both skills training and livelihood opportunities, helping them develop resilience to economic shocks. This initiative promotes self-reliance among refugees, especially in the context of decreasing humanitarian funding.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

IOM Shelter NFI will provide support exclusively to host community households located near camps that have been impacted by sudden disasters and are in need of emergency shelter assistance, whereas the IOM SMSD project, especially the SD works will indirectly benefit the host community living inside the camps. These activities are not associated with development initiatives nor aligned with the development priorities of the Cox’s Bazar district.
