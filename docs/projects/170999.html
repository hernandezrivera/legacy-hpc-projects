Project Summary

Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRC) will improve access to food to the most vulnerable urban poor and displaced (IDPs and returnees) while strengthening access to seasonal livelihood inputs support to non-displaced rural in Qardo, Tallex and Hudun districts.
The food security interventions will have conditional, and unconditional cash and vouchers transfers as modalities of interventions with each modality guided by market dynamics- availability of goods in the food baskets within the local markets, ability to source and prices, and availability of service providers for respective modality.
Unconditional transfers will target vulnerable beneficiaries- pregnant and lactating women, under-five children, elderly and sickly. Conditional transfers will target labor-rich but vulnerable households or communities as well as pregnant and lactating women linked to nutrition-sensitive food security interventions for instance nutrition classes. The conditional transfer will also target the rehabilitation of community productive assets. The beneficiaries will do jointly identified productive community assets- feeder roads, market infrastructures, livelihood infrastructures including secondary irrigation canals, dykes, river embankments. The vouchers will target the rural and urban poor. Social Mobilization and community sensitization will precede any screening and registration.
The interventions will benefit 7632 beneficiaries who will be screened and registered (displaced- 3,119, non-displaced-4,513) and vulnerable beneficiaries HouseHolds supported with the provision of conditional and unconditional transfers, and livelihood support.
Livelihood support will include the provision of agricultural input support; seeds/seedlings, fertilizer, irrigation hours, technical support to farmers, and farm implements. Livestock support will entail; range cubes, emergency vaccinations, multi-nutrient blocks, destocking, and restocking, reseeding rangelands and rangeland management support, emergency water trucking. Rehabilitation of livestock infrastructure will be undertaken (water infrastructures, cattle dips).
On access to food assistance, QRC will undertake the provision of conditional transfer support through cash or voucher to 2,805 vulnerable households ( 9,256 female, 7,574 males) in Qardho, Taleex, and Hudun districts. The provision of unconditional transfer to 3,428 vulnerable households (12,340 females,8,228 male). 1400 drought and flood-affected households with livelihood will be supported by input support (400 agricultural and 1,000 livestock). (Female =4,620 Male=3,780).
Post distribution monitoring will be undertaken to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention as well as its effect on the market.

Project Activities

1. Mobilization and sensitization of communities at the district level as well as elders representing all sectors of the community on the project purpose. One mobilization session will be held per project location during the project duration per district. The social mobilization will be done through the local authority and other stakeholders including district elders and representatives of all communities in Qardho, Taleex, and Hudun districts. At least 5% of the population beneficiaries will be Persons with Disability (798 households,4788 Persons with Disability beneficiaries). 2. Undertake market assessments to gauge the capacity to effectively respond to project requirements. At least one market assessment will be undertaken per district. 3. Screening and registration of 7,632 (displaced- 3,119, non-displaced-4,513), vulnerable beneficiaries Households for the provision of conditional and unconditional transfers, and livelihood support. 4. Provision of conditional transfer support through cash or voucher to 2,805 vulnerable households ( 9,256 female, 7,574 males) in Qardho, Taleex, and Hudun districts. The conditional transfer will target 1,122 lactating mothers attending nutrition classes through nutrition-sensitive vouchers based on the market assessment. Additionally, 1,683 beneficiaries will do jointly identified community assets- feeder roads, market infrastructures, livelihood infrastructures including secondary irrigation canals, dykes, river embankments. This group of beneficiaries will be targeted through cash for work. Further, the women-led households will be given priority through engagement in light jobs that support the core conditional transfer works. 5. Provision of unconditional transfer to 3,428 vulnerable households (12,340 females,8228 males) in Qardho, Taleex, and Hudun districts. The transfer modality will be either cash or voucher, based on market assessment. 6. Support of 1,400 drought and flood-affected households with livelihood support (400 agricultural and 1,000 livestock support). (Female =4620 Male=3780). Agricultural livelihood support will include seeds, farming implements, fertilizers, irrigation hours, and agricultural technical support. Livestock interventions will target vulnerable households with reduced mobility, as these will be among the most affected by limited feed availability. Livestock-based livelihoods will be protected through the provision of supplementary feed where pasture has been severely affected by desert locusts. These will include range cubes or multi-nutrient blocks, water for livestock through infrastructures rehabilitation, seeding of community pastures, training on rangeland management, emergency vaccinations, and emergency water supply. Other support will include destocking and stocking. The transfer for livelihood support will be through vouchers. Where plausible cash plus will be adopted for the severely affected households responding to the market analysis. 7. Undertake post-distribution monitoring and evaluation to see the effect of the intervention on the households and the market.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project justification for adopting cash and vouchers:The ability of the markets to supply goods and services at an appropriate price and quality. The project locations under consideration have an adequate number of vendors who can adequately handle cash transfers or voucher redemption. Access of beneficiaries to service and good providers: The beneficiaries will be required to travel short distances to redeem the transfers. Predicted effectiveness: The different transfer modalities have been considered to meet the appeal desired outcomes in the areas under consideration. Additionally, they offer timeliness in their delivery. Different kinds of transfers will result in different results since people will rarely buy exactly the same items that are chosen for them by aid agencies (e.g. they may prefer to buy cheaper or different kinds of food). Beneficiaries' actual choices are hard to predict since different households will make different decisions, based on their circumstances and priorities. The method chosen will be based on an assessment of how sensible any assumptions (e.g. about likely expenditures or consumption outcomes) and how well-reasoned the link is between the choice of transfer and desired outcomes at the decision-making stage. Timeliness is an important aspect of effectiveness, particularly in emergencies. The time required to set up and deliver an intervention is considered in decision-making when urgency is a priority. Impact: Potential impact beyond immediate project objective: Potential desired outcomes could include supporting the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs (i.e. not those covered by the objective), providing recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets, and promoting financial inclusion. Efficiency and cost: Cash and voucher transfers are considerably lower and require less effort to deliver in comparison to in-kind. Protection and gender: The transfer and delivery mechanisms aids are accessible to all genders and those who face constraints related to gender, age, disability, and other factors influencing access. The effect of transfers on the safety, dignity, and integrity of recipients is a consideration.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project considers that the feasibility of cash and vouchers rests on the expected future ability to appropriate price and quality and markets to respond to increased demand if cash or vouchers were provided as the modality. Thus, access of beneficiaries to markets and not simply on the state of the market before any intervention. As part of planning, consideration of possible measures that could be taken to address any market constraints (e.g. addressing constraints facing traders, such as capital, credit, storage, and transport) are factored. Notably, seasonal, local, regional, and global trends can all influence prices. Possible price rises caused by external factors do not rule out the use of cash and vouchers if they can be managed through the program design. If in-kind assistance/voucher is purchased locally or regionally, the ability of traders to ensure timely supply at an appropriate price and quality may be critical. The potential impacts on markets of different types of transfers should also be considered. Capacity: Some market players have more experience managing different types of transfers. There is a responsibility to provide appropriate assistance, and so choices should not be justified based only on pre-existing skills and experience: there is an obligation to ensure that necessary market skills and experience are recruited. However, it is unreasonable to expect the capacity to deliver ‘new’ transfer types to be built up immediately. Thus, evaluation should consider whether consideration is given to the vendor's ability to build its capacity to the scale of the project being undertaken – a massive, urgent emergency response may not be the time to depend on newly acquired skills. Delivery Mechanisms: The project's reliable delivery mechanism will be determined by the vendor supply chain., financial sector, and Communications Technology (ICT) capacity or in-kind systems. Qatar Red Crescent will consider the different delivery mechanisms that could be used with different transfers. The choice of transfer will consider the reliability of delivery mechanisms that exist or can be established.

Mitigation Measures

The risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.-Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence.-Install lights outside and on the access route to the distribution centers.-Use crowd control mechanisms including ensuring different lines for genders.-Staggering registration/distribution times/days to reduce overcrowding that could trigger violence. The risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities.-Ensure FSL staff and relief committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability).-All staff and members receive protection mainstreaming training. -Sensitize staff in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented. -Minority groups' representatives will be sought, engaged, and consulted in planning projects and implementation. -Ensuring registration/distribution is accessible to Persons with Disabilities.-Collect accurate information about marginalized groups' specific needs and preferences for registration/distribution center location, design, and services. The risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms-Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that does not require the beneficiary to expose themselves to project staff.-Respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place.-Organize awareness-raising sessions so that people know how it the mechanism works.-Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion. Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.-Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).- In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of workers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.-Siting of project centers in areas that do not pose risks-Ensuring activities are completed on time to reduce exposure during beneficiaries accessing and returning.

Please provide further explanation

Qatar Red Crescent will undertake all measures for ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability, and participation of beneficiaries. By mainstreaming protection into programming, QRC will maximize the positive impacts of food security programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations' access and enjoy their rights. Key actions undertaken will include:-Prioritise safety & dignity and avoid causing harmEnsure the location and access routes to services are safe. Data confidentiality.Avoid community tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to services.In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents. -Promote equitable and impartial access for all-Collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming.-Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.-Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the project locations are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. Community Engagement -The project will work with the existing community and local structures from the project outset. These include community elders, existing project committees, and local authorities.-Identify power dynamics in the area and within households and adequately respond, strengthen, and integrate into the project. Use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. -Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them to reduce tensions. -Set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct.-Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to their needs.
