Project Summary

Acute malnutrition is a public health concern in South Sudan driven by both natural disasters and manmade disasters. The GAM prevalence in the Country is 16.0% (FSNMSN 2022) higher WHO thresholds of <15%. Approximately 2.1 million people are at risk of acute malnutrition, with 1.4 (SAM 348,890, and MAM 1,057,700) million children under five years and 700,000 pregnant and lactating mothers expected to suffer from acute malnutrition (FSNMS Report 2022). According to Nutrition cluster PIN, (2024), the GAM rates for Raga, Wau, Budi and Terekeka Counties are 9.6%, 14.0%, 12.1%, 16.7% and 18.3% respectively with total people in need of 93,101 (Wau County 37,023, Raga County 8,320, Budi County 20,397 and 29,115 for Terekeka County) for SAM, MAM and PLW (NC PIN 2024). These GAM rates are high requiring targeted interventions.
This project will be implemented in Counties of Budi, Terekeka, Wau and Raga targeting total of 332,162 beneficiaries. Through early detection and case management, targeting, 14,185 for SAM, 32,249 for MAM, PLW will be 19,276,) and 266,452 shall be reached through preventive activities (MIYCN 54,657, VAS 11,146 and deworming 95,649) in all the four Counties with estimated cost of $1,262,608. The project intends to address the following objectives; 1) to increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2024; (2) to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in prioritized locations by the end of 2024; (3) to enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2024; and (4) to strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2024. In order to achieve the above objectives, AFOD will adopt combination of strategies for this nutrition response such as establishment of static services, mobile OTP and TSFP site, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening and assessment of immunization status using child health card, distribution of LLITN), WASH (provision of safe water, soap for hand washing, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL (vegetable gardening, cooking demonstration, and ), Education (establish ECD kits) and protection especially on GBV basics, including how to safely and appropriately respond to GBV disclosures, for complementarity and synergy and preposition of nutrition supplies for desired treatment outcome. Key stake holders shall be engaged in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complaint desks, and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback.

1. Needs

Acute malnutrition is a public health concern in South Sudan driven by both natural disasters and manmade disasters, particularly floods and dry spells that affect agriculture, disrupt livelihoods, and hamper humanitarian access, conflict leading to loss of productive assets as well disrupting humanitarian intervention. The economy is experiencing economic crisis caused by the depreciation of local currency and low productivity due to flooding that affects farmland, conflict and traditional farming methods. Evidence from IPC, 2023, show that 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.16 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM), and 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. The key drivers of food insecurity are mainly the economic crisis, forced displacement and disruptions of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance because of localized conflict, insecurity, and climatic shocks (flooding, dry spells). In Western Bahr El Ghazal State, an estimated 274,000 people (49% of WBG population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2023, of which 54,000 are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 220,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity (IPC, 2023). While in Central Equatoria State, an estimated 622,000 people (47% of CES population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2023, of which 175,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 447,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. All the 6 counties in Central Equatoria are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. In Eastern Equatoria State, an estimated 364,000 people (37% of EES population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2023, of which 119,000 people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 245,000 people are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Budi County like other 6 counties is classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. The key drivers of food insecurity include conflict, insecurity, raiding, violence and looting, dry spells, weed and pest infestations and lack of agricultural tools that have impacted negatively on crop production and first season harvest (IPC, 2023).Wau, Jur River, Terekeka, Budi and Raga Counties are categorized in IPC Phase 3, due to high burden of acute malnutrition caused by food insecurity arising from low crop production, insecurity/inter-communal conflicts, diseases burden and high food prices (IPC, 2023). The situation is likely to worsen due to climatic shocks (floods, dry spells, and droughts) which are in extremes, continued insecurity, population displacements, persistent annual cereal deficits, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, limited access to basic health services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion that continue to erode households’ coping capacities, and the loss of livelihoods (IPC July, 2023). According to Nutrition cluster PIN, (2024), the GAM rates for Raga, Wau, Budi and Terekeka Counties are 9.6%, 14.0%, 12.1%, 16.7% and 18.3% respectively with total people in need of 93,101 (Wau 37,023, Raga 8,320, Budi 20,397 and 29,115 for Terekeka) for SAM, MAM and PLW (NC PIN 2024). These GAM rates are high which requiring interventions to reduce malnutrition rates. It is on this basis that AFOD is submitting this proposal for funding.
