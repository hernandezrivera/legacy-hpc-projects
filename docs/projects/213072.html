Project Summary

According to HNO 2024, deteriorating economic conditions, longstanding and ongoing impacts of conflict, and poor access to basic services continue to drive widespread humanitarian needs across Syria. Lack of electricity access exacerbates pressures on households: Access to electricity remains a critical challenge in Syria, exacerbating household pressures. In northeast Syria (NES), only 46.2% of households are served by the main network, leading to a heavy reliance on neighborhood generators for a mere 7.63 hours of electricity daily. Recent airstrikes have further damaged NES's electricity infrastructure. In northwest Syria (NWS), there's a notable shift towards renewable energy, with 54% of households using solar panels, managing an average of 15 hours of electricity per day, signaling an adaptation to the energy crisis. Escalation in severity of conditions following earthquake: Recent data highlights a significant escalation in hardships within certain northwest Syria (NWS) sub-districts following the earthquake, marking a rapid deterioration compared to last year.
Protection Risk Analysis: Risk: Poor coordination among cluster members might lead to duplication of assistance. Mitigation: 1. Constantly liaise with the SNFI Cluster, submit 4Ws and plans, attend Cluster meeting and seek Cluster support prior to the implementation; 2. Coordinate with other actors to prevent duplication; 3. Coordinate with local councils and being active in field coordination groups. Risk: poorly planned distribution of cash might expose beneficiaries to risk of exploitation. Mitigation: 1. Be open with the community about the project from design to implementation; 2. Use clear targeting criteria; 3. Work closely with the community leaders; 4. strong data management. Risk: Eviction from shelters units and camps due to lack of approval from property owner. Mitigation: 1. Work closely with local authorities to ensure there is a written approval from the owner for a specific period to use the property to develop the site; 2. Start developing the site only after written approval; 4. Advocate with local authorities to find alternative accommodations for those at risk of eviction. Risk: Risk of exclusion from shelter assistance due to lack of civil status document/ID. Mitigation: Work closely with communities, community leaders and authorities to identify alternative way of identification.
PESA: The project aims to address the heightened risks of neglect and exclusion faced particularly by women and girls with disabilities in NW Syria. Those individuals often experience neglect and isolation within their families and communities, making them more vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). To reach both genders, the project ensures a gender-balanced team. All staff receive training on protection principles, preventing SEA (PSEA), and gender-based violence (GBV). Additionally, focal points for PSEA and GBV are designated in each area, and a feedback system is established to facilitate reporting of complaints and suggestions from beneficiaries.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Through this project, HIHFAD aims to meet immediate and protracted humanitarian needs through (1) Supporting the maintenance of, and improving equitable access to, basic services; (2) Increasing access to livelihoods and job opportunities. Sector partners will undertake activities including rehabilitating promoting renewable energy, building service provider capacity, providing material and equipment support to services providers, and mainly for bakeries in addition to conducting light rehabilitation of schools.10 schools will be rehabilitated and provided with focus on schools that are out of service due to the earthquake or conflict. the schools will be made accessible to all students taking into consideration the needs of children with disabilities by construction ramps, hand-rails where needed in the entrance and in the WASH facilities. 15 bakeries will be rehabilitated and equipped with solar system in addition to parts of production line the rehabilitated bakeries will be accessible to men, women and PWDs through rehabilitation the selling point of each bakery so access is enhanced. The bakeries rehabilitatio and school rehabilitation will include cash for work activities. The project will also include rehabilitation of two water pumping stations involving the mechanical and civil rehabilitation to these stations and make them operational. Before the implementation starts, the people in the targeted locations will be consulted with focus of women and PWD this will be through a survey for men and women and through focus group discussions for girls and boys and their preferences will be taken into account. the selected school will also contain both girls and boys students to ensure that the assistance reach all equally. after identifying the persons with disabilities in the targeted locations, they will be consulted about the barriers and HIHFAD will try to address these barriers whether in the accessibility to schools or bakeries.
