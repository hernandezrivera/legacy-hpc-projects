Project Summary

Over the past 10 years, the delivery of health services to over 4 million displaced persons living in northwest Syria has been provided by a wide range of relief and non-governmental agencies, both national and international. To ensure a common standard of care delivery and enable coordination among these various providers, the WHO Gaziantep office has developed an Essential Health Services package in 2016-17. The package describes a standardized priority care package, including specific human resource, medicine, equipment and infrastructure requirements as well as standardized operating procedures for priority health areas (including reproductive maternal neonatal and child care, non-communicable diseases and mental health among others).
The implementation of this package in Idlib governate was promoted through an integrated service delivery network in Harim District, which has been piloted since October 2017. Having evolved over nine phases, UDER is currently including 65 facilities within phase 9 of the Harim network (with a potential further expansion during this phase) providing different levels healthcare in terms of complexity and capacity (PHCs, hospitals, specialized centres and mobile units). The approach aims at integrating the operations of health facilities and ensuring a coordinated and comprehensive delivery of care services, with an ultimate aim of increasing access to health services equally for the displaced and host population in the region and ensuring efficient service delivery.
The project relies on WHO's health system building blocks to introduce a transition from the emergency service-based health response in NWS to a more robust health system through a phased approach which introduces upgrades to PHC networks in each of the six health system building blokes. It established local governance/coordination bodies at the field and management levels to oversee the implementation of these upgrades with the overall aim of reestablishing a robust system that connects and mainstreams healthcare services across all health facilities in NWS.
UDER has been leading this project over the past two years, and succeeded in expanding the number of participating facilities to 65 and the partners to 13 in the 9th phase. The project was independently evaluated and was praised for its impact and partner satisfaction.
The plan is to continue this project and expand to 80 facilities covering most of districts of Harim, Jisr Ash-Shoghour and Idleb and west Aleppo.

3 - Needs

In 2019, WHO conducted revision of EHSP package, during which critical recommendations were identified by the technical team for WHO’s follow up. This project aims at continuing the network expansion and applying key recommendations from the recent EHSP exercise to continue focusing on short term stabilization of improving health service delivery and building medium-term resilience, as follows: i. By ensuring access to essential primary healthcare services and complimentary specialized services, the project will increase overall equity in access to healthcare and ensure continuity of care while improving the health status.ii. Build medium-term resilience through upgrading the capacity of PHCs, strengthening the skills of health workers, and install a well-established mechanism to promote integration of services and de-centralized coordination. iii. In the longer term, the project may serve as a platform for strengthening PHC systems The project is comprised of four components: a) provision of essential healthcare services to catchment population; b) planning, management & organization of network of health facilities to enhance efficiency and ensure coherence among providers in terms of service delivery and undertake support functions systematically through a network coordinatorc) capacity building of contracted service providers; and d) project management and monitoring The network will link and coordinate the operations of 65 facilities of different levels.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project employs 3 different teams to deliver and oversee the implementation of the following:1) Monitor the infection prevention and control practices and triage procedures at the network health facilities.2) Ensure a better commitment to referral protocols within the Harim referral mechanism. 3) Support the implementation of the Essential Healthcare Service Package4) Support the commitment to standard referral procedures5) Expand the capacity of human resources for heath within the network6) Address critical needs in health facilities in terms of equipment and rehabilitation. 7) Improve the implementation of a standard Health Information Management System8) Strengthen the medical supply chain management9) Strengthen grievance management and patient feedback mechanism10) Promote a standard outreach service through CHWs linked with health facilities11) Provide health facilities with equipment and supplies to strengthen the covered technical components12) Strengthen health facility management systems13) Promote guidelines and SOPs for rational use of antimicrobials in the network facilities14) Build the capacity of health staff on more advanced components according to the needs of the health system (screening of CD, NCD, RH conditions) The teams include:-A network coordination team comprising NGO representatives and local heath actors .-A managerial team comprising facility managers-A technical team comprising technical supervisors and trainers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Discrimination in health treatment: Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation: The Tech Team makes regular visits to the facilities, conducts exit interview with beneficiaries and promote a specific component on confidential complaint mechanism using partners' communication channels.-Health staff are infected with COVID-19.Probability: low. Risk severity: moderate. Mitigation: Facilities are provided with PPE, and trained on IPC. The Tech Team monitors the adherence to IPC measures and encourages partners to adhere to isolation measures.-Household data is exposed to leakage or destruction. Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation: - Anonymized beneficiary data is collected through Kobo surveys using smart phones on an online data base stored on a server that can only be accessed by Turkey staff. This prevents data storage on hard or soft copies in Syria which are exposed to be stolen or destroyed. Family records are coded anonymously before sharing with other parties.-Tech team or CHWs are harassed by local entities. Probability: moderate. Risk severity: high. Mitigation measures: CH team members are recruited from the local community to make sure trust is exchanged between them and communities. The project is backed by local health authorities (Independent Health Directorates) and community.-Tech team sustains injuries or accidents during rounds. Probability: low. Risk severity: moderate. Mitigation: staff trained on safety instructions using UDER's car


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project promotes PSEA measures at all HFs.Abuse or harassment by CHWs or medical staff towards patients and HH members. Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation measures: -Existence of female CHWs and limited the recruitment of male outreach workers. Organizing teams in two CHWs per household visit and conducting random supervision visits by supervisors; -Training staff on PSEA, code of conduct and ethical data collection; -Ensuring the commitment of staff to UDER’s PSEA, Child Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies; -Contracts including a strict term of dismissal towards any misconduct of SEA; -Maintenance and enhancement of Complaint and Feedback mechanism including hot lines for receiving reports and allegations of SEA misconducts; -Regular surveys conducted by MEAL team to monitor the conducted visits. Surveys include question about team members behaviors, misconducts and their respect of privacy; -Allegations are seriously investigated, where aid workers are subjected to contract termination and further disciplinary actions if allegations were proven. Awareness of GBV and PSEA issues will be incorporated into project activities and beneficiary satisfaction will be verified during household and facility visits which will include female employees to facilitate collecting suggestions/complaints from female beneficiaries especially in relation to GBV and PSEA. All of the CHWs under this project are trained on the PSEA policy during their initial induction.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:-Permission for UDER to operate in Idleb and Aleppo continues.-Staff infected with COVID-19 do not exceed 2% at all times.-Community acceptance for this project continues.-Current conflict doesn't affect program activities.-Drugs and medical supplies continue to be received through WHO medical supply-line. -Complementary projects continue to be in place, including referrals and Harim PHC network. Risks:-Potential increase in violence in Idleb and Aleppo through a military offensive or fights among Organized Armed Groups. Probability: moderate. Risk severity: high. Mitigation: UDER continues monitoring security conditions and receiving security briefing through INSO and UNOCHA. Staff continue to receive orientation on safety and security procedures. Safety and security policies and procedures are regularly revisited to adapt to context changes.-Most of the staff is infected withCOVID-19 and activities are suspended or terminated. Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation: Staff are trained on COVID-19 preventive measures, provided with PPE, monitored to strictly commit to IPC. A record is maintained on infected and isolated staff to prevent a major infection spread within the project team; -Acceptant of Tech team decreases: UDER continues sharing information about activities, consult partners before dispatching Tech team, consult communities and health directorates about CH activities. Strengthen coordination with local health directorates.
