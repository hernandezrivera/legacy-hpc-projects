Project Summary

Repeated episodes of sub-national conflict, general insecurity and adverse weather conditions in South Sudan, coupled with an avalanche of returnees fleeing the Sudan crisis have plunged the country into a protection crisis that has lasted for many years. According to UN-OCHA, an estimated 9.18 million South Sudanese are at risk of protection concerns, with 1.58 million IDPs and 840,000 returnees being in need of humanitarian support. Apart from displacement, killing of men, women and children, high levels of violence, rape, as well as abductions have been reported in multiple parts of the country, with housing, land and property rights violations, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as other human rights abuses being widespread.
In many Unity and Jonglei states, cattle raiding conflicts, displacement by flooding, clashes over housing land and property, have further compounded the protection concerns in these counties. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic proportions. A rising number of IDPs and returnees has worsened the protection situation, with UN-OCHA estimates putting the number of persons in need of protection services at 5.6 million.
Women and children are sexually abused by family enemies during cattle raising and cases of revenge killing. Such cases are not reported due to shame and embarrassment. Abused women lack access to essential services in GBV case management, and children suffer unattended, sometimes being abducted by armed thugs. Displaced communities lack access to basic needs, stay in congested areas without access to water and sanitation and have no access to redress mechanisms. Congestion for those internally displaced does not provide privacy to women and girls. Children have invariably been affected by the repeated cycles of violence and are equally in need of protection services.
Other protection concerns that have increased include child labor, child marriage, human trafficking, grave violations such as killings, targeting of civilians, child abuse, restriction of freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abduction, and constraints on safe and equitable access to services. This project will target 40,000 individuals in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Guit, Rubkona, Fangak, Uror, Pibor, and Pariang counties of South Sudan.
Coalition for Humanity proposed the following activities:
1. Provision of protection monitoring, risk assessments and analysis of protection concerns
2. Provision of capacity building trainings to local authorities and humanitarian staff
3. Provision of capacity building trainings on PSEA and Psychological First Aid (PFA)
4. Awareness creation on key protection concerns through outreach activities
5. Provision of case management, referral and individual protection assistance for vulnerable persons
6. Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) through women and girls friendly spaces
7. Strengthening community based structures and networks through capacity building and mentorship
The response will require 800,000 USD.

2. Project activities

Coalition for Humanity will deploy both static and mobile response to implement activities and achieve the proposed targets across different counties. Implementation will be based on context and feasibility:1. Provision of protection monitoring, risk assessments and analysis of protection concerns – Coalition for Humanity will conduct monthly protection assessments, protection risk assessments and analysis of protection concerns and human rights violations to inform response and advocacy and evaluate and measure the progress. Beneficiaries will be reached through KIIs and FGDs. CH team will also carry out direct observations, safety audits, meetings, community mobilization and home visits.2. Provision of capacity building trainings to local authorities and humanitarian staff – Local authorities, women and youth leaders will be trained by CH legal officers on key protection concerns as well as the redress mechanisms available to persons displaced by either violence or flooding. 3. Provide capacity building trainings on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Psychological First Aid - CH will provide capacity building trainings to local authorities and humanitarian staff) and Psychological First Aid (PFA) and establish mechanisms to monitor and report PSEA in the school environment: Coalition for Humanity will advocate for PSEA code of conduct mainstreaming it in all work places, in schools and in the community. CH will hold forums with employers and partners to ensure code of conduct is adhered to. CH will develop a PSEA pocket guide to be utilized by employers. We will establish referral pathways and reporting mechanisms for PSEA and offer a day training. 4. Awareness creation on key protection concerns through outreach activities – CH will conduct awareness raising in the community to sensitize community on the available redress mechanisms and actions they can take on critical protection concerns, rights violations protection risks, referral pathways, redress mechanisms, and how to enhanced resilience in the community. Coalition for Humanity will reach people throw radio talk shows, door to door and community Public Address System.5. Provision of case management, referral and individual protection assistance for vulnerable persons – CH will provide case management to persons most in need. Protection Cases that need special attention will be referred to partners in the region who can handle them. CH GBV and protection officers will refer child protection issues, GBV cases that require specialized attention and eviction from housing or land that are beyond its scope.6. Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) through women and girls’ friendly spaces - Coalition for Humanity will hold 2 PSS sessions per day, twice per week. The PSS sessions will target GBV survivors and women and girls at risk of GBV. The participants will be trained on how to stitch school uniforms, and sanitary pads from locally available material for their own use, use by their children and sale to community members as an income generating venture.7. Strengthening community-based structures and networks through capacity building and mentorship – Different leaders have different roles in the community. The project will target women leaders, youth leaders, opinion leaders from religious groups, teachers and local authority. The training will focus on their role in general protection, how to identify protection concerns, how to seek further assistance, the protection referral pathway, and how to resolve protection concerns.
