Project Name

Project Summary

Intervention 1: Strengthen the local capacity and health system for universal health coverage and primary healthcare in a more stable protracted humanitarian crisis
1. Community protection and empowerment to improve access to essential health services, including mental health, focusing on the most returnees and IDPs in resettled communities
2. Scalable, quality care and improving community-based water, sanitation hygiene and infection prevention and control (IPC) practices in communities
3. Promote learning among health sector partners, healthcare providers and community stakeholders on coordinated response to GBV/PRSEAH across the BAY states.
4. Integration of mental healthcare services in PHC centres across the BAY States.
5. Provision of capacity building and commodities for the treatment of SAM with medical complication(s)
Intervention 2: Strengthen capacities to prepare, detect, prevent, and respond to all hazards
1. Establish an emergency management team (health security workforce) ready-to-deploy (within 24 hours of the declaration of an emergency) with predictable core capacities (SURGE, EMT or critical care and isolation) in LGAs of returnees, resettled communities and inaccessible settlements as an exit strategy towards transition in line with Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN)
2. Facilitate BAY States and LGA assessments of Public Health Risk for operational planning, readiness and prioritisation of health emergency preparedness and disaster risk management strengthening
3. Strategically Intensify Health Risk Communication and Community Engagement to empowere communities on ownership of public health intervention
Intervention 3 – Strengthen Governance and Coordination towards Building Back Better (BBB) / Humanitarian Development Nexus (HDPN)
1. Lead and Coordinate an effective evidence-based health sector at state response and LGA level across the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.
2. Coordinate the sectoral Cluster Coordination Performance Monitoring (CCPM) and National Health sector Performance Monitoring (NHSPM) yearly and dissemination of the outcome across the BAY state.
3. Strengthen State capacities to lead and coordinate an effective evidence-based health sector response at the State and LGA level

1 - Needs

The continuum of conflict, insecurity, displacement, climate change implications, and food insecurity are the leading causes of pressing humanitarian needs in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. WHO dedicated efforts to assess those needs and produced public health information products, including Health Resources and Service Availability Monitoring System (HERAMS), Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MNSA), and Health sector Overview Needs Assessment for 2024 HRP.The health system in northeast Nigeria faces significant challenges in adequately meeting the healthcare requirements of displaced populations. Ongoing incidents of Boko Haram attacks and terrorism have resulted in a multitude of needs and obstacles for the health system. These include deteriorating infrastructure and equipment, a shortage of qualified personnel, gaps in the supply of medicines and medical resources, insufficient funding, and markedly high demands, evident in low life expectancy rates and the world’s highest infant and under-five mortality rates.The humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s northeastern BAY region—comprising Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe—remains deeply entrenched, with an alarming 8 million people teetering on the brink of impoverishment and vulnerable to waterborne diseases. The situation has been compounded by the fallout from the COVID-19 pandemic, further exacerbating widespread WASH (water, sanitation and hygiene) needs in northeast Nigeria, home to over 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs). Health facilities, communities, and camps confront daunting challenges in delivering essential WASH services and upgrading infrastructure.Also, there are low vaccination rates, recurrent large-scale epidemics and disease outbreaks, issues related to malnutrition, gender-based violence, and mental health concerns, among other challenges. The May 2023 to April 2024 IPC AMN’s projected worsening nutrition situation was confirmed by the Cycle 18 Sentinel Surveillance Alert, which reveals that 11 local government areas (LGA) have SAM (severe acute malnutrition) rates of 2% or more, while 9 out of the 22 assessed LGAs have global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates above 10%. The majority of the LGAs are hosting IDPs and returnees. This acute malnutrition is exacerbated by food and economic crises. The contributing factors include high children’s morbidity of malaria and diarrhoea and poor hygiene due to poor population access to essential health and WASH services. The escalating insecurity and hostilities have severely impacted health service delivery, impeding the movement of both healthcare workers and patients, as well as disrupting the supply chain for drugs and essential commodities. Consequently, there is a shortage of skilled healthcare professionals, particularly doctors, nurses, midwives and community health extension workers to deliver crucial services.Health sector Needs Overview Assessment for 2024 HRP for BAY States reveals that 0 LGA are minimal, 14 LGA are stressed, 31 LGA at severe, 16 LGA at extreme, and 0 LGA at catastrophic levels.
