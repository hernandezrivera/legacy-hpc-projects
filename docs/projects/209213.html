Project Summary

ADRA has been working in Sudan for over four decades, providing both direct relief and development projects. Currently, ADRA Sudan is present in 6 out of the 18 states in Sudan; Khartoum, Aj Jazirah, White Nile, Blue Nile, West Darfur, and Central Darfur, with plans to expand to more states such as River Nile.
Over the past four decades, ADRA has gained extraordinary experience in the construction and rehabilitation of water points, not just in urban areas but also in fragile, remote, and conflict-affected areas. This applies not only to water supply but also to sanitation and hygiene sub-components. ADRA has constructed and rehabilitated latrines and provided various hygiene-related non-food items, in addition to working on capacity building with the people in the targeted areas. ADRA is implementing WASH activities under many donors such as BHA, DANIDA, IHE, SIDA, EU, UNICEF, SHF, etc. Through its work with communities, ADRA has succeeded in reaching remote and war-torn villages in BNS and providing lifesaving assistance.
In WD and CD, ADRA has significant experience in the construction and rehabilitation of hand pumps and water yards. As a result of the armed conflict that erupted in April 2023, ADRA plans to rehabilitate the disrupted hand pumps and water yards in 2024, and provide water tracking services as an immediate response to urgent needs. Moreover, communal and household latrines will be rehabilitated, and WASH NFIs will be distributed to support hygiene, accompanied by hygiene promotion activities (hygiene campaigns, hygiene kits, menstrual & dignity kits, and NFIs distribution).
In Aj Jazirah State, as a response to the armed conflict and high influx of IDPs, ADRA has rehabilitated three water yards by installing three solar systems that supported more than 9000 IDPs and host communities. However, there is still a great need for water supply. Thus, ADRA plans to install more solar systems for additional water yards and also rehabilitate more institutional and communal latrines. ADRA has already rehabilitated 28 latrines, besides providing WASH NFIs and hygiene kits, along with health and hygiene awareness, as the state is suffering from hygiene-related diseases such as diarrhea. Moreover, ADRA will have a major response to the cholera outbreak by rehabilitating CTCs throughout the state.
For WN, which has also been affected by a high influx of IDPs, there is a huge need for lifesaving assistance. ADRA has already started to respond by providing cash for NFIs. Thus, NFIs will be provided either in kind, voucher, or via cash, and this will be determined based on the market assessment which will determine if the market is well-functioning to provide NFIs. Moreover, there is a need for latrine rehabilitation, hygiene kits, and dignity kits for women, besides hygiene education sessions and awareness, as the state is flooded with diseases related to incorrect behaviors from IDPs.
RN State has been flooded with many IDPs, particularly from Khartoum. Thus, lifesaving assistance was needed and is still due to the recent armed conflict that happened in Madani. As a result of the Madani conflict, many cholera cases have appeared. Thus, ADRA will focus on the rehabilitation of existing CTCs and support them with required hygiene materials such as soaps and cleaning materials. Besides, rehabilitation and upgrading of water points to ensure the provision of clean and safe water will be tracked via regular tests in cooperation with the Water, Environment, and Sanitation cooperative.
In BN region , Solarization of water points, rehabilitation of water yards and hand pumps, rehabilitation of latrines, distribution of NFIs and menstrual and hygiene kits have already been done, and still, there is a huge gap that needs to be covered. Furthermore, ADRA will contribute by rehabilitating the existing CTC centers to be prepared for any outbreak, particularly cholera, as many IDPs have left Aj Jazirah and Sennar states into BN.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ADRA Sudan, with its long-standing commitment to providing both relief and development projects, places a high priority on the safety and security of women and girls in its provision of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services and facilities. Their active involvement is deemed crucial in the process of identifying water points and new water sources, a strategy aimed at safeguarding women.Sudan’s deeply ingrained patriarchal culture, which is even more pronounced in Blue Nile State (BNS), West Darfur State (WDS), and Central Darfur State (CDS), coupled with its low ranking of 139 out of 155 in the UN Gender Inequality Index, exacerbates the challenges faced by women and girls. They often find themselves victims of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and other forms of abuse, especially during routine tasks such as water collection, firewood gathering, and market visits.Water collection is primarily undertaken by girls, women, and young boys, with ages ranging from as young as six to as old as 60 years. Due to restrictions on access to services and limitations on movement, many individuals choose to move under the cover of night.Despite older women possessing an increased capacity for community leadership, they face numerous cultural and socioeconomic barriers that hinder their active participation. These barriers include the fact that income from farming and herding activities, though carried out by women, goes to men. During public gatherings, women and men are segregated, with women sitting with their backs toward the men. Women are usually not permitted to voice their concerns in public.Domestic violence and rape are unfortunately prevalent but are rarely discussed openly or reported, leading to increased psychological trauma. ADRA Sudan advocates for female participation throughout the project cycle. Both genders will be consulted during project design through focus groups and needs assessments.ADRA Sudan leverages its deep understanding of the local context and staff consultations to design approaches that are sensitive to gender issues. It ensures equal representation in Water User Committees (WUC) and consults females, particularly young girls who are the main water collectors, before undertaking the rehabilitation of water taps.During distributions, separate queues for women will be established, and females will be consulted on the most suitable time and place for these distributions. Protection-focused awareness methods will be inclusive of the community’s different segments, and every WASH training will include protection advocacy sessions. This comprehensive approach ensures that the needs of all community members are addressed, contributing to the overall success of the project.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) empowers program participants to voice their opinions on the program’s design, execution, systems, and personnel. This feedback can influence program-related decisions and prompt necessary adjustments. Communities can choose the most effective feedback methods for them, whether existing or new, and ADRA will provide appropriate support and collaborate with the community to deliver prompt feedback and corrective action.Successful existing mechanisms include complaint boxes in centers and health facilities. ADRA has also set up hotlines, ensuring that complaints received are handled appropriately and confidentially by the protection officer. Complaints traditionally come through local leaders CDC and community elders, and in this proposed Action, they will also come through the CPBNs.ADRA recognizes the importance of a community-centered CFRM, ensuring that community members, particularly women and PSNs, are extensively consulted and can choose the methods that best suit them. In areas like West Darfur, where populations may struggle to lodge complaints via hotlines due to network and phone access, ADRA will consult with local partners to establish secure whistleblowing mechanisms. One method adopted by ADRA is face-to-face complaints via the Kobo toolbox, as project staff will have a tablet with CFRM in Kobo. This is one way ADRA management can receive complaints. The complaint/help desk is also a common tool used to receive feedback during any distribution activity.Project staff ensure that ADRA Sudan’s CFRMs are distributed across the project target and verified by the project MEAL Officer and ADRA Sudan MEAL Manager. CFRM is delivered by staff during community mobilization and sensitization, supplemented by regular follow-up visits and monitoring visits by the ADRA Sudan team. The ADRA Sudan hotline number is disseminated using posters, banners, and small cards, especially during any distribution activity. SMS is one tool used to share the hotline number, and recently, WhatsApp has been adopted as a channel, especially in Khartoum where people have smartphones and better internet access.According to ADRA Sudan’s CFRM Policy, feedback and complaints are categorized as information and feedback for any normal feedback such as gratitude, minor complaints, major complaints, and sensitive complaints which could include PSEA cases, fraud, and corruption. The complaints are confidential and will be sorted by the MEAL Manager and the complaints focal person. They will then be addressed according to their severity. Minor ones will be handled by the project manager and his team, major ones will be handled by ADRA Sudan’s Country office team in close coordination with the project team, and sensitive ones will reach the highest level, involving the ADRA Sudan CD, ADRA MENA Regional Director, and ADRA International.As stated above, any complaints and feedback reach ADRA’s higher management for any necessary corrections or actions for any activity in its current projects after consulting with donors or when drafting future projects.By integrating community engagement, feedback mechanisms such as complaint boxes, hotlines, and face-to-face complaints via Kobo toolbox not only identify community needs and challenges but also ensure the community’s active participation. This approach helps prioritize and tailor WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) activities to be culturally appropriate and effective. Real-time monitoring and evaluation of the activities are enabled, allowing for necessary adjustments for maximum impact. Thus, the community will be empowered to place it at the core of every decision and action, ensuring activities are not only impactful but also resonate with the community’s needs and preferences.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Community engagement is key to the project. ADRA will maintain open communication channels throughout the project cycle for successful service delivery. At the project’s start, meetings will be held with various communities, including tribal leaders and vulnerable groups, to inform them about the approved project. The project objectives, expected outputs, activity methodologies, and timelines will be collectively reviewed and agreed upon. Further activity-specific details will be decided through consultations with diverse groups. The community will be engaged throughout the project via semi-annual and ad-hoc meetings and through the M&E system. A PM&E is anticipated to enhance local ownership and empowerment, ensure improved information-sharing, provide joint learning opportunities, identify and implement timely corrective actions, and enhance accountability and transparency.ADRA adheres to a zero-tolerance PSEA policy. If any stakeholder related to ADRA’s operation believes that an employee or another partner entity has engaged in any action that violates any applicable law or regulation, that person is expected to immediately report such information using the established channels. The Internal Controller and Deputy Finance Director are designated as the Fraud Focal point. The Human Resources Manager is designated as the Safeguarding Focal point. The Monitoring and Evaluation, Accountability and Learning Manager is the Programs Focal point.The identity of the individual sharing a complaint or feedback is protected. All feedback and complaints are logged in the Feedback and Complaints Logbook by the Focal point. The logbook also records the remedial measures taken to address the complaint and measures to prevent recurrence in the future. A statistical analysis report of the feedback/complaint’s logbook is reviewed by ADRA Sudan ADCOM in regular meetings.ADRA will ensure high coordination with actors at the state levels in terms of active presence in the WASH sectors meetings, NFIs, and also protection and being a part of the protection and referral pathways to actively refer any GBV cases to other humanitarian partners for case management and legal support.
