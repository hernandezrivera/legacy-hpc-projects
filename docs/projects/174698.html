Project Summary

The socio-political crisis in the NW and SW regions which turned violent in November 2017 and further compounded by the recent COVID-19 pandemic has triggered increased humanitarian needs for people in the two restive regions. In order to ascertain specific needs surrounding Legal and Civil Documentation (LDC) and Housing, Land and Property (HLP) Rights of beneficiaries in view of seeking durable solutions for the affected populations, NRC carried out a study on LCD and HLP Rights in the two regions which also served as leeway for the introducing the ICLA Program in the affected regions.
For 12 months the ICLA program will target crisis-affected populations in the two restive regions with interventions aimed at increasing their ability to claim and exercise their LCD and HLP rights. In order to ensure that IDPs, returnees and host populations have their capacities enhanced to prevent and address COVID-19 related HLP disputes and tensions. NRC will;
• Promote HLP rights of displaced people, returnees and host populations during the COVID-19 crisis through provision of information to contribute to social cohesion and further their integration into host communities. Information sessions will also cover access to essential services such as healthcare;
• Train local and regional authorities, and humanitarian actors on HLP rights and Collaborative Dispute Resolutions (CDR) to enhance their capacity to protect HLP rights of those affected by COVID-19; and
• Support capacity building on HLP and CDR for local and administrative authorities who have in-depth knowledge of HLP and conflict resolution to effectively ensure the restitution and protection of the property of returnees and IDPs. Humanitarian actors will also benefit from training sessions in HLP and will be able to better secure land made available to IDPs and returnees.
• Strengthen the capacities of local partners on the protection of the rights of IDPs and returnees and the collaborative resolution of different HLP claims to ensure the sustainability of the gains made.
• Provide counselling and legal assistance to communities to prevent and address disputes over HLP assets amongst IDPs, community leaders, local authorities and social cohesion actors.
Primarily, the ICLA Program will target IDPs, returnees, vulnerable host families, and other vulnerable populations facing difficulties in exercising their HLP or LI rights in displacement and return areas. Priority will be given to women, including widows, as well as to people with disabilities/special needs, and other persons facing challenges in realizing their HLP rights or to obtain/recover civil or identity documents due to the conflict/crisis. Beneficiaries of other NRC Core Competencies who need assistance on HLP or LI to effectively enjoy humanitarian assistance will also be considered as priorities.
The secondary target group will consist of national, regional, customary and traditional authorities, including mayors, civil registration officers, sub prefects and heads of villages, health centres, social affairs departments, regional and local courts. These are the main actors providing HLP rights and/ or LI services and will be targeted with training and advocacy initiatives. Primary school teachers and agents of other education department services will be trained on civil and identity documents procedures so that they are involved in sensitizing communities. Advocacy actions will be directed towards health, justice and administrative authorities in order to reduce costs and facilitate access to civil documentation. Support in terms of materials, staffing, etc. will also be provided to CRC on needs basis in order to strengthen their capacity to respond to demand.
Finally, NRC will also engage with humanitarian actors (mostly protection, shelter, education and Food Security) to promote and integrate HLP and LI issues into their interventions.

1 - Besoins / Needs

ICLA mobile teams will also provide orientation and counselling to the above target population in displacement and return areas, including IDP sites and refugee out-camps. These populations will be informed and assisted to access and secure customary and formal rights over HLP assets.Additionally, ICLA will provide legal assistance to conflict-affected population (IDPs, refugees, returnees, vulnerable host community) in need of securing their HLP rights. ICLA will assist vulnerable individuals to obtain signed agreements from local authorities and traditional leaders (chief of villages) for beneficiaries at risk of forced eviction . Priority will be given to female heads of household, widows, displaced persons, and vulnerable or affected host community members. This process will contribute to the prevention of forced evictions. Furthermore, ICLA will build the capacity of local and traditional authorities on HLP issues, including through the provision of training and coaching on HLP rights and CDR, to strengthen their HLP response. ICLA will also train humanitarian actors operating in sectors such as protection, shelter, WASH, education, and food security. This will help ensure that these sectors take in account HLP rights in their humanitarian response and prioritise land tenure agreements instead of the direct and customary land transfer in their interventions and prevent or solve land-related conflicts. 2. conflict and Dispute resolution mechanismNRC will conduct a rapid needs assessment to identify the various community conflict resolution mechanisms that exist at the community level. If mechanisms do not exist, NRC with the support of local partners, will support the creation of conflict management committees. ICLA continues to deliver trainings and coaching to local authorities (religious leaders, head of villages, community leaders, heads of cadastre, etc.) on CDR. Together with other volunteers from the community (trained community volunteers), ICLA will identify HLP issues in discussions with beneficiaries and work with community leaders to resolve them. ICLA teams will engage and discuss with community leaders, administrative, local and religious authorities on the aspects of prevention and resolution of HLP-related disputes, as a follow up of the ongoing and to be strengthened with trainings on CDR. 3. Integrated (multi-sectoral) ResponseHLP and LI being crosscutting issues, ICLA will work with beneficiaries of other NRC Core competencies mainly Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS), Shelter, and Education programmes to secure access to land, especially for LFS beneficiaries who need secure land tenure for agriculture. In order to avoid or reduce forced evictions, ICLA will assist Shelter and LFS beneficiaries to access land with increased security of tenure. ICLA will also work with the Education programme to raise awareness of the importance of birth registration and how to follow the procedure. ICLA will help identify children at the end of the primary school cycle without birth certificates and provide them with legal assistance to sit for the end of cycle examination.
