Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to improve nutrition security and enhance the resilience of drought and flood affected communities through a comprehensive set of integrated lifesaving Nutrition interventions in line with the National Nutrition Strategy and the SGDs. The 2018 National Nutrition survey identifies the need to introduce integrated community services through multi-sectoral partnerships to tackle under nutrition. The targeted districts (Harare, Chitungwiza, Mt Darwin, Mutoko, and Gokwe North) have nutrition structures at all levels though poorly resourced, managed and lack capacity to sustainably drive nutrition-relevant action forward especially in the face of drought and difficult economic conditions. The MoHCC is overall in charge of implementation and monitoring of health and nutrition interventions at district level while ADRA will complement them with resources for enhanced monitoring, mentoring and timely accurate reporting. Leveraging on its global and local context experiences on health, food & nutrition, availability of technical expertise and physical presence in the target districts, ADRA will work through strengthening of local structures (including duty bearers) to improve delivery of health and extension services and integrated nutrition education and counselling at all levels. ADRA will provide transport and capacity building to MoHCC (National& Provincial, Environmental workers, Ward Nutrition Coordinators) to regularly monitor, review and report progress on health and nutrition interventions. Support will also be extended to VHWs to actively screen children, facilitate support group meetings (cooking demonstrations) and regularly report to health facilities. Through VHWs, timely & appropriate IYCF/Nutrition practices are promoted through CARE and Support groups. The village health days will be used to reach out to remote areas and areas with low coverage figures. The project will coordinate documentation and effective sharing of good practices at all levels through coordinated and integrated review meetings and joint monitoring visits. ADRA will also build awareness of project through community dialogues and campaigns (MNPs, health and hygiene, VAS) for local leaders. ADRA will place nutrition officers in each district to facilitate and support timely implementation, monitoring and reporting. The integrated approaches builds resilience of communities improved & equitable use of nutritional services, care and stimulation practices for infants, young children and WOCBA. The project will strengthen local capacity and structures and prevent and respond to malnutrition through strengthening the Ward and District food and nutrition security committees and male champions. ADRA has physical presence in the 5 districts and has / is implementing Nutrition projects including in the urban areas Harare and Chitungwiza 150000 children 0-59 months will be reached through active screening and growth monitoring (Harare-36040, Gokwe North-34250, Chitungwiza-27365, Mt Darwin-31438 and Mutoko-21271). The same numbers will be targeted for Vitamin A Supplementation. 51849 children 6-23months will be targeted for MNPs (Harare-10152, Gokwe North-7706, Chitungwiza-12811, Mt Darwin-12657, and Mutoko-8532). 17353 children with SAM and MAM admitted for treatment (Harare-10616, Gokwe North-1604, Chitungwiza-1941, Mt Darwin-2032 and Mutoko-1161). 44044 caregivers reached with key IYCF and Hygiene messages (Harare-5345, Chitungwiza-7740, Mt Darwin-11136, Mutoko-6960 and Gokwe North-12863).

Needs

The need to strengthen local structures is key for creating an enabling and sustainable environment to prepare for and respond to shocks and will see revitalising of ward and village committees and capacity building of local level health teams and VHWs and lead mothers for Nutrition security in the drought affected areas. The district and ward nutrition security committees will be strengthened through supporting regular meetings and developing action plans. Village action plans will outline how the local villages mobilise for identification and referral of malnourished individuals. Nutrition and WASH integration at all levels will be ensured due to the direct link between the two, the emerging rise in diarrhoeal diseases and the way drought affects the two. Transport to regularly monitor, review and report progress on nutrition interventions will be provided. This will include mentorship support for VHWs and clinic level staff and on job training for the management of acute malnutrition package and IYCF- e. Support will extend to VHWs and individual mothers to actively screen children and refer appropriately, facilitate support/ care group meetings (cooking demonstrations, appropriate IYCF- e) and regularly report to health facilities. Enhanced knowledge and skills among mothers and caregivers will come through the care groups which will establish and support the following: (1) exclusive breastfeeding, (2) meal frequency, (3) food diversity and (4) preservation and storage. The severely malnourished children below the age of 5 years will be referred for treatment with RUTF and appropriate medication whilst moderately malnourished children will be referred for supplementary feeding. Malnourished pregnant and lactating women will be referred for counselling and supplementary feeding. The high rate of food insecurity (27.2%, ZimVAC 2018) will be addressed through referral to targeted supplementary program prioritizing children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women. Awareness of project through community dialogues, IEC and campaigns (MNPs, health and hygiene, VAS) for local leaders and champions will be key. The village health days will ensure services reach traditional hard areas. 150000 children 0-59 months will be reached through active screening and growth monitoring (Harare-36040, Gokwe North-34250, Chitungwiza-27365, Mt Darwin-31438 and Mutoko-21271). The same numbers will be targeted for Vitamin A Supplementation. 51849 children 6-23months will be targeted for MNPs (Harare-10152, Gokwe North-7706, Chitungwiza-12811, Mt Darwin-12657, and Mutoko-8532). 17353 children with SAM and MAM admitted for treatment (Harare-10616, Gokwe North-1604, Chitungwiza-1941, Mt Darwin-2032 and Mutoko-1161). 44044 caregivers reached with key IYCF and Hygiene messages (Harare-5345, Chitungwiza-7740, Mt Darwin-11136, Mutoko-6960 and Gokwe North-12863).

Activities

The outputs and activities will include increasing access and utilization of services which protects and supports nutrition security (Training of health staff on IMAM and IYCF-e and the VHWs and lead mothers on community IYCF-e, supporting district and local level mentorship, messaging for both IYCF, health and hygiene). The training sessions will be on site sessions and will present key IYCF messages for a drought emergency. The training sessions for VHWs and mothers will include MUAC measurements and interpretation and record keeping. VHWs and lead mothers will be equipped with nutritional supplies to support growth monitoring and active screening (MUAC bands, weighing scales) and to improve micronutrient intake (Vitamin A capsules and Micronutrient powders). Improved coordination and implementation (Support WNCs and clinic staff to mentor and supervise VHWs, support the monthly VHW meetings and DFNFCs and WFNFCs, support the village health days, support development of village plans). Dissemination of critical health and nutrition messages through IYCF counselling, support/ care groups (cooking demonstrations and key messages, IEC material development & distribution, community dialogues to trigger community leaders for action on screening, MNPs, VAS, breastfeeding). The community sensitization dialogues will target influential leaders such as village heads and headman and faith and church leaders in order to agree on standard operating procedures and gain general support and buy- in of the leaders. The IEC material reproduction and distribution will include tailoring materials to further marginalised groups such as the disabled and elderly. The project will seek to establish linkages with livelihood programming and will also use platforms of livelihood projects to disseminate nutrition education and counselling
