Project Summary

YPSA's WASH project will improve the lives of Rohingya refugees and the host community in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, by providing clean water, upgraded sanitation facilities, Solid Waste Management, promoting good hygiene practices and support with Clean Energy activities. The project will focus on camps 1W, 4, and 17, as well as selected host communities.
The project will follow the WASH Sector's Joint Response Plan-2025, which emphasizes cost-efficient operation and maintenance of existing WASH services, ensuring the quality provision of water and sanitation facilities, and addressing solid waste management through infrastructure support and community engagement. The project will actively promote adequate hygiene behavior and distribute hygiene items using a multi-sectoral approach. In host communities, the project will work to support equitable access to quality WASH services that align with national development plans. Added to this, energy support will also be provided in both host and camps.
The project will pay particular attention to the empowerment of women and girls by actively involving them in decision-making processes and addressing their specific needs, such as providing essential hygiene supplies.
Beyond WASH, the project will integrate clean energy solutions by installing and maintaining solar lighting around WASH facilities. These lights will provide both illumination and security, particularly for the most vulnerable. Apart from this, support will be given to the biogas plant in the host communities as a part of energy component.
The project will ensure that all actions and interventions align with Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) guidelines, with a strong emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks. The project will be accountable to the individuals it aims to assist and will consider age, gender, and disability inclusion. Careful management of the environment will also be a priority.
The project will aim to provide quality water supply, improve sanitation facilities, and promote sound hygiene practices for approximately 28,265 individuals and 5728 HH in four blocks within camp 1W, camp 4, camp 17, and two host community unions.
The primary focus will be on achieving high standards of quality and resilience building through active community engagement. The project will also be prepared to provide emergency support as needed, responding to crises such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), scabies, fires, floods, monsoons, and cyclones.
In essence, YPSA's project will strive to enhance the living conditions and WASH services for Rohingya refugees and the host community, tackling significant challenges and working towards the long-term sustainability of these vital services while adhering to established sector standards and guidelines. The goal will be to make a meaningful impact and empower these communities to thrive.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Given the substantial refugee population, it remains essential for WASH actors to sustain and enhance efforts to ensure high-quality, adequate, equitable, and dignified access to WASH services for all individuals, including women, men, children, elderly individuals, and those with disabilities. Water: An assessment by the WASH Sector indicates that 85% of the Rohingya population in FDMN camps report access to adequate water to meet their family’s needs. 26% of the household still use tube-wells water (with hand-pump) as a primary source of drinking water (many of those tube-wells offer shallow and contaminated water and were built during the emergency phase). And 6% of households having their own private piped-water connection, which could lead to further diarrheal diseases if water is unsafe. This is a huge issue and from early research, WASH sector team found strong correlation between diarrheal diseases and drinking from tube-wells instead of water-networks. Furthermore, data from the WASH sector monitoring report shows that 68% of households rely on tap stands as their primary drinking water source. However, declining groundwater levels have impacted many tube-wells, particularly during the dry season, making hand pumps a critical source of drinking, bathing, and domestic water. This project will thus emphasize household-level water quality, recognizing its importance in maintaining a safe water chain. Sanitation: According to the latest WASH Sector report, 96% of sanitation facilities are operational, reflecting a satisfactory status. Nonetheless, ongoing maintenance is crucial to preserve this functionality, given that one latrine currently serves approximately 20 individuals in FDMN camps, highlighting a need for more facilities. Solid waste management is another key challenge, with only 94 grams of waste collected per person per day. Although 84% of households segregate waste, challenges in effective waste and fecal sludge management, particularly during monsoon season, persist. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: The October 2024 WASH Sector Monitoring Report shows that 85% of the community has access to soap, and 83% of women and girls have access to menstrual hygiene materials, though the practice levels are still insufficient. Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities remain inaccessible for many females due to societal stigma, taboos, and cultural restrictions, and Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials for MHM are not always culturally relevant. The prevalence of communicable diseases, including Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Dengue, is also rising within the camps. Considering these critical factors and identified gaps, YPSA will continue its WASH program across three camps and two host community locations with technical and financial support from Community Partners International (CPI). YPSA's initiatives will support the operation and maintenance of WASH systems, aimed at expanding and ensuring the sustainability of WASH infrastructure. This will be accomplished through active community engagement, employing a multi-level strategy to address WASH challenges at individual, household, community, and environmental levels in a comprehensive and integrated manner.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

YPSA’s WASH project in Cox’s Bazar will incorporate protection mainstreaming to ensure the safety, dignity, and equitable access to resources for all beneficiaries, particularly the most vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. In line with Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) guidelines, the project will establish safe, accessible water and sanitation facilities with enhanced lighting and gender-segregated spaces to mitigate risks of exploitation and violence. Community engagement will prioritize the empowerment of women and girls, actively involving them in decision-making processes and addressing their specific needs, such as access to hygiene supplies and safe bathing areas. Through regular training and awareness sessions, the project team will promote inclusivity, ensure responsive feedback mechanisms, and reinforce community-based protective practices, particularly during crises or public health emergencies. The project will align with national and sectoral protection standards to uphold a secure and dignified environment, fostering resilience and agency within the refugee and host communities. Moreover, all staffs and community WASH volunteers will have training and orientation on PSEA and GVB.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This project will adopt an inclusive approach that ensures equitable access and addresses the diverse needs of individuals across age groups and abilities. The project designs water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities to be accessible to all, with age-appropriate provisions, such as child-friendly sanitation facilities and hygiene education tailored to children and adolescents. For elderly individuals and persons with disabilities, infrastructure adjustments include ramps, handrails, and non-slip surfaces, making it safer and easier for these groups to use the facilities. Hygiene promotion activities incorporate materials accessible to people with visual or hearing impairments, while community awareness campaigns emphasize respectful support for vulnerable individuals. The project actively engages community members, including the elderly and individuals with disabilities, in decision-making to ensure their voices are heard and needs addressed. By fostering inclusive WASH committees and implementing feedback mechanisms, the project empowers these individuals, helping to identify and overcome barriers they may face. This approach aims to create a protective environment that supports dignity, safety, and well-being for all, reflecting the core principles of the JRP’s cross-cutting issues guidance.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This YPSA's project will uphold Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as a core principle, ensuring that all project activities are transparent, inclusive, and responsive to the needs and feedback of beneficiaries. The project establishes clear communication channels, providing accessible information about available services, project goals, and timelines through community meetings, IEC materials, and trusted local facilitators. Feedback mechanisms, such as suggestion boxes and structured focus group discussions, are set up within the camps and host communities, enabling individuals to voice concerns or recommendations confidentially and without fear of reprisal. Additionally, regular feedback sessions are organized to discuss project progress, and community members are actively involved in decision-making, which empowers them and strengthens community ownership of the WASH facilities. The project team is trained to uphold principles of confidentiality and respect, with dedicated staff responsible for addressing community concerns swiftly and effectively. By embedding AAP throughout its design and implementation, YPSA’s WASH project ensures that the needs, preferences, and rights of the affected populations are respected and prioritized, thereby enhancing service quality, trust, and project impact.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

This YPSA's project integrates Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming as a foundational aspect, prioritizing a safe and respectful environment for all beneficiaries, particularly women, children, and vulnerable individuals. The project enforces strict PSEA guidelines by training all staff and volunteers on ethical conduct, respectful engagement, and clear procedures for reporting misconduct. Safe spaces are established within WASH facilities, with gender-segregated areas, adequate lighting, and secure access points to reduce risks of exploitation and abuse. Accessible, confidential reporting mechanisms—such as designated contact points, community-based complaints systems, and awareness campaigns—are introduced, allowing beneficiaries to report concerns safely. The project also actively engages the community in PSEA awareness, working with community leaders to reinforce a culture of zero tolerance toward abuse and exploitation. Monitoring and accountability processes are put in place to respond to complaints swiftly and appropriately, ensuring that affected individuals feel supported and that incidents are managed transparently. This commitment to PSEA mainstreaming reinforces a protective and respectful environment, empowering beneficiaries and upholding the dignity and safety of all involved.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, YPSA’s WASH project incorporates climate action through targeted adaptation and mitigation strategies, addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and environmental resilience in response to climate change. Recognizing the vulnerabilities of Cox’s Bazar to climate impacts such as monsoons, flooding, and cyclones, the project integrates disaster-resilient infrastructure, including flood-resistant sanitation facilities and elevated water points to maintain functionality during extreme weather. Moreover, this project will also be equipped with contingency stocks (jerry cans, aqua tabs, desludging machine, bamboo, bleaching powder etc.) to provide immediate support during and after the disaster. Anticipatory actions include community training on emergency preparedness, safe water storage, and hygiene practices to prevent disease outbreaks in the wake of climate-related events. The project also emphasizes environmental sustainability by incorporating clean energy solutions, such as solar-powered lighting around WASH facilities, reducing reliance on non-renewable energy sources and minimizing carbon emissions. Additionally, support for biogas production in host communities not only provides a renewable energy source but also contributes to sustainable waste management practices. Through these adaptation and mitigation measures, YPSA’s WASH project strengthens the resilience of both refugee and host communities, helping them adapt to and mitigate the impacts of climate change effectively.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project embeds Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to ensure swift, coordinated action during crises. The project includes a business continuity plan that outlines essential operations and resource allocations to maintain critical WASH services during emergencies. Standard operating procedures (SOPs) guide the project team’s response to common crises, such as floods, cyclones, and disease outbreaks, focusing on rapid restoration of water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities. To bolster preparedness, contingency funds are allocated to support immediate needs, such as emergency water supplies, water & sanitation repair materials, and hygiene items, reducing reliance on external assistance and speeding up response times. The project also trains community members in basic emergency WASH practices and establishes clear communication channels to disseminate early warnings and response instructions. Through these EPR measures, YPSA’s WASH project enhances community resilience and ensures continuous, adaptable support in times of crisis, protecting the health and safety of vulnerable populations.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, YPSA’s WASH project actively engages youth (ages 15-24) as key participants and contributors to community awareness and resilience. Youth are involved in WASH committees, where they assist in the planning and implementation of hygiene promotion activities, sanitation upkeep, and environmental awareness campaigns. The project provides targeted training for youth on safe water practices, sanitation maintenance, and emergency preparedness, empowering them with practical skills and knowledge to lead and support community initiatives. Additionally, youth are encouraged to take on roles as youth leaders to lead youth groups, spreading awareness on hygiene and climate resilience among their peers and within their families. By engaging youth in these meaningful roles, the project not only leverages their energy and creativity but also fosters a sense of responsibility and leadership, strengthening community resilience and promoting sustainable WASH practices for the future.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project promotes self-reliance and resilience among refugees by empowering them with skills and knowledge to manage WASH facilities and sustain hygiene practices independently. The project trains refugee volunteers in essential WASH maintenance tasks, such as managing water points, sanitation upkeep through the initiative of Latrine user groups, and safe waste disposal, enabling them to take ownership of these facilities. Community-led WASH committees are formed, including refugee members, to oversee hygiene promotion and encourage active participation in maintaining a clean, safe environment. Additionally, the project supports small-scale local initiatives, such as broom making for the solid waste management, which not only promotes clean environment but also provides inspiration for other community members. By fostering these skills and community structures, the project enhances refugees' capacity for self-reliance, helping them build resilience against future challenges.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, YPSA’s WASH project activities in the host community align with Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Priorities (Dec 2022) and broader district-level initiatives. The project supports equitable access to clean water, improved sanitation, and solid waste management, directly contributing to the district’s goals of enhancing public health and environmental sustainability. By integrating clean energy solutions, such as solar lighting and biogas production, the project aligns with sustainable development efforts and reduces dependency on non-renewable resources, a district priority. The project collaborates with local authorities and health agencies to ensure that infrastructure improvements, like flood-resilient water facilities, align with disaster risk reduction strategies in the region. Through these development linkages, the project strengthens community resilience, supports public health, and contributes to long-term sustainable growth in Cox’s Bazar.
