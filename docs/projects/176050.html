Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to enhance emergency food production, diversify food consumption and strengthen the resilience of targeted communities. The project aims to achieve this purpose by distributing crop and vegetable seed kits, agricultural tool kits, fishing kits, provide agronomic training for 8,400 households, approximately 50,400 individuals. Food production trainings will be through provision of farmer extension services, establishment of farmer field schools and vegetable gardens in schools and nutrition centers. Diet diversity will be achieved by increased consumption of vegetables, increased availability of fish at household level and local markets as well as through targeted sensitization on food preparation (cooking demonstrations), IYCF and basic household hygiene. Further, the resilience of the target communities will be strengthened by encouraging them to participate in construction or rehabilitation of community assets through cash for asset approach. This approach is supplementary expected to provide an alternative source of income to targeted individuals. Additional measures of addressing underlying causes of food insecurity shall include capacity building in disaster risk reduction, natural resources management and establishment of referral pathways to other services including nutrition health care, education, protection, and WASH.

1 - Needs

Analysis of IPC data for the period October 2020 to July 2021, estimates that 7.24 million people country wide (60% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 2.4 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 31,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5), and of these, 1.38 million people are in need from the project targeted larger Northern Bar el Ghazal and Unity states. The analysis further projects that populations in the targeted Counties of Panijiar, Aweil South and Aweil East will be in IPC 4 in the lean period of 2021 with 7000 individuals in Aweil South expected to be in IPC level 5. Given that the areas have an extreme lack of food, the population is basically malnourished and the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence, as measured by weight-for-height z-score (WHZ), exceeds 30 percent. The food crisis scenario is because of the ongoing economic crisis, low household food production associated to climate variations, inadequate skills and poor post-harvest practices, depleted household asset base and limited income generating opportunities. Food insecurity in the target counties has also been compromised by prevalence of localized conflicts, flooding and the impacts of COVID-19 on the socio-economic set up and national economy. that continues to constrain availability of income for most households. The ensuing erosion of household purchasing power limits their access to food from markets characterized by high food prices. GAM prevalence is projected to deteriorated due to the limited access to and availability of food during this period coupled with seasonal increase in morbidity – such as malaria, diarrhea, and lack of adequate access to WASH, health, and nutrition services.In line with the FSLC cluster objectives therefore, immediate survival of households without means of livelihood will be ensured by availing kits (crops, vegetables, fishing etc) for agricultural production, capacity building (in agricultural practices, assets creation), cash through cash for asset approach to the general population as seasonal response to cover basic food needs. The targeted population will therefore have means to produce food, diversify their food consumption, earn income, and improve on community assets which expected to increase access to availability, and consumption of food as well as strengthen their capacity against immediate shocks. The intervention will also seek to fill the seasonal hunger gap for those who produce insufficient food as well as addressing underlying causes of food insecurity through strategic advocacy and basic training on food production, post-harvest losses, gender issues, management of natural resources, disaster risk reduction and creation of productive assets through voluntary community participation. To further address the high incidence of malnutrition, tailor-made training on IYCF, nutritional needs of PLWs, and hygiene will be rolled out. At the same time referral pathways to Nutrition and health services will be established in collaboration with service provider agencies operating in respective locations. Target groups will additionally be linked to existing WASH interventions implemented by Welthungerhilfe and other agencies so that they can benefit from ongoing services. Welthungerhilfe is currently implementing WASH and FSL projects in Panijiar and Aweil South with funding for similar projects for Aweil East being negotiated.

2 - Project activities

Distribution of seeds and tools: Sorghum, ground nuts and sesame are the main staple foods of the target group. To increase food production among the target group, Welthungerhilfe will support 6,000 identified vulnerable households, with locally purchased crops seeds during the cultivation season of 2021.Welthungerhilfe will also distribute tools such as hoes, maloda, sickle to each household. In northern Bar el Ghazal, Seed and tool distributions will be through seed /tool fairs. Households will be issued with vouchers that can be redeemed at pre-identified outlets during seeds fairs. For Panijiar direct distributions will be held because the markets are not functional. Welthungerhilfe has successfully implemented the “voucher for seeds” approach in previous projects. Distribution of Vegetable kits: Welthungerhilfe will support 1,500 vulnerable households with assorted vegetable seeds comprised of tomatoes, onions, kales, spinach, eggplants etc. To enroll the most vulnerable households in this activity, Welthungerhilfe will prioritize households with children under five years and pregnant and lactating women and experiencing moderate and severe malnutrition. Distribution of fishing kits: The target group, especially those communities along river basins and those not able to cultivate crops for subsistence, greatly relies on fish as a source of protein as well as for household income. Welthungerhilfe will support 900 households with fishing kits consisting of hooks, gillnets, twine, buckets, and tarpaulins for fishing. Agronomy trainings: Framers will be trained on basic agronomy practices including post-harvest management: Individuals will be trained on best post-harvest handling practices such as drying, storage, threshing, sorting, and grading to minimize post-harvest losses. Training approach will entail practical on-farm demonstrations to be carried out by project staff and up to 120 pre trained community own resource persons. Establishment of vegetable gardens in schools and nutrition centers: To promote consumption of vegetables by children and PLWs, Welthungerhilfe will support 15 selected schools and 15 nutrition centers in establishing vegetable gardens, covering approx. 9.0 hectare of land in total, for vegetable production; the institutions will receive vegetable seeds and tools. Training of teachers, CNV and school children on vegetable production: Welthungerhilfe will train Parent-Teacher-Associations and nutrition center staff on production, maintenance and replanting of gardens. Project staff will conduct regular follow ups to ensure adherence to good practices. Sensitization on basic nutrition IYCF: This will focus on topics such as food hygiene, nutritional value of different foods, cooking classes and nutrition requirements of children and PLWs. Sensitization sessions will be held at nutrition centers, and schools with a target of reaching up to 3000 women. Distribution of fuel-efficient stoves: To enhance environmental conservation by reducing the amount of firewood used for cooking, which helps to reduce deforestation which in turn contributes to lessen the impacts of seasonal flooding and will also contribute to improved nutrition by enabling the women to prepare well cooked meals, the project will distribute 3,000 fuel efficient stoves to households. This is also expected to contribute to promote gender equality by saving the among of time girls and women take to cook and collect firewood. Rehabilitation and construction of feeder roads and protective dikes: To increase the income of the target group, strengthen their resilience and while at the same time creating community assets and boosting the local economy, Welthungerhilfe will implement CFA-activities to construct small access roads and dikes for flood protection.3000 households will be targeted for this activity with each receiving two rounds of cash transfers amounting to 100USD after completing pre agreed work norms.

Welthungerhilfe is a member of the South Sudan cash working group and receives regular updates on markets from the clusters which have been used in the design of the proposed intervention. 5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfers will be implemented to target groups participating in Cash for asset activities. Vouchers will be employed in distribution of seeds and tools in Northern Bahr el Ghazal only. Welthungerhilfe is opting for cash and voucher approaches because:1. Cash can be used for multiple purposes by communities thus improving livelihoods beyond the food dimension.2.Use of cash and vouchers avoids transportation and handling costs of materials with the possibility of utilizing the cash earmarked for such costs on other activities.3.Welthungerhilfe has standing agreements with cash transfer agents which ensures that target groups benefit from pre-negotiated exchange rates.Use of cash and vouchers build local traders and rural economies.Local procurement of seeds stimulates production.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All WHH staff are contractually bound to appreciate and disseminating the organizations policy on PSEA. Refresher trainings for staff will be held at project inception and staff prepared to disseminate to communities and report on violations. Communities will be informed during project inception meetings and all contact sessions with project staff. Moreover, reporting on PSEA will be an Integral part of community trainings on the feedback and complaint mechanism.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Multiple channels of communication including hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint desks at all project activity sites will be availed to communities. The project will additionally organize regular feedback meetings for special interest groups at community level. Project staff will be trained on how to handle special interest groups’ complaints with staffing for complaints desks and hotlines being made gender and age sensitive.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project inception meetings to be attended by communities and stakeholders will be held at all locations. Issues to be covered in the meetings will include explanation of project objectives, methodology, activities and beneficiary endowments, responsibilities, and the rights thereof. Participants will additionally be sensitized on the project complaints and feedback mechanism and its application. Contacts details and reporting tools for the mechanism will be shared and their use explained. Regular reminders on the importance of the complaints and feedback mechanism will be communicated during activity implementation and field monitoring visits. Project communication including adverts and visibility materials will have messaging on CFRM as well as the reporting hotline contacts.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be collected through the feedback mechanisms and directly during deliberately organized feedback meetings at community level. Normally, all Welthungerhilfe projects and locations have a feedback analysis committee chaired by respective project managers. The committee analyses feedback immediately it is received and decides on responses. Responses are then directed to the responsible units for implementation which includes adjustment of approaches and communication to affected parties. The project manager follows up to ensure implementation and refers issues to higher levels and third parties as may be deemed necessary. The response mechanism has clear guidelines of protecting information sources.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Referral pathways and linkages to other service providers including those in protection, education health and nutrition will be created. Already Welthungerhilfe has established working relations with actors in all the selected locations and is an active member of the UN-Cluster system through which specialized service providers can be identified.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Reduction of the proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4, 5 will be addressed through provision of inputs and training to enable them restart household food production. They will also be supported to create assets that have a bearing on food production like flood mitigation dikes that protect farmlands and access roads that improve access to markets. Additional measures will include sensitization on climate change and community-based disaster risk reduction.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will advocate for equitable access of services at cluster meetings at field and country office level. Factors contributing to inequalities will be addressed in trainings and community level meetings to be organized by the project. Communities will additionally be supported to address access challenges through mainstreamed sensitization on existing services, how and where to access them.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will prioritize targeting households hosting children under 5 years who are affected by severe malnutrition, levels 3 and 4. Initial selection will involve collaboration with nutrition service providers and will be focused on those admitted to for treatment. Such households will be enlisted in cash for asset activities to improve market access while also being supported with inputs to engage in own production. The households will be further supported to grow vegetables or engage in fishing as a way of diversifying their diets. Care takers of severely affected children will additionally undergo targeted IYCF trainings that shall include cooking demonstrations, breastfeeding and household hygiene.
