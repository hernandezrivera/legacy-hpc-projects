Project Summary

The project aims at emergency support for Gaza farmers and fishermen in order to improve their economic situation through rehabilitation of their agricultural facilities and their fishing means . The project targeted will be vulnerable households engaged in farming, animal breeding or ﬁshing activities with limited financial capacity to cover the costs of the needed production inputs and needed materials for the rehabilitation of their agricultural facilities. Vulnerable households affected by the IHL/IHRL violations, deteriorated economic situation, C19 pandemic, and/or other types of shocks and engaged in farming, animal breeding or ﬁshery will receive livelihood support in-kind through the rehabilitation activities to help them maintain their production capacity and protect their livelihoods, while the most acutely vulnerable households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities will be supported through creating temporary job opportunities (CFW) to cover their immediate basic needs. UAWC focuses also on the infrastructural challenges for protecting agricultural livelihoods in the Gaza Strip such as; damaged agricultural land , greenhouses and poultry farms through land , greenhouse, poultry farms rehabilitation; shortages of electricity and water in agriculture through provision solar PV system for agricultural wells and water ponds and rehabilitation of fishing boats and boats engines . The Action will enable vulnerable households engaged in farming, animal breeding, or ﬁshery, including vulnerable women, to maintain their agricultural activities and enhance their coping capacity to future shocks.
The project will contribute to the following overarching country strategic objectives: SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. It specifically will contribute to the food security sector objectives: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced (CO1) and Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs (CO2).
The planned action was closely coordinated with the Food Security Sector, ministry of Agriculture (MoA), ministry INGO and NGOs

1 - Needs

The ongoing deterioration of humanitarian situation has markedly affected the households’ ability to generate a decent income to meet their basic needs, with the weak purchasing power and limited cash ﬂow. Nearly 72 per cent of the food insecure, or 1.6 million people, live in the Gaza Strip, where the pandemic and the PA’s financial crisis have compounded the socio-economic impact of the longstanding Israeli blockade, the internal Palestinian divide and the frequent escalations of hostilities.The unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip during the second quarter of 2020 reached 49.1 per cent, an historical high, up from 45.5 in the previous quarter. The sharpest increase in unemployment was recorded in restaurants, hotels, commerce and construction activities. Additionally, some people employed in Israel have lost their jobs following the outbreak of COVID-19. People who are dependent for their livelihoods on the ARA at land and sea are exposed to another layer of vulnerabilities due to the enforcement of these areas. Vulnerabilities are acknowledged and reﬂected in the HNO and the HRP, and place them among the most vulnerable groups in Gaza . In October (UAWC) held a meeting with MoA regarding the urgent needs in the fisheries sector and here are the main needs highlighted by the ministry: 200 boats are in need of urgent rehabilitation, 150 boat engines need to be repaired, all types of fishing net to be distributed and there is a need to distribute Marine navigation devices such as: Fish finder and GPS ( unfortunately not available in the local market and currently impossible to import them). Moreover the ministry added that the sea was closed direct after the detection of the first infected person by C19 pandemic inside Gaza community on 24 August 2020 for duration of 11 days and as a result of stopping the fishing activities during this period the loses in the fishery sector is estimated by 594,020 USD In September 2020 , UAWC agricultural local committees made a needs survey through contacting farmers in individual form in all Gaza Governorates and localities because it was impossible to conduct workshops during August and September as result of COVID 19 spread inside the Gaza communities and the imposing of curfew and movement restriction. this survey indicated that out of about 11000 donum of existing greenhouses in the Gaza strip ,there are 5340 donum GH are in need for the rehabilitation, 11500 donum of open field are damaged and uncultivated and in need for the rehabilitation,, 1120 water ponds are in need for rehabilitation and 122 poultry farms need to be rehabilitated . moreover the most interviewed farmers confirmed that the power supply needs for the irrigation is one most problem that they faced and they are in urgent need for support in order to overcome this problem. 4 months ago UAWC conducted several meeting with farmers and the majority of the participants reported that the restrictions and blockade imposed on Gaza affected their ability to generate income. Due to the ongoing economic crisis as a result of salary cut by PA as well as the delay of MoSD subsidies, farmers are forced to sell their products at lower prices resulting in low proﬁt, the loses through the price decreasing are estimated by 25% drop in the profit. Some farmers reported incurring losses due to selling at prices lower than the production cost. 62% of surveyed farmers reported to have a reduced income, while 20 % reported having no income at all. All farmers conﬁrmed that they have accumulated debts that they cannot pay back, and that most agro-suppliers are not providing assets or input on credit anymore. Access to water for irrigation is considered among key challenges for farmers in the ARA. This can be attributed to the infrastructure challenge related to the shortages of electricity, which directly affect the irrigation water availability and supply due to the fact that the majority of the agricultural wells are operated by electricity. Lack of job opportunities was raised by the most participants. In the other hand, UAWC designed the intervention in coordination and consultation with MOA and FSS through participation in several meeting with MOA and took in consideration the recommendation and priorities of MOA related to the planed agricultural project activities .

2 - Activities

UAWC will target the following target groups: (1)Acutely vulnerable households with limited access to food, income, and/or employment opportunities (Unemployed workers ), (2) Vulnerable small-scale farmers, breeders, greenhouse and open field farmers and fishermen with limited capacity to acquire the essential production inputs, access market, or with livelihood assets affected by IHL/IHRL violations in the ARA and other types of shocks, (3) Farmers with limited/interrupted access to water due to energy crisis.All targeted households will be selected according to the shocks incurred, protection-related, and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. Selection criteria will include: 1. Vulnerable fishermen whose productive assets are affected by natural and man-made disasters (mainly IHL/IHRL violations).2. Vulnerable fishermen who do not have boats (to work on boats rehabilitation)3. Small and medium scale poultry farmers4. Small-scale farmers (Open field up to 2 dunums)5. Small-scale greenhouse farmers (up to 1 dunum)6. unemployed workers 7. Priorities will be given to women headed families ( women are women in agricultural activities )7. Priorities will be given to HHs with big family member and HHs with disable member8. priorities will be given to women who own agricultural facility ( open land greenhouse, poultry farm) 9. Priorities will be given to farmers affected by COVID-19 Activity1: Rehabilitation of 20 poultry farms, after the rehabilitation of the farms, 20 solar units will be installed for the ventilation and lighting of poultry farms. Activity 2: Rehabilitation of 200 dunam of destroyed or uncultivated agricultural land, the rehabilitation will include, plowing and cleaning the land, installation of irrigation networks and cultivation of vegetables seedlings and supply organic fertilizers to the rehabilitated land to speed the vegetables seedlings growth and agricultural productions.Activity 3: Rehabilitation of 100 greenhouses the rehabilitation will include the replacing of ; defected plastic sheets, defected woods for greenhouses drainage canals and replacing the old and defected anti insects GH nets, after the completion of the rehabilitation the GH farmer will receive vegetables seedling to cultivate it inside the greenhouse Activity 4: Rehabilitation of 4 agricultural wells and installation of 4 solar units to operate these wells, the targeted wells will be technically evaluated , then rehabilitated through maintenance of defected parts and connection fitting, the parts that can not be maintained will be replaced by new ones ( such as some well pipes , well pump, gear head ), after the completion of the well rehabilitation , solar units will installed to operate the well through using solar energy Activity 5: Rehabilitation of 20 water ponds to store and harvest the water for the use of irrigation.the rehabilitation of the pond will include ; the replacing of defected plastic sheets, installation of water pump with the capacity of 2 HP, extending of PVC pipes and connecting it with greenhouse rain drainages canals on the greenhouse ceiling in order to harvest rain water, covering the pond with shading net 90% and protection the ponds through surrounding it by metal fence to protect the farmers children against any sinking inside the ponds ( saving life), finally, installation of solar unit to operate the water pump for the irrigation of the greenhouses Activity 6: Rehabilitation of 100 fishermen boats , the rehabilitation will include maintenance defected boats through using fiberglass and gauze specialized for boats rehabilitation and replacing the old defected boats wood then painting the boats with special paint that can resist the sea water impaction on the boat Activity 7. Employing of 130 unemployed workers for the duration of 25 days each, out of these workers 30 will be skilled and 100 unskilled, these workers will be employed through. creation of 3250 working days during the implementation of the project activities ( GH and land rehabilitation , boats rehabilitation)

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Before starting any activity, the UAWC project team assesses the security situation in the place and make sure that there are no suspicious objects or obstacles, that can lead to harm the personnel and the project equipment during the implementation, in the case of detecting any suspicious object in the place , the project team contacts immediately the responsible competent authorities in order to inspect the place and remove these objects. This procedure is considered as one of the most relevant procedures within the mechanisms followed by the UAWC for the safety of the project staff and workers , more over the UAWC will work only in safe and accessible areas, and will work as much as possible on the safety of the project staff and workers , UAWC will withdraw immediately workers and employees in the event of a deterioration in the security situation in any area where the project activities will be carried out The Union of Agricultural Work Committees is careful not to cause any harms or damages to the target groups during the implementation of any service of the project inputs, whether financial extortion, sexual exploitation or any physical damage, through intensified follow-up and continuous evaluation of the performance of employees and hold them accountable for any mistakes they may commit With the principle of not harming the target groups or the local community, in addition to thatUAWC is fully committed to the do no harm principle, and promoting safety and dignity. For each phase of the proposed intervention, UAWC will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. This will be done through assessments, continuous evaluation and consultations with the targeted groups . The targeted communities and beneﬁciaries will be actively involved in all phases of the Action and all feedback will be taken into account in best possible manners to avoid any unintended harm which could be caused to BNFs by the action. The inclusive approach and the close cooperation with communities, CBOs and agricultural local committees and beneﬁciaries is designed to ensure privacy, safety, dignity and accessibility for men and women, with or without disability and to contribute to the enhancement of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and contribute to the Action at all stages of the implementation. Lastly, all involved staff is aware in PSEA policies and the action continuously engage in GBV mainstreaming by as an example having staff trained in identifying and referring GBV survivors as non-specialized actors.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with speciﬁc needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is speciﬁcally designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, UAWC will analyze possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with agricultural and fishermen local committees, CBOs and beneﬁciaries with a speciﬁc focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore, the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneﬁciaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with speciﬁc needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities as income providers, to ensure equal access to productive resources, facilitate access to market while promoting joint decision-making in income use. Obstacles to as an example female headed households’ economic participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved beneﬁciaries on challenges and possible solutions. UAWC is working to ensure that programmes and services are available and accessible to persons with and without disabilities. Therefore, they work on a non-discriminatory approach, where a person with a disability is able to exercise their protected rights on an equal basis with other members of the community. UAWC As civil NGO is also committed to protect Rights of Persons with Disabilities and inclusion principles and practices of the most vulnerable. UAWC ensures that persons with disabilities , women and their families are involved and consulted at all stages of the project cycle. For instance, during the needs assessment phase, UAWC includes women who identiﬁes as local committees member to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities. As a result, UAWC will identify and work to remove any barriers that may prevent persons with disabilities or women who are working in the agricultural sector from effectively and safely using services and participating in projects as well as build on identiﬁed enablers. During the planning phase, UAWC ensured that the disability-related ﬁndings and recommendations from the assessment stage have been carried through into the project design. UAWC then, make adaptations when necessary to ensure accessibility and inclusiveness of project services. Further, during the implementation phase, service delivery mechanisms can be adapted to improve access for women and persons with disabilities.Moreover, UAWC will announce the project activities and project interventions in the project targeted areas , where every vulnerable farmers ( women, men, disables person) will have access to fill application for the benefiting from the project inputs , after that the UAWC team will evaluate the applications and select the beneficiaries without any discrimination between men or women or any other groups , all applicants will be given the equal opportunities to be selected when they match the selection criteria of the project, in addition to that , the targeted groups will have access to all UAWC offices and they can submit complaints if they have any, here is worth to mention that UAWC have complaints boxes in all of its offices in all Gaza Strip.
