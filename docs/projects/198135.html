Project Summary

The project aims to address the WASH needs of a targeted total of 117,000 conflict affected populations across Borno and Adamawa States. The overall project objective is to ensure that conflict affected have reduced exposure to WASH related illnesses through improved access to potable drinking water, appropriate/dignified sanitation facilities and strengthened hygiene practices. Prioritization of interventions will be based on the needs identified to and tailored to the needs for the disabled, elderly, different age groups and displacement status. The project will address the needs for the internally displaced persons (newly displaced and protracted crisis), returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities. The targeted communities will contribute to the implementation of the project through further needs assessments, contributing to the design, and participation during project monitoring and giving feedback during the project surveys and post distribution monitoring. The project will also be tailored to address the emerging and re-emerging epidemics, related to provision of adequate water, sanitation and hygiene services. This will include responding to such outbreaks such as cholera, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and other related epidemics. More specially, the project activities will include the following; Rehabilitation/upgrading and construction water points. This includes optimization, solarisation, replacing pumps to optimize the capacity of the boreholes, improvement of the water collection points, replacement of taps and old pipes. Water treatment and water quality monitoring. This includes introduction of inline water chlorination, monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels both at the point of collection and at the household level. Samples will be collected for bacteriological analysis from households that will have zero free residual chlorine levels. Emergency water distribution/treatment via water trucking (where all other water supply options are not available). This activity shall be limited to a period of not more than 3months. Establishment and capacity building of water management committees. Water committees will be equipped with tool boxes in order to undertake minor works Construction of new latrines and bathing shelters. Priority shall be given to communities who practice open defecation and do not have latrines. Operation and maintenance of latrines, including minor repairs and maintenance, desludging and minor rehabilitations such as replacement of broken doors, handles and some missing part of the super structure. Hygiene Promotion messages will be delivered through community based trained hygiene promoters and the information, education and communication materials will be adopted from the sector approved materials. Distribution of Hygiene Non-Food Items (NFIs), including the installation of hand washing stations. The distribution will be targeted, and will be done through both in kind and the use of vouchers (where markets are functioning). The NFIs will also be adopted from the sector approved kits, which includes family hygiene kits, latrine cleaning kits, cholera kits etc. Trainings and capacity building, this will be done for community based hygiene promoters, WASH committees, adolescent girls and other targeted trainings for water, sanitation and hygiene staff implementing the project.The project will target Monguno, Jere, MMC and Gwoza Local Government Areas (LGA) of Borno State and Yola North, Yola South and Mubi North LGAs.The implementation of this project shall be closely coordinated with the relevant government ministry (ministry of water resources), Rural Water and Sanitation agency(RUWASA), UN agencies (UNICEF, IOM), other WASH implementing partners and local Non-Governmental Organizations.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria entered into its twelfth year in 2022. The situation in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States continues to deteriorate as the effects of shrinking humanitarian space impacts programming. The number of people in need according to the Humanitarian Programme Cycle, Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) February 2022 is 8.4 million. The shrinking humanitarian space according to European Commission is largely due to deteriorating security and restrictions imposed by the government and mistrust towards humanitarian actors. Endemic Cholera and flooding has made WASH needs and response more complex because of existing vulnerabilities in BAY States. Self-organized and government-led movement of people, (new displacements and returns) have constrained development of durable WASH solutions as the outlook constantly changes. On the other hand, protracted displacement is straining host communities because of the high pressure on the existing, already-overburdened water points and sanitation facilities.Access to sanitation is fragile from wear and tear of emergency-type construction, and seasonal flooding. Such erosion of sanitation access raises rates of open defecation and reduced handwashing elevates the risk of a major outbreak within the susceptible community. A critical gap for the sector is sanitation for persons with special needs, women and children to ensure safety and equitable access; solving these issues of proximity and design of the facilities is likely to require additional resources OCHA, 2021. Effects of extreme weather conditions have worsened the situation, the scale of needs dramatically increased due to Cholera and floods, further exacerbating existing challenges including climate change, insecurity, displacement, human right violations and access restrictions. Across the targeted areas of under this intervention in Maiduguri and Jere in Borno state and Yola North, Yola South, Girei, Mubi in Adamawa state, emergency and makeshift shelters are the shelter options for the majority of IDPs in host communities and IDP settlements. Currently in Northeast Nigeria there are 1.3 million people in need for shelter and NFI assistance as per the 2021 HRP. Shelters constructed in informal settlements have been predominantly temporary and are emergency shelters not meant for long-term use. Outside of camps, semi-permanent shelters used by displaced people are dilapidated. Nearly half a million homes and 700 public buildings have been destroyed by the conflict, along with 1,200 schools, nearly 800 health facilities, and 1,600 water supply sources (various assessment reports). The government plan for camp closure may further deteriorate situation as it may lead to the secondary and even tertiary displacement of IDP population. Similarly, n Maiduguri and Jere in Borno state and Yola North, Yola South, Girei, Mubi in Adamawa state access to WASH services continues to be a challenge with gaps in services delivery and growing needs across the sector. Moreover, communicable diseases are highly prevalent, with IDPs and other vulnerable groups at high risk due to overcrowded conditions and limited services. According to the HNO, 2021 WASH facilities including access to potable drinking water are often lacking or insufficient in schools and are a major barrier to attendance by families for both boys and girls (19% and 18% respectively) in North East Nigeria, especially for children living with disabilities. This poses a challenge and an opportunity to NRC Education and WASH Core Competencies for an integrated approach to contribute to solving this issue. In addition to these humanitarian needs in relatively accessible areas, most recently the humanitarian community has identified approximately 1,000,000 individuals staying in hard-to-reach areas very difficult to reach with humanitarian assistance in the Multisector Need Assessment (MNSA) for Covid-1

2 - Activities

Emergency response: This response focuses on any unforeseen natural disasters, displacements or epidemics and will be implemented jointly with the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The aim is to provide immediate assistance to the affected populations or basic survival before the conventional WASH response. The response will be closely coordinated through the RRM working group. These specific emergency response includes emergency water trucking, which will only be limited to a period of no more than three months, while looking for other more sustainable water supply options. Response to outbreaks including disinfections, water point treatment, distribution of kits, awareness raising and coordination, provision of communal latrines and rehabilitation of existing ones, distribution of essential NFI hygiene kits that will includes soaps, detergents, water collection containers, menstrual hygiene items amongst other items. Faecal sludge management will also be a key component of this response. WASH response to protracted crisis: The WASH response will focus on improving service coverage, building community structures and setting up more sustainable systems beyond the emergency phase. The response includes the following; Water Supply: This activity will be conducted and guided by the needs identified through rehabilitation of existing water both functional and non-functional water sources, water point treatment and drilling of new boreholes. NRC will work closely with the state, local government and federal ministries of water resources. For sustainability of the infrastructure provided, NRC will take into consideration the issue of operation and maintenance on all the water facilities provided. To address this, NRC will establish a water user committee in close consultation with the target communities within camps, urban displacements and out of camps. Before handing over the constructed water points, NRC will ensure that water quality tests are conducted, and the results meet the recommended standards for drinking water. Batch chlorination will be conducted at the water distribution points/boreholes. Water quality monitoring will be done at the point of distribution and at point of use to ensure that it is in line with the SPHERE standards. Sanitation: NRC will provide user-friendly and culturally acceptable latrines and bathrooms (shared household latrines, communal latrines or emergency latrines) that are gender-segregated to prevent and mitigate the risk of potential GBV-related incidents, including harassment against women and girls. NRC will work closely with ICLA-HLP colleagues to negotiate for land for latrine construction. All facilities constructed will be equipped with a cleaning kit and community mobilization will be done to ensure proper use and maintenance. Hygiene promotion: Implementation will target behaviour change and prevention and mitigation of epidemics. WASH will lead in the harmonization of messages to be shared, should there be need to jointly disseminate some messages along with the implementation of activities from other core competencies. To address outbreaks, and with the understanding that behaviour change is a process that might take time, the hygiene promotion teams will work directly with the target beneficiaries in denying the health risks and economic burdens associated with these practices rather than disseminating direct messages. Contextualized Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials addressing the key risks will be developed and adopted tore in force the messages. Hygiene Non Food Items (NFI) Distribution: Supplies to support the adoption of good hygiene practices will also be provided either through in-kind supplies or through the use of cash & vouchers, subject to proper markets assessments. The use of vouchers will be preferred over in-kind as it gives the beneficiaries a variety of choices to purchase the actual items needed and in the desired brands.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming NRC will put in place some key protection mainstreaming elements in this project. NRC will ensure that the proposed intervention provides safety and dignity, and prevents harm to the target beneciaries. NRC recognises that the individual needs of women, men, girls and boys differ based on the person’s health, age, physical and displacement status, and living conditions. Therefore consultations shall form a key component on project implementation. WASH needs are also different for women and girls compared to men and boys, and also the general protection risks are highly dependent on gender, age, displacement status, ethnicity, geographical area and poverty level. NRC has developed detailed protection guidelines and will deliver trainings to all project staff, to increase staff capacity to identify protection issues and refer accordingly. NRC did map out the protection risks affecting the displaced populations in some locations in Borno will continuously monitor the information throughout the project life. In WASH, some of the common mitigation measures will arise as a result of siting and appropriateness of the WASH facilities. NRC will ensure that r women and girls (mainly charged with the responsibility of fetching water for household chores) are consulted on safety and security concerns, and site the water points to ensure they are close enough to the community to mitigate GBV risks for women and girls. For sanitation, NRC will mark the latrines for use, ensuring clear gender segregation, more applicable in camp-like settings. To further improve on protection, in regards to utilisation of WASH facilities, NRC will install lockable doors in latrines and bathing shelters and equip the beneciaries with solar lamps for use at night. The latrines will be located near theshelters to mitigate possible cases of sexual harassment. NRC will ensure that an effective complaint and feedback mechanism is in place to receive opinions from beneciaries and local authorities. A stronger NRC presence on-the-ground will ensure the ability to identify quickly and refer (to other partners) protection concerns through NRC’s eld staff. Finally, NRC has established a specific Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) developed for crowd control during distributions, which will reduce the risk of attacks towards beneciaries. The SOP consists in limiting the number of people during distributions (focusing on the most vulnerable) and coordination with local authorities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback from the targeted beneficiaries will be implemented throughout the project, addressing the needs for the vulnerable communities. In order to collect feedback, beneficiaries shall be involved at different stages of the project, in which feedback will be collected. It includes the following; - Involving the target communities in the initial needs assessments. The beneficiaries will give feedback to the project design, this will ensure that the program delivery is based on the actual needs assessed and contributions from the community. - Establishment of a community feedback mechanism. This will be done by conducting scheduled meetings with the beneficiaries to receive their feedback on the project. Information provided by the beneficiaries will be used to improve the programme quality, and the complaints and issues will be addressed. - Organizing for Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), the project addresses the needs of different groups and through focus group discussions (FGDs) with men, women, elderly, and people living with disabilities, to collect their feedback on the appropriateness of the project design. - Identification of focal point persons from within the affected communities, such as through the WASH committees who will be the contact persons for the target beneficiaries. Any complaints or concerns about the project can be channeled through them. They will also ensure that the target beneficiaries get their entitlements as per the project description. For example, for the water trucking, community representatives will be required to verify the quantities of water deliveries, along with NRC staff and the water vendors.
