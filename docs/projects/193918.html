Project Summary

The project aims at improving shelter and living conditions for PwD and people with chronic diseases with priority to women headed HHs in Gaza Strip. This will contribute to their protection and create safe, adequate, and healthy living environment. The intervention will target 200 families from 5 governorates who suffer poor economic and social status, with an estimated number of 1200 beneficiaries; disaggregated as follows: 265 men, 260 women, 275 boys, 270 girls, and 100 people with disabilities and 33 elderly) living in substandard shelters and lacking basic infrastructure. 100 HHs will have at least one person with disability and 100 HH will have at least one person with chronic disease.
The average cost per HH is $3,600. QRCS will follow its rigorous procurement process to contract a local contractor to implement required rehabilitation works that will consider the adaptation for PWD a priority through installation of ramps, handrails and any other needed adaptation tailored to the PWDs needs , in addition to regular rehabilitation including fixing/replacing deteriorated doors and windows for safety and protection purposes and roof rehabilitation and/or replacement if necessary to protect families from winter storms and flooding and displacement and from summarization problems. Works will also include but are not limited to solar system, plumbing, electricity works, and tiling, which are important for hygiene and good health.
The project is in line with cluster objective 2 (Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities) and is in line with cluster priority #1 (Rehabilitation of damaged and/or substandard shelters to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, including adaptation interventions for the persons with disabilities and elderly and upgrade/separate shared toilet and kitchen facilities).
QRCS has extensive experience in the shelter sector. Over the past 2 years, QRCS has been involved in rehabilitating 120 substandard houses, with budgets of 446,951 USD. Work is finished in 50 units and is still ongoing in 70 others. Shelter rehabilitation is an ongoing program, which means that QRCS can have a contribution of 20-25% towards the proposed intervention's budget.
ORCS will coordinate with MoSD and Shelter Cluster in all phases of the project. QRCS will select the beneficiaries based on agreed criteria with MoSD considering the humanitarian situation, economic status, health and social status of the beneficiaries. QRCS will ensure that the selection criteria consider women, men and people with disabilities. QRCS will give special attention to women-headed families as they face triple sufferings; consequences of political situation, being women in a relative gender-unequal culture, and low opportunity to work and participate in the community.
QRCS will contract company to do the rehabilitation works based on the need of each household and within budget. In addition to the rehabilitation work, QRCS will provide solar systems for those in need in order to ensure the availability of power that is needed to operate the medical devices for PwD and chronic diseases patients.
As part of beneficiaries' participation, QRCS will engage the beneficiaries in all phases of the project especially the first phase of the project. QRCS will take into consideration the preference of the beneficiaries in the rehabilitation work within the scope and capacity of the project.
QRCS will have direct follow up and supervision to the implementation of rehabilitation activities, following the project's MEAL plan and reporting during all stages of the project.
The intervention pays a close attention to equal and impartial access to aid, mainstreams gender and age, disability and protection and is specifically designed to benefit the most vulnerable population including people with disabilities and chronic diseases patients.

1 - Needs

Gaza Strip continues to suffer from a chronic housing shortage, high costs/unavailability of construction material due to restrictions imposed by the Israeli blockade, and recurrent escalations of violence (HNO 2022). According to MSNA 2022, 1.6% of Gaza households were reported to be living in inadequate shelters at the time of data collection, and 59.8% of households reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues. The impact of the May 2021 escalation of violence on shelter conditions in Gaza remains apparent one year later, with 37.3% of households reporting that their shelter was damaged during the escalation and 44.4% of households reporting having taken any kind of measure to protect themselves or their shelter from armed conflict. Shelter repair (14.0%) was the second most frequently reported primary reason for taking on debt by households in Gaza. The project aims to improve shelter and living conditions for PwD and people with chronic diseases (giving priority to women headed HHs) in Gaza Strip. This in turn will contribute to their protection and create a safe, adequate, and healthy living environment. The intervention will target 200 households that suffer from poor economic and social status, with an estimated number of 1200 beneficiaries; disaggregated as follows: 265 men, 260 women, 275 boys, 270 girls, and 100 people with disabilities and 30 elderly). Gender role dynamics: The intervention contributes to improving living conditions for 1200 people, including 260 women, who will directly benefit from rehabilitation works related to WASH facilities. Cultural rules in Gaza Strip stipulate that the women are the ones responsible for house chores including the washing and cleaning. Rehabilitation of those facilities will help ease the burdens carried out by the women living in the targeted households. This can also, indirectly contribute to lesser incidents of GBV, given that women have improved facilities (kitchens and bathrooms) and more privacy, and men get to experience lesser pressures due to the difficult living conditions. More privacy issues will be considered through installation/repair of doors, locks, and other needs At least 100 people with disabilities will directly benefit from the intervention leading to facilitated access to bathrooms as well as better living conditions in general which could contribute to better health status. Some of the households will benefit from solar energy, whether electricity or warm water, which will also contribute to improving their lives.Better hygiene and health in general are expected outcomes of the intervention, especially with winterization activities including window repairs, doors, ceilings, provision of hot water, etc. This is particularly important for the health status of younger children who are more vulnerable to harsh weather conditions, therefore the intervention will benefit 545 children in this regard. QRCS has conducted a rapid assessment was carried out during the week of October 16th, via field visits and direct communication with 45 participants (8 women, 37 men), heads of households were: 84% males, and 16% females, 43% of the families assessed included PWD and 57% families had people with chronic illnesses. The surveyed families represent 15% of a total sample of 300 vulnerable households nominated by the MoSD. The results showed that 80 % of the targeted HHs were found to be in need for urgent shelter assistance. And the average area of housing units/shelter is 119 M2, and 71% of the HHs live under nonconcrete roofs (29% are of metallic sheets). Regarding the age of the unit/shelter, 39.5% are older than 20 years, 30.9% are between 10 to 20 years old and 29.6% are younger than 10 years old. Regarding floors, 36% of HHs’ floors were of concrete layers or on sand with no tiling works. Regarding building works, 36% of HHs have partially damaged/deteriorated external walls, and 50% of HHs have partially damaged/deteriorated internal walls; 14% of these walls aren’t finished. Moreover, the rapid assessment includes the assessment of safety aspects. The results showed that 43% of HHs have no safety measures (lack of windows protections, …etc.), 57% of HHs reported suffering from rainwater flooding for at least 1 time every winter season. Also, 29% of surveyed HHs reported that there are no separate bedrooms allocated for women nor girls. Only 33% of them were adapted partially or equipped for PwDs use. In addition, about 43% of toilets need repairing works, and 72% of the assessed HHs need an urgent intervention to repair and upgrade kitchens. Moreover, 50% of them have no source of hot water.To ensure PSEA, training has been provided to relevant staff members males and females. In addition, the contracts with contractors include a PSEA clause that ensures the commitment of the contractor to protection and exploitation procedures. Moreover, QRCS will provide the target HHs with a protection sheet that includes definition of PSEA, referral paths and protection procedures. Accessible and clear awareness-raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to target beneficiaries and other stakeholders. QRCS has a PESA trained focal point whose role is to support project staff/unit to ensure that they consider PESA in designing and implementing a project gender mainstreaming strategy and building and sustaining gender responsive M&E system. QRCS focal point for PSEA is registered to receive an upcoming 3-day training as well.At the project level, feedback and complaint mechanisms will be clear and explained to beneficiaries. Satisfaction surveys will be filled in during and after the intervention. Any complaints raised will be handled in a manner that ensures privacy and follow the no-harm regulations.

2 - Activities

The project is designed to provide shelter rehabilitation to 200 vulnerable HHs where at least one member of the family is with disability or suffers from chronic disease to meet basic shelter needs and minimum standards. The project will target 200 families from five governorates. The average cost of the rehabilitation per HHs is 3600$. The project was developed and identified in close coordination with the Ministry of Social Development and shelter cluster where the target group was recommended during a meeting between QRCS and shelter cluster team, also the type of intervention and beneficiaries’ data were identified as a result of close coordination with the MoSD. Needs assessment that was carried out during the week of October 16th reflects a direct role of beneficiaries in the design phase, and this will be further expanded once the project starts. ORCS will coordinate with the MoSD, shelter cluster and Ministry of public works and housing in the implementation phase to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. Priority will be given to: 1. Families that have one or more person with disability 2. Families that have one or more person suffering from chronic disease 3. Women headed households (widows, divorced, etc.) 4. The ceiling of the house is made from corrugated metallic sheets 5. Crowded houses. QRCS will develop a technical assessment tool in close participation with Local community members that will address the detailed and specific needs of each HHs. Once the needs are decided and BoQ is ready, QRCS will follow its rigorous procurement process to contract a local contractor to implement the required rehabilitation works that will consider the adaptation for PWD as priority intervention through installation of ramps, handrails for stairs and bathrooms, and any other needed adaptation tailored to the PWDs needs , in addition to regular rehabilitation including fixing/replacing deteriorated doors and windows for safety and protection purposes, and roof rehabilitation and/or replacement if necessary to protect families from winter storms and flooding and displacement and from summarization problems. Works will also include but are not limited to solar system, plumbing, electricity works, and tiling, which are important for hygiene and good health. The project will target the rehabilitation of 100 houses of PWDs and the other 100 homes will be for people suffering from chronic diseases. QRCS has a well-experienced staff and has its own procurement system that is intended to facilitate work and implementation. QRCS takes into consideration the quality of works, duration for implementation, and prices, adopting international integrity and transparency standards. In addition, QRCS has an extended expertise in the Shelter sector where over the past 2 years, it has implemented 3 rehabilitation projects for substandard houses covering 120 houses (some are still in progress).As part of beneficiaries' participation, QRCS will engage the beneficiaries in all phases of the project and will take into consideration the preference of the beneficiaries in the rehabilitation work within the scope and capacity of the project. Moreover, QRCS will conduct regular and quick feedback to maximize the quality & transparency of services. QRCS will make sure that beneficiaries (women, girls, boys and men in all their diversities) are informed about the presence of the complaint mechanisms and encouraged to use them, complaints will be assessed, and proper action taken, and feedback will be shared with the beneficiaries. All above activities will be conducted under clear regulation to ensure maximum protection of vulnerable beneficiaries while any sensitive complaint of PESA or incidences will have a certain pathway internally or by referring to other protection service provider. Furthermore, there are clear lines for the privacy and confidentiality that are shared between the working staff, and there are clear rules for data collection that include not collecting unnecessary information nor sharing information with irrelevant parties. To ensure PSEA, training has been provided to relevant staff members males and females. In addition, the contracts with contractors include a PSEA clause that ensures the commitment of the contractor to protection and exploitation procedures. Moreover, QRCS will provide the target HHs with a protection sheet that includes definition of PSEA, referral paths and protection procedures. Accessible and clear awareness-raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to target beneficiaries and other stakeholders. QRCS has a PESA trained focal point whose role is to support project staff/unit to ensure that they consider PESA in designing and implementing a project gender mainstreaming strategy and building and sustaining gender responsive M&E system. A focal point for PSEA from QRCS is already registered in an upcoming 3-day training as well.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The implementation modality will be service delivery. Even though the cash modality is preferable by the beneficiaries, service delivery modality in some cases is a key factor of success specially when targeting very vulnerable groups like families PWDs and illness with low capacity to manage the cash and the rehabilitation activities. This modality has been selected as it builds on QRCS experience in this field. Contracting a local contractor to carry out needed rehabilitation based on expert assessment by relative project staff will ensure that the houses are rehabilitated to the highest standards and meet people’s needs. The local contractor shall be able to provide maximum safety measures in worksites and work under full technical support of the QRCS. Full consultations with targeted HHs will continue to make sure that gender and age preferences and needs are covered. In addition, using the service delivery modality will contribute to protecting the targeted vulnerable population from any possible exploitation by workers had they been the ones implementing the work by themselves.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project implementation strategies adhere to the social, political & economic context of targeted populations and are based on comprehensive understanding of the targeted contexts through the conducted participatory needs assessment. In terms of social sensitivity, QRCS has a clear understanding of the conservative culture in Gaza, as it has been working there for past 14 years & act on that in all project phases from the announcement that takes place in a wide, easily accessed area where women can feel comfortable to join & share their needs. At the economic level, the project interventions aim at assisting the targeted HHs to adapt positive coping strategies and improve resilience towards risks QRCS will follow up the consultations of the beneficiaries to analyze potential risks and the possible mitigation methods to reduce the impact of the rising risks. Furthermore, there are clear lines for the privacy and confidentiality that are shared between the working staff, and there are clear rules for data collection that include not collecting unneeded information nor sharing information with any except the working staff. If any risk to the clients is shown, QRCS will act in a timely manner to protect their safety and dignity.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

QRCS is a neutral organization that does not lean toward any group over the other in Gaza context. It is impartial and provides aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. QRCS is an independent organization that is not affected by commercial nor political powers. Its nature is humanitarian, and it provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. QRCS follows strict rules to maximize the geographical, financial, and cultural accessibility especially for women, children and PwDs and the working staff consider providing the beneficiaries with enough information about their rights. The organization will use both verbal and written information to overcome intellectual differences and barriers. QRCS will monitor service delivery and procurement at beneficiary levels to ensure all items are made available
