31/12/2022

Project Summary

The project will support over 40,000 OPD (5,500 boys, 4,900 girls, 19,000 women and 10,600 men including 850 persons with disabilities) consultations in static facilities/mobile clinics, facilitate 1,900 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, and facilitate the immunization 8,500 children (Girls 3,825 and Boys 4,675) with 3 doses of PENTA-3 vaccine. It will also provide quality maternal healthcare services to 4,650 women of child bearing age of IDPs, returnees and host community including FP, ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT immunization and further provide child healthcare services (routine immunization, Vit A supplementation and child growth monitoring) to 7,815 girls and 8,919 boys, including accelerated mass measles and polio campaigns.
In addition the project is designed to provide psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 75 (45 male and 30 females) mentally ill persons and provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non-specialized) MHPSS to 700 persons living with disabilities (PLwD's), including referrals for specialized services from other partners. The project will support screening 6,700 pregnant women, lactating mother and children under 5s for malnutrition and refer them to for treatment/management.
In the management of common illnesses, the project will facilitate the management of 7,400 uncomplicated diarrhea cases with ORS and Zinc, management of 11,200 uncomplicated malaria cases with ACT, management of 7,400 uncomplicated respiratory infections (pneumonia) treated with Amoxicillin.
In collaboration with community health workers, health promoters and Boma Health Workers, About 40,000 people (5,500 boys, 4,900 girls, 19,000 women and 10,600 men including 850 persons with disabilities) will be reached with health education and promotion to prevent disease outbreaks. In addition, 27,500 (Females 16,500 and Males 11,000) will be reached with awareness through community engagements strategy on GBV prevention, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, drug abuse, peace building and peaceful coexistence.
In each target County, CASS will conduct 12 community meetings in each Payams of the targeted Counties to provide accountability to the communities (AAP strategy). Monthly joint supportive supervision visits to increase quality of services will be facilitated to all health facilities and field mobile teams and linkages with Boma Health Initiatives (Boma Health Workers) for community-based disease surveillance and referral will be strengthen.
Finally, the project will provide care to physical injuries and CMR including level 2 and level 3 MHPSS to survivors of GBV, support active epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and respond within 48 hours and support 52 timely and weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from each of the 3 focus Counties, in addition it will support IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities.

1 - Needs

The people of South Sudan have limited access to quality health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system including inadequate health infrastructure, human resources and health funding and essential commodities for implementation health services have resulted in poor health outcomes. The health situation has further worsened due to a third consecutive year of major flooding, communal conflicts including the macro and micro impact of COVID-19. The South Sudan HNO 2022 stipulates that the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's populations, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of about 600,000 more compared to 2021. In the last quarter of 2021, some 760,000 people were affected by flooding in areas along the Nile and Lol rivers and Sudd marshlands since, according to field reports, schools, homes, health facilities and water sources were inundated, impacting people’s access to basic services, Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile state are the worst affected states (OCHA, Flooding Snapshot, 4th Nov 2021).According to Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, Mayendit County (Aug 2021) stated that Malaria, Diarrhoea and injuries were highly recorded in the health facilities and there were 22 snake bites reported by the communities, as a result of moving through water bodies. Personal hygiene of women of childbearing age and adolescent girls was compromised and was reported that they faced increased of women and girls’ related health and hygiene risks.Recently, an IRNA Report (Feb 2022) from Leer County, reported Three Payams in Leer County and other three Payams in Mayendit County have been affected by the conflict which erupted in Mirmir then extended to Tutnyang and other villages in February 2022, left 3 PHCUs looted in Mayendit County, in addition 3 health workers were attacked when going for supportive supervision and shot at but no death was recorded. The Health Cluster estimated a population of 36,575 as PIN in Guit County, targeting 18,288 people (Male 8,928 and Female 9,360) including 3,985 children under-5 years (Boys 2,140 and Girls 1,845) and in Mayendit County, PIN is 46,115 people and Health Cluster is targeting 36,892 people (Male 27,426 and Female 27,426) including 6,456 children under-5 years (Boys 3,527 and Girls 2,929). Similarly in Manyo County of Upper Nile State, the PIN is 63,147 people and Health Cluster is targeting 31,574 people (Male 16,162 and Female 15,412) including children under-5 years (Boys 3,252 and Girls 3,041). In summary, PIN in the 3 targeted Counties by CASS for HRP 2022 is 145,837 people and Health Cluster targets 86,754 people (Male 43,779 and Female 422,975) including 16,734 children under-5 years (Boys 8,919 and Girls 7,815). The key populations in all the 3 targeted counties are vulnerable children, women, men, the aged and persons with disabilities including survivors of GBV among returnees, IDPs and host communities and hard-to-reach communities or community’s cut-off by flooding. In the 3 targeted counties, CASS is implementing HPF-3 in Lot 20, Guit County with Cordaid as the lead partner managing 1 PHCC and 4 PHCUs including BHI component similarly in Lot 21, with IRC as the lead partner, CASS in managing 2 PHCCs and 10 PHCUs. In Manyo, CASS is a Sub IP to Cordaid as the Lead Partner in UNICEF-World Bank CERHSP project, Lot 1. CASS is Managing 3 PHCCs and 3 PHCUs.Irrespective, of the ongoing projects in the 3 counties, the needs are still dire and if CASS is to reach the health cluster targets in 3 counties CASS is targeting for HRP 2022, additional 5 PHCCs, 14 PHCUs including 9 Mobile Clinics, so as to increase access and utilization of health services by people in hard-to-reach communities including communities heavily affected by flood.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries will be engaged and involved at different stages of the Action from the design until end evaluation. During the assessments conducted to inform the Action, CASS engaged with the local communities (potential beneficiaries) involving them in the HH survey, FGDs and KII, identifying their needs, gaps, priorities, concerns, preferred assistance and way of communication. The findings have been incorporated in this Action. Targeted communities will also be involved in the selection criteria and selection of beneficiaries through their participation in the Project Management Committee (PMC), including women and youth representatives. Once selected, CASS will involve the beneficiaries once again through actively asking feedback prior to and during distributions, and during their visit to the mobile clinic; for this purpose, information on the feedback and complaint mechanism will be put on banners, and there will be a face-to-face complaint desk. The CMAM program works with the communities to identify, manage and prevent acute malnutrition. This increases community ownership of malnutrition, which in turn increases participation and prevention activities. Community involvement will be ensured through the community mobilisers, and community nutrition volunteers per village/site who will support screening activities, looking for defaulters and making home visits for children who were not gaining weight. They will also be involved in supporting the health mobilisers and nurses in their sensitization activities around basic household-level practices.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The mobile clinic team will work through and with the community mobilizers to raise alerts, do screening and consultations during the weekly visits, follow-up on patients and coordinate referrals for complicated cases. Volunteers are recruited from the community, including female and male volunteers, aiming for equal gender representation. Beneficiaries will also be involved at other moments, including randomized surveys or community meetings to determine the quality of the mobile clinic services, and during post distribution surveys beneficiaries will be asked for their feedback on the assistance. Community leaders will be encouraged to involve the community to protect women and other people with specific needs (PSN) who Community leaders will be encouraged to involve the community to protect women and other people with specific needs (PSN) who have received assistance. PWD, elderly and chronically ill will be identified through the community leaders, PMC and female FGDs, and will be visited at home. Their concerns and needs will be included in the assistance, location etc. Those selected for the assistance but unable to come to distribution points will be receiving their assistance at home. At the end of the project, beneficiaries will also be involved in the internal end-evaluation. Their opinion of the assistance will be obtained through FGDs and KIIs and one- on-one interviews. Findings of the evaluation will also be shared with the community.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will use CASS PSEA policy to manage and protect both staff and beneficiaries against acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff will be trained and retrained on the policy. Safe and accessible community-based reporting mechanisms will be established, and the investigation channels will be discussed with the community including how to obtain assistance and the possible disciplinary measures will also be explained. The project will adaptPSEA Network information campaigns with linked information, education and communication (IEC) materials. The information campaigns will target county health departments, partners and project staff on one hand and the community members on the other hand. All IEC materials, as a part of the campaign, will be translated into the local language and be distributed widely

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Action will specifically target vulnerable female headed HH, PLW and malnourished children under the age of 5, and PWD as assessments show they are disproportionately affected by the different crises in Rubkona. Targets have been established to ensure their inclusion. Furthermore, the Action will promote women and girls’ participation, to achieve this, the Action will be implemented in a gender and culturally sensitive way, informed by the long standing involvement in the area and the knowledge and experience CASS has with gender relations in the communities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CASS is mainstreaming protection throughout the intervention, this as well to avoid any negative side effects from the intervention, and to prevent conflict. As such, displaced as well as host families will be part of the targeted population, and the protection-related issues will be taken into account (security risks for specific groups; safety, access, dignity issues to be considered in all designs). Potential harmful outcomes for women (increased workload, reduced time for other livelihood opportunities) and children (increased child labor) will be monitored carefully and mitigated through active involvement of the communities, open dialogues, and in the planning and roll out of the activities, to ensure that activities can be integrated in the daily workload of the targeted families. In Unity State, Mayendit and Guit counties and Manyo County, Uper Nile State, the negative effects of climate change are observed in the increased flooding over the last years. Therefore, CASS will as much as possible reduce practices that increase climate change. Transportation of items and people will be planned carefully, and number of supervisory trips from Juba-based staff to the field will be limited as much as possible. Also, the procurement and logistical processes will focus on using only the absolute necessary packaging material.
