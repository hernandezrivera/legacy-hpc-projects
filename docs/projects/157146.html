Project Summary

This project is designed to address the needs indicated in the Plan International RRP for 2019 and 2020 as well the identified needs by the Participatory Assessment in Refugee camps which was conducted in Kosti of White Nile State. The assessment was carried out in December 2018 and led by UNHCR and Plan International and other stakeholders including COR and other humanitarian actors participated in this assessment. The assessment ensured that, the refugees, host communities and other persons of concern were fully involved to identify the real needs on the ground.
Plan International has accordingly prioritized a multi-sector intervention that includes protection, livelihoods & resilience, energy & environment and WASH in both the SS refugees’ camps in White Nile and the surrounding host communities to avoid and mitigate the conflict that might arise. The project will focus on empowering vulnerable SSR and host communities children and adolescent especially girls and young women.
The project will be implemented with two national NGO partners namely Elithar Charity Organization and
Friend of Peace & Development Organization whom Plan International do have a long history in working with them particularly in the project targeted geographical areas and thematic sectors.
The project will be part of the on the ground coordination mechanisms to ensure that no overlapping with other actors occurs and that the obtained resources and well managed with greater impact in the targeted beneficiaries.
The project will target 128,811 SSR out of which 55% are females for the first year and 140,121 SSR in the second year in White Nile in addition to 20% beneficiaries from the surrounding host communities in Elsalam and Aljabalain localities where those camps are located.

Needs

By November 2018 the number of SSR reached to 256126 individuals with 5533 new arrivals in Elsalam and Al-Jabalain localities, with already deteriorating basic services & not fairly reached by humanitarian actors hence HC demanded a similar assistance. Despite the efforts made lack of livelihoods & sustainable coverage of WASH services remain high and gender insensitive (UNHCR, 2018). In March 2018 a serious fuel crisis hit Sudan and strongly affected the humanitarian situation. Fuel for water transport & pumps is limited & water supply for drinking & hygiene is severely limited. Thus, people return to fetching often contaminated water directly from the river. Fuel crisis also constraint the agricultural season and HH food insecurity contributing to the critical above 15% GAM in the refugees camps in WNS according to the preliminary results for the Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) which also pointed to the wider effects of chronic under funding for the refugee response, especially for water, sanitation, hygiene, health and livelihoods assistance.The situation is further worsened by the cash crisis and had affected the purchasing powers of the vulnerable people on the other hand this resulted in shot-up of commodities prices which forced many refugees to request to be supported with livelihood opportunities based on a UNHCR participatory assessment in refugees camps in Kosti. This situation revived the black market and has hiked the prices of petrol and diesel to exaggerated prices over five times the official prices. The report also indicated that SSR have limited access to sources of energy such as firewood, charcoal, LPG cylinders due to associated protection risks for women and girls when walking for long distances to collect firewood as a result many refugees are forced to adopt other alternative negative solutions/choices such as selling considerable amount of their monthly food ration or NFIs.According to UNHCR the core issues to be addressed for SSR are children at risk/UASC to be provided with foster care arrangements, FTR, through reinforce CBCPN as well to increase number of alternative care arrangements support programs in the camps. Effective outreach services needs to be extended by deploying more social workers and community volunteers “build their capacity” to respond to the needs of children at risk. SGVB awarness raising among SSR and HC through tarinings and capacity building with specific focus on youth. SGBV survivors from SSR are facing challenges to report their cases for timely access to medication and legal aid which subjecting them to serious risks. Efforts needed to contribute to peaceful co-existence between Nuer and Shuluk tribes and between the SSR and HCs in general including girls and boys and promote livelihoods activities specific to youth to enhance harmonious co-existence environment.

Activities

In the first year the project will target 128,811 SSR through the below activities and will be implemented with two local partners as in CP:• 8755 UASC receiving appropriate interim or long term alternative care - Updating the case load in the targeted SSR Camps- FTR & prevention of family separation & violence against children referred to appropriate services- Capacity building for Train social workers & CBCPNs members- Access to NFI/FIs to support home-based care- Provide immediate relief services for most vulnerable UASC’s families/caregivers• 8 Awareness raising campaigns on SGBV prevention & response• 8060 adolescents and youth participated in different targeted programs (participation, planning, organizing & decision making & organize cross gender forums) • 16 community based committees/groups working on SGBV prevention and response• 3000 SSR participated in other activities in Protection Obj. 2WASH:• Construct & dislodge 3000 HH latrines• Distribute soaps to 10600 SSR • Recruit and train 52 HP• Distribute women hygiene kits to 400 SSR• 71240 SSR benefited from different awareness, Hygiene promotion and sanitation activities. Livelihoods and resilience:• Conduct market assessment & CBT feasibility study• 7440 SSR have access to agriculture & livestock-based IGAs especially women & girls and relevant activities & training (vertical bucket gardening, back garden farming, poultry farming, improved seeds, tools and fertilizes, VSLA groups, fish trade ) and vocational trainings including youth living with disabilities• Train 30 Animal Health Workers from the HCEnergy & environment:• Awareness creation and capacity building for 3600 PoC on environmental conservation & climate change mitigation actions in the camps & HC• Train and support 240 women & girls ToTs on utilization of local/ceramic cooking stoves & the distribution of improved stoves, Bio Gas as alternative cooking fuel• Train and support SSR&HC on tree nurseries and seedlings

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will address the overuse of the deteriorating environment by the refugee community excessively cutting trees through provision of alternative power and energy sources to limit cutting of trees and safe the environment through provision and support in training of trainers for women on utilization of local/ceramic cooking stoves and the distribution of improved stoves to SSR and HC households. Introducing the use of bio gas as an alternative cooking fuel will be piloted as an innovative modality to test its effectiveness and draw lessons for potential replication and up-scaling. In addition the project will establish tree nurseries for the purpose of re-forestation in the refugee camp, and the host community.As the construction of house holds latrines affects the environment serious mainly through the contamination of the underground water hence the project considered the site selection to be located distance from the water sources and according to the applicable standards as well the project consider in the design to have an improved under surface structures to use materials that prevents the infiltration of dirties to the water sources and finally dislodging of those latrines regulatory to safe areas away from the communities and dumped.The project will also conduct several environmental awareness creation sessions.
