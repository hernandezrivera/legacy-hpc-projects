Project Summary

Needs;According to Humanitarian Needs overview(HNO) 2020,the number of people in need of GBV intervention stands at 1,684,665. GBV continues to be an issue of great concern in Federal Members States (Galmudug, South West State, Jubaland, Hirashebelle) Puntland and Somaliland worsened by multiple displacements and forced evictions due to flooding and armed conflicts. Women and adolescent girls from 18 – 50 years old (including those living with disabilities) are subjected to Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse in Middle Shabele, Lower Shabele, Gedo, Snaag, Awdal, Biadoa, Bay, Mudug and, Banadir. A CCCM assessment in Dinsor in Bay region and a joint Humanitarian Gap Analysis (July 2020) in Middle Shabelle region (Balcad and Jowhar) in IDP camps found that distant water points, markets, health facilities/schools, poor lightning, lack of doors on toilets and lack of disaggregation of sanitary facilities are some of major factors that increase GBV exposure. Also, a July 2020 “GBV/FGM COVID19 assessment indicate that 38 percent respondents affirm increase in GBV incidents; while 35 percent inform that GBV incidents are the same. The number of GBV survivors call to GBV hotlines increased by 283% in FMS and 767%in Somaliland. Similarly, GBVIMS 2020 data show increase in intimate partner violence (55%); Physical Assault and rape at 61% and 12% respectively in contract with Physical Assault and rape at 56% and 14% of 2019. Also, draft January – August, 2020 MARA report increasing incidence of GBV among adolescent girls. Out of a total of 189 GBV incidents involving 214 survivors; 196 were girls with 16 women and 2 boys). Low reporting of GBV cases persist due to stigmatization, victim blaming, shame and interference by clan and religious leaders, slow access to justice and weak or lack of legal framework for the protection of women and girls from GBV.
Women and girls living with disabilities are the most impacted during the event of COVID19 because of their severely disadvantages of exclusion and marginalization. Restrictions to movement impacted on their ability to access the limited services that meet their needs due to location, geographical and financially related factors. Isolation and disruption of services for women and girls living with disabilities ensured that they are trapped in physically unsafe and threatening environment.
The objective of the project is to Sustain the lives needs of 4 million people requiring humanitarian assistance, including 2.4 million non-IDPs, 1.6 million IDPs and people with disability across 74 districts, by ensuring safe, equitable and dignified access to food and essential services by the end of 2021.
The project will strengthen case management for 1000 survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV).The project staff who will be providing immediate assistance to the survivors and vulnerable population will be trained on case management and survivor centered approach. WOCCA with the help of community support workers will include persons with disability in the project.1000 survivors,persons with disability and vulnerable person at risk of GBV will be provided with tailored material assistance, basic emotional support and follow up on the services provided. WOCCA will also be providing 5000 solar lanterns to reduce risks to the most vulnerable members of the community and also increase outreach and build networks by awareness raising to 12,500 community members including traditional birth attendants, imams and elders to promote awareness of GBV risks and to mitigate exposure to Gender-based violence.A total of 100 service providers,teachers,community leaders and religious leaders will be trained on GBV risks and referral pathways. 400 women at risk of GBV will be trained on life skills education to allow them cope up with difficult situations and overcome stress and pressure.

Mitigation Measures

-Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) as mitigation measure to this risk WOCCA will focus on strengthening of gender inclusive community-based protection mechanisms and robust information campaigns to ensure impartial and timely access to protection services, also in situations of displacement -Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities as mitigation measure WOCCA will do community engagement of all groups as a key requirement in the response mobilization phase and ensuring that marginalized groups are by default among the prioritized groups. With the help of community support workers WOCCA will carry out inclusive community and authority consultations to identify vulnerable, under-represented HHs, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability and clan representation.-Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms as mitigation measure to this risk WOCCA with the help of community support workers will train the community members on how to use and access the complaint and feedback mechanisms so as to get feedback from the beneficiaries.

Please provide further explanation

WOCCA has established direct relations with clan elders, protection and GBV cluster members and local authorities in targeted regions to gain access, enhance program awareness and monitoring on issues including complaints/feedback about operations. WOCCA closely engages local leaders and government officials to avert any security risk and possible threats. The project will help in training the community members comprised of men, women, girls, boys and even religious leaders on GBV risks, referral pathways, and services provided by WOCCA and other actors in the area. The project will also include vulnerable groups like the IDPs, women and youth in awareness raising to make them feel not left out. The project will have reporting mechanisms like suggestion box and mobile phones where the hotline will be shared with the IDPs to make it easy for them to report cases of SGBV to WOCCA for immediate help. The project will leave behind a well informed and educated community reducing the number of people undergoing GBV risks. WOCCA will train community leaders on psychosocial support meaning they can give proper counselling to those in the community with GBV issues even after WOCCA exits showing a long term solution. The community support workers can easily direct survivors for medical referral because they will be trained on referral pathways in the area. The religious leaders are the most respected in the community with the training they will be given on GBV they will continue to guide the community not to do any harm especially when it comes to rape, FGM and child marriages. The religious leaders will also train the community what Koran says on SGBV and the consequences of outdated practice like FGM.
