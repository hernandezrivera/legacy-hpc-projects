Project Summary

The NGO will respond to the children educational needs which have been affected by the conflict, And the outbreak of Cholera in 3 governorates in NES and the fears of infections in educational centers.. and will be providing education services including ECD to 11657 clients in Hassakeh and Raqqa governorates. NGO will connect children to education through its supportive learning program, to give those who have been out of schools the opportunity to catch up with their education. NGO will help children aged 6–15 Yrs remedy their basic education at learning points (children tents) and community learning centers in Raqqa city and informal camps around, in locations where adequate educational spaces and functioning schools are lacking. Also, NGO will ensure the distribution of education assessitive kits for both education personnel and children to assist them maintain attendance with their schooling and deliver quality education programing. NGO will focus on the capacity building of the education personnel to support quality education and best use of positive class management mechanisms. besides the training. For a maintain safe environment and better education results for the children, NGO will rehabilitate the schools as per the standards to be safer and more disability-GBV sensitive. Scientific evidence shows that the type of experiences a child has in their first five years of life plays a critical role in later development, as 85 - 90% of brain development occurs during this period. Conflict situations commonly expose children to extremely stressful and terrifying events that traumatize them. Young children growing up in crisis are vulnerable to a range of compounding risks that threaten their long-term developing and well-being. In early childhood, research on the biology of stress shows how major adversity, such as extreme poverty, abuse, or neglect can weaken developing brain architecture and permanently set the body’s stress response system on high alert. Science shows that providing stable, responsive, nurturing relationships in the earliest years of life can prevent or even reverse the damaging effects of early life stress, with lifelong benefits for learning, behavior, and health. and can provide a buffer against these risks and help children reach their potential. Thus, the NGO will provide high-quality ECD programs to mitigate the impact of crisis and displacement, and help building the foundation for children’s future academic success, health, prosperity, and wellbeing. NGO will assist the children aged 3-5 with programs that targets children and support developing their social emotional skills, in addition to programs that target their caregivers as well which helps them manage their stress and developing more positive discipline mechanisms for more nurturing relationship. The NGO will consider more community based interventions to ensure engagement and scaling up ECD program. NGO will ensure the inclusion of the children with disability through building the capacity of the teachers on use of IAE( Identity, Abilities, Experiences) guidance when delivering the education programming which focus on creating safe space for the children which is bully, discrimination, violence free and creating accepting environment in the class, in addition to assistive teaching tools that considers the disability, disability_GBV sensitive wash facilities, Feedback mechanisms boxes and other tools have been placed to be in the reach of disabled children. choosing teh class or the place of the children in the class in away that serve their access to better participation. NGO has a strong MEAL system that built on regularly collecting, analyzing and reporting of data. And consists of dedicated component of accountability and quality control of data. 3 - Needs

An estimated 6.9 million people (97% children),1.1M(15%) were PwD, need humanitarian education assistance according to HNO2021. Education services, both formal and non-formal remain underfunded and operating over capacity, with teachers receiving limited support to effectively engage children with trauma and those who have been out of school for multiple years. different barriers continue affecting enrollment of children into quality education in NES. Conflict impact, deteriorating economic situation, displacement, COVID-19 interruptions has negatively impacted the systematic delivery of education services to children. Increasing the school dropout numbers of children. And leaving another number of children who left the school behind, facing uncertain future. About five million children have been born in Syria since the conflict began ten years ago. This means that more than one in five babies born last year spent their earliest moments in communities affected by the chaos of conflict, often in deeply unsafe, and highly stressful environments. Experiences of severe, prolonged stress and psychosocial deprivation affect the child and the effect can extend to subsequent generations and the broader community through biological, behavioral, and socioeconomic processes, leading to the intergenerational transmission of adversity, disadvantage and violence, and the reinforcement of inequities. This threatens the future peace, social cohesion, and stability of society in NES.

4 - Implementation of activities

NGO will deliver education activities including ECD ones in Raqqa and Hassakeh governorates in a fixed center for early childhood and two mobile centers in Qamishli villages, which will include non-formal Leaning, ALP, SLP considering specific approaches to respond to the needs of vulnerable children including but not limited to Child labor, children with disability. By providing formal education in non- informal framework in 4 educational centers, and through a mobile team that provides education in informal camps ,Besides rehabilitation of schools and its WASH facilities, distribution of teaching and learning supplies, training of schools’ personnel and their management. Furthermore, NGO will conduct ECD activities that target both children and caregivers, and establlish ECD Committees. NGO will consider multiple modalities in implementaton : static programming allows for sustained delivery of education services, Mobile teams permit NGO to flexibly respond to the needs of children lack access to centers due to the far distance or lack of the education facilities in the location. NGO has a strong MEAL system which regularly will collect, analyze and report the data. And will consist of accountability and quality control of data. In addition, NGO will establish trackers that will be used for tracking the data of outcomes of the education program delivery to the clients. through the use of multiple trackers. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk; children being exposed to child protection concerns while trying access the non-formal education. Especially in informal camps, where children are forced to travel long distances of up to 3 km to reach the nearest village where there is a school. Children may be subjected to bullying and assault by older children, in addition to road accidents. Likelihood; High impact, High mitigation: Ashti formed mobile team that goes to the children tents to provide ALP, SLPs. . Risk: Barrier to beneficiaries’ access of education services due to physical impediments, Unsafe access to schools pushes a lot of children to dropout, especially girls, who are not allowed to socialize in schools, especially in bathrooms. Likelihood: Medium impact, High mitigation: rehabilitation of the schools to be more inclusive of people with disability and GBV sensitive WASH facilities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The Major risk: Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls clients by staff and field facilitators. Likelihood: Medium/Low Impact: Very High Mitigation: information materials to the clients demonstrating how the reporting channels are functioning within the NGO regarding PSEA and ASH. in addition NGO had a strong internal code of conduct in place that clearly related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding the clients, specifically the children. and firmly shows the Zero Tolerance policy for any sexual misconduct. furthermore the NGO and through its reporting mechanism allow for anonymous reporting any misconduct to ensure all possible violations of the policy are reported by staff and clients.Educating beneficiaries of the available reporting mechanisms while respecting the privacy and confidentiality of reporting, with trainings for the staff on special policies to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Change of context and limited access to the supported sites. Due to ban cases between cities and regions, the team may not be able to move around or work in new locations other than the ones they worked on in previous years Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: activate the links established throughout the program lifetime with the stakeholders and start negotiation through the NGO Access team. All NGO staff are from the targeted community to ensure the continuity of the program during the access limitation Risk: Security tensions and COVID-19 lockdowns that will restrict the access of the clients to the activities conducted in the learning centers or lock the movement of the teachers who deliver the SLP. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: NGO will depend on revising the modalities where possible benefiting from the successful experience of remote delivery of the program. Ngo will work through the community-based structures the NGO established. With Access team continue the assessment of the situations and inform the program delivery.In addition to some volunteers who provide support for children in between periods of visits to students.
