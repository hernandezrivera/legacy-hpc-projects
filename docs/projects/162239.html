Project Summary

The project aims to “Ensure quality inclusive education in a protective learning environment for X girls and boys in the Mopti, Timbuktu and Gao regions”.
Output 1.1: Schools’ capacity in host communities has increased enough to absorb displaced girls and boys and students have necessary school materials to adequately attend schools
The project will respond to the educational needs of all children through equitable access to inclusive quality education. The project will contribute to enhancing the quality of educational supply and expanding existing structures to better address the strong demand in the IDP, refugee and returnee hosting areas in central and northern Mali, considering the specific needs of girls and children with disabilities, the aspects related to protection and the retention of girls.
The protection of IDPs, refugees and returnees especially girls as well as vulnerable children and those with basic needs who do not have or no longer have adequate access to quality education in a secured environment, remains an utmost necessity in the Sahel region. In recent years, UNHCR has dedicated itself and will continue to take appropriate action to address child protection needs through education by facilitating the integration of IDP, refugee and returnee children into the national education system as outlined in the UNHCR Strategy, “Refugee Education 2030: A Strategy for Refugee Inclusion”, which was developed after two years of consultation and collaboration with UNHCR stakeholders, United Nations, NGOs, education networks, multilateral organizations and governments. The aim is to ensure the equitable and sustainable inclusion in national education systems for IDPs, refugees and returnees; that IDP, refugee and returnee, and host community students are equitably prepared for success in national systems, no matter where they live; and that the specific learning needs of displaced and host community students are met by expanding existing programs and partner investments in innovative local solutions.
Further, the UNHCR strategy sets out a vision for the inclusion of displaced children in a fair and quality education system, which contributes to their resilience and prepares them with youth to participate in social cohesion. It further aims at fostering conditions, partnerships, collaboration and approaches bringing IDPs, refugees and returnees in these communities to fairly and equitably have access to an education enabling them to learn, flourish and develop their potential.
Output 1.2: Primary and post – primary school environment is safe for girls and boys
In collaboration with the Mopti, Timbuktu and Gao Education Offices and the Ministry of Education/Teaching Resource Centres (CAPs), teachers and education personnel will receive training on inclusive education, psychosocial support (PSS), special needs education, peace education and Education in Emergencies (EiE), and consequently monitored on their pedagogical practices within classrooms. They will be supported by educational advisers to improve their learning processes. Moreover, efforts will be also focused on awareness raising and community mobilization for the reopening of schools in these zones.

Their capacity to protect themselves and their community from the risk of explosive ordnances and weapons will be also enhanced.
Latrines with water points will be rehabilitated and made accessible. Special attention will be paid to girls in terms of hygiene and sanitation. In a more suitable WASH environment, we observed that pupils remain at school by improving their health condition. Especially girls can attend school even during their menstruation period and children with disabilities can be enrolled as they simply have access to the facilities.
