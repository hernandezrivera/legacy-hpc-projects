Project Summary

The Provision of Integrated Life-saving WASH Services project aims to provide essential Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services to vulnerable populations in South Sudan's Aweil East, Aweil South, Aweil North, Bor South, and Pibor Counties. It focuses on addressing the dire humanitarian needs of displaced populations and host communities, with the overarching goal of reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality. The project also seeks to improve access to safe water and sanitation while decreasing water-related Gender-Based Violence (GBV) in flood-prone, drought-affected, and conflict-impacted Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South, Bor South, and Pibor Counties directly targeting an estimated 18,500 (3,700 Women: 5,606 Girls: 3,345Men and 5,550 Boys) including persons with disabilities.
The project will take a multifaceted approach, rehabilitating broken boreholes and solar-powered mini water yards, constructing new solar-powered water yards at nutrition centers, and providing household water treatment options. Hygiene kits, which include soap, buckets, aqua tabs, and dignity kits for menstrual hygiene management, will be distributed. Mass hygiene awareness campaigns and water quality analysis will be conducted, alongside the building of flood-resistant water points and latrines to mitigate environmental risks and ensure sustainability.
A needs assessment identified several challenges, including inadequate access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities. Vulnerable populations, particularly women and girls, face issues such as open defecation, contamination of water sources, and a lack of healthcare facilities, exacerbating health risks like diarrhea, typhoid, and GBV. In response, the project will address WASH-related health threats and mitigate GBV risks, especially those associated with accessing water and firewood. High levels of physical and sexual violence were noted, particularly among women and girls.
The project activities include the rehabilitation of 29 boreholes with climate-resilient structures, the construction of 1 new solar-powered water yards, and the rehabilitation of 1 existing ones. It will also conduct 120 hygiene campaigns across the target areas, distribute 18,000 bars of soap and other hygiene items, and provide dignity kits to 750 girls and women. Furthermore, the project will strengthen GBV referral pathways and conduct safety audits.
Protection and gender considerations are a priority in the project design. Gender-responsive services will be provided with a focus on meeting the specific needs of women, girls, persons with disabilities, and the elderly. While there is a low likelihood of direct protection risks, issues such as GBV, explosive hazards, and exclusion from services are of high concern. The project will mainstream Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to ensure accountability and prevent exploitation.
Expected outcomes include reduced WASH-related morbidity and mortality through integrated programming in Nutrition Centres/OTP sites, improved access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities, especially for vulnerable populations, enhanced community resilience to environmental hazards like floods and droughts that affect access to WASH services, and a reduction in WASH-related diseases and GBV by improving and increasing access to safe water, WASH-related services and creating safer environments for women and girls. The project also supports climate change mitigation by building resilient WASH infrastructures.
This comprehensive life-saving WASH intervention is designed in line with the WASH cluster strategies and 2025 HRP guidelines to address both immediate WASH needs and build long-term resilience in South Sudan's most vulnerable communities, improving overall health outcomes and safety.

1. Needs

The impact of the Sudan crisis resulted in the arrival of vulnerable populations that require assistance, increasing market costs and further stretching people’s coping capacities. People in South Sudan suffer the consequences of development deficit characterized by chronic poverty, lack of livelihoods and limited access to basic social services. South Sudan is one the Countries badly affected by climate change. Dry spell and flooding contribute to people’s food insecurity situation (Humanitarian needs and response plan 2024).In South Sudan, only 10% have access to improved sanitation, about 60% of people practice open defecation, risking contamination of water sources and 58% of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the last 12 months(Ibid and HNRP, 2024). The IRNA Quick Needs Assessment (May 24-28, 2024) reports 7,000 returnees in Northern Bahr El State due to Sudan's conflict. Aweil East County recorded 6,920 households of IDPs. These vulnerable people reported suffering from access to safe water, severe hunger, acute malnutrition, poor healthcare, inadequate sanitation, and rampant GBV and discrimination that require multi-sectoral life-saving services for a total of 11,383 households (RRC May 2024) in NBEG. The Protection Cluster’s Rapid Joint assessment (Feb 13-20, 2024) highlights increased cases of sexual abuse as women and young girls who fetch water and firewood are faced with risks while accessing these services and resources. The IRNA Assessment (Dec 2023) identified, skin infections, diarrhea, urinary tract and eye infections are high yet health facilities are understaffed, referral systems are poor and WASH services are limited. Aweil East ranks second in GBV cases after Aweil South; women and girls face physical, emotional, and sexual violence from traumatized men yet they lack GBV prevention, response, awareness, psychosocial support, dignity kits, and referral systems. Women and girls remain at risk of gender-based violence (GBV) as they try to undertake their daily activities. Violence, displacement and high levels of deprivation is felt in some parts of the country. (HNRP, 2024). A national prevalence study on violence against women and men conducted in 2022 revealed that . There is an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation and scale-up of response services in 25 priority counties.According to LM assessment (Dec 2023), 61% of respondents in Aweil East practice open defecation, 33.7% use water points susceptible to contamination (rivers, streams, hafirs, ponds, unprotected wells); 56% of respondents suffered from diarrhea in the last month, 58% of respondents suffered from typhoid in the last month; 71% of female adults and 19% of females under 18 years are responsible for collecting household water compared to only 3% of male adults and 7% of males under 18 years; 26.3% of respondents have a waiting time of 1-3 hours; 4.3% wait between 30-60 minutes; 30.7% wait between 10-30 minutes during peak hours of fetching water and there is high congestion due to densely populated areas and few protected water points and only 28% of respondents treat their drinking water.In South Sudan, the communities suffer from limited safe water, hygiene, and sanitation, with returnees sleeping and defecating in open areas, heightening waterborne diseases. Community engagements confirmed all above. There is a need to minimize these effects and improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable people.

2. Project activities

The project activities includes a comprehensive set of activities aimed at rehabilitating existing infrastructure, constructing new facilities, and strengthening community engagement and capacity for water management. Key project activities: • Rehabilitate 29 broken boreholes in the targeted counties, including 15 boreholes equipped with climate-resilient structures to enhance long-term sustainability. These boreholes will be located in IDP centers, host communities, and nutrition centers to ensure access to safe water. • Rehabilitate 1 broken down solar-powered mini water yards will be rehabilitated, improving the capacity to provide clean water to communities, particularly in flood-prone and drought-affected areas. • Construct 1 new solar-powered water yards at nutrition centers, Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) sites, and health facilities to improve access to clean water for both the general population and patients receiving nutrition and health services. • Carry out water quality analysis for all 31 water points, ensuring that the water being provided meets safety standards and reduces the risks of waterborne diseases. • Train 31 Water management committees, each consisting of eight members, will be established at the 85 rehabilitated and newly constructed water points. Training will empower these committees to manage the water points effectively and ensure sustainable water provision. • Produce and disseminate 250 Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials across the target counties. These materials will focus on promoting hygiene practices and raising awareness about the importance of safe water, sanitation, and GBV prevention. • Conduct 60 radio hygiene and sanitation campaigns in the 5 Counties. 1 talk show per County per month and run hygiene jingles on local radios in the five targeted counties. This will help reach a wide audience with important messages on water, hygiene, and sanitation. • Carry out 120 hygiene and sanitation awareness sessions across the target areas, with 24 sessions in each county (2 sessions per month). These campaigns will engage communities and promote healthy practices. • Procure and distribution soap to 6,000 vulnerable individuals over six months. Each person will receive 400g of soap each month, resulting in a total of 18,000 bars of soap or 720 boxes. This distribution will support better hygiene practices and help prevent the spread of diseases. • Distribute water storage and treatment materials (1,000 jerrycans and 1,000 buckets) with lids to households in the target areas. Additionally, 12,000 aqua tabs (2 strips per household per day for six months) will be provided to 1,000 households, supporting water purification at the household level. • Rehabilitate 10 latrine stances in nutrition facilities/OTP sites, and construct 10 emergency latrines at sites identified by the cluster. This will ensure safe and hygienic sanitation facilities for the beneficiaries, particularly in high-risk areas. • Conduct safety audit and gender sensitivity survey and analysis at all 31 water points and 15 latrines to identify and address potential risks, such as GBV and exclusion of certain groups, ensuring that water points are safe and inclusive for all members of the community. • Distribute dignity kits for menstrual hygiene management kits to 500 adolescent school girls and 250 young women. These kits will include items to support menstrual hygiene management and improve the dignity and health of girls and women in the targeted areas. • Strengthen GBV Referral Pathways in the targeted locations, ensuring that survivors of GBV have access to the appropriate support services. Tailored referrals will be made for each location, promoting a coordinated and effective response to GBV.
