Project Summary

This intervention aims to link to the strategic objectives of the HRP 2020 by providing a life-saving humanitarian intervention through an integrated health, nutrition and WASH project for displaces population, their host communities, returnees and other vulnerable groups in West Darfur, West Kordofan and South Kordofan states. The intervention will work with the existing health/nutrition and WASH systems in the targeted locations, and will improve the available resources (human capacity, infrastructure) to be able to deliver min. standard for the most in need. Moreover, the project will improve the early warning capacities of the health/nutrition and WASH systems so as to reduce the risk of possible outbreaks, and increase the preparedness.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 draft report, health service providing complete health packages are lacking in Sudan. Currently only 33% of the health facilities offer the complex basic PHC package including maternal and reproductive health care services, nutrition services, immunization, and free medicines. Only 51% of the women received the minimum 4 anti-natal care visit during pregnancy across Sudan, lowest in West Kordofan state (41%). Vaccination coverage remains below standard, EPI is approx. 60% . Measles outbreaks and cholera outbreak indicated the clear gap of EWRS in place, with adequate health staff to provide support. Medicines prices increased with more than 100% resulted significant gap in the medical pipeline system. There is a need to strengthen quality, access and coverage of PHC and reproductive health and emergency care, referral services in both rural and urban settings among IDPs, returnees, nomads and host communities. Approx. 20% of the pregnant women experience complication and need emergency obstacle care and referral services. Case management of SGBV services across Darfur states is very low, and in the HFs don’t have adequate PPS services besides the clinical response. Rural health facilities lack infrastructure development to perform PHC services, with an integrated hygiene improvement (malaria and water born disease is still critical in the country). According to S3M (2018) report, GAM rate in Sudan u5 14.1% (approx. 2.4M children) with aggregated factors e.g. cholera outbreak, malaria, and flooding, this figure remains critical in 2020. Concern would like to focus on the following geographic locations because GAM rate is high: - West Darfur state (GAM rate 8.4) critical locations Jabel Moon (10.5), Kulbus (12.6), and El Geneina (9.2) localities. Target: 11 HFs/OTP in the three locations (Jabel Moon, Kulbus and El Geneina hospital and catchment HFs/OTPs. - West Kordofan (GAM rate 12.7) in El Nuhud, Ghebesh. Al Debab, and Al Meriam localities (13. ), Wad Banda (19.8!) is critical high. Target: 6 HF/OTPs in 5 localities. El Nuhud, El Ghebesh and El Meriam hospitals and catchment facilities- South Kordofan (GAM rate 9.5) Tadamon and Abbassiya has over the critical 15.0. Target: 12 HF/OTPs in 4 localities. Micro-nutrient deficiency among children and their mothers is common, including iron-deficiency, anemia. Stunting is high 36.5% in Sudan with an additional poor hygiene practices, lack of potable water contributes infections that contributes to malnutrition. There is a very weak nutrition monitoring system in West and South Kordofan, that contributes to high malnutrition case, so it is important to strengthen the mobile nutrition services linking with existing nutrition and stabilization centers. Linking nutrition services with other sectors e.g. health, WASH and food security is critical to eliminate the root causes of malnutrition that links with poor water and hygiene practices, lack of food diversity and basic health care. In Concern's operation areas, institution WASH is one of the most critical needs besides health and nutrition support. The health and nutrition facilities don't have basic, potable water therefore the life-saving service delivery is not functioning: old, non functional pipe system, unclean facilities as a lack of water is an undermining problem of quality service deliveries. Hygiene promotion knowledge and practices, even among the health/nutrition staff in the facilities are weak, required strengthening with a sustainable institutional WASH system improvement. Many of the rural health/nutrition facilities lack adequate sanitation services: old, non functional latrines, non-segregation is a critical problem. Community/health/nutrition facility staff education is important about hygiene promotion to improve the situation.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During the project design and implementation climate change adaptation approaches will be considered particularly for the renovation of health/nutrition facilities, and the water source renovation and management. During the technical assessment and technical design of the renovation work, the reduction of water waste will be one of the important elements to consider. During the renovation of health/nutrition centres, solar system and related innovative solutions will be considered. Nutrition and health activities will also consider the environment by using environmentally manageable materials for building renovation, and waste management (debris) will be organized during and after the renovation. However the project is not directly impacted by the environment, during the health and nutrition capacity building training environment protection particularly related to medical waste management (storage and incinerating medical waste) will be considered, and a daily waste disposal will be organized around the health and nutrition facilities.Other aspects related to designing and constructing HH latrines will use locally available, natural resources to reduce the negative impact on ecological footprint.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups( IDPs, refugees) within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. - The affected population, who live very far from the health centers will be in focus. Mothers who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way.- Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers in the localities plays an important role in health, with only 43% of the affected population seeks medical treatment at facility level when their children got sick. While 11% visited traditional healers, exposing their children to harmful practices, such as children's removal of uvula using unhygienic materials, a very common practice in the targeted localities and South Kordofan in general. These issues will be addressed via behaviour change activities outlined in this project.- Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be identified and they will be responsible for dealing with sensitive issues e.g. GBV cases and related protection issues. - Mobile services (nutrition/health) will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly female) for dealing with sensitive issues - Children will be in high focus during the field activities by the field staff and they will also report any protection related case through the child protection reporting mechanisms, and available referrals - Other issues e.g. youth (children) and refugee youth migrating from the targeted localities to other locations due to livelihoods opportunities will be a central focus for the IGA and related activities to encourage them to stay in the region. - Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services).- In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.- During renovation of health/nutrition facilities, project staff will inform beneficiaries, parti

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

This project will contribute to the HRP 2020 health/nutrition WASH project with a special focus on IDPs, returnees, pastoralist groups and host communities, therefore, the Accountability Mechanisms will be developed as a joint exercise with the beneficiaries. The intervention will build on MoH strategic partnership in South/West Kordofan and WDarfur, to ensure that Concern meets PHC minimum standards. Concern is a member of HAP, hence, the principles and values of humanitarian accountability will be applied over the course of the project. Given the project is developed to fill the gap in the services provided previous interventions, and it will extensively focus on the refugees needs. This will be clearly communicated to all segments of the targeted population. The selection of the beneficiaries for particular activities will be carried out by the community management committees representing the different segments of the targeted communities based on set criteria that will be mentored by CWW and its national partner NGO. Applying the "'Do No Harm"" concept, services between the different kind of groups e.g., IDPs, returnees and host communities, will be carefully distributed as per their population proportion in the community and level of vulnerability. Concern has an updated Community Response Mechanism (CRM) guidelines which has been developed in consultation with community members. There will be various CRM tools applied for the intervention e.g. feedback boxes, focus group discussions, one-to-one discussions/meetings and record on feedback will be documented and responded. Concern, the affected populations, MoH, the national partner NGO, MSGs and community health and MSG, will select two community members, including one female, to represent their community and to be part of CRM committee to participate in the opening of the CRM boxes, complaint analysis and giving feedback to the community for the relevant complaints. During the project implementation, focus group discussion, informal/formal meetings and discussion will be held with various groups with a special focus on refugee groups, and their host communities, MSGs, youth group to have their feedback about the intervention. As much as possible Concern field team will use the feedback to build on the next step in the project implementation, and where it is not possible, it will be clearly communicated to the local community.
