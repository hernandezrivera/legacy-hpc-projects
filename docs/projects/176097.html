Project Summary

An estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis of December 2020, a 15 per cent increase compared to the 6.7 million people who were acutely food insecure in 2020. The number of counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is projected by the peak of the lean season (May to July) to increase to 45 as compared to 28 in 2019. Closely associated to the food insecurity, an estimated 1.9 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021, including 313,391 children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,078,867 children with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW) thus calling for urgent action to address some of the underlying course of food insecurity and malnutrition. Centre for Emergency and Development Support (CEDS) is proposing to carry out a 12 months food security project that will reach a total of 189,000 beneficiaries (40,824 males, 47,173 females and 101,003 children) in a total of 7 counties in Northern Barh El Ghazal, Lakes and Warrap states. The project targeted to contribute to reduced food insecurity related morbidity and mortality will be implemented in Aweil North county reaching XX beneficiaries, Aweil South county reaching 27,000 beneficiaries, Aweil East county reaching 27,000 beneficiaries, Tonj South county reaching 27,000 beneficiaries, Tonj North county reaching 27,000 beneficiaries, Rumbek East county reaching 27,000 beneficiaries, Cueibet county reaching 27,000 beneficiaries. As part of this project, CEDS will secure and distribute both wet and dry season seeds to selected beneficiaries, secure and distribute fishing kits to targeted beneficiaries, capacity build selected farmers on good agricultural practices and vegetable farming and selected fishermen of fishing and fish preservation techniques as well as kitchen gardening. As a result of this proposed project, the targeted households and individuals will have necessary skills and agricultural input to produce staple food and vegetables sufficient for their households consumption and enough to meet their households food and dietary needs. The targeted fishermen will also be able to harvest enough fish for household income and extra for sale. This activities are cumulatively expected to address some of the caused of food insecurity and malnutrition among the targeted households.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC, people’s acute food insecurity situation has been worsening over the past two years, from an estimated 6.35 million people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity during the 2019 lean season to 7.24 million people in the same conditions by the 2021 lean season. The states with the highest number of counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), projected at the peak of the 2021 lean season are Jonglei and Upper Nile. In the official IPC report issued by the government in December 2020, Akobo, Aweil South, Pibor and Tonj North counties have pockets of populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity at one or more periods between the end of 2020 and mid-2021. Differing to the IPC official release, the IPC Global Support Unit released two reports from the Famine Review Committee, classifying parts of Pibor County as ‘Famine Likely’ and indicating a likelihood of populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo, Aweil South, Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South. The number of displaced people living in settlements without access to a food market was highest in Jonglei, Lakes and Upper Nile; and returnees in Central Equatoria and Unity. More than half of IDPs and a third of returnees lived in settlements relying on food assistance, cash assistance or host community donations as their main source of food. The proportion of IDPs and returnees living in settlements relying on donations or assistance as their main source of food was highest in Unity, Jonglei and Northern Bahr El Ghazal. Urban areas and larger host-community settings fare better in terms of access to food markets for both IDPs and returnees. According to a key informant assessment, every other household does not have adequate access to food. Around 1.4 million South Sudanese children under age 5 and another 483,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. This is the highest number in three years and related to increased food insecurity and the decrease in coverage and uptake of nutrition services due to conflict, flooding and impacts of COVID-19. Lack of nutrition in young children results in negative impacts on their physical and mental wellbeing, affecting their educational outcomes and livelihood opportunities at a later stage. Malnourished people are more vulnerable to common and infectious diseases such as cholera, malaria, diarrhoea diseases, acute respiratory infection and measles. The proxy prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among under-five children ranges from 1.3 per cent to 33.4 per cent. In total, 47 counties out of 78 have a proxy prevalence of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent.

2 - Project activities

CEDS is planning to initiate main season and dry season agricultural activities, train lead farmers to support the targeted beneficiaries and support kitchen gardening at the OTP sites and at the household levels for mothers with children admitted at nutrition centers in the seven counties targeted. CEDS will procure and distribute main season agricultural seeds and tools to 625 HH in each of the targeted 4 counties which include Aweil South, Aweil East in NBeG state, Tonj North and Tonj South in Warrap state with a total of 2,500 HH targeted for the main season FSL activities. Each beneficiary will receive one piece of hoe and malloda, 7kg of Sorghum, 7kg groundnuts, 2.5kg of cowpeas, 25g of pumpkin, 50g of okra, 30g of water melon and 25g of eggplant seeds respectively. CEDS will also train a total of 100 lead farmers to support the beneficiaries on carrying out the main season agricultural activities.CEDS will target another 2,500HH with dry season vegetable production with 625 HH targeted in each of the 4 counties mentioned above. Each household will receive 25g of Amaranth, 25g of onion, 25g of tomatoes and 25g of kales seeds respectively. In addition to the seeds the beneficiary will be provided with watering cans. CEDS will initiate dry season vegetable production in groups of 25 around permanent water sources in the targeted villages. To support the groups in dry season vegetable farming, CEDS will train lead farmers, one for each group, to support the group members in vegetable production.CEDS will procure and distribute fishing kits to 267 HH beneficiaries per county with a total of 1066 HH targeted for fishing activities. Among the targeted beneficiaries CEDS will select and train 50 fishmongers in each county to support other group members.In order to equip mothers whose children are admitted at nutrition centers with skills on good agricultural practices, CEDS will set up kitchen gardens at nutrition centers that will serve as learning points for the mothers with the help of the lead farmers and the extension workers. The mothers will also be supported to set up kitchen gardens at their homes.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CEDS will during the information sharing sessions inform the beneficiaries about its policy of zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse and tell the beneficiaries how to report suspected cases of sexual exploitation or abuse. CEDS will also produce IEC materials on PSEA and distribute to the targeted beneficiaries. The project committees that will be formed will also be trained on PSEA and how to report the same. The committee will also have an individual tasked as the focal point for PSEA issues of the project implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEDS will make intentional efforts to ensure that different categories of the targeted beneficiaries are given a chance to air their views on how they ant the project implemented. CEDS will carry out community meetings with the different vulnerable groups such as the disabled, the elderly and women on their own to get their views about the project that will then be incorporated into the project implementation processes. CEDS will also have representatives of the different vulnerable groups in both the project implementation committees and so that their interests are represented in the project implementation processes.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEDS appreciates that it key to share information with the targeted beneficiaries for effective implementation of the project. CEDS will arrange community meetings where information about the project will shared. Additionally, CEDS will use community leaders to pass project information. Prior to distributions, CEDS will hold community meetings where beneficiaries will be told on their entitlements and how to give feedbacks in any case they are not satisfied with the distributions. CEDS will equally set up different feedback channels including but not limited to hotlines, feedback volunteers, suggestion boxes in order to provide the targeted beneficiaries opportunity to give feedback about the project. CEDS will also set up project committees to whom the beneficiaries can give their feedback about the project. CEDS AAP officer will will also hold feedback collection meetings with different groups and the feedback collected will be incorporated into the project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback that will be collected from the project through the various channels that will be set up will be channeled to AAP officer who will then sort and classify them according to their nature. The feedback will then be cascaded to the various levels of project management team. Those feedback that will need immediate response will be acted on as fast as possible and feedback shared with the respective beneficiaries who raised them. Sensitive feedback will be handled with the sensitivity that it requires. All in all the feedback that will be received during the project implementation will be used to better the project implementation process for instance distribution processes, beneficiary selection criteria among others will be modelled based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries. Feedback collected could also be used in the design of the new project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

There is a risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance. The likelihood of this risk occurring is high and the impact is high particularly in violence prone areas such as Tonj and Rumbek where the project will be implemented thus CEDS will mitigate this by ensuring that registration and distribution points are as close to the people as possible with each village having more than one registration and distribution point to minimize the distance to be covered by beneficiaries that exposes them to violent attacks.There is a risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The likelihood of this risk occurring is medium and the impact is high as these are the most vulnerable groups that the project targets to help thus CEDS will ensure that meetings are arranged that target only the specific age groups, gender and persons with disability to give them opportunities to make their views heard. Also, when registering beneficiaries, CEDS will allocate specific beneficiary numbers to these vulnerable groups to ensure that they directly benefit from the project and are not left out. There is an anticipated risk that beneficiaries will not be accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The likelihood of this risk occurring is high since the targeted beneficiaries do not know that their views count in a project they are benefiting from and the impact to the project is medium. In order to mitigate this, CEDS will continuously educate the beneficiaries on their roles and the benefit of them freely sharing their views through the various feedback channels that will be set up and let them know that it is okay for them to give feedback whether positive or negative at all stages of project implementation.There is a risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. The likelihood of this risk occurring is low but the impact is high. To mitigate this risk, CEDS will engage all the stakeholders and agree with them on the selection criteria before registration of the beneficiaries so that there is consensus on the selection criteria. CEDS will also hold community meetings prior to the registration to explain the selection criteria to be used giving opportunity to community members to agree on what selection criteria is to be used.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the two Collective Outcomes, i.e. Food insecurity and Gender-Based Violence?

Yes

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

CEDS understands that a proper state of mind and health are essential for a human being to be productive. And to achieve this among the targeted beneficiaries, CEDS will mainstream GBV activities together with the planned FSL activities to ensure that those beneficiaries that will need psychosocial support will be provided with the service. CEDS will also work with other partners in the area that are providing health, child protection and GBV support referring the cases that will be beyond its reach to make sure that women and children who are at risk are supported.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

CEDS plans to distribute farm tools, main and dry season crop seeds, fishing kits and also train farmers and fisher mongers on good agricultural practices and fishing techniques. In doing so, the targeted communities will be able to produce their own food and have increased access to diversified food and improved livelihood through sale of the farm produce resulting into decreased proportion of population facing IPC 3, 4 and 5. The counties targeted are currently facing IPC 3-5 and thus the planned project activities will help in reducing the proportion of the population facing the same in the country.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will be all inclusive without biasness on gender, age or disability status. Though the project only targets to initiate activities that will increase food production and improved livelihood through sale of excess agricultural and fishing produce, from the sale of the produce the targeted population will be able to purchase other cross-sectoral basic services. The project will also link the targeted beneficiaries with other partners in the area that are offering humanitarian services in other sectors such as WASH, health, GBV among others enabling them to receive cross-sectoral services. CEDS will also mainstream GBV in the project to ensure that GBV needs of the beneficiaries are catered for.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Majority of the beneficiaries that will be targeted by this project are mother with children admitted at OTPS and by equipping these mothers with agricultural inputs such as seeds and tools, the mothers will be able to produce diversified food to feed their children on thus reducing malnutrition cases among the under 5 in the targeted IPC 3 and 4 counties. Additionally, CEDS will be setting up kitchen gardens at nutrition centers and support mothers whose children are admitted at nutrition centers to set up their own kitchen gardens and thus producing food to feed their children on thus resulting into decreased cases of malnutrition in the targeted IPC 3 and 4 locations.By empowering the targeted beneficiaries to be able to produce their own food through the various agricultural activities that will be initiated, CEDS will helping to reduce future cases of malnutrition among the targeted beneficiaries. The agricultural skills that will be imparted among the targeted beneficiaries will enable them to produce food beyond the project period leading to long term reduction in the cases of malnutrition among the targeted IPC 3 and 4 locations.
