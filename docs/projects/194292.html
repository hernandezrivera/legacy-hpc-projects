Project Summary

The proposed World Vision International South Sudan's education intervention is aligned to the Humanitarian Strategic Objectives 2 & 3; the sectoral Objectives 1, 2 & 3, and falls directly under the two Strategic Objectives. Given WVI-SS's strong presence and the sector needs identified, World Vision proposes to implement the project in the counties of Baliet, Fashoda, Melut and Renk in Upper Nile; Tonj South, Tonj East, Tonj North in Warrap; Yambio, Nagero, Tambura in Western Equatoria; and Juba in Central Equatoria which are geographical areas affected by conflict displacement or facing acute food insecurity levels.
World Vision has education programs in partnership with ECHO, Education Can't Wait, HPP IrishAid, UNHCR, SSSHF, and Japanese Platform, and CEN. Through this intervention, WVISS will continue to give focus on emergency affected rural and urban areas. This will further consolidate on the efforts on the ground while reaching further and deeper into the areas with dire needs, in hard-to-reach area.
In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WVISS will ensure that through this project, 29,357 children will have access to safe and equitable quality education and 645 teachers and 715 PTA’s will be trained and supported with supplies and materials; 1800 youth will be provided life skills and vocational training with starter kits. Out of the 29,357 learners, the project will target about 500 children (200 boys, 300 girls) with disabilities. The learners will also be categorized under social/residential status as internally displaced, host community, or refugees during the actual enrolment in the project.
WVSS will also prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will be supportive and will enhance motivation of children to go to and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote their overall wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite Gender, disability or ethnic background.

1 - Needs

The effects of prolonged violent conflict, limited investment in the country’s infrastructure, natural disasters such as floods, have taken a heavy toll on availability of, and access to, basic education services. According to 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, about 5,862 schools were operating across the country, of which 560 were damaged due to flooding and 535 due to conflict. An additional 17 were being used as temporary shelters, disrupting access to education, and a further 1292 schools were closed for unspecified reasons. In 2022, 3.6 million people, are in need of education services (HNO, 2022). Of the rest of the schools that are operating, one in three reported having no access to a functioning safe water source and less than half have clean water and soap available. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities as well as the destruction or occupation of facilities during conflict were reported to limit access for children to schools. The main barriers to learning in the country include distance of schools from settlements and limited financial resources within communities to send their children to school. Girls face additional challenges, including cultural perceptions within communities that being educated would lead them to engage in criminal activities or risk becoming pregnant, which would in turn result in them fetching low dowries when they are married. Lack of and delayed teacher salaries and incentives result in teacher apathy, high turnover and poor-quality teaching. Teachers’ salaries in South Sudan have not been adjusted for inflation—salaries that were around $100 per month in 2011 are currently worth less than $5.According to UNICEF Needs Assessment (2021), less than half of learners that participated were able to access the national radio distance learning programs during the COVID-19 school closures (March 2020 – May 2021).WVSS proposes an aligned response to the Education cluster sectoral objectives 1(: Improve equitable access to safe education), 2 (: Improve the quality of inclusive education services) and 3 (Strengthen/enhance the education system); WVSS will implement EiE projects in the following counties where it has strong and operationally meaningful presence, and with very high needs, especially in education and other related humanitarian programmes: Central Equatoria state Juba (Rajaf, Mangala), Upper Nile (Malakal, Baliet, Fashoda, Melut and Renk), Western Equatoria (Yambio, Nzara, Tambura, Nagero), Warrap state in Greater Tonj (North, South and East) targeting populations affected by conflict or disaster in 2022. In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WVSS will ensure that through this project, 32,258 children (49% girls, 51% boys ) and 1800 youth have access to quality education and 645 teachers (390 male, 255female ) and 715 PTA’s will be trained and supported with supplies and materials. WVSS will also prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will support and enhance motivation of children to enroll and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote their overall wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite gender, disability or ethnic background. To enhance the localization agenda, WVSS through its partnership department, will enhance capacities of CSOs, CBOs and national organizations through collaborative implementation and knowledge sharing. World Vision is implementing projects with funding from ECHO, ECW, IrishAid, UNHCR, SSHF, and Japanese Platform but significant needs widely exist; these other resources will leverage on and complement towards consolidated efforts to meet the needs.

2 - Project activities

Proposed Activities. - Enroll children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers - Establish and/or rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms - Provide age 3-5 children with ECD kits - Establish and/or rehabilitate gender-segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion - Procure and supply sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to pupils/students and teachers. - Provide crisis affected children with quality education recreational materials. - Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents.- Train teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching - learning process for crisis affected children - Train and update teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms - Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents - Train teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition. - Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) - Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and disease prevention and mitigation measures - Organize conduct back-to-learning campaigns - Provide cash transfer for vulnerable students to retain then in school to completion (Cash for Education). - Provide cash transfers to volunteer teachers for training (Cash for Training) and incentives (Cash for Work) - Support the capacity of MoGEI on communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures- Contain the COVID-19 outbreak and mainstreaming interventions such as distance learning and the safe return to schools; - Awareness creation and key messaging on pandemics and diseases that arise in the course of 2022 including COVID-19.- Procure and distribute PPEs, relevant hygiene, and handwashing facilities to maintain school health practices and disease prevention.- Adapt relevant innovative learning platforms such as digital equipment to ensure learning continues despite interruptions by conflict or disaster for vulnerable communities. For example, can’t wait to learn digital platform to improve learning for disadvantaged children in hard-to-reach areas.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project is designed in a way that cash interventions are based on the actual context of the local markets. Cash will be used where markets are accessible, have the capacity to provide the needed quantities and qualities of commodities, and the operating conditions remain stable. World Vision will conduct regular market assessments to inform the decisions around the use of cash. The assessment will capture the functionality of market in the target areas and also consider the changing needs of the target population. More so, the effect of the use of cash on the local markets will be carefully assessed to make sure this modality doesn't do harm to the local economy.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

WV recommends the use of cash because using cash helps revive the local economy, promotes beneficiary dignity, and empowers the household to make choices that best suit them. Before embarking on the actual modality selection, WV will conduct a quick market assessment for actual modality recommendation. Moreover, WV is not recommending cash for all counties but for counties where the market is functioning well enough to support the right quantities and access to commodity/service needs of the affected populations. Currently, World Vision uses the cash modality in its operations using funds obtained from different donors; thus, WV has the experience and expertise to implement the modality.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During feedback collection sessions, the team will take deliberate steps to make sure the opinions of the vulnerable groups are recorded and their needs met. Other feedback mechanisms will also be made accessible to people with special needs, girls, and boys.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information sharing with the beneficiaries will be done through the channels that will be preferred by the community at the time. This could be through public announcements, radio messaging, open meetings (mindful of Covid-19), project management committee, PTAs, or through local authorities. Community mobilizers/facilitators will also help in information sharing at the community level

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Mass awareness will be done to the beneficiaries on PSEA and other protection concerns; this will include topics such as prevention, reporting of PSEA cases, and services related to psychological first aid

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be used to make specific decisions such as the choice of sites for erecting the TLSs, beneficiary selection, distribution protocol, meeting special needs of vulnerable groups and also to determine local preferences for teaching/learning materials.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The specific risks identified above will be addressed as follows:8.1: Registration and distribution points will be thoroughly assessed and located at the safest points in the village. Distributions will be done at appropriate times in the day to allow closure before the dark8.2: Teachers, girls and boys will be sensitized on protection concerns, prevention, and where to get psychological first aid in case of violations8.4: During training of teachers and PTAs, the participants will be sensitized and informed about the available GBV referral services and where they could get such services when such cases arise8.5: When doing assessments, information sharing, and awareness sessions, the team will ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project by directly involving them in the discussions. 8.6: The project will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are accessible to all members of the community through increasing access points, diversifying feedback channels, etc. Overall, the project will be based on a protection risk analysis considering the specific needs of different population groups. The project will do protection risk analysis by identifying various possible risks and ascertain their level of severity, likelihood and impact. Barriers to access for different groups will be identified and addressed. The team is mindful the barriers to access could be social, distance, or security concerns. These barriers will be identified and addressed as appropriate.
