Project Summary

The Logistics Cluster as a service-based cluster and provider of last resort aims to facilitate the timely delivery of humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations through its logistics coordination support, information management and provision of common services to the humanitarian community in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

Given the scale and complexity of the humanitarian response in South Sudan, millions remain displaced and in dire need of humanitarian assistance. As of mid-2019 nearly 6.4 million people or 54 per cent of the population were severely food insecure and are being targeted for humanitarian assistance. Even though the overall situation improved since the signature of the peace agreement in 2018, the country continues to face instability. The South Sudanese population still faces intersecting threats of violence and insecurity, large-scale population displacement, extreme food insecurity, declining economic conditions, infrastructural deficits, disease outbreaks and climatic shocks. South Sudan presents one of the most logistically challenging countries requiring a coordinated logistics response to ensure timely, effective and efficient delivery of relief items. Although the peaceful environment helped to improve physical access across the country, the rainy season which lasts from May to November renders over 60% of the country inaccessible. Consequently, road access to numerous areas of humanitarian intervention becomes impossible increasing the reliance on air and river transportation for half the year. Underdeveloped infrastructure, lack of road maintenance and long rainy season patterns in recent years have severely impacted the humanitarian response leaving major supply routes with acute humanitarian needs. Increased access in critical regions (Jonglei, Unity etc.) in 2019 has enabled humanitarian actors to use the dry season to pre-position relief items in strategic locations with the objective of ensuring an uninterrupted supply chain throughout the year. Efforts to increase pre-positioning and improve logistics and cost efficiencies of the overall humanitarian response are still to be made to ensure a decreased reliance on costly air operations.

2 - Activities

This project will facilitate the delivery of humanitarian assistance to affected persons by augmenting the capacities of the wider humanitarian community through logistics information management (IM), coordination, and common services, where necessary, to respond to the crisis. In terms of Information Management, the Logistics Cluster will support by collecting and sharing operational information including providing GIS support to partners, mapping physical access constraints as well as supply routes along primary road and river corridors. IM products will be made available on the Logistics Cluster website and shared via the dedicated mailing list. At the national level and in strategic field locations, the Logistics Cluster will hold regular coordination meetings to address logistics gaps and challenges. Coordination meetings serve as a platform for organisations to discuss the integration of their response plans, enhance partnerships and share crucial information affecting the wider humanitarian community. Other activities including inter-agency road convoy coordination will form part of the cluster's coordination mechanism and through priorities determined by the Inter Cluster Working Group (ICWG) support will be adapted and scaled up where necessary. The Logistics Cluster facilitates common logistics services, including storage and air, road, and river transport across South Sudan. The cluster will avail common warehousing space in its operational hubs and maintain a stock of Mobile Storage Units for deployment to augment storage capacities in deep-field locations. Based on the demand from its users, the Logistics Cluster will maintain a fleet of aircraft to transport cargo to remote areas inaccessible by road or river. As an alternative to air transport, the Logistics Cluster will also expand its river transportation services with regular barge trips between Bor and Renk and will utilize boats on the El Zharaf and Sobat Rivers. The cluster, in conjunction with IOM’s Common Transport Service project to truck supplies to locations surrounding Bentiu, Malakal, and Wau via the Beyond Responses, further reducing the reliance on air operations to locations reachable by road.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The Logistics Cluster project aims at ensuring the most efficient transport modalities are used when possible. Improving cost-efficiency by decreasing air operation is essential to establish a longer-term strategy in the South Sudan humanitarian response as funding is expected to decrease over the year. Additionally, reducing operational and logistical costs would ensure received funding can focus more on response directly targeting displaced-affected communities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The Logistics Cluster being a service based cluster provides common logistical services (cargo transport, coordination, information management and storage) to the humanitarian community depending on partners' demand. Consequently the cluster will tailor its response to ensure it is always aligned with national objectives.
