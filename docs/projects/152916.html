Project Summary

This project seeks to provide safe, protective and inclusive education to conflict affected boys and girl in South Sudan in alignment with HRP Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3 and Education Cluster Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3.
WVSS proposes to implement the project in the counties of Renk, Fashoda, Melut, Baliet (Upper Nile), Yambio, Nzara, Nagero (Western Equatoria), Rajaf (Juba), Tonj North, Tonj East and Tonj South (Warrap) which are geographical areas affected by conflict, displacement or facing acute food insecurity levels. WVSS continue to give focus to emergency affected rural and urban areas with significant IDPs presence. It is good to note that WVSS has presence in these locations and is able to deliver quality education programming.
In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WVSS will ensure that through this project, 21,000 girls and boys have access to quality education and 3,500 teachers and SMCS will be trained and supported with supplies and materials. WVSS will also prioritise establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will be supportive and will enhance motivation of children to go to and remain in school. In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote their overall well-being. All learners will be given equal access to education despite gender, disability or ethnic background.
WVSS will implement the education project in complementarity with other sectors: a) with WASH through connecting schools with safe water points and handwashing facilities, b) with Protection by providing safe learning spaces and psychosocial support, c) with Food, Health and Nutrition for children to access health, food and supported with referral pathways to health centers (e.g. for malnutrition). WVSS will work on contributing to the overall well-being, safety and protection of children and mitigating the impact of crisis to them.

Needs

Prior to the outbreak of the current civil war in December 2013, education indicators in South Sudan were already extremely low. According to the National Bureau of Statistics’ 2011 Statistical Yearbook, National Education Statistics 2016 and Ministry of Education’s 2013 Education and Management Information System (EMIS), 73% of men and 84% of women above 15 years of age were illiterate, with 3 out of 5 school-age children not enrolled in primary school, and the pupil-classroom ratio averaging 100 per teacher. According to Education Cluster HNO, the number of boys and girls in need represent a 67% increase from 2018. Plausible reasons include deterioration of systems and services whereby the resilience of households to cope with the economic downturn is seriously tested and collapses in the absence of support. Since the start of 2017, 95% of schools in Warrap closed temporarily and 75% of schools report having suffered an attack on education facilities. Up to 22,862 children (15,391 B; 7,471 G) were affected by the temporary closure of schools due to attacks on school infrastructures in Warrap. In Upper Nile, more than 50% of schools Nile were closed and in Western Equatoria, only 49% of schools were open. In UNS, 8000 learners (4000 B; 4000 G) were affected by the insurgency in Fashoda county in Sept 2017, which affected learning in Kodok, Abrouch and Oringa. Fighting and displacement have been the main drivers leading to the interruption of education and lack of protection of 3-18-year-old children. Poor school infrastructure coupled with dilapidated classrooms meant that over 63% of schools in Warrap, 47% of schools in WEQ and 70% of schools in Upper Nile conduct learning in open air . 17,213 children (9,799 B; 7,414 G) in Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, and Warrap states, the majority of whom were displaced by the conflict, are studying under trees, unprotected and in unsafe learning environments. Thus, school hours have had to be shortened even at the slightest change in weather patterns, greatly interrupting the learning and completion of the school, and impacting learning outcomes. Learning and reading materials are inadequate, with very few textbooks available to children, greatly hampering literacy and numeracy abilities, and skills acquisition. Over 50,000 assorted reading and learning materials are required to support the learning and teaching of school children. In a country where children represent 47.75% of the population, they receive the greatest and most immediate impact and long-term effects from crisis, violence and displacement. The interruption of education activities will have both short and long term consequences for the stability and development of South Sudan as children who are not in school will lack the structure and stimulation for healthy cognitive, social, and emotional development. The education crisis also risks fuelling new conflicts as an estimated 19,675 children are already recruited into armed groups. The longer children stay out of school, the more likely it is that they are recruited into armed groups. A recent needs assessment survey from the Education Cluster found that on average, schools lost 3-4 weeks of education in the latest academic year, and over 50%% of the assessed schools reported that this interruption was due to insecurity. Some 21 per cent of assessed schools were non-functional, with insecurity being the major cause of school closure. Of the assessed schools, 15 per cent reported having experienced an attack on the school, teachers or pupils and theft or looting by armed forces and groups. Deterioration in food security was also reported as one of the main reasons for children dropping out of or missing school, as families preferred to keep them at home to seek livelihoods. For those children that remained in school, the effects of chronic hunger affect their learning.

Activities

Objective 1: Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies Key Activities:• Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces and/or construction of TLSs where there is influx of new IDPs• Establishment or rehabilitation of wash facilities, including latrines (gender segregated), water points and provision of soap for handwashing, provision of dry chlorine• Emergency school meals in areas most affected by food insecurity Following the series of violence which destroyed many structures across the three states, there is a need to rehabilitate classrooms and schools. WVSS proposes to construct/rehabilitate 28 TLS and/or semi-permanent structures with temporary latrines and water and hand washing facilities (GUN: 8, WEQ: 11, Juba: 3, Warrap: 6). These learning spaces will also be provided with benches/desks/chairs and chalk boards. In areas most affected by food security, WVSS will provide food and water to 8,500 children in schools. Objective 2: Improve the quality of education to ensure continuity of relevant education services for children and youth in emergencies Key Activities:• Training of teachers • Provision of textbooks, learning and teaching materials• Provision of hygiene kits for girls to support their menstruation• Provision of school furniture and blackboards• Provision of recreational materials• Regular follow up and support through monitoring and supervision of learning activities in schools• Accelerated Learning Programme• Technical and Vocational Education Training (TVET) based on the youth’s education levels and labour assessments to identify existing and emerging livelihood opportunities WVSS proposes the distribution of the following supplies: student kits (GUN: 85, WEQ: 85, Juba: 60, Warrap:85); recreational kits (GUN: 85, WEQ: 85, Juba:60, Warrap: 85); ECD kits ((GUN: 85, WEQ: 85, Juba:60, Warrap: 85); school bags (GUN: 7300, WEQ: 5600, Juba: 2800, Warrap: 5,600); sanitary kits; teachers’ kits; and life skills and psychosocial support training materials as well as hygiene kits/sanitary to adolescent girl. Distribution of all supplies will be tracked by a detailed report outlining the distribution lists, pictures, location, date and quantity. WVSS also proposes to conduct life skills and peacebuilding training for teachers. The training will be based on life skills, conflict sensitive education, and peace education. Moreover, WVSS will train teachers and other stakeholders on courses in intensive English, curriculum delivery and basic pedagogy, life skills and classroom management.Objective 3: Strengthen the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth • Trainings of PTAs/SMCs on PSS, • Trainings of SMCs and county education officials on school management, record keeping and monitoring of education activities.• Creating school hygiene clubs to support messaging, provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines• Behavior change communication 60 caregivers/facilitators will be trained on classroom management, teachers’ code of conduct, lesson planning/scheme of work, teachers’ role in managing children with disabilities (CWD) and use of the ECD kit. The training package is intended to explain necessary skills to caregivers for ECD programming in emergencies and in transition contexts. This activity is projected to reach 24,500 beneficiaries (21,000 girls and boys and 3,500 women and men) who will be sensitized on the importance of education.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes.Gender disparity in education enrolment is a large issue, as 1 out of 5 adolescent girls in South Sudan is out school. More boys (29,832) than girls (24, 463) are enrolled in the 101 WV supported schools. It is estimated that only 7 girls for every 10 boys attend primary education in South Sudan. Parents and most of the communities still look at girls as a source of family wealth (bride price) where girls are forced to marry early, with unequal support given to girls compared to boys in areas of scholastic materials, limited awareness and negative perception on benefit of educating all children, and inadequate menstrual hygiene supplies to support girls to stay in school during menstrual cycles. Additionally, boys are also forced into early marriage and taking up family responsibilities impacting education enrolment and opportunities. Girls specifically must overcome many obstacles to enrol, stay and complete primary school education. Many adolescent girls chose not attend schools during menstrual periods. Out of school children have a particular profile: poor, rural, female and over-age. Thus, girls from rural and poor families are at greater risk dropping out of schools due to: inadequate knowledge in menstrual hygiene management, custom and local beliefs that hinders discussions on menstruation as a taboo, and inabilities to acquire basic hygienic supplies such as soap, sanitary kits/pads. Thus, this project will scale up awareness campaign on holistic and positive parenting (behaviour change on negative perceptions/attitude/practices), support girls with MHM kits, train teachers and ‘expert volunteer mothers’ to support adolescent girls in areas of MHM and ensure that all communities are empowered on giving girls and boys equal opportunities to education.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes.One of the main roles of any school supervision system is to monitor the quality of education, such as of the schools and teachers. This monitoring is expected to have a positive impact on quality. As such, supervision forms part of an overall quality monitoring and improvement system, which includes other devices such as examinations and achievement tests, and self-assessment practices by school and teachers. The project proposes a check list to be produced prior to every monitoring visit to the school. Those involved in this activity will be representatives from the payam, county, state education offices as well as the PTA. WVSS will support logistically and financially to facilitate monitoring and supervision visits at county and state levels. All the schools will be visited each term by this team, in addition to the routine visits conducted by the payam or WVSS staff. Additionally, in an effort to boost monitoring and supervision, WVSS will support a 7-member team led by the MoE to visit selected schools across all counties within the State. The project will procure 1 land cruiser hardtop (for Renk), 4 quad bikes (UNS: 2, Warrap: 2) and 2 motor bikes (for WEQ) to boost monitoring and supervision.

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes.Safety Audits are completed for each project site and/or service point, with recommendations used to informed the continued use of the location and any adaptions that are required to improve the safety of the site / service point (or reaching this location). Safety Audits are completed prior to establishment of the project site or service point (ex. CFS/Help Desk) and repeated quarterly or ad hoc, for example in the instance of population displacement or significant event that may be perceived to change local dynamics. Safety Audits are complemented by Safety Perception mapping, which captures the safety issues or unsafe areas perceived by beneficiaries, which may be different from observation; this is completed quarterly or ad hoc as necessary and used comparatively to inform project implementation and any adaptations as necessary. Safety perception mapping using specific participatory child-friendly, age-appropriate tools to map and seek feedback from girls and boys.
