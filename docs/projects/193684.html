Project Summary

• The project aims at improving equitable access to quality lifesaving curative and preventive services through the provision of primary health care and nutrition interventions in Al Salam, Belil, Gereida and Dimso localities in South Darfur, Yassin, Adilla, Abukarinka, Abujabra and Aedein localities in East Darfur, Bahri, Khartoum and Omdurman localities in Khartoum, and Wad Madani, Alkamleen,Hasahisa localities in Al Jazeera with focus on vulnerable groups including IDPs and people in hotspots resulting from ongoing clashes between the two armies. The project target to reach out 535,284 beneficiaries of whom 23% are women, 28% girls, 22% men and 27%boys while 0.02% of total reach are expected to be persons living with disability.
• Through this project, Alight will provide primary health care through curative lifesaving services and preventive interventions, and this will be done by supporting 24 health facilities to deliver quality services plus mobile clinics in hotspots and new IPD gathering areas. To achieve this, Alight will improve infrastructure including with light rehabilitation and medical equipment with the aim of improving service provision environment. Alight will ensure supply chain of medicines, medical supplies and nutrition commodities in close coordination with other stakeholders including WHO, WFP, UNICEF and respective SMOHs. Primary health care package will include outpatient consultations, short stay for observation purpose, laboratory services, provision of medicines, reproductive health services including 6 BEmONC , EPI and minor surgery/dressing services. Referral system will be in place for cases beyond PHCCs levels, where funds allow, referred patients among the most vulnerable will be supported to access secondary care including transport for emergency cases. Alight will also support in management of trauma and through integrated approach will protection sector will provide MHPSS.
• Nutrition package will include early detection of malnutrition through mass MUAC screening, management of malnutrition cases as per CMAM guidelines including targeted supplementary feeding program, outpatient therapeutic feedings, managing 3 stabilization centers and where there no SC, caregivers are facilitated to access them.
• The project will use integrated response modalities between health, nutrition, WASH, protection and other sectors. The selection of areas of focus was based on localities ranked with severity level 4 and 5 as per HNO 2023. Alight plans to prioritize the most vulnerable including IDPs, vulnerable residents in isolated areas where there are no other health and nutrition services. In addition, Alight plans to provide services through existing health and nutrition facilities and where the need arise, use approach of mobile or satellite clinic. Prioritization will be based on vulnerability and available resources; Alight will prioritize the youngest for both health and nutrition while focusing at provision of MISP for reproductive health if any emergency. Also, the poorest will be prioritized and those at high risk of mortality such as children with acute malnutrition with or without medical complications.
• Expected risk include the political situation which is flagile, continued clashes between RSF and SAF, intercommunical conflict which affect access , high inflation rate, inavailability of supplies and rain. Also, targetted areas maybe affected by outbreak, high cases of malnutrition resulting from food insecurity, lack of water, poor sanitation, lack of surveillance,. Alight will continue to monitor the situation, where applicable prepositioning of supplies will be adopted to ensure continuity of services. Alight will participate in different coordination mechanism including health and nutrition cluster to remain updated and ensure coordination efforts in line with sector strategies.

1 - Needs

Health: As per the draft of HRP 2023 health sector, Sudan's deteriorating economy and protracted health crisis have increased the number of people without access to basic health services and weakened the health system's ability to provide the population with the basic health care they need. In addition, brain drain continuously affects the ability of the health system to provide needed health services. People living in host communities, IDP camps, and refugee camps still rely on humanitarian agencies to provide basic medical services and support the communities with needed medical supplies. Providing basic health services to affected populations is essential to addressing high maternal and child mortality. Further, improving declining immunization coverage in conflict areas is critical to preventing recurrence and reducing the impact of vaccine-preventable outbreaks such as measles. Ensuring the availability of essential medicines is a priority. This is especially true in remote areas and regions that suffer from annual floods and droughts, exacerbating food insecurity and further deteriorating health conditions.low access to safe drinking water, suboptimal vaccination coverage, inter-communal conflict, and lack of the infrastructure to mitigate the effects of seasonal floods are existing hazards contributing to the resurgence of vaccine preventable, water-borne, and vector borne disease outbreaks including malaria and arthropod-borne viral diseases Nutrition:As per the draft of HRP 2023 nutrition sector, the humanitarian nutritional needs in Sudan are projected to remain at elevated high levels or worsen further in 2023, making Sudan one among the top countries in the World with highest estimated acute malnutrition caseload. While the overall Global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at 13.6%, the prevalence of GAM varies significantly across the localities with very high situation (GAM above 15% and above as per WHO classification) reported in 64 localities. The projected worsening of the specific drivers of malnutrition including food insecurity, deterioration in provision of Health and WASH services, economic decline and suboptimal caring and feeding practices for under-five children are likely to contribute to deterioration of nutrition situation. Besides, funding constraints may cause temporary suspension of over 52% of the TSFP programmes. In addition, the 18% increase in number of localities in severity scale 4, will likely contribute worsening of acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies among the under-fives, pregnant and lactating women in Sudan. Given the high risks of mortality and morbidity associated with acute malnutrition in normal and humanitarian situation, the sector has developed four objectives, interventions and targets as summarized below guided by envisaged response capacity and funding requirements. Based on the sector analysis, a total about 4 million children under-five and Pregnant and Lactating women (PLWs) are estimated to be suffering from wasting of whom over 611,000 have severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 2.44 million have moderate acute malnourished (MAM). Acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women is also very high with over 936,000 cases in need of treatment. The sector is targeting about 2.38 million acutely malnourished children under-five, pregnant and lactating women representing about 60% of the total people in need. The GAM rates in localities being targeted by Alight are either above or very close to the WHO emergency threshold of 15 per cent with the highest rates recorded in the localities of Yassin (22.09%), Adilla (20.17%), Assalaya (19.99%) and Dimso (19.96%). Also, the survey revealed high prevalence of anemia in Abukarinka, Adilla, Al Salam, Assalaya, Beleil, and Gereida localities. Alight plans target for 2023 is 122,778 people which represents 5.6% of the cluster target.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ALIGHT will continue using environmental-friendly approaches such as use of solarenergy instead of fossil fuels to power its health facilities. ALIGHT uses local materialsfor construction such as grass and bamboo sticks. These are easily renewable andhas less environmental impact. Health facilities will produce medical waste including infectious waste and sharps. To minimize the impact on environment, Alight will ensure segregation of waste, collection and disposal as per MOH and WHO guidelines. Alight will coordinate among its health facilities and SMOH in respective areas for possible exchange or donation of drugs with short shelf -life to minimize expired drugs to be disposed. For expired drugs, Alight will engage SMOH for appropriate disposal. As nutrition supplies are packed in plastic sacs , to minimize their effect on environment, Alight will engage care givers to return empty sacs after consumption and collected sacs will be incinerated.

ALIGHTprovides special consideration to making its services (Livelihood, WASH, Health andNutrition services) accessible to everyone as well as persons with disabilities andpeople with specific needs and ensure their accessibility across the age categories andvulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for pregnant women/ new mothers andinfants, older, women headed families, widow’s orphans and severely ill and peoplewith disability. ALIGHT has a dedicated protection team to work with all thematicsectors to strengthen the capacity of staff in protection mainstreaming but not limitedto child protection, older persons, and people with disability, GBV prevention andpsychosocial support. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ALIGHT is focused on implementing systems and methods that reveals customerexperience of organizational services so that it can align a response and re-establishtrust between ALIGHT, refugees, IDPs and the host communities. Trust works in bothdirections and must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability.ALIGHT commitment to accountability to the affected population will focus on thefollowing; - governance, transparency, feedback, participation, design, monitoring andevaluation. ALIGHT will work with community structures in increasing the role of thecommunity participation in maintaining the services. In addition, through theestablished community feedback and response mechanism, ALIGHT will continue tofacilitate community consultation meetings to ensure active community participation,transparency and handling community feedback. The community members andbeneficiaries provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities provided byALIGHT and other development partners in their communities in a manner that is safe,non-threatening and accessible to improve accountability across ALIGHT operationalareas. ALIGHT’s Feedback/Complaint Response Mechanism (F/CRM) provides aformal channel through which ALIGHT beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can registervoices and complaints. The F/CRM is centrally operated through feedback forms anda toll-free hotline number from all mobile phone networks companies (Sudani, Zainand MTN). ALIGHT has developed awareness materials including banners and flyersplaced at ALIGHT project service delivery points such as health centers, water pointsetc. with the hotline number and explanations on how the FCRM works as well asinformation about our accountability standards and practices to which customersshould hold ALIGHT accountable. In addition, through the MEAL department, ALIGHTconducts regular customer interviews and post distribution monitoring using standardsurvey feedback forms. As per ALIGHT procedures, the analysis of feedbackinformation from communities is fed directly to senior management for action. Thesemechanisms have proven to be an effective tool in providing beneficiaries andcommunities with a voice and ultimately improving ALIGHT operations and identifyingmisconduct within the organization. A well-structured FCRM also enhances lessonslearned and good practices across our network.ALIGHT is a signatory to the JointFramework for Action on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.
