Project Summary

The project intervention area will be Alroug Plains location. HIHFAD has conducted
rehabilitation of the irrigation system (wells and irrigation channels) in this location of Al
Roug plain and we established association to manage the water usage in the area in 2018.
HIHFAD has good relationship with the local councils and stakeholders. The average household (HH)
size in Syria is 6 members according to the most recent assessments. HIHFAD
will target the big HHs, which includes more than 6 members or HHs with disables or
elderly, also women headed households or households in which the women play an
important role in income generation.
The project is intending to raise their percentage of income from the crops production.
Also, HIHFAD will target the host communities who have small land size between 5 to 10
dunums, depending on the results of our assessment on land size owner in the targeted
areas.
This project will target 500 beneficiaries in the target location by supporting them with
vegetable inputs to cultivate 2 dunums. The modality of the response will use the voucher
system with 120 USD value for each beneficiary to cover the cost of seeds,
fertilizer and irrigation cost.

Needs

In 2018, a nation-wide, WoS coordinated needs assessment was conducted to complement sector-specific assessments. While sector assessments – where feasible- form the basis of the sector specific needs identification process, by ensuring needs are identified at a more granular level, the multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA) provides an overall representation of the situation and helps ensure inter-sectoral coherence.Overall, more than 95,000 interviews were conducted, of which 99 per cent comprised in-person face-to-face interviews. • According to HNO 2019, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion of the most vulnerable populations.Moreover, 2018 witnesses lack of rain during January and February and it was the worst drought in 30 years and affected the crops production as it is stated in the HNO "The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production during the 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with a shortfall of 1.2 million metric tonnes of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes"• There is an urgent need for multiple actions to provide food and livelihoods assistance through the most feasible modality to people in deed and at risk, as established by the sector.According to a needs assessment survey conducted by the MEAL team of HIHFAD in Al-Roug plain, 64% of the interviewed farmers are willing to plant their land this year if necessary assistance provided. Once vegetable/wheat production starts and harvest succeeds.

Implementation of activities

The criteria for the targeted HHs will be according to the most vulnerability and eligibility ones and the priority will be given to the big HHs and HHs with elderly and disables also women households.Each beneficiary will receive 3 vouchers with total value of 120 USD. This number has been determined according to our quick market assessment to the production costs for one Dunum from Spring-Summer crops production, which was about 60 USD for seeds, fertilizers pesticides, and tools, and irrigation (Fuel) costs. So, we will support each beneficiary with voucher enough for planting maximum 2 Dunums, then the vouchers value should be 120 USD.The total number of the beneficiaries which we intend to reach in this project will be 500 farmers (350 male farmers, 150 female farmers) and each farmer should be the head of the household which means 500 HHs will benefit from this project (3000 people), The accessibility to these areas is possible from our side in this area, and the field staff are already from the area, so they will not face any problems in accessibility to the targeted areas. 1- Distribution MethodHIHFAD will have our own field staff on this project and they will be the responsible for implementing this project from the beginning to the end, including monitoring and evaluation, so the M&E staff will be the responsible for monitoring the distribution process, and we will follow all HIHFAD MEAL department accountability mechanisms.In this project we will follow the same modality for vouchers distribution and redemption, and accordingly there will be one distribution point in each targeted village, and this point will be agreed in consultation with the local Council in this village.In general, we are using the signatory for distribution, and the ID or family card for cross check.There is no any possible risk for HIHFAD or NPA due to collecting the beneficiary’s data in this area.The distribution will be in the distribution points which will be determined in coordination between HIHFAD field staff and the local council in each targeted village, also HIHFAD has its complained disk which is already used in other project areas, so we will use the same mechanism in this project, and our MEAL team is the responsible for receiving the complains and dealing with them in the best ways depending on the complaint situation.The distribution will be in three rounds, the first one the seed voucher (Tomatoes, Cucumber, Eggplant and Pepper seed) will be in early March enough to plant maximum 2 two dunums, after fifteen days HIHFAD team will conduct verification to make the sure that all farmers have planted their lands, then we will distribute the second tranche, fertilizer voucher, after fifteen days HIHFAD team will distribute the fuel cost for irrigation. three distributions and redemption of the vouchers.

Protection Risk Analysis

The risk is: sexual abuse against women in the field. Likelihood, medium. The mitigation measures :1. one of the project team members will be female to contact women. 2. All the team members will be trained on the code of conduct. 3. Effective complaint mechanism to receive feedback from the ground. 4. Participation of the Local Council in all stage and activities of the project.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Low rainfall during winter for Wheat production. Provide a complementary irrigation.• Aggression from other Farmers. Explain the selection criteria to both the local council and the WUA in addition to all beneficiaries• Closing of Turkish Borders. Procurement from the Local Market• Increase in the prices of Fuel. contracting a supplier and fixing the cost for long duration• Self-consumption or sell of seeds particularly among IDPs. Include this point during the training, and emphasizing seeds is only one-off distribution and no further distribution for the next seasonIntervention from non-cooperative military groups and worsen security situation. In the worst case, the project will provide remote assistance to the farmers. As they have solid experiences in farming, once inputs were distributed, they can continue the project with minimum supports
