Project Summary

As of October 2024, South Sudan faces immense humanitarian challenges, with over 880,000 people displaced across 124 formal and informal sites. Vulnerabilities remain high, especially in self-settled IDP sites that lack essential services due to resource constraints, leaving over 320,000 people without CCCM support in 2024. Despite these challenges, IOM CCCM is committed to scaling up its efforts in 2025 to address the needs of IDPs and returnees, particularly those affected by the Sudan crisis.
Building on lessons from 2024, the 2025 response plan emphasizes transition, resilience, and sustainable development. IOM will prioritize fostering self-reliance among displaced communities, empowering local stakeholders, and transitioning from a site-centric approach to an area-based model. This strategy aims to bridge the gap between immediate humanitarian needs and long-term solutions.
Key interventions include lifesaving flood responses, emergency camp management, and enhanced services at critical sites such as Bentiu IDP Camp, Malakal PoC, and transit centers like Bulukat and Panakuach. As part of its durable solutions approach, IOM will focus on community-led disaster mitigation and strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) mechanisms to ensure inclusive and effective programming.
IOM will also invest in preparing IDP sites for potential closure or handover, by engaging communities and government authorities in the process. Infrastructure improvements, such as sustainable flood protection and solar-powered stormwater management, will be a cornerstone of these efforts. Through these measures, IOM aims to create conditions that enable displaced communities to thrive beyond camp settings.
In 2025, IOM’s efforts will target over 365,000 IDPs. By coordinating with partners and leveraging its role within the CCCM Cluster, IOM aims to deliver equitable assistance while laying the groundwork for long-term recovery and resilience in South Sudan.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to grapple with widespread displacement and recurring climate shocks, as evidenced by Round 15 of IOM DTM's Mobility Tracking. While over 2.8 million returnees have been recorded, more than 1.59 million individuals remain displaced across the country. Unity State, home to over 421,000 IDPs, leads in displacement figures, with Rubkona County alone hosting 224,383 IDPs, including South Sudan's largest IDP camp under IOM CCCM’s management.The humanitarian landscape remains precarious. Flooding continues to threaten Malakal, Bentiu, and Rubkona, compounded by the arrival of returnees fleeing the Sudan conflict. This influx risks straining limited resources and escalating tensions among IDPs, returnees, and host communities. Accessibility challenges, such as disrupted supply lines and rising commodity prices, further complicate relief efforts. Strategic corridors like the Renk-Malakal route remain critical for onward transportation assistance, with CCCM transit operations playing a vital role.Sub-national conflict and climate shocks, including flooding, exacerbate the crisis, leaving many displaced in informal settlements or collective centers in remote areas. In 2024, 320,000 out of 880,000 individuals staying in displacement sites, did not receive CCCM support, highlighting a critical gap in humanitarian coverage. To address these challenges, IOM CCCM will prioritize flexible, mobile responses that empower communities, foster self-governance, and promote resilience. These approaches will target displaced populations and returnees whose coping mechanisms have been depleted by repeated crises.Climate-related shocks demand robust mitigation efforts. IOM’s engineering teams will implement flood protection, site development, and care and maintenance initiatives to create safer environments and ensure continued access to aid. Community-led interventions will remain central, building local capacity and fostering resilience to sustain these efforts.IOM CCCM’s operations will focus on sustained and emergency interventions across diverse contexts, including Bentiu and Malakal PoC, as well as mobile responses in Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei. While maintaining essential services, IOM will also work towards durable solutions for displaced populations. By 2025, the program aims to reach 365,000 individuals with life-saving assistance, adaptive responses, and pathways to long-term stability.

2. Project activities

In 2025, IOM CCCM’s key priority will be facilitating the transition from emergency humanitarian assistance to sustainable solutions for displaced communities in South Sudan. Central to this effort will be the implementation of transitional and adaptive support strategies, particularly in Bentiu IDP Camp and Malakal PoC Site, while continuing to provide critical care and maintenance for IDPs and returnees in transit centers across the country. Sustained flood-response activities will remain a core focus in areas like Rubkona and Bentiu Towns, addressing the ongoing impacts of climate change and ensuring resilience for communities against future shocks. Mobile CCCM teams will play a vital role in reaching underserved and hard-to-reach locations, promoting self-governance and empowering communities to take ownership of their future. At the national level, IOM will co-lead the CCCM Cluster and act as State Focal Point (SFP) in key regions, ensuring a coordinated, accountable response that integrates humanitarian and development approaches. Transition efforts will also be supported by robust community engagement through the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group (CCEWG), advancing accountability to affected populations and ensuring displaced voices shape sustainable solutions. With an emphasis on resilience and self-reliance, IOM will bridge the gap between emergency aid and long-term development, paving the way for durable solutions and a path forward for displaced communities in South Sudan.
