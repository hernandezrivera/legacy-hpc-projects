Project Summary

According to the Health Cluster Response Plan for 2024, 8.9 million South Sudanese are in need of Humanitarian Aid, with 6.3 million among them requiring essential lifesaving healthcare services. Insecurity, long distances, limited healthcare supplies, inaccessibility of healthcare facilities and inadequate healthcare workers have disproportionately affected access to healthcare, especially among women and children. Unity State counties such as Leer, Koch, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pariang have been adversely affected by these emergencies and currently lie in severity 4.
In Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar and Pariang, an estimated 405,576 persons are in need of emergency life saving healthcare. The rates of immunization are extremely low, malaria cases remain high, while acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea are endemic in multiple counties. Thousands of hepatitis cases have been reported due to a dire WASH situation, while infectious diseases such as COVID-19 continue spreading, with significant portions of the population depending on traditional healers as a coping mechanism.
The proposed project shall extend primary health care and reproductive health services to vulnerable persons, including IDPs, returnees and host populations in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar and Pariang.
This project is in line with the health cluster objectives and the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan which seek to:
1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.
2. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.
3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
This project is targeting 45,400 beneficiaries and will support them with lifesaving emergency health care and reproductive healthcare services through 12 Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), 3 Primary Health Care Centers and mobile clinics that CH has been supporting in the targeted counties. CH will work with the State Ministry of Health in Bentiu and the County Health Departments in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar and Pariang, as well as the nutrition cluster for nutritional supplies to PHCCs, Protection and GBV sub cluster for reproductive health supplies and clinical management of rape, and the WASH cluster for WASH in health facilities. CH is a strong member of these clusters.
The project requires USD 1,100,000 to reach 45,400 vulnerable individuals in Leer, Koch, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pariang.

1. Needs

According the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2023, and the Health Cluster Response Plan for 2024, 6.3 million South Sudanese are in need of lifesaving healthcare services, with 1.6 million among them being internally displaced, and 1.3 million returnees. Only less than 2% of the government's budget goes to healthcare. Insecurity, long distances, limited healthcare supplies, inaccessibility of healthcare facilities and inadequate healthcare workers are some of the barriers to accessing healthcare. For example, there are only 3.5 healthcare workers per 10,000 population against the WHO recommended 44.5 per 10,000. Flooding in large areas of Unity state has further compounded the healthcare needs of huge populations. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with WHO estimating that 1223 in every 100,000 live births ending in maternal death. Only 46.7% of pregnant women attend at least one antenatal care visit, while skilled birth attendants only assist 19% of deliveries. Teenage pregnancy in the country remains high, with 30% of deliveries occurring among girls aged 15-19 or younger. Only 19% of healthcare facilities are able to offer the full package of the basic package of health and nutrition services. The rates of immunization are extremely low, malaria cases remain high, while acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea are endemic in multiple counties. Thousands of hepatitis cases have been reported due to a dire WASH situation, while infectious diseases have continued spreading, with significant portions of the population depending on traditional healers as a coping mechanism. In the targeted counties, healthcare has been adversely affected by flooding and conflict, with healthcare facilities either dilapidated or completely destroyed. Facilities like Thonyor PHCC in Leer have lost whole inpatient wings, while Guat and Lual PHCUs in Leer, Madol and Pabuong PHCUs in Mayendit, and Boaw PHCC in Koch, have been completely destroyed, which has increased mortality and morbidity rates in the affected counties, especially among vulnerable women and children under 5. Thousands of returnees have been flocking into Unity State through Rotriak in Rubkona, Adok in Leer and Nyaruop in Koch, courtesy of the civil war in Sudan, further straining the healthcare needs in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pariang. An estimated 405, 576 people are in severe need of lifesaving primary healthcare and reproductive health services. CH is targeting 45,400 persons in the named counties.

2. Project activities

CH will offer complementary services in the target locations through:1. Refresher training of frontline healthcare workers in provision of life-saving emergency services such as IMNCI, ICCM, BEmONC, CMR and PMTCT2. Carrying out vaccination campaigns such as EPI3. Providing case management for endemic diseases such as malaria, acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea through outpatient services in targeted facilities4. Carrying out rapid response and risk communication in the outreach locations earmarked for the project activities in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar and Pariang.5. Providing antenatal care (ANC), Skilled Birth Attendance and Postnatal Care services at the health facilities6. Conducting community-based outreach services in the PHCU’s and Mobile Clinic’s7. Promoting family planning through distribution of pills and condoms8. Minor rehabilitation of health affected primary healthcare facilities9. Strengthening Health information system (HIS) at implementation sites
