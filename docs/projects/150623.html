Project Summary

Mental health and psycho-social issues represent one of the most significant public health challenges across the oPt. Although the precise extent of mental disorders in oPt is not known the findings of a study published in 2017 (The Burden of Mental Disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, 1990-2013) indicates that Palestine had the largest burden of mental disorders among Eastern Mediterranean Region, oPt- which has seen almost 50 years of conflict, has the highest burden due to mental disorders in the EMR. 54·4% of Palestinian boys and 46·5% of Palestinian girls (age 6–12 years) were estimated to have emotional and behavioural disorders. The situation has been significantly exacerbated by the Great March of Return that started in Gaza in March 2018. WHO plans to respond to the growing mental health and psycho-social needs of young Palestinian men and women and provide support for the health teams in the PHC on dealing with emergency cases that would suffer from stress, PTSD , grief, moderate -severe depression and risk of suicide. In addition, support will be provided to leading E. Jerusalem hospital on management of mental health interventions. WHO will also support the resilience building initiatives amongst affected population, including building the capacity of health workers, building community awareness about common mental health problems during emergency.

Needs

Mental Health and Psychosocial issues represent one of the most significant public health challenges across the oPt .Although the precise extent of mental disorders in oPt is not known the findings of a study published in 2017(The Burden of Mental Disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, 1990-2013) indicates that oPt had the largest burden of mental disorders among Eastern Mediterranean Region, Palestine- which has seen almost 50 years of conflict, has the highest burden due to mental disorders in the EMR. 54·4% of Palestinian boys and 46·5% of Palestinian girls (age 6–12 years) were estimated to have emotional and behavioral disorders. Females are more likely to suffer and contribute more to the burden of mental disorders across all age groups over 14 years in the EMR. In Gaza, the violence has also generated widespread MHPSS consequences with approximately 52,098 people, including 26,049 children, in need of MHPSS responses; this is in addition to the estimated 210,000 already acutely vulnerable suffering from severe or moderate MH disorders.A study by WHO in 2009 in Gaza showed 38% of patients’ adults in PHC had CMD’s. The mean age of participants was 40.2 and 71.6% were females. Women had higher rates of CMD than men.This is a massive burden as there were over 1.2 million visits seen by physicians in PHC during 2016 in Gaza. Given the scale of the needs; the existing MH workforce is overloaded and unable to cope. Out of a health workforce of 14,248 in Palestine in 2016, there is only 227staff working in mental health( around 122 in the West Bank( 50% Female 50% Male and 105 in Gaza 60% Male and 50% female ) This is of particular concern during emergencies, specifically in Gaza after the last escalation began on 30th March 2018; which has led to an increase number of affected people who were exposed to violence and injuries, as well people who have severe mental health problems would need protection since they are at higher risk of human violation.In East Jerusalem, high levels of stress due to the protracted occupation and related political violence, imprisonment, house demolitions have all compounded mental health problems, with a range from 95,000-120,000 people, including children, youth and women are in need of MHPSS support. In 2016 a study by the Palestinian counseling Centre in Jerusalem found that in 2015 some 450 neighborhood children and adolescents were stopped and detained by Israeli security forces. 850 were detained for that same year, among them 31 girls and 105 children between the ages of 7-12.In East Jerusalem, Palestinian mental health services are very few and scarce and do not meet the needs of the people. Palestinians in East Jerusalem would seek mental health support provided by local Palestinian mental health services rather than Israelis mental health services that have few Arabic speaking professionals available in East Jerusalem). Young people who would suffer from mental health problems specifically conflict- related; would rather get services from Palestinian MH services or would not get any for security and confidentiality reasons. There are 6 Palestinian general hospitals in East Jerusalem that provide health services for Jerusalemites as well for WB and Gaza patients, some of these hospitals have minor MHPSS support for patients and would need further development to respond to the MHPSS needs of people in Jerusalem. The stigma attached to mental health is still an obstacle for for support. Therefore there is a need to provide community psycho-education interventions.

Activities

The MoH in Gaza provides primary care services through 56 PHC centers distributed across the five districts of the Gaza Strip. All PHC centers are physician-based and provide a range of public health and clinical services. The MoH, supported by the WHO, has started a process of integrating mental health services into all PHC centers. PHC general practitioners and nurses are trained and supervised in the implementation of a mh GAP program that aims at detection of mental disorders in PHC, provision of first level intervention, and referral of severe cases to CMHCs. The stepped care – mh GAP model was introduced during 2013 till 2018 into North Gaza and Rafah Districts, as well UNRWA 23 PHC clinics, where 3,000 new cases( 60-70 % female and 30-40% male) were detected and 3,626( 60-70 % female and 30-40% male) follow up sessions provided to PHC patients suffering from Common Mental Disorders (CMDs): depression, anxiety and unexplained medical symptoms.In this project we will provide support for the health teams in the PHC on dealing with emergency cases that would suffer from stress, PTSD , grief, moderate -severe depression and risk of suicide through mhGAP humanitarian intervention Guide 2015.In this project health teams will be better equipped in dealing with mental health emergency cases, will work on providing awareness to the community through various psychoeducation materials, as well the need to protect people with severe mental illnesses and provide them with the needed medication and basic needs in PHC if they will not be able to access the specialized mental health services (6 CMCHs for MoH are already functioning in Gaza) during an emergency. The project will have three inter-linked components. Under component 1, WHO will:1.1 Provide a suitable space in 6 centers of PHC in MOH (one in each District except Gaza District there will be 2 centers one in the East Region and other one in the West Region) for the emergency teams to provide MHPSS intervention, and to be as alternative spaces for CMHCs if cannot be reached during emergency 1.2 Provide a suitable space in 6 centers of PHC in UNRWA for emergency teams to provide MHPSS intervention, and to be as alternative spaces for CMHCs if cannot be reach during emergency.1.3 Build the capacity of 6 emergency teams in PHC in MOH by using the mhGAP -HIG1.4 Build the capacity of 6 emergency teams in PHC UNRWA by using the mhGAP -HIG1.6 Build the capacity of health workers and volunteers in 10 Trauma Stabilization Points (TSPs) that have been established after beginning of Great Return March in March 2018 by using Psychological First Aid guide and (mhGAP-HIG). (According to Health Cluster and coordination team the 10 TSPs, 5 of them are directed by MoH and the other 5 are directed by PRCS, in each TSP there are about 20 – 30 health workers (GPs, Nurses, Logistics, and volunteers. These teams will refer cases to PHC centers in MOH and UNRWA if needed.1.7 Provide supervision for the 6 PHC teams by a Mental Health specialist, once a month for 12 months.1.8 Procure psychotropic drugs used in PHC facilities and for emergency situation 1.9 Develop guidelines for the use of psycho-tropics at the PHC facilities.Under component 2 the following activities will be implemented:2.1 Provide a space to provide MHPSS interventions for inpatient at 2 general hospitals in Jerusalem ( Augusta Victorai and Al Maqassed)2.2 Build the capacity of a specialized Mental Health team at the Augusta Victoria hospital and Al Maqassed Hospital to respond to the MHPSS needs Under component 3 the following activities will be implemented:3.1 Building capacity of community leaders about common mental health problems during emergency (including mothers, teachers, religious leaders, and others3.2 Develop Psychoeducation materials to increase the understanding of MHPSS problems in emergency situation
