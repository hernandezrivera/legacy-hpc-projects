Project Summary

South Sudan’s level of protracted conflict has put the country in a very difficult situation due to the prolonged nature of the security situations that have further been compounded by natural calamities caused by climate change such as floods and drought. The civil war that commenced in 2013 added to the previous wounds faced by citizens during the liberation periods of the SPLA armed struggles. The further development of more armed groups in many parts of the country such as in Central and Western Equatoria has brought more displacements, loss of lives and property, and worsened economic situations for the population, this has worsened the dire situations of health and nutrition services which were already facing more problems due to low funding, inaccessibility to populations, inadequate qualified and skilled human resources, poor infrastructures, inadequate supplies, and low irregular pay of personnel that has led to high levels of attrition in all sectors.
Lainya and Morobo counties being in IPC4 (emergency) level with influx of returnees, and IDPs that are not settled to the host communities would mean that women and children will continue to suffer the brunt of high levels of unacceptable severe acute malnutrition in these counties. According to CSRF Report, the Morobo population is 116237, (IDPs=27378, Returnees=26860; Host Com= 61,999); and the Lainya population is 113928 ( IDPs= 21668; Returnees= 25789; Host Community = 66,471) who will need to be supported with necessary live-saving essential nutrition program. Assessments conducted in the 2 counties show low WASH practices among the communities of the 2 counties.
In this regard, there is high need for accelerating the nutrition interventions in Lainya and Morobo to curb the dire needs and ensure holistic synergistic approaches that gives a complete package to the people in need of the services. SSUHA therefore proposes to support the implementation of Nutrition services in the 2 counties by implementing activities that are aligned to the strategic objectives and Nutrition Cluster objectives and priorities in order to save the lives of women and children in Lainya and Morobo Counties that would cost about $800,745 for 12 months in 2025 to reach 53343 individuals in need of nutrition services and treat 7,018 children with acute malnutrition. The provision of health services in the 2 counties being under SSUHA gives leverage for the organization to be able to support the delivery of nutrition services to the targeted groups of people. This project will not be implemented independently but shall be integrated with existing project of Health, FSL, WASH, and other safety net programs under SSUHA.

1. Needs

The continued existence of the level of conflict, violence, and natural disaster due to climate change, and the failure of the state to address the major shocks affecting the general population of South Sudan has led to many people suffering uncontrollably. It is a known fact that women and children suffer from high levels of acute malnutrition in South Sudan, with the high GAM rates of >15% across the country. Key drivers of the high level of acute malnutrition are high food insecurity with >5.8 million people who are food insecure; poor WASH practices, high child morbidities caused by common childhood illnesses such as malaria, diarrhoea, URTIs; high GBV concerns; poor child feeding and care practices with a low MAD (5%); These are made worse by various deprivations and marginalization of communities. According to the IPC report, over 2 million children and women need to be treated in 2024 across the country. The Counties of Lainya and Morobo are still engulfed in active high levels of insecurity due to elements opposed to the government. Many locations are still inaccessible due to insecurity. The 2 counties however have pockets of locations that have relative peace and calm. Such locations have experienced an influx of returnees from Uganda, Congo, and Central African Republic. Due to the insecurity caused, there are IDPs settled in many areas of the 2 counties who are not being provided with the necessary nutrition services. According to the IPC report for 2024, both Lainya and Morobo are IPC4 (Emergency). The market structures have been affected by the civil war and hence high market prices and unavailability of some key commodities for the community. According to CSRF Report, the Morobo population is 116237, (IDPs=27378, Returnees=26860; Host Com= 61,999); and the Lainya population is 113928 ( IDPs= 21668; Returnees= 25789; Host Community = 66,471) who will need to be supported with necessary live-saving essential nutrition program. Assessments conducted in the 2 counties show low WASH practices among the communities of the 2 counties. SSUHA currently leads the implementation of Health services under the HSTP-funded program in both Lainya and Morobo Counties, supporting 12 health facilities in Lainya and 8 health facilities in Morobo. These health facilities need to be the pivotal locations for the delivery of nutrition services in the 2 counties. It is therefore important that these communities of Lainya and Morobo be supported with nutrition services that shall be integrated with the Health, WASH, & FSL services that SSUHA is already providing.

2. Project activities

SSUHA is the lead implementing partner for Health in Lainya, Morobo, Kajo-keji, and Yei river Counties of Central Equatoria State. SSUHA has a deep understanding of the community and the network for the provision of humanitarian services in the 2 counties. Due to the increased need caused by influx of returnees, IDPs in the 2 counties, SSUHA will strengthen the service delivery of Nutrition Services with other existing services that SSUHA is implementing such as WASH and Health. SSUHA will integrate the nutrition services with other sector services to ensure a holistic and synergistic approach in service delivery.
