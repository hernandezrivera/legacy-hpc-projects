Project Summary

The recurrent instabilities in most parts of South Sudan both political and inter-communal conflicts, coupled with migration of refugees and returnees in addition to the effect of floods including the limited food security and livelihood opportunities result in the displacement of communities and the economic regression exerting much stress on already limited WASH services across South Sudan.
PAH experience in successfully implementing both static and rapid responses in various sectors will use its strong WASH expertise to robustly respond with emergency lifesaving-assistance to vulnerable communities across South Sudan by improving access to portable water, sanitation services and the knowledge and capacity for the affected communities for a better wellbeing.
PAH will prioritize the vulnerable people affected by high level of WASH vulnerability especially in locations faced with IPC levels 3, 4 and 5, displaced people due to a variety of factors, returnees and host communities with over-stretched resources due to migration of IDPs, in locations with outbreaks waterborne disease such as cholera, HEV, Mpox and where refugees and returnees have settled or in transit to their resettlement destinations. To improve the access to clean and safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene plus other domestic uses, PAH will conduct repair/rehabilitation and construction of water points mainly boreholes, water yards, shallow wells, rain-water harvesting systems etc in the affected communities as well as in institutions especially nutrition and health facilities for integration of the response. PAH will prioritize sanitation at the institutional level, while at community level, PAH will encourage community participatory approach at community and household levels for sustainability as well as exit strategy. To enable a positive behaviour change of the affected population and the sustainability of the operation and maintenance of the WASH infrastructure, PAH will design a community led hygiene and health promotion messaging, combined with protection, health, nutrition and other cross-sectoral topics are integrated in the messaging. PAH will chose from the affected population people who will be trained as hygiene promoters, water management committees, sanitation committees, pump mechanics etc and engage them within the community.
PAH will ensure accountability to affected population (AAP) is key in its programming by ensuring that the beneficiaries are aware of the services at all stages and can report complaints through established complaints and feedback committees established within their community. Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of WASH, PAH will integrate the WASH with other sectors by providing dignity kits and NFIs, Protection mainstreaming etc to ensure that exploitation of the beneficiates especially the women and girls is minimized.
PAH, were feasible, will implement flood resilient infrastructure construction, such as elevated platform boreholes, community latrines and will encourage construction of flood resilient household level latrines, with use of local materials.

1. Needs

Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes persist across South Sudan despite the start of the green harvest and some stabilization in cereal prices in unimodal areas, albeit at above-average levels. This persistence is driven by ongoing sporadic conflict, underlying poor macro-economic conditions, limited income-generating opportunities, slow livelihood recovery prospects, and localized poor harvests. As PAH responses in both static and mobile modality the submitted proposal refers to the general needs of the country due to possibility of mobile responses in multiple locations. According to the latest HNO - 2024, more than 8.5 million people need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. Based on recently issued IPC, between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal county (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the northern counties bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. Sporadic attacks and road ambushes continue in several areas of South Sudan, leading to disruption of livelihood activities and trade flows, as well as loss of lives in some incidents. The main drivers leading to poor WaSH services include:• Insecurity and conflict - Forced displacement, interruptions in humanitarian aid delivery, and localized conflict and insecurity - primarily in the Greater Tonj area of Warrap, Luakpiny/Nassir County of Upper Nile, and parts of Jonglei, Lakes and Central and Western Equatoria states. also rising community tension over land resources and looting of cattle.• Economic crisis - South Sudan’s economy is experiencing a macroeconomic crisis, characterized by worsening currency depreciation, high food prices and declining household purchasing.• Climate Shocks - Floods and dry spells contribute to low agricultural production. Flooding was 10 percent higher in 2024 compared to 2023 and the floods not only displaced people but also disrupted delivery of humanitarian support, and contributed to low access to health services in the face of increased disease burden, driven by poor sanitation practices, thus increasing the risk of acute malnutrition.• Low Agricultural production- Unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming methods negatively impacted agricultural production, resulting in increased humanitarian food assistance needs.• Political instability – following the postponement of general elections in the country, tensions have increased overall the country. As an example of the tensions and internal conflict happening in November 2024 in Juba.

2. Project activities

1. PAH will conduct multisectoral assessment with a focus on WaSH and protection analysis using risk equation in cooperation with clusters and I/NNGOs to provide platform for gap analysis and needs.2.Rehabilitation of existing water points. PAH will target water points at the community level, with special attention to point in both Health and Nut facilities. In grazing areas water points will be designed in a manner to ensure safety for people and animals. In areas with high food insecurity PAH will assess the irrigation practices and possibilities and may give instructions and encourage people for sustainable and safe solutions for watering the crops. This will provide synergy between WASH and food production/livelihood. 3.Establishment of new water points. New water points development will be considered in case of communities, whose water points are overcrowded or there is limited access to portable water. New water points will also be established in permanent/semi-permanent IDP location depending on the need and population. The water points may be developed/upgraded to solar powered water yards. For both new and rehabilitated points the water user committees (WUCs) will be trained and equipped to ensure sustainability and maintenance cost will be instituted. Pump mechanics will be trained locally to ensure urgent repairs in case of break downs. 4.PAH will consider construction of new or improving existing sanitation facilities at local institutions or crowded locations. PAH will establish gender and disability segregated sanitation facilities provided with proper doors and inside locks to enhance privacy and mitigate risk of GBV. The sanitation facilities will be handed over to the institutions management and monitored to ensure sustainability. For a sustainable sanitation approach in IDP, host community, refugee and returnee settings, PAH will form, and train sanitation committees selected from across the villages and provide the committees with sanitation kits. The provision of these kits to enhance household latrine construction will be accompanied with awareness on the importance of latrine use, the dangers of open defecation etc. 5. Flood resilient constructions solutions will be implemented for both new/rehabilitated boreholes and sanitation facilities to avoid inflow of contamination. PAH has designed mentioned facilities, including household level latrines to be constructed with a use of local material. 6.Providing WASH NFIs. Where possible, the CBI modality will be implemented. As a member of CWG and JMMI PAH will monitor closely the market situation. Provision of solar lamps, dignity/hygiene kits and soap will be ensured for those most at risk of GBV. PAH will provide vouchers for soap which will cover three months’ supply. Depending on availability, items will be procured from the local markets in the project locations. The market and risks assessment will be conducted to later decide if vouchers modality can be used. Within an integrated approach, the scale-up of cash transfers will be promoted when market, security and technical conditions are met. During the provision of the items, PAH will rely on core pipeline or local market for CVA while ensuring meaningful access - separate queues for elders, PWD and pregnant women. Carriers will be considered for people with limited mobility.7.Community awareness on hygiene, sanitation and household water treatment. awareness raising will be delivered to the community at the HH level, including monitoring of implementing the measures, with special focus on hygiene and sanitation, mother and caretaker of severely malnourished children. As part of protection mainstreaming, protection training and awareness raising sessions will be provided to community hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, WUCs members, women groups and others. PAH will work closely with NUT/Health and FSL partners to integrate the project with their activities. PAH will provide trainings to NUT staff/partner
