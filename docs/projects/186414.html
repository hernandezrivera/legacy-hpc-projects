Project Summary

In its Round 23 Report on displacement conducted in the Far-North Region in July 2021, IOM indicated that more than 357,631, persons are internally displaced in the Far-North Region due to the Boko haram and other groups attacks in the Lake Chad Basin. According to HNO analysis, more than 85% of these displaced persons need protection response. UNHCR Statistics of refugees at the end of October 2021 indicated that 17,099 Nigerian refugees are living in the Region, both in Minawao camp and outside.
The Boko Haram insurgency that started in Nigeria spread quickly in the Far-North Region of Cameroun early in 2014, with abduction and killing of civilians, destruction of villages, attacks on military positions, and massive violation of human rights. This led to an endless movement of population, both internally and externally with refugee’s movement from Nigeria to the Region. The protection situation in Far-North Region became therefore mixed situation, very complex to handle, and marked by violence inflicted upon the most vulnerable civilians by the insurgency groups. In 2021, the security situation in the region remained extremely volatile with, terrorism attacks and crime incidents. The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated the protection context of the Far North Region in Cameroon.
To contribute to the protection response and fully play its role as the provider of last resort as well as the one of Protection Group Co-lead lead, UNHCR identified 4 key priorities in 2022 and beyond in particular: 1) Strengthening the centrality of protection, 2) Coordination of lead Sectors 3) Emergency preparedness and response and 4) Securing solutions.
Through a community-based approach, UNHCR intends to secure protection and provide solutions through a comprehensive response including reinforcing protection monitoring and strengthening the ability of communities to participate in their own protection particularly to respond to protection incidents as well as to contribute to the search for solutions. The interventions will focus on community empowerment initiatives, responding to critical GBV and child protection incidents, including legal assistance and support for civil documentation as well as psychosocial services. In-kind support, Cash-Based interventions, and referrals to available services in the identified communities will be an integral part of the response.
The use of an area-based approach instead will consist of selecting specific areas in the 3 Divisions affected by the displacement and working with all stakeholders in the priority locations including displaced, host community, IDPs and refugees, local authorities, civil society, agencies working in the areas.. The approach will ultimately contribute to build sustainability of key services and consequently prevent displacement as well as guarantee greater accountability to affected populations (AAP).
Based on the above, UNHCR will target 3 the Divisions (Logon et Chari, Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Sava) with an estimated reach of 350,000 persons and specific targets include:
• 20 community empowerment initiatives (or some 3800 people) to contribute to solutions in the targeted areas.
• A comprehensive approach to respond to GBV will include:
• Provision of psychosocial services (PSS) to 50 GBV survivors and persons with specific needs
• Provision of Legal services to 50 GBV survivors
• Provision of material assistance (dignity kits) to 4000 GBV survivors (figure to be confirmed)
• Provision of cash grant support to 2000 GBV survivors and vulnerable persons to mitigate protection risks, establish a small business and restore dignity. (figure to be confirmed);
• 10000 POCs including children will be supported to obtain civil documentation.
To ensure accountability to the affected population and PSEA related support is available, UNHCR will arrange for a joint UNHCR-WFP hotline.
It's in line with Cameroon HRP 2022 Protection Framework, 2019 HC engagement in IDPs.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Protection: Protection monitoring and analysis and community engagement mechanism will be used as essential mechanisms to identify, prevent and mitigate conflict and violence, and their consequences, including forced displacement. These monitoring mechanisms and systems, including collective analysis through the Protection Group, will be regularly used for advocacy, interventions, programming, resource mobilization and communications. UNHCR will establish its protection monitoring mechanism in the 3 indicated Division Logone et Chari, Mayo Tsanaga, and Mayo Sava. Respond to GBV and child protection incidents. UNHCR will implement direct interventions in prevention and holistic support to GBV survivors and will strengthen the community-based approach while ensuring that interventions focus on the protection of survivors, especially women and children. While 50 survivors of SGBV, 50 children in risk and persons with specific needs will be supported with case management, psychosocial and medical support and with cash-based interventions to mitigate protection risks and respond to incidents, 4000 GBV survivors and vulnerable people will benefit from material support (dignity kits and mama kits). In addition, legal assistance will be provided to 50 victims of the incidents in need of legal support. Community empowerment initiatives. 20 community empowerment initiatives towards protection solutions will be supported with direct support to approximately 3000 individuals. The capacity building of community-based protection committees will also contribute to build peaceful coexistence. In addition, 200 women and socially and economically vulnerable persons will benefit from community empowerment initiatives for protection through vocational training and startups as a part of protection response. Civil documentation. In 2022, UNHCR in close collaboration with civil documentation authorities aims to improve IDPs access to civil documentation. Capacity building for the support for the establishment 10000 civil documents of children and adults identified through the protection monitoring or referred by others will be provided. Community mobilization: IDPs as well as the communities that host them will be consulted through the organization of group discussions considering criteria of age, gender and diversity. Strengthening partnership and coordination of protection activities. UNHCR is the Co-lead for the Protection Group in Far North region and UNHCR and its partners regularly participate in coordination meetings and initiatives with other actors for synergy of activities, including with members of the GBV Sub-Group led by UNFPA, Child Protection AOR led by UNICEF and Housing, Land and Property AoR led by NRC. UNHCR will closely coordinate with UNFPA, UNICEF, NRC and other actors on coverage of the localities to avoid duplication of interventions and for complementarity of the activities. Increase government’s participation and ownership. UNHCR will continue building its collaboration with the relevant state services, particularly the ‘’Comites Mixtes de Protection’’ to prevent and reduce human rights violations, while promoting the government institutions involvement in the coordination structures. With a view to sustaining UNHCR's varied humanitarian responses, collaboration will continue with the State technical services, such as Delegations for Social Affairs, Health, the National Commission for Human Right and Freedom (NCHRF), the Regional BUNEC and Courts. Protection Group leadershipUNHCR through the protection Group, ensuring regular attendance and reporting of protection activities of at least 20 protection Group members, run at least 05 capacity building sessions on human rights law, conduct advocacy activities on behalf of the Protection Group, strengthen local NGOs, and CBOs in providing first response to protection needs.
