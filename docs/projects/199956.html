1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The 2023 refugee project aims at addressing the protracted refugee situation in Maratane settlement in Nampula and in urban areas throughout the country and has the overall objectives of (1) strengthening the protection environment and enjoyment of rights by refugees and asylum seekers as well as (2) finding durable solutions for refugees and asylum seekers in Mozambique. In 2023, UNHCR will maintain and strengthen its leadership role in managing the protracted refugee situation. More specifically, in order to strengthen access to protection, UNHCR and its partners, including the government, will: • support government efforts to improve access to and the quality of the national asylum system through capacity building and technical support, and conduct status determination under its mandate for selected individuals.• continue to undertake the biometric registration of refugees and asylum-seekers and issue them with individual documentation with the support of UNHCR. • support access to civil documentation • provide adequate services to persons with specific needs, including persons with legal and physical protection needs, persons with disability (PwD), GBV survivors, as well as children, older persons, and women at risk.• strengthen protection and empowerment of communities through the expansion of community-based-structures and activities, including of women, PwD, children and youth, and to mitigate and respond to effect of climate emergencies.• enhance livelihoods and economic inclusion opportunities to strengthen self-reliance, enhance social cohesion with host communities and empower women. • provide lifesaving assistance to the most vulnerable refugees and asylum seekers, including food and nutrition.• Strengthen access to basic services such as health, WASH and education. Beyond strengthening of the national asylum system, the focus of the project will be on supporting durable solutions for refugees and in particular:• UNHCR will facilitate the repatriation of refugees and asylum seekers willing to return to their countries of origin and promote this repatriation when conditions in the country of origin allows. • The Government pledge for the local integration will be supported through support to naturalization of eligible refugees and a mobilization of other stakeholders to enhance the support the provinces and national services where refugees are located, including education, health, social services, etc. The response will also support the government’ “out of camps” policy turning the refugee settlement concept into a village and urban integration. • Based on needs and criteria as well as available allocation to the operation, selected individual will be resettled to third country. The project will target 33,691 including refugees, asylum seekers, and where feasible vulnerable host community members. Particular focus will be placed on the following target groups: persons with specific needs including persons with legal and physical protection needs, persons with disabilities, survivors of torture, persons with serious medical conditions, unaccompanied and separated children, as well as older persons, women and children at risk.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The 2023 refugee project will be targeting the following areas: Maratane Refugee Settlement, Nampula and Maputo provinces. The project will prioritize the provision of individual support to refugees and asylum seekers, including the further strengthening of case management, provision of inclusive and specialized services through a multi sectorial service provision, legal aid and access to civil documentation, mental health and psychosocial support, distribution of assistive devices and livelihood activities (including life-skills and vocational training to enhance self-reliance of refugees in Maratane and support local integration while facilitating the host community’s access to livelihoods). The project will also focus on achievement of durable solutions by supporting refugees with Voluntary Repatriation, Resettlement and complementary pathways as well as local integration. Such support will be available in Maratane Refugee Settlement as well in urban areas.The referral mechanisms at district, provincial and central levels will also be strengthened to ensure that refugees and asylum seekers are receiving adequate support while they are integrated into the national systems. The mobilization of relevant stakeholders at provincial and national level will also contribute to the support and the strengthening of these systems. Furthermore, advocacy will be in favor of full implementation of the Government’s pledges, and for the inclusion of refugees and asylum seekers into area-based development projects in Nampula province, with the support of financial institutions and development agencies, as well as labour mobility outside the settlement, including to urban areas.Furthermore, the project will also strengthen community-based activities in Maratane Refugee Settlement through the diversification of the various community structures and full presentiveness of age, gender and diversity, strengthening joint projects between host communities and refugees and asylum seekers especially in the area of livelihoods, as well as enhancing community-based PSS. Advocacy activities will be carried out for the withdrawal of key reservations to the Convention relating to the status of refugees, as well as enhancement of the refugee status determination process. Capacity building and training activities on Disaster Risk Reduction and protection mainstreaming in DRR to implementing partners, including INAR, refugee and host community leadership, community members and other relevant stakeholders will be conducted with the vision of strengthening resilience of the maratane settlement population and surrounding communities to climate shocks that have proven to have severe impacts on overall protection environment, infrastructure, wellbeing and livelihoods of beneficiaries' area.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR as the protection mandated agency ensures the centrality of protection throughout all its work. UNHCR’s workforce and partners are trained on humanitarian principles and the key elements of the centrality of protection: do no harm principle, safety and dignity, effective access to services and assistance, accountability to affected populations, community participation and empowerment as well as protection mainstreaming throughout all sectors. UNHCR’s focus is on the quality of protection services. In line with its AGD policy, UNHCR’s GBV policy, the Best Interests Procedure (BIP) guidance and guidance on case management, UNHCR and partners will ensure the respect for culture and religion, a survivor-centered approach, the best interest of children and the respect of the individuals.Furthermore, Govt registration data using UNHCR progress tool, protection-related information management system such as safety audits, protection monitoring reports, participatory assessment reports, allows for protection analysis, feedback on areas of protection risk, causes, gaps that need to be addressed and potential solutions. Moreover, community engagement activities provide for platform for feedback by the community in areas risk and potentially causing harm. Complaints and feedback mechanisms as well referral by actors through case management provide for timely response averting harm. Majority of women and children continue to be most as risk and this is compounded by effects of climate related impact such as cyclones. Climate related effect affect livelihood further reducing social safety nets of earning, this could lead to other effect like poor nutrition and poor coping mechanisms

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNHCR takes the ‘do no harm’ and age, gender, and diversity approach to ensure meaningful access to project activities very seriously. UNHCR has the capacity to identify and respond to risks through protection, including case management and participatory assessment outcomes that are designed in our programs to capture discussion and inputs with communities about their needs and capacities through the project cycle . UNHCR will leverage its current work in protection coordination in Nampula to mainstream GBV across all responses , using the IASC GBV guidelines. UNHCR and authorities have developed GBV referral pathways and trained SPAS, INAR, Health and community leaders on GBV core concepts. UNHCR is working with government GBV integrated one stop services in Nampula. Furthermore, UNHCR, including in its role as Chair of the National Community Engagement and Accountability to Affected Population (CE-AAP) Working Group, will engage in a dialogue with the refugees and asylum seekers, and host community to ensure that accurate, accessible, and timely information is shared with them in their own language, and that their feedback and participation are integral parts of humanitarian response to ensure they are actively involved in the decisions that affect their lives. There are groups for women, elderly, PWD mainstreamed in the community structure.Throughout the project UNHCR will also carry out the following:• Two-way communication with communities will be strengthened. Means of communication with refugees and asylum seekers will be adapted to the preferred communication channels based on age, gender, and diversity as well as local (urban, rural, site) and messages to communities will be developed together with the communities. • Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be enhanced and will remain diverse to ensure they are accessible by all. They include regular presence in Maratane settlement, suggestions boxes, protection helpline, Linha verde, regular consultations with various community forum with an age, gender and diversity focus, participatory assessments, staff presence during food and NFI distributions (Maratane), post-distribution monitoring, feedback, and satisfaction survey. • Regular feedback to the communities will be enhanced.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will work with affected communities to actively engage persons with disabilities in the program design and implementation. Govt registration data using UNHCR progress tool includes specific needs among them disability that are diverse as registration focuses on specific needs. UNHCR has conducted a review and mapping of the disability needs in Maratane and urban area of Nampula. The use of Washington group of questions was introduced to volunteers through training). UNHCR will continue work in mainstreaming protection in all the sectors of humanitarian assistance in Maratane. Capacity building sessions will be conducted to partners as well as the beneficiaries to improve the protection situation of the settlement and ensure persons with specific needs and persons with disability more specifically have access to humanitarian assistance. UNHCR will work closely with its protection partner in Maratane Refugee Settlement and government counterpart SPAS (Social Affairs) by providing capacity building in Disability inclusion. Furthermore, Social Affairs activist will map and collect disaggregated information on persons with disability. In order to fully achieve that UNHCR will reinforce its work and collaboration with Maratane Settlement community structures and leadership and other influential community based groups as they play a paramount.Furthermore, UNHCR with partners plans to provide targeted assistance based on needs, taking into account vulnerability criteria and the new standard specific needs codes in order to fill gaps in assistance while continuing to work on disability inclusion – thus strengthening relationships with local disability actors, and strengthening their capacity where possible.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR ensures environmental considerations across the project. Materials used for community built safe spaces are local, natural, and sustainable, the work on the spaces is conducted directly within the community. Furthermore, as part of its community-based protection strategy, UNHCR ensures that the refugee and asylum seeker communities are sensitised and engaged on the protection of the environment, emphasizing their responsibility for the local environment where they settle. Additional efforts to sensitize and promote green practices among the communities will be implemented throughout all interventions. Capacity building to implementing partners as well as community leaders on Disaster Risk Reduction will be conducted. Efforts will include strengthening community ability to cope from environmental shocks as well as improve preparedness and resilience in case of disasters. Disaster committees will be created and equipped and those will be responsible for community mobilization, sensitization and early warning.
