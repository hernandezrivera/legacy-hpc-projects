Project Summary

The intervention strives to fortify response and prevention services in target communities for a total of 241,915 individuals, with 198,255 individuals for CP and 43,660 for GBV, by strengthen the protection system, through conducting 10 CP and 5 GBV monitoring, assessments, and safety audits, these efforts intend to illuminate grave violations against children in humanitarian contexts, emphasizing the protection of children in conflicts. The assessments will specify sources of threat and vulnerabilities, particularly for children at high risk of abuse, neglect, exploitation, or violence. Additionally, the development and enhancement of safe and ethical CP and GBV referral pathways, ensuring that the most vulnerable children have access to health, psychosocial, legal aid, and other essential services. Moreover, the initiative plans to capacitate 650 protection and humanitarian actors, and other duty bearers, aiming at empowering them to prevent and respond to grave violations, CP, and GBV concerns, and further enhance their protection skills, integration, and coordination by in cooperating Centrality of Protection, PFA, and Safe Referral and Identification. In addition to, the training of 200 non-GBV service providers in GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response strategies. Enhancing the protection of Children at Risk including UASC, CAFFAGs, and GBV survivors through specialized Case Management and FTR services is prioritized. Where comprehensive individual assessments are planned to be conducted for a total of 3,690 children (3,350 CP and 340 GBV) based on their BIAs. The result includes providing appropriate interim or long-term alternative care for 300 registered vulnerable children, including UASC, while actively tracing their biological families. Furthermore, direct cash support will be offered to extremely vulnerable children and their families based on a best interest assessment. The initiative also plans to identify and train 2,997 alternative caregivers and foster parents, focusing on positive parenting using SCIs' common approach to Safe Families, child safeguarding, CP reporting, and referral mechanisms. Monthly Parental and Children's sessions will be conducted with foster children and alternative caregivers. Additionally, to ensure the quality of the provided Case Management service the case officers, assistants, case workers, and facilitators will undergo training on case management, FTR, alternative care, the CPIMS+, and Caring for Child Survivors (CCS). Also, the response will provide MHPSS specialized services to 2,150 children. The response will enhance the protection status of the most vulnerable children by reinforcing monitoring, reporting, and follow-up through community-based protection mechanisms in the affected area. By the establishment of 55 CBCPNs each comprising 15 members and representing various groups within the community. These networks will be supported with necessary equipment and capacitated on CR, CP, and GBV relevant aspects, as well as monitoring and reporting according to CBCPN's TOR. The strategy will also include capacity-building for 825 community members on GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and/or response. Also, community outreach sessions and campaigns will be conducted in the target communities, reaching 72,240 individuals, with a specific focus on preventing and responding to CP and SGBV risks. Additionally, the plan involves organizing 70 awareness campaigns on GBV. In addition to, enhancing the protection status and psychosocial well-being of affected children through a multi-faceted approach. Through the establishment and/or rehabilitation of permanent/semi-permanent safe spaces, MPCs, and CFSs dedicated to delivering unstructured play activities and basic PSS to the most vulnerable boys and girls, targeting 65,000 children, and providing PSS dignity kits and hygiene items to 12,360 children. Finally, supporting 850 girls and women through the distribution of dignity kits

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Save the Children's mission is to enhance safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, with a particular focus on addressing the unique needs of individuals and groups, including girls and boys. The program design and implementation are grounded in a thorough context analysis that considers local power dynamics and conflict intricacies. Collaboration and coordination with various stakeholders, such as community members, government partners, local NGOs, and INGOs, are paramount. This involves strategic information sharing based on identified needs to keep relevant actors informed, maximize resource utilization, share best practices, and prevent unintended negative consequences or exacerbation of community tensions. Save the Children is committed to programming that safeguards, protects, and empowers vulnerable and marginalized individuals, ultimately contributing to tension reduction within communities.Save the Children will be participating in the Child Protection and GBV AoRs at national and sub-national levels to coordinate, provide, and advocate for a comprehensive approach to addressing the diverse protection needs of children and individuals. This collaborative effort brings together specialized expertise, enhances effective coordination, ensures consistent standards, and facilitates capacity building among practitioners. In addition, SC will leverage is position in the technical groups at national levels including Case Management (CMTF), Case Management Task Force (CMTF) and the County Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR), and MHPSS WG, to safeguard the well-being of vulnerable populations, in ensuring unified and comprehensive approach to individualized support for those facing protection concerns, emphasizing the best interests of the child, collecting and disseminating data on protection issues, fostering situational awareness, accountability, and advocacy, addressing the mental health and psychosocial needs of affected individuals, promoting resilience and recovery through coordinated, culturally sensitive responses. Save the Children Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. SCI regional Gender and GBV Specialist alongside the CO capacity will supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will actively utilize community feedback through various mechanisms, including call-in numbers, feedback and complaint boxes, and direct collection during training activities. A hotline number will be distributed across all project sites to facilitate feedback. All received feedback and complaints will be meticulously documented and analyzed, contributing to the learning processes and decision-making for project improvement. Accountability information sharing materials, encompassing program standards, will be disseminated during project activities and at community information centers.Following Save the Children's global Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) guidelines, a robust monitoring and evaluation system, along with an Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT), will be established for tracking project indicators. The IPTT will facilitate data collection, analysis, reporting, reflection, and learning throughout the project's duration. The produced information will be shared with relevant staff, including the Senior Management Team, for informed decision-making, and the CP and GBV AoR coordination mechanisms and the partners to maximize the learning. Additionally, Quality Benchmarks (QBs) will be employed to ensure intervention quality, with project staff using QBs for maintenance, MEAL staff utilizing them during field visits for monitoring, and debrief sessions organized to develop action plans based on field monitoring findings. A tracking system will ensure effective translation of plans into actions, emphasizing continuous improvement.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Save the Children (SC) maintains a zero-tolerance stance against all forms of abuse and exploitation, particularly with a focus on safeguarding children and families in contact with SC. The child safeguarding policy aims to protect children from intentional or negligent actions that could expose them to abuse, sexual exploitation, injury, or other forms of harm. Rigorous reporting procedures ensure swift investigation and resolution of cases, with disciplinary measures, including reporting to local law enforcement, taken against any staff found in violation. SC's Whistleblowing Policy enables individuals to directly voice concerns to senior leaders if conventional reporting channels seem inadequate. All staff, volunteers, and partners engaged with SC sign the safeguarding policy before any beneficiary interaction, reinforced by regular training to ensure awareness of child safeguarding issues. Focal points and champions at each field and area office coordinate safeguarding matters, with inclusive information-sharing mechanisms, feedback, and complaint avenues accessible to diverse gender and age groups. Feedback collection, facilitated through meetings and an accountability phone number, allows beneficiaries to connect with SC and local partners. Received feedback and complaints contribute to learning processes, decision-making, and quality improvement. SC maintains a functional accountability and Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) system, offering various channels like call-in numbers and complaint boxes. Categorized feedback is directed to relevant staff, ensuring comprehensive addressing, documentation, and analysis to enhance program delivery. SC also produces accountability information-sharing materials for supported facilities, emphasizing program standards and relevant contact information. Moreover, SC prioritizes the involvement of persons and representatives from vulnerable groups in the project's implementation and monitoring processes, with committees, networks, and women's centers playing a crucial role in beneficiary identification, referral, advocacy, psychosocial support, awareness-raising, and sensitization. All these actions align with SC's commitment to the "Do No Harm" approach.
