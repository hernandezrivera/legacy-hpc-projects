Project Summary

Limited access to quality education in a protective environment remains a challenge within Syria and requires concerted efforts among education members to enable an effective response. The complexity of the humanitarian crisis and humanitarian coordination architecture in Syria demands larger investments in strengthening the existing coordination structures to support its membership to deliver a predictable, effective and inclusive response that is guided by the population’s priorities and preferences, and to facilitate informed donor decision making. As co-leads of the education sector, UNICEF and Save the Children support advocacy and resource mobilization efforts, promote synergies and information sharing between the hubs and focus on key strategic activities related to the six core coordination functions, namely:
• Support service delivery based on needs;
• Inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis;
• Plan and implement cluster strategies;
• Monitor and evaluate performance;
• Build national capacities;
• Support advocacy efforts

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has entered its tenth year. The complexity of the humanitarian response to address protected and acute needs demands larger investments in the existing coordination mechanisms namely, in Damascus, Gaziantep and North-East Syria, as well as a Whole of Syria overall coordination system that ensures coherence across the various hubs. As per the HNO, there are 94% of school-aged children living in areas with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditions, an increase to last year’s 86%. In 2020, there were over 200 education members across the three hubs operating from within and outside of Syria. Building on the investments of previous years and placing increased focus on localization, in 2021 the Education Sector will continue to prioritize strengthening the capacity of the education system to deliver and sustain education investments. COVID-19 and worsening economic conditions have exacerbated the consequences of systemic underinvestment in education service delivery. The Education Sector aims to improve the capacity of education members and will implement capacity development programs that provide targeted and needs-driven opportunities for national members for both professional technical development and action-oriented learning and equip them with the knowledge and skills required to plan, implement and coordinate high quality emergency education responses.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented through the WoS Education coordination group (Hub cluster coordinators and subnational coordinators, Hub IMs and Hub Cluster Co-leads) supported by the WoS Education Sector Coordination team. This project covers both the staff costs related to the coordination functions, including dedicated information management and coordination capacity, as well as the costs of key strategic activities that are related to the six core coordination functions, namely: (a) support service delivery based on needs; (b) inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis; (c) plan and implement cluster strategies; (d) monitor and evaluate performance; (e) build national capacities; and (f) support advocacy efforts. At the WOS level and under the cluster co-leadership of UNICEF and Save the Children, concerted efforts will focus on enhancing the effective coordination among the hubs namely through:- Ensuring effective information management in all hubs, and at the WoS level;- Development and dissemination of timely information materials;- Strengthening evidence-based advocacy on attacks on education and other key issues that need to be advocated on;- Promoting the exchange of best practices and technical standards;- Representing and advocating on behalf of the sector in the relevant fora, at hub and WoS level and at intersectoral level, including key donors and stabilization actors among others;- Facilitating of capacity building initiatives to strengthen capacity of education members across the hubs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Children in insecure areas are denied their right to education due to lack of capacity and ability of education members to respond. Mitigating measure include, ensuring that education members are adequately trained on contingency planning and response, in addition to strengthening linkages with other clusters and promotion of duty of care commitment. Risk: Children and education personnel are unduly exposed to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) from aid workers. Mitigation measures will include the familiarization and dissemination of education members with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies and promote within education members to adopt policies within their organization.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Potential of conflict escalation remains high in some parts of the country with reduced access by education members to provide quality education interventions. As a mitigating measure, the cluster will ensure that education members are adequately supported through capacity building packages that strengthen preparedness and response, including strengthening linkages with key sectors. As per the 2021 HNO, attacks on education facilities continue, while education members have limited influence on preventing attacks on education, in 2021 the education cluster will focus its efforts on mitigating the impact of attacks and maintain its attacks on education surveillance system that feeds into the MRM4Syria. This includes strengthened reporting, advocating that schools in insecure locations are structurally strengthened, safety protocols are in place, preparedness trainings regularly practiced and that psychosocial support services are provided. Mitigation measures will include timely evidence-based advocacy efforts by WOS co-leads with the donor and wider humanitarian community.
