Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide immediate access to food and livelihood support to the vulnerable and food insecure populace of the target locations in Somalia that were most affected by the deteriorating food security situation.
The project entails a cash for work component for pastoral livelihood locations which involves the rehabilitation/construction of 20 communal water catchments in the target locations in- order to reduce the vulnerability and enhance resilience of the target beneficiary households composed of 50 skilled labourers who earn 5.5$ a day for 20 days a month for four months and 100 unskilled labourers who earn $5 per day for 20 working days in a month ($100) for 4 months and an unconditional cash grant aspect for 400 labor inactive rural households in the target locations for 6 months.

Needs

Based on the FSNAU Food Security and Nutrition Post Gu technical release 2018, the food security situation in Somalia has continued to improve due to the above average rainfall that contributed to seasonal improvements to food and income sources and market conditions and the positive impacts of sustained and large scale humanitarian assistance.However, the food security situation in Somalia remains precarious and the figures of 1.5 million people still facing acute food security crisis or worse despite the improvements are truly unsettling.Acute food insecurity is prevalent among populations who lost most of their animals and became destitute during the 2016-2017 severe drought, those affected by the flooding in April and May 2018 and others who were affected by the large scale and protracted displacement due to a combination of conflict and natural hazards. These population groups are expected to face large scale food consumption gaps and need humanitarian assistance to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation.The FSNAU and FEWSNET East Africa reports consistently identify the Juba, Galgaduud, Mudug and Nugal regions populace as some of the areas of concern. Over 16% of the of the population in Lower Juba is in crisis and emergency, 13 % of the population in Mudug is IPC Phase 3 and 4, over 7 % of the population in Nugaal is in crisis and emergency while a further 14 % of the total population is in crisis and emergency (IPC phase 3 & 4) in Galgaduud region. A significantly higher proportion of the population in the aforesaid regions is in Stressed phase. Majority of the affected populace are the IDPs, pastoral and the agro-pastoral livelihoods. The IDPs remain extremely vulnerable and represent a major proportion of the total number of people who face crisis and emergency in these regions and across Somalia. Limited humanitarian access in some of the affected areas of the target regions further exacerbates the situation.

Activities

• Establishing project committees composed of seven persons (4 men and 3 women) from the community in each location of the target regions.• Conduct identification and selection of 400 most vulnerable women headed households, pregnant and lactating mothers and elderly persons from the IDPs and Host Communities, and pastoral and agro-pastoral households in the target locations;• Conduct the distribution of cash to the selected 400 beneficiary households through the identified money vendor. Each household $80 for 6 months;• Undertake community mobilization, conduct re-identification and rehabilitate /construct 20 strategic communal infrastructure including river banks, water pans, irrigation canals, feeder roads and culverts;• Conduct identification and selection of 150 poor and drought affected households to be engaged in CFW exercise targeting poorest and unskilled households in critical livelihoods crisis in Agro pastoral and Pastoral Livelihoods. Each unskilled labourer will earn $5.5per day for 20 working days in a month ($110) for 4 months;• Conduct identification and Engage 50 skilled laborers in the rehabilitation/construction of the strategic communal infrastructure identified. The 50 skilled labourers working as foremen will earn $6 per day working for 20 days in a month for 4 months.• Provide Business skills training and a seed grant of 100 USD to 100 IDP/Returnees and Host Community households prioritizing vulnerable women and youth in the target population.• Provide seasonally appropriate and livelihood specific agricultural inputs such as certified quality seeds, Farm tools, training, land preparation and irrigation support 100 households in Lower Juba and Nugaal region.• Training of the Project staff and local authority representatives with at least 40% representation of female on standard program implementation, Monitoring and evaluation and project objectives.• Monitoring progress of implementation of unconditional and cash for work activities

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project seeks to provide immediate access to food and to help those affected to recover from the impact of the drought through livelihood support and restoration of productive community assets. Southern Aid seeks to utilise the cash for work mechanism to rehabilitate community production assets.The Organisation will further will include a training aspect targeting the beneficiary communities under the proposed cash based intervention approach so as to ensure market sustainability and also include a nutrition awareness training, this will help to enhance market sustainability and diversity of food consumption at household level. Southern Aid will engage beneficiaries during selection of resilience based activities in order to ensure that communal structures are prioritised to ensure sustainability and continued community ownership of the structures after rehabilitation. Further the Organisation will train community project committees to oversee the sustainability of the communal projects after the end of the project period.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risks facing the displaced population is resource based marginalisation and conflict with the host community and the project has factored the possible risks in its targeting criteria that the displaced population will be targeted with a host community population. The Organization further anticipates that there is risk of security in some of the project locations and to avoid the risk of exclusion, Southern Aid will do an assessment of the security situation to establish the feasibility of operation. During the formation and community entry process Adeso will ensure members from the minority and displaced person will be part of the village committees that are task to facilitate project beneficiary selection. Further, Southern Aid will use the FSC Protection Mainstreaming checklist as a technical guidance. This seeks to ensure that interventions are done in a non- discriminatory and impartial ways that promote the safety and the dignity of the target population.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Southern Aid appreciates and considers the significance of gender mainstreaming in its program design. Gender mainstreaming is prioritised in the entire project cycle.In a bid to ensure attention to priority cross cutting issues (sex, age, disability and health), the project will take into consideration gender mainstreaming and will give priority to women led households, households with children aged below five years, older persons,the disabled and those with sick persons). The daily work load will be reduced to encourage women participation. The project will ensure the significant representation of either sex in project committees. The elderly and the minorities will be considered in targeting and enlisting of the beneficiaries.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Southern Aid has a long established presence in the regions and a good understanding of the context. The Organisation appreciates the context of operation and thus established a good rapport and direct relations with the local elders and the community to promote humanitarian principles, gain access and ensure the effective implementation of the program. The organisation is also cognisant of the clan/sub clan dynamics of the area and considers this in the implementation process. This measures strengthen the local acceptance and protection of the organisation and its activities by the communities and the the local authorities.The organisation further analysed the security situation the target areas and it has been relatively calm, although there has been recent sporadic attacks in the some of the localities. No prohibitive security challenge is foreseen at this stage of the project given that the organisation has other ongoing activities in the area and they remain accessible. However, the organisation will continue to monitor any change of the situation and will update the donor and adjust accordingly.
