Project Summary

This project builds on INTERSOS’ existing activities and geographical coverage in Syria to support livelihood restoration and economic empowerment in Hama Governorate and build the resilience of vulnerable individuals, with a particular focus on youth and women from IDPs, returnees and host communities. In alignment with HRP objectives, the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor (MoSAL) national strategy as well as the Early Recovery and Livelihood sectors priorities, this intervention has been designed under the SHF Standard Allocation Strategy 2020 with the aim to improve and expand access to livelihood and income generating opportunities for the local population. In particular, INTERSOS intended to contribute to the 2 main pillars of the SHF Standard Allocation Strategy 2020, notably (1) address humanitarian needs in underserved areas, as based on the severity of needs and the current humanitarian response, (2) support mitigation of the socio-economic impacts stemming from economic and financial crisis in addition to restrictive measures to prevent COVID-19. Hence, the action encompasses the following key components: 1. a thorough Labor Market Assessment (LMA) directed at the analysis of demand and supply sides of the local labor market 2. provision of technical and vocational trainings in the construction sector to build and strengthen beneficiaries’ skills in the following domains: electricity, plumbing, painting, carpentry 3. delivery of business and marketing skills workshops to transfer critical skills in communication and marketing to better connect with customers, promote local products and boost business activities, 4. provision of start-up kits to trainees to foster self-employment and broaden income generating activities 5. support to micro and small enterprises in the agri-business sector (olive oil, diary production, beekeeping) by provision of assets which are necessary to increase production. As part of the action, INTERSOS staff and SARC volunteers are provided with briefing sessions on core concepts and policies related to humanitarian principles, Child Protection and PSEA. Moreover, awareness-raising sessions are delivered on hazards, risks, preventive and mitigation measures related to the COVID-19 pandemic. This intervention has been developed and is implemented with the Livelihoods Department of the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) and in cooperation with SARC Hama Branch. INTERSOS coordinates with the ERL Sector at the national level and sub-sector covering the Hama Governorate alongside with other INGOs present in the same areas to ensure optimization of resources, high complementarity and enhanced overall impact.

4 - Needs

Ten years of crisis in Syria have eroded the socio-economic foundations of the country, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, soaring inflation, and increased enforcement of international sanctions. Needs in the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector are considerable with an estimated 11.6 million people seeking for dignified early recovery and livelihood support. Over 90% of the population are living under the poverty line and COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated an already dire situation. In 2021, the number of PiN in Hama Governorate has reached 0.8 M (more the 50% of the overall population) with an increase of 80% of people in need compared to 2020 (2021 HNO, 2021 Needs and Response Summary; UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment; HNAP). Besides secondary data analysis, INTERSOS relies on a set of different analyses: findings from needs assessments conducted by SARC Hama Branch in prioritized areas; ad-hoc consultations with SARC Livelihood Department; Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) conducted in January 2020 with a follow-up in early July 2020 in Karnaz, Muaradah, Oqeirbat and As Suqaylabiyah in the Hama Governorate; 2021 LMA in Suran and Muardeh sub-districts, whose findings shed the light on soaring poverty, food insecurity and lack of income-generation opportunities. Higher protection risks have been detected (child labor, school drop-outs) and negative copying mechanisms increasingly adopted by the local population. The project addresses needs by strengthening vocation and technical skills in the construction sector with start-up kits to foster self-employment, providing productive assets in the agri-business sector and enhancing business skills and market linkages.

5 - Implementation of activities

Preliminary briefing and awareness-raising sessions on Protection principles, CP and PSEA; risks, preventive and mitigation measures related to the COVID-19. MoV training reports. Labor Market Assessment (LMA): appraisal of supply - demand of the local economic ecosystem to identify stakeholders, constraints and main drivers. I: # LMA Report (MoV: LMA Toolkit, KII, FGDs, Final Report). Delivery of tailored vocational and technical training for unskilled and semi-skilled vulnerable beneficiaries (specific focus on youth at risk in underserved areas) in the construction sector in the following domains: electricity, plumbing, painting, carpentry. Indicator: # beneficiaries provided with VT trainings (MoV: Training Needs Assessment Report, Attendance, Pre-post test, Satisfaction Surveys). Provision of business and marketing skills workshops: 1-day workshops to provide self-employed and micro-entrepreneurs with critical skills in communication and marketing. I: # beneficiaries provided with business coaching (MoV: Workshop reports, Attendance, Pre-post test, Satisfaction Surveys). Distribution of start-up kits to foster self-employment: VT Trainees receive start-up kits to initiate self-employment initiatives in relevant sub-sectors of construction. I: # beneficiaries supported to start their income-generation (MoV: Delivery forms signed by start-up kits recipients, Self-employment initiatives periodic reports; Monitoring rep). Support to rural enterprises and entrepreneurs: provision of productive assets to promising rural micro-enterprises of the agri-business sector (olive oil, honey and dairy production). I: # of rural enterprises provided with productive assets (MoV: enterprises scorecards, monitoring rep).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1: Risk of discrimination, perceptions of discrimination/exclusion of vulnerable groups, especially women and girls, employment/productive sectors usually dominated by male. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Comprehensive consultations are undertaken with communities, in a way to tailored and adapt the intervention according to needs (achieved through the LMA exercise); Productive/employment sectors are selected in a way to ensure participation of all target groups; c) Adopt a lean and effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms. 2: Exclusion (physical access) of People with Disabilities (PwDs) due to poor infrastructure or lack of facilitation. Likelihood: High; Impact: High. Mitigation: selection of training venues with available access for PwDs; Consultations and engagement of PwDs throughout the implementation with regular monitoring visits; awareness-raising and briefings for SARC staff and trainers on measures to be taken to properly address PwDs needs. 3: Increase of tensions between Returnees and HC due to unequal provision of assistance by the project. Likelihood: Low-medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Secure that a thorough mapping of affected communities and conflict-sensitivity analysis are performed at the onset of the project; Consultations and transparent communications with both groups to foster awareness, cooperation and social cohesion; Opportunities for assistance are distributed evenly and according to severity of needs. 4: Exposure or exploitation of Child Labor. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure proper selection of beneficiaries and regular monitoring; Inception trainings to SARC staff on CP; Inform local communities about core protection principles.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risks: Staff recruited has prior conviction for SEA or related offence; Implementing partner, staff member, suppliers, (etc.) do not comply/accept PSEA policy; SEA perpetrated by staff members/service providers; reinforced stereotype of beneficiaries through wrong communication and images. Mitigation: job opening state that INTERSOS has PSEA and CP Policies that all staff is expected to comply with; tests include the Attitude Scale; assessment about the partner’s commitment and capacity to prevent and respond to SEA is conducted; where possible, training are offered to partners to identify SEA risks and define related SEA mitigation measures throughout programming; SEA risk analyses and mitigation strategies are elaborated at the country level: categorization of interventions, implementation modalities by risk significance level, structure to report, investigation structure; “Do No Harm” principle guide the collection and use of images and information about beneficiaries; images and stories only with the full understanding and permission of the subject. The informed consent needs to be signed and filed in a secure location; all staff and affiliated workforce must read, understand and sign the INTERSOS PSEA/CP Policy Declaration prior to contract start date; all staff must receive the PSEA/CP Policy induction training; all staff must receive one PSEA/CP Refresher training per year; awareness raising sessions are to be conducted with communities and other actors by PSEA FPs.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

INTERSOS Syria has developed a Contingency Planning Process (CPP) which foresees risk analysis and related mitigation measures: Reputational Risk: unable to deliver on-time, breach of sanctions and/or do not harm principle. Procurement protocols are in place and into force, thorough market research and due diligence exercises are conducted for verification of quality standards and legal requirements, strong communication, reporting mechanisms and robust monitoring strategy are followed; Security and Access: unable to reach and implement in targeted areas. INTERSOS relies on security assessment and protocols, combined with SARC guidance, supervision and networks in the field. GBV and PSEA: risk of reinforcing gender stereotypes by excluding women from participation; risk of PSEA for beneficiaries. Gender-sensitive and inclusive approach is mainstreamed; complaint and feedback mechanisms in place, mandatory PSEA training for staff and implementing partner; Financial Risk and Accountability: exchange rates fluctuations affect achievement of targets; lack of transparency; diversion or misuse of project financial resources. INTERSOS relies on a very strong internal system and SOPs to secure compliance and accountability: careful, systematic and rigorous monitoring both of project activities and project spending is carried out; financial controlling rules and processes are in place; regular communication with the donor in case of economic shock and re-alignment of targets.Health Risks linked to the COVID-19 pandemic: staff and beneficiaries might be exposed to health hazards and risks of contagion while participating in project activities. INTERSOS takes into account precautionary measures related to COVID 19.
