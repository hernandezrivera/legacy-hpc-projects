Needs

Enduring almost eight years of convict and violence in the country, Syria has experienced a significant internal displacement that left more than quarter of the population in dire need for humanitarian assistance. This expansion reflects a continued decline in Syrians’ overall levels of resilience, and the persistent barriers to addressing these needs. According to REACH assessment released in August 2018 in several subdistricts, with the raise of 18.8% of the shelter severity form last year’s, in Northern Aleppo, and Idlib Governorate and Surrounding areas, a percentage of 24% and 47% respectively, of the assessed households in those two locations reported shelter adequacy issues, and almost 80% of those needing to make repairs in both areas were unable to do so. This actuallysuggests that most of the IDPs are facing severe limitations to live in decent living conditions and are bound to live in damaged houses or unfinished buildings. In terms ofavailability of shelter materials and skills necessary to construct / repair shelters, the assessment revealed that most of the materials are available in assessed sub-districtshowever, in Idlib, 37% of households living in damaged shelter cannot access these available shelter materials and skills mainly due to financial constraints. This findingsuggests that unless the population in need is provided with sustainable means of economic activity, only then would they be able to acquire the needed shelter materials. Otherwise, in the current uncertain scenario, they will continue to rely on humanitarian assistance in order to rebuild their homes. women, girls, elderly and people with special needs are highly considered through effective architectural simple solutions. 4.4 million people require NFI assistance, a 6 per cent decline from the 2018 HNO, and the second year in a row that the total number of people in need of NFI assistance has reduced.However, within the situation in some areas, such as the northwest, is in fact deteriorating amidst and increase in people in need. The overall decrease in people in need reflectssome incremental improvements in market functionality in some areas, specifically where hostilities are subsiding, and which are now witnessing increased stability. Access to NFIshas generally become more stable over the last year, with 14 per cent of communities reporting a deteriorating situation, compared with 24 per cent last year. However, needsremain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary andseasonal assistance, as well as longer-term needs, among those communities requiring resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance theability of families to withstand future shocks and stresses. Relatedly, findings show a decreased need for standard/core NFI items and a significant increase in requests foritems specific to the situation faced by certain communities or population groups. Such items include clothing, heating fuel and heaters. Different gender and age needs includingwomen children elderly and people with special needs will be taking in consideration while designing the project. In 2019, these needs will continue to compromise the ability of NFIpartners to tailor responses based on the intersection of individual needs and vulnerabilities with the localized capacity of markets and supply chains.

Implementation of activities

NFI (Winterization) interventions will carry out through: Act.1.1 and Act.2 1. Survey of the needs in areas in Northern Syria regarding NFI. Coordinating with active partners on theground. 2. Situation analysis which includes but not limited to women’s cultural practices,cooking practices, specific clothing or NFI etc. 3. Procurement and selection of needed items, based on not only needs but also age, gender and diversity. 4. Before distribution, Registration of all targeted households. And cross checking with available data by the sector (Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) data; gaps analysis; priority location mapping and results of multi-sectorial assessments / RRM) and/or by collecting information from different partners. 5. Distribution, Coordinate with head of Community/IDP representative and insure that the distributing team have all their essential equipment for distribution (safety equip. First aid kits, drinking water etc.) 6. Post distribution monitoring, a strong mechanism to assess the extent to which these distributed ES-NFI items are according to the expectations of the beneficiaries and/orhave being properly used or not. The Findings will be indicative of errors throughout distribution process, and/or some IDPs are selling or exchanging the items to obtain essential items that have not been distributed. Shelter implementations will carry out hrough:Act 2.1: 1. Survey of the existing population in relatively safer areas in Northern Syria for their residential needs, 2.Finalization of houses and shelters to be rehabilitated and renovated; Finalization of households based on the selection criteria and signing of MOU. 3.Development, verification and approval of the BOQs for rehabilitation and renovation, insuring durable shelter solutions, including assistance with community/public infrastructure and facilities, women, girls, elderly and people with special needs are highly considered through effective architectural simple solutions. 4.Tendering and Finalization of contractor, contracts signed off, specifications of therehabilitation work Finalized with contractors, quality criteria agreed, documented and signed off. 5. Start of rehabilitation work, completion and handing over of the houses to the beneficiaries. HLP (Shelter): in addition to the main shelter activities, due diligence guidelines will be integrated throughout the shelter program cycle. and will fulfill all the guidelines milestones checklist before, during and after the implementation and that includes: Planning and before implementation: - identify local HLP actors - establishing feasibility of intervention in local HLP context - establish a relationship with community members and relevant stakeholders - establish HLP eligibility and exclusion criteria - ownership verification - sign agreements by all relevant parties. During implementation: - establish a monitoring system for HLP issues - have referral system in place for when HLP issues arise Evaluation: - establish a feedback mechanism - HLP relevant questions integrated into post-implementation monitoring.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Access restrictions to geographic areas due to active fighting and/or shifting of front lines would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to theaffected population Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: 1- Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground 2- Develop contingency planning 3- Set-up multiple backup distribution centers 4- Liaison with the local authorities 5- Explore different distribution modalities 6- Elaborate different plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible.2- Closure of the border for commercial shipments or not renewal of SC would have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities for both NGOs and UN agencies as it would prevent the transshipment of the NFIs across the border from Turkey into Syria Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: 1- Advocate with Turkishauthorities to keep the border open 2- Explore alternative supply lines through localprocurement 3- Preposition items through UN shipment inside Syria 4- Provide logistics capacity.3- Poor coordination of type of aid delivered among agencies might lead to creation of double standards and different quality/quantity of NFIs distributed to neighboring communities (or even among the same community to different groups). This in turn might lead to inter-communities’ tension and trigger populations movements to 'better served' areasLikelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: 1- Constantly liaise with the SNFI Cluster, submit the 4Ws and planned activities report, attend the Cluster meetingand seek Cluster support prior to the implementation of the activities 2- Harmonize the assistance delivered as per SNFI Cluster instructions and SNFI guidance 3- coordinate with local councils in the targeted areas as well as being active in field coordination groups. 4- Indirect purchase of conflict associated resources (fuel) might involuntary contribute to fighting parties Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: 1- Monitor the lines of supply of the fuel and track the source down and ensure fuel is not procured from parties involved in the conflict 2- Explore ecofriendlier and conflict-free available fuel or hear resource types 3- Consider where possible fuel resources that are not harmful to thehealth of beneficiaries .5- Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from propertyowner and/or exposing bene􀂡ciaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: 1- Abide by the HLPguidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster 2- Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLPDD 3- Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed 4- Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form.6- Risk of overcrowding living conditions and absence of adequate services might expose to risk vulnerable beneficiaries Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation: 1- Ensure that adequate space is provided by family 2- Monitor the Shelter living conditions periodically after the completion of the rehabilitation 3- Raise awareness among the beneficiaries of the dangers of overcrowding 4- Ensure that management system is in place for big collective shelters rehabilitation 5- Consider sphere standards when planning the response 6- Ensure that management are coordinating services with other actors to cover needs and gaps. In addition to it, Do No Harm Principle and the humanitarian principles will be effectively implemented in beneficiary selection and in all the project proceedings. Core humanitarian standards will be followed for response wide effectiveness.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations.Mitigation: Experience from previous operations found that while conflict occurred, a cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Further, the project design flexibly targets at a unit level of “sub district.” By using this target instead of specific Communities, the project can respond district-wide, wherever non-conflict areas with access allow. The project design is based on the assumption of unknown displacement patterns and a highly fluid context, minimizing interventions from static locations.Risk 2: Current controlling entity or local council no longer supports QC activities or directly interferes with activities threatening humanitarian imperativeMitigation: staff/partners keep in close contact with the various local councils in order to be able to address any issues at an early stage. QC is transparent throughout all actions and communications with all stakeholders to ensure our status as impartial, neutral, and independent is unquestionable. QC will suspend operations in one location while continuing in another, until such time as delivery of aid is assured to be unimpeded.Risk 3: bad weather conditions.Mitigation: By starting all Shelter rehabilitation activities during spring until the end of summer to avoid bad weather conditions which will result in delay on rehabilitation works, and cut access to Targeted locations.
