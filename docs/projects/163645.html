Project Summary

Recurrent fighting in Libya since 2011 has led to widespread contamination from explosive hazards, often in urban, densely populated areas. Even when active hostilities come to an end, the dangerous contamination continues to pose a considerable threat to civilians, impedes the return of displaced persons, and significantly impacts the ability of humanitarian and development actors to implement activities towards early recovery and stabilization. The outbreak of hostilities in 2019, which was concentrated on, but not limited to, the greater Tripoli area, has only exacerbated the threat from explosive hazards.
The Libyan national capacity to mitigate the threat through clearance activities, Risk Education (RE), and Victim Assistance (VA) interventions is insufficient in comparison to the growing threat to the population. With the existing managerial and coordination capacity in place, governmental and non-governmental actors have a solid base for programme growth, yet are lacking qualified personnel, equipment, and technical expertise to scale up to meet the demand. This project includes an Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) component, as well as three components that focus predominantly on the enhancement of technical skills and physical capacity of established local actors.

1 - Needs

The threat from explosive hazards has been exacerbated by the outbreak of hostilities in 2019, which has been focused on, but not limited to, the capital Tripoli. As all parties to the conflict are increasingly relying on the use of explosive and heavy weaponry in densely populated areas, often in a disproportionate and indiscriminate manner, reports of civilians injured, maimed, and killed by explosive hazards are increasing. Three distinct needs for Humanitarian Mine Action (HMA) response can be identified: 1) Removal of explosive hazards: Given the scope of the explosive hazard threat, there is an urgent need for permanent removal of explosive hazards. Similarly, there is an urgent need for qualified national EOD teams in Tripoli and other areas affected by the conflict. This is particularly pertinent in the ongoing crisis as national and international HMA non-governmental actors are facing challenges in acquiring authorization to conduct EOD tasks in the current security environment. While the ability of national security institutions to operate is less restricted, their EOD capacity to assist communities is hampered by a lack of adequately trained and equipped personnel. 2) Increased awareness of the risks of explosive weapons: Residents of all areas affected by the conflict will likely be faced with explosive hazard contamination. Regardless of how long the fighting continues and of emergency clearance efforts, the risk of death and injuries from explosive hazards may likely persist for years to come. Risk Education (RE) aims to minimize the impact of explosive hazard contamination by providing information, raising awareness, and building capacity of affected communities. 3) VA: Improved access to services to assist victims of explosive weapons: War-related injuries caused by explosive hazards and Small Arms and Light Weapons (SALW) often result in impairment, life-long disability, and psychological trauma. Explosive hazard and SALW survivors and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) are amongst the most vulnerable groups affected by the conflict, especially when access to adequate emergency health care response, and comprehensive rehabilitation, both physical and psycho-social, is hindered by the conflict, and the access to, and availability of services is limited.

2 - Activities

In relation to the needs outlined above, UNMAS proposes three areas of activities: 1) Explosive Ordnance Disposal: - EOD spot task operations in priority areas: Conduct spot task clearance activities in accessible high priority areas that are identified in close coordination with the Libyan Mine Action Centre (LibMAC) to reduce the threat of explosive hazards, to facilitate the safe return of displaced populations, and to enable the safe implementation of humanitarian, early recovery, and reconstruction efforts. -Building of sustainable Libyan national EOD capacity: The National Safety Authority has been, and continues to be, the only civilian entity able to conduct EOD operations in and around Tripoli since the conflict started in April 2019. However, their ability to provide EOD response to assist communities is hampered by a lack of adequately trained and equipped personnel. The proposed project will provide EOD and emergency medical response training for 12 EOD operators, and will supply the trained teams with appropriate equipment. 2) Explosive Hazard Risk Education for at-risk populations:UNMAS proposes to support lifesaving RE activities targeting at-risk-populations through capacity building; public information dissemination; education and training sessions; and community liaison outreach. 3) Victim Assistance: UNMAS, in close coordination with the LibMAC, the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Social Affairs, and other relevant stakeholders, proposes to develop and support an emergency referral system in order to improve the VA response across multiple sectors. Concretely, the referral system will help to better identify explosive hazard/SALW survivors, and PwDs; help facilitate their access to needed health, rehabilitation, and mental health and psycho-social support services; improve information management and needs analysis; and improve the coordination between key service providers and other stakeholders.

4 - Protection risk analysis

The main protection risks are: (1) Safety and security risks for implementing partner staff operating in conflict areas; (2) security threat to implementing partners due to association with the United Nations; (3) presence of mine/ERW survey or clearance teams in vulnerable mine-affected communities could potentially be a catalyst for sexual exploitation or abuse of women and children at risk in the local population.

5 - Mitigation measures

(1) UNMAS Libya will monitor the security situation on the ground and coordinate accordingly with the selected national Mine Action organisation. Mine Action clearance will be conducted depending on the evolving security situation. (2) UNMAS supports the risk assessment of partner organisations, who often have a good understanding of the local political/security situations of the communities they operate in. In addition, UNMAS encourages partners to adopt a low profile, especially when operating in sensitive contexts. (3) UNMAS will make sure contractors or implementing partners adhere to the relevant Codes of Conduct and will apply a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Accountability to affected populations will be integrated during project design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. Risk Education activities, by definition, require close personal interaction with the affected population. Information and preferences communicated by local residents, both men and women, will be taken into account when prioritizing and targeting areas for clearance. Clearance will be determined and carried out in close consultation with local authorities, local communities and the LibMAC. Emergency hotline numbers will be communicated to local populations during the Risk Education sessions so any reports or explosive items can be reported to local organisations and the LibMAC. Quality Management visits will be carried out by UNMAS Libya to ensure that the interventions comply with International Mine Action Standards and the Gender guidelines for Mine Action Programmes and are in line with PSEA policies and the United Nations Code of Conduct. Any training activities conducted or supported by UNMAS include a training evaluation exercise in which trainees can provide anonymised feedback to the trainers, implementing partner and UNMAS.
