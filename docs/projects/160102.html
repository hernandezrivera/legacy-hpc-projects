Project Summary

Terre des hommes (Tdh) is the leading Swiss child relief organization, and has been operating in Juba PoCs since 2014, and in Yei River State since 2016, with extension of its education and child protection services to rural Yei (Mugwo and Otogo counties) since 2019. It works to improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable children in an effective and sustainable manner, by providing direct child protection services aiming at preventing and responding to cases of abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children; as well as by strengthening the resilience of children and communities through a holistic and child-centred approach, considering children’s intersectoral needs in protection, education and WASH. Tdh has embraced a holistic approach to programming whereby an integrated, multi-sectoral program aims to improve outcomes for children and to contribute to the improvement of the broader child protection outcomes, including a focus on improving children’s sense of security and self-confidence. Tdh’s integrated approach links CP interventions with its WASH and education interventions. WASH/CP integration will be achieved by increasing sustainable use of protection sensitive WASH services such as water supply and latrines, alongside comprehensive hygiene promotion and ensuring appropriate, sustainable WASH in Schools, with the aim of ultimately leading towards improved child protection outcomes: children are able to live and learn in a clean and safe environment. The Tdh CP approach will prioritize rigor in assessments and ensuring that programs are directly addressing the specific needs of the targeted beneficiaries. In addition to implementing direct child protection activities, including case management and psychosocial support for specific vulnerabilities among children, particularly UASC and CAAFAG; Tdh will also ensure that CP is mainstreamed in all of its activities, including training other actors on CP concepts.
In 2020, Tdh will continue to provide direct child protection services to children and to enhance community-based child protection mechanisms. With the objective to handover activities throughout 2020, Tdh will aim to progressively decrease its presence in Juba PoCs and transition to urban response. On the other hand, Tdh will maintain a strong presence in Yei town and will seek to increase its presence in rural Yei, through 2 mobile teams providing comprehensive case management services for vulnerable children.
As one of the principal CP actors in South Sudan, Tdh is noted for its commitment to effective coordination and communication within the sector. Tdh will continue to coordinate with all CP and Education actors first to ensure a comprehensive understanding of the services available in the targeted areas, and second to avoid any overlap of locations or activities. Tdh shares the experiences related to its projects through the Protection Cluster, the CPSC, the PSS Working Group (WG), the UASC WG, the CAAFAG WG, the MHPSS Technical WG and the GBV Child Survivors WG at national level. Tdh is also a member of the CM Task Force and co-chair of the CPWG at Jubek State with the Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Welfare. Through these coordination mechanisms, a strong and effective coordination is in place which ensures information-sharing and effective collaboration among different service providers. Tdh actively participates in 4 clusters in Yei town as the CPSC, WASH and Education clusters. Tdh also works closely with the MoGEI and MoGCSW at county and state level to ensure that national guidelines and strategies are considered during implementation. Given its longstanding presence in Yei River State and Juba PoCs 1 and 3, Tdh benefits from favorable acceptance from the communities and will continue to collaborate with all relevant actors, including through joint needs assessment missions, to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication.

1 - Needs

The breakout of violence in South Sudan since 2013 led to massive displacements, disrupted livelihoods and increased food insecurity. Children have been particularly affected, and account for 60% of the population in need. Girls and boys face continued threats of forced displacement, recruitment into armed forces and armed groups, neglect, abuse, abduction, violence, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), child and forced marriage and child labour. Young women and adolescent girls are particularly at risk of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). From November 2018 to September 2019, Tdh provided comprehensive case management services to 273 vulnerable children in Juba PoCs (124 boys; 167 girls), including 47 UASC; and to 386 vulnerable children in Yei (177 boys; 196 girls), including 114 UASC. More than half of the cases managed by Tdh in Juba PoCs during the period were related to child neglect, while nearly one fifth of the vulnerable children considered under case management demonstrated signs of psychosocial distress. 1 case of child marriage and 9 cases of child abuse were also reported. Neglect was also the first protection concern in Yei town, although Tdh recorded higher numbers in the prevalence of child labour and harmful child labour, rates of children heads of household; as well as girls and adolescent girls victims of sexual violence, sexual exploitation, and child marriage in Yei town over the same period. The prevalence of GBV in Yei persists at high levels, both connected to conflict related violations - reported and detected by UNMISS Human Rights and detailed in the 2019 report, but also evident through numbers of complaints picked up by the CBFM, which led to a joined risk assessment to the area as the incidence seemed to be higher than average. Yei and the surrounding areas rove to be extremely challenging for work with GBV cases, and child survivors. Under-reporting, not solely of conflict related issues but also of endemic practices that amount to GBV particularly around forced and early marriage, is prevalent . Tdh has engaged with the MoGCSW to commit to the roll out and support on legislation and necessary awareness raising to support local enforcement of prohibition of underage marriage. Tdh has also adhered to the Child Survivors TWG in hope of extending its technical expertise and analyzing its intervention around this intersection. PSEA, Child Safeguarding issues have been prioritized to the forefront both in terms of internal capacity building and from a dire need of extending awareness raising to the general population, key stakeholders, our CBCNs, Schools and Incentivized teachers, PTAs and women groups. The psychosocial wellbeing of IDP children is also worrying. In June 2019, Tdh conducted an outreach household (HH) needs assessment in the nine zones of Yei town. 682 households were surveyed, including 161 (24%) IDP households who arrived in Yei town in 2018/2019. 64% of the households surveyed declared that children did not have access to a safe place to play. During the same period, Tdh provided PSS to 3,490 children and adolescents in Yei town, and recently started implementing PSS activities in rural Yei through 3 CFS, with an average of 112 children registered in each. In Juba PoCs, Tdh maintained PSS activities for 3,490 registered children and youth (1,559 boys; 1,931 girls) in 2019. Although the current case management caseload does not demonstrate significant increases in the number of CAFAAG (mostly case-by-case release and/informal releases and departures), Tdh is planning to strengthen its 2020 CP programming to respond to a possible release of CAFAAG through Comprehensive Case Management, strengthening referral pathways and PSS support and youth programming (in conjunction with its education actions) to ensure that CAFAAG are duly served. This is of particular importance considering the 3 cantonment sites and numerous army barracks in the outskirts of Yei town.

2 - Activities

In 2020, Tdh will continue to provide child-centered protection and specialized services to vulnerable children in Juba PoCs 1 and 3, in Yei town and hard to reach areas outside Yei. In Juba PoCs 1 and 3, Tdh will: - Provide comprehensive and gender-sensitive case management and referral services to 300 vulnerable children (180 girls; 120 boys), including identification, documentation and family tracing and reunification for 60 UASC (35 girls; 25 boys). - Continue the provision of focused and non-focused psychosocial support services to 2,000 children and youth (1,100 F; 900 M) through 3 CFS and PSGs during the first half of 2020. - Tdh will Identify, screen, and train an NNGO/CBO to takeover PSS activities during the second half of 2020. The identified NNGO/CBO will be provided with technical and thematic support to develop/adapt PSS curriculum following Tdh MHPSS approach to PSS group activities. Training will also be provided in CPiE, CPMS, and possibly in technical components such as project management and fundraising, according to identified needs. - Identification of possible solutions for urban programming on CPiE, including provision of PSS and alternative care solutions for highly vulnerable UASC, will be conducted. In Yei town, Tdh will: - Provide comprehensive and gender-sensitive case management and referral services to 400 vulnerable children (200 girls; 200 boys), including identification, documentation and family tracing and reunification for 150 UASC (75 girls; 75 boys). - Provide focused and non-focused psychosocial support services to 3,000 children and youth (1,650 F; 1,350 M) in Yei town through 5 CFS, 4 WGFS and PSGs. In rural Yei, Tdh will: - Provide comprehensive and gender-sensitive case management and referral to 100 vulnerable children (50 girls; 50 boys) through 2 mobile CP teams and 6 help desks established in schools. - Provide focused and non-focused psychosocial support to 450 children and youth (225F; 225M) through 3 CFS. Tdh will also contribute to the prevention and mitigation of protection risks through enhance preparedness and resilience of community-based child protection mechanisms and will: - Strengthen community-based protection preparedness and risk mitigation through training of 500 women and men on basic CPiE; including 400 key community members (150F, 250M) trained on referral pathways and prevention messages. - Provide training and/or refreshers training to 45 Tdh frontline staff in Juba PoCs, Yei town and mobile clinics in rural Yei, on CPiE, including case management and referral pathways and/or PSS, as well as on PSEA, GBV mainstreaming. - Strengthen foster care networks and continue identification, screening, training and monitoring of 150 foster caregivers (100 women; 50 men). Tdh will assess whether to provide small multi-purpose cash or vocational support to vulnerable foster carers. - Conduct age-, context- and gender-specific awareness raising on child protection issues and community means to prevent protection risks to 5,000 individuals Juba PoCs (3,000F, 2,000M); 10,000 individuals in Yei town (5,000F, 5,000M) and 1,000 individuals in rural Yei (500F; 500M). - Support 31 child protection committees/networks, including 13 CBCPN (2 in Juba PoCs, 9 in Yei town, 2 in rural Yei) and 18 parent-support groups (8 in Juba PoCs, 8 in Yei town, 2 in rural Yei). Tdh will continue to coordinate with the MoGCSW at Jubek and Yei River States level, in order to build capacity of frontline and technical staff. As part of a broader multi-year planning, Tdh will: - conduct an assessment of the capacity of states’ MoGCSW and will map-out training needs and available resources; - lead, as co-chair of Jubek and Yei River state child protection working groups, an updated mapping of community-based child protection networks. - Support in the roll-out of a written SOPs for the identification, registration and alternative care processes for UASC within Yei town.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Tdh does not provide legal recognition as an actor and operating upon a basis of need and vulnerability criteria will work for service provision to all identified within its capacity. Clarification will need to be required on legal entitlement and the impact of formalisation by the government of foster care networks but this can take place throughout 2020 and the subsequent year.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Focus group discussions are vital for the implementation of protection activities, these are therefore carried out across various stakeholders, and thus ensure that displacement affected communities, whether host or IDP can access services which they have contributed to develop. Development and improvement occur through regular and ongoing consultation. Tdh has extended its consultation group by extending to un-serviced rural locations and thus integrates broader perspectives into its implementing strategy.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Tdh focuses on enhancement of the Protection environment through direct engagement with the surrounding context, key stakeholders including authorities, community leaders, religious leaders, community elders, CBOs and more. These are engaged primarily through awareness raising and the establishment of Community Based Child Protection Networks. Training the latter allows for a broader understanding of Protection issues, particularly CP matters. Their enhanced involvement, especially in locations which are under-serviced, such as rural Yei, is vital for the identification and referral of vulnerable cases. While currently the receiver of referrals is Tdh, the same system would work upon handover of the mechanisms put in place to the state, thus catering for a long-term vision that falls within the durable solutions framework. Enhanced community capacity is also complemented through the set-up of foster care networks. High numbers of UASC will continue to be identified as the country transitions and as the impact of conflict continues to be felt. Thanks to already existing efforts in set up support and training of foster carers, Tdh will feed sustainable solutions for alternative care. While in Yei this is already done with the authorities in view of a future formal recognition and handover to the state, Tdh seeks to get involved in extending this activity in Juba too. Lastly elements of support and regular work with the State Ministries effectively contributes to long term solutions and a broadening of the protection environment to the authorities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project focuses on CP and for this matter will look primarily towards CP-related frameworks namely: - South Sudan Child Act No. 10 of 2008; - South Sudan Strategic National Action Plan on the Prevention of Child Marriage 2017-2030. In addition, capacity building activities will always be done further to bilateral consultation with state authorities, notably the MoGCSW, to ensure alignment with their specific objectives. Furthermore, the project is aligned with the National Refugee Return Framework (aware of its redrafting) in view of its recognition of the possibility of supporting returnees as a beneficiary group due to non-discrimination on the community of origin of children. Vulnerability criteria are applied across host and hosted alike, refugees, returnees would be catered to accordingly.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

In Juba, Tdh feels that it needs to start to pave the way for extending parallel CP services outside the PoC throughout 2020 in a transitionary manner. This is why it is looking at multi-year planning. As for Yei, while there is a constant growth in cautious optimism in the country, Tdh is well aware of the sheer volatility and proximity of conflict. The state is a peculiar and yet literal representation of the humanitarian development nexus. While there is increased stability and access in certain locations, the population remains vigilant due to ongoing clashes outside the town between non-signatories and government forces. Tdh is of the opinion that the programming it rolls out needs to straddle the reality, anticipate for future implementation and yet maintain its capacity to respond to shocks be they brief or small, or protracted and of a larger scale. The outlook changed drastically throughout 2019, particularly around Yei where, as of April, increased movement was a possibility - this has allowed for extended remit of planning and a notably palpable sense of stability in certain locations. The patchwork of the area outside Yei town nevertheless proves to be tedious and open to stark and sudden changes, or slow onset of renewed conflict should all collapse. Tdh has sufficient reason to believe that the Protection environment has the potential to be safer and more stable.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project fits within a broader multi-year planning around Child Protection activities which will operate across three measures. Tdh will seek to ensure financial and programmatic sustainability for all package measures, through: Measure 1 – capacity building of formal and informal child protection mechanisms, in particular for the State offices of MoGCSW to increase its capacity as coordinator for case management and referrals; as well as CBCPN and community leaders to effectively refer cases and address protection concerns. Tdh will provide technical and thematic training to enhance the capacities of these actors, in view of handing over case management and FTR. This is planned over multi-year programmatic planning and is not specific to the project herein alone. Measure 2 – PSS. Tdh under ongoing funding with the German government is working to identify possible partner/handover NNGO/CBO to train them in the provision of PSS as well as on specific technical needs that may be identified, such as project management, fundraising, etc. Its ongoing intervention is geared towards partial handover in a phased manner, of PSS in CFS in Juba PoC. Tdh is also focusing on CFS in Yei, to be handed over to CBOs while it phases into a role of protection monitoring and lead in case management.Measure 3 – A strong focus of the multi-year planning is focused on alternative care. Tdh will establish/strengthen foster care networks and seek official recognition from the state authorities, so to ensure that future screening, training and monitoring is provided by the MoGCSW.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Few specific risks have been selected since Tdh attempts to plan its intervention aiming to minimise the multitude of risks above through varied consultation with multiple actors. Nevertheless, particularly in the context of Yei matters related to high incidence of GBV, child safeguarding and its impact on the communities in and outside town is extensive (as can be corroborated by a recent PSEA task force risk assessment exercise). Due to a limited presence of actors in town, limited access to services and possibility of redress, prevailing cultural norms and social and anthropological factors the impact of GBV and child safeguarding risks is high for both the population of concern, beneficiaries and national staff of Tdh (and other organisations). This has led the organisation to prioritise Gender and Diversity mainstreamining components across the whole country program thanks to direct support from Headquarters. By putting explicit focus on the matter Tdh has shifted to dedicating clearly defined time for programme staff to undergo and roll out training around PSEA and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms internally and externally. This has supported in the hand in hand roll out of the complaints and feedback mechanisms available to beneficiaries and staff alike and will continue to be an area of regular refresh to counter the turnover of staff and beneficiaries. As for data protection , Tdh seeks to operate within the EU's GDPR regulation and thus increase efforts around encryption of lap tops and extend use of CPIMS and IM tools.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members through: - Surveys and FGD with specific age groups, women, men, students, youth out of school and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Quarterly focus group discussions will be conducted with children and communities to assess child protection knowledge, attitudes and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with participatory assessment. - Tdh will maintain and raise awareness on its Complaints, Feedback, and Response Mechanism. This mechanism will follow Tdh guidelines, ensuring multiple methods for submitting complaints and receiving responses, including CFRM boxes in schools and safe spaces, as well as through static helpdesks. Where applicable, this CFRM will be adapted to fit with existing Community-Based Feedback Mechanisms already used in the location.

9.15 - If you have not selected any of the items 9.1 - 9.13, please explain why here.

In regard to 9.2, current M&E tools do not automatically cater for self assessment of needs, but rather focus on focus group discussions which lead the process in determining needs. Comprehensive case management services provided by Tdh is also critical for individual needs definition. In regard to 9.4, assistance preference is currently built into the evaluation of the existing assistance methods rather than embedded into our current new programming modalities. This will be considered for future use.
