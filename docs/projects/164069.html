Project Summary

Provision of information, legal counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) on Civil Documentation (CD) and Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights to people affected by crisis and displacement inside Syria. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable populations throughout Syria through the delivery of a multi-faceted response. Through its Capacity Building Programme and ICLA core competency NRC’s work is intended to contribute to HRP Protection Sector Objectives 1 and 2.
NRC will deliver this project through a combination of direct, partner-led and mixed modalities. Partner names have not been included in this submission for reasons of confidentiality.
Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable crisis and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities, IDP and refugee returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the crisis. All interventions will seek to prioritize the most vulnerable households and pressing needs.
NRC’s intervention forms part of integrated programming inside Syria, in close collaboration with the Shelter & WASH, Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS) and Education programmes, NRC will aim to meet its overall objective of enabling conflict and displacement-affected populations to meet their basic needs, enjoy their rights, and benefit from pathways to durable solutions. The NRC response in Syria will consist of two components:
a) Increased Knowledge and Capacity to Respond: Legal research and analysis, capacity building, HLP and CD coordination and preparation for durable solutions including return
b) Direct Assistance: NRC will assist target groups on issues that link to accessing essential services and CD, as well as improving protection in accessing livelihoods and HLP rights. In 2020, NRC will increase its support for Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) techniques, such as mediation and negotiation. NRC will continue to carry out HLP due diligence to support shelter/WASH, education and livelihood interventions

4 - Needs

Conflict has severely curtailed access to CD, leaving an entire generation potentially undocumented & unable to enjoy their right to legal identity. CD issues has been reported as a major protection concern, affecting all HNO 2020 assessed communities. HLP and freedom of movement issues were reported in 60% and 58% of assessed communities respectively. Rental problems (landlord/tenant issues), damage/looting of property and lack of documents are described as the major HLP concerns. Research by NRC shows a challenging picture of lost HLP documents, uncertain property ownership, informal, coerced transactions, weak security of tenure and disputes. Displacement results in different claimants and occupants for the same parcel of land, weakening security of tenure. The scale of the problem is vast, profoundly impacting those who lack these documents and hindering access to humanitarian aid, education and freedom of movement. The long-term risk of missing, expired/incomplete documentation could stand in the way of durable solutions.Camps and informal sites across Syria continue to host more people than they can absorb, many missing civil documentation. Returnees and people in areas that shifted control continue to report the need for information to access essential services. Conflict related displacement continues to exacerbate dire conditions in contested areas across Syria and critical infrastructure continues to be affected. Lack of awareness or adherence to IHL and humanitarian norms continues to jeopardise the life of millions of people. NRC’s research and experience shows that women face increased barriers when trying to claim their HLP rights, especially in the case of divorce or the death of their spouse. Youth continue to require information & counselling about employment laws & procedures and how to establish businesses.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC will continue to undertake legal research and studies, including desk research, using quantitative and qualitative methods, on legal identity, HLP, employment laws and return-related topics. Research will inform the production of in-depth fact sheets and training on CD and HLP provided to duty-bearers, local and international humanitarian partners, and community leaders. NRC will continue to lead national and hub-level coordination on HLP and CD, to engage with legal service providers, seeking to build capacity and highlight gaps. NRC will collaborate, coordinate and partner with specialised actors to exchange experiences, improve understanding and contribute to the protection of civilians in Syria. NRC will provide information and assistance on access to essential services, livelihoods and legal identity and on HLP and employment, with particular consideration given to return and durable solutions for IDPs and through NRC’s regional durable solutions collaborations. NRC will increase its support for Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) techniques in 2020. Support will continue to ensure HLP due diligence for shelter/WASH, education and livelihood interventions is undertaken. NRC will utilise the most appropriate modality to respond to the diverse needs of people across Syria. NRC will aim to utilise information and feedback to inform programme, advocacy and preparedness. Seeking regular feedback will inform NRC’s understanding of people’s needs and reactions, and capture unintended harmful consequences. NRC will aim to open more channels of communication for communities to voice their concerns and suggestions. NRC has a detailed M&E plan to track output and outcome indicators.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Threats to health, safety and security of staff, partners and beneficiaries linked to conflict or criminal activities: NRC has dedicated Health, Safety and Security staff, both at coordination and field level, to ensure a close context follow-up and the correct implementation of NRC Security policy and standards. NRC produces regular risk assessments and updated security and contingency plans for all operational areas. All NRC staff receive proper security and safety training.Limitations by local authorities preventing NRC to access or work in specific areas and to independently select beneficiary or staff: networking with peer organisations through different fora to advocate for unified policy positions, regular meeting with local key stakeholders to reinforce humanitarian principles, transparent recruitment policy and criteria for selection of beneficiaries, clear red lines identified while negotiating with local authorities, careful liaison work.Non-renewal of UNSC resolution 2165 after 10 July 2020: Continue negotiations with GoS to expand access from Damascus. Scale up internal procurement to guarantee access to supplies. Neighbouring states push for large-scale return of refugees to Syria (including coerced return): Collaboration and coordination with other NRC country programmes in the region. Development of specific contingency plans. Adaptation of programmes. Advocacy efforts.Fraud/Corruption/Bribery: Enforcing NRC’s policy of zero tolerance of corruption through the continuous strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the “whistle-blowing” policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures.Changes in territorial control: good understanding of context, contextual analysis, security plans and procedures, contingency planning, community acceptance and good relationship with local authorities.
