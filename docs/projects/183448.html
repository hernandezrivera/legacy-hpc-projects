Project Summary

The unique socio-economic conditions of the blockade, with its movement restrictions and destruction of productive capacity, are further compounded by repeated cycles of conflict and political instability, undercutting employment and livelihood opportunities in the Gaza Strip. UNRWA’s emergency food assistance provides a critical humanitarian lifeline responding to the basic needs of over one million Palestine refugees, tempering food insecurity and improving access to food. It accounts for approximately 200,000 Palestine refugee families including families with persons with disabilities and female headed households. On quarterly bases, eligible families receive a unified food basket that is composed of a range of basic food commodities.
Through its well-established monitoring system, UNRWA will continue to evaluate the food distribution system including the distribution modality that follows strict safety and security measures responding to changing environment around unpredictable situation, while guaranteeing a consistent support to Palestine refugees in Gaza.

1 - Needs

UNRWA’s emergency food assistance provides a critical humanitarian lifeline to Palestine refugees including supporting price stability under the distorting conditions of economic blockade. Restricted access to land, water, production inputs and markets constrain productive capacities and limit economic opportunities (HNO 2022). According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 50.2 per cent during the third quarter of 2021. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 68.6 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2021), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 71.8 per cent (PCBS, Q3-2021). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs. The poverty rate increased from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent in 2017 (SEFSec Survey, 2018), and is expected to have increased to 56.4 per cent as a result of the May hostilities (World Bank, 2021: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/07/06/the-rebuilding-of-gaza-amid-dire-conditions-damage-losses-and-needs). Persistently high levels of food insecurity in Gaza are not due to the unavailability of basic commodities in the local market, but rather to households’ lack of economic access to food, which, if unaddressed, would have lasting consequences on both the extent and severity of food needs. In 2022, while the target under the HRP plan is to meet the needs of 1 million Palestine refugees in Gaza, UNRWA’s emergency response targets up to 1.2 million refugees, reflecting the broader collective impact of this sustained relief, which is a vital intervention for the stability in the blocked enclave in ensuring the most basic food needs are met and mitigating the risk of market volatility.

2 - Activities

In 2022, UNRWA will continue adapting its food intervention approach to more clearly delineate food assistance as an emergency programme in response to the ongoing blockade imposed since 2007 with the resulting decrease in productive capacity and rise in unemployment. One unified food basket will be provided to all eligible refugees on quarterly basis, and consists of basic food commodities including wheat flour, rice, sunflower oil, sugar, whole milk, lentils and chickpeas. The Agency will continue to conduct verifications and work to implement the agreed employment exclusion criteria in providing for new caseload – although even those with steady employment are often faced with delays and disruptions in salary payments. The broader collective impact of this sustained relief is relied upon to protect against market volatility and contributes to stability in the blocked enclave in ensuring the most basic food needs are met. In delivering this assistance amidst a global pandemic, enhanced hygiene and physical distancing measures are incorporated into the Agency’s emergency food operation at UNRWA distribution points. As a crowd management and COVID-19 safety measure, UNRWA Gaza Field Office (GFO) has increased the number of distribution points from 10 to 15. An appointment system is implemented to limit the number of refugees attending at any one time to receive their food baskets by providing families with specific dates and time slots through SMS messages. In 2022, UNRWA will continue to deliver food assistance in these safe conditions and stands ready to rapidly adjust its operations to the local context and the epidemiological situation.The COVID-19 pandemic has affected food prices and logistics costs globally, also increasing the cost for this intervention in Gaza. UNRWA will continue to use its global and local procurement channels to provide high quality food in the most cost-effective way. Under the supervision of Relief and Social Services Programme (RSSP), trained social workers make systematic home visits to the beneficiaries including specific cases of wives from polygamous marriage, divorced women and separated or abandoned women to guarantee that they receive assistance fairly with dignity. Particular attention is given by the social workers to the identification and assistance of “abandoned” women and their children who, beside social stigma, are also exposed to specific risks such as not receiving any regular support from the husband and often have to rely on ad-hoc and weak social networks. UNRWA makes sure that vulnerable families do not fall through the system and are able to receive assistance independently. Through its well-established monitoring and evaluation system, UNRWA will implement quality assurance measures, and will actively engage with refugees to inform future planning to ensure continuous improvement in the food assistance operations.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Unit, in coordination with the other implementing units, prepares internal quarterly food monitoring distribution reports including number of families received food, quality of food assistance, beneficiaries’ feedback, and any obstacles encountered. The reports are sent to the Agency’s management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required. Monitoring and evaluation reports feed into informing planning for Food assistance intervention. The collection of beneficiaries’ feedbacks through both UNRWA M&E and RSSP units contributes to continuously improve the food distribution mechanism at different levels.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

UNRWA Relief Social Services Programme (RSSP) regularly conducts focus groups to discuss key items such as the quality of the food assistance, the quantity of the food basket and the process of food distribution. UNRWA-RSSP has a complaint system in place to receive beneficiaries’ feedback. An appeal mechanism as well as a committee feedback system are also in place for the families who were confirmed “not-eligible” from the intervention based on the results of the criteria. Anyone can access to RSSP area offices across the Gaza Strip. UNRWA RSSP replies to the families regarding their status based on their complaint or appeal. UNRWA’s Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) unit is also involved through regularly conducting interviews with beneficiaries across all distribution centres as well as other distribution points, beneficiaries’ homes, or via phone calls, to collect their feedback regarding the quality of the received commodities, the behavior of the staff in charge of distributing the food and any other issues related to their satisfaction.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. Distribution centres and social services are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. During the food distribution cycle, UNRWA staff based at the food distribution centers including social workers and M&E teams, ensure that the targeted beneficiaries receive their food assistance with dignity and respect. In addition, regular monitoring work is conducted to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on key items of the programme e.g. the quality of the food distribution process, the content of the food basket, the overall quality of the food distribution or the behavior of the staff involved in the food distribution. These feedbacks help to continuously improve the intervention.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all eligible Palestine refugees with food assistance, without any discrimination. Information to beneficiaries, eligibility criteria and other services are available to all refugees where application and claim can be made to Relief and Social Services Programme offices, often located in the same area of the distribution centres. Location of distribution centres are following safety measures, especially for women and girls, recognizing that catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. The distribution takes into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize assistance to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Furthermore, distribution centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Staff in the distribution centres, whom is regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to social services for follow up. A crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance, especially amidst COVID-19 emergency. In order to ease the process for targeted persons with disabilities, there is a delegation system in place that enables those people to delegate one of their family members to receive the food assistance on their behalf. The delegation is set during the home visits of the social workers and following their reports’ recommendations.
