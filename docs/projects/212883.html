Project Summary

- In the eastern rural of Aleppo, a lot of educational infrastructure has been affected by the crisis and destroyed by heavy fighting, and above all of this, the earthquake disaster has greatly added major damages to most schools in the area and rendered them out of service. As a result of the lack of educational infrastructure, children cannot attend school, and a lot of existing school buildings are unsafe and unconducive to learning.
- Learning environments are unconducive due to overcrowding, lack of school furniture/school supplies, and operational WASH facilities. Lack of electricity is affecting education supply, as schools lack heating, cooling, and lighting to operate in multiple shifts. The education infrastructure is also insufficient prior to the earthquake.
- 1019 schools in Aleppo city and rural damaged, and 694 schools in Aleppo city and rural need maintenance and rehabilitation after the earthquake in 2023, whereas 132 schools were used during the displaced people last year to accommodate roughly 64,000 individuals.
- Furniture is the most consumable item, therefore the following numbers demonstrates the needs: (5000) Teacher chair, (500) Teacher Table, (5000) Wooden cupboards, (1500) Whiteboards, (2000) Fans, (1000) Heater, and (5000) school desks.
- The geographical scope of the project was defined based on the gap analysis of the education sector (people in need vs. sectoral reach) and all targeted locations are rated from 3 to 4 on the Education Cluster Severity, Target, Pin.
- Within Education Sector Response Framework 2022-2023, the project will contribute to:
-Increased safe and equitable availability and access with enhanced quality, of formal and non-formal education for about 10000 crisis-affected children and youth in the areas of high needs and low levels of education sector response and reach.
The project activities include:
1- Rehabilitation of 20 schools including WASH facilities, and solar panels to enable the schools to operate in multiple shifts.
2- Provision to about 6000 children and youth remedial classes targeting children at risk of dropping out, and provision of Non-Formal Education, including support for transitioning to formal education.
3- Supporting the delivery of quality, protective education services (training, incentives, supplies, school desks, furniture and awareness sessions).
The main protection risks :
The following lays out some of the main protection risks that emerged in the evidence, which can either arise in formal and informal targeted spaces of learning in this project:
1- Physical safety – schools and school routes under attack including other grave violations.
Risk Evaluation: Impact: Low (Unlikely to occur) - Likelihood: Low.
2- Recruitment into armed groups and armed forces.
Risk Evaluation: Impact: Low (Unlikely to occur) - Likelihood: Low.
3- Sexual and gender-based violence.
Risk Evaluation: Impact: High (Likely to occur) - Likelihood: Low.
4- Negative coping mechanisms linked to economic vulnerability: early marriage and child labor.
Impact: High (Likely to occur) - Likelihood: High.
5- Trauma and mental health conditions.
Risk Evaluation: Impact: High (Likely to occur) - Likelihood: Low.
6- Peer violence.
High (Likely to occur) - Likelihood: High.
Project Request Justification
1- People of the region appealed many agencies to support the education sector first.
2- As a result of the devastating earthquake, this project is highly needed to meet the increasing educational needs of children and bridge the education gap, as well as enhance the quality of education.
3- The project is of particular importance because it covers the neediest areas of the countryside of Aleppo, and priority was granted to schools in very bad conditions (damaged and destroyed), with the unavailability of a source of rehabilitation according to damage level.
4- There were no rehabilitation interventions that included the targeted schools for ten years at least.
