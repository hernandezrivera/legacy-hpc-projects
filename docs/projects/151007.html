Project Summary

Under this project, WARDI proposes to improve access to safe water, sanitation facilities and services and promote good hygiene practices in an effort to improve the health status of 51,220 disaster affected communities and internally displaced men, women, boys and girls in Hiran, Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions of south Somalia. Lack of access to safe and adequate water and sanitation facilities coupled with poor hygiene knowledge and practices put these communities at risk for waterborne diseases and loss of life particularly women and children. Key activities that WARDI would like to implement in the target locations include:
WATER: Provision of emergency safe water to 6000 IDPs through voucher; access of lifesaving water through rehabilitation of 3 public diesel powered boreholes through hybrid solar powered system, rehabilitation of 14 shallow wells (12 with hand pumps, 2 with pump powered with solar; construction of 8 water kiosks supplied water through piping system; capacity building training to 17 WASH committees and 34 well operators on operation and maintenance. As cross cutting, WARDI will conduct DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms workshop to 200 community members.
SANITATION: To improve the sanitation of the IDP camps and institutions, WARDI will construct 8 twin block institutional latrines in 2 school and 2 health facilities; construct 200 IDP latrines; and Carrying out CLTS to 12 villages.
HYGIENE: WARDI will train 100 CHPs to carry out comprehensive hygiene promotion campaigns to educate the community better hygiene promotion practices including hand washing with soap practices through visiting house-to-house, schools, feeding/nutrition centers and health centers; in this project, WARDI will provide 1500 HP kits to 1500 households and IDPs who are at risk of AWD/cholera and malnutrition diseases.
The proposed response took into consideration the impact of the disaster on women, and children in terms of access to WASH services; it proposed specific actions to prevent risk of gender based violence and hence will contribute to greater gender equality. WARDI top management will monitor the quality and quantity of the WASH intervention during the project implementation and its completion.

Needs

Heavy rains, which caused flooding in Jubba and Shabelle rivers' levels in the mid-April, led destruction of property, inundation of croplands and displacement. WASH infrastructures suffered from important damages; most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and lack of investment in WASH infrastructures, the affected people continued to suffer AWD/Cholera in 2018 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots. Due to lack access to WASH facilities, women and children are increasingly exposed to violence while trying to access basic services. A recent forecast issued by the GHACOF indicated a greater likelihood of normal to above normal 2018 Deyr rains across Somalia, which may cause flooding in some low-lying and riverine areas. A joint assessment have been made in southern regions of Somalia from 20 to 27 July 2018, which WARDI was part of it aimed to find out the effects of the flood to the assessed locations in terms of livelihoods, shelter, WASH, education, health, nutrition protection and shelter. The team observed that WASH services are the most challenging need to address. 90% of the water sources are destructed/contaminated due to the seepage of latrines; 87% of the latrines are destructed; in rural villages 65% of the latrines were destructed; the children usually defecate around the shelter structures and many poor hygiene practices were observed. However, these communities, which fall into very low income category, cannot afford the initial capital investments required for establishing the required WASH services. Furthermore, hygiene practices are also quite limited at institutions due to lack of functional WASH facilities. Thus, to improve the WASH services, WARDI will carry out emergency WASH services to the target areas and as an exit strategy will improve the access to sustainable and safe WASH services.

Activities

1. Provide safe water to 6,000 men, women and children affected IDPs for 60 days (15 l/p/d).2. Rehabilitate: (1) 3 public diesel powered boreholes through hybrid solar powered system. The solar powered system will ensure cheaper and reliable access to safe water to the IDPs and host communities. Construct 18 water kiosks supplied water through piping system at reliable distance to 3 IDP settlements in Banadir region to reduce the risk of GBV against women and girls. (2) 14 flood proof shallow wells (2 solar power pumps and 12 hand pumps) to schools/heath facilities and community shallow wells. Chlorinate the rehabilitated water sources on daily basis. 3. Train: (1) 17 WASH committees’ members (68men, 51women) on better water source management. (2) 34 Well operators (17men, 17women) on O&M and well chlorination. (3) 200 community members (100men, 100women) on DRR and establish community structures to prepare Emergency Preparedness Plans detailing perceived risks to the community and suitable responses to scenarios.4. Construct 200 flood proof, desludgable and gender sensitive latrines with lockable doors and hand washing facilities to IDPs and urban low income HHs to reduce open defecation and risk of GBV against women/girls.5. Construct 8 flood proof gender sensitive institutional twin block latrines with hand washing facilities and lockable doors to 2 schools and 2 health centers. 6. Conduct CLTS to 12 villages to eliminate open defecation practices and to trigger to a great extent of community with a desire for change with regards to OD.7. Conduct comprehensive HP awareness campaigns including hand washing with soap to 51,220 (13983men, 13881women, 11985boys and 11371girls) IDPs and host communities. Conduct KAP survey at the inception and the end of the project. 8. Conduct HP training to 100 CHPs (50men and 50women). 9. Distribute 1,500 HP kits to 1,500. Each HP kit contains (200 aqua tabs, 1 Jerry can (20l), 1 bucket (20l) and 5 bars soaps (800mg).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

WARDI will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders including the local authorities to officially launch the project. During the sensitization meetings, the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles and duties of each stakeholder including M&E roles will be discussed and agreed. WARDI will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project and keeping the sustainability of the WASH facilities after the face out of the project will be done through formation of structures such as Beneficiary Representatives, WASH Committees, and Community Hygiene Promoters with equal share of men and women. In order to meet the WASH needs of the affected community, WARDI will involve the target community through its leaders and engendered committees in the first stage of planning of the project to ensure community participation. The organization will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled to the right direction. WARDI will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries. WARDI will adhere to these principles of “do no harm” at all times throughout the project cycle by involving all categories of the community. The project activities has a strategy of emergency and resilience to enable durable solution for the target beneficiaries. WARDI will provide emergency safe water to the vulnerable disaster affected community and IDPs through voucher, to reduce their water need during the rehabilitation/construction of WASH facilities. As an exit strategy, WARDI will also provide the beneficiaries sustainable WASH services to enable durable solutions to the affected communities. To keep the sustainability of the WASH services, WARDI will train WASH committees, well operators and Community Hygiene Promoters, in order to maintain the WASH services after the project is faced out and to keep the ownership of the community to the project. After the project activities is completed, WARDI will hand over the rehabilitated/constructed WASH facilities to the WASH committees with the presence of the local authorities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Insecurity and conflict due to insurgency and clan conflict is now one of the main causes for displacement. Drought, floods and cyclone are also the root cause of the displacement in Somalia. Somalia faces a dire humanitarian situation, having endured drought and famine as well as continued fighting and heavy rains, all of which have aggravated the conditions of IDPs. People denied the rights of the IDPs as human being and they are facing evictions, rape and looting due to the fragility of the central government who was under their responsibility to protect them. Most of the IDPs left behind their livelihood assets and displaced to the big towns with empty hands. Thus, the IDPs have the right to receive necessary service, such as protection, FS, shelter, education, WASH, health and nutrition services in order to safe their lives. Unfortunately, conflict sometimes is preventing humanitarian agencies from delivering assistance to the IDPs. In this project, WARDI will try to reduce the burden of IDPs (women and children suffer most) and bring the services their WASH requirement to their settlements, in order to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls for searching needed services. WARDI will also include the IDPs (giving women IDPs their share) to the established committees for the project implementation to get their views and to be heard their complains if arise. The IDP rights will be availed in the course of the project implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The overall goal of the proposed project is to reduce the burden of women/girls and time spent in search of water regarding dangers of long distance or secluded places, for example safety and security measures were taken into account by close consultation with women and people with disabilities during the project design like latrines to be constructed close to their settlements and distribution of water inside their settlements. This project is totally Addresses the needs of water and latrines to the drought affected men, women, girls and boys in the three target regions for easily accessible, instead of women and girls walk long distance for searching safe and reliable water (women and girls are responsible for fetching water to their households) and for searching place for their latrine needs; this will reduce the exposure of women and girls to SGBV, while providing more time for livelihood activities for women and give spaces for girls education. In this project women, men, girls and children will give equal opportunity to take part the project design before its implementation and also to be equally heard their voices. Women, men and marginalized communities will equally share the members of the WASH committees, Community Hygiene Promoters and also the project staff. All men, women, boys, girls and disables will equal have access to the WASH infrastructures constructed/rehabilitated under this project.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Security in Somalia is dynamic and the districts security focal point will analyze security situation and advice the frontline staff the situation every morning. It is anticipated that the security will remain calm and allow smooth implementation of activities within the project time frame. WARDI will identify the risk of conflict in the project area and immediately avert this risk, as water and pasture are important commodity to the drought affected community and conflict between neighboring clans starts the management of those resources, WARDI will closely engage the local leaders, government officials and the community to sort out any possible threat and the community prominent members as cross-cutting will be trained on Disaster Risk Reduction and conflict resolution mechanism measures. WARDI is currently implementing WASH, food security, Education, Nutrition and Health projects in Hiran, Banadir and L/Shabelle regions with out any security problem, although sometimes there is security issues in L/Shabelle region. Safety and security for the project staff and beneficiaries are our top priority, there will be security briefing every day during staff meeting to minimize risk. The project will be implemented in accessible villages/camps where the community settles to reduce risk of walking long distance to women and girls. Rapid needs assessment and technical assessments were done in the target locations by WARDI staff without security challenges.
