Project Summary

After eleven years of conflict, extensive and multifaceted humanitarian needs across Syria remain. The conflict has grave direct and indirect health implications for civilians, including women and children. The collapse of public services, health systems, and social networks places a disproportionate burden on women and children's health, which can be further exacerbated when they are forcibly displaced from their homes and communities. Building on its experience providing lifesaving health services for the past several years, RI proposes comprehensive health, institutional WASH and protection intervention in NES. The services are designed to provide an integrated health, institutional WASH, and protection monitoring package. The intervention will target all affected populations, focusing on the inclusion of vulnerable groups.
Using health as an entry point, RI will support essential lifesaving health services provision in Syria along with the integrated WASH in Health Facilities and protection services. RI will provide primary and secondary healthcare services through integrated health and protection activities at the community level by providing information and education to minimize the risk of diseases along with GBV and RI will conduct protection monitoring activities in NES. RI will utilize evidence-based information based on protection monitoring in implementing health and protection activities to inform advocacy efforts to address critical needs.

3 - Needs

In Syria, most functioning health facilities are dependent on humanitarian aid for their operations and sustained services provision. In the absence of any sustainable support from the local health administration authorities, RI’s health assessment highlighted the need for ongoing support to ensure the availability and continuity of humanitarian lifesaving reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health services is critical in humanitarian settings in humanitarian settings which typically see rises in maternal deaths, unintended pregnancy, sexually-transmitted infections, unsafe abortion, and gender-based violence. In NES, lack of functioning health facilities presents a considerable challenge to provide adequate healthcare to cope with the increasing need for healthcare services and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. Of the 279-public health centers in NES, only 26 are fully functioning (HeRAMs), and the operational health facilities are pandemic.

4 - Implementation of activities

In line with HRP 2021’s strategic objectives, RI will support lifesaving humanitarian assistance, integrating protection mainstreaming to support the most at-risk, under-represented and vulnerable individuals.In NES, RI will support static health facilities in Deir Ez Zor and Hassakeh governorates to provide primary healthcare services, as well as medical mobile clinics (MMC) to cover different areas in West and East line of Deir Ez Zor governorate. These HFs (Jdidet Bikara Hospital, Shiheil Hospital, Hejneh PHCU, Mhemida PHCC, Al Hol PHC, Washokani camp PHC, Kobar PHC, Hawaij PHC, Basira CPHC, Sabha PHC, Kishkiyeh (Alsalam) CPHC, Sha’afa PHC) were chosen because the local Health Committees identified these as facilities with the greatest need and they cover essential health services gaps in the target communities.The target facilities and units will receive support from RI to ensure the provision of quality primary healthcare services including communicable and non-communicable diseases and mental health, RH (including maternal and childcare) and community-based activities through CHWs to deliver awareness-raising and nutrition assessment and referrals as needed. In one PHC, RI will also establish an isolation unit/intensive care unit to cover critical medical cases for stabilization, including COVID-19 moderate/severe cases. RI will provide referrals to more specialized services as needed. The intensive care unit's establishment will utilize in part existing equipment.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Main risks: Blockade of border crossings; non-renewal of the cross border resolution; increased conflict or terrorism; partial or full suspension of RI’s activities due to deterioration of security situation (including besiegement of operational areas). Main assumptions: Border crossings will remain open for humanitarian access and delivery of goods inside Syria; Continued ability to operate in areas of intervention; RI will be able to relocate HFs to other accessible areas if required; In the event of loss of access, private contractors will be available to transport humanitarian goods and supplies; RI will maintain smooth operations at all supported HFs without fraudulent activities or corruption.Mitigating measures. (i) Border access blocked: RI would increase procurement within Syria and improve control measures to assess these commodities' quality, especially pharmaceuticals. Major procurements (medical pharma/consumables) are shipped via the supplier to the RI warehouses since commercial shipment suppliers have export licenses. If supplier shipments are affected, then this would impact RI programs. RI will maintain buffer stocks for HFs to respond to multiple scenarios that involve border closure and large IDP movements based on available resources. (ii)Loss of access: RI will adapt the modality of implementation from static HFs to mobile medical units if static HFs are rendered inoperable, or HFs would be suspended or relocated as needed.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project aims to provide lifesaving health services in conflict -affected communities in Northeast Syria. However, such services can increase risks of exploitation by service providers or by other actor in the community, and RI will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the program 2. Ensure participants know services where to disclose if violence occurs; 3. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report 4. Ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all women and girls.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Main risks: Blockade of border crossings; high probability of non-renewal of the cross border resolution; increased conflict or terrorism; partial or full suspension of RI’s activities due to deterioration of security situation (including besiegement of operational areas). Main assumptions: Border crossings will remain open for humanitarian access and delivery of goods inside Syria; RI anticipates continued ability to operate in areas of intervention; RI will be able to relocate any HFs to other accessible areas if required; In the event of loss of access, private contractors will be available to transport humanitarian goods and supplies; RI will maintain smooth operations at all supported health facilities without any threat of fraudulent activities or corruption.Mitigating measures. If supplier shipments are affected, then this would impact on RI programs. RI will maintain buffer stocks for HFs to respond to multiple scenarios that involve border closure and large IDP movements based on the available resources. (ii)Loss of access: RI will adapt the modality of implementation from static HFs to mobile medical units as needed.
