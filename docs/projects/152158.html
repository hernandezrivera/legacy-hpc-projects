Project Summary

The project will be implemented in the north east Nigeria, BAY state. Plan International Nigeria has a field office in Maiduguri, which will provide technical and administration support, and will be responsible for the implementation of the project. The project will focus on two main areas with its response i) Treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition through comprehnsive Community based Managament of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach, Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) and Micro Nutrient Powder (MNP); ii) promoting behavioral changes with a focus on sanitation and hygiene.
In the targted LGA, the health and nutrition support consist of primary health care, general hospital and Maternal and Child Health (MCH) clinics. The project will focus on providing life saving support for vunerable children and PLW by providing treatment of uncomplicated Sever Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in decenteralized Mobile outreach actvities Out Patient Therapeutic Program (OTP), treatment of complicated SAM in Stabilization center (SC), community Mobilization/out reach (community sensitation, active case finding and follow up of admited SAM cases). The house to house sensitization and screening of children will be conducted by Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNV), and referred to the nearest Mobile Outreach OTP community if needed. The beneficiaries identified by the CNVs in each communities will include children under five as well as pregnanat and lactating women. The CMAM officer/health workers within the Mobile Outreach OTP sites will support children identified as malnourished with RUTF and medecine as per the CMAM protocol. The CMAM officers will furthermore monitor the screening activities, including delivering IYCF message as per Plan International's messaging, based on national established manuals. The treatment of uncomplicated SAM for children under five will be done by CMAM officers at Mobile Outreach OTP sites, while for the complicated SAM children age under five will be supported SC officers (Nurse), SC coordinator ( Doctor) and Nutrition assistant. On top of this in each SAM treatment sites the psycho social support counselling will be provided by trained CMAM/ SC officer, health workers and psycho social support officer. Furthermore this project will organize basic CMAM and IYCF training for government health and nutrition wrokers and plan International nutrition staff to strength the capacity of governemt in treatment of acute malnutrition and prevention of under nutrition in the local government area. As part of the emergency nutrition support, therapeutic food support will be provided to caregivers of children with complicated SAM receiving treament at SC at general hospital.
For IYCF project plan international will established a Care Group using care group model, focusing on pregnant women, and mothers of children under two years (the ‘first 1,000 days’). Each care group will be composed of 12 volunteer Lead Mothers who will be trained on gender integrated Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF). The target group for IYCF activities will include lactating mothers with children less than 24 months and pregnant/non-pregnant women of reproductive age aimed to improve child caring and IYCF practices.
The purpose of the project is Improved nutritional status of vulnerable groups, particularly acutely malnourished girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The target group for this project is children under five year and pregnant and lactating women.

Needs

According to the most recent five round Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in May, 2018 report depicted that in Borno state 10.1% and 0.8% GAM & SAM rate respectively and in Adamawa state 8.2% and 0.4% GAM and SAM rate respectively, Yobe state 12.2% and 2.1% GAM and SAM rate respectively. In the same survey in Central Borno LGAs [(Damboa), Gubio, Kaga, Konduga, Mafa, Magumeri, (Marte), Monguno)] assessed by a weight-for-height survey currently sits at 14.9% with 2.4% SAM rate. Survey findings indicates that an increased prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in Borno. With nutrition acutely linked to the insecure food and livelihoods crisis, malnutrition rates across the Borno state are alarming, with the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate reaching emergency levels in some LGAs including Kaga & Konduga with further aggravated by in the rainy season and the increase in malaria and AWD and cholera. The trends have been noted in severe cases without and with medical complications. Therefore, in all LGAs the nutrition status classified as per WHO classification as serious, which needs immediate humanitarian assistance. In addition, plan international Nigeria has conducted mass MUAC screening for children under five years in all communities of Kaga Local government area in September, 2018 the result depicted that 16.1 % GAM rate and 1.8% of SAM. Therefore, the LGA nutrition status has classified as WHO classification critical nutrition status. Similarly, the GAM rate in Kaga IDP camps showed that 10% and SAM rate showed that 2.4%. which is also classified as serious nutritional status. On top of this in the recent nutrition surveys undertaken in August 2018 illustrated alarmingly high Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence among new arrivals, with 37.5% recorded at reception sites versus 7.8% in rest of camp locations. As per plan international Nigeria rapid need nutrition assessment conducted in September, 2018 showed that in Chibok and Hawul LGA there is no stabilization center in both general hospitals for the management of SAM with medical complication and failed appetite test but in both LGA there is functional general hospital run by the SPHCD and Borno state hospital board with nurse, laboratory technician. Key gaps in the proposed all LGAs are week monitoring, supervision and the technical backstopping of field staff, weak community mobilization, coordination and linking up between treatment, prevention and cross-referrals within the different nutrition services available. In addition, there is no stabilization center for complicated SAM children under five treatments in Konduga, Mafa, Asikira/Uba, Kaga, Chibok and Hawul LGAs.According to UNOCHA, “Nigeria-Northeast: humanitarian situation update September 2018”, there is an urgent need to increase the number of stabilization centers to manage SAM cases with medical complications., Furthermore, routine screening activities need to be scaled up for the early detection of acute malnutrition and referral for treatment. While it is difficult to anticipate the exact humanitarian needs resulting from new arrivals from hard-to-reach areas, past experience has shown that emergency health, food security, nutrition, shelter, NFIs, water, sanitation and hygiene and protection assistance are frequently required upon arrival. These new arrivals are often fleeing conflict and insecurity and, as a result, many are in poor health conditions and nutrition status. To address the needs of children under five and pregnant and lactating plan international will do the following activities in the proposed LGAs- OTP for uncomplicated SAM in Kaga NRC, Migstreate, and lawanti B IDP, - SC for complicated cases at Konduga general Hospital, Beneshiek hospital, Hawul and chibok general hospitals- Community mobilization including screening at reception center.- IYCF and IYCF-E - Management of moderate acute malnutrition for 6-59 month and PLW.

Establishment of # Mother-to-Mother support groups or care group Practical gender integrated demonstrations and lessons on common child care practices, breast feeding, complementary feeding and healthy diet/meal preparation and feeding will be provided. Attitudes about modern health services, particularly family planning, hygiene, and sanitation will also be explored through this forum. Therefore, PIN will implement this activity through integrating into the CMAM program the strategy called care group model. This approach has a design to improve community services and behaviour of people. The Care Groups model will focus on pregnant women, mothers and fathers of children under two. Each care group will be composed of 12 volunteer Lead Mothers or Fathers who will be trained on a gender integrated IYCF. Then the volunteer lead mother/father will share the information with 10 to 15 neighbouring households.
