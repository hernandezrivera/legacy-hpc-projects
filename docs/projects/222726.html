Project Summary

By the JRP project 2025, Habitat for Humanity will create and strengthen access to safe and equitable shelter and settlements assistance for Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMN) and Host Community while improving living standard and empowering communities. Beside considering the continuation of current 2024 phase intervention and HFHI-B mandate for Rohingya response, 2025 plan is directly based on assessment, latest J-MSNA report, shelter standard assessment report as well as guidance and instruction from SCCCM, Protection, ERP, EEN clusters/working groups of ISCG. Habitat is aiming a well-coordinated Shelter project covering specified areas in the FDMN and host communities. HFHI-B will serve total 2395 Households with necessary Shelter and NFI assistance and services. Out of these households, 2095 FDMN families (1220 HH in Camp 19 and 875 HH in Camp 13) and 300 Host community families shall be covered. Habitat has aligned his major activities with the official JRP 2025 by considering construction of new Mid-term Shelters (MTS) (200 HH; 100 HH in camp 19 block A and 100 HH in Camp 13 block G), Regular Shelter Reinforcement via SUM approach (1635 HH; 960 in Camp 19 block A and 675 in camp 13 block G), RDV and Emergency shelter support (200 HHs; 100 in camp 13 and 100 in camp 19), Slope Stabilization (20 HH), Drainage Repair (20 HH), Shelter training including DRR (1635 HH) Community Awareness session (1635 HH), NFI Solar distribution (600 HH), Streetlight Solar installation and repair (20 HH). Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) on shelter and NFI shall be conducted covering 2395 HH. Habitat will also explore around the possibility of using weather resistant materials and piloting 1 new design subject to government and sectors approval. In the host community, Habitat will support via Shelter renovation and repair maintenance (300 HH), shelter training including DRR (300 HH) as well as Solar NFI distribution (300 HH). Beside assessment, training, materials, distribution and technical support, Habitat will provide labor support for construction/porter to elderly people, persons with disability, women headed households. HFHI-B will stay in close collaboration with other major actor Caritas in camp 13 and 19 to provide with shelter assistance in timely fashion, whereas collaboration with area focal agency IOM for RDV emergency support provision. Protection mainstreaming will be diligently implemented through active participation and accountability to the affected populations, ensuring access, privacy throughout the project cycle. HFHI-B will coordinate with the PSEA network, the protection sector, utilizing designated focal points for PSEA, child and safeguarding. HFHB will ensure consistency and transparency in the shelter assistance that are compliant with sector and Government specifications. Consultation will be done with communities benefiting from HFHB assistance to give the opportunity to the beneficiaries to play an active role in the activities implemented. Shelter/NFI beneficiaries will be actively engaged in designing PSEA related prevention and reporting mechanism, while relevant IEC/BCC materials shall be produced and circulated. Habitat will ensure climate change adaptation and mitigation while working on shelter construction and repair in Rohingya camps and host communities, that requires careful planning, sustainable practices, and materials that reduce environmental impact. HFHI-B will develop capacity of community towards appropriate construction /maintenance practices to ensure shelter maintenance aligns with climate adaptation goals. Community based and integrated approach with maximum priority to include different gendered and aged population and persons with disability in all phases for each targeted intervention. HFHB will actively participate in the CCCM meetings and will keep the sector inform of the activities implemented or planned by completing on time the 4W and other coordination tools.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

By the end of 2024, the situation in the Rohingya camps remains challenging, exacerbated by a decrease in funding across the sectors. The critical needs for both shelter materials and non-food items (NFI) persist both regular phase & emergency, making them top priorities. Shelter essentials such as bamboo poles, tarpaulins, and ropes require regular replacement due to their limited lifespan and exposure to harsh weather conditions like cyclones, flooding, landslides, heavy rain, and winds. Additionally, the risk of fire adds another layer of urgency to this matter. Despite improvements in shelter design (bamboo posts raised up from the ground on steel footings) and the use of some better-quality material (treated borak bamboo), due to conditions in the refugee camps and surrounding communities, weathering and the lack of quality materials and disaster resilient building techniques, shelters have still a very limited capacity to resist these events and consequently require regular maintenance and replacement. These interventions directly contribute to protecting the affected populations, ensuring security, privacy, and health for the beneficiaries. The need for an increased budget to address repairs and maintenance as well as alternative construction materials design and usage (subject to government and sector approval) of shelters has become evident in recent years. Under 2025 JRP, regular and emergency shelter repair activities are considered as firewall priorities. Though The MSNA’23 revealed 72% of households report shelter damage, 41% still seemed unable to ensure assistance in 2024, mainly due to funding gap. ISNA’24 and Shelter Performance Standard, 24 portray a grim reality. The difficult living conditions in the camps affect the physical and psychological well-being of refugees, especially children, women, elderly, persons with disabilities. Appropriate protection of refugees from abuse and exploitation remains inadequate. In addition, there are seasonal environmental impacts, especially in the cyclone and monsoon seasons, during which floods and landslides regularly occur in the camp. Shelter Performance Standard’24 report specifically highlights that only 29% of households possess fire-protective walls, 30% has structural bamboo with zero insect infestation, and only 16% has reported no leakage of rainwater entering. Though beneficiaries acknowledged receiving certain assistance, more than 40% pointed out the inadequacy of received shelter materials. ISNA’24 report clearly revealed the insufficient cooking space for 40% households. The large-scale deforestation that took place at the beginning of the refugee crisis further increases the disaster risk. Moreover ISNA’24 found out that more than 30% shelters are suffering due to lighting issues and indicates the need for solar lamps or repair of lamps to ensure dark time security and safety. The ISNA’24 report also revealed ongoing insufficiency of non-food items for affected populations. CFRM mechanism requires greater attention as according to ISNA'24, 31% of households has reported not receiving follow-ups on complaints. Support in host communities should prioritize shelter reconstruction or repair, training on building back safer shelters, and household-level lighting initiatives. Micro-level site planning for new shelters and household-level site improvement are deemed necessary to mitigate additional risks faced by shelters at both Rohingya and Host communities. Also, it is noteworthy to mention the dire need to build capacity of FDMN and host population (making equitable for different aged and gendered groups) and promote self-reliance and resilience in the community by meaningfully engaging them in shelter initiatives. Fire and weather resistant materials test and implementation can be another key major step in 2025 for Government and SCCCM sector.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Shelter/NFI assistance provided by HFHB aims to enhance the safety and security of families living in the camps and host communities. Local communities, including the most vulnerable groups will be involved at every steps of the program. Volunteers and CFW from the community and vulnerable groups will be recruited and trained for different roles throughout the implementation of the activities. HFHB will host consultations with vulnerable groups to identify specific needs and develop protection sensitive shelter material distribution, training arrangements and construction/maintenance modalities. HFHB will ensure that the implementation team and other associated members involved in the process are trained on the basics of GBV, disability inclusion and PSEA. HFHB will give special consideration to all women or households with many women and girls to ensure the mitigation of GBV risks. Protection mainstreaming will be diligently implemented through active participation and accountability to the affected populations, ensuring access, safety, privacy, and security throughout the project cycle. The selection of targeted HHs will follow specific criteria based on protection needs, assessments, referrals from the protection sector, and concerns raised in the FCRM platform. To ensure the utmost confidentiality, guidelines and policies of HFHI-B from Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Protection and Safeguarding will be strictly followed. HFHI-B will coordinate with the PSEA network, CPSS, GBVSS, and the protection sector, utilizing designated focal points for PSEA, child protection, and safeguarding. Transparency and accountability toward beneficiaries will be a top priority. Special focus will be given to addressing the needs of vulnerable groups, including extremely vulnerable individuals, female-headed household, households with special needs. HFHI-B will ensure real time analysis of protection priorities across entire project life cycle.

2.3 - Age & Disability

HFHI-B will continue diligently following persons with disability and older people inclusion related IASC and Humanitarian standards and guidelines. As Shelter/NFI assistance provider, HFHI-B will make necessary coordination with appropriate agencies to identify special needs and requirements of target participants. Also, HFHI-B is prepared to go beyond 3% PWD and serve according to the context and necessities. All staffs and volunteers will be adequately capacitated to provide with inclusive services for vulnerable population with shelter/NFI assistance. Noteworthy to mention that porter and construction support is mandatory from HFHI-B while serving PWD and elderly people including special/separate priority que/venue, while Habitat can go further by serving women and elderly person with disability at their door-steps. Also rightful priority will be given on accessibility of distribution points and training venues. The project aims to improve access to services for persons with disabilities through activities and referrals, with coordination maintained through meetings and capacity building with the Age & Disability Working Group. Habitat allocates an inclusive budget to address the specific needs of persons with disabilities to fostering an equitable and accessible environment. People with disability will be supported by disable friendly access to the shelter with handrail and household level site improvements. The data collection/assessment team shall be well oriented to get engaged with persons with disability and their special context. PWDs will be appropriately engaged in volunteer and CFW schemes based on capacity and safety consideration. Moreover, elderly people and persons with disability will be prioritized during emergency preparedness and response as well as engage them in training, community consultation and relevant committees.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HFHB will ensure consistency and transparency in the shelter assistance that are compliant with sector and Government specifications. Consultation will be done with communities benefiting from HFHB assistance to give the opportunity to the beneficiaries to play an active role in the activities implemented. Complaint mechanisms will be functional and explained to the communities. .Communication will be maintained with community committees and local authorities. If any changes occur in the project, the information will be shared immediately. Awareness sessions will be conducted in the communities where HFHB activities are implemented. The session will take place at the beginning of the project to inform the communities of the type of activities, budget, timeline, donors, selection criteria…HFHB staff will be trained on accountability and will be in charge of informing local authorities of the HFHB interventions. HFHB will listen to and record beneficiary initiatives and comments on HFHB interventions by designing monitoring tools for community mobilizer to collect feedback. It is noteworthy to mention that the HFHI-B project design is heavily covered by different latest participatory assessment including ISNA 2024. The beneficiaries will be selected based on transparent assessment criteria in coordination and consultation with local authority and community. HFHI-B, in coordination with SCCCM sector and camp/local authority will disseminate JRP plan/priority related information to community in understandable methods and platforms. HFHI-B will remain accountable regarding information sharing related to feedback and complaints received. Different gendered and aged groups representation and engagement will be ensured at different AAP planning level.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

HFHB will ensure to create and maintain a safe organizational culture for those who work for and with HFHB, as well as vendors and communities where HFHB implement shelter activities. HFHB will put in place strong prevention and response work, offering support to survivor and holding persons responsible for committing sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse. HFHB will use safe and accessible channels for employees, related personnel, vendors, beneficiaries, volunteers and others to safely report sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse. All stakeholders, including the direct beneficiaries will have the necessary orientation and information about how to access safe reporting. HFHB will provide training to staff, volunteers and related personnel for receiving complaints to ensure they understand their obligations. Emphasis will be made on confidentiality. HFHB will respond to all concerns or allegations by conducting independent investigations, ensure proper recording and management of cases, take appropriate actions against employees or related personnel and provide support to survivors. PSEA will be integrated in HFHB programs through recruitment, by conducting background check and request ISCG support. Orientation/training on policy, reporting and response will be provided to newly recruited staff. In addition, refresher training will be organized throughout the project timeline. HFHB employees will be supported to take pro-active programming measures to increase awareness and maintain a safe environment. HFHI-B is an active member of PSEA network, while constantly upholding the importance of PSEA mainstreaming for shelter sector. Habitat’s Code of Conduct explicitly includes PSEA related guidelines and principles. Shelter/NFI beneficiaries will be actively engaged in designing PSEA related prevention and reporting mechanism, while relevant IEC/BCC materials shall be produced and circulated.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Adhering to the EEN Climate Action Strategy, HFHI-B will integrate Climate adaptation techniques and Climate Action while implementing shelter assistance to the Rohingya camps and host communities. Habitat will actively advocate and get engaged in Energy and Environment sectors reforestation and plantation agenda. Also, eco-friendly/solar energy practices will be incorporated during shelter assistance program, while prioritizing different gendered groups linked with most vulnerable group’s safety and well-being. Subject to Government and sector approval, flood and wind resilient design shall be incorporated in shelter assistance program. Special attention will be given towards long term drainage and slope stabilization solutions to mitigate climate change related hazards. Habitat will use locally sourced, renewable materials to minimize carbon emissions and support local economies. Habitat will ensure climate change adaptation and mitigation while working on shelter construction and repair in Rohingya camps and host communities, that requires careful planning, sustainable practices, and materials that reduce environmental impact. HFHI-B will develop capacity of community towards appropriate construction /maintenance practices to ensure shelter maintenance aligns with climate adaptation goals. Habitat will regularly assess shelters for wear and weather impact, updating designs as climate conditions evolve.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

HFHI-B prioritizes the tight connection between Shelter assistance and emergency response and preparedness in the context of Cox’s bazar due to possible cyclone, flash flood, fire incident and other hazard, while adhering to ISCG Multi Hazard Response Plan. During project implementation process in 2025, Habitat will make sure it is an active member of ISCG EPR Working group. Having previous experiences not only in FDMN response but also globally across different emergency context, HFHI-B shall contribute in risk assessment and site planning. It is important to identify potential hazards specific to the camp area, such as flooding, landslides, or fires, and plan shelter locations accordingly as well as plan on evacuation routes and assembly points. In coordination with area focal agency and local authority, Habitat will ensure the emergency shelter assistance based on the rapid damage verification. Necessary drainage repoair and slope stabilization work has been incorporated in the implementation plan. Habitat will organize trainings for communities and volunteers covering emergency preparedness and response. Habitat will prepare effective EPR plan in coordination with EPR working group. As per Habitat’s practice and sector’s need, minimum contingency stock will be prepared while ISCG SOP and guidelines will be strictly followed. Persons with disability and different gendered and aged groups will be systematically involved in EPR planning, implementation and monitoring process.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

HFHI-B will be engaging youth population both from Host and Rohingya population while designing and implementing shelter assistance project which can provide meaningful integration, enhance youths’ capacity, fostering social cohesion, and giving them an authentic sense of purpose. A special focus will be given towards youth segments of targeted population while arranging training/capacity building sessions, community consultations as well as CFW and volunteer selection process. Youths shall be included training sessions in construction skills, such as basic carpentry, masonry, and durable building techniques. These skills can help youth contribute to shelter construction and also benefit them in the long term. Habitat will incorporate disaster preparedness and emergency response training to help youth gain practical knowledge on how to protect their community during crisis. Habitat will arrange regular feedback sessions where youth can share thoughts on the project’s progress and suggest improvements, fostering a sense of ownership in the project.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Habitat recognizes the importance of endorsing self-reliance and resilience for FDMN population to ensure safe shelter solution. As a consequence, community shall be involved in the entire project design and implementation process. Such inclusion will aid not only into shelter quality but also enhance local capacity and ownership. Rohingya population will be diligently oriented and trained over shelter construction, repair and maintenance in order to make them self-reliant and confident over their capacity to ensure safe habitation facility. Rohingya population including different gendered and aged vulnerable groups and persons with disabilities shall be given opportunities under volunteer/CFW scheme, while engaging them meaningfully not only in shelter work but also emergency preparedness and response. If government permits and SCCCM sector suggests, Habitat will also do piloting on shelter construction with weather resistant materials, where FDMN will be meaningfully involved.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

HFHI-B is dedicated to foster social cohesion between FDMN and host communities by promoting adaptive and contextual shelter solution for local communities in Cox’s Bazar, considering Cox’s Bazar District Development plan. Habitat will conduct participatory needs assessments and feedback sessions to understand unique preferences, risks, and community suggestions, ensuring the shelters align with local needs. Habitat will promote locally sourced materials whenever possible, which can reduce costs, support the local economy, and ensure materials are accessible for future maintenance. Habitat will promote shelter design that leverages climate-smart, energy-efficient technologies. Designed shelters that are resilient to Cox's Bazar’s climate and frequent natural hazards, such as heavy rains, high winds, and flooding will be prioritized. Habitat will arrange sessions in construction techniques, shelter maintenance, and resilient building practices.
