Project Summary

Through the proposed program outlined herein, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to housing, through restoration of community infrastructure, mitigation of safety risks, and support returning and affected families with shelter and housing-related services for later rehabilitation phase in Rural Damascus, Daraa, and Lattakia.
The Project will support return families with shelter rehabilitation, as well as revive neighborhoods by rehabilitating and improving essential infrastructure and housing-related services of vulnerable communities where those interventions are identified as a priority under the area-based recovery plan of the target municipalities.
The activities outlined in this program respond to the needs and priorities foreseen in 2024, but in the event of developments in situation that force a change in the program, UN-Habitat will inform OCAH and Shelter Sector to make appropriate adjustments.
Project Components:
Proceeding from UN-Habitat’s program of area-based local recovery planning, which is a participatory process includes representatives from local community, local and national authorities, line ministries and, where applicable, the international organizations, the program aims to facilitate access to housing and basic services through a combination of integrated activities including:
- Rehabilitation of 100 damaged houses
- Rehabilitation of common spaces in 75 damaged buildings including the entrance, stairs, staircase, etc. to secure accessibility to apartments.
- Assessment and repair of 20 structurally damaged buildings
- Whenever needed to secure access to houses, debris removal and management in streets and public spaces.
- Where imposing high risks, fill in underground tunnels that were dug during military operations beneath residential buildings to protect buildings from collapsing.
- Support to local relevant cadastral entities (Municipalities and Cadastral Departments) to restore its functions and to provide services in regards of ownership and tenure documentation to all affected families when needs are presented, including advocacy, technical support, and necessary trainings.
Specific activities per each target location will be prioritized and designed upon consultation with local communities and other stakeholders.
The selection of locations was based on a combination of criteria including
• Shelter Sector strategic priorities and severity scale in HNO and HRP 2024
• Large population presenting shelter needs, returnees, host community and high number of people in need
• Complementarity with other ongoing/planned interventions by UN-Habitat, and other programs in WASH and ERL sectors
• Level of damage and needs identified by UN-Habitat municipal recovery plans in target locations.
Based on the above, the project targets the following sub-districts:
Rural Damascus: Arbin, Nashabiyeh, Malihah.
Daraa: Daraa, Izraa

0 - Project Summary (extended)

With the crisis approaches its 13th year, 6.8M will require shelter support as a result of unprecedented economic downturn, limited availability of adequate shelter, limited longer-term solutions, and the additional damage resulted from the devastating earthquake hit Syria in Feb 6, 2023.Needs related to basic service delivery, livelihoods and income generation have grown consistently across Syria, these needs require a more comprehensive longer-term response and will remain inadequate and hampered by the lack of infrastructure, investment, human resources and, increasingly an unreliable electricity supply. In this plan, UN-Habitat is proposing shelter support to 9,500 individuals in the most vulnerable communities as follows: Rural Damascus: Arbin, Nashabiyeh, Malihah. ( Target: 6,200) Daraa: Daraa and Izraa (Target: 2,500)Lattakia: Jablah (Target: 300)Raqqa: Raqqa (Target 500)Implementation will adopt the following sequence: - Update the local area-based recovery plans identifying the priorities and areas of intervention through a participatory approach aimed to target speciﬁc geographical areas and vulnerable communities, through an integrated and comprehensive response. - Secure the authorities approval - Conduct detailed assessments for physical activities to update the action plan reflecting current and immediate needs. - Tendering process, awarding, and contracting • Implementation, completion and handover to stakeholders- Post Implementation Monitoring Through the project implementation, and in coordination with Municipalities and local authorities. The local community will have a signiﬁcant supervisory role in terms of projects’ selection and implementation. In addition to regular reporting to the FTS, all activities and indicators will be reported to the Shelter sector through regular 4W mechanisms. UN HABITAT has developed monitoring mechanisms including site visits reports, progress reports, inspection tools and ﬁnancial tracking tools. All these tools and templates will be utilized during the implementation to enhance the monitoring process. The Municipalities will have a key role in monitoring the implementation through the technical committees that shall be formed to supervise the work and issue the oﬃcial handover reports, also the implementing partners are required to provide regular reports showing the progress on the ground.The key risks identified in this project are:- Risk: Selection criteria is influence by local authorities may contribute to tension in the community. Mitigations: Engage with community leaders to raise awareness in the community, and agreed on the criteria among stakeholders and clearly communicated with the community. Standardize approaches among target locations.- Risk: Lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights incl civil documentation. Mitigations: Work with MOLAE to ensure alternative tenure documents are respected. Establish a mechanism to deal with the issue at local level.
