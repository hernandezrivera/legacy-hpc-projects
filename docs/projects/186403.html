Project Summary

Nkafamiya Rescue Mission will address the critical barriers to quality learning and retention for 500 conflict-affected children across some selected communities in Michika LGA, Adamawa State. The focus will be on both improving access to quality, protective education for out-of-school children and improving the quality of teaching and learning for children in formal schools. Teachers will be trained on PSS and child protection to ensure that schools are safer, more protective spaces for all children. Nkafamiya Rescue Mission will work
closely with communities and with other partners and stakeholders to build the capacity
of SMBC Members and CBCPC, improving sustainability and contributing to long-term systems strengthening.
In addition, Nkafamiya Rescue Mission integrated approach to EiE and CP in areas of operation will ensure that spread of COVID19 among vulnerable population is prevented through a continuous awareness campaign and that personnel involved with children are organized and trained to respond or prevent protection concerns with a system for safe reporting of incidents and are equipped with preventive and protective equipment. The targeted communities are Michika I and II.

1 - Needs

A characteristic of the conflict in North East Nigeria has been the targeting of schools, teachers and students by armed groups. The protracted conflict has severely impacted the already-weak school system in the North East, leaving the limited system unable to absorb the high numbers of displaced children looking to attend school. In addition to high numbers of out-of-school children, learning outcomes for children in-school (including foundational literacy and numeracy rates) are low and drop-out rates are high. An estimated 1,060,129 conflict-affected children across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states are estimated to be in need of education services; 697,683 of these children are displaced.A further 17,598 teachers are in need of training on psychosocial support (PSS). Factors limiting access to education in North East Nigeria have affected both boys and girls, but access to quality education has tended to be even more limited for girls than for boys, and adolescent girls are amongst the most vulnerable to dropping out of school. Rates or enrolment, retention and learning are even lower for children with disabilities (CwD) who are often excluded from mainstream learning and social life.Challenges faced by CwD includes high levels of stigmatisation, a lack of trained teachers, a lack of inclusive facilities and inclusive learning aids, and the high cost of education and specialised education materials. There is a need to better integrate and respond to the barriers that particularly vulnerable groups, including girls and CwD, face in accessing quality learning opportunities in safe and protective learning environments.Recognising the key role education plays in providing immediate physical, psychosocial and cognitive protection in emergency contexts, there is a need to build safe and protective learning spaces. Building the capacity of teachers on psychosocial skills and child protection knowledge is crucial to ensure that education is protective and builds life-savings knowledge on topics such as health, hygiene and GBV (gender-based violence). At the same time, given that the low educational outcomes in NE Nigeria pre-date the current crisis, there is also a need to ensure that the support provided contributes to strengthening the system as a whole and in the long-term. Northern Nigeria has a large Islamic and Quranic school sector.

2 - Activities

Nkafamiya Rescue Mission will provide Education in Emergencies support to a total of 500 (300 Formal; 200 Non-formal) children in the selected communities in Michika LGA. 20 (15 formal; 5 non-formal) teachers will be involved in the project with 60% of them female. The OOSC will be placed into temporary learning spaces (TLCs) supervised by Community Volunteer Teachers (CVTs). CVTs will be trained and receive an allowance to facilitate these non-formal classes, including training on PSS and child protection. The learning progress of children attending Temporary Learning Spaces TLCs will be regularly assessed, with the aim of mainstreaming children into the formal system to allow them to continue their education. NRM will work closely with the local and state education authorities to facilitate this mainstreaming. Similarly, NRM will work with the education authorities to achieve cordial accreditation of CVTs through the Federal Teacher Scheme, allowing thesecommunity members to become government teachers and be included on the government payroll for sustainability. To support the strengthening of the formal education system and improve learning outcomes in formal schools for all children, NRM will train government primary school teachers on psychosocial skills. This will include training teachers to implement the innovative Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) approach, with the aim of remediating lack of skills in foundational literacy and numeracy. Formal school support will also include the provision of teaching and learning materials and adaptation of school structures to be moreinclusive, and the training of teachers on inclusive, child-friendly pedagogical skills. This will empower teachers to be able to meet the differing learning and support needs of different children, particularly with the aim of ensuring the needs of CwD in accessing education and quality learning are met. Community Education Committees (CECs) or School-Based Management Committees (SBMCs) will be established or strengthened, and trained on children's rights and protection mechanisms, inclusion, school management and disaster risk reduction. These committees will be responsible for managing and maintaining the learning centres, and for sustained community engagement on the importance of education. In addition to building sustainability, this will also support the process of community entry and acceptance. In line with NRM’s commitment to integrated education and child protection programming, teachers, teacher mentors, CEC/SMBC members and other school personnel will be trained on basic child protection principles and on how to prevent, identify and respond to child protection concerns. This will include supporting schools to develop safeguarding clear preventive and responsive safeguarding mechanisms, in order to build more protective learning environments. Covid-19 prevention awareness messaging will continue through targeted awareness raising so that children, teachers and the entire community continue to protect themselves, to reduce the spread of the virus. These messages will be provided in local languages (Hausa and Kamwe) and child friendly tailored. Several means of disseminations will be utilized according to their availability in each targeted location as audio and visual materials.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Throughout project implementation, NRM will use proven mechanisms to engage community members and stakeholders and to ensure accountability and transparency. Community participation will be integrated into all stages of the project, from the involvement of community leaders in beneficiary selection to putting in place effective Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) to collect feedback to inform monitoring and evaluation. All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with NRM’s commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning. A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) assistant will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and NRM to share this feedback. The CRM assistant will hold monthly help desk sessions in each community, where beneficiaries and community members can share feedback. A register of all complaints made, and responses to these complaints, will be kept. The help desk will also be set up during all key activities, such as grant disbursement. In addition to programmatic feedback mechanisms, NRM has a toll-free line which can be called to report emergency incidents and safeguarding concerns. The number for this line will be displayed at all project sites.
