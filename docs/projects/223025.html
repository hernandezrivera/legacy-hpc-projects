Project Summary

The project aims to (1) improve the quality, accessibility, and inclusion of response services for GBV survivors, reinforcing the linkages between case management, MHPSS, medical care, livelihoods, education, and legal services for the most vulnerable groups; (2) mitigate GBV and PSEA risks through multi-level and cross-sectoral strategies, including capacity building and community-led (especially by women and girls) initiatives and; (3) prevent GBV by motivating significant changes in social norms undergirding violence, systematic discrimination, and gender and age inequalities through structured and evidence-based programs. Highly vulnerable and underserved populations represent UNHCR priorities, including people with disabilities and gender-diverse groups. UNHCR and partners work based on community, right-based, and survivor-centered approaches, engaging refugees as active agents and emerging leaders for behavioral changes within their communities. We focus on increasing women's and girls’ participation in decision-making and leadership platforms by building their confidence and capacities in skills-building and vocational training sessions, as well as offering livelihood opportunities and entrepreneurship training. Engaging female refugees of all ages in community-based protection plans and involving men and boys as allies in preventing GBV are UNHCR priorities to defend gender equality in all sectors. Key components of the 2025 GBV programming include improving the quality of safe spaces, delivering life, vocational skills, and continuous MHPSS, enhancing community volunteers’ capacities on how to work with people with disabilities and GDP, engaging men in accountable practices, encouraging women-led initiatives, and delivering women’s leadership training to make them empowered to make their own life decisions. Considering the recent trends and the increase in GBV incidents due to the security situation in the camps and the harmful impact of food insecurity, UNHCR and IPs will continue to provide case management and specialized MHPSS to survivors with particular attention to strengthening the survivor-centered care practices within the multi-sectoral referral network and increasing accessibility for and capacity to serve target populations, gender, and age-wide. A more integrated approach to GBV response services is prioritized to streamline our services and improve free access. Capacity-building components include caseworkers’ minimum training package on case management and IASC Minimum standards. GBV risks will be mitigated through a three-tiered strategy: (i) working with the community, (ii) UNHCR multi-functional, and (iii) inter-agency levels. Community-level work will include GBV risk mapping to develop a comprehensive and multi-sectorial risk mitigation strategy using the results obtained during the implementation of safety audit tools by all technical units. Finally, the inter-agency work will be done in collaboration with other agencies to mainstream GBV risk mitigation across all sectors in the Rohingya humanitarian response and an initiative for protecting refugees from SEA. UNHCR and its partners will implement structured and evidence-based GBV prevention activities that take a whole-of-society approach (SASA! Together) and those that target specific groups (Girl Shine, and Male Role Models), promoting the centrality of protection and AGD approach. Despite UNHCR mainly focusing on the refugee camps, we are also present in 6 Host Community areas, Teknaf, Thaingkhali, Whykong, Leda, Ukhyia, and Cox's Bazar, implementing and assisting the most vulnerable groups at six GBV and health facilities dedicated to LGBTI individuals, sex workers, and extremely vulnerable adolescents (EVA) at risk, as per sector strategy. These facilities are managed by Bandhu as UNHCR IP and work based on an inclusive perspective, delivering unique services to refugees and members of the host community.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Rohingya refugees who fled Myanmar in 2017 were escaping violence that included widespread gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse against females and males, which inflicted lasting traumas on the population. These and more recent arrivals joined an existing Rohingya refugee community in Bangladesh, which shares social norms that maintain and reinforce gender inequalities and systematic discrimination that enable and perpetuate gender-based violence, especially against women and girls within their communities. According to the main findings of community consultations conducted in 2024, the risk of rape and sexual abuse was the top concern among girls 18 years old and under, followed by child marriage and human trafficking. Although the risk of rape and sexual abuse remains high across all age groups, statistics on intimate partner violence (IPV) increase as a concern with age. The threat of trafficking is also most significant for young women and adolescent girls, now with the harmful use of technologies and a new spick on cyberbullying being reported. GBVIMS report shows that incidents in Q2 2024 increased by 8% compared to Q1 2024, and according to recent statistics and the protection scenario in the camps, the trend is to increase the number and severity of GBV cases significantly. Recent assessments (ISNA, SASA! Surveys, FGDs, GBVIMS) have revealed a grim protection picture. Refugees feel unsafe both within the community and inside their shelters, particularly women and girls. During community consultations, women have reported violence in the home and violence in the community as their top two perceived risks. According to the GBVIMS Report in 2024, intimate partner violence is the most reported form of GBV, with 78% of the total. GBV cases are chronicle underreporting, meaning that reports can’t show an accurate and global reflection of reality. From the same report, we observed that 99% of survivors are female, and there is an essential lack of information related to the violence perpetrated against the LGBTI population. Social norms restricting females’ public movement create barriers to services and reduce the number of GBV reports. The camp design presents other risks, and access to functional latrines and lack of proper lighting remain a substantial GBV risk. Parents fear for the safety of their adolescent daughters and restrict their movements to prevent sexual harassment. Others encourage child marriage, trusting that their daughters will be safer outside the camps where they are protected by the husband’s family. In 2024, UNHCR observed an exponential increase in protection cases involving women at risk due to severe stress, anxiety crises, and PTSD effects. After having their fundamental rights continuously denied - mainly related to the lack of food, freedom, and shelter, women are experiencing more severe mental health conditions that require attention and specialized services. Men in the camps are opportunistically practicing polygamy, while men in neighboring countries are arranging the smuggling of young brides despite the journey’s grave risks. Human smuggling and trafficking in the region have become ‘feminized,’ with more women and girls forced into dangerous journeys. Online gambling became a GBV contributing factor, as well as the consequent and persistent food insecurity. Pre-existing risks have been further exacerbated for critical populations in host and refugee communities, including gender-diverse people and individuals engaging in sex work. Male survivors of sexual violence are similarly underserved, but the GBVIMS has captured an increasing trend of male survivors seeking services. Despite protective legislation, access to justice for GBV survivors—both refugee and host—is limited by justice system inefficacy and distrust. Rohingya refugees are reluctant to seek remedies in the formal legal system.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNHCR Implementing Partners, as well as UNHCR staff members, work based on the four principles of GBV programming: (i) Safety and Security, (ii) Confidentiality, (iii) Dignity and Respect, and (iv) Non-discrimination. UNHCR’s protection programs are built on analyzing community power structures and cultural norms and aim to strengthen existing community capacities while ensuring access to and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. UNHCR and partners provide access to services through community centers that provide integrated protection interventions in a non-stigmatizing manner and also ensure outreach activities reach those who cannot or do not feel comfortable attending centers. UNHCR conducts regular community consultations, risk assessments, and safety audits that engage men, women, boys, and girls, including persons with disabilities, persons with diverse sexual orientations and gender identities, and persons with specific needs to identify and mitigate risks, and to optimize safety, dignity, and equity. UNHCR works closely with HI to proactively identify people with disabilities living in all camps refer cases for further protection in third countries and deliver immediate assistance to mitigate protection/GBV risks. All IPs also conduct disability assessments to make facilities more welcoming and accessible to all individuals. UNHCR works from a protection, gender, and disability mainstreaming perspective. All activities implemented by partners and UNHCR aim to identify potential protection risks confidentially and make safe referrals to local service providers. Activities are led by the Do not Harm principle and survivor and rights-centered approaches, and all partnerships are signed based on a previous and transparent assessment of the organizational capabilities to prevent and respond to SEA/SH during the execution of their contract, in line with the zero-tolerance policy advocated by the UN system.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR protects children by responding to their specific needs and mitigating risks they face before, during, or after displacement. Considering 2024 GBVIMS statistics, 93% of child marriages are reported by girls and 7% by boys, representing the main GBV risk highlighted by this population. The same report informs that underreporting of child marriage has been a constant challenge, interlinked with other types of GBV. Recent trends show high risks related to the harmful impact of cyberbullying affecting adolescent girls that may lead to human trafficking or even suicide. From the GBV programming, significant efforts are dedicated to coordinating and creating awareness campaigns focused on mitigating the risks and supporting child survivors. UNHCR works to ensure immediate access to appropriate services and provides durable solutions based on the child's best interest, also capacitating staff on child-sensitive techniques for case management. UNHCR implements the Girl Shine program as an evidence-based curriculum and also oversees a mentorship program led by adolescent girls in 17 camps. These programs aim to offer an alternative life perspective to develop their skills and confront gender social norms. To keep enhancing community empowerment, UNHCR works based on an inclusive approach to promote gender equality and protect children, GDP, and people with disabilities, at higher risk of GBV and SEA. UNHCR and IPs engage outreach volunteers to reach out to and include refugees in all activities, focusing on adolescents, youth, GDP, adults, elderlies, and persons with disabilities. UNHCR GBV facilities are child and disabled-friendly, with trained national staff and volunteers able to provide services and conduct safe identification and confidential referrals of GBV cases, based on an integrated approach without discrimination. UNHCR's partnership with HI is also an asset that added value to our program as all cases receive proper attention and specialized services.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNHCR programming is defined based on the 2020 Global GBV Policy, which determines three working pillars: (i) Prevention, (ii) Risk Mitigation, and (iii) Response. Under the Risk Mitigation pillar, UNHCR adopts the protection and gender mainstreaming approach, working with all technical units to create dedicated work plans to mitigate GBV and SEA risks. Effective and accountable humanitarian responses require (i) continuous and meaningful engagement with persons that we serve, (ii) understanding their needs and protection risks, (iii) building on their capacities, and (iv) pursuing protection, assistance, and solutions that consider their perspectives and priorities. Our responses include continuous and meaningful engagement with the people we serve by assessing their needs through continuous community consultations and the FDG process, besides building their capacities with training and monitoring strategies. In 2024, UNHCR successfully launched a comprehensive complaint and reporting mechanism (CAIPP), uniting our helpline and the CFRM desks located in all 33 camps to increase communication with communities and accountability to the population we serve. The UNHCR helpline is fully operational and reformed, aggregating a new, innovative, and inclusive online platform with accessible information oriented towards refugees of all ages and genders, which makes the service more streamlined and active 24/7 to receive complaints, deliver helpful information about service providers, and make confidential referrals. UNHCR is therefore supporting strengthening AAP, including protection from SEA, within the wider humanitarian community. All staff working under a UNHCR Partnership agreement are trained on CoC and PSEA annually. Internal focal points are assigned, together with PSEA FP, to ensure the implementation of a zero-tolerance policy on misconduct and to promote an equal and healthy environment.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

PSEA/SH is a crucial cross-cutting area for all UNHCR programs. Since its conception, UNHCR has played an active role as a member of the PSEA Network, a coordination structure to prevent, mitigate, and harmonize the response against SEA/SH incidents in Bangladesh, having a dedicated group organized for the Rohingya Response and a second one based in Dhaka. Together with IOM, UNHCR will lead the exercise to review the internal SoP and harmonize the document with the global template, organizing consultations and defining the main areas of improvement. UNHCR is also involved in the 2025 Inter-Agency Risk Assessment that will be conducted in all camps through FDG and KII dedicated to identifying and mitigating SEA risks. All UNHCR Implementing Partners are also members of the Network and follow the Inter-Agency SoP review in 2024. In 2023, UNHCR assessed all Implementing Partners working under a partnership agreement on their capacities to prevent, mitigate, and respond to SEA/SH, guaranteeing excellence, safe and confidential deliverance of services. UNHCR staff is annually trained on CoC and PSEA/SH, as well as all personnel working for UNHCR Implementing Partners, including volunteers. UNHCR delivers an annual refresher to Focal Points from NGOs and INGOs and monitors their performance to cascade the sessions, focusing on the safe identification and confidential referral of cases. UNHCR's priority is to enhance two-way communication with communities. Our outreach activities use non-verbal and accessible language, as well as pictorial posters that motivate the debates around important protection subjects, such as SEA. Outreach activities as well as structured-prevention activities reinforce the availability of our complaint and reporting system, highlighting the importance of a timely report. Videos, podcasts, and posters created in the Rohingya language are used to raise awareness and deliver clear information concerning the referral pathway.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR will remain a central player within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for the monsoon and cyclone responses. UNHCR includes monsoon, cyclone, landslide, fire, and other disaster preparedness aspects in communicating with communities’ activities and community initiatives. UNHCR trains community members to mitigate risks and quickly respond to climate concerns. When disasters occur, UNHCR targets protection services to respond to the needs of those affected, focusing on women, children, and persons with specific needs. UNHCR-built facilities include consideration of environmental and climate concerns and DRR. Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) consisting of 55 teams with 330 members (consisting of staff from agencies and refugee volunteers) including PERU members with specialization on CP and GBV, supported by the Protection Sector, will continue to engage with the community to disseminate key protection messages, identify urgent cases and referrals, and monitor the situation of Persons with Specific Needs. During emergencies, PERU teams ensure that protection mainstreaming and strengthening of community involvement in all Sectors is met, and the response on the ground is in line with Protection principles and sensitive to Age, Gender, Disability, and Diversity concerns. As part of the Protection Emergency Response Preparedness Plan (linked to the ISCG Multi-Hazard Response Plan), the Rapid Protection Assessment (RPA) approach was revised and reformed into 3 Rapid Protection Assessment teams (consisting of General Protection, CP, GBV, Inclusion, and Gender Diverse Population specialists) with a focus on protection risks after an emergency, response, advocacy, and mobilization.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR developed a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) which was implemented during the political crisis in late July and August 2024. The BCP, which does not require additional cost, relies on refugee volunteers who are present in the camps, utilizing remote modalities such as mobile phones, SMS texts, and other means, to connect with refugees facing dire protection needs, conduct advocacy with the authorities, and facilitate necessary interventions. UNHCR has also developed a relationship with the authorities to allow limited convoys of staff into the camps even under crisis conditions like those that occurred in July/August 2024. Furthermore, a BCP was developed for assistance distribution using the offline modality of the Global Distribution Tool (GDT), a system to track and record the distribution of assistance connected with proGres database, was successfully implemented during the internet outage in July/ August 2024.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

According to recent assessments, root causes and contributing factors of GBV and child abuse are increasing within the Rohingya Camps. Online gambling, food insecurity, polygamy, and abuse of illegal substances are frequently mentioned by GBV survivors as factors that contribute to violence in a society where gender equality is far from being achieved and systematic gender discrimination is predominant. Beyond the classic types of GBV and the substantial predominance of physical abuse incidents, a new wave of online violence (cyberbullying) is increasing and impacting the lives of female adolescents. Identifying new communication channels to raise awareness among adolescents and adapting the formal language used during the outreach session remains a challenge. UNHCR is working in an offline and online campaign oriented to youth to mitigate the risks of the harmful use of the internet, also dedicated to raising awareness on the new types of GBV. With limited access to socio-educational opportunities, more boys are engaging in negative coping mechanisms, leading to an increase in GBV incidents. Qualitative data collected from the community show an increasing number of boys and youth involved in online gambling, demonstrating the urgent need to work closely with this group to mitigate its consequences. Since 2023 UNHCR has been delivering numeracy and literacy sessions to boys and girls participating in the GBV prevention structured activities as an extra curriculum activity, offering new educational opportunities tailored to their specific needs. This program achieves excellent results and brings youth new hope. UNHCR created a new program to propose mentorship to adolescent girls in 17 camps. Since July 2024, 140 girls have been capacitated and are already working within their communities to establish trustful safe spaces and disseminate key messages related to GBV prevention and mitigation, as well as the referral pathway.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR GBV program has shifted its focus to endorse a more sustainable approach, prioritizing gender equality/inclusion as the main strategy. After 8 years of emergency mainly focusing on establishing the response centers and capacitating staff, UNHCR is now putting the focus on women’s empowerment and leadership as a way to break to cycle of violence. GBV survivors and women-at-risk are actively participating in capacity-building sessions delivered by members of the host community, following a community-based approach, and focusing on enhancing peaceful coexistence. Structured sessions on beautification, farming and gardening, tailoring, and hand painting were simultaneously delivered in 10 camps. In partnership with other units and sectors, 126 females were trained as firefighters and basic electricians, and are now being integrated into the daily camp activities to ensure their participation, but also inspiring and mentoring their peers on how to challenge common gender stereotypes
