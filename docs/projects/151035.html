Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to improve access to immediate food needs for 40800 vulnerable conflict affected IDPs while improving Sustainable reintegration of Returnees and IDPs within their original place of return in agropastoral and riverine Communities in the lower Shebelle and Bandir regions of South Somalia.

Needs

Somalia’s overall progress after decades of conflict and political turmoil continues to be encouraging with mixed reactions pushing progress back and forth.. As a result of all of these preceding factors, FSNAU report indicates over 2.3 million people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 802,000 are in Emergency (IPC Phase4), totaling over 3.1 million people who need urgent humanitarian assistance. Those facing crisis are the IDPs across Somalia who remain extremely vulnerable. A majority of them are acutely food insecure. IDPs in the main settlements have also been affected by rising food prices and cost of living in urban areas. With significant influx of newly displaced people into IDP settlements in Mogadishu, many of them reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security and lack of access to basic needs. Specific concern is the large number of IDP communities from conflict areas who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks related to hazardous livelihood activities. Equally, rural communities in project target areas of Lower Shebelle has been severely affected by the impact of conflict. Refugee returns have increased substantially compared to previous years. Refugee returns are taking place in the context of ongoing conflict, violence, insecurity, absence of effective institutions and lack of infrastructure, which continue to undermine conditions for conducive solutions. The main challenges being faced by returnees is lack of access to employment opportunities and basic services such as health, education, food and shelter (UNHCR).

Activities

1. Provision of Unconditional cash transfers to 1500 IDP (60% women) households living in Daynille and Kaxda District of Banadir region. Target IDP HH’s will receive $65 unconditional cash per cycle for 3 distribution cycles. The transfer value is based on the CWG recommended 100% of the revised food MEB for food security cash transfers2. Provision of seasonally appropriate agricultural farm inputs (certified quality seeds and fertilizers) to 1200 beneficiaries for one seasons3. Training of 300 ToT farmers (of which 50% are women) on good Agricultural (GAP) and postharvest practice for Riverine farmers in Afgooye District. Each of the trained ToT farmer will be tasked to pass knowledge to at least four (4) active farmers in their respective community. The use of farmer to famer ToT approach is to provide GAP training at a more cost effective and safe approach of knowledge transfer, in addition, the approach is expected to promote internal learning and knowledge transfer within the community.4. Provision of 5 small generators and 5 low cost hey making machine (such as money maker hey pallor) to improve local fodder production and management among 50 small scale farmers in Afgooye District. (Targeting 5 groups @ 10 small scale farmer)5. Training of Community based animal health workers to equip them with professional knowledge on livestock treatment & diagnostic skills to provide access to quality vet services to the people.6. Provision of start up drug KIT for 10 CAHWS7. Support the strengthening of private agro vets and link them to a major drug stores known to provide quality drug in order to increase access to quality drugs8. Rehabilitation of 6 water catchment in six rural villages through cash for work9. Identification and selection of vulnerable 300 households. It is envisaged that the target group shall be 100% returnee Households. 10. Training of 300 returnee household beneficiaries on the technical aspects of setting up an Agri business, effective

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention shall be community based and driven; it seeks to support the needs and rights of both displaced populations and the wider-host communities in which they are settled. The projects activities aim to build the agricultural livelihoods of displaced women, women, girls and boys, and their host communities through a two pronged approach of (i) Providing immediate life-saving assistance and (ii) reinforcing community structures by building the knowledge and skills of target groups to improve resilience against future shocks as well facilitate return, resettlement or integration.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The number of displaced persons in the target locations is growing by the day; vulnerable groups such as minorities and weaker clans, women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities and older people are not receiving adequate or appropriate assistance. These groups are being , harassed, discriminated , abused and extorted simply because among other things they are newly displaced and lack social connections in their new places of settlement, negatively impacting their dignity and ability to access humanitarian services. The project seeks to mitigate the above risks by means of scaling up assistance to IDPs and providing similar assistance to host community groups, where they are experiencing similar lack of livelihood opportunities. IDP and host community leadership structures shall be involved in jointly assessing and identifying livelihood needs with a view to reducing tensions and conflict between host and IDP communities. All targeted beneficiaries shall be identified and registered to prevent exclusion, especially for vulnerable groups.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The current humanitarian context in the target location is contributing to among other risks, increased cases of Gender Based Violence, especially for women and girls, often with inadequate access to reporting and referral mechanisms or accountability for perpetrators.The project shall seek to promote the equitable participation of both men and women in the planning and decision making processes; this will ultimately help to ensure that the entire affected population and specifically targeted vulnerable groups (women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities) are granted adequate and appropriate livelihood support and food security solutions.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

IDP and Host Community families in the target locations are severely resource-poor, with livelihood opportunities and food sources being very limited as a result of the continued armed conflict and violence. The competition among these groups for the scarce available resources is also a potential catalyst for violent conflict between communities, groups, clans or families. The project seeks to identify and register all targeted beneficiaries, with the aim of firstly, preventing any potential exclusion and secondly, ensuring the provision of adequate and appropriate assistance, especially to vulnerable groups. Measures shall be put in place to ensure all livelihood opportunities and food security solutions provided, promote the safety, security, and dignity of beneficiaries, with special focus given to women, children, persons with disabilities and the elderly.
