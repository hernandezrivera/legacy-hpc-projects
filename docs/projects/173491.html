Project Summary

The Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group (GiHA WG) for the Rohingya Refugee crisis response in Cox’s Bazar provides coordination, monitoring, information sharing and advocacy across and within sectors to ensure that gender equality commitments are at the core of the response. Furthermore, the GIHA WG through its co-chairs and gender focal points - and with the additional dedicated leadership, expertise and resources provided through the Gender Hub - is contributing to technical support, capacity development and analysis for gender mainstreaming in the response. The GiHA WG members work in both camps and host communities including with Rohingya women CBOs and host community women’s rights organisations. The GiHA WG is co-chaired by UN Women and UNHCR and its members gender focal points representing sectors, WGs and humanitarian organisations. UN Women will maintain the secretariat role of GiHA WG.
The Gender Hub’s directive is to strengthen accountability of all humanitarian actors for gender equality and empowerment in line with the IASC Police on Gender Equality and Women’s Equality and Women’s Empowerment in Humanitarian Action and its Accountability Framework.
The Gender Hub works to strengthen the accountability of humanitarian actors for gender equality and empowerment of women and girls from Rohingya refugees and host communities. To do so, the Gender Hub will support all actors, including local agencies and government, to plan, program and implement the humanitarian response in a gender and diversity sensitive and responsive way. In coordination with the GiHA WG members, the Gender Hub frequently brings together women’s rights groups from both Rohingya and host communities to support capacity building and coordination across the women’s rights movement. The Gender Hub has three programme pillars to humanitarian actors: technical assistance, capacity development, and knowledge management to increase access to gender-related technical guidance and support at a strategic level.

1 - Identified Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated gender discrimination and inequality among the hundreds of thousands of Rohingya living in refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar and among the surrounding communities. Women and girls face increases in unpaid care work at home, safety risks inside and outside their homes, mental health problems, and simultaneously, less access to life-saving services and support. Vulnerable groups and people who already were marginalized and excluded in society such as LGBTQI persons, people with disabilities, and sex workers, have all faced greater challenges and risks. Rohingya women and girls are not merely vulnerable victims. They play a key role in increasing the resilience of families and communities and in building social cohesion across the Rohingya and host communities. Changing family structures due to the humanitarian crisis are creating new duties and new activities, which is opening space for new gender roles to emerge. There are Rohingya women leaders, including survivors of conflict related sexual and gender-based violence, that are self-organizing and forming their own groups in the camps to advocate for their rights and call for justice. Leveraging women’s participation and leadership capacities is not only a way to ensure humanitarian efforts respond to the specific needs and vulnerabilities of affected communities – whether women, men, girls or boys- but also a strategic investment in whole community resilience. For these reasons, integrating gender equality measures as well as age and diversity dimensions into the cross-sectoral crisis response efforts is critical to ensuring that women, girls, boys and men — particularly the most vulnerable and marginalized — have equitable access to (and benefit from) relief, services and information. This will improve humanitarian and COVID-19 programming and make it more rights-based and effective in meeting the needs of Rohingya Refugees and the host community. Sectors and working groups, including ERP, should make gender equality a central priority in Sector Response Plans and Emergency Contingency Plans, which will guide project partners and the overall response in designing projects that meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men equally/equitably. While the humanitarian response is based on the IASC Policy and Accountability Framework on Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women and Girls in Humanitarian Action, and the Protection Framework, and efforts have been made to increase integration of gender equality and women’s empowerment programming into the response, COVID-19 has set back many efforts, and significant gaps remain. These include gaps in collecting sex and age-disaggregated data, understanding of the gendered impacts of crisis, incorporating substantive women’s participation and leadership in humanitarian response, provision of targeted livelihood opportunities for women and gender diverse populations, and insufficient protection mechanisms for women and girls continue to impede the realization of effective gender-responsive policies and programmes in humanitarian and crisis response. Through the rapid gender analysis, “In the Shadows of the Pandemic: the Gendered Impact of COVID-19 on Rohingya and Host Communities”, the midterm review of the JRP 2020 and the COVID-19 Addendum, achievements have been made by various sectors however there is still a lack of understanding among humanitarian staff on the importance and need to mainstream gender and protection across the response; a lack of prioritization of the issue; and persisting gaps in inclusion and access to services across camps and sectors for persons with specific needs, in particular adults and children with disabilities, older persons, chronically or severely ill persons, and persons living with HIV/AIDS, LGBTI persons, sex workers, adolescent girls, female headed households and unaccompanied and separated girls and boys.

2 - Activities or outputs

Coordination and Technical Assistance:- Convening regular meetings of GiHA WG members, gender focal points, advisers and advocates from each ISCG sector and key organisations to exchange information and address gender equality issues in the humanitarian actions (needs, gaps, response efforts) of the sectors.- Develop, provide assistance in the implementation and monitor Sector Gender Action Plans to accompany the implementation of Sector Strategic Responses 2021.- Identifying opportunities for learning of good practices across sectors and organisations regularly undertake capacity development on gender-responsive humanitarian action, preparedness, resilience and recovery, with an additional focus on gender inclusion in action and recovery from COVID-19- Support humanitarian actors on disaster preparedness and response (DDR) through support to and engagement in the Emergency Preparedness and Response Working Group (ERP WG), as well as through supporting sector specific gender mainstreaming in emergency preparedness, including Food Security, Health, SMSD, Shelter, Education and WASH.- Support Energy and Environment Technical Working Group (EETWG) on incorporating gender perspectives and considerations in environmental sustainability activities- Including local women’s rights organisations and networks and local gender equality actors in the GiHA WG to promote their influence and engagement in all aspects of response, including through engagement in ISCG coordination structures and camp management and oversight committees.- Strengthening cohesion across Rohingya and host community CBOs and women’s rights organizations through joint events to identify shared challenges and opportunities - Convening sub-WG on women’s leadership and support implementation of their annual workplan to promote women's leadership.- Supporting the sub-WG on gender diversity and implement key agreed joint activities to promote rights of gender diverse people.Advocacy:- Promote ISCG sectors and organizations’ commitment to gender equality and women’s empowerment in humanitarian action by all humanitarian actors in the Rohingya Refugee crisis response and additional COVID-19 related activities. - Promote accountability on gender equality results.- Organise joint advocacy campaigns and events e.g. 16 days of activism against GBV, International Women’s Day, World Refugee Day, etc.Assessment, Analysis and Monitoring- Support GAM review of JRP COVID 19 Addendum and GAM analysis of JRP 2021, monitoring of the JRP 2021, and design of JRP 2022.- Conduct comprehensive inter-agency gender analysis of the Rohingya Refugee crisis - Conduct joint GiHA WG field monitoring in camps and host communities to measure sectors’ progress on gender mainstreaming through use of gender indicators and the IASC gender with age marker, and feed findings and recommendations back to the Gender Hub, Sectors and the ISCG.- Promote gender-equal community led monitoring and feedback mechanisms including through participation of women and women led CBOs.Capacity Building:- Develop and share learning manuals for gender mainstreaming within sectors through the Gender Capacity Development Specialists- Develop capacity and support learning activities conducted on Gender Mainstreaming in the nexus humanitarian and long-term development including training on gender mainstreaming in sectors, gender induction for humanitarian staff, disability inclusion, training of trainers for pool of local trainers, gender monitoring and evaluation, GAM refreshers, and other training as required Information Sharing and Management:- Identify, analyse, and report on needs, gaps, good practices - Routinely meet, share information and issues of concern on gender - these issues can then be raised with the ISCG, SEG, and the UNCT Cox’s Bazar Task Force in conjunction with the Gender Hub.- Ensure linkages and information sharing- Share lessons learned and good practices
