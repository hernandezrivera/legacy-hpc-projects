1 - Needs

Around 1.8 million Palestinians across the oPt, 64 per cent in the Gaza Strip and 36 per cent in the West Bank, experience or are at risk of conflict, violence and displacement, including forcible transfer. The overall deterioration in the severity of the protection risks in the oPt has continued in 2021, exacerbated by socioeconomic distress in the context of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, as well as related movement restrictions, school closures and job losses. Through this project, the Protection Cluster will coordinate responses aimed at addressing the following specific protection risks that arise in the context of the protracted protection crisis in the oPt: 1. MARKED DETERIORATION IN PHYSICAL WELL-BEING, DEPRIVATION OF LIBERTY AND ACCESS RESTRICTIONSThe most serious escalation of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups in Gaza since the 2014 conflict took place between 10 and 21 May 2021. In parallel, there was a sharp rise in protests, clashes and arrests across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, as well as in some communities in Israel. These events have exacerbated the already severe protection needs throughout the oPt, particularly in Gaza. Overall in the oPt, protection vulnerabilities continue to be driven by the longstanding Israeli occupation and Gaza blockade which continue to physically and administratively restrict and interfere with freedom of movement and the ability of the conflict-affected populations to access assistance and services. 2.DISPLACEMENT AND RISK OF FORCIBLE TRANSFERHouseholds in Area C, East Jerusalem and the H2 area of Hebron city continue to face a coercive environment, placing Palestinian individuals and communities at risk of forcible transfer due to a range of longstanding Israeli policies and practices. A number of significant legal and administrative developments in the West Bank, including efforts affecting the settlement of land title in both Area C and East Jerusalem, new building permit and access regimes in Area C and the Seam Zone respectively, as well as the application of policies that have led to a sharp rise in so called “self-demolitions” in East Jerusalem, and an increase in seizure and confiscation of structures and material goods in Area C. These developments are expected to further intensify the coercive environment in 2022, hinder the delivery of humanitarian assistance, and result in greater humanitarian suffering. Communities are in need of multiple legal aid services, as well as increased advocacy requiring further monitoring, documentation and legal analysis. In Gaza, around 8,250 persons remained displaced as of end-August, primarily those whose houses were destroyed during the May escalation, or so damaged as to be uninhabitable. According to local authorities, over 2,000 housing units were totally destroyed or severely damaged, and an estimated 15,000 housing units sustained some degree of damage. This has led to a significant increase in demand for legal aid support for affected households, including for GBV survivors and widowed women relating to issues of denial of resources, custody of children and the loss of identity documents due to destruction of houses or while taking refuge. 3. PSYCHOLOGICAL DISTRESS AMONGST CHILDREN AND VULNERABLE GROUPS During 2021, child protection needs in the oPt have been compounded by the continuing COVID-19 pandemic, as well as an increase in conflict-related violence in the West Bank including East Jerusalem and the May 2021 escalation in Gaza. Against the backdrop of multi-sectoral and household vulnerabilities contributing to child protection risks, there are considerable needs for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) interventions including specialised case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers, particularly males. The Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR) estimates that some 678,000 children (345,800 boys, 332,200 girls) across the oPt are in need of child protection and MHPSS services, including 122,000 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialised services. More than half (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need, and 12 per cent of all children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Additionally, 185,000 caregivers overall, including 137,000 caregivers in Gaza, are also in need of various MHPSS services. 4. SPECIFIC RISKS FACING WOMEN AND GIRLS In 2021, decades of Israeli occupation and conflict related violence, compounded by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, has exacerbated pre-existing risks facing women and girls in the oPt of discrimination and gender-based violence (GBV), including early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Some ten per cent of households report that women and girls avoid areas near settlements, checkpoints, community areas and markets, and using public transportation because they feel unsafe. In the West Bank, 31 per cent of households and 19 per cent in Gaza are concerned about the safety and security of girls, while 24 per cent and 17 per cent respectively are concerned about the safety and security of women. The disproportionate impact of these contextual factors on women and girls is underpinned by socio-cultural patriarchal norms, which continue to undermine women and girl’s rights. Furthermore, COVID-19 and the May 2021 escalation resulted in an increase in reported GBV cases. SAWA, an emergency helpline, reported a 135% increase in caseload in May 2021 compared to May 2020. 5. EXPLOSIVE REMNANTS OF WARThe recent escalation in Gaza has left severe contamination of explosive remnants of war (ERW). Explosive ordnance exacerbated an already volatile living space with ERW from past escalations, and given rise to rise to an urgent need for humanitarian mine action interventions.

2 - Activities

Given the large membership of the cluster and the broad scope of the issues coordinatedunder the cluster, sufficient human resources are essential to ensure leadership andcoordination, as detailed in the project budget. In line with below-outlined activities, andworking with the chairs of the Protection Cluster sub-groups and with the Clustermembers, the coordination team will: 1. Provide vision and leadership for the ProtectionCluster, based on consultations with members and stakeholders; 2. Undertake day-to-daycoordination of Protection responses, including convening meetings, information sharingand ensuring Cluster inputs to humanitarian processes; 3. Co-Chair the Legal Task Forcein the West Bank, 4. Ensure coordination with the work of the PC sub-groups and AoRs.Related activities include ensuring coordination between working groups to ensurecomplementarity 5. Identify and propose action to address gaps, including in informationgathering, management, analysis and coordinated response to priority protectionconcerns, with a focus on increased analysis on gender-specific vulnerabilities andgender-specific impact of ongoing violations of IHL and IHRL, and persons with disability;6. Strengthen detection and referral for protection cases; 7. Strengthen ProtectionInformation Management under the protection cluster and feed into inter-sectoralresponse monitoring including 4Ws, demolitions and settler violence response trackers; 8.Coordinate joint protection monitoring and information analysis; 9. Coordinate jointhuman-rights based advocacy for affected populations under relevant priorities of theHCT Advocacy Strategy, including through liaising with UN human rights procedures andmechanisms, and link Protection Cluster advocacy to the work of the HCT AdvocacyWorking Group; 9. Through the Legal Advice Group, coordinate the discussion ofquestions of international human rights and humanitarian law, and the provision ofinternational law analysis to the HCT; 10. Through the Legal Task Force, which OHCHRwill co-chair with NRC, ensure that up-to-date information, legal advice, and analysis onemblematic cases, trends and protection risks is available to the ICCG and the HCT; 10.Work with the other clusters and sectors to mainstream protection, in order to ensure thatAccountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments are fulfilled and thatprotection is made central to the humanitarian response in all stages of the HPC.Activities in this regard include trainings and technical support to the provision ofguidelines, checklists, and coordination of operational responses to protection issues; 11.Build capacity of cluster members to strengthen communication with and accountabilityto affected populations (AAP), including through beneficiary feedback on protectionresponses, and specifically, by establishing a tracking tool to monitor progress on AAPthroughout the Humanitarian Program Cycle for all funded HRP projects under Protection;12. Facilitate regular support to Protection Cluster partners on PSEA and related capacity building, monitor compliance with minimum standards on PSEA 13. Needs assessments: work to identify baseline data and needs of persons with disabilities in the context of humanitarian action; support the efforts of working groups to address assessment gaps as identified in the HNO; 14. Under the lead of the ProtectionCluster gender focal point, provide support to cluster partners in gender mainstreaming alltheir activities, including by providing capacity-building support to partners to enhancegender sensitivity in the planning, implementation and monitoring of projects submitted tothe HRP; 13. Support resource mobilization by cluster partners; 15. Support resource mobilization by cluster partners; 16. Prepare Protection Cluster submissions to the 2023 HPC, including consultations with partners and AoRs on needs, priorities, development of new intervention areas and HRP project vetting; 17. Update West Bank and Gaza contingency plans for the Protection Cluster, and feed into Inter-Agency contingency plans and emergency preparedness as required.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The Protection Cluster is continuously engaged with Cluster Partners and the broader humanitarian response through the ICCG and the HCT, on ensuring the principle of Do No Harm remains central to all project planning and interventions in the oPt. HRP and HF project submissions are carefully vetted to ensure adequate risk analyses have taken place, and regularly provides support to other Clusters, primarily through its engagement with Protection Mainstreaming Focal Points, to ensure the tools and capacity are in place for all sectors to adequately address Do No Harm considerations.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Equal and impartial access to aid is highly prioritised in the vetting and assessment of HRP and HF projects submitted to the Protection Cluster. The Protection Cluster will hold regular technical support sessions on accessibility, and has partnered with Disabled People's Organisations (DPOs) to ensure adequate representation and technical expertise is available within cluster discussions, in project vetting and for general support of cluster partners. In 2022, the Protection cluster will prioritise initiatives aimed at addressing current assessment and information gaps particularly with regard to persons with disabilities in the oPt, including on their specific and compounded needs, to complement information gathered in 2021 through the MSNA. In addition, Protection Cluster team members will partake in capacity building initiatives, primarily facilitated through the Global Protection Cluster, to ensure team staff have the technical capacity to continue to provide adequate support to cluster partners.
