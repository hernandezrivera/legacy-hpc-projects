Project Summary

DRC intervention will particularly be focused on providing access to basic, safe, and dignified Shelter solutions, and having access to sufficient and safe water for domestic use which will assist in enabling access of the most vulnerable population groups to safe and dignified shelter solutions which contributes to the 2020 Shelter HRP Objective2: “Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.”
The conflict-affected population is assisted in compliance with the Sector and MoLAE standards. DRC seeks to engage with other DRC sectors to complement DRCs work this coordination and complementarity mechanism needs to be expressed accordingly between the shelter activities and achieving the principles of protection DRC will achieve this through the following outcomes:
1. Increased access to adequate gender- and disability-sensitive shelter, water, and hygiene infrastructure
among conflict-affected persons in Dara'a, Homs, and Aleppo.
DRC aims to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support through addressing overburdened communities with a high ratio of returnees and, members of host communities and IDPs in the rehabilitation of partially damaged apartments, DRC is aiming for a multi-sectoral integrated intervention of Shelter, Education and WASH sectors to increase access to life-saving emergency and life-sustaining assistance while also transitioning to more resilience/early recovery-oriented activities in selected locations of intervention to maximize the impact of humanitarian actions, and enabling access of the conflict-affected population to safe water for drinking and domestic use and safe excreta. A total of 15,00 beneficiaries and 30 PWDs will be reached out in different locations with a 3 and above severity scale.
DRC’s programme strategy is realized through two programme objectives which are responding to emergencies and addressing solutions to protracted displacement. When combined, these can provide a basis for a comprehensive response in all phases of the displacement cycle transitioning.
DRC will respond to emergencies where sudden population movements cause conflict-affected populations to be living in conditions posing immediate life-threatening risks as well as address needs arising due to the covid-19 pandemic.
IDPs assistance follows SHELTER Sector Standards will be provided in collective shelters, and private unfinished/demolished buildings in both, urban and rural areas. Newly displaced populations get prioritized as an emergency response including COVID 19 response, according to the Sector’s directive. The intervention will concentrate on areas where DRC has a current presence.
Selection of targeted beneficiaries will be done by DRC in close coordination with MoLA and SARC with verification of needs and beneficiaries by DRC. DRC will liaise with the Mukhtar, with any humanitarian actors present in the area, and with local residents to identify HHs, ensure coordination and avoid any overlap. DRC staff will have free access to move around the target areas scoping houses and approaching HHs directly to explain the scope of the intervention and offer to conduct an initial technical assessment. Initial technical assessments will provide an understanding of each family’s situation/vulnerability and determine the feasibility of works.

4 - Needs

The scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Entering its tenth year, the impact of present and past hostilities on civilians remains the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria compounded by the covid-19 pandemic and increasing financial hardships.According to HNO 2021, 5.9 million people across Syria are in need of shelter assistance. The shelter situation has continued to deteriorate giventhe unavailability of adequate and affordable shelter, the limited response for longer-term solutions, the new challenges for communal shelters with COVID-19, and the displacement trends in NES and NWS. An estimated 31 percent of the overall population continue to live in inadequate shelter conditions, with returnees and IDPs being the worst affected. The Shelter PiN increased to 5.88 million in 2021 which is a 4 percent increase in PiN from 2020 (5.7 million). Needs assessment conducted by DRC in November 2020, with the coordination with the Education sector, and as included in the new signed MOU with MOE and during education section meetings, found that all schools need repair, with most lacking doors, windows, electrical systems, and WASH facilities. Key informants estimate at least 15,000 returnee families in the areas of intervention( ALEPPO, HOMS, HAMA, DARAA), pressing the need for more spaces in schools.This lack of access to basic services including safe and adequate shelter increases the vulnerability of households and forces the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms such as selling assets or keeping children out of education, especially among female-headed households, households including youth, and those with functional difficulties.

5 - Implementation of activities

DRC will provide safe and dignified shelter solutions by repairing partially damaged houses/apartments for 300 HH and will pilot a shelter package comprising of sanitation items for 200 HH, electrical repairs, windows and doors, accessories for WASH facilities. DRC will prioritize apartments hosting IDP families alongside members of the host community and returnees for repair. DRC will target the old cities, Rural areas. Additionally, activities will include the installation of public lighting/solar system panels. DRC will focus on the installation of a public solar lighting system to promote the safety and access of beneficiaries in areas of intervention. DRC will be responsible for the procurement of these items, as well as installation. The skilled labor will be identified based on criteria set by both the Shelter team. The installation of the panels is expected to take 10 days 6-8 hours per day, and workers will be engaged for a period of 10 days over one month and will be paid 9$ per day. DRC shelter team will overlook the works to ensure that they are done with the required quality. Once works are finalized, they would be handed over to DoLAE/municipality. The maintenance of the solar panels will be done by the technical team in the municipality in the future. The evaluation of the outcome of the final executed work is taken over by the DRC internal MEAL department in conducting a satisfactory survey and other monitoring processes involving different stakeholders’ interventions will be in close coordination with the Sector, SARC, MoLA, and MoWR.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria, or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between the host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance from the previous. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria are uniform/agreed upon among sector members and with the government and are clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. Establish a complaint mechanism at the community level. 2.Selection criteria are influenced by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, the tension in the community, and the risk of the most vulnerable being left out. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community.3. COVID 19 movement restriction, that negatively affected the time required to implement projects and the modality of communication with the beneficiaries, authority, contractors, Sector members, and DRC partners. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation: DRC will respond to all needs arising due to the covid-19 pandemic in close coordination with the Sector, SARC, MoLA, and MoWR, with the verification of needs and delivery of assistance done by DRC.
