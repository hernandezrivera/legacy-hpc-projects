Project Summary

Syria's humanitarian crisis, now in its thirteenth year, has left millions in need and caused widespread damage to infrastructure. The recent earthquake in the north further compounded existing hardships. According to the 2024 HNO, a 16.7 million people are in need, with children making up 45%, PWDs 17%, and women 30%. ERL sector alone has identified 15.4 million individuals in need. Among the key challenges facing the ERL sector:
- 70% of households are unable to meet their basic needs.
- 49% of households in NWS, the loss of job or unemployment was reported as a barrier to meeting basic needs.
- 30.4% of households in NWS, even those with full-time employment, cannot meet basic needs.
- Households facing significant income-expenditure gaps, In NWS, the household incomes at 85 USD and expenditures at 183 USD per month, a 53.5% shortfall.
- 86% of households in NWS rely on borrowing and informal credit for essentials.
According to the REACH Report issued in March 2023, livelihoods consistently emerged as a top priority need among all population groups in northwest Syria. However, only 1% of the surveyed communities (787 communities in total) reported the presence of livelihoods assistance. In the areas of Idleb and northern rural Aleppo, the most common source of meeting basic needs, reported in 92% of the survey communities, was daily labor. These findings align with the NRC report, which covered the majority of Syrian governorates. In response to questions about the solutions for overcoming economic hardships, the majority of respondents (73%) expressed the need for job creation. Additionally, cash assistance (60%), food aid (29%), and vocational training (23%) were also mentioned as potential solutions.
The planned intervention aims to enhance the resilience of crisis-affected populations in northwest Syria for 2024.
This response mainly falls under the specific sector objectives 1 and 2 of the early recovery and livelihoods sector, focusing on improving equitable access of the crisis-affected populations to the basic services and enhance access to livelihoods and job opportunities.
The project targets 47,250 beneficiaries who in need for ERL assistance, that includes people who are directly benefiting from the assistance and those who benefit of accessing rehabilitated markets and enhanced services.
The type of assistance proposed within this intervention is:
- Rehabilitation of 4 markets.
- Provision of vocational and business skills training for 3,000 beneficiaries (male and female),
- Support MSMEs to continue and scale up for 1,500 enterprises,
- Create short-term jobs opportunities linked to infrastructure rehabilitation and to internships opportunities for 500 beneficiaries (male and female).
- Build the capacity of youth and enhance their opportunities to access employment through apprenticeships opportunities for 500 beneficiaries (male and female).
- Provision of solar systems to micro and homebased businesses for 200 businesses.
All activities are to take place in different locations in northwest Syria.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Risk: Children are left at home with an unqualified caregivers during the daily training hours for the mothers.Mitigation:- In each training center there will be a child friendly spaces with a trained and qualified caregiver.Risk: women participating in the vocational training are exposed to GBV. Mitigation: - Ensure safe transportation of women to/ from training centers.- Organize separate session for women, and make sure of contracting with female trainers. - Ensure that multiple feedback and complaint mechanisms are in place and easy to access by all beneficiaries.Risk: CFW beneficiaries working on rehabilitation of roads are exposed to risks of land mines explosive remnants. Mitigation: - Provision of Risk Education sessions to CFW beneficiaries, mainly Mine Risk Education when undertaking activities deemed to be risky.- Coordinate with partners concerned with Mine Action about the safety of the specific area in which the rehabilitation activities take place.Risk: Escalation of the security situation. Mitigation:- Prioritize vocational training in relatively stable areas. -Project implementation through partners, whose staff are embedded in the community and maybe able to retain access for a longer period, particularly for CFW activities. -Ensure there is a process established that allows consulting the communities on howto proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment. To mitigate PSEA related risks: - Ensure that all newly recruited local staff and members of the affiliate workforce are provided with the knowledge and skills to handle PSEA issues.- Make sure that all staff sign the Code of Conduct declaration. - Ensure that PSEA focal points are appointed in each targeted area.- Strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising and make sure that people of concern are aware of their rights and entitlements.- Coordinate with other actors on the ground and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities and partners. The participation of PWDs in the activities through conducting surveys is enhanced, focus groups, or interviews with people with disabilities and organizations representing them. This will help to understand the specific challenges they face in accessing livelihoods and plan accordingly to mitigate the challenges. Assessing physical accessibility of workplaces, transportation systems, and training facilities. Additionally, identifying gaps in communication methods and formats of information.
