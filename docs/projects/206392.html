Project Summary

In 2024, the South Sudanese returning home due to Sudan situation are facing serious challenges in terms of shelters, NFIs while at the same time Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) continue to face multi-faceted crises due to conflicts and protracted displacement from locations of their habitual residence.Since 16th April 2023, a total of 326,11 South Sudanese individuals (73,731 HHs) have been returned at several entry points along the border among Sudan and South Sudan.

The project also aim to support the affected refugees coming from Sudan and will address their Shelter and NFIs needs with an overall allocation of USD 1 million.
Flooding and climate changes impacts is another challenge which aggravating the situation for humanitarian response and the communities. Displaced and the recent refugees’ returnees from Sudan often have inadequate shelter and household goods which expose them to heightened protection risks. Increased mobility, continued insecurity and protracted displacement has over the years eroded household coping mechanisms and livelihood options especially considering the poor nature of local markets without stocks for household goods and shelter materials.
UNHCR’s project will focus on responding to the humanitarian needs of IDPs and refugee returnees with targeted and protection focused core relief items (emergency shelter and non-food items – S/NFIs) prioritizing the most vulnerable households and persons with specific needs especially women and girls.
The project will contribute to the following sectoral objectives:
SO 1 – percentage of the targeted population that is able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition.
The project will identify the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees through protection monitoring and assessments including through RMS,IRNAs and RRMs. The assessments and inter cluster discussions will inform the decisions regarding the specific response modalities between in kind and cash assistance. Unique contextual factors including market conditions will be considered.
SO 2 - Enhance community dignity and engagement in the provision of S/NFI services. The project will deliver timely lifesaving activities while streamlining protection-oriented initiatives. The project will strengthen community-based protection through the involvement of community-based structures for self-management in response delivery, ensuring that their capacities to cope, recover, contribute to and achieve solutions are enhanced.
SO 3 - Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures.
In 2024, UNHCR will increase its active participation in humanitarian coordination forums, including the ES/NFI cluster, both at national level and in the field, strongly advocating for protection mainstreaming and joint vulnerability assessments, the results of which will be very useful in the optimization of the limited resources. UNHCR will endeavor to provide timely response to the most critical identified needs of vulnerable IDPs and Refugees returnees, among other affected populations. Protection risks remain high among women, girls, boys and persons with specific protection needs which may include transitional shelters and other material assistance.

1. Needs

As of 30th September, some 287000 returning refugees who had been living in Sudan since fleeing South Sudan’s own brutal civil war. In addition, continued displacements because of subnational violence hampers sustainable strengthening of households coping mechanisms to various man made and natural shocks.Since 16th April 2023, a total of 326,11 South Sudanese individuals (73,731 HHs) have been returned at several entry points along the border among Sudan and South Sudan.Reference :UNHCR IOM joint dashboard ,endorsed by RRC Microsoft Power BI UNHCR is focusing the prioritized counties across South Sudan where UNHCR and partners have active presence and will do need assessments through nominated MFT teams and wherever possible will opt for interagency shelter and housing assesments. Similarily the state level cluster assessments will also be used for selection of Bomas and payams to reach to the most vulnerable beneficiaries. In 2021 and 2022, South Sudan experienced severe floods comparable to the unprecedent flooding of 1962. The floods resulted in damages to houses and livelihoods which has affected around 529,000 individuals in many locations across the country. Individuals and households in Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei states were the worst affected. South Sudan’s fragile economy, security situation and unemployment taking numerous manifestations which include shortages of commodities in the local markets and weakened capacity to spend. These shocks continue to drive the need for various forms of humanitarian assistance including emergency shelter and NFIs. Localized and sporadic sub-national violence continues in many counties resulting in new displacements and affected households losing their shelters and household property. Continued violence from one year to the next is creating increased dependence on humanitarian assistance to provide emergency shelter and temporary solutions. While there are pockets of hope for durable solutions for IDPs and returnees in locations affected by intermittent violence, there remains huge needs for transitional shelters and rehabilitation of damaged vital infrastructures which are important in building community resilience. In 2024, country wide return movements will revolve around progress in the implementation of the peace agreement and presence of humanitarian and development actors in areas of return. However, sporadic and area specific spontaneous returns and relocation movements will continue. Support for solutions and building community resilience to shocks will remain challenging and urgent in many of the return locations. Up to 88% of IDPs live in host communities and spontaneous settlements, some of which are in hard-to-reach locations with limited infrastructure and services.

2. Project activities

- Improve access of persons with special needs (PSNs) to emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs through in-kind or cash assistance. UNHCR will target vulnerable new IDPs and refugees returnees. - Conduct protection, needs and market assessment, verification and NFIs distributions for vulnerable households in targeted areas. - Engage in joint needs assessments and gaps analysis - Support persons of concern with in-kind standard emergency and transitional shelters and lifesaving NFIs. - UNHCR will contribute and also use the SNFI Cluster Pipeline and Humanitarian hubs for the distribution of NFIs. - Conduct local market analysis on availability of materials - Support persons of concern with cash in hand for emergency shelters depending on market conditions. - Mainstream AAP through IDPs, returnees’ consultation; in interagency participatory assessment (PA). - UNHCR will continue to assess the HLP and conflict sensitive issues with focus on protection response to support the most vulnerable IDP and returnee families to reduce the protection risks and associated vulnerabilities. - Strengthen coordination with ES-NFI cluster and other partners on ground to ensure protection mainstreaming in overall interventions process. - UNHCR will continue evidence-based programming to assist the vulnerable households, including responses from feedback mechanisms. - Reporting on activities including post-monitoring missions. - UNHCR will continue to advocate for Protection mainstreaming awareness raising in the community through their partner organizations. - UNHCR will continue to strengthen the area-based coordination to assess the protection risks and needs of returnees through co-leading solutions working groups at state and national level. - UNHCR and partners will further strengthen and expand its outreach activities in line with the IDP protection strategy and the needs. - UNHCR through an expanded operational coverage will ensure protection monitoring and participation in RMS,RPAs, IRNA and RRM. - Ensure regular inter-cluster dialogue for context appropriate response
