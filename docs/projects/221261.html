Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face a complex humanitarian crisis due to prolonged conflict, climate shocks, and economic instability. This project aims to provide critical food assistance to vulnerable populations in Aweil North County (Northern Bahr el Ghazal state) and Gogrial West County (Warrap state), addressing the urgent needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), refugees, and returnees. According to the latest IPC analysis, 46% of South Sudan's population (5.83 million people) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity. In Aweil North, 45% of the population is classified in IPC Phase 3 or above, with projections indicating this could rise to 55% by June 2024. A SMART survey in Aweil North (April 2023) revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 24.7%, significantly above the 15% emergency threshold. As of July 2023, approximately 2.32 million South Sudanese refugees reside in neighboring countries, while about 2 million people remain internally displaced. Over 808,000 people, mainly South Sudanese returnees, have arrived from Sudan since April 2023 due to ongoing conflict. Up to 3.3 million people could be affected by floods between September and December 2024. The project will provide 20000 households/people with agricultural inputs/vegetable & fishing kits; 400,000 livestock vaccinated/treated; 150 kitchen gardens setup at Nutrition facilities/Schools/Household; and 5,000 people benefiting from capacity building & training, improved livelihood productivity, market development, Anticipatory Actions. Carryout information center operation, price monitoring and market linkages.
The project will coordinate with other UN agencies and the South Sudan government to maximize impact and avoid duplication. Regular monitoring and evaluation will utilize RACBO's established systems for tracking food security indicators, nutrition outcomes, and beneficiary feedback, allowing for adaptive management. By providing this critical food assistance, the project aims to stabilize food security, improve nutrition outcomes, and support the resilience of conflict-affected populations in Aweil North and Gogrial West counties. This intervention is an essential component of the broader humanitarian response in South Sudan, contributing to the country's path towards recovery and sustainable development while directly addressing the FSL Cluster Objectives 2 & 3.

1. Needs

The counties of Aweil North County (NBeG state) and Gogrial West County (Warrap state) face severe humanitarian challenges characterized by critical food insecurity, deteriorating livelihoods, and complex emergency conditions. Current IPC classifications indicate Crisis (Phase 3) to Emergency (Phase 4) levels of food insecurity, with some areas experiencing Catastrophic (Phase 5) conditions during lean periods. Agricultural productivity has declined by 40-60% below the five-year average, while livestock health has deteriorated due to disease outbreaks and limited veterinary services. Market functionality is severely compromised, with food prices 150-200% above seasonal norms due to poor infrastructure and broader economic instability. Assessment data reveals that 68% of households in Aweil North and 72% in Gogrial West cannot meet basic food needs without assistance. The average household food consumption score shows over 55% of households in the "poor" category, while Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates exceed emergency thresholds at 18.7% and 19.2% respectively. Approximately 78% of households report adopting crisis or emergency coping strategies, including reducing meals, selling productive assets, and withdrawing children from school. Over 80% of households have depleted food stocks three months earlier than normal, while 65% report significant loss of productive assets. Women-headed households, comprising 45% of the population, face disproportionate challenges in resource access and livelihood maintenance. Current response capacity reaches only 40% of vulnerable populations, leaving critical gaps in several areas. An estimated 245,000 individuals require immediate food or cash assistance, while 35,000 farming households lack essential agricultural inputs. Livestock support services reach less than 30% of pastoral households, and market support initiatives are virtually non-existent. Only 15% of vulnerable households have access to livelihood diversification programs, and merely 25% of agricultural land has basic irrigation systems.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Agricultural Inputs and Kit distribution activities: The project's activities are structured across four key intervention areas. Agricultural input distribution activities begin with comprehensive beneficiary identification using community-based targeting committees. Key activities include: detailed vulnerability assessments (Months 1-2), establishment of procurement and quality control systems for inputs (Months 1-3), setup of decentralized distribution points (Month 2), and phased distribution events reaching 5,000 households per quarter. Each distribution will include certified seeds (2kg vegetables, 10kg staples), tools, and fishing equipment where appropriate. Supporting activities include establishing community seed banks (Months 4-6), deploying mobile agricultural extension teams, and conducting post-distribution monitoring at 30, 60, and 90 days. Seed multiplication training for model farmers will ensure sustainability (Months 6-12). Activity 2: Livestock Health Campaign activities: The livestock health campaign activities align with seasonal calendars and migration patterns. Initial activities include livestock population assessment and disease surveillance (Month 1), selection and training of 100 CAHWs (Months 1-2), and establishment of cold chain infrastructure (Months 1-3). Quarterly vaccination campaigns target 100,000 animals each quarter, prioritizing cattle (250,000 head), small ruminants (100,000), and poultry (50,000). Additional activities include mobile veterinary clinics, community-based disease surveillance systems (Month 3 onwards), monthly CAHW coordination meetings, and implementation of cost-recovery systems through community-managed veterinary pharmacies (Months 4-12). Activity 3: Kitchen garden activities will focus on establishing 150 sustainable demonstration sites. Implementation includes site assessments and garden planning (Month 1), procurement systems setup (Months 1-2), and phased garden establishment (25 per month). Each site will receive water-efficient irrigation systems, composting facilities, and comprehensive training for caretakers. Regular activities include crop rotation planning, organic pest management, and monthly learning visits between sites. Monitoring systems track production levels and produce utilization continuously. Activity 4: Capacity building and market development activities will emphasize practical skills transfer and system strengthening. Core activities include training needs assessment (Month 1), farmer field schools for 2,000 participants (12 monthly sessions), business skills workshops for 1,500 traders (quarterly), and financial literacy training for 1,000 beneficiaries (monthly sessions). Market development includes establishing three information centers (Months 2-4), organizing quarterly market linkage events, and implementing early warning system training.
