Project Summary

The tripled effect of covid-19, flooding and food insecurity, as well as cyclical climatic shocks, amid compounding political and socioeconomic factors, continue to drive and impact the humanitarian crisis in Somalia. Such factors have resulted in protracted economic vulnerability and the loss of livelihoods, particularly among agro-pastoralists, rural pastoralists and riverine farmers. Violence and limited access to humanitarian assistance and basic social services in rural and hard-to-reach areas has spurred mass population movement toward urban and peri-urban areas, further straining the limited resources and absorption capacity of host communities, this has been worsened by global COVID-19 pandemic and recurrent flash floods. The combined impact increasingly exposes households to risks of violence, exploitation and abuse. As a result, in 2020, 3 million Somalis in IDPs, host communities, returnees and refugees are in need of humanitarian assistance across the country and as well as in need of physical and mental health as well as in humanitarian crisis.
continues in several parts of the country. Confirmed cholera cases from the beginning of the year to epidemiological week 31 (2nd August 2020) recorded 5,341 cases, 29 deaths with a case fatality rate (CFR) of 0.5%, this coupled with COVID-19 pandemic which continues to spread in Somalia with a total of 3,212 COVID-19 cases confirmed; including 93 associated deaths as of second August 2020. The large majority (83 %) of cases are aged between 20 and 60 years, with a Case Fatality Rate (CFR) 3% and 1,827 recoveries. Since the onset of the outbreak in Somalia, 3212 laboratory-confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported including 93 associated deaths. The large majority (83 %) of cases are aged between 20 and 60 years, with a median age of 32 years (ranging from 1 to 110 years). As in previous weeks, 73% of the laboratory-confirmed cases were amongst male. The number of samples tested during this reporting week is higher than in previous weeks, but the positivity rate is significantly lower than in previous weeks (from 6.7% the previous week to 3% during this reporting week)
Therefore, this action will focus on provision of both preventive and curative health services at primary level through 6 fixed MCH and 4 mobile /outreach team to provide holistic and integrated support. DEH an actor in the target regions will leverage on existing support & activities to ensure the gains made in the post-drought response are not rolled back. Building the capacity of health workers through training on Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC) will be done to equip health workers with the required skills to deliver quality health care services to the affected population. The project with the technical guidance of WHO and the Health Cluster will also integrate surveillance and emergency preparedness in the program so that enough steps are taken in ensuring prompt response. The project will also contribute to strengthening early warning disease surveillance through weekly submission of eDEW reports from all the functional supported health facilities.

Project Activities

1.Provision of routine treatment for communicable and non-communicable diseases, child health, sexual and reproductive health (including ante/post-natal care), under five consultations and immunization, injury care, mental health, and basic laboratory services for rapid health check-ups to 3,000 Women, 5,000 boys, 4,000 girls, 1000 Men through 6 fixed MCH and four outreach sites in Ceelbur, Hobyo and Haradheere district. 2.Conduct Disease Surveillance and Response activities through 6 fixed MCH and four outreach health facilities. 3.Conduct training to 20 Female and 30 Male CHWs on prevention and integrated community case management (CCM) for communicable diseases,COVID-19 prevention and triage for selecting our target operation areas/sites.4. provide integrated mental health and psychosocial services including clinical rape case management to the vulnerable population through 6 fixed MCH and four outreach sites in Hobyo,Ceelbur,Ceel dheer and Dhusamareeb target health centers

Mitigation Measures

The project areas have high number of mixed pastoral and IDPs as well as scattered rural areas in Ceelbur and xaradhere as well as Dhusamareeb/Guriecel, and Hobyo district with different livelihood status, due the diverse in the nature of the target beneficiaries the rural communities displaced might not welcome positively to the IDPs and conflict between the previous establish IDPs managers and newly displaced might conflict hence creating community tension, DEH will ensure to provide better coordination and information sharing when such eventuality hits the target community, also on the employment issues there might be discrimination or disparities between the women and men and to address this DEH will ensure to competitively sources the local community with expertise for employment considering women in equal measures this will then reduce the risk of gender disparities and minimized community expectation. Location, distance and access routes to the outreach clinics sites and services might be a challenge of preventing factors limiting the access of the access to sites DEH will enable its beneficiaries have accessible to all of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women DEH will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities as well as the IDPs and host communities this will further enhanced inclusion of disable and elderly people with easy to access program intervention, limited information and communication might hinder the understanding between DEH teams in the field and community leaders, however, to mitigate any such eventuality DEH will create feedback mechanism and reasonable compliance officer within its office as well as hotline mobile services to ensure all beneficiary complains are addressed timely. Project staff will be trained on the basic human right issues and approaches to enable them understand the principle of equity and ensure those fleeing communities were well attended, the project will consult the communities where treatment centers will be place in avoidance of near military settlement as well as to avoid the risk associate with landmines and explosive targets. DEH will ensure all the structure within the mobile clinic sites are user friendly to disable and marginalized and minority children boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women.

Please provide further explanation

Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic emergency health care services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of health centers attacks, to increase community uptake of provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. DEH will strengthen the community capacity through training of active community health workers who will in turn provide the required services for referral and treatment as well as information sharing within the community hence creating ownership and sustainability of the program even after the end of the donor funds.
