Project Summary

In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021, this is according to the situational update by UNOCHA Feb 2022. An estimated 835,000 people have been affected by severe flooding between May and December 2021, and 80 per cent of those affected were from Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states. Many flood-displaced people, including those who were displaced by the 2020 flood were unable to return to their homes until early 2022. Covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, for example, gender based violence (GBV) and poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a threat; women and girls are exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities, which is further exacerbated due to flooding that limits livelihood opportunities (e.g. collection of firewood and charcoal production). Feeling unsafe, some women and girls avoid areas such as water points, latrines, distribution areas, markets and firewood gathering sites. 2022 HRP will provide any opportunity for Health Link South Sudan to continue with provision of GBV Prevention and Response services to the affected selected counties of Bor South, Twic East, Melut, Kapoeta East, Magwi and Lainya which are within the severity priority locations mapped 3, 4 and 5. With the proposed intervention HLSS will use the already existing strategies in the mentioned affected counties with the interventions of risk mitigation, establishment of Women and Girls friendly spaces, community outreach strategies, community engagement. Through the proposed intervention Health South Sudan will focus its intervention beneficiaries to Women, girls, Men, Boys and persons with disability and vulnerable populations with the different intervention. These intervention shall include Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support, Establishment and / or strengthening GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support, Implementation Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment, Provision of dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls as part of GBV risk mitigation, Conducting Fuel Efficient Stoves ( FES) programming to reduce risk of gender-based violence and generate income, Provision of livelihood and economic support including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls, Conducting community awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principles, Implementation of primary GBV prevention interventions to addresses harmful practices, systemic gender inequality and power imbalances, Provision of capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline service providers and Enhancing GBV preparedness, contingency planning and resilience building to prevent and respond to GBV

1 - Needs

Ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021, this is according to the situational update by UNOCHA Feb 2022. An estimated 835,000 people have been affected by severe flooding between May and December 2021, and 80 per cent of those affected were from Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states. Feeling unsafe, some women and girls avoid areas such as water points, latrines, distribution areas, markets and firewood gathering sites. Twic East is experiencing high influx of returnees without repatriation arrangement in place so the reception of this group is done by their relatives resulting to inadequate food, shelter, and clean drinking water, accommodation space for settlement and protection/GBV services. The IDP settlement in the location is informal and spontaneous in nature and most people lack basic services such as kitchen setting, Mosquito net, plastic sheet as some items were lost in abrupt evacuation due to deteriorating flood devastating situation as rain intensify during rainy season. Most people prefer coming back home to their place of origin but the flood phenomenal is eventually worsening adding more damages and increasing level of vulnerability to the residential population in the islands and site settlement. The residential sites splitted into islands and Panyagor as the main land settlement base at a county headquarter. There are about 52 islands mapped during IRNA and verified by RRC and local authority as residential sites for people displaced from their homes. While over 2,748 households translating to 14,872 people got displaced by the floods in threepayams (Thiangrial, Melut and Paloich) in Melut County which has left over 180 villages with a totalpopulation of 12,551 HHs translating to 46,176 individuals are affected by the floods in 7 payams of Melut who are temporarily sheltering in schools as well the local authorities confirmed that the IDP camps – Dengtome 1 and Dengtome 2 are equally affected and displaced the IDPs along the roads and other higher grounds. The emerging support that were identified during the assessment included Shelter and NFIs, Food, Health, Nutrition, WASH and Protection services. As in Bor South County, Subsequently, the water levels increased led to flooding which IPDs that had spontaneous settlement and exposed the affected population to different risks of protection/GBV being one of the key risks due to open sheltering, open use of toilet in night without flash lights.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance is one of the inter-agency minimum standards in GBV programming in emergency (Standard 11: Dignity Kits, Cash and Voucher Assistance). In addition to this, HLSS opts to implement cash assistance based on lesson learnt and feedback from women and girls on importance of Cash assistance as lifesaving; for example, it can help a survivor meet the costs associated with fleeing an abusive relationship (e.g., rent, temporary shelter, transportation, food, clothing, etc.). In addition, according to the IRNA report conducted in different locations affected by flooding, women and girls including PWD preferred cash as it gives them more independence to meet their most urgent needs that will restore their dignity. Upon comprehensive assessment by the case worker, vulnerable women and girls identified to need emergency support appropriate to the incident and vulnerabilities will be provided with emergency cash assistance. The cash will be delivered directly to survivors and at-risk groups for the purpose of supporting them to meet essential needs related to their case action plan. The beneficiaries will be identified in the case management caseload as well as referred by other service providers, women leaders and through the women and girl safe spaces by the women support officer using the set vulnerability criteria. The case worker will assess the case vulnerability based on the set criteria. The assistance will be a one-off emergency cash assistance to survivors in support of GBV case management. A committee consisting of HLSS response team will sit in monthly basis and approve the beneficiary to receive assistance according to the vulnerability criteria. In all the encounters, HLSS will ensure that the identity of the beneficiary is protected. In case of a sensitive case, that will need immediate response, an adhoc meeting will be organized to ensure response within the shortest time possible. The assistance will be delivered considering necessary GBV risk mitigation measures. In addition, HLSS will ensure that relevant staff are aware of the referral mechanism, GBV and protection guiding principles and data management and sharing protocols. Further, will ensure implementing staff do not expose cash assistance recipients to further harm.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

With the Cash assistance HLSS will focus the support shall be more a lifesaving aspect of survivors; for example, it can help a survivor meet the costs associated with fleeing an abusive relationship (e.g., rent, temporary shelter, transportation, food, clothing, etc.) Looking at this in line with market functionality HLSS with the proposed intervention will use the already different findings of assessment conducted by the Cash and voucher working group in south Sudan which gives different options of how feasible this support is in relation to market functionality. HLSS will ensure that the selected counties have functional markets which shall support the Cash assistance and also work closely with other partners who are already implementing cash assistance in the proposed locations with the focus that this does not posse further risks to survivors of GBV

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The main risks generally might run the same in the proposed intervention location and these being: Armed conflicts; crimes; cessation of activities due to community tensions (e.g. Cattle raiding and revenge back of the raiding); flooding resulting in population displacements and movement. To mitigate the risks in the intervention areas, HLSS shall work with local authorities on ground and liaise with relevant line ministries at the state and National level. This will support in implementation of the activities within the proposed period within the communities and ensure mitigation some of the aforementioned risks. Furthermore, as part of the project, HLSS will work with community leaders and members on peaceful coexistence and prevention of violence in general. Dealing with data management and disclosure of GBV sensitive incidences, HLSS shall GBV case workers who shall be trained on GBV case management which training content entails management of disclosures of sensitive informations and further the risks of data management shall be mitigated through the use of incident coding for both survivors and the case workers who shall be handling the cases and filed in lockable carbines and the soft copies entered in the information management systems saved with protected password access format. All staffs that shall be hired under this project shall sign code of conduct and as well shall be trained on Prevention Sexual Exploitation and Abuse with emphasis of zero tolerance, reporting mechanism for sexual exploitation and abuse against beneficiary at the service points shared with beneficiaries and as well awareness raising with message on reporting of humanitarian worker who asks service exchange as all services under the proposed project is totally free of charge. further HLSS shall ensure that persons with disability that do have access to information on services available due to their difficulties in access the points where these information are shared shall be prioritized through door to door approach with information on protection/GBV related services and as well key GBV related topics, importance of timely reporting of sexual assault cases, and as well other services that can be received through referrals from other partners. Through establishing and putting the above measures much of the risks shall be mitigated.
