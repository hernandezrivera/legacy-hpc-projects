Project Summary

The project consists of two main phases and target 25 schools in Idleb and Aleppo as the following:
1-Academic year phase: this phase includes light rehab of schools and the provision of formal Edu to 25 schools:
1-1-Light rehabilitation for the 25 schools, the scope of rehab will include the classrooms (windows and doors) as WASH facilities and adapt them to receive children with disabilities, and provide school with furniture like (students’ desks, Cupboard, teachers’ tables and whiteboards),as integrating the solar power system to provide the needed lighting for classrooms including CFSs, WASH facilities and other electronic devices charge such as (laptop, printer, speakers, and projectors),GAN will operate CFSs in schools (as some were supported before by GAN but not functioning because of lack of fund and facilitators) and fill the needed materials shortage to provide structured PSS activities, these rehab activities will improve and increase the safe access to schools and ensure children are receiving education in a safe and protected environment, as parents may not send their children to schools due to the inappropriate schools’ condition. Rehabilitating the targeted schools will make sure that the absorption schools’ capacity is increased and more students could benefit its education services, as the result of NA, the northern schools overcrowded and in many cases 45 children per classroom at the same time which affect the learning outcomes, GAN will ensure that the targeted schools are ready to receive more children as a result of recent displacement waves from the southern and eastern locations.
1-2- Academic year phase, provision of formal education for the 25 schools, schools, first, GAN will ensure provision of running costs (stationery, cleaning materials, water filling ..etc.) which considered as the basic component of support because the lack of fund, ASER test will be conducted to help better plan and focus on the needed type of education based on results, also to capture the intervention impact, children will be provided with learning kits according to the gender and age (education level), schools will be equipped with FAKs and fire-extinguishers and children will be trained on the safety, security plans, and risk education of explosive hazards (as coordinated with mine actions sub-cluster) the PSS activities will be provided for all students as one session a week following the CP-sub cluster guidelines and referral pathways (according to service map updated by CP sub-cluster) and identified map by GAN protection team, teachers and education personnel will be provided with incentives for 5 months (Jan to May 2020),the curriculum used is SIG and high level of coordination with EDs and other working organization will be ensured to avoid duplications.
2-Provision of non-formal education for 25 schools for children who are at risk of dropping out or have poor education score by remedial classes, the curriculum is SIG one, GAN will conduct BTL campaigns in the communities to raise awareness about the importance of education for both boys, girls, and flag the risks of not enrolling children in schools, GAN will provide also PSS activities for all children in schools as basic learning supplies (stationery, notebooks,..etc), the recreational activities are the key to motivate them to attend school especially in summertime, teachers and education personnel will be supported by incentives for 9 months,
monitoring summary:
GAN will use individual interviews and FGDs to evaluate activities in addition to collecting observations from the field. GAN will also analyze student data and the results of the quarterly tests, and all beneficiaries will be able to submit complaints and suggestions through the complaints boxes and hotline (WhatsApp) and through the M&E field team while touring the project sites.

4 - Needs

Almost a decade into the crisis. Humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2020.4M people live in northwest Syria, of which 2.14M school-aged children (51% Girls).Since 1 December 2019, almost 600,000 people fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb, moving further north away from the hostilities, 80% of whom are women and children. Many fled their homes within the space of hours without any belongings while others managed to take with them what they needed to survive, most of the recently displaced people moved to urban centers and IDP camps in northwestern Idleb. Tens of thousands have reportedly moved to areas such as Afrin and A’zaz in northern Aleppo governorate seeking safety and access to services.Over half a million school-aged children (6 to 18 years) in NWS are out of school, 34% of the total 1,712,468 children in the same age group.As of 28 January, due to the unfolding situation in Ma’arrat An Nu’man, Saraqab, Ehsem and Mahambl education have been suspended, impacting the learning of 59,231 school-aged children and showing a 36% increase of displaced school-aged children in impacted areas. A total of 199 schools are out of operation, impacting 59,231 children and 3,703 teachers: 135 schools in Ma’arrat An Nu’man and neighboring communities, impacting 48,649 children and 2,704 teachers; 23 schools in the communities of Ariha, impacting 5,681 children and 384 teachers; and 41 schools in the communities of Saraqab, impacting 4,901 children and 615 teachers.The education system in displaced areas are already overstretched and suffers from overcrowded classrooms, with 60 to 80 students per class. This continues to limit absorption capacity of newcomers.Protection risks have increased with the ongoing displacement, lack of shelter options and sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement, specifically related to child protection (CP), and exposure to explosive hazards. Overcrowding in IDP sites and makeshift shelters.

5 - Implementation of activities

-Provide children and youth with formal education: GAN is committed to providing the official curriculum issued by SiG, in addition to measure the learning outcomes using ASER tool for Arabic, English and Math subjects. - Provide children and youth with non-formal education programs (including catch-up classes, SLP, remedial education, literacy and numeracy classes): GAN will provide the non-formal education for those who have poor education score or dropped-out in the summer (June, July, August and September 2020) for 2000 students including about 1020 male, 980 female. - Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns (media outreach, awareness-raising activities, community mobilization): GAN will conduct 14 campaigns, 10 campaigns before the academic year and 4 campaigns before the summer phase, brochures will be distributed, and posters will be posted in the targeted communities, in addition to outreach posts via media. - Establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms (temporary or permanent): GAN will contract with a rehab service provider from inside Syria to conduct the rehab needed for schools according to the deep need assessment that will be conducted, the rehab scope will cover classrooms, windows, doors, walls, and school yeard, - Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive WASH facilities: GAN will conduct a deep need assessment in the targeted schools according to the cluster guidelines document which is developed by the task forces recently, then after the contracting with the rehab service provider and conduct the needed rehab considering to finalize the rehab process before the academic year or proceed the rehab after-school to protect students, in addition to take into consideration the disaggregation between boys, girls and adapting the facilities with disabilities, - Provide students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks), school bags in formal and non-formal settings: GAN will proceed the procurement form Turkey

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: - access to the target schools' locations. - the availability of the needed materials and equipment. - the good security situation, - access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries. Risk: 1- the risk of shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies in the targeted locations or and infighting between different armed groups continues causing loss of access to the targeted locations inaccessible: Mitigation: GAN will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again. 2- the heavy shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies for a long time (months) or the target locations becomes under its control Mitigation: Shifting program locations to more accessible areas in coordination with Education cluster, HFU, and other partners and relevel stakeholders. 3- Communities and key stakeholders reject our project/plan; Mitigation: Include the local and IDP community in the design and the implementation of the activities of this project, conduct community mobilization ahead of the project promoting to its proposed activities and ensure the community acceptance. In addition to the close coordination of relevant stakeholders including MoE, EDs and EAs and MoU to be assigned by education authorities. 4- A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the organization and negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria. Mitigation: As a registered NGO in Turkey, GAN is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, GAN will adapt their modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure continued assistance, although GAN has its operational offices inside Syria that can continue following up all the needed activities with the remote management from GAN headquarters in the neighboring countries.
