Project Summary

This proposed intervention in Bardheere district (Gedo Region, Somalia) targets 8,130 vulnerable men, women, boys and girls in 1,355 Non-IDPs (agro-pastoralists & riverine farmers in the rural areas) and IDP households for 12 months (January 2022- December 2022). The project will prioritize the most vulnerable HHs/population including: women (women headed households, single women, pregnant and lactating women-PLW, children, persons with disabilities, persons with minority clan affiliations, older persons, persons with chronic illness, large families and/ or those with babies or with children attending school. The project will select the target vulnerable beneficiaries from 38,960 individuals (in approx. 6,493 HHs) currently under IPC 3&4 in Bardhere district (as per the FSC 2021 Overall targets of People in Need-IPC 3&4 in Bardhere). Project activities are aligned with the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP)/Food Security Cluster (FSC) Objectives prioritizing lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance, as detailed in the UN OCHA (HRP-Somalia. Humanitarian Programme Cycle 2021-Issued in February 2021). The FSC Objective 1 of improving immediate access to food will be achieved through: (i) 600 men, women, boys and girls from 100 vulnerable HHs benefiting from Unconditional cash transfer (UCT) for 3 months ($ 70 per month per HH) and (ii) 4,350 men, women, boys and girls from 725 vulnerable HHs benefiting from conditional transfers-Cash For Work-CFW (rehabilitation of community infrastructure- Irrigation canals)about 70 Kms for 30 days @ $8 per HH per day. These will improve immediate access to food, prevent selling of assets, bridge the food gap for beneficiary farmers as well as increase skills and ultimately improve food production, soil and water conservation and HH income. The FSC objective 2 of protecting and restoring livelihoods related to food and income sources will be achieved through: (i) 520 vulnerable agro-pastoralists and riverine farmers HHs (3,120 men, women, boys, girls) supported with seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs (e.g quality fodder seeds, farm tools, training on fodder production, conservation and utilization, seed bulking, group dynamics, business skills, DRR; land preparation and irrigation support). This will ultimately protect and restore livelihoods as well as enhance HH feed security, food security, nutrition, income generation and resilience. The main approaches that will be used will include: Needs/market assessments/baseline survey; community based targeting; adult learning techniques; use of lead farmers as ToTs; conflict sensitive programing; protection mainstreaming; Gender sensitive and human rights based approaches. The main common project activities will include: (i) Conducting internal planning meeting for project staff to acquaint on project objectives, targets and outcome.(ii) Introduction of the project to local authorities and launch .(iii)Conducting community mobilization, sensitization, selection of beneficiaries- in the targeted 5 sites (Mardha, Hureena, Sarinleey, Bender and Shimbiroole); verification and registration (iv)Conducting a baseline survey to determine the benchmark and (v) M&E of project activities. VSF-Suisse has vast experience in UCT and CFW activities and for over 6 years (2015-2020-through USAID/OFDA(BHA funding): supported 1,700 male & female fodder farmers and 1,270 Irrigation farmers in Gedo Region (including Bardhere district) practicing fodder activities, EWS and DRR; distribution of agricultural inputs and promoted linkages. VSF-Suisse supported rehabilitation of 74 Kms of Irrigation canals through CFW approaches and water supply infrastructure (13 water pans & 12 shallow wells) in Gedo. VSF-Suisse will work closely with stakeholders including communities, local authority/leaders, relevant technical ministries, NGOs and adhere to the FSC cluster & CWG guidelines, LEGS, Sphere standards, while observing CDC, WHO and government COVID-19 health measures.

The project's overall objective is to improve access of men, women, boys, girls from vulnerable agro-pastoral, riverine farmers in the rural areas and IDP HHs in Bardhere district, Gedo Region, Somalia to food and other basic needs through cash transfer modalities (UCT and CFW- rehabilitation of Irrigation canals) and fodder production, conservation and utilization. Project outcome: Vulnerable men, women, boys, girls have increased access to basic needs (food and other needs) and improved livelihoods. 2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The decision to use cash transfers was based upon careful consideration of the type and urgency of needs identified, results of market assessments, seasonality and needs of existing livelihoods, labor capacity, predicted effectiveness and gender issues. The advantages of using Cash instead of in-kind are: cash is a fast intervention process; a flexible resource that allows people to spend money according to their needs and priorities; it does not rely on heavy logistic system/infrastructure; cash stimulate recovery by creating a short-term income flow and increases purchasing power; it easily encourages participation of women, youth, marginalized groups; cash eliminates the possibility of beneficiaries re-selling the distributed items (to buy preferred goods & services) and it reduces accumulation of debts. The transfer value of USD 70 will be paid per UCT beneficiary HH per month for a period of 3 months and USD 8 per CFW beneficiary HH per day for a working period staggered in 3 months. Money will be paid directly to the beneficiaries using mobile money transfers, which is in line with VSF-Suisse audit and financial requirements. The primary purpose of using UCT and CFW in this project will be to provide short-term income boost to vulnerable households-men, women, boys and girls in IPC phase 3 & 4. Vulnerable HHs-men, women, boys and girls will receive UCT and CFW to help them improve immediate access to food and other basic needs from the local markets, stimulate the local economy and prevent vulnerable HHs from selling their assets. CFW will help rehabilitate irrigation canals that will contribute to improved agricultural production (including fodder production) and food security. The income earned by the cash for work laborers will improve their household incomes and contribute to food security and nutrition. The current needs and challenges facing the vulnerable houesholds-men, women, boys and girls (especially the IDPs, riverine farmers, agro-pastoralists, people in the rural/urban areas as elaborated below), justifies the need for the provision of cash to increase access to food. Conflicts, cyclic droughts, poor and erratic rainfall distribution, desert locusts risks, seasonal flooding continue to impact negatively on vulnerable groups-men, women, boys and girls- food security situation in Gedo Region, leading to food consumption gaps, depletion of livelihood assets, displacement, damage of crops, decline in food and income sources, livestock migration, scarcity of milk, loss of income from agricultural employment, rising costs of food and NFIs. Per FAO and SWALIM-Somalia Rainfall Forecast (Issued 28/10/2021), Southern Somalia is currently facing serious drought condition. According to WFP-Joint Market and Supply Chain Update (24/10/2021 -31/10/2021), Gedo region remain dry and water prices are high.; fuel prices are increasing (following the global oil trends), leading to increased transport costs and food prices. VSF-Suisse (June 2020) Baseline survey report in Gedo, describes the problems facing riverine farmers as: floods, drought, the poor state of irrigation canals, lack of certified seeds, crop pests and diseases, poor soil fertility, lack of training/extension services on irrigation farming, inadequate water pumps, tractors, lack of spare parts for irrigation pump sets, expensive fuel and gene-sets spare parts and poor storage facilities. The project will establish protection, gender mainstreaming (including Gender Based Violence) to ensure safety, dignity and integrity of the target beneficiaries while instituting transparency and accountability.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project design considered the vital role the formal and informal markets in enhancing people’s livelihoods, promoting food security and enabling them to meet their essential needs. The project design took note of the affected population needs, market assessments and the planned baseline survey, taking into consideration the core humanitarian standards, to determine access to markets, financial capital, livelihoods and economic vulnerability and their link to commodity prices, income-earning opportunities and wage rates which all affect food security. The needs assessment is critical and helped determine the gaps as well as understanding the community coping mechanisms. The local Bardheere market is functional, accessible (to all the targeted sites of Marda, Hureena, Sarinleey, Bender and Shimbirooole) and there is availability of products (including staple foods). The market assessment took note on goods and services likely to be on high demand with the current drought and the ability of Bardheere market to respond to demand and the field monitors will continue monitoring the prices of foodstuff during cash transfer/distribution period. The aspect of market functionality has been guided by the status of Bardhere market and the WFP (October 2021) Joint Market and Supply Chain Update (24th October 2021 – 31st October 2021) that indicates an increase in the prices of water and fuel (that is negatively impacting on food prices and transport costs) as well as the socioeconomic impacts brought by COVID-19 pandemic. Consequently, the interventions will accommodate and address any market constraints, such as issues of access, price increase, seasonal changes, disruption of the supply chains and taxes.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The following protection risks have been assessed to be low in Bardhere operational area: Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian assistance; Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse; Exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements; Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors and the Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards. Despite the risks being low, VSF-Suisse will continue mitigating the risks during implementation. VSF-Suisse ensures participation of the community at all stages of the project cycle. VSF-Suisse will strengthen participation and empowerment by actively engaging/consulting with the vulnerable beneficiaries-women, girls, men, boys, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, marginalized persons etc. The local administration, community leaders (including religious leaders/women/youth representatives, elders, people with disabilities etc) and target community members will also be engaged in: Sensitization sessions, assessments, formation of all inclusive cash transfer committees (CTCs) & members, beneficiary selection process (including identification and verification process), compliant and feedback mechanisms and PDM. VSF-Suisse will facilitate through Community Based Targeting to enhance inclusion. We shall engage community beneficiaries and committee representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and decision making process to promote a sense of ownership, build self-esteem and enhance sustainability. VSF-Suisse will conduct dialogue sessions to ensure that unique needs and voices of the most vulnerable and minorities in the community are heard and considered. VSF-Suisse will ensure that the project does no harm and enhance safety, dignity and integrity of the women, men, girls and boys and that the project is implemented in ways that respect their rights and does not reinforce oppressive gender stereotypes as well as enhancing protection and gender mainstreaming-including addressing cases of Gender Based Violence (GBV), transparency and accountability to affected populations (AAP). The organization will ensure AAP through: being transparent by providing access and timely information on selection criteria, organization procedures and donor requirements; setting up accessible and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints as well as organizing awareness raising sessions. The field team will ensure that village committees are representative of all groups including gender, age, disability and socio-economic groups. The organization will ensure that both men and women have equal and fair access to cash transfer (e.g UCT and CFW opportunities), distribution of agricultural inputs and trainings. VSF-Suisse will work closely with the local administration and community, so that should they notice anything irregular, precautionary measures are taken. VSF-Suisse will work closely in coordination with other protection actors to prevent and respond to physical or any cases of sexual violence. VSF-Suisse will work harmoniously with Non-IDPs (host community) and IDPs to minimize community tension. VSF-Suisse will identify power dynamics to help inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access. VSF-Suisse will promote equitable and impartial access to all by ensuring that assistance reaches the most vulnerable and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.
