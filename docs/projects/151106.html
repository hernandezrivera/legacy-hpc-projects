Project Summary

The aim of the project is to provide quality and inclusive basic education to conflict/crisis affected children in Somalia. Focus will be made on improving the enrollment and retention of affected children through the provision of quality and inclusive basic education and this includes having access to a safe and protective learning environment. Conflict / crisis affected children are often subject to drop and remain out school as they face difficulty to rejoining school because livelihood becoming a pressing priority. The project will adopt an integrated approach to promoting safe, inclusive and quality learning opportunities for affected children. This includes the construction of temporary learning spaces, as well as semi-permanent and permanent classrooms rehabilitation/construction. The project will also support children with the provision of lifesaving school meals, and the construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in school. This includes, the rehabilitation of water storage facilities, construction of water tanks, drinking water supply, gender segregated latrines and hygiene and water service promotions. Moreover, for improving the quality of education, the project will provide pedagogical Skills training, DRR and psychosocial support to teachers, including provision of incentive for teachers. The project will support a total of 39000 (50% are girls) crisis-affected children with scholastic materials and uniforms CECs, head teachers in school’s management, community members and key staff within the ministries of Education in Somalia will have their capacity built in in planning and responding to education in emergencies, as well as resilience building of communities.
Beneficiaries will be selected through participatory and predetermined selection criteria prioritizing the most affected and needy people. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. Fifty percent of the total beneficiaries will be female. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.
NRC will ensure adequate participation of the project by the target communities. Besides, Monitoring, evaluation and reporting plans and tools will be developed. Information will be collected on regular basis using Koobo toolbox and analyzed so that achievement is measured and changes made when necessary.

Needs

In Somaliland, a Joint Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment conducted by various organization, including NRC, found that fewer school-aged children (5-17 yrs) attend schools in Hargaisa (16%), Burco (16%), Eirgabo (40%) and El-afweyn (14%) districts. In Somaliland, an assessment conducted by SL-MOE (June 2018) in Sagar cyclone affected areas indicated that 40 schools damaged by the cyclone in the different regions with a student’s population of 7256 (3265 girls) and 290 (26 female) teachersIn South & Central Somalia, a multi-cluster assessment conducted by NRC (2018) in Mogadishu indicates that there is a limited learning facilities in and around the IDP settlements and only 13% communities send their children to schools outside the settlements. In Galmudug state, an inter-agency assessment reports that more than 9,000 school-aged children lack education support. In South west state, assessment conducted by the ministry of Education April 2018 in IDPs in Baidoa district showed that total numbers of children who are not attending school from the IDPs are 84,753.In Puntland, an assessment conducted by Ministry of Education and Higher Education in January 2018 in over 100 drought-affected school across Puntland State found that 14,538 (6441 F: 8097 boys) students dropped out of schools and led to the closure of six rural schools. In response, this intervention will assist children and youth whose education is disrupted by emergencies to continue their education in safe and protective learning environment by providing basic education integrated with live-saving assistance such as school meals, drinking water and hygiene promotion services. The intervention will also provide learning opportunities for out-of-school children. Child safeguarding and well-being will be given priority. The intervention will target vulnerable and emergency-affected primary school students between 5-17 years (45% girls) specially the most vulnerable ones.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will contribute to durable solutions efforts particularly in locations such as Kismayo, Garowe, Galkayo, Bossaso, Baidoa, Dhusamareeb and Burao where the government has shown keen interest in locally integrating internally displaced people. The support by the government offers a protection environment that if well utilized will see many IDPs reintegrated. Through various donors including but not limited to DFID, UNHCR and NMFA, NRC has been at the forefront of this agenda and has supported thousands of reintegrated learners acquire appropriate education and through integrated approaches, acquire land obtain security of tenure. The project contributes significantly to the Resilience Recovery Framework and the National Development plan which vision is to “fulfill the right of every Somali to education and build an adequate, well educated, better skilled and competent workforce that contributes to the economic and human development of the nation”. The project also contributes to 8 of 11 NDP education targets. This project aims to increase resilience of children through expanded access to quality of education by: i) supporting in development of quality learning spaces, ii) Provision of scholastic materials, iii) Capacity building of stakeholders within the ecosystem who will provide continuity and sustainability beyond the life of the project. NRC's Education projects will be integrated with NRC's other sectors (WASH, LFS, CCCM, Shelter and Protection) and will adopt area based integrated programming approach to have maximum impact and have more sustainability, resilience and durability. NRC will also advocate through the Education cluster, other available coordination mechanisms and local authorities to mobilize different organizations to work in the same areas to achieve the goal of integration, resilience and durable solutions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

NRC recognizes the risks of exclusion based on displacement. Displacement affected learners may be excluded from learning due to access constraints and the location of the school. Learners with disabilities face the risk of exclusion if structures that have been constructed or rehabilitated are not disability friendly. To mitigate these risks, NRC will conduct frequent community engagements where possible, hold separate meetings among households clusters to understand the different education needs and priorities for each member of the family. NRC will also set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all. Further NRC will ensure that education facilities are disability friendly.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Due to vulnerability, women, children and elderly are more vulnerable to exclusion, based on gender biases. School going female learners may be subjected to exclusion as preferences may be given to male learners. Learners also face risks of gender based violence in the instances where they have to walk long distances to access education facilities. To mitigate these risks, NRC will ensure the participation and proportionate representation from men, women, older persons, persons with disabilities, minority communities in all consultation and planning meeting and that their opinions are represented. In the meeting, all participants will be given equal chance to speak, to be listened and taken their input seriously. When necessary, different community members will be consulted at different times to hear their opinion so that delivery approaches are relevant to respond their needs. Besides, all consultation will be in Somalia language so that everyone understands. Further, NRC will ensure the location and access routes to education facilities are safe. In addition, regular monitoring and discussions will be made with the community to hear their concerns and act up quickly.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Armed conflict can affect the delivery of service for the affected communities and access to the assistance by the beneficiary. Therefore, NRC will conduct security and access assessment prior to the implementation and monitor the situation regularly to inform the relief services such as delivery and distribution. Furthermore, NRC will assess its assistance against the do no harm principle and ensure that all assistance program are conflict sensitive to avoid perpetuation of inequality, conflict or create new conflict among the communities.
