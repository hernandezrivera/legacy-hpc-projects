Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the delivery of quality protection services, improving the protection environment, and supporting durable solutions for IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees and vulnerable host community members in Northeast Nigeria. UNHCR interventions will strengthen the protection environment by identifying and minimizing protection risks to which the affected populations are continually exposed and by expanding the breadth and quality of protection response in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa through:
1. Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring, including through community-based mechanisms, and protection by presence;
2. Quality of registration and profiling of forcibly displaced persons;
3. Enhanced access to legal assistance and legal remedies;
4. Enhanced access to Civil registration, including civil status documentation;
5. Advocacy and capacity building in support of the development of Law and policy, including the domestication of the KC;
6. Promote Self-reliance through access to protection safety-net activities;
7. Support the discussions and the processes for durable solutions

1 - Needs

The protection crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States persists despite the continued surrendering of members of the Non-State Armed Group (NSAG). The protection environment continued to witness human rights violations, attacks, abduction, sexual and gender-based violence, family separation, and forced displacement. Widespread abuses, coupled with the absence in civil administration structures in hard-to-reach areas, has seen the erosion of the protective environment for the forcibly displaced persons and conflict affected individuals. Today, over 2 million people are internally displaced with new displacements from inaccessible areas into camps and host communities being witnessed regularly. This continued displacement has subjected the lives of forcibly displaced persons of to crowded camps and shelters with insufficient humanitarian assistance. New arrivals also put additional strain on the existing scarce humanitarian resources, compelling the most vulnerable to resort to negative coping mechanisms including taking uncalculated risks, exchange of sex for cash and forced marriage for their survival. The ongoing crisis has impacted women, men, girls and boys, and persons with specific needs differently; and their vulnerabilities as well as coping mechanisms varies which requires support and assistance. The most vulnerable civilians face heightened protection risks. These include female and child-headed households, unaccompanied/separated children, adolescent boys and girls, the elderly, persons with medical conditions people with physical and mental disabilities, pregnant and lactating women within IDP camps, host communities and return areas. Recovery efforts continue to be hampered in many areas by lack of basic infrastructures, limited availability of basic services and limited presence of the relevant national actors and key humanitarian partners in some locations, all of which exacerbate protection risks for the most vulnerable populations. Protection threats are often compounded by camps and settlement congestion, lack of basic resources for survival and vulnerable individuals in isolated, insecure, or inhospitable places. Multiple rounds of household assessment, ongoing protection monitoring, and protection assessments show the severity of protection needs for those most affected by conflict and displacement. Insecurity and desperation have resulted in additional challenges for female headed households, increasing their vulnerability to sexual violence and forced/early marriages among others. Unaccompanied and separated children, orphans, and child-headed households, due to the crisis, have critical protection needs. Many children have resorted to hawking/begging as means of survival. NSAG has been perpetrating attacks, harassment, abduction and GBV when women and girls venture outside sites' security perimeter to search for firewood or access their farmlands. The challenges for reintegration of boys and men formerly associated with the NSAG are critical. The relative absence of civil authorities in accessible areas and civil authorities’ functions undertaken by military has undermined the humanitarian and civilian character of IDP sites. There is a critical need to continue to scale up efforts to prevent, refer and respond to cases with identified protection risks with effective protection interventions. Strengthened engagement, collaboration, and coordination with all stakeholders on durable solutions is highly required. In line with the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy, the protection of affected persons must be at the forefront of decision making, informing strategic response. IDP returnee and refugee returnees, including new displacement from inaccessible areas mostly women and children are in dire conditions and in need of urgent, life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection interventions including access to basic services, infrastructure, and livelihood opportunities.

2 - Activities

A wide range of activities will be implemented under 5 main axis of intervention namely favorable protection environment, Fair protection processes and documentation, Security from violence and exploitation, durable solutions and Community Empowerment and Self Reliance and eight objectives. Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring and protection by presence: UNHCR will • Strengthen its protection monitoring system and enhance presence in the field to support affected populations with the identification of protection risks and incidents as well as human rights violations faced by their community members through community-based protection monitoring systems (Protection Action Groups), strengthen access to protection services through construction and rehabilitation of protection desk; training of existing protection action groups and capacitate them to continue to refer/respond to issues, and provide comprehensive protection response including through referrals to other stakeholders; • Continue and strengthen detention centers and facilities monitoring ; • Analyze information on protection risks and incident trends as well as develop and disseminate high quality protection products as a basis for evidence-based advocacy and programming in order to ensure appropriate response by humanitarian actors, as well as to ensure protection mainstreaming within other sectors. Quality of registration and profiling: UNHCR will • Record and track vulnerable IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees through the household assessment and border monitoring tools to allow for delivery of assistance tailored to specific needs, including referrals for specialized services such as child protection, MHPSS, legal protection and medical response by UNHCR and Protection Sector partners. • In collaboration with the competent national institutions, continue the recording of spontaneous refugee returnees to ensure the planning of comprehensive reintegration programs. Access to legal assistance and legal remedies: • Ensure enhanced access to quality legal aid and services, including legal advice, counselling, and legal representation on civil and criminal matters. This includes legal assistance on GBV, Matrimonial issues, housing, land, and property issues among others including through mobile and regular courts. • Conduct advocacy interventions for IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees’ access to national justice systems. • Contribute to the capacity building of relevant actors on ADR mechanisms to support the resolution of civil and social issues within the community. Civil registration and civil status documentation: UNHCR will scale up its support to national institutions for the issuance of civil status documentation namely birth certificates, National ID Card, and certificates of indigene. The number of beneficiaries in 2024 will also be increased. Law and policy developed: Advocacy efforts for the domestication of the Kampala Convention on IDPs through the adoption of a national law for IDP protection in Nigeria will be pursued and intensified. Capacity building activities of relevant national institutions will be conducted to support advocacy as well as extended to humanitarian actors to ensure that assistance is provided in line with international standards and that core protection principles are adhered to by service providers. Durable solutions for returning refugees and IDPs: UNHCR will implement activities that will promote the reintegration of the returnee population.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

Aligned with the principles of the Global Compact on Refugees (GCR), strategic linkages will be actively pursued to facilitate the integration of refugees, asylum seekers, and IDPs into comprehensive, longer-term recovery, and development plans. This integration notably extends to frameworks like the United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) for the period 2023-2027. The engagement of local and state authorities is considered fundamental in this process, aiming to align service provision with national systems wherever feasible. The approach involves targeted interventions to fortify these systems and enhance local capacities, resulting in mutual benefits for refugees, asylum seekers, IDPs, and host communities. Complementarity will be sought with development partners such as UNDP, FAO, IOM, UNICEF and the World Bank to ensure sustainability.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • Additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. • All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
