Needs

An estimated 800,000 people are in need of humanitarian assistance in Libya as a result of political instability, conflict and insecurity, a deteriorating public sector and a dysfunctional economy.These include internally displaced persons, returnees, non-displaced conflict affected people and host communities, refugees and migrants. Most of the people in need of humanitarian assistance are located in urban areas in the Western and Eastern regions of Libya. Lack of access to safety for civilians fleeing conflict has been a major barrier to upholding international legal obligations in the Humanitarian Response. Conflict and insecurity have been the main drivers behind population movements in Libya, with fighting leading to several waves of displacement over the last years, with violence that has forced people to flee their homes and created continus humanitarian needs. In this Volatile situation and prolonged instability, despite the availability of soma aid resources in Libya, the population needs to be better informed on available services, furthermore through this application humanitarian partners will be able to reach with awareness messages the people in need also in locations where they are not present.

Activities

An accessible and easy-to-use digital mobile live platform through which any individual throughout Libya (with and without access to internet) has the possibility to: - receive updated information on the geographical presence of different available services (provided by humanitarian actors or Government authorities, national CBOs) - access information on how to address specific needs without any risk to be stigmatized while associated with being a Refugee, Migrant or IDP (e.g. registration, repatriation and any kind of available help)- receive communication on initiatives carried out by different service providers (e.g. vaccination campaigns, awareness campaigns) A monitoring and trend-analysis component is also factored into the project, as the data collected through the app will allow gathering information on the most searched and used resources and therefore to plan for future sector specific interventions. The application is including an additional feature, which will require data management consent and registration of the user. Through this feature, the individual will be able to provide more bio data and information on his/her status and access to a “chat service” where he/she can comunicate with an operator about the different information and service availability. Information about available services will be collected through existing coordination structures and approved by relevant sectors. Data security, consent and data management to be checked and in place. The intervention will be developed based on existing service mappings collected through and developed by coordination mechanisms. Throughout the development, implementation and monitoring phases of the project, extensive consultation will take place at both sector and inter-sector levels.

Protection Risk 1:Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to beneficiaries through the provision of incorrect information if staff is not adequately trained/ and information on services are not regularly updated Mitigating Measure 1:1. Ensure safety and security measures related to information management are in place and staff is trained and mentored accordingly. 2. Ensure only authorized staff has the capacity to upload and update information in the system3. Ensure that staff is trained, mentored and coached on how to manage sensitive information and disclosure 4. Ensure that a age and gender sensitive complaint and feedback mechanism is in place, staff is trained and beneficiaries are aware of itProtection Risk 2: Risk of doing harm if quality and accuracy of provided information is not ensured: Mitigation Measure 2: 1. Ensure that any information intended for dissemination through the system, will pass a revision and approval process before being published in coordination with all relevant sectors and at inter-sector level 2. Ensure that any change related to availability of services will be promptly reflected in the app (after being checked and cleared) 3. Ensure that information on services will include only those services that have been cleared and approved by sectors to ensure services are following minimum standards of quality, safety and accessibility.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

INTERSOS ensures that all staff are trained once recruited on; code of conduct, policies against PSEA and the Child Safeguarding Policy, to prevent workplace and protect the PoCs from any form of exploitation or discrimination, plus. An established reporting mechanisms will include emails or phone calls to the code of conduct intake committee at the country office, regional or HQ. The committee receives and reviews all reports on misconduct from the staff and beneficiaries, at times investigates and provides recommendation for appropriate action. INTERSOS also will maintains a complaint response mechanism in Libya, managed by project staff, through which PoCs call in to register complaints or make inquiry. INTERSOS staff record all cases received through CRM and make follow-ups till the case is closed. The MEAL unit in INTERSOS, once established will gradually take over this CRM. Safety – Similar to the above, using a combination of protection monitoring, and community based protection mechanisms, this project will measure beneficiary's reported feelings of safety and dignity.
