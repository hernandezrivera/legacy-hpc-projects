Project Summary

Ethiopia is categorized as a high-risk country for humanitarian crises, ranking second in the International Crisis Group’s list of conflicts to monitor in 2022, ranking second in the International Crisis Group’s list of conflicts to monitor in 2022, and comes to the front row when considering levels of exposure to hazards, vulnerability, and lack of coping capacity. Therefore, this project aims at enhancing access to equitable and inclusive education for 48,140 students (50% girls, 50% boys in Hudet and Moyale woredas of Somali Regional State, Dallol and Kunneba woredas of Afar regional State and Bulen, Dibate, Mandura and Wembera woredas of Benishangul regional state for emergency-affected school-aged boys’ and girls’. The project is in line with the 2022 Ethiopia Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). It will support the education service delivery system by strengthening its institutional and technical capacities as well as by building the resilience of children, communities, parents, and the existing education structures through capacity building, coordination and information management. To meet the set objectives and to ensure that children can exercise their right to education and also are protected from risks, ASDEPO will provide the service in safe and protective learning spaces for children affected by the conflict in the selected project areas. This project is guided by the revised education in emergency response strategy (2021-2023) developed by the education cluster in collaboration with the Federal MoE. The project activities include the provision of temporary learning spaces (TLS), teaching and learning materials, recreational kits, rehabilitating of schools/classrooms and building the capacity of teachers for the implementation of accelerated learning programs (ALP) and school readiness programs (ASR) modalities. Teachers will be trained on accelerated learning programs, gender-responsive and sensitive curricula, and psychosocial support (PSS). The project will implement safe school protocols stated by the MOE, for protection against COVID-19 by providing PPE such as facemasks and also by sensitizing teachers, school principals, center management committees/PTAs, children, and the communities on ways of COVID19 transmission and its protection methods.
ASDEPO has been in Benishangul Gumuz, Afar and Somali regions for more than 5 years, currently working in Tigray, Oromia, Amhara, Gambella and the SNNP Regional States. ASDEPO has closer access to the targeted areas with field offices located in Kamashi, Moyale and Awash 7. ASDEPO has regional offices in Assosa, Jigjiga and Semera and have adequate skilled manpower and a well-functioning and efficient logistics system that will be leveraged to support this project.
Presently, ASDEPO is implementing education in emergency project in Kamashi, Sedal and Miziga woredas of Kamashi zone of Benishangul Gumz regional state which runs to October 31st 2022 with the fund secured from UNOCHA. ASDEPO actively participates in regular coordination meetings at national and sub-national level -both education and other clusters, and submit 5W reports on consistent basis.
Finally, the proposal includes cross-cutting activities related to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA); and ensuring accountability to affected population. ASDEPO will mainstream gender and age throughout the implementation of the project by ensuring the participation of women and men and including gender and people with disabilities (PWDs) sensitive selection criteria. The project will be implemented for consecutive eight months with the total budget of 800,000 USD.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

To further ensure accountability of the project and project staff, a feedback mechanism will be put in place (suggestion box, suggestion registration book and hotline calls, as well as case, can report anonymously. This will primarily consist of community meetings, segregated by sex and age and with an emphasis on persons with specific vulnerabilities such as persons with disabilities, in which beneficiaries are encouraged to give feedback or voice complaints/in person at the field office. This method is proposed given the low level of literacy which rules out written feedback. Moreover, ASDEPO M&E staff, not involved in project delivery, will also be involved, so beneficiaries feel confident to report problems. Feedback collected from these meetings will be reviewed by program staff, who will adjust interventions accordingly, where appropriate. Notes will be taken, and actions taken reported back to the next community meeting. ASDEPO has a participatory approach to ensure the active involvement of beneficiaries and local authorities at all levels of the project (from design to M&E). ASDEPO works towards delivering assistance and services in a responsible manner that respects and fosters the rights of beneficiaries by taking account of the diversity of communities. ASDEPO has PSEA/AAP policy. This helps to certify that the organization conveys eminence and reactive programming in line with the surfacing needs of beneficiaries, affected populations and communities and enforce ASDEPO’s zero-tolerance against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and other misconduct. ASDEPO also recognizes that information sharing, listening to and involving affected community’s decision-making contributes to more effective programs and improves the quality of ASDEPO service delivery.
