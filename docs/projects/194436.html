Project Summary

The project will strengthen its initiatives that aim at improving the welfare and protection of Children by increasing resilience and empowering community members through the caregivers and the survivors. To ensure resilience, this will be done through strengthening households and communities with knowledge, skills and services to reduce Human rights violation and vulnerability – This will be conducted in areas of Central Equatoria (kajokeji, Lainya,) Lakes (Rumbek North, Cueibet) and Eastern Equatoria States(Magwi) to most vulnerable populations.
The target population will be IDPs, Returnees, and the host community. The main purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors such as risks of family separation, recruitment,
and use of children by armed forces and groups, psychosocial distress, sexual violence, neglect, abuse and exploitation and the COVID-19 outbreak protracted situation, community displacements, flooding and ongoing conflict which contribute to and aggravate the prevalence of violence against children.
The approach TITI Foundation is using is family-centred; The family-centred approach will be at the focal point targeting different levels: the child, the members of the family, the community and the indigenous organizations and local governments that have the responsibilities with regard to those Children, women's rights and protection. The family-centred approach is also about comprehensive service delivery, i.e. a wide array of prevention, treatment and other support services.
The project will target 18,000 beneficiaries in Kajo-Keji, Lainya Counties in Central Equatoria State; Magwi in Eastern Equatoria; Bor South county in Jonglei, Rumbek North, and Cueibet Counties in Lakes. The Project will increase access to gender-sensitive and age-appropriate services for 18,000 beneficiaries where local leaders and community volunteers will serve as focal points for case management. The Team will develop and test a two-sided approach to case management– one that emphasizes both community- and facility-level referrals together with household-level demand for services.
The project envisions a case management approach that includes a ‘pull’ from service providers and a ‘push’ from parents/guardians and community members as they seek out services for their children due to a higher level of understanding of the benefits of each service.
The Team will meet with key stakeholders to validate the strategy and adopt/adapt materials from other programs eg SASA initiatives to facilitate training and implementation of the project. The project will reference the Inter-Agency Guidelines for case management and child protection, national SOPs and cluster strategy. The response is strategic to the attainment of Cluster objectives and aligns with activities, indicators, and outcomes of the HRP 2022. The project will work closely with GBV, FSL, Health, S/NFI, WASH, Education and Nutrition partners to ensure an integrated approach for a holistic service delivery that entrenches resilience and sustainability of the services rendered to the affected children and their caregivers affected by multiple shocks.
The project activities will focus on mitigating child protection risks, preventing and responding to child protection cases through; CFS programming, capacity building of frontline service providers on CPiE, Case management including FTR services, Structured/non-structured PSS, Community Outreach and dialogues, and safe referrals. TITI Foundation will conduct in and out-of-school campaign/advocacy programs on CP, and participate in the national and international forums/events like the 16 days of activism, International OVC day, and The day of the Africa child and world aids day to ensure the children's voices are advanced.

1 - Needs

Children in Need (CIN) increased by 25% compared to 2021. An estimated 2.9 children will need immediate life-saving child protection (CP) services. Children and adolescents in South Sudan continue to be exposed to a wide range of protection risks including, risks of family separation, recruitment, and use of children by armed forces and groups, psychosocial distress, sexual violence, neglect, abuse, exploitation and child survivors of GBV requiring expansion of CP services, in turn, increases resource requirement in 2022. The COVID-19 outbreak protracted situation, community displacements, flooding and ongoing conflict have exacerbated this situation; hindering adequate, safe access and full utilization of lifesaving humanitarian services in South Sudan. The Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CPAOR) Plan to reach 930 K children,adolescents, and caregivers across 65 counties with the highest needs and 9 counties with high severity particularly Kapoeta South, Bor South, Fangak, Pibor, Rubkona, Akobo, Tambura, Ezo and Twic East with life-saving Child Protection (CP) services. Continued outbreaks of violence, COVID-19, food insecurity, and floods in some parts of the country, including Western Equatoria (Tambura), Jonglei, Upper Nile, Warrap, and Lakes states, continue to weaken the protective environment for children and significantly impair the capacity of families to appropriately care for children, putting them at risk of violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation. According to the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS+), the number of documented child protection cases including Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) increased from 26,009 (12,128 girls and 13,881 boys) in 2020 to 30,441 (14,185 girls and 16,256 boys) by the end of 2021, with Unity, Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Lakes states have the highest number of documented child protection cases, including UASCs, child neglect, child labour, domestic violence and child trafficking. In addition, 2021 FSNMS+ survey results show that 3.7 per cent of interviewed households indicated that their children were missing or not living in the household at the time of the assessment and are at risk of abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Although the concentration of child protection cases in the above locations is mainly based on partners reporting child protection cases and FSNMS+ assessment findings, the actual situation of children in termsof protection, issues may not significantly vary across states.Similarly, violence against children and adolescents, including grave violations, remains a major concern in communities in South Sudan. The Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting on grave child rights violations verified 196 grave violations including the recruitment/use of 129 children (five girls) in 2021, compared to 62 children (one girl) in 2020, presenting a surge in recruitment despite the signing of a Comprehensive Action Plan by the Government to put an end to the six grave child right violations. In addition, according to the GBVIMS, of the 6,237 GBV incidents reported between January and September 2021, 28 per cent of the cases are child survivors under the age of 18. It is worth noting that the percentage of child survivors increased from 21 per cent in 2020 to 28 per cent in 2021.

2 - Project activities

TITI Foundation will continue to strengthen Community engagement modalities which form a critical part of all protection interventions, incorporating such approaches as media campaigns, community consultations on feedback, complaint boxes, hotlines and institutionalization of complaint and feedback mechanisms that respond to CP issues. Titi Foundation will enhance CPAOR to scale up critical CP intervention to address and respond to the increased protection risks facing children and adolescents in South Sudan. This will include expansion of the comprehensive case management services for children and adolescents identified with critical protection risks including family tracing and reunification services (FTR), reintegration for Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG), and support to children survivors of GBV. TITI Foundation will also work with the National coordination through technical working groups (CMTF &UASC WG, and CAAFAG WG), and the utilization of the CPIMS++ to contribute and improve the quality of case management services. Support the expansion of child protection services which will include the provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services for the affected children, adolescents, and caregivers. These include the establishment/rehabilitation of child-friendly spaces and identifying community spaces supportingdisplaced persons and those in PSS needs, roll out of structured MHPSS programs, referrals for specialized MHPSS services through established CP case management referral pathways, life skills training and recreational activities. Integration of CP with other sectors will be prioritized to optimize child protection outcomes. Collaboration and coordination with GBV AOR will be reinforced to address the increasing needs of child survivors of SGBV. Education and child protection partners will continue to work together on improving children’s psychosocial well-being and protective environment in schools. Preventive and responsive child protection programming will be promoted through awareness-raising on prevalent child protection concerns in the communities; door to door visits; campaigns and dialogue engagements through Community Based Child Protection Structures, establishment and operation of integrated Child Help Desks (CHDs) at service points such as schools or health centres, situation and response monitoring; and the use of different communication platforms and IEC materials to maintain the information among the masses to promote resilience and reduce negative coping strategies. Community-based child protection will be strengthened, and the technical and institutional capacity of frontline child protection workers and community volunteers will be enhanced in order to expand coverage in under-served areas and improve the quality-of-service provision. Community-led innovations by community structures, leaders or parent groups, would also be supported to enhance ownership and sustainability of prevention purposes. Expansion of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services, Strengthen Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group Community-based child Protection approaches, capacity building, and Coordination strengthened Strengthen Case Management approach including through cash-based assistance (including for UASC, GBV survivors, and CAAFAG) and Community-Based Child Protection (CBCP), capacity building and outreaches.

The survivors will be supported on a conditional basis that they attend the sessions and access post defilement care or seek health care services. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All of Titi Foundations Local Monitoring Groups are inclusive of different population groups, to ensure that the staff are receiving feedback from even the most vulnerable members of a community. The establishment of the complaint desk with a focal person allows for people who are not able to utilize the suggestions box to also provide feedback. The Local Monitoring Groups also consult with the broader community and are able to advocate on behalf of their most vulnerable members. Titi Foundation staff also consult with communities to identify vulnerable members to ensure that they have access to the program’s activities and services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Men and women are systematically asked to share information about needs and cultural preferences. TF tries to adjust the intervention accordingly. M&E team is monitoring the community engagement through a questionnaire designed to monitor the safety of beneficiaries during assistance delivery and AAP. Accountability to affected populations and community engagement will be considered at all stages of the project. A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed in all locations to ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. As outlined above, the CBPP will ensure the transparency, accountability, acceptance and ownership of the project for the targeted community. Project Management Committees will be established in each location to ensure that there is ongoing communication and coordination between the beneficiaries and TF. The PMCs will monitor the project activities from the beneficiaries' perspective, provide feedback and identify issues through meetings with TF. In addition, post-distribution monitoring/ rapid assessments will also be conducted over the course of the project and will serve as another form of AAP by evaluating the effectiveness of the project in addressing the beneficiaries’ most immediate needs, according to the beneficiaries themselves, as well as their level of satisfaction. The PDM will consist of representative surveys, as well as key informant interviews (KIIs) with beneficiaries to gather a more in-depth body of information. TITI Foundation will utilize and follow the CP Cluster South Sudan AAP tools (e.g. Rapid PDM guidelines and questionnaire).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA and child protection policy that all staff have signed as part of the code of conduct and are trained on, including those located in the various field locations. Additionally, whenever services or projects are implemented, communities are provided with information on the PSEA policy, awareness of what PSEA is, and the process of reporting any incidents. Beneficiaries are encouraged to come forward with any concerns or information they have to any member of Titi Foundation and are also provided with information on referral pathways for each community. Throughout the project, basic PSEA training will be cascaded to our targeted beneficiaries and community volunteers during our baseline surveys, focus group discussions, protection training and campaigns. Titi will work in collaboration with other protection partners on the ground to ensure that our beneficiaries are included in ongoing PSEA training. Also, IEC materials on PSEA will be sourced, translated and distributed in project locations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Titi Foundation has established Local Monitoring Groups in each of the locations that it works in, to ensure that in addition to M&E mechanisms, community members are able to provide regular feedback on project assistance. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback it receives through these mechanisms and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referral services needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce the risk of harm to beneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organization's policies and code of conduct.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Titi Foundation maintains strong ties with the community leaders and members in all the locations that it operates in, reducing the risks of tensions around the implementation of services. Titi Foundation has digitized its data collection procedures for M&E protocols to ensure both data privacy and quality due to the sensitivity of the issues being reserved. All staff are trained on ethical protocols for data management. The locations of the suggestion box and the desk for the focal point to receive complaints will be decided in conjunction with communities through a joint participation strategy. Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy which all staff are trained on in all locations. The organization’s management works closely with field-based staff to ensure that they have the resources and support needed to address any issues that may arise in this area. Communities are also provided with information at the beginning of each project to ensure that they are familiar with the mechanisms within Titi Foundation that are available to support them if they raise any concerns or complaints.Titi Foundation will meet with community members before the implementation to outline the plan and discuss any concerns that community members may have, including risks they may encounter when accessing services. For community members that may encounter barriers or difficulties in accessing humanitarian assistance, Titi Foundation will work with the households and community members to identify safer alternatives, i.e. a household member can collect items on behalf of a person with disabilities, or Titi Foundation’s staff to deliver to their household while accompanied and monitored by community members. Establishment of, and communication about, referral and protection services are communicated with each of Titi Foundation’s projects and services, to ensure that all community members have the information they need to access assistance. This is provided in all locations that the organization is working in.Titi Foundation monitors the security context and any threats or risks posed to its beneficiaries in all of its locations. Services will be postponed when the risks of violence to beneficiaries and staff are too high. In cases where alternative accommodations can be made, Titi Foundation will work to establish a secondary plan with community members.
