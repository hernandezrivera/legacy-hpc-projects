Project Summary

This project will provide 100% in-kind ES/NFI support to new IDPs, people with new vulnerabilities and protracted IDPs, returnees and HC who are not able to support themselves. The total target is 11,400 individuals (2,900 HHs). Through gift-in-kind (GIK) distribution, the project intends to support 3,300 individuals (550 HHs) with emergency shelter materials and 8,100 individuals (1350 HHs) with life saving non food items (NFIs). The items will be sourced from pipeline through signing of MoU with cluster and raising pipeline request to cluster for approval upon approval of verification/registration reports in order to access the items for distributions. The items will be transported for distribution by log cluster.
CHAS will conduct joint in depth needs analysis and protection risk assessment with the involvement of protection staff and the participation of the targeted populations through consultative community meetings, key informant interviews, focus group discussions and household level and individual interviews with men, women, boys and girls .
During assessment, CHAS will use the SNFI Toolkit as a guide to review conflict sensitivity, HLP issues, GBV referral pathways, and safety audit findings. CHAS will work with HLP Partners - NRC to promote security of tenure for IDPs and returnees. CHAS do in-depth HLP assessments before provision of emergency shelter material to the affected population.
CHAS will use advantage of its static presence in Uror to respond rapidly to any S/NFI emergency and engage more with communities within the county at cost effective manner. CHAS will coordinate its activities with Jonglei SFP and other partners (ADA, Nile Hope,HDC,Reach-Africa&Tearfund) in order to share resource and avoid duplication. Since CHAS is statically present, CHAS employ coordinated response with other partners in its area of operations by providing relevant information about the area and planning joint responses. CHAS will act as a SiFP
CHAS will employ integrated approach through coordination of activities with SFPs and SiFPs other clusters –FSL, WASH, Health, Education, Protection and CCCM and including partners out of cluster system in the targeted location through newly proposed humanitarian hub. CHAS will use the hub for coordination of joint missions with other S/NFI and non S/NFI partners, especially the mobile partners from Juba and static partners on the ground and for sourcing items for quick response to emergencies in hard-to-reach areas.
CHAS will establish complaint and feedback mechanisms in its intervention locations through establishment of community led complaint and feedback committees/ relief committees and engage them throughout the project cycle. CHAS will provide all categories of affected population (men, women, boys, girls,) with accurate and updated information about the intervention through preferred community communication channels and will clearly explain any feedback to the community and ensure that information on interventions is provided in formats and appropriate languages reaching everyone. CHAS will as share feedback from post distribution monitoring with the community.
CHAS will enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills through conducting training for community lead committees.
Joint Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted 1 month after the intervention by the gender balanced M&E team, RRC leaders beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to measure the impact of the projects and to determine if the assistance reached the most vulnerable groups. Community committees will be formed for complaint/feedback mechanism and to insure community participation and empowerment at all phases of project cycle. Whereas continuous rapid monitoring will be conducted during distribution days. Feedback on the findings will be shared with affected population and with SNFI Cluster which will feed into ICWG.

1 - Needs

According to UNOCHA Humanitarian Snapshot of September 2019 there are 7.2 Million people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan and at least 184,753 people are in Jonglei State. According to HNO (pg 1), 2.3 million people are in needs of S/NFI assistance in 2020, which includes the IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities and 1.02 million are targeted for S/NFI assistance in 2020. More than 70% of newly displaced population outside PoCs and collective centres are unable to meet their basic household needs and will require interventions from S/NFI cluster. More than 80% of the returnees (both former IDPs and Refugees) are in need of SNFI assistance. And According to HNO (pg 3), Uror County is one of the Counties in severe humanitarian need. Host Community constitutes the third most affected population in need, standing at 16%, the Host Community copying mechanism have been eroded due to sharing of resources with IDPs and returnees, therefore, the cluster has calculated to include approximately 5% of the most vulnerable Host Community to be in need of SNFI assistance as they shared their resources with the displaced population. This based on SNFI distribution data in 2019. REACH assessment of hard to reach areas of June 2019 Uror County reported 17% of IDPs living in the open and 33% of the IDPs primarily living in temporary structures or in the open while reporting 78% of the IDPs receiving NFIs from the local community. This indicates highest need for NFIs and slightly high need for shelter materials. This project will respond to needs of the new IDPs, protracted IDPs, returnees, host/affected non-displaced people in Uror County. Uror is home to many displaced persons and there is high expectation of returns if the new government is formed so CHAS will respond to the needs of the returnees in this location. With the resources of host communities being over stretched with the overwhelming number of IDPs and returnees sharing shelter and NFIs with them which has increased the vulnerabilities of the host households the project will also support them, more so if the peace agreement did not hold and there is return to war and more displacements occur CHAS will address S/NFI needs of the would be IDPs.

2 - Activities

Conduct joint, detailed Shelter and NFI needs analysis and protection risk assessment with focus on risks level, vulnerability and protection threats using paper or mobile data collection method by use of Kobo software. Prior to any needs analysis, CHAS will conduct desk research and situational analysis of the conflict/disaster affected population. Conduct joint verification/registration of target beneficiaries as per the agreed target criteria with support from CHAS’ protection team and focus on community coping mechanisms. Upon identification of needs and approval of needs analysis report by the cluster, CHAS will verify/register beneficiaries with support of CHAS’ protection team, relief committees, local community members and local enumerators. Conduct protection and GBV risks awareness/sensitization. Protection team will conduct practical community awareness and sensitization sessions on protection and GBV risks, verifying vulnerable persons with specific needs (people with disability, elderly, unaccompanied minors). Facilitation of referrals with be done by the protection staff during NFI intervention cycle. The protection officer will support the NFIs team train to tommunity leaders, complaint and feedback committee in Psychological First Aid arising from violence and to continuously create awareness on prevention of malarial disease, Ebola, HIV/AIDS and HEV. Submit pipeline request.CHAS will raise PLR to core pipeline and logistics cluster for delivery of the S/NFIs to distribution locations. Conduct distribution of the essential NFIs and emergency shelter materials to targeted (verified/registered) most vulnerable affected beneficiaries. CHAS with support of protection team, relief committees, local community volunteers will distribute S/NFIs to identified and registered most vulnerable population affected by crisis in the targeted locations in line with Cluster’s approach. Conduct demonstrations and community awareness/sensitization on proper and careful use of S/NFIs at distribution points. CHAS' distribution team, relief committees and local community volunteers will conduct demonstrations on proper and careful use of the items with special focus on insecticide treated mosquito nets. CHAS will also conduct community awareness/sensitization on prevention of malarial disease, EVD, HIV/AIDS and HEV. These will continue even after distributions since CHAS has amble time to engage more communities due to its static presence. Conduct protection monitoring, sensitization and referral as part of protection mainstreaming in S/NFI. The protection team will be present at every phase of the project cycle; during needs analysis, verification/registration, and distribution and at PDM phases respectively to ensure protection mainstreaming. The protection team will also train volunteers, casual laborers and complaint/feedback committee on risk of GBV, be open to receiving GBV cases without judgement and provide accurate, up-to-date information on the available services. Conduct live skills training with focus on S/NFI. These trainings will be specific according to community needs. However, CHAS will also introduce new ideas and will train communities upon acceptance. CHAS Will encourage affected communities to acquire fibre producing plants from which local rope can be got for shelter construction and for making animal knots instead of using mosquito nets which are meant for addressing malaria cases. Conduct rapid monitoring during interventions and Post Distribution Monitoring between (2-4) after distribution where possible. CHAS' gender balanced M&E team will conduct rapid monitoring during distributions in order to monitor the quality of the interventions and programming. Post-distribution monitoring (PDMs) will be conducted for 50% of distributions if access and security permits. Representatives of affected population both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, and local authorities will participate.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

CHAS will engage local authorities, community leader and beneficiaries in targeting and entitlement. During distributions, beneficiaries will sign against their name to confirm the receipt of the aid.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project is designed to address the perspectives and challenges of displacement affected communities in the way that the IDPs are targeted for S/NFI support and capacity building training aimed at recovery and resilience

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will build on already existing community coping mechanism and will promote resilience through the existing community/government systems. CHAS will work with community members and local authorities through the entire project cycle. Humanitarian support is acceptable by communities and government of South Sudan.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The intervention will provide relief aid to the conflict and disaster affected population and help to settle the returning vulnerable population which is in line with the national ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management (MHADM) strategic plan 2018-2020. Therefore CHAS' response will complement on efforts of government in achieving its objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Security situation and humanitarian access has slightly improved as a result of R-ARCSS, though some parts of the country continued to still experience armed conflicts. With the access improvement, humanitarian actors would be able to deliver much needed humanitarian services in hard to reach areas. However, if the R-TGoNU is not formed, the security situation is predicted to worsen in 2020. CHAS protection team will conduct protection risk analysis in the targeted intervention locations before conducting any intervention. The finding will be shared with protection cluster who will then advice on the next steps.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The training component of the project is aimed at ensuring sustainability. CHAS will organize skills and leadership trainings to the community members. CHAS will look into avenues of encouraging community to acquire fiber producing plants from which they can access ropes locally for their future shelter construction instead of using mosquito nets as rope for shelter construction

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Prior to any intervention, CHAS will conduct in-depth protection risk assessment with its experience protection team in order to understand possible protection threats and risks. CHAS has PSEA policy and has incorporated it in its code of conduct which is signed by all its staff and volunteers. CHAS through its protection team, will conduct protection trainings stakeholders in its locations of intervention. CHAS will fully engage the stakeholders including the affect communities in project design and targeting criteria and explain clearly that what category of people will and will not directly benefit from the assistance. CHAS will enlighten the stakeholders on national S/NFI cluster strategy show that they are aware that CHAS is implementing what has already been agreed upon at the national level. CHAS will incorporate HLP issues in to its programmings. Before distribution of shelter materials to the affected population, CHAS will ensure that HLP issues are addressed in order ensure land tenure security. This will be done with involvement of HLP partners.CHAS will continuously communicate to the beneficiaries of the complaint and feedback mechanism through channels, formats and language that is appropriate for everyone. CHAS will engage the beneficiaries to understand why is it important to get complaint and feedback from them. CHAS in consultation with affected population will allocate the distribution site closer to the settlement area, in a safe location. CHAS will avoid distribution near military barracks. Distribution exercise will be closed a bit earlier on every day of distribution in order to allow beneficiaries reach home before it is late in the evening.Women will be advised to move in groups to minimize rape cases. CHAS' protection team will conduct service mapping and identify referral-pathways for referral of protection cases (GBV,SGBV) to relevant partners.
