Project Summary

The purpose of the proposed interventions is aimed to enhance the national capacity to
respond to the continuing health needs for the affected displaced people, returnees, and
host communities in north east Syria, challenged with protection concerns.
VIYAN will provide an integrated package of services spanning public primary health
sector including strengthening mental health and psychosocial support component.
The supporting modality for the public PHCs will cover infrastructural rehabilitation,
facilities operationalization, comprehensive and standardized PHC services provision both
diagnostic and treatment procedures, strengthening the inter-sectorial and external referral
system as part of strengthen the national capacity by incentiving drivers, maintenance for
the vehicles as well as covering the operational cost for 24/7 work to the designated
hospitals (HSK national hospital, Derek national hospital, Sur Hospital and Abu-Alhamam
Hospital) with case-by-case follow up by VIYAN medical staff, promoting the knowledge
of the targeted populations by conducting continuous awareness activities in areas of
need, carrying out a capacity building component throughout the project lifetime covering
topics such as (PM+, PFA, EWARS, first aid, IPC practices, SRH and medical case
management) for the national healthcare workers.
Services provided under the proposed interventions will address specific needs of women,
men, girls, and boys, including older persons and persons with disabilities. All phases of
the intervention will rely on close coordination and consultation with affected populations,
as well as key stakeholders, including national and sub-national clusters, Directorate of
Health, and relevant line ministries.
The target beneficiaries will mainly be IDPs out-of-camps, returnees, and host populations
in areas of high severity of needs that continue to experience inadequate public basic
services, overwhelming existing resources and are yet to experience relatively stable
security as well as areas out-of-coverage and hard-to-reach areas.

4 - Needs

Against the background of the continuing humanitarian crisis since 2011, an estimated of 12.4 million people need health assistance and over 47,000 confirmed COVID-19 cases across Syria as of March 2021 (2021 Needs and Response Summary) with only 58 per cent of hospitals and 53 per cent of primary health care centers (PHCs) in Syria are fully functional (HERAMS, Q3 2020). Prone to political instability, violence, corruption, armed conflict, and natural disasters and disease outbreaks, Northeast Syria deemed to be at “very high risk” of a humanitarian crisis requiring international assistance. Health Actors will therefore focus on delivering sustained and qualitative essential health services, strengthen medical supply chains across all modalities, ensuring availability of trained, qualified health care workers, enhancing early warning surveillance systems and ensure health system and laboratory capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver timely response to disease outbreaks, Strengthening COVID-19 preparedness and response pillars (2021 HRP). Health Infrastructure in NES is under considerable strain, which according to (2020 HeRAMS annual report) in DEZ: out of 107 public PHCs (33% are partially functioning and 66% are non-functioning while 9% fully damaged and 21% partially damaged), in Al Hasakah: out of out of 100 public PHCs (54% are partially functioning and 46% are non-functioning while 7% fully damaged and 23% partially damaged).

5 - Implementation of activities

A1: Provision of quality primary healthcare including NCD care, MHPSS, trauma care and referrals in DEZ and HSK, through supporting four public PHCCs (Tal Kocher & Sofya in HSK – Abu Alkhatir & Western Jarda in DEZ). Assistance will be through the supply of essential medicines, equipment, medical supplies, incentives to PHC staff, PPEs, minor rehabilitation works, purchasing of essential furniture and ensuring a stable power and water supply as well as supporting the referral system. VIYAN will ensure that PHCCs are staffed (12 Doctors, 10 M-Nurses, 10 F-Nurses, 6 APHs, 3 PHs, 6 lab technicians, 8 Psychologists, 16 CHWs, 4 Data Entries, 8 Service Staff, 8 Guards, 6 Ambulance drivers) A2: Contribute to the improvement of health services through capacity building and supportive supervision of health personnel and CHWs, in which they will be trained on mhGAP, PM+, PFA, COVID19 case management and IPC. The proposed activities will be delivered through well-established coordination and reporting mechanisms including regular attendance of NES HWG meetings, MHPSS Sub-working group & health authorities’ meetings, reporting on 4Ws, Activity Info and EWARN. The project will be monitored and evaluated by MEAL Manager Erbil and MEAL Officer NES who will ensure data collection forms are designed and completed using quantitative & qualitative tools. A3: Rehabilitation of the targeted PHCCs with the aim to restore the national minimum building requirements.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

RISKS: 1. Sexual abuse and exploitation 2. Sexual activity with beneficiaries 3. Sexual harassment 4. Sexual violence 5. Physical/Emotional Abuse 6. Child marriage MITIGATION AND PREVENTION MEASURES: The above acts are prohibited and considered a violation of VIYAN Code of Conduct which all VIYAN employees must adhere and sign, and as such, subject to disciplinary actions including termination. Mandatory trainings and refreshers on PSEA and safeguarding issues for all VIYAN employees and volunteers. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism. Availability of effective, safe, and congenital reporting mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISKS: 1. Duplication or mis coordination in activities 2. Limited availability of medicines, delayed deliveries at boarder points 3. Fraud and aid deviation 4. Accountability to affected populations 5. Deterioration of security conditions in the areas where the project operates. ASSUMPTIONS: 1. Coordination network among partners is good 2. Local market is stable 3. Antifraud policy in place and being implemented 4. Local community members are well involved in the project design and implementation 5. Security conditions remain stable and predicated. MITIGATION: 1. improve networking with NES HWG and LHA to avoid duplications 2. conduct medical quality assessment both in KRI and NES 3. Actively participate in the UN & Health Cluster coordination meetings and strengthening policies on antifraud and misappropriation of humanitarian aid usage as well as staff are sanitized and signed code of conduct 4. conduct key informant interviews with IDP, host community populations, project staff and LHA officials about the project (all gender & age) in addition to conduct patient satisfaction surveys in the target locations, VIYAN will hire local staff providers for service delivery, which allows for a better understanding of the local operational context 5. Arrange provision of larger quantities of medical supply in to overcome protracted interruption of services as well as constantly monitor the security situation through our security focal point sources.
