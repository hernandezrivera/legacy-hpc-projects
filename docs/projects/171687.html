Project Summary

In the midst of increasing displacement caused by conflict and natural disasters in Somalia, AVORD is submitting this proposal with the aim of enhancing the living conditions of affected communities through the provision of shelter and NFIs and rehabilitation of critical infrastructure. Program activities will target 6,040 households (36,240 persons) from affected communities in need in Afgoye, Marka, Benadir, Baidoa, Belet Weyne, Jowhar, Balad, and Belet Xaawo. Combined, these districts host 737,537 IDPs, or 30% of the IDP population in Somalia. Where market conditions allow, AVORD will use a cash/voucher modality for implementation. Otherwise, an in-kind modality will be used during which AVORD will distribute NFIs and emergency shelter kits directly to beneficiaries.

Project Activities

Following the need-based and transparent selection of beneficiaries, AVORD will provide emergency shelters and Non-Food Items to 36,240 vulnerable persons (6,040 households) from the IDPs, refugee returnees, and host communities in specific districts of Somalia where these groups are concentrated. The target group will receive Non-Food Items including core items such as blankets and plastic sheets that will provide protection from inclement weather conditions while providing basic levels of privacy. The content of each NFI kit will be based on the most recent Shelter Cluster Standards and will be gender-sensitized to ensure various items cover the specific needs of women, girls, men, and boys. Where market conditions, AVORD will use a cash/voucher modality for the provision of NFIs and emergency shelter kits. Otherwise, an in-kind modality will be used during which AVORD will distribute relief items directly to beneficiaries. 6,240 persons (1,040 households) belonging to the most vulnerable households will be assisted with emergency shelters in addition to NFIs. Site planning and/or re-planning will be an integral part of shelter construction, enabling safe and secure access to essential services and facilities while ensuring appropriate privacy and separation between individual household shelters and that pathways exist to provide all-weather access to individual dwellings and facilities. In addition to shelters, small-scale, community-level infrastructure will be identified and rehabilitated to enhance affected communities' access to basic services. The design of activities will always be informed by context-specific need and protection analysis and will be completed in close coordination with target communities to ensure their priority needs are met. AVORD will also adopt a systematic approach to beneficiary selection with a clear criterion to prioritize the most vulnerable in target communities including unaccompanied children, older persons, persons with a disability, single women, pregnant and lactating mothers, single headed-households, persons with chronic illness or psychological condition, large families and/or families with babies, toddlers or children attending school and persons at risk as identified by protection officers. Data collected to inform beneficiary selection will be segregated by gender to ensure that no group is left out. The effectiveness of the aid package will always be evaluated through Post Distribution and Post Construction Monitoring exercises, field visits, and other evaluation methods thus ensuring that beneficiaries access their services safely and in a dignified manner. Elaborate feedback and complaint mechanisms will be set up to ensure accountability to the affected population.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Where market conditions allow, AVORD will use a cash-based approach to provision of NFIs and ESKs. Cash-based programming provides greater choice and empowerment to affected people and strengthens local markets.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Since NFI market conditions in Somalia vary by location, AVORD will conduct market assessments as part of program design to explore market dynamics and inform the appropriate implementation modality.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation measures to prevent exposure of beneficiaries to violence during registration, distribution and accessing aid: 1) Continuously assess local dynamics and make plans accordingly. 2) Ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution. 3) Undertake program activities during daylight hours, ensuring ample time for beneficiaries to go back home before nightfall. 4) Adapt and observe the Do-No-Harm principles at all times to prevent unintended negative consequences. 5) Provide maximum protection to beneficiary data and householdinformation to prevent misuse and exposure of target persons to risk.Mitigation measures to prevent exclusion of beneficiaries from decision-making processes:1) Establish Joint project implementation committees involving different fabrics of target communities thus creating a platform where marginalized communities can share their grievances if any. 2) Operationalize a project-level hotline where target beneficiaries can share views and/or concerns. 3) Encourage communities to engage in various phases of project implementation from need identification, design, implementation, monitoring, and handover.4) Organize periodic mobilization meetings to keep community leaders informed about project activities and outputs. Mitigation measures to prevent interference from external stakeholders::1) Undertake direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without the use of middlemen or gatekeepers. 2) Adapt the highest levels of transparency in beneficiary selection and provide target communities with timely information on the selection criteria, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them.3) Develop and enforce elaborate selection criteria with priority accorded to most vulnerable households including those from marginalized and minority groups. 4) Work closely with local authorities to mitigate against stakeholders who may undermine project success. Mitigation measures for beneficiaries not accessing feedback and complaint mechanism:1) Ensure that target beneficiaries are closely involved in all project phases.2) Establish elaborate feedback and complaint mechanisms with each project and encourage beneficiaries to use them.3) Operationalize hotlines and mobilize communities to submit feedback and/or complaints 4) Organize periodic meetings with community leaders to document feedback and address any challengesMitigation measures for risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project areas:1) Secure land titles from relevant authorities/land owners before construction of shelters2) Ensure that project resources are utilized only in areas were tenure is secured for a minimum of 5 years.3) Issue land titles in the name of beneficiaries and register the same with relevant authorities. 4) Ensure that shelters are not constructed in areas where risk of eviction is high
