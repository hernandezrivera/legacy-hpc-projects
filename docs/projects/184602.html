Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, about 6.4 million people are still in need of humanitarian WASH support in a country that has been in a state of protracted emergency for decades. Unreliable access to water often from unimproved sources is still evident feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. In most part of south central severe drought have affected almost all the regions, water scarcity is a leading cause of displacements and conflicts. Protection risks are worryingly high in all parts of Somalia, resulting in all categories of users being at high risk of violence when using facilities.
Unreliable access to a temporary water source and/or unprotected water is still an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one region to another, however, Bay and Bakol region below 25% each. In most urban and sub-urban hubs and IDP settlements, access to an adequate quantity of water is often granted but water is unsafe and often microbiologically contaminated due to cross-contamination by poorly design water supply and sanitation systems. More than half of the population lack access to adequate sanitation. The situation is particularly critical in Bakool region below 25% and in IDP settlements in Bay and Hiran region, Hand washing with soap at critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional hand washing point is limited. This percentage drops to below 30% in Hirshabelle, despite that these two states were badly affected by AWD/Cholera recently. The chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure le Somalia continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2020 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots Hiraan and Bay region included. HRDO will continue to focus activities on to most vulnerable populations in hard to reach locations; IDPs, people at risk of AWD /cholera, acutely malnourished and people in drought-affected areas in Bay, Bakol and Hiran region. Water is the greatest need in the community and has remained a serious challenge during this period where the communities are experiencing the triple effect of the COVID-19, Locust infestation and drought .IDPs are mostly affected by the water problem and as per the HIDIG assessed IDPs it indicated that IDPs cannot also afford to buy water since they don’t have an income to sustain this. The sanitation situation in the area is 1 latrine per 500 persons. Many people are living with disabilities inflicted from conflict and are unable to access any water consumption points. Protection concerns among women and PLWD are also at an alarming rate where GBV cases are reported daily during HIDIG rapid assessment.
HIDIG therefore proposed intervention will provide immediate access to safe drinking water to 12,000 vulnerable population boys, girls, women and men affected by the drought and other natural disaster, while ensuring 10% PLWD, HIDIG intends to rehabilitates 15 shallow wells beneﬁting 7,500 men, women and children to enable enhanced the community access to safe drinking water. The project will also provide the target beneﬁciaries with hygiene kits as well as provide hygiene promotion to ensure community is aware of the water born risk as well as sanitation and other vector born disease. Through this HIDIG will ensure to establish 160 water management committee at IDPs and rehabilitate 20 water sources to enable ease the water related protection risk among boys, girls, women, men and these with disability while also ensuring greater consultation and inclusion of these vulnerable group in project implementation

1. - Projects activities

1.1. Provide safe and clean water to 1,500 drought/ﬂoods affected households (9,000 persons) through voucher system. Every household will receive 7.5 liter/day as SPHERE standard for 60 days. The selection of the beneﬁciaries will be done with the consultation of community leader1.2. 1.2. Rehabilitate 10 shallow wells (8 with hand pump and 2 with animal trough) beneﬁting around 5000 men, women and children. The community leaders including women and PLWD will consulted the design and the location of the shallow wells. 1.3. Rehabilitate 4 water pans beneﬁting 2,000 men, women and children including PLWD while also saving animals from the recurrent drought. The action will include: de-silting, increasing the volume of the water pan and protecting its areas from animals and human, in order to avoid the contamination of the water pan. 1.4. Establish and train: (a) 22 water management committees (WMCs) at community and IDP level from the rehabilitated 22 water sources, composed 130 members shared equally by men and women (70 men and 60 women) on water source management and water source maintenance to keep the sustainability of the water source. (b) 38 well operators shared equally men and women (19 men and 19 women) on operation and maintenance (O&M), chlorination of water sources and collection of revenues. (c) 100 community members from community leaders, community elders, women groups, youth and people living with disability shared equally by men and women (50 men and 50 women) on Disaster and Risk Reduction mechanisms (DRR) and conﬂict resolution mechanisms. 1.5. Rehabilitate/Construct 200 gender-sensitive and ﬂood proof IDP latrines (10% reserved PLWD) benefiting a total of 6,000 vulnerable IDPs and host communities. The action includes: desludging of the ﬁlled septic tank, repairing of broken ﬂoors, replacing the damaged walls and doors. 1.6. construct 150 gender-sensitive, desludging and ﬂood proof IDP latrines with hand washing facilities, HIDIG propose 10% (15) the latrines constructed is preserved for people with disability, this will ensure inclusion of vulnerable and disadvantage group in the project services delivery 1.7. Conduct comprehensive community hygiene promotion sessions to promote good hygiene and sanitation practices in targeted communities and IDPs in Baidoa, Beletweyne, Ceelbarde and Rabdhure district

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Beletweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing Wash service and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. HIDIG will ensure to provide hotline/call centers within the community WASH committees who will in turn report to the project team for quick feedback and attention these vulnerable children and mothers. HIDIG will ensure awareness creation to reduce the occurrence of domestic violence by conducting joint community and other stakeholders meeting within the project area Conﬂict a rising from water point. HIDIG will mitigate this menace through building the capacity of WASH committee as well as improving proper coordination with government line ministry for sustainable water services. The WASH committee will be selected among the communities with the project location as well as provide them with infrastructural equipment that will enable them to perform the minor breakdown within the water sources. Based on consultations with the community and elders within the target IDPs, the need for the project to continue, to be expanded and integrated became evident. HIDIG shall be able to take into consideration the needs at the ground and the support required so that the project responds to their needs and priorities and that the project activities do not go against. Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneﬁciaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor Wash service, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, HIDIG will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identiﬁcation and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women into WASH committee, HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. Building community structures including local water point that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conﬂict and violence. Communities ﬂeeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate Wash service without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence
