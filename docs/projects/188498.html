Project Summary

UNHCR as a lead agency coordinating the NFI responses in Syria and from Gaziantep (Turkey), will provide through this project the lifesaving and life sustaining NFI support, through distribution of NFIs and seasonal supplementary items to IDPs, returnees and affected host communities.
The project is in line with both the priorities of the HRP and Sector Strategy and the project will be completed in line with Sector guidance. UNHCR’s NFI programme recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group through a range of NFI interventions.
Emergency NFI response will be provided through inter-agency convoy deliveries to hard-to- reach locations, NW Syria, NE Syria situations and camp context and through distribution of seasonal and complementary items. UNHCR planning to allocate almost 50,000 kits as contingency planning for emergency response in 2022.
This project aims to assist some 2.4 million individuals with emergency NFIs kits and seasonal NFI kits (winter/summer items). The response will be implemented from Turkey and from inside Syria. The overall project targets for two types of assistance package, however a portion of beneficiaries will receive both types of kits, depending on their needs and the season in which assistance is provided. NFI kits will be provided to IDPs, returnee families and host communities, in line with vulnerability criteria. A standard winter kit will be provided as one-time distribution in addition to the NFIs.

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 5% in 2022 to 4.91 million. Unaffordability is the main driver of need, with 67% of the population reporting that they are unable to afford NFIs available in the marketplace.Around 70% of the overall population reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs. Consequently, basic items are further out of reach as families’ purchasing power is diminishing over time. The number of people in need of NFI support continues to increase in 2022 and is expected to continue to grow if a further worsening of the economic situation continues.UNHCR’s NFI response will focus on newly displaced populations as identified by the interagency assessments, areas hosting high number of new returns, as well as persons of concern in camps and settlements.UNHCR will give special attention to the needs in North-east Syria in and out of the camp situation as it is estimated that 300,000 Ind will be supported with NFI and winterization kits during 2022 in NE Syria. UNHCR also is planning to support NW Syria IA interventions with almost 10,000 NFI kits.According to 2022 Syria HNO, there will be slight increase in the need for NFI items during 2023. So, UNHCR is planning to keep on the same level of support from NFI perspective.

5 - Implementation of activities

Resources permit, UNHCR plans for the provision of regular and seasonal core relief items for over 450,000 households from inside Syria including a contingency stock that can cover the need of 35,000 HH will be maintained for emerging needs that may arise inside Syria. UNHCR also plans for the provision of CRI items to cover 35,000 Household through cross-border modality. A core relief items kit includes blankets, mattresses, sleeping mats, plastic sheets, jerry cans, plastic bucket, and solar lamps amongst others. Vulnerable groups such as unaccompanied minors, women and elders, female headed household physically or mentally challenged, will be prioritized. Identification of the beneficiaries is conducted by UNHCR Field Offices in close coordination and reference from authorities and sector partners to minimize gaps and avoid duplication. Partners are required to submit regular performance reports. Accordingly, UNHCR will conduct regular PDMs to ensure proper monitoring and evaluation of NFI distribution. Furthermore, feedback will be obtained from POCs through compliant boxes and while monitoring distribution sites. UNHCR will also provide regular detailed 4Ws progress reports. In 2022, UNHCR will advocate to monetize assistance to afford POCs more flexibility in determining their personal requirements. New CBI SOPs are being developed for pilot projects planned in Aleppo and Tartous, for potential expansion in 2023.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply the sector' criteria in the selection process. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism. Risk 3: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Mitigating measures: all staff members are required to sign and comply with the Code of Conduct, and also ensure procedures are in place to report perpetrators.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closelywith other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA networkto ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regardto the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition tomandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment hasbecome mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet thestandard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership withUNHCR in the following year.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potentialloss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access tobeneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/thirdparty monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Supportpartners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staffon monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms.Risk 2: Lost and damage of items in warehouse and during distribution. Mitigatingmeasures: Ensure timely coordination to minimize the period of items stored in partnerwarehouse. Training and monitoring on warehouse management of partners Strengthenpartner distribution management through SOPs and trainings Risk3: Interference withbeneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial orreputational risks.
