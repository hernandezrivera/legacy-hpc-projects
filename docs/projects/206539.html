Project Summary

Protracted crises in South Sudan, including climactic shocks and continued conflict, have resulted in 9.4 million people projected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023. On top of these multilayered shock the Sudan crisis has adversely impacted South Sudan with more that 320,000 people having crossed the countries northern borders, predominantly from Renk. As a result of the shocks, such as flooding, inter-communal violence (ICV) and ongoing armed clashes, the majority of the population are facing increased food insecurity, lack of access to basic services, and protection risks and mass displacement. As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices, lack of shelter and survival means, will negatively impact the most vulnerable South Sudanese.
Since 2006, Solidarites International has become a key humanitarian actor in South Sudan, implementing emergency lifesaving responses across the country and providing integrated WASH services to crisis-affected populations in areas of static presence in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Western Bahr el Ghazal. Since 2012 SI has played a key role in the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) strategy in South Sudan, implementing multi-sectoral assessments and delivering emergency responses to crisis-affected populations all over the country.
In 2024, SI will continue to provide emergency live-saving WASH and Shelter responses across the South Sudan to restore basic access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation while ensuring equitable access, safety, and accountability to supported populations. Through its Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism team, SI will deploy to emergency-affected locations to provide first phase and second phase responses including distribution of household water treatment items and soap, emergency provision of safe water, shelter kits, and rapid repair of emergency-affected infrastructure.
At the same time, SI will expand its presence in Upper Nile, and Unity to provide basic integrated WASH, and Shelter services that target the most vulnerable, including those suffering malnutrition, food insecurity and protection threats (including GBV survivors). Static programs will focus on providing community-based solutions for sustainable WASH services that ensure that improvements in access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene are durable and can be maintained independently by communities. Engagement and joint

programming with Protection and Nutrition actors will ensure that WASH services can be accessed by the most vulnerable and will have a tangible impact on reducing overall priority humanitarian needs. The increased integration of protection, inclusion, and conflict sensitivity analysis in all programming will ensure that WASH services and resources can be safely accessed by all in the community. Greater engagement of communities and local markets will support the design of long term and flood resistant WASH infrastructure and supply chains to ensure that interventions produce durable results for communities.

1. Needs

Access to basic services, including safe water, hygiene items, and sanitation is extremely limited in many parts of the country, especially in Upper Nile and Unity states. In many areas, underground water presence is limited, leaving to the communities no alternative apart from surface water. In other areas with access to boreholes, rehabilitations typically have limited sustainability due to the impacts of flooding, emergency nature of repairs, and lack of maintenance. Economic vulnerability of communities and lack of functional supply chains prevents full handover of both surface water and groundwater supply infrastructure to communities. Sanitation facilities are very rare, with the majority of the population practicing open defecation. Rates of good personal hygiene practices are low, and low access to soap contributes to high diarrheic diseases. In Fashoda, Manyo, Melut, Baliet, Panyikang, Canal-Pigi, Fangak and parts of Malakal Counties, between 60% - 100% of the population report relying on surface water as their main source. In Fashoda, 80% of the population report relying on surface water, and safe water is primarily provided through humanitarian organizations. In terms of sanitation, almost no households use latrines in Fashoda. In Manyo and Canal-Pigi, virtually all of the population rely on surface water. In recent SI assessment in August 2023, majority of respondents indicated that both in Upper Nile and Unity state open defecation has been a common practices, and morbidity from water borne diseases is common.There were also protection concerns about accessing the few latrines there were because of no gender segregation. As in other parts of South Sudan, the majority of the communities use open defecation and lack of money and access to market prevent most households to have soap. In all the aforementioned counties, flooding since 2019 has resulted in displacement of households and reduced access to basic WASH services as well as increased protection risks. Water points and sanitation infrastructure have been submerged, contaminated, and damaged. Assessments in Fashoda, Manyo, and Melut found that SWAT systems had been submerged by flooding. Even in areas with normally better water supply, flooding and displacement have had significant effects. Flooding is seasonal in South Sudan and will further impact already affected-populations, especcially in Unity State (Bentiu, Rubkona, Leer etc) flooding has cause huge loss in land for farming and livestock. On top of the needs identified, major clashes have been reported in SI area of intervention in 2022 and 2023 (namely Fashoda and Panyikang/Malakal, Tunga, Aburoc counties), with around 40,000 people displaced by the clashes. WASH and food security related needs are huge, as newly displaced people are completely lacking of access to basic wash services (i.e access to safe water and ensuring the environment is free of faeces). This lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene and continuing shocks has increased the risk to vulnerable populations of water-borne diseases, in turn influencing rates of malnutrition (affecting especially children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women). The lack of access to WASH services, in particularly the distances to water points increase the risk of GBV for women and girls, as well as increasing their burden of work. People with specific vulnerabilities, such as People with Disability, also face many additional challenges to accessing WASH services.

2. Project activities

SI’s 2023 strategy will focus on 1)Short-term emergency response to reduce mortality and morbidity for the most vulnerable population in priority and WSC level 4&5 locations and in IPC4+ counties through static and emergency responses (corresponding to WASH objectives CO1,CO2.1,CO2.2) 2)Ensuring medium term safe, equitable and dignified access to essential WASH services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of WSC 4&5; (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2.1,CO2.2) 3)Identify and implement long term solutions for sustainable community-based WASH services through piloting innovative solutions and engagement with communities and local markets, and increased flood resilience of infrastructure (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2.1,CO2.2,CO3) -Distribution of essential WASH NFIs to children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) suffering from malnutrition through referral from nutrition centres -HP trainings at nutrition and health facilities -assess the WASH conditions at nutrition facility level and support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water in the facility to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission -Distribution of MHM kits following referral from Protection services -Distribution of hygiene items and essential hygiene messages to vulnerable households affected by emergencies. -Using CVA modalities where possible. -operation and maintenance of water supply infrastructure in SI areas of operation -Improved rehabilitation and upgrading of SWATs and water points to reduce operational costs and maintenance - Rehabilitation/Construction of communal latrines for densely populated areas with high risk of disease outbreak -Supported construction of household latrines for the most vulnerable households in communities. -Engage with vulnerable members of communities (e.g. women, girls, people with disabilities) through joint activities with protection partners to implement WASH infrastructure solutions that increase safe access. -Community hygiene promotion campaigns -improve access to safe water in communities with high GAM prevalence -Pilot alternative means of localized and sustainable safe water supply with communities that provide improvements in access to safe water, e.g. adapted shallow wells, sand filters, and non-chemical household water treatment. -Long-term capacity building for WMCs with community engagement on contributions to WASH service management-construction of community-led household and community latrines using only local materials -Assessment of and support for local markets to supply WASH items and materials for activities. Use of local labour and contractors in infrastructural works -Rehabilitate and construct water points with flood resilient designs -Incorporate flood resilient designs for all construction of communal and institutional latrines. WASH safety audits and AAP consultations will be conducted before all interventions. Shelter-Provision of emergency Shelter support for newly displaced individuals through the distribution of Shelter kits. Modality: Cash for Shelter would be preferred where markets are strong and security allows. Alternatively, we would opt for shelter kits distribution (hybrid approach).While specific high priority counties have been identified by SI as key locations for intervention, SI will have the flexibility to respond to emergencies in other locations through the ERRM team.
