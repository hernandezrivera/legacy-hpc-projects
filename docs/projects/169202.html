Project Summary

IOM will provide health services to migrants and vulnerable host communities in areas affected by the explosions. IOM will prioritise the most vulnerable migrants and host communities, in coordination with other health actors, and complementing other IOM services to migrants. Migrants will be provided with health information, and access to essential supplies such as personal protective equipment, hygiene, and dignity kits. IOM will support access to testing and care of people living with HIV and TB though the National AIDS programme and National Tuberculous programme.
The project will also support and compliment on-going rapid needs assessments (Multi-sectorial Needs Assessment_ MSNA) in coordiantion through the Assessment and Analysis Group, as well as provide information on the impact of the Beirut Port Blast on migrant worker populations through [DTM].

1 - Needs

On 4 August 2020, a massive explosion at the Port of Beirt (POB) destroyed much of the port and severely damaged the dense surrounding residential and commercial areas in Beirut Lebanon. The Beirut Port explosions killed 178 persons, with more than 6,000 injured. In terms of health infrastructure, six major hospitals and 20 clinics sustained partial or heavy structural damage. A preliminary rapid assessment within a 15-kilometre radius of the explosions, has revealed that, out of 55 medical facilities, only half are fully operational and around 40 per cent have suffered moderate to serious damage and need rehabilitation. Healthcare services and resources at all levels - tertiary, secondary, primary and emergency medical services – have been significantly impacted. As of 20 August 2020, Hospitals are still overwhelmed. There are a record number of COVID-19 cases per day, and a high numbers of those affected are health workers. There is an increasingly high risk of exposure for health workers, and patients due to overcrowding of emergency departments; shortages of personal protection equipment (PPE); exacerbated risks of accelerated community transmission; increased population vulnerability coupled with poor compliance to preventive measures. There is also a significantly reduced ability of health care service availability and service provision. It is estimated that there are approximately 250,000 migrant workers in Lebanon, the majority being women from Ethiopia, Kenya and the Philippines. As initial rapid assessment conducted by IOM indicated preliminary figures of 24,600 migrant workers that had been directly affected by the blast. IOM’s emergency response is therefore prioritizing support to migrant workers, while also addressing the immediate and recovery needs of vulnerable groups inclusive of refugees and Lebanese affected populations. According to Human Rights Watch, the COVID-19 pandemic, along with August’s explosion have caused hundreds of employers to abandon domestic workers outside their embassies - often without money, passports or return-tickets home, and lacking access to essential services, such as health care, among many others. In addition, data released daily from the Lebanese Emergency Operations Center indicates that COVID-19 daily cases continue to rise at alarming rates, and migrants currently account for 13% of reported COVID-19 cases. With the recent explosion also displacing hundreds of thousands, migrants along with the Lebanese community find themselves living in shared temporary, and often overcrowded, accommodation spaces, further exacerbating the situation and likelihood of infection. IOM Lebanon’s recent Migrant Worker Vulnerability Baseline Assessment Report (May-July 2020), was conducted as even prior to the explosion; migrant workers in Beirut have tended to be an overlooked population group, and it found that prior to the blast, migrants had limited information about, and access to, healthcare facilities in Beirut. According to the assessment, 14 per cent of surveyed migrant workers reported having existing medical conditions while 26 per cent were unwilling to seek healthcare services in medical emergencies, and only 23 per cent of females, and 31 per cent of males were aware of which clinics/hospitals they could attain services. This illustrates that there are many obstacles in accessing health care at all levels, including service fees, limited information and stigmatization. These will have only been further compounded by the Port Explosion.

2 - Activities

ORIGINAL PROJECT PLAN: Support damaged primary health care centres (PHCC), infrastructure rehabilitation / reconstruction, equipment, medical consumables and staff training. IOM will work in close coordination with MoPH, Health Sector partners and the and PHCC working group to identify PHCCs for support including coordinating on assessments. IOM has not identified PHCCs. IOM will prioritise PHCCs in areas of large numbers of vulnerable host , refugee and migrant workers. Distribute personal protective equipment and hygiene and dignity supplies at PHCs. Initial procurement of PPE supplies to support already selected PHCCs is ongoing. Subsequent procurement will be coordinated within the UN system Support testing and care of people living with HIV and TB through the NAP and NTP, including rehabilitation of offices, basic equipment, procurement of stock, and outreach activities. Support 2 community events-based surveillance targeting migrant and refuge dense areas, including training and equipment. Support 1 quarantine and isolation space, including rehabilitation and restructuring, furniture, equipment, PPEs and food and other consumables. Support COVID-19 testing (2000), including paying for testing fee and transportation costs. Support safe return of migrant workers. Data collection including mobility tracking, thematic assessments on migrant workers needs in disaster affected areas by IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix.
