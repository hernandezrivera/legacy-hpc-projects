Project Summary

In 2013 South Sudan descended into a civil war that displaced more than 4 million people into the neighboring countries, Uganda, Kenya Sudan, DRC, and Ethiopia. This political instability affected health service delivery and destroyed health infrastructure. This has led to millions of South Sudanese lacking life-saving health services increasing maternal mortality rates and child mortality rates to unmatchable figures around the globe.
Communicable disease burden is a challenge whereby malaria, measles, acute watery diarrhea, and others continue to ravage the country causing untold suffering to vulnerable and affected populations in the country. Access to health facilities and medical supplies is also a big challenge with populations having to walk more than 7 hours in some cases to access health facilities that also lack essential medical supplies and qualified middle health cadres.
6.37 million South Sudanese citizens are seriously food insecure which is more than 52.6% of the total population and thus becoming even more vulnerable to diseases (IPC OCT 2020). The overall GAM rate in the country is more than the 15% WHO emergency threshold this also increasing vulnerability due to high rates of malnutrition.
The country has been hit by severe flooding which has led to the destruction of health facilities loss of medical supplies and displacement of populations. The floods have affected almost half of the counties in the country displacing almost 1million people in 33 Counties with 11 counties in Jonglei severely hit by the floods.
Immunization services are very poor in the country and also utilization of maternal-child health services is wanting. Cold chain availability and functionality low and transmission of infections prevented by immunization like measles remain very high. In 2019, there were more than 17 counties that had confirmed measles outbreaks and in 2020 the country had 8 counties with measles outbreaks as reported in IDSR reports in week 52 as shared by the health cluster.
Despite efforts by health partners and the ministry of health to improve health service delivery in the country only less than 50% of the population are able to access health services with SARA report in 2019 showing that 44% of the populations in South Sudan are able to access health services. This clearly shows the need to improve health service delivery through static health facilities and also mobile clinics.
Medicair will ensure that in line with the Health Cluster strategic objectives improve access to health services to the vulnerable and affected populations in Akobo, Ayod, Nyirol, Pibor, Manyo, Panyikang, and Maiwut Counties that have been hard hit by floods. Medicair will deploy 4 mobile health teams in Akobo, Ayod, Nyirol, and Panyikang counties to improve the accessibility of health services to the populations affected by floods. Medicair will have 3 PHCUs in Pibor, Manyo, and Maiwut Counties.
Medicair will offer consultations and management of common illnesses like malaria with ACT, diarrhea with Zinc for children U5. Deliveries will be attended by skilled birth attendance to ensure reduced maternal mortality rates. Immunization services will reduce vaccine-preventable infections, health education especially in the context of COVID 19 will be very key to ensure that key prevention messages of COVID 19 are passed to the community.
Medicair will train health workers on IPC, IDSR, integrated WASH to ensure that the health workers have the knowledge to attend to health emergencies.
Medicair will screen children for malnutrition and give necessary management and refer them to nutrition centers for further management.
The project will target 8,035 ( 3937 male and 4098 female) with 1205 people with disability beneficiaries in Manyo, Maiwut, Nyirol, Panyikang, Akobo, Ayod, Pibor Counties. this will be in Coordination with other partners and County Health Departments.

1 - Needs

Jonglei and Upper Nile states have experienced on and of, the worst floods ever witnessed in recent years where populations have been devastated by floods, lost property, lost livelihoods, and crops and animals were destroyed by the floods. Flood snapshot 4 showed that by end of October 850000 people had been affected by the floods and the majority were in these two states (OCHA flood Snapshot issue 4).The floods have not been kind to health infrastructure and health facilities have therefore been destroyed medical supplies taken by floodwaters and health workers displaced by the raging floods. The floods have also made it very difficult for populations to access health facilities.COVID 19 pandemic has challenged the already weakened health systems in the country. The health facilities don’t have the capacity to fully manage this viral infection, therefore, leaving the country and populations in dire need of health care.Health facility functionality bulleting shows devastating results in the Upper Nile and Jonglei states, the target counties for this project have high functional facilities with more than 50% of the facilities in Uror County functional and very low immunization coverage with most of the counties having EPI coverage of less than 40% with Nasir the worst with 15% vaccination coverage. Pibor County has struggled to manage measles outbreaks and this can be attributed to low immunization coverage with the county having concurrent measles outbreak in 2019 and 2020(IDSR reports, week 52/53 2020)The country is experiencing high levels of food insecurity and all the counties are in IPC phase 4 with Pibor County in IPC phase 5. (IPC analysis Oct 2020) This calls for urgent humanitarian assistance to avert loss of life through disease due to vulnerability increased by food insecurity.Protection threats, Lack of WASH facilities, and High GAM rates in the three counties compound the already precarious health situation, and therefore integrated health service delivery will be key in reducing maternal mortality rates. This will ensure, protection needs, nutrition needs, WASH interventions in health facilities, and provision of quality health services to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates in all the target counties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Posters printed and showing that Medicair is non, and does not entertain sexual exploitation and abuse and if possible they will be printed in the local language. Awareness of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) will also be created during the project implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Medicair will engage local specialists to get feedback from Beneficiaries with special needs. local community leaders ad representatives of the people with disabilities will be engaged to give feedback on project implemtation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through Constant engagement with the beneficiaries, Medicair will ensure that the beneficiaries aware of the available feedback and complaint mechanisms and this will be done even during project design implementation and closure. This will be done through focused group discussions with the beneficiaries and also having suggestion boxes in the facilities to support health service delivery.Medicair will dedicate a toll free line that will support the beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints as well as formation of the beneficiary accountability committees.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All the feedback from the beneficiaries will be taken very positively and will inform change in project design and implementation to cater for the needs of the vulnerable populations.
