Project Summary

PUI, jointly with Aquassistance, carried out a WaSH, Health and Nutrition assessment in July and August 2018. As a result, PUI developed an integrated approach in these three sectors that aimes at reducing public health risks. Thus, the intervention proposed is integrated in this approach, with the objective to improve access to quality WaSH infrastructures for both Rohingya population and host communities. More specifically, PUI teams will evaluate WaSH infrastructures, and ensure the maintenance, repair and improvement of those that require it. PUI will also built a rapid treatment system for fecal sludge. This intervention also include information, training and sensitization sessions for the community as well as good monitoring and coordination mechanisms, in order to ensure a relevant and coordinated response, based on the needs.

Needs

Through the 2019 Joint Response Plan, over 1.2 million people are identified in need of humanitarian assistance in Bangladesh from both Rohingya refugees and members of host communities. In July 2018, Première Urgence Internationale and Aquassistance carried out a WaSH, Health and Nutrition assessment in both Teknaf and Ukhia Upazilas. The results showed that, 12 months after the beginning of the crisis, the acute emergency phase is moving to a more stable emergency phase but challenges are still substantial. In particular, WaSH, Nutrition and Health needs are major priorities. The assessment revealed the inadequacy and insufficiency of WaSH facilities. The lack of access to drinking water, the epidemic risks resulting from poor WaSH infrastructures and the density of housing contribute to an increased health risk that is already particularly high in the area . Similarly, the poor quality of water represents a risk to public health. For example, during the evaluation, bacteriological analyses of 18 water sources in both Kutupalong and Teknaf camps showed that 100% of the samples were positive for the presence of fecal coliforms and eight specifically in E. Coli.Regarding access and quality aspects of sanitation facilities, the majority of households have problems accessing latrines and spaces for showers and personal hygiene. In this regard, many latrines require repairs and maintenance activities. As reported by all WASH actors met in the Teknaf region, more than 80% of latrines need pit emptying operations. In addition, during the rainy season, access paths to these structures suffer from severe erosion damage or are overtaken by water. This factor has a considerable negative impact on the most vulnerable populations in terms of physical access to water and sanitation facilities. Gender issues are quite problematic regarding WaSH facilities: latrines are not clearly enough separated between men and women. There is also a lack of basic measures regarding Protection. Locations are not easily accessible for women and girls given gendered mobility restrictions and regarding safety issues that they face. For example, in 57% of surveyed households, women and girls feel unsafe using latrines at night (REACH).Finally, during the assessment, the community highlighted that hygiene habits are poor, with open defecation practices still common for example. Moreover, summary findings of the SMART survey conducted by Action Contre la Faim in the Teknaf region in February and March 2018 showed that only 5.1% of respondents wash their hands before cooking, 4.0% before feeding children and 2.7% before eating food which contributes significantly to the transmission of acute watery diarrhea.Health, Nutrition, and WaSH are interconnected needs and should be approached together. Indeed, insufficient quantity and poor quality of the water as well as inadequacy and insufficiency of WaSH facilities led to health risks and diseases and is also a determinant of acute malnutrition. Health and Nutrition are also cross cutting issues that can impact hygiene practices at households level: if most of the population receives hygiene kits regularly, many of the refugees sell the products to pay rent and buy food and medicine instead. Thus, a comprehensive approach on Health, Nutrition and WaSH helps to reduce public health risks and to act on the main drivers that reduce the morbidity and mortality of the most vulnerable people. On a community basis, beneficiaries will be involved in all phases of the project in order to ensure their adherence and involvement throughout the project for a sustainable impact.

Activities or outputs

Output1: Population affected by the crises benefit from an improved access to qualitative water and sanitation installations, as per humanitarian WASH standards 1.1. Structural evaluation of WaSH infrastructuresPUI teams will systematically evaluate the facilities, including communal latrines, bathing cubicles, water supply points, drainage systems as well as incinerators and waste pits, in coordination with WASH focal point and SMS agency to determine the structures that require urgently an appropriate maintenance intervention. 1.2. Maintenance, repair and improvement of WaSH infrastructuresOn the basis of continuous evaluations carried out by the technical teams, facilities that are non-functional or pose a health risk will be repaired, maintained or decommissioned and replaced. 1.3. Construction of a rapid treatment system for fecal sludgeA team will be trained to carry out latrine emptying operations in a way that respect the environment and the population. This activity includes the disposal of fecal sludge in suitable treatment sites already in operation and/or the construction of a rapid fecal sludge treatment system according to the priorities identified by the sector and avaibility of plots in the Camps for this purpose.1.4. Set up proper WIN facilities in primary health care centreWithin PUI PHCC, adequate hygiene and sanitation conditions to contribute to the proper care of patients will be ensured. A water supply and treatment system will be set up in each facility as well as rainwater harvesting for structures that do not have one. The water collected will then be treated and made drinkable for consumption by patients and for use in healthcare. Cleaning products and hygiene equipment will be provided. Output 2: Population affected by the crisis benefit from information, follow up and sanitation good-practices training2.1. Maintenance training for manual pumps and hydraulic equipment for local volunteersVolunteers with required technical skills will be trained and equipped with appropriate tools for manual water pumps reparation. 2.2 Awareness campaigns on good sanitation practices A team of health and hygiene promoters will be trained and equipped with IEC tools to raise awareness of good health practices. Hygiene promotion and water disease prevention sessions will be implemented. 2.3. Information campaigns on available health and sanitation services and referralsCommunity teams will inform people about the health and nutrition services offered in order to change behaviour in seeking care and encourage preventive care and limit delays in caring for patients. For this purpose, teams will be regularly in contact with health structures medical staffs. Output 3: Safety and dignity of the population are improved through a better access for people with specific needs and promoted by proper monitoring and evaluation mechanisms 3.1. Improve access to the WaSH facilities for people with specific needsPUI will promote and develop means to improve safety and privacy for PWSN taking into account gender aspects. In coordination with the SMS Agency and the camp strategy, equipment such as streetlights will be installed.3.2. Setting-up complaints and feedback mechanismsCFM desks will be set-up in order to receive complaints from community members regarding PUI WaSH activities. Complaints will be properly addressed, either on-site when possible or referred to the SMS Agency or the appropriate partner. 3.3. Regular assessments and monitoring of the activitiesThe quality of the response and of the activities provided by PUI will be regularly assessed through individual field visits, satisfaction surveys or trainings. 3.4. Active participation to WaSH coordination mechanisms PUI teams will participate actively to the sectoral coordination meetings and will regularly share monitoring and evaluation data with the partners to improve collective knowledge and to ensure a coordinated response.
