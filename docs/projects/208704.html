Project Summary

IOM will expand its Health and Nutritional programme to a more systematic and comprehensive approach with nutrition community mobilization volunteers and utilizing the rapid response mechanism. Access to quality emergency health care and nutritional services remains a challenge, especially for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and migrants at reception centers and arrival camps. IOM intends to enhance quality access to comprehensive services through the integration of emergency mobile health care and nutritional services at the deep field locations with other health services at outposts. IOM will also continue working with health partners in outbreak prevention and response efforts, capacity building of health, nutrition, health information management, and emergency referral and support systems. As part of the health promotional and preventive activities, IOM will partner with UNICEF and local NGOs to implement health outreach through health promoters and community volunteers. Under this action, IOM will also strengthen its collaboration with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and will continue to support the State Ministries of Health.
IOM will partner with the Borno State Ministry of Health and Borno State Primary Health Care Development Agency in providing health needs in the hard-to-reach areas. IOM will work with the agencies to develop a response strategy primarily targeting the populations on the move, persons living with disabilities, and refugees including vaccination against vaccine-preventable diseases among these vulnerable populations.
In coordination with the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), the Federal Ministry of Health and related departments, World Health Organization (WHO), and the Health sector in Maiduguri, IOM will leverage on its health capacity to contribute to the government’s effort to respond to epidemics.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, which has contributed to the destruction of health facilities and an incapacitated healthcare sector. More than 42 per cent of healthcare facilities in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states are non-functional due to the conflict. Additionally, the quality of care is unstable due to the volatile security situation and lack of standardized health care packages. An increase in quality service provides enhanced technical capacity for the healthcare providers, an emergency health response team and the establishment of a nutrition rapid response mechanism are thus noted as critical issues by the agencies responding to the crisis. Furthermore, gaps in referral services have been strongly tagged by Health Sector partners currently providing the responses at reception centers, camps and primary healthcare centers (PHC) in host communities, as a key need specifically the inability of patients to receive critically needed health care or timely access to emergency care in hospitals. Health facilities were targeted during the conflict and as a result, many health facilities across BAY states are still non-functional or partially functional while those that are functioning do so with the minimum capacity to deliver standard health care packages. In hard-to-reach areas communities face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons, including lack of medical staff, unavailability of essential emergency drugs and supplies, security barriers, food transportation issues, malnutrition and damaged/destroyed health facilities. Critical gaps also remain in functional primary and secondary health services. Other common challenges in accessing health services, especially for persons with various disabilities, are related to high direct and indirect costs associated with healthcare, including the price of quality medicines. The disruption in the supply chain of medicines and medical supplies has compounded the problem. The dire living conditions of these IDPs together with the often-poor water and sanitation conditions contribute to increased health risks and higher vulnerability to the spread of outbreak-prone communicable diseases, such as cholera. IDP returnees in newly accessible areas or IDPs living in “satellite camps” have reportedly limited or even no access to health care services. There is an urgent need to expand the provision of integrated basic PHC services through mobile emergency health and nutrition services to prevent further deterioration of the health status of affected populations and to reach the most vulnerable and disabled persons. Health services also need to be made available and accessible at the different levels of care, supported by a well-functioning referral mechanism, at and between the community, primary and secondary/higher care levels. While the Ministry of Health has made efforts in integrating emergency health services at the PHC, there is a need to further this approach to ensure increased access to emergency health and nutrition services.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Set up 3 Mobile Health Teams at the selected locations that will provide an integrated and standardized primary and preventive healthcare service package, including referrals. Activity 2: Identify and train local community health volunteers and influencers on health promotion and community-based health interventions, including infection, prevention and control measures.Activity 3: Support the national disease surveillance system and contribute to the control of disease outbreaks and other public health events through the training of healthcare workers on epidemic reporting pathways and IDSR. Activity 4: Refurbishment and equipping of 9 selected public health facilities to resume their function and to provide integrated primary health services. Activity 5: Strengthen referral and provision of specialized Health services in collaboration with the state’s tertiary and secondary health facilities to vulnerable persons living with disabilities. Activity 6: Strengthen emergency health care services provision at the deep field locations through capacity-building activities in close coordination with the State Ministry of Health Age and gender Targets- The emergency response activity and vaccination target all age groups and gender diversity, particularly pregnant and lactating women (PLW) among the vulnerable population.- Community health promotion activities will target young adults and nursing mothers (17 – 49yrs) in the IDP camps and host communities.- Disability inclusion support will target mostly children (5 - 14 years) and older persons across all genders among the vulnerable groups.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

Through the provision of life-saving healthcare services, IOM aims to respond to the emergency health needs of IDP, returnees, and vulnerable population, thereby reducing morbidity and mortality in these populations. IOM will also complement these efforts with building the capacities of local healthcare workers and ensuring a community-based approach to the services provided to the target populations, reinforcing target populations and authorities’ ownership of Health programs. Coordination with the government, donors, national and international NGOs as well as UN agencies engaged in interventions across the nexus will ensure the continuity of Health programs and that individual efforts are contributing towards collective outcomes. The key actors are the state Ministry of Health and Primary Health Development Agency. Other health partners implementing in the filed (UN, INGO, NGO), and the interaction will be through the health sector, Nutrition sector, and the Public Health Emergency Operation Center meetings. Enhancing free access to basic health care services through: a) Deployment of dedicated emergency mobile health teams for the provision of a range of healthcare services. This will ensure greater access to health services by all groups of the affected populations b) Increased emphasis on a community-based approach and outreach to bring services to the affected populations c) Improved coordination across humanitarian and development actors, as well as donors, at different levels This will strengthen tjhe overall health response and avoid the duplication of activities.d) Adapted package of health services delivery platform with a focus on both access and quality, e) Capacity building of healthcare workers, with a strong focus on LGA’s health management teams f) g) Provision of integrated or complementary support between humanitarian partners for unstable areas and development investments in more stable parts, including a relatively stable location for referral capacity within the LGAs or the nearest stable LGAs (clustering LGAs for hospital referral purposes)
