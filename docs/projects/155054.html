Project Summary

This project has been designed as part of the shelter/NFI interventions of Christian Aid. Based on the multiple needs assessments conducted by shelter/NFI for both camp and host community shows the larger needs on transitional and mid-term sheltering in terms of Sphere minimum standards. These assessments also exposed the priorities of monsoon preparedness & DRR as well as highlighted very specific priorities to take measures to reduce environmental impacts both for camp and host communities.
This project, however, complying with all four sector objectives for shelter/NFI sector targeting 13,500 households directly in the camp and host community with 67,500 individuals or family members of these total targeted households. Therefore, this intervention has planned, firstly, to provide lifesaving emergency shelter/NFI support to the affected households by natural disasters or other shocks considering the mid-term shelter needs and distribution of stockpiled emergency shelter kits; This intervention, secondly, will also improve the living conditions contributing to reduced suffering, disaster risk reduction, and enhanced protection, dignity, and safety of the targeted families; thirdly, this intervention has considered both camp and host community based on their practical shelter needs to ensure inclusion or to improve social cohesion and enhance resilience for the community; and finally, planned to fit alternative sustainable solutions to reduce impact on the environment.
The major focus of this intervention will provide mid-term shelter for 1,000 camp-based households. Also, will cover - 6,000 households with improved lighting solution targeting both camp and host community; and 6,000 households with individual kitchen support i.e. LPG cylinders, gas stoves and periodic refills for the targeted families as part of social cohesion creation and reduction of environmental impacts. Lastly, as part of monsoon and cyclone preparedness will distribute 5,000 in-kind contingency stockpiling of emergency shelter/NFI kit (as per sector standard) for the camp households at risks.

Needs

Camps and sites remain congested seriously impacting physical and psychological well-being, especially of children, women, and persons with disabilities. The round 12 NPM report clearly indicates lower allocation of living spaces per person than Sphere standard i.e. an average area of 18.76 sqm per person against the recommended standard of 45 sqm/person and most shelters do not adhere a living space of 3.5 sqm/person. Inadequate lighting in shelters & public spaces was reported as a top safety concern followed by danger/violence when collecting firewood. Female key informants highlighted that the recent monsoon season had weakened the shelters. Bamboo has also deteriorated due to beetle infestation, resulting in a situation in which shelters can easily be broken into and emphasized the discomfort in existing shelters due to humidity, especially during the hottest portions of the day. The families in these camps are still in critical need of cooking fuel. Furthermore, Shelter/NFI Sector triggered shelter survey in Jul-Aug of 2018 also aligned with the findings of NPM round 12. The major concerns were related to the strength of shelters, quality of materials, lack of private bathing & cooking spaces, lack of ventilation & privacy and DRR training for protection against adverse weather conditions. This technical assessment found over 78% of HHs had to purchase additional bamboo, tarpaulin & ropes; 62% of shelters had no ventilation and 40% of shelters had constructed individual makeshift bathing facility inside the shelter or connected to it. Also, provision of bamboo treatment has remained as a major concern to prolong the lifespan and durability of bamboo utilized for shelters, reducing the need to source immature bamboo in future years. As per the relocation dashboard of site management sector, 871 HHs need to be relocated in camp 14 & 15 due to landslide risk. Emergency Shelter/NFI for Monsoon and Cyclone preparedness and response remains as a high priority as this certain geographic area has witnessed devastating cyclones over the past decades. Nearly half of the Shelters in Camp 14 and 15 are situated in mid hill to hilltop area, which is at risk to high wind. Shelter/NFI Sector partners have initiated host community needs assessments in 2018. Initial feedback from partners clearly indicated also the need for shelter, NFI, and DRR assistance to increase the host communities’ resilience against adverse weather conditions.
