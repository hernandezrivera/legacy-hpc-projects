Project Summary

The objective of this project is aimed at improving access to a safe, inclusive, protected and quality education system for children of school going age. The project will facilitate access to education for vulnerable children to enable them to obtain the bases for integration into the formal education system, while supporting those out of school acquire foundational skills and knowledge required for learning and integration into formal learning opportunities.
The project will target the most vulnerable children and adolescents aged between six (6) to eighteen (18) years of age. The focus will mainly be on IDPs, returnees and affected host communities, paying more attention to children from hard-to-reach areas, including those with disabilities. A total of 12,500 children of school going age will be targeted. Education response will be based on identified education needs across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states and will be designed around major outcomes:
• Increased Access to education for emergency-affected girls and boys
• Increased continuity of education for emergency-affected girls and boys
• Learning environments are child-friendly, equitable, and inclusive
• Improved quality of learning for emergency-affected girls and boys
Additionally, children who dropped out of school will be supported to re-enroll back to school. The project will aim at addressing multiple social, cultural, economic, gender, status and geographical barriers that limit children from enjoying their right to education across the target states. Teaching and learning materials will be provided and teachers will be equipped have sufficient knowledge and skills to deliver quality education. Psychosocial support will also be integrated into the teacher training packages to equip them with skills to integrate PSS into learning activities as this is crucial so social and emotional wellbeing of learners. Learning facilities will be supported to be inclusive, disability- friendly, and gender sensitive through teacher training, PSS integration, and establishment of referral pathways for specialized services beyond the scope of the project. To ensure a consistent and comprehensive response, the project will be developed through the prisms of gender and conflict mitigation, which are two concomitant perspectives to the dissemination of the principles of protection in the project. Protection principles will be mainstreamed across the intervention by prioritizing safety and dignity as well as ensuring meaningful access, participation, and empowerment. To achieve this, Salient will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Borno and Adamawa State, to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The projects result will be achieved through activity packages aimed at strengthening the capacity of education activities at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 indicated that 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022. Of these, 2.2M are internally displaced; 1.5M are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors. The Education Sector Response Plan 2023 documents a high number of People in Need, highlighting high number of IDPs and returnees in some LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states. The HNO 2022 further indicates that 1.9 people are in need of education across the states. The conflict and displacement has negatively affected the social structures and the economy of the region. Boko Haram opposes “Western” values, particularly the formal and secular education provided by the government and their factions regularly target education, destroying or damaging schools, threatening and killing teachers and abducting students for ransom and publicity. The impact of this crisis on the education system has been severe, leaving generations of children without opportunities to learn and even more vulnerable. According to the EiE Working group strategy 2021-2023, the number of children with chronic needs in education remains high across the BAY states and the crisis has put additional pressure on an education system that was already fragile. Estimates indicate that nearly 60% of primary school-age children are not attending school, with girls disproportionally affected. 41.8 % and 42.2% of primary school age children are not attending school in Adamawa and Yobe states respectively, compared to 27% at national level. Children and teachers in the BAY states have also a continuing need of psychosocial support to cope with the negative effects of the conflict. Poverty remains the greatest barrier to access education, with parents being unable to afford education fees and levies. The poor quality and lack of infrastructure such as classrooms, school furniture and WASH facilities, are also critical barriers. The insufficient number of qualified teachers and lack of teaching and learning materials have also contributed to poor attendance, retention and learning outcomes of learners in schools and learning centres. Salient has been at the forefront in delivery of Education alongside other actors in BAY states since 2021. However, glaring gaps remain prevalent which justify the need for continued support, expansion of services and diversification of strategies to reach the neediest communities in both accessible and hard to reach areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SHO will ensure Mainstreaming protection through the inclusion of humanitarian protection principles into the crisis response by ensuring that any response is provided in a way that avoids any unintended negative effects (do-no-harm), is delivered according to needs, prioritizes safety and dignity, is grounded on participation and empowerment of local capacities, and ultimately holds humanitarian actor’s accountable vis–à-vis affected individuals and communities. SHO will seek to ensure that affected communities’ needs are addressed and human rights are upheld and their needs fully subscribes to the concept of Protection Mainstreaming and Centrality of Protection. SHO will ensure that it provides humanitarian assistance that is impartial prioritizing all without due regard to legal status since all affected population irrespective of their status are entitled to humanitarian assistance and protection

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SHO will conduct a project inception workshop with all project stakeholders. During this workshop, Salient will detailed project information including budget. Salient will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, SHO will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with special Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision-making process. SHO will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the key stake holders. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the SHO Protection unit. SHO has already established complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM) in many sites across Borno State which has been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.SHO has already taken several steps to ensure accountability to affected population: findings from beneficiary surveys, some of which have already been incorporated into the design of this project. SHO will ensure that all interventions are tailored towards monitoring by conducting PDM, Post intervention monitoring and FGCs throughout the project circle.
