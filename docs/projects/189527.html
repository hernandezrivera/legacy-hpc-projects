Project Summary

The food & Livelihood insecurity situation in Guit and Rubkona counties of Unity state remain miserable due to prolonged flood situation, existing conflict & insecurity, the impact of COVID-19 and persistent poor macro and micro economic conditions. Localized tensions compelled by Intra and inter community clashes, revenge killings, cattle rustling, burglary, theft continue to pose a great threat towards community livelihoods. Targeted individuals as a result of revenge killings are threatened to pursue livelihoods and economic opportunities and hence continue to remain vulnerable. Almost 90% of the Rubkona & Guit county is inundated in prolonged flooding in the state. It’s very unlikely that the water would recede in near future and water logging in many agriculture lands may become permanent. To address the flood & Conflict induced acute food insecurity situation as well as livelihood need of most vulnerable community, DRC intends to achieve following results –
1. Agriculture & Food Security: Flood and conflict affected Agro-pastoral IDP & Host communities’ food security and livelihoods are protected and restored through the provision of emergency and early recovery and flood adaptive agriculture and fisheries practice for most vulnerable households
2. Economic Recovery & Market System: To ensure improved access to income & livelihood opportunities through Cash / Market based Livelihood interventions vulnerable Households.
DRC vision is to provide a dignified life for all people affected by displacement. As such,
DRC Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) activities are sectorial inter-linked with Protection
[(including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV)] and Armed Violence Reduction
(AVR) response. DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming in all the project activities and assessment tools will also be adopted and specfic criteria for inclusion will be developed to priorities the most vulnerable households in the targeted communities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is experiencing unprecedented levels of acute food insecurity as per Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) sectoral analysis estimates that 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) in 2022. This is an increase of 7 per cent above the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. The main drivers are floods and dry spells, inflation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence and household stressors (such as death of the bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services), which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. (HRP/ FSNMS Analysis 2022)The persistent floods in 2021 because of heavy rains and over-flow from Lake Victoria in Uganda have devastated livelihoods and living conditions of most vulnerable population in Guit (Approximately 49,000) and Rubkona (149,000) counties in Unity State. The food security situation is projected to worsen with likely risk of households facing severe hunger and famine due to flooding and displacement and reduced seasonal crop production in 2021. Flooding has destroyed crops; shelter, infrastructure /facilities and displaced over 160,000 people in Guit and Rubkona counties through August to December 2021, displacement caseloads are likely to increase in these two counties in next rainy season. The elderly, disabled, women and children will become susceptible to hard living conditions with limited or no access to sufficient household food and other basic needs, sanitation facilities and information on protection services. This situation necessitates the need to address the critical food, protection and GBV, social cohesion and other basic needs of the affected population. Floods destroyed farmland and crops, affecting seasonal harvest, placing at risk the next planting season, and killed a large number of livestock. The floodwaters remain standing and stagnant, with no sign of receding resulting in longer term implication in livelihood of the displaced community. Most affected will continue to be children, people with disability, older persons and women-headed households identified as most vulnerable: all identifying food security and livelihoods as the most needed forms of assistance for men 55% and women 52% of assessed settlements & with children 29% behind their prioritization for education 48% (REACH, May 2021)

2 - Project activities

Under this project, DRC proposes to implement following activities;CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);Under partnership with WFP, DRC will provide conditional general food assistance through Food for Assets will improve the community infrastructures, flood dykes and swallow wells to channel the water for irrigation. These assets would be identified by communities based on their needs and provision of general food assistance will also increase the food production and access to diversified food.CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);DRC will provide the emergency access to agricultural inputs to most vulnerable to increase the food production in Unity and Upper Nile states. Households will be prioritized who rely on the agriculture, have lost the agriculture tools due to the displacement and or don’t have self -capacity to buy the seeds and inputs. DRC will also provide the training on best agronomic practices to increase the food production, reduce the post -harvest damages and building on seed multiplication approaches. The intervention will ultimately increase Households self- reliance capacity and will reduce the dependency on humanitarian assistance in coming time. DRC will also encompass efforts to increase the local seeds demand in the respective counties to develop the market systems. DRC will also use the Cash & Voucher assistance through seed vouchers to enable the local procurements of seeds. Both crop and vegetable seeds would be provided through seed vouchers so that beneficiaries can buy seeds on their preferences.CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);DRC will continue working with local agriculture extension workers to extend the services on promoting climate smart agriculture practices to increase the food production, promote local seeds and seed multiplications approaches, reduce post harvest losses and increased crop diversifications. As mentioned above in objective 02, DRC will promote to local procurement of seeds and support the seed fairs through seed vouchers enabling communities to buy their preferred seeds for both vegetables and crops. The intervention also be imbedded with best agriculture practices to reduce the post harvest and seeds perseveration by using the hermetic storage bags. DRC will also conduct the market assessments in Rubkona and Guit to understand the market systems, identification of key value chains ( agriculture, fisheries and livestock productions and will engage youth, market actors and farmers in order to provide them income opportunities. Building on previous experience on vocational and life skills, DRC will engage project development committees to identify the youth and women groups for vocational skills in Rubkona, Jamjang and guit in community services centres. The vaarritey of life skills courses i-e tailoring, solar maintenance, butchery, baking, garments, leather crafts, and start up kits will also be provided to students to start their business and ear livelihoods.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedbacks are collected regularly through the different international activities on the ground; daily operational information desks, daily visits, committees weekly meetings, trainings, workshops, and other means. The information collected from the beneficiaries serve to ensure the full inclusion of the affected persons in the humanitarian programming, and to ensure their influence to DRC programming, approaches and activities, mainstreaming AGD, DRC will regularly seek the feedback from the community it serves, to ensure providing the relevant assistance that will help in building their resilience and to achieve the ultimate participation level of full ownership. Feedback will be shared with the relevant project managers and be used to adapt the project implementation whenever required and inform future project design.At the beginning, Project management Committees (PMC) will be formed. Community consultations would be made before implementing each activity to finalize the BOQ for inputs support. Participatory exercise will be conducted to identify community work for CFW. People will be consulted on preferred food before designing commodity vouchers. Post distribution monitoring and other complain desk will register beneficiaries’ complaints and feedback to take necessary measures to resolve / address concerns which are with-in the scope of the project

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has a functional FCRM mechanism that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC FCRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. Feedback and complaints can be provided through the FCRM desk but also through community engagement activities and visits and through intermediaries working with DRC e.g. implementing partners and community governance structures/representatives. Under this project, DRC will seek feedback from various groups, including the most vulnerable and adjust the feedback mechanisms as appropriate. Whenever possible DRC will establish a joint protection and FCRM desk to ensure protection concerns are recorded immediately. DRC has a team composed of both male and female staff and conducts age and gender-segregated focus group discussions whenever required to gather inputs regarding issues affecting them as well as feedback and recommendations. Continuous training on participation and its importance, as well as close collaboration with the protection and SGBV departments will be ensured. DRC CCCM considers the AGDM approach in its activities, and sensitizes its staff and community leaders equally to its importance.DRC will set-up complaint mechanisms under this project and community members including vulnerable groups will be informed about the complaint mechanisms. Different methodologies will be developed to receive complaint / feedback like complaint desk, Hot-lie number and also periodic focus group discussions. Participation & Feedback of most vulnerable community will be ensured in planning process (PRA exercise, planning meeting, etc) to Implementation. PDM will also cover most vulnerable HHs to get this feed-back.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC staff were trained and informed on the DRC Code of Conduct and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DRC has a zero-tolerance for cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. This has promoted staff accountability and transparency to persons of concerns in service delivery. DRC provide regular refresher PSEA training to the staff and to the community it serves, to ensure their knowledge of the appropriate channel of communications and reporting if encountered with a potential case. Under this project, DRC will have IEC materials stationed at strategic locations such as the information desk, community centers which include contact information on PSEA reporting mechanism. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, DRC will inform them about their right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including DRC's commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

As a certified member of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), DRC fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. Fundamental to all DRC activities is adherence to Age and Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM), achieved through engagement with the various community members and existing institutions. Through formal and informal interactions with representative groups and other community members, beneficiaries are empowered to inform the type of activities and services provided by DRC and ensure their implementation is relevant, effective, and responsive to the needs of displaced populations DRC engages different community representatives across all the project locations, including women, men, youth, elderly PWD, community leaders, and community-based committees. DRC ensures information reached and is received by all affected population through different mediums such as community leaders, community mobilizers, women and youth groups. DRC information desks are accessible and inclusive of everyone, either in-person or one-on-one meeting with DRC field staff on monitoring visits. To strengthen its accountability to the affected population, DRC establishes complaint and feedback mechanisms to provide community members and other stakeholders a platform to provide feedback on the activities. The exact complaint and feedback mechanisms will depend on the target location and activities being implemented but examples of complaint and feedback mechanisms DRC has established includes complaint and feedback desks at key project sites, hosting of focus group discussions to receive feedback from beneficiaries and installation of complaints and feedback boxes at static protection desks. Beneficiaries will be informed on the feedback mechanisms during community engagement activities, at the beginning of the intervention and throughout the project. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.DRC will engage and ensure participation of community through-out the project cycle. Project management committee will be formed to ensure active engagement of community in the project and also finalizing the targeting criteria and beneficiary selection. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas to get community feedback. DRC will set-up robust complaint mechanism as per DRC’s programme quality guidelines. MEAL team will collect, analyze and refer complaints received through established system under this project. The main feedback and complaint collection mechanisms will be complaint desk, community reference groups & FGD. A hotline system will also be established with non-charge facilities for community to share complaints with DRC. All complaints will be registered by MEAL team and refer to respective team for resolution. MEAL team will generate report on the complaint reports, nature and resolution status in every month.
