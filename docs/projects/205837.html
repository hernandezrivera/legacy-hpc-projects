Project Summary

This project intervention is meant to increase access to, and quality of GBV response, Prevention and Risk mitigation targeting 26881; 15053 Women,6451 men, 2756 Girls and 2581 boys of the returnees, IDPs and Vulnerable host communities including people living with disability (PLWD). Women and girls in South Sudan including the one fleeing conflict in Sudan continue to face high risks of GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), intimate partner violence and physical violence. Adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in many locations across South Sudan. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence have been documented within the country and Sudan crisis. The impacts of this are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in the country including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support. In addition, due to economic stress, flooding and dry spell that is being experience currently in the country and which is expected to spiral to the year 2024, Cases of gender-based violence is expected to rise due members of the household being more likely to perpetrate violence. Overall, the capacity and coverage of multi-sectoral GBV responses including health, GBV case management and PSS, higher-level mental health and other services remain scarce in the targeted counties of Malakal, Melut, Akobo, Fangak, Pigi, Pibor and Terekeka in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Central equatorial states where SAADO has predominantly presence.
The increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services in Malakal, Melut, Pigi, Fangak, Pibor and Terekeka counties, coupled with shrinking humanitarian funding, highlight an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, scaling up of specialized GBV response services and Prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in targeted mention areas where SAADO operates. Specifically, there is a need to strengthen GBV case management, health response to GBV survivors, psychosocial support and GBV referral pathways. Primary GBV prevention interventions such as Community Cares program, integration of GBV actions into other sectoral responses including safety audits, strengthening of GBV coordination, and provision of dignity kits are high priority interventions for the GBV in 2024 South Sudan.
For accountability to the affected population (AAP) and in order to involve all the beneficiaries in the response, SAADO will create a committee in each county that will compose of 50% women, 20% Men and 30% people living with disability (PLWD) to oversee the project implementation as from its initiation. The beneficiaries will be relying their concerns and feedbacks to the committee after a series of consultation, then the committee will share feedback with the partner to enhance the quality of the project. The involvement of the community will be key in building trust and creating a sense of ownership thus increasing the sustainability of the project in the targeted locations. SAADO will mainstream protection in this project by contacting risks analysis and continuous monitoring access, Safety and dignity of all targeted beneficiaries. Through this intervention and response, SAADO estimate the cost of this project to be 940,835. SAADO has prominent presence in this targeted location, implementing GBV project in Malakal, Melut, Pigi and Fangak; FSL and WASH in Northen Bahr-el-gazal and central Equatorial including Terekeka and Livelihood in Pibor. SAADO propose to expand its GBV programming to area that it operates to enhance integration. All the mentioned area, we do have established offices and mean of transport thus reducing it operation budget. Integration of GBV activities with other sector including Protection, WASH, FSL, Health, Education and Nutrition will be key to enhance synergy in the targeted area.

1. Needs

South Sudan after independence has descended into a protracted conflict characterized by high levels of violence and disintegration of its social fabric. According to UN Commission on human right report, it shows a surge in GBV incidences, fueled by persistent conflict, climate related crisis, disruption of community and family structures, breakdown in conflict resolution mechanisms, weak security institutions, and tension among ethnic groups and between those who have been displaced and host communities, have left South Sudanese people, especially women and girls, exposed to many security risks and violence. Prominent forms of gender-based violence include rape, sexual exploitation, intimate partner violence and physical violence. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) have been documented within the country and Sudan crisis. The impacts of this are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in the Hard-to-reach areas of Fangak, Pigi, Malakal, Melut, Pibor and Terekeka including CMR, GBV case management and PSS. While men and boys are also affected by GBV, the majority of survivors are women and girls. According to a report from global women’s institute and the IRC, up to 65% of South Sudanese women and girls in conflict affected area have experience physical or sexual violence. Despite efforts to prevent GBV, little progress has been made in the targeted areas thus leaving a greater number of women traumatize. Economic stress that is being experienced and expected to spiral to 2024 will led to higher number of GBV cases due to members of the household being more likely to perpetrate violence. According to HRP 2023 report, Women and girls are continuously at risk of being attacked while carrying out their daily routines as they care for their families’ needs. An estimated 2.8 million people risk physical violence, rape, and other forms of gender-based violence as projected in the year 2023 which is expected to rise 2024 due plan General election. Climate change and displacement play a considerable role in the increase of GBV. Recurrent flooding and dry spells have left many households losing their homes and crops, causing financial hardship and internal displacement. More people move to POC camps due to natural and man-made disaster, heightening the risks of gender- based violence. The influx of returnees and refugees due to Sudan crisis have further exacerbated the humanitarian situation in a country where 9.4 million need humanitarian assistance in 2023 and much higher numbers expected in 2024. As of 27th October 2023, according to the report from UN-OCHA bulleting report, more 341,967 have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan. With soaring inflation and economic hardship, women and girls are be pressured to negative coping mechanisms to provide for their families, such as gathering firewood and early marriages. Increased GBV can limit women’s access to public spaces and health care as well. The 2018 R-ARCISS has attempted to address GBV but the response has been inadequate. The security sector in the county is weak, limiting the state’s ability to protect citizens. People still hold senior positions even after perpetrating GBV, indicating the issue is not taken seriously. Formal justice is not accessible by survivors thus relying to customary courts and traditional leaders for remedy, which often leads to forced marriage between perpetrators and survivors. In patriarchal society where women are not considered equal to men, poor families in the targeted areas end up 'selling' off their daughters to provide for the family. Women and girls are not given a chance to learn life skills or receive educational training that would allow them to utilize secure forms of earning money. Female survivors may be abandoned by their partners and families after attacked due to perceived loss of honor. Generally, women are underrepresented in the government thus losing ability to air out their voices.

2. Project activities

To ensure the project deliver the key needed services to the needy beneficiaries, SAADO will implement activities that are aligned with GBV cluster response; Survivor response, Prevention and risk mitigation, to provide quality services to the vulnerable returnee’s, IDPs and Host communities including PLWD survivors of Gender Base violence. This will include; • Identify and provide support to 6 Women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) and 6 mobile team/ Outreach team • Provide comprehensive GBV case management services and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and at-risk women, boys, girls including persons living with disability (PLWD) of the returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities in the WGFS • Provide tailored materials (PSS) support to extremely vulnerable survivors of IDPs, Returnees and host communities including PLWD through the GBV case management services in the women and Girls friendly spaces (WGFS) in the six targeted counties (Malakal, Melut, Pigi, Fangak, Pibor, Akobo and Terekeka) • Establish referral pathways in the targeted counties of Malakal, Malakal, Melut, Pigi, Fangak, Pibor and Akobo• Provision of referral services to survivors of gender base violence to receive health and other services in the WGFS, and through Mobile team efforts to reach the most vulnerable women and girls in hard reach area or in case of emergency • Provision of Dignity kits to the vulnerable women and adolescent girls of returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community • Supporting and establishing communities’ structures to mitigate risks, prevent and respond to GBV in the six targeted counties (Malakal, Melut, Pigi, Fangak, Pibor, Akobo and Terekeka) • Provide cash and voucher assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls of the IDPs, returnees and host communities. • Distribution of fuel -efficient stoves. • Conduct GBV awareness raising on on GBV risks, available response services, PSEA and timely reporting to Women, Men, Girls and boys including person living with disability (PLWD) of the returnees, IDPs and Vulnerable host communities through campaigns (16 days of activism and international women day) door to door, radio talk, and Focus group discussion and printing IEC material with local languages on BCC. • Conduct safety audits to identify GBV risks and implement actions to address them • Training of service providers on GBV integration in their sectoral response and Prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) • Provide training on CMR to the health personnels in the Hospital and PHCC to be improve knowledge and skill on how to Manage rape cases. • Trained community leaders and staffs on GBV basic survivor centred approach, referral pathway and community facilitation skills.• Training of Community structure members on GBV and survivor cantered approached including PSEA• Training of grass root women led organization on advocacy and campaign on policy reforms • Prevention of GBV through engaging men and boys for accountable practice; confront harmful practices including child marriage, violence against women and girls including the genuine empowerment of girls and women
