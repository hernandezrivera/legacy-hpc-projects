Project Summary

AET is proposing an emergency response that not only raises awareness of protection and GBV risks facing displaced children, youth and women but also addresses and mitigates the risks through a blend of interventions. The project proposes to comprehensively address the second priority identified by the cluster: addressing critical protection concerns with the persistent displacement towards IDP sites, including heightened protection risks/threats that have emerged in the failure to end displacement through appropriate solutions (local integration, return, and resettlement). The proposed project has three outcomes:
(1): Strengthened advocacy influenced by HCT protection (including for protection funding), and enhanced communication on protection, tailored to the socio-cultural context of Somalia;
(2) Strengthening systematic data collection, analysis informing response, and establishing a repository on protection risks/threats, that adhere to global safety and ethical standards;
(3): Strengthened mainstreaming of key principles concerning Protection, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) across all sectors of the Humanitarian response.
The proposed project is a multi-pronged response to the increasing and multi-faceted humanitarian need in Somalia, that has arisen as a result of severe drought compounded by a perilous security situation and surging COVID-19 cases. In October 2021, Somalia saw an increase in deaths from COVID-19 and almost double the number of confirmed cases from April 2021 (13,812 to 21,998). Recent conflict in Mogadishu, Guriceel and Las Anood has hampered humanitarian service delivery and will drive further need if the security situation deteriorates as more people become displaced. The recent Protection Cluster updates indicate that more people are affected by compounded crises of drought, conflict and the Covid-19. The internally displaced people (IDPs) across the country are thus dealing with unique needs at a time when funding flows are attempting to support myriad competing demands. Children, adolescents, unemployed youth, women and people with disabilities are most vulnerable and require urgent attention, as the current crisis compounds vulnerabilities arising from chronic conflict and crises in Somalia.
In situations of conflict and humanitarian and disaster situations, GBV cases are common; women and girls are more vulnerable to different forms of GBV with acts such as rape, confinement and sexual slavery. GBV has serious and far-reaching negative effects including physical injuries resulting in death or disfigurement, psychological trauma, infections, unwanted pregnancies, social stigmatization and exclusion and economic deprivation among others.
AET, FENPS and Candle Light are therefore hoping to implement this intervention to reduce the protection and GBV risks that currently affect the most marginalised groups of people in Somalia. Through a consortium approach, we will deliver the project with guidance and expertise from Street Child, a protection specialist, of which AET is an affiliate. AET will build the ability and capacity of local partners in a bid to strengthen localization, diversify our approach to humanitarian response and handle protection programs with expertise. Street Child will provide global level support, and share best practices from similar programmes in crisis situations in Nigeria, Afghanistan and South Sudan. The proposed project leverages the specific experience and expertise of each partner to ensure comprehensive coverage of all proposed activities in target areas, with optimal effectiveness, efficiencies, and equitable access for affected populations. The program team will assess, develop a strategy and carefully monitor activity implementation to ensure that girls, boys, men and women are equally benefiting from the activities administered under this program.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

AET conducts a range of activities to mitigate and manage risk, including (i) analysing risks and creating a comprehensive list of indicators and triggers; (ii) ensuring all necessary measures are taken to prevent or contain potential risks to a minimum, including regular monitoring of risks and review of risk registers; and (iii) creating and communicating a chain of authority to ensure that indicators of risk are recognized in time, and information is shared and investigated. Whilst there are a range of risks associated with the proposed project, 4.1 (Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance) and 4.2 (Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance) are deemed to be the highest risks. AET will conduct camp-level assessments to identify and map minorities as well as existing inequities. Inclusive project strategies will then be developed to ensure that no one is left behind. The strategy will include targeting of specific minority groups, such as Somali Bantus in Jubaland camps and the Rahanweyn in Somaliland and Puntland. We will ensure that these groups are included in project design as well as hosting regular feedback sessions to ensure that they continue to be included in the project throughout its duration. A strong feedback and accountability mechanism will be established where beneficiaries can provide concerns and suggested adaptations throughout the project cycle. AET and the wider SC group have protection policies where staff-including partners-are forbidden from indulging in exploitative behaviour. All the project staff, teachers and stakeholders will undergo safeguarding and GESI training so they are able to prevent, respond to and refer any cases that they may identify. Referral mechanisms will be established and existing systems will be strengthened so that girls and women are aware of how they can report any incidences that may occur. Additionally, education is key to reduce gendered forms of abuse, violence and exploitation. We will therefore incorporate sessions during which we explore this topic in the curriculum, as well as hosting community sensitization sessions with key community members.
