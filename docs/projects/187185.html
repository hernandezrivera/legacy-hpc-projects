Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to improve access to reproductive, maternal, newborn, child, and adolescent health (RMNCAH) services through timely, integrated, and targeted service provision at health facilities and IDP Camps and Host Communities in Borno and Yobe State. The project will also ensure the last-mile distribution of essential lifesaving drugs and medical supplies for IDPs (including persons with disabilities) with critical needs in line with the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan.
The project will scale up and sustain a continuum of care for beneficiaries by strengthening the capacity of human resources to provide Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services such as Maternal and Child Health (ANC, Support safe delivery, PNC & FP),Health education & Hygiene promotion among women and girls, SRH sensitization, awareness & sensitization messaging within targeted Health facilities in IDP camps and Host Communities in Four Local Government Areas namely Dikwa and Damboa LGA (Borno State) and Damaturu and Potiskum LGA (Yobe State)
By the end of the project cycle, the proposed project will make significant strides in improving the health outcomes for 7000 women, newborns, children, and adolescents in the Four Project LGAs. This will be achieved through the provision of life-saving drugs, engaging qualified health care service providers for the provision of quality sexual and reproductive health services, essential family planning services, SRH/PSEA awareness education, and sensitization.

1 - Needs

The 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that over 600,000 women need access to safe delivery attended by a skilled birth attendant and an estimated 700,000 women and adolescent girls need access to family planning to avert an unintended pregnancy. OCHA November 2021 Nigeria Situation Report shows that in Borno State insecurity remains the main challenge with hundreds of incidents perpetrated by NSAGs endangering humanitarian personnel and assets. For instance, there has been a recent influx of new arrivals and rehabilitated members of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and their associates in Dikwa LGA hereby creating a surge in need for services and NFI which have impacted primary and secondary health care services, protection assistance, and other services support for thousands of women and girls. In Pulka Town, Gwoza LGA, there have been major health response gaps since the suspension of services by MSF Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF).. Furthermore, cases of acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and suspected cholera continue to rise in the state. The total number of suspected cholera cases reported as of 5th December 2021 stands at 5,797 with Gwoza accounting for 1,003 cases (Borno State Ministry of Health Cholera Outbreak Situation Report No. 16, 12th December 2021) In Yobe State, gaps in health response/services, including the decreased availability of medical supplies and services across IDP camps remains a major problem.

2 - Activities

The provision of life-saving and life-sustaining health services will be driven by increasing access to maternal and child health services, sexual and reproductive health services, MHPSS, and the strengthening of referral services. Thus, the intervention seeks to: 1. Build the capacity of Voluntary Healthcare Workers on the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) to implement coordinated activities that will enhance Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, and Adolescent Health (RMNCAH) outcomes in Gwoza and Dikwa LGA. 2 Conduct Mobile Health Outreaches in IDP Camps and Host Communities to enhance access to RMNCAH services. The mobile health team will carry out mobile health outreaches, targeting the most vulnerable populations across camps and host communities as part of efforts to mitigate the risk of disease outbreaks and spread including COVID-19, measles, cholera/acute watery diarrhea (AWD) in the Two LGAs. 3 SRH/GBV information, education, and communication (IEC) sessions will be conducted at identified Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in the LGAs to reach adolescent girls and young women with information and services.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

AHI will actively promote adherence to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) that describe the roles, responsibilities, guiding principles, and procedures for prevention of and response to any form of gender-based violence affecting the community. All the healthworkers will be sensitized about PSEA and Do No Harm, and sign a Do No Harm Pledge. AHI will ensure that the health workers apply culturally sensitive approaches as well as understand the structural and cultural factors that contribute to women’s and girls’ vulnerability to GBV and SRH risks. Thus, the application of the humanitarian guiding principles of “do no harm”, confidentiality, safety, and nondiscrimination, in every implementation phase. The service providers will include both male and female health workers so that it is more acceptable for women to benefit from services. The project is targeting the most vulnerable populations and its main beneficiaries are adolescent girls, women and persons living with disabilities. Comment boxes will be placed in strategic locations where beneficiaries can put in their anonymous complaints, questions, or suggestions. These comments will be reviewed in a systematic way and beneficiaries will also be oriented by project staff on how they can share their feedback.
