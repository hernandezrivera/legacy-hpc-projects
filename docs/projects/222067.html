Project Summary

In the proposed action, Save the Children aim to implement integrated WASH interventions to reduce the burden of WASH related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition locations presenting an extreme WASH vulnerability and high prevalence of malnutrition. Services provided will break the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhea), thus improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition. The proposed action will also seek to address WASH needs during emergencies such as response to floods, returnees and disease outbreaks. The proposed interventions will target WASH Cluster priority 1 and 2 locations of Abyei Administrative Area and Akobo County in which Save the Children has already established good operational presence.
The proposed WASH activities include; Distribution of WASH lifesaving kits/Dignity/Hygiene kits among SAM/MAM children and caregiver families, hygiene promotion sessions conducted at Nutrition sites and catchment areas, construction /rehabilitation of gender-disaggregated latrine facilities at nutrition sites with consideration of People with Special Needs based on area analysis and if required, latrines constructed/rehabilitated as per WASH Cluster guidelines with flood resilient designs, construction /rehabilitation of water sources/boreholes at nutrition site and catchment areas with consideration of People with Special Needs based on based on area analysis and if required, water sources/boreholes will be constructed/rehabilitated as per WASH Cluster guidelines with flood resilient designs. water quality monitoring, safety audit for construction of borehole/handpump/water yard drilling/rehabilitation, deployment of mobile Sanitation Teams to clear faeces from environment, dissemination of IEC material and using mass media for hygiene promotion, MHM kits distribution to IDPs and returnees, distribution of WASH Emergency Kit (buckets, aquatabs, PuR Sachets, filter cloth and soap) during Emergency response, Safe water sources developed (rehab/new) as per the WASH Cluster guidelines for flood resilient structures, barrier analysis conducted for disability inclusion, hygiene promotion and construction of latrines in communities using CLTS approach.
Activities will be monitored by the field visits, construction progress reports, 5Ws, interim reports and will be reflected in Save The Children Indicator Performance Tracking Tool (IPTT).
Save the children targets a total of 53,333 beneficiaries (M: 9,067, W: 10,133, B: 17,066, G: 17,067) across the targeted locations. The estimated cost to deliver the proposed WASH interventions is USD 2,400,000.

1. Needs

The WASH needs In South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity, coupled with public health issues (including waterborne diseases e.g. Cholera, Mpox, HEV, etc.) has been an underlying driver of WASH conditions in almost all states, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services while also restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. The Nov.2024 IPC report revealed that between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. MSNA report conducted by SC in August 2024 in Abyei revealed that 76.2% of the sampled Households drink untreated water, while 3.4% sometimes treat their drinking water using chlorine, filter clothes and boiling water, 68.9% of the sampled households practice open defecation in the bush, the majority of the population practice open defecation both children and adults in open area due to lack of latrines in their households. Additionally, the survey revealed that prices of hygiene items are too high because households have lost income and cannot afford. Some of the items are not available in the market. Outbreaks of diseases has been observed frequently in areas with limited access to essential water, sanitation, hygiene, and health services, and may affect several hundred to several thousand persons. On 17 July 2024, the state Ministry of Health Abyei Administrative Area declared an outbreak of the hepatitis E Virus in the Abyei Special Administrative Area, as of 4 Sept 2024, 34 HEV cases has been identified with 3 confirmed and 31 probable cases whereas death toll remains at 3. The highest case incidence is within Ameit IDP camp In Abyei AA. Outbreaks of this disease has been observed frequently in areas with limited access to essential water, sanitation, hygiene, and health services, and may affect several hundred to several thousand persons. The same MSNA report conducted by SC in August 2024 in Akobo County revealed that 19% of respondents indicated that access to non-food items (NFIs) was essential for their well-being. NFIs may include essential supplies such as soap, hygiene kits and other basic necessities that support daily living, which are especially crucial in contexts of displacement or crises. Therefore, SC proposes to continue provision of access to clean drinking water, sanitation and improved hygiene practices across SC’s operational areas in South Sudan

2. Project activities

The proposed WASH activities include; Distribution of WASH lifesaving kits/Dignity/Hygiene kits among SAM/MAM children and caregiver families, hygiene promotion sessions conducted at Nutrition sites and catchment areas, construction /rehabilitation gender-disaggregated latrine facilities at nutrition site with consideration of People with Special Needs based on based on area analysis and if required, latrines constructed/rehabilitated as per WASH Cluster guidelines with flood resilient designs, construction /rehabilitation of water sources/boreholes at nutrition site and catchment areas with consideration of People with Special Needs based on based on area analysis and if required, water sources/boreholes will be constructed/rehabilitated as per WASH Cluster guidelines with flood resilient designs. water quality monitoring, safety audit for construction of borehole/handpump/water yard drilling/rehabilitation, deployment of mobile Sanitation Teams to clear faeces from environment, dissemination of IEC material and using mass media for hygiene promotion, MHM kits distribution to IDPs and returnees, distribution of WASH Emergency Kit (buckets, aquatabs, PuR Sachets, filter cloth and soap) during Emergency response, Safe water sources developed (rehab/new) as per the WASH Cluster guidelines for flood resilient structures, barrier analysis conducted for disability inclusion, hygiene promotion, construction of latrines in communities using CLTS approach using the approved government guidelines, soap distribution and mitigation of Gender Based Violence, GBV through training of WASH staff on basic skills to provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support and GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) are incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility. The proposed action will adopt mitigation measures that address barriers to access to WASH services for people with disabilities and the elderly. To better understand the needs of the people living with disabilities and address their barriers to accessing WASH services, the WASH cluster Disability Inclusion checklist will be updated, and the findings used to inform the program design. Priority communities and villages will be selected in consultation with WASH cluster subnational coordinators, WASH cluster partners and with support of the Nutrition partner. Communities and villages with nutrition facilities with and no/limited access to WASH services, highest GAM prevalence and diarrheal disease incidence based on data available at nutrition facilities and sourcing drinking water from an unsafe source with a high risk of contamination with human excreta will be selected. Within the selected communities and villages, the most vulnerable households will be targeted. The most vulnerable households within the community are the households with a pregnant and lactating woman, female-headed household, households with a disabled and/or elderly, etc. Activities will be monitored by the field visits, construction progress reports, 5Ws, interim reports and will be reflected in Save The Children Indicator Performance Tracking Tool (IPTT).
