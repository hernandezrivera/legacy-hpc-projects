Needs

The province of Idlib is hosting the highest number of IDPs in Syria . It is estimated that almost half of the 2,9 Million people in Idlib are internally displaced and half of them are children. Most of the families live in poor housing conditions (informal settlements/unfinished buildings) with no regular electricity or water supply and also the provision of food and basic medical health facilities is inadequate. Moreover the host communities are overwhelmed by the numbers of IDPs. The infrastructure and the administration/financial systems are overstretched and are completely insufficient to cope with the huge burden of IDPs. More and more informal settlements announce that they cannot take any more refugees. It is estimated that 1,39 Mio people in Idlib are in need of humanitarian assistance and 350.000 people are in dire need of humanitarian help. An assessment done by the WASH cluster in 2018 revealed the complete insufficient provision of drinking water in Idlib and North Aleppo. It is saying that where only around 13 per cent of the population is served exclusively via piped water The study shows that water trucking is the only source of water for 35-45 per cent of the population in Idlib. This situation also results to an increase in water-borne diseases. Studies of the CCCM for 2018 show that WASH interventions have the highest priority in almost 30 % of the informal settlements

Implementation of activities

Activity 1.: Daily distribution of drinking water by water truck to 5.000 IDPs in 11 informal settlements. Activity 2.: Two times per week the solid waste of 5.000 IDPs will be collected in 11 informal settlements. Activity 3.: Emptying wastewater in 11 informal settlements for 5.000 IDPs.Activity 4.: Construction of 200 latrines for 4.000 IDPs in 11 informal settlements. Activity 5.: Purchase and distribution of 400 water tanks to 5.000 IDPs in 11 informal settlements.Activity 6: Distribution of 8.640 hygiene kits to households in 6 communities for a period of 24 months.

Protection Risk Analysis

risk: Risk that target areas are inaccessible or insecure; or that staff or beneficiaries may be injured, killed or kidnapped by armed groups during the implementation of activities because of advance of armed groups and/or aerial shelling • Mitigating measures: 1) Strategic targeting of areas which offer highest humanitarian needs with highest potential for accessibility based on conflict trends analysis. • Constant monitoring of the security situation; extensive acceptance measures to sustain good relations with communities for a continuous understanding of local context and to facilitate unimpeded access to target areas; engagement with WoS (whole of Syria) to devise contingency access plans. Risk : Risk of theft of NFIs and chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups inside Syria.AN will provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to users;Work closely with all partners on the ground; Risk : Quality and/or quantity of the water Trucking is not adequate as per humanitarian standardsEnsure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils.Ensure and comply with minimum humanitarian standard (SPHERE) at 15 litre/person/day and establish water quantity tracking & monitoring system involving beneficiaries; Risk: Bathing spaces are installed inappropriately and culturally insensitively AN will Consult with women and adolescent girls and ensure regular access of water and hanging spaces/options for towel and clothes. Risk: Movements of garbage trucks in congested, camp like settings; Reliability of services.Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Provide household level solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease disposal of waste from H/H to the public SW collection points. Establish clear routes and schedule for garbage trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school. Risk: Movements of desludging trucks in congested, camp like settings;Consult community regarding desludging services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Establish clear routes and schedule for desludging trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators;

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: The security situation in the project region is getting worse due to military and/or political interventions. Mitigation: Constant monitoring of the security situation, building up security management and back up structures. Establishing a contingency plan. Security training for staff. Risk: Border crossing points between Turkey and Syria will be closed. Mitigation: Items will be bought on the local market as well as in Turkey which reduces the risk of limited supply.
