Project Summary

The project aims to offer a comprehensive and coordinated approach. Shafak's plan aims to assist farmers, livestock keepers, and Small-Medium Enterprises serving vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and HCs. Throughout 2024, the project will focus on the following main areas:
1. Supporting 1500 targeted winter crop farmers (wheat and barley) to restart agricultural activities, increase household income, and enhance consumption. This involves providing agricultural startup kits covering farming inputs' expenses, water access, pest control, harvesting, extension services, and wheat purchasing. Modality: Restricted Cash for Ag inputs and services, Value: 450 USD per Ha per farmer.
2. Assisting 500 targeted olive tree farmers to resume agricultural activities, thereby increasing household income and consumption. Similar to the previous activity, this involves providing startup kits covering various agricultural needs. Modality: Restricted Cash for Ag inputs and services, Value: 25 USD per Dunum, Max 5dunums support.
3. Supporting 700 targeted forage crop farmers to resume agricultural activities, increase household income, and improve consumption through the provision of agriculture startup kits. Modality: Hybrid Cash and Restricted value vouchers for Ag inputs and services, Value: 300 USD per Ha, Max 1 Ha support.
4. Assisting 3000 targeted households to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through the provision of livestock inputs, associated training, and vaccination campaigns. Modality: InKind and Service. Value: 500 Kg Fodder enough for 10 animals for 50 days, Service: 4 vaccination campaigns.
5. Helping 500 targeted households stabilize, maintain, or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which involve building and strengthening community-related value chain infrastructure, disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest and vole control, olive trees harvesting, purification of wheat fields, and afforestation. Modality: Conditional un Restricted CASH, Value: 5$ unskilled and 6$ skilled per day, Duration 20 days round.
6. Rehabilitating 30 related value chain infrastructures such as irrigation channels, markets, mills, bakeries, storage facilities, wells, nurseries, seeds multiplication, fodder mixers, feeder roads, and silos.
7. Providing Graduation Business Grants to 404 Small-Micro Enterprises through business skills training and financial or in-kind support, aiming to improve job skills and linkages to the job market.
8. Supporting 1000 households with regular food baskets over eight rounds. Modality: Cash in Hand, Value: 65$ max per HH, 70% SMEB and 70% Kcl.
9. Supporting 3100 households with cash/voucher-based assistance based on population preferences and market dynamics. Among them, 300 HHs with malnutrition and PBWG will be supported by supplementary food assistance for eight rounds. Modality: Value vouchers, Value: 65$ max per HH and 35$ top-up for Malnutrition, 70% SMEB and 70% Kcl.
10. Distributing 2500 Ready-to-Eat meals in response to emergencies and shocks. Modality: Inkind, weight: 12 KG, enough for five days/HH
11. Distributing 470 metric tons of flour to bakeries to subsidize bread prices in communities. Modality: Inkind, Qty: 50% of daily capacity
12. Distributing 768 metric tons of flour as free bread to populations in camps. Modality: Inkind, Qty: 250gr/day/person, Duration: 4 Months, 26 days a month.
13. Facilitating increased access to productive assets for 700 smallholders' vegetable producers, along with technical agriculture training and support for climate-smart farming practices. Modality: Hybrid Cash and Restricted value vouchers for Ag inputs and services, Value: 200 USD per Dunum, Max 1 dunum support.
14. Building the capacity of 3400 farmers and livestock keepers supported to strengthen their technical skills and capabilities.
It is worth mentioning that about 25 % of the 2024 budget is secured up to date for this
submission.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

This intervention is highly relevant to the needs in northwest Syria for several reasons:The project encompasses various initiatives aimed at bolstering the agricultural sector, livestock keeping, economic empowerment, food security, and infrastructure rehabilitation in Northwest Syria. Targeted support for winter crop farmers, olive tree farmers, and forage crop farmers aims to revive agricultural activities and boost household income. Assistance for livestock activities, including inputs, training, and vaccination campaigns, ensures the sustainability of this crucial livelihood source. Economic empowerment measures, such as supporting SMEs and providing Graduation Business Grants, enhance economic activities, particularly benefiting vulnerable populations. Interventions like regular food distribution and infrastructure rehabilitation efforts, including repairing irrigation channels and markets, address immediate food security concerns and contribute to the region's long-term economic resilience and development. Quality Control Measures for Bread: Monitoring begins at the baking stage, ensuring each bread bundle maintains a weight difference of 10-25 grams, verifying the number of loaves produced, and checking loaf diameter, baking degree, separation, edges, and cooling time. Packaging is scrutinized for design adherence. Technical specifications demand a clean environment, isolated flour bags, regular safety checks, and preparedness for emergencies. Production inputs, from flour quality and quantity to yeast type and diesel quality, undergo a thorough examination to guarantee excellence in every loaf. Referral and coordination: The process of referring households for protection services involves several key steps and considerations. Initially, community leaders and local councils nominate households, but this method carries inherent risks. To mitigate these risks, Protection Cluster referrals complement these nominations. The process of referring households for protection services involves several key steps and considerations. Initially, community leaders and local councils nominate households, but this method carries inherent risks. To mitigate these risks, Protection Cluster referrals complement these nominations. Partners in the Protection Cluster utilize a Joint Food Security-Protection Checklist to identify households facing severe protection and food security risks. Importantly, they only identify households already receiving protection services, ensuring a level of familiarity. Coordination between the Shafak and Protection Cluster partners is vital, with joint planning meetings recommended to establish transparent processes and communication channels.
