Project Summary

This project is aligned to HNO 2018-2020 Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 & 2. UNFPA and its and implementing partners (IPs); Ministry of Health (MOH) and Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) aim to save women’s lives and ensure that, women, men and girls living in Bethlehem, Jenin, and Hebron (H2 and Area C), have access to essential maternal and reproductive health (RH) services, counselling and information, including on well-being and protection from gender based violence (GBV).
This will be achieved through filling gaps in RH services, including access to essential maternal health care and RH service provision and preventing excess mortality and morbidity by;
1) Ensuring a continuum of care and provision of life sustaining RH services, counselling and information including strengthening community preparedness.
2) Provision of essential life-saving maternal health equipment and supplies.
3) Implementation of MISP and training on emergency obstetric care, safe birth etc.
4) Actively engaging communities, strengthening their resilience, supporting coping mechanisms, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach of life saving approaches for RH, including maternal health.
5) Support linking and informing communities to available services with a special focus on marginalized communities and groups including the disabled, elderly etc.
This project is primarily meant to address the immediate response to urgent and critical needs of pregnant and lactating women (around 4,216), especially those living in areas recognized by OCHA as high and medium vulnerable communities (60,310 in area C of Bethlehem, H2 and Hebron). It will also support women of reproductive age (7,245), men (5,175 adults aged 22 years and above), and other vulnerable adolescents; boys (3,060 aged 11-18 years) and girls (5,220 aged 11-18 years), and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of the community and health system for absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises.
MOH shall serve as the backbone and main partner for specialized service provision, particularly secondary health care, including emergency obstetric care, safe birth, and high risk pregnancy management and follow up. PMRS, in close collaboration with Village Council Committees, will use mobile clinics (MCs) to conduct community based RH interventions through service provision and awareness raising in the target areas.
UNFPA will strengthen RH care and complement UNICEF and WHO interventions targeting <5 and neonates, including nutrition activities. UNFPA will synergize activities for maximum benefit for pregnant and lactating women, women in reproductive age, men and adolescents, to utilize the complete package of life sustaining interventions available. UNFPA will coordinate closely with UNICEF, WHO, and other partners through the Health Cluster and bilateral coordination meetings that already occur on a regular basis to ensure synergies and avoid duplication. This entails sharing information, coordinating activities for target communities & facilities, and reviewing and verifying equipment and supplies to be procured; to ensure alignment with common frameworks and cooperation plans i.e. Every Newborn Action Plan (ENAP 2017-2020).
The project objectives and activities are aligned with the 2020 health cluster HRP strategy, including responsive and remedial components and is guided by the UNFPA Arab States regional framework for resilience and the UN strategy for Hebron and latest OCHA multi-sectoral needs assessment. UNFPA aims to improve access to RH services and information, through a strengthened continuum of care, MISP implementation at the primary health care level, support to mobile clinics, reinforcement of referral pathways, increase of community awareness and preparedness for emergencies, and increase of resilience for women, men and girls in these communities.

1 - Needs

The challenging situation in Palestine is well recognized as a protracted crisis withhumanitarian consequences, driven by a lack of respect for international law by theOccupation and its measures to prevent Palestinians from attaining quality standards ofliving and access to resources, including health care. Palestinian women, men, girls andboys in the West Bank, especially in seam zones, H2, Area C of Hebron and Bethlehem,face a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life,settler violence, house demolitions, and liberty and security. The effects of the occupationare far reaching and include severe repercussions on RH. GBV, as an integral componentof RH, continues to be a key protection and health concern in Palestine, as it exacerbatesthe already existing vulnerabilities of women and girls’ and increases the pressure onalready limited health resources, including life sustaining RH services and information.According to OCHA’s vulnerability analysis, there are 532 Palestinian communities(300,000 people) located in Area C. The OCHA report highlights lack of healthinfrastructure and adequately trained staff, long distances to health facilities, limitedopening hours, and high cost of transportation to facilities as the key barriers to addressin order to ensure access to healthcare. In this regard, the geographic distribution ofcommunities in southern West Bank, with Bethlehem, Hebron and south Hebrongovernorates being most affected, indicates high vulnerability. This includes 98communities (60,310 people) with a medium or high vulnerability profile living inBethlehem and Hebron (H2 and area C). Women and mothers in remote locations,especially in Area C, H2, East Jerusalem and outside the wall have particular difficulties inaccessing essential health care, including maternal health care and information andprotection services. A study by the Palestinian Family Planning and ProtectionAssociation (PFPPA) found that while the national antenatal care coverage is 99%,antenatal care coverage in south Hebron is only 70%. In addition, prevalence of anemia ishigh among pregnant women (38%) and lactating mothers (28%), which poses seriousshort and long term threats to the health and well-being of the mother and child.Previously, many communities received assistance from mobile clinics supported by MOHand humanitarian actors. However, according to the Health Cluster, there are now 165 communities (162,663 people) in need of primary healthcare services, compared to 2019 where there were 77,002 people in need of services. This leaves women, children and peopleliving with disabilities at particularly increased risk, particularly in health emergencies, includingchildbirth, heart attacks, and accidents. Access to quality emergency obstetric care is alsohindered, as hospitals in Hebron, particularly Alia Hospital, Muhtaseb in H2, and Yattahospitals, suffer from significantly restricted capacity to cope with high caseloads andreport occupancy rates beyond 100%. In most cases, mothers and infants are notsystematically assessed, nor is the mother properly counselled, partly due to lack ofessential ICU and maternity ward equipment. According to the 2020 HNO, there are 29,500 people in need of life-saving secondary health services in H2, including safe delivery. The need to strengthen the linkage betweencommunities, primary care, and hospitals to ensure adequate lifesaving obstetric care iscrucial. Without intervention, the lack of access to quality RH services and information,including maternal health care, nutrition, protection from GBV, and other RH needs, in theWest Bank, a protracted humanitarian crisis, not only negatively impacts the health andwellbeing of women and children, but causes significant short and long term financial andsocial strain on families and health systems leading to decreased resilience to crises.

2 - Activities

This project will strengthen the preparedness and capacity of the National Health System, UNFPA, and IPs will target the most vulnerable women in marginalized areas in Bethlehem, Jenin, and Hebron (H2 and area C). The project will equip health service providers with basic emergency skills, life- saving supplies, and commodities to strengthen the response and resilience to life threatening conditions. The project, will fill gaps in the continuum of care using holistic approach, focusing on strengthening linkages between the target facilities and the communities, as to increase access and utilization of services by the target group, through the following domains; -Strengthen access of people with focus on “pregnant and lactating women, and girls” to life saving sexual and reproductive, including maternal health services, counselling and information, throughout the continuum of care linking communities to primary health care centers to maternities/hospitals in target areas in H2 and Area C of Bethlehem, Hebron, and Jenin. (Response) -Improve Health facilities capacities, provide equipment/supplies, operationalize mobile clinics services and training of staff on life saving skills (the services shall be coordinated with IPs and local actors, villages councils in target communities or annexed to ongoing mobile clinics by other actors etc. Mobile clinic staff will be trained on detection and referral of GBV. UNFPA is not planning to purchase vehicles for mobile clinics. (Response) -Support networks of community activists and implement community based reproductive health interventions “campaigns” including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspective via educating families and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of healthy outcomes and reproduction, strengthening resilience and positive coping mechanisms, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies. (Remedial) Activities: -Provision of basic essential lifesaving drugs, medical equipment, and supplies to hospitals and primary health care facilities in the targeted Area C communities in Bethlehem, Jenin, and Hebron/H2. UNFPA shall focus on facilities that would work as referral points, recommended by MOH and those were supported by UNFPA before" i.e. Alia, Yatta, Beit Jala and others (i.e. Muhtaseb). -Operationalization of mobile clinics (MC) providing the national standardized package of services for mobile clinics (primary health care level 2), including ANC, PNC, FP services, counselling and other RH, maternal health care in the targeted communities. The mobile health clinic teams are trained in providing care for people living with disability and conduct home visits. -Implementation of minimal initial service package for RH (MISP) at primary health care clinics and targeted communities. -Supporting referral pathways linking communities, operating MCs to PHC and maternities in the catchment areas. (Reducing 2nd delay) -Training of health providers on (MISP), emergency preparedness, emergency obstetric and maternal care and referral.(Reducing 3rd delay) -Community awareness sessions targeting women, men, young boys and girls on RH issues, including danger signs of pregnancy, family planning, breast cancer and self-breast exam, GBV protection and emergency preparedness. (Reducing 1st delay) UNFPA shall coordinate with all stakeholders providing mobile clinic services, including WHO and NGOs, toensure the coordination in targeting selected communities, operations and scope of MCs. Note: the final list of communities, facilities to be targeted, scope of support shall be endorsed by MOH and after coordination with the Health Cluster, UNICEF, WHO, upon implementation, to accommodate emerging needs and changes and ensure complementarity.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Yes, do no harm is a central component of this project. When dealing with sensitive issues, such as SRH, it is critical to include Do No Harm principles, which is always included in UNFPA programming. For example, all providers will be trained in safe GBV detection and referral, using the principle of do no harm. This includes collection, storage, and use of data.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project ensure equal access to aid through, for example, home visits for people living with disabilities. For the mobile clinics, the providers visit the homes of the people who are not able to travel to the mobile clinic for reasons of disability.
