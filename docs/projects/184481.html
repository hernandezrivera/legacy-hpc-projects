Project Summary

As the humanitarian situation in Somalia worsens, so does the plight of the displaced and displacement affected population in the country. Currently, the Humanitarian Needs Overview points to a growing number of persons in need with 7.7 million expected to be in need in 2022. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced with children accounting for 64%, women 16%, men 16% and the elderly 4%. More concerning is the existing weak camp and shelter infrastructure in the country with 3.6million people reported to be in need of Shelter and NFI. In the South especially where issues of land tenure and consequent forced evictions are rampant, IDPs continue to face the brunt of inadequate service provision and heightened protection risks. In fact, data from the Somalia Protection Monitoring System (SPMS) shows forced evictions as one of the main causes of secondary displacements and that IDPs, persons with disabilities, marginalized groups, and adolescent girls as being the highest impacted by exclusion from assistance. These groups report that access to humanitarian assistance is hampered by gate keepers forcing many to overly rely on INGOs in the field to provide more support.
With the triple threat of COVID 19, Conflict, and the negative impacts of climate change, many IDPs now find themselves caught up in repeated cycles of either/both climate-induced and/or conflict-induced displacement. In the Southern parts of Somalia especially in the conflict hotspots of Jubaland, SWS, and Galmudug, IDPs are forced to move and settle in neighboring camps and settlements. These camps and settlements are often overcrowded with poor quality of existing infrastructure, inadequate sanitation facilities, inadequate IDP settlement design, and heightened protection risks. Displacement further carries additional risks such as family separation which exposes separated families at further risk as they often lack the necessary safety nets such as secure housing during displacement. Furthermore, flash floods common in Southern region lead to destruction of infrastructure in established IDP sites.
Informed by these realities in Somalia, DRC proposes to work to support IDPs in Banadir, Galmudug, Hirshabelle, Jubaland, South West State, and Puntland through establishing and strengthening multi-sectoral services through improved site management and coordination, strengthening information services and sharing mechanisms, and supporting decongestion initiatives. Activities to be implemented will include conducting monthly eviction risk mapping exercises, conducting site verification exercises, service mapping of partners working in IDP sites, collaborating with the government and target communities to develop contingency plans for future IDP influxes, conduct site coordination meetings, building capacity on existing governance structures, supporting site improvement activities through cash for work, procure mobility aids for persons living with disabilities, conducting safety audits, providing training to Camp Management Committees (CMCs), and raise awareness and information sharing within the sites on various issues such as hotline numbers, partners present, and services available. The project aims to reach 552,325 flood and conflict displaced women, men, boys and girls in Somalia while also supporting communities in IDP sites towards self-reliance especially in areas where land tenures are stable.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash has been selected as the preferred transfer modality for a specific component of this project which is the site improvement work. This cash, understood as being conditional to work undertaken, will not only provide a source of income for beneficiaries but will also allow them actively participate in the project ensuring buy in and a sense of ownership in the long run. This cash provides the added benefit of i) improving IDP sites, ii) improving access to cash which enables beneficiaries purchase their goods and services, iii) stimulates the local market economy and iv) allows beneficiaries prioritize their own needs and improving a sense of dignity among targeted populations.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The current needs and gaps in camp management and camp coordination in Somalia points towards the need to support decongestion of current IDP sites as well as improving existing infrastructure within these sites. Furthermore, DRC having worked in the proposed target locations previously, has assessed the prevailing market conditions to determine the likely impact that cash transfers could have on the local economy. Cash transfers were thus deemed to be beneficial as the local markets are well stocked with common basic goods that beneficiaries could use the money to purchase. Furthermore, this increasing purchasing power will stimulate increase in demand and supply which will stimulate local economic growth.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The risks identified, including the risk of exposure of boys and girls to violence caused by living in highly insecure settlements and the risk of dispute on land ownership. To mitigate against these risks, DRC will mainstream protection throughout all phases of the project cycle and in the implementation of the project ensuring that all members of the community and site residents are consulted and their specific needs addressed. DRC will also hold separate focus group discussions and meetings with boys and girls, and women to encourage feedback which will be used to tailor the project. DRC as a member of various clusters in Somalia will use these avenues as advocacy avenues to highlight the plight of insecure land tenures while urging the government and other local stakeholders to play a role in providing durable solutions for IDPs. During the rollout of cash for work, DRC will ensure participation of women and men as well as ensure that site maintenance activities are agreed upon prior with local communities. DRC has a presence and other active interventions in the targeted locations with a close relationship and consultation with the community members, local authorities, partners, and other service provides. this will help to be easily sorted out in case of any issues. DRC has also a strong community feedback mechanism in place which enables to get information about the possible risks and conflicts before it happens. This will also support solving any issues or taking mitigation measures.
