Project Summary

Households unable to meet needs and facing significant income/expenditure gaps; Depreciation of the SYP undermines household purchasing power; There are confirmed lack of access to jobs, and services; Therefore households are relying on negative coping strategies (ERL, Chapter for HNO 2024). According to the latest shared WFP Syria Price Bulletin reflecting November 2023, the monthly minimum wage (185,940 SYP) covers only 20% of the total cost of the Standard Food Basket (920,157 SYP); the cost of living, as measured by the Minimum Expenditure Basket, reached SYP 2,307,201 for a family of five. November's MEB indicated a twofold increase in the cost of living within the year 2023 alone and a fourfold rise over a two-year period, while real wages remained stagnant, despite the doubling of the minimum wage in August 2023. AAH assessments and studies in Hama governorate confirmed the gap between what the households are able to generate in income and the significant cost needed for accessing basic food and non food needs. Households are applying negative coping strategies to cope with the lack of income to access basic needs. AAH has secured fund over 3 years including 2023/24/and 2025 for providing 100 women with vocational and skill-building training (theoretical and practical) in food/dairy processing, preservation, and manufacturing for the purpose of producing local and traditional market-based types of Syrian food. A grant will be given to the females in order to start their small businesses. The grant will be a conditional and restricted grant and will be split over two tranches, women need to attend vocational training in order to get the first tranche and later on, they need to spend the money on purchasing what they need to have from equipment, raw materials, and source of energy for applying what they have learned in producing market based Syrian foods, in order to receive the second tranche. The training will include a session on the establishment and management of their small businesses. AAH will ensure Accountability to the Affected Population by including them in all phases of the project, consulting them about the intervention, and sharing information; Community Feedback and Response Mechanism is set by AAH through different channels. AAH staff and volunteers are training on the code of conduct and PSEA policy. AAH will ensure proper monitoring in order to mitigate risks related to Protection and PSEA. Assumptions are related to having the project’s approval on time by the concerned line ministries. The security situation is stable; safe access will be guaranteed. Increasing tension between BENs will be mitigated by ensuring accountability to the affected population considering gender and age aspects. All assistance provided by AAH will be provided in a safe and dignified manner.
