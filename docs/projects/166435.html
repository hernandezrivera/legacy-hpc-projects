Project Summary

The project aims at providing integrated CCCM, shelter, NFIs in the response to the outbreak of COVID-19 in Borno state. To assist and prevent the spread of the virus by integrating CCCM/Shelter/NFI approach across MMC, Jere, Rann.
This project will target 40,000 beneficiaries for the CCCM support, Shelter construction/repair, shelter reinforcement/emergency shelter kit and NFI kits to fill gaps in IDP camps situated in Jere, MMC and Rann.
More so, their poor living conditions does not allow any social distancing hence the high risk of contraction of COVID-19 and consequently even the protection of the most vulnerable (mostly single women and children) is not guaranteed.
Furthermore, the CCCM project will improve the quality of management of the women, men, boys and girls IDPs within the camp settings to coordinate, monitor and assist those out of camps and some vulnerable persons in host community in prevention of COVID-19. The project will support systematic follow up of the IDPs and returnee’s new arrivals particularly vulnerable women and girls. The project seeks to provide quality services during the registration process and will ensure that reception facilities are equally accessible to women, men, boys and girls newly arrived and kept in isolation centres. Camp managers will ensure proper relocation of new arrivals from the reception centres/ self-isolation centres to the shelters constructed taking into account the needs of the most vulnerable. Through this project, CCCM interventions will also provide monitoring and making referrals, sensitization, awareness creation etc for prevention of COVID-19 and report on all protection gaps and needs to be addressed by different sectors in order to improve the living conditions of women, men, girls and boys displaced. The CCCM project will promote the participation of the IDPs based on age, gender and diversity in the life cycle of camps particularly care and maintenance of infrastructures, awareness sessions, advocacy etc. In case of Rann, the Reception center will be reactivated providing the basic needs of new Arrivals.
As the case of COVID-19 increases in Borno state, more preventive measures need to be put in place to reduce the risk and provide mitigation, in doing so, community workers will be greatly utilize for achieving this purpose. And also working closely with sector guidelines for COVID-19;

management staff or through the Community Volunteers.
• Camp sanitation through daily disinfecting and cleaning the camp led by community volunteers
and community groups.
• Promoting hand washing stations through ensuring hand washing stations are stationed and
functional in all camp entry points and focal areas. Continuous and increased messaging will be
carried out with communities about handwashing beyond the 5 critical times to ensure it takes
place before and after touching any communal surface. (i.e before touching the latrines door
handle, before touching the water stand tap, etc.) training and awareness will support this
activity
• Community Engagements; all messaging will be done at household level, while still maintaining
a distance of at least 1meter between the CCCM partner and the participants as directed by
the WaSH sector

1 - Needs

According to DTM for Round 31, 2,046,604 or 420,072 households were recorded as displaced, an increase of 7,512 persons against the last assessment conducted in December 2019 when 2,039,092 internally displaced persons (IDPs) were identified. Also, a total of 1,673,862 returnees were recorded in the DTM Round 31 assessment, an increment of 62,186 or 4 per cent from the 1,611,676 persons that were identified as returnees in the last round of assessment that was conducted in December 2019. The increase in number of IDPs indicates a continued plateauing in numbers of displaced persons in the region over the last couple of rounds. As per the Round 29 assessment that was published in November 2019, 2,035,232 IDPs were recorded. A similar trend was observed in previous rounds of assessment since August 2019. The number of displaced persons in the region is now well above the number recorded in Round 25 (2,026,602), which was conducted before escalating violence was observed in October 2018 eventhough accessibility remains lower. During Round 25, a higher number of Local Government Areas (LGAs or districts) and wards were accessible. Given that the numbers of IDPs is increasing slowly although accessibility remains low, it can be inferred that the actual displacement figures could be much higher. To gain insights into the profiles of IDPs, interviews were conducted with 4 per cent of the identified IDP population — 86,268 displaced persons — during this round of assessments. The information collated and analysed in this report includes the reasons for displacement, places of origin and shelter types, mobility patterns, and unfulfilled needs of the displaced populations. Additionally, site assessments were conducted in 2,372 locations (down from 2,375 in the last round of assessment, conducted in December 2019). The purpose was to better understand the gaps in services provided and the needs of the affected population. These sites included 290 (down from 293 in the last round of assessment) camps and camp-like settings and 2,082 locations (no change since the last assessment) where IDPs were residing with host communities. Site assessments included an analysis of sector-wide needs, including shelter and non-food items, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), food and nutrition, health, education, livelihood, security, communication and protection.Also, due to the global COVID-19 pandemic which is ravaging the world, with total number of cases raising to over 2000 as at the 05 of may 2020. There is need for holistic response and approach towards prevention and responding to out break;The CCCM Technical Working Group has since adapted the recommendation of the IASC guidelines for COVID-19 outbreak readiness and response in camp and camp-like settings, UN Nigeria recommended guidelines on COVID, WHO Covid-19 guidelines and the federal government guidance’s on COVID-19 in addition to its sector specific recommendations. As such a tailored preparedness, preventive and response plan will be applied continuous provision of essential lifesaving humanitarian assistance as well as compliment the support services of the health emergency to cover COVID-19, Lasa fever and cholera outbreak preparedness and response planning and implementation.Existing coordination mechanisms will be used including the ISWG, sector TWGs and close collaboration with relevant sectors such as Health, WASH, Education, Protection, Food Security and Livelihoods, to streamline the information shared and preparedness.The sector has categorized high priority LGAs and camps in view of the congestion levels, population numbers, camp management and site facilitation capacity. 9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SHO has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. SHO will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, on CCCM activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to SHO’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. SHO will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. SHO’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable SHO to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. SHO will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project. Lastly, SHO's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance.
