Project Summary

The distribution of NFI activities include:
I. Provision of core NFIs to families in northwest Syria through IOM’s Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) in coordination with the SNFI and CCCM clusters (activity 1.1.1.1) through both in-kind and cash modalities.
II. Provision of procurement, storage and logistics support to humanitarian partners in Syria under the Single Source Procurement (SSP) pipeline (activity 1.1.1.1).
II. Provision of seasonal NFI winter assistance (activity 1.1.1.2) through both in-kind and cash modalities.

3 - Needs

Displacements due to extreme poverty and hyperinflation have become major humanitarian challenges in NWS. Nearly two-thirds (61%) of all displacements occurring in August 2022 were a result of the deteriorating economic situation in the location of origin, and a further 37 per cent of displacements occurred due to a perceived increase in livelihood opportunities in their new location (HNAP Monthly Mobility and Needs Monitoring reports - August 2022). Currently, 1.7 million people are residing in over 1,400 unplanned, self-established IDP settlements (Northwest Syria: Unmet Shelter Needs). These camps are severely overcrowded, with most families living in old tents or makeshift shelters, often with also no access to WASH or health facilities. According to the S/NFI Cluster, 54 per cent of people report cold and damp shelter conditions, 38 per cent report a lack of heating, and 33 per cent report a lack of space as their main shelter issues (Unmet Shelter Needs Advocacy Paper). Recent winters have been exceptionally cold and wet, with massive snowfall, widespread flooding, and sub-zero temperatures. In March 2022, 19 displacement sites were affected by floods, 217 sites by strong winds, and six sites by heavy snowfall, damaging over 3,000 tents and destroying 2,400 others (Situation Report NWS 20 Apr 2022). Based on recent trends and forecasting, the humanitarian situation is likely to be similar during the upcoming winter.

4 - Implementation of activities

IOM will implement activities 1.1.1.2 and 1.2.1.1 through the SNFI Cluster Rapid Response Mechanism, which uses a pool of preselected IPs with proven SNFI capacity to respond to urgent SNFI needs with NFIs, tents and cash. Activity 1.1.1.1 will be implemented in part through the RRM, with other stocks being shipped to partners through the Single Source Procurement Pipeline. IOM will coordinate with the S/NFI Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure needs are addressed and there is no duplication. IPs will select locations and beneficiaries in coordination with clusters on the basis of emerging needs and vulnerability assessments and will be verified by TPMs. For cash, IOM will request market assessments prior to approval. PDMs will be conducted for both activities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

In NFI/cash distributions, tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees is a risk. IOM will mitigate this by involving all stakeholders, communicating the selection criteria, distributing according to verified needs, and establishing a complaint mechanism. Given the unstable security situation in NWS, there is the risk that conflict may impede humanitarian access to certain locations. IOM continuously monitors the security situation and has contingency plans in place, and IOM and IPs maintain communication with local authorities. Additionally, some of the access risks associated with transshipments could be mitigated through the use of cash modalities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All IP staff are required to have undertaken training on PSEA and signed a PSEA Code of Conduct. Details of IOM’s AAP mechanism are communicated to participants, as well as the Interagency PSEA hotline where appropriate. Gender segregated lines and gender-mixed distribution teams are always used by IPs to further mitigate SEA risks. As IOM is the leading agency for PSEA, all programmes are required to follow the network’s recommendations and incorporate them into each project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks include access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines, aid diversion by armed groups, loss of assets, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, inflation of market prices and COVID-19. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will be in regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents and conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations. IOM also has a dedicated access unit that monitors and facilitates principled access for partners within Syria, and IOM will continue to participate in OCHA’s access working group. On aid diversion and loss of assets, IOM will ensure warehouses are securely guarded and give awareness sessions to staff on how to handle attempted aid diversion, while also ensuring that IPs provide timely incident reports to its access unit and share the information with relevant partners. For a potential closure of Bab Al-Hawa, IOM can preposition items inside Syria and will work with the broader humanitarian community to further mitigate the risk of non-renewal. To mitigate the impact of inflation on cash recipients, IOM will conduct strong and constant market monitoring and will distribute USD to prevent currency devaluation. Additionally, there is the risk that beneficiaries will sell in-kind items or use cash for unintended purposes which IOM will mitigate by tailoring cash to SMEB needs and distributing items to those with verified need of S/NFIs but whose basic needs are already met.
