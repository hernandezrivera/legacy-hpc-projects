Project Summary

This project aims at improving and creating better educational environments, via the rehabilitation of the war-torn infrastructure, for the crisis-affected school-aged children of IDP, non-displaced, and returnee families, across Cyrenaica, Tripolitania, and Fezzan regions and enhance their well-being and personal development, via the provision of child protection services and referral to adequate psychosocial support.

Needs

Education in Libya as well as the educational infrastructure have been deeply impacted by the war. According to 2014 Save the Children “Education under attack” report, during the 2011 uprising against the Gaddafi regime, armed groups used over 200 schools. In addition, based on the same report for 2018, several explosives including aerial bombings, car bombs, and grenades damaged and destroyed over 1,900 schools. According to the Ministry of Education in Tripoli and Benghazi, as of November 2016, the crisis affected 558 schools hindering education for 279,000 students.Serious water and sanitation needs have been identified in several Libyan schools. According to a UNICEF/Ministry of Education ‘Assessment of Water Quality, Sanitation, and Hygiene in Schools’, 67% of surveyed schools have limited drinking water. In addition, 10 % of schools were contaminated with harmful bacteria, and 31% of schools tested positive for high levels of nitrates in the water. The lack of functional toilets in some schools is a major need to be addressed, moreover in the schools where there are functional toilets, the average number of students per functional toilet is 71, while the Ministry of Education standards are 1 toilet per 25 students. According to UNICEF Libya annual report 2017, the situation of many children in Libya is highly precarious, with child rights violations being continuously reported. Emotional and physical violence against children is an important concern in the Libya, also in places that should be safe for children, such as schools and their homes. All over the country there is an observable lack of children friendly spaces and recreational opportunities for children, their caregivers and the communities they live in. This reduces the possibility to identify vulnerable children suffering from distress or trauma and to refer them to specialized psychosocial services.

Activities

ACTED will implement school rehabilitation works to provide children in conflict affected and previously conflict-affected areas with improved educational infrastructures. During previous interventions ACTED has undertaken needs assessments in the 3 targeted regions (Participatory need assessment in Tripoli and Nafuza mountain region and Benghazi and greater Benghazi area conducted before the implementation of UNCHR funded QIPs intervention in 2018; Local Perceptions of Humanitarian Needs in Southern Libya , conducted jointly with PUI in 2018 and still ongoing). Nevertheless, the specific needs to be addressed and the schools to be targeted will be confirmed through a participative approach that foresees the conduction of 6 FGDs (2 per region) with key stakeholders (community leaders, representatives of local institutions etc.). Drawing from the results of the FGD, at least 12 Schools (4 per region on average) will benefit from rehabilitation works conducted by ACTED and provision of essential equipment that will aim at improving the overall learning environment. However, based on the needs mentioned in the previous session the project will prioritize water and sanitation works. Moreover, ACTED will identify and support at maximum 8 holistic community based safe spaces for children and their caregivers that will be used to implement tangible activities, including recreational activities, awareness raising on relevant protection risks faced by children, child rights. Moreover, ACTED will provide psychological first aid (PFA). These spaces will be recognized thorough needs assessments conducted with local partners and communities. The assessments will also take into account the kind of materials that would be needed for these safe spaces.The spaces will be located in neighborhoods which can be safely and easily accessible to all target populations of children and which can add to social cohesion amongst communities. ACTED will prioritize Est (with a particular focus on Benghazi, Ejdabiya and Derna) and South regions for the establishment of the safe spaces, but depending on the needs identified and the possibility to have access to target areas some spaces might be also established in the West. The safe spaces will be provided with the equipment needed and recreation kits will be distributed to the children registered and will be open to Libyan and non-Libyan having access to schools. To make this programming sustainable, ACTED, where possible, will rely on its community based approach to work with local service providers in a referral system to provide specialized child protection services and psycho-social support, wherever the context allows us to do the same. To strengthen further the protection environment that communities are living in, the actual spaces will be supplemented by thematic awareness raising sessions and training addressed to parents, caregivers, community members and local service providers emphasizing the importance of the safe spaces and the services available through the referral system. Training for both service providers and school counselors will revolve core themes such as around child protection, child rights and SGBV, mine risk education, Psychological First Aid, , community outreach activities, non-discrimination and inclusivity, case management, identification of at risk cases and referral system etcDuring the project implementation linkages with other agencies working on similar topics ( such us IOM; UNHCR; UNICEF) will be ensured through coordination meetings and information sharing . Moreover, giver the inter-sectorial nature of the projects regular communication and coordination will be ensured with the different sectors and working groups that cover the areas of intervention of the project ( WASH, Health, Education, Protection, child protection etc.)

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

ACTED AME team will elaborate a checklist and will conduct regular spot checks in the targeted schools in order to measure assess safety and dignity of beneficiaries. Moreover a baseline and an endline survey will be conducted to assess the impact of the intervention on beneficiaries. Furthermore, ACTED understands that it is important that awareness raising activities and Focus Group Discussion (FGDs) are conducted with community representatives and beneficiary groups to gain in depth knowledge of the protective environment that beneficiaries live in and to ensure that the response offered is in line with the needs identified. Through ACTED’s C CRM (complaints response mechanisms) beneficiaries will also have the opportunity to submit their complaints through hotlines, sms, Viber and email. According to ACTED procedures the complaints will be addressed with a maximum delay of 15 days. ACTED adopts a zero tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. ACTED is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within the organization and within the framework of ACTED programmes and ACTED beneficiary populations.In this context, the aim of ACTED’s PSAE policy is to define expressly sexual exploitation and abuse, to set up a reporting process in the case of a violation of the rules and a specific disciplinary sanction procedure based on the seriousness of the acts committed.
