Project Summary

The project will aim at strengthening the capacities of the National Child Protection and Community Child Protection Mechanisms in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem in responding to the rehabilitation and reintegration needs of children affected by Military Detention Trauma. The project will specifically support the successful reintegration of 210 ex-detainee Palestinian children within their families and local communities through psycho-social and legal support. It will also involve and benefit 70 ministerial employees and 70 Community-Based Organization and NGO staff members through capacity-building activities.

Needs

Approximately 800-1,000 children are detained each year, and children as young as 12 years can be prosecuted in the military courts. Approximately half of all detained children are arrested at night and report physical and psychological abuse during arrest, transfer, and interrogation (according to Military Court Watch). Approximately 50% of Palestinian child detainees are held in prisons in Israel in breach of Article 76 of the Fourth Geneva Convention. Violations against minors include late night arrests, being transferred to interrogation and detention center without their parents` knowledge, questioning for long periods of time without a lawyer, rest, or food and drink. Confessions are often extracted under threats and intimidation, usually written in Hebrew, a language they do not understand. According to the Israeli Prison Service data published by Military Court Watch, at the end of March 2018, 273 Palestinian minors were held in Israeli prisons as security detainees and prisoners - 50 of them being under-16. Even after release, ex-detainee children have little access to psycho-social support and often drop out of school, meaning the impacts of their arrest affect them for years to come. (Exceptions are children who are part of Save the Children`s Rehabilitation and Reintegration program for child ex-detainees, implemented together with the YMCA). According to Testimonies collected by Military court (Military Court Watch, briefing note May 2017 - http://www.militarycourtwatch.org/files/server/BRIEFING%20PAPER%20-%20MAY%202017.pdf):• 93 percent of children report being hand tied upon arrest and frequently remain tied for extended periods including during interrogation; in some cases their hands become swollen or bleed. • Children continue to be shackled by the ankles during military court appearances. In 76 percent of cases where children are restrained, the evidence indicates non-compliance with the military's own standard operating procedures introduced in 2010. • 80 percent of children report being blindfolded or hooded upon arrest in disregard of a recommendation made by UNICEF that this practice should be prohibited in all circumstances. • 63 percent of children report being subjected to various forms of physical abuse during arrest, transfer and/or interrogation. The types of reported abuse includes: head-butting, kicking, beating, punching, struck with objects including weapons; and the deliberate over-tightening of restraints. Once released from prison, children in general develop common symptoms that include: low self-esteem, depression, lack of trust, poor communication skills especially with family members, insecurity, immense fear, loneliness, nightmares, anxiety and sleeping disorders to name a few. Further, it was observed that the impact of a child’s detention results had a negative impact on parents and the social cohesion of the family as a whole. More than the 90% of the total population of ex-detainee children is boys; girls however face additional challenges in their reintegration into their families and communities due to the stigma associated with detention. The relevant ministries of the Palestinian Authority in charge of the protection of ex-detainee children, among which the Commission of Detainees and ex-Detainees (CDED) and the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), are not yet able to provide effective and systematic support to child ex-detainees. This results from the still precarious effectiveness of the Child Protection Networks and the National referral Mechanism for Child Protection; the fund limitations and staff capacity.

Activities

The project will aim at strengthening the capacities of the National Child Protection (CP) and Community CP Mechanisms in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem in responding to the rehabilitation and reintegration needs of children affected by Military Detention Trauma. The project will support the successful reintegration of ex-detainee Palestinian children within their families and local communities. It will target 210 ex-detainee children with psycho-social (PS) support (PSS) activities; among them, 50 children with legal support during detention. In addition, 105 family members/parents will benefit from PSS. The project will also involve 70 ministerial employees and 70 Community-Based Organization and NGO staff members. Activities will include: Output: Strengthening resilience and capacity of ex-detainee children to cope with trauma Identification of new cases of detainee and ex-detainee children and outreach visits to families to identify the needs with the participation of the child and familyEvaluation of child's trauma and PS condition; inclusion in the case management systemIndividual and/or group PSS for ex-detainee childrenRecreational activities for ex-detainee childrenReintegration services including support for the return to school through catch-up classes and vocational trainings to facilitate access to the labour marketLegal counselling and assistance for detainee children to be included in the rehabilitation programmeWorkshops with school counselors and children to raise awareness on detention, its impact and rights in detention. Output: Improvement of families` and communities` awareness and ability to support the social reintegration of child ex-detaineesIdentification of new cases and evaluation of the PS condition of the families of ex-detainee childrenIndividual and group PSS for the families of ex-detainee childrenRecreational days for the families of ex-detainee children and creation of community support peer support groups Output: Improvement of the child protection system in the West Bank and East Jerusalem Capacity building of the CDED, MSoD, MoE and Ministry of Labour to strengthen their capacity to respond to the needs of ex-detainee childrenImproving the coordination between relevant Ministries and NGOs members of the CP networks to consolidate the referral mechanism to the services in charge with reintegration of ex-detainee childrenParticipation and support to the UNICEF-led CP Working Group and the technical committee on child military detention. The project will have two implementing partners: East Jerusalem Young Men Christian Association (EJ – YMCA): has been working in partnership with SC since 2007 and will focus on the rehabilitation and reintegration programme for ex-detainee Palestinian children. EJ-YMCA has developed a unique expertise in this sector and their field offices in all governorates of the West Bank have become the main address for the ex-detainee children and their families. Defence for Children – Palestine (DCI-PS): will work in partnership with SCI-oPt and EJ-YMCA on the rehabilitation and reintegration programme for detainee and ex-detainee children in Israeli prisons to provide legal assistance and collect and report data on violations. DCI is lead actor in the provision of legal services to children experiencing detention; since 2017 they are involved in a collective effort to improve the quality of legal representation for children in Military Courts and have provided trainings for lawyers of different Palestinian agencies on quality standards. SC has been working with ex-detainees in the target locations since 2008 and has secured access for the implementation. SC has coordinated this project with TdH Switzerland (Lausanne) and MdM Switzerland to ensure there are no overlaps in terms of geographic coverage offering the same services. SC will continue liaising with both partners to avoid duplications should there be any changes in locations.

Indicators

A comprehensive M&E plan will be developed and disaggregated data will be collected over the course of the project. Data will be collected using tried and tested methods including surveys, focus groups and interviews.
