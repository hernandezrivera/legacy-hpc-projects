Project Summary

The project aims to support the access and retention of children with disabilities in formal and non-formal learning environments in Northern Syria through a twin-track approach in the context of Inclusive Education in Emergency (IEiE) which consists, on the one hand, in making educational services more inclusive by reducing existing barriers that prevent the inclusion and retention of children within existing educational programs and, on the other hand, in providing personalized/tailored support to respond to the specific educational needs of children with disabilities identified.
Protection Risk Analysis: INGO prioritizes staff safety with low visibility, employing a Duty of Care policy during healthcare attacks. PSEA policy is applied, ensuring accountability through investigations. Training addresses vulnerabilities of persons with disabilities, older individuals, and girls/women. Protection and referral task forces optimize mainstreaming.. Safe facility closure and data protection measures are implemented. Implementing partners undergo thorough assessments, with reporting, monitoring, and feedback mechanisms in place.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy The INGO is committed to combating sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Robust policies and a Code of Conduct are in place, with mandatory and refresher training. Recruitment includes background checks, and PSEAH policies apply to staff, partners, and accompanying individuals. Information sessions for beneficiaries, complaint mechanisms, and gender balance in staff address cultural sensitivity. Any partner involved in abuse faces case follow-up and.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures risks: Risks in the intervention include cross-border resolution uncertainty, security deterioration, incidents involving staff, aid diversion, and currency depreciation impacting budget. The Syrian pound depreciation and essential goods price surge affect livelihoods. Mitigation involves conflict analysis, contingency planning, security guidelines, advocacy for border access, stock securing, donor communication, and capacity building for local staff. Effective coordination with authorities and diversified pre-selected suppliers helps avoid price fluctuations.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Violent conflicts and disasters disrupt lives, especially affecting children who are highly vulnerable to trauma. Without proper tools and safe spaces, their development can be severely impacted. Accessible learning environments, like schools, play a crucial role in providing protection and promoting positive development. Schools offer a sense of normalcy, routine, and opportunities for life-saving education on topics like WASH practices and inclusivity. This project aims to create a safer learning environment for all children, including those with disabilities, prioritizing the well-being of every individual. Protection actions focus on preventing violence, supporting survivors, and addressing psychosocial issues. Inclusive education will incorporate protection mechanisms to support children with disabilities and their families. The organization is dedicated to combating sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment through robust policies, training, background checks, and accountability measures. Cultural sensitivity is emphasized through information sessions, complaint mechanisms, and gender balance in staff. Any partner involved in abuse will face termination consequencesThe main activities that will be implemented by the INGO will be:a. Identification of in-school and out-of-school children with disabilities: initial screening of children in and out of school, identifying children with disabilities and those who are at greatest risk of exclusion from a learning system.b. Barrier and facilitator assessments: Evaluate the inclusiveness of the schools and service provisions, including accessibility, social environment, staff and teacher competencies from the perspective of users and providers.c. Community sensitization, mobilization and awareness raising focused on (i) the importance of children attending school and learning opportunities: emphasis on ensuring enrollment of children most vulnerable to exclusion or neglect; (ii) children's rights, child protection risks and psychosocial advice on how to cope with reactions to severe stress or trauma.d. Addressing educational barriers through (i) identification and implementation of safe and inclusive formal and non-formal learning spaces; (ii) teacher training on Inclusive Education; (iii) support to teachers through specific peer support groups and PSS training.e. Direct support to children with disabilities and their families through (i) referral to health (main focus in rehabilitation, PSS and specialized health services), protection and social services; (ii) specific educational support to children with disabilities regarding their needs including inclusive remedial classes and provision of specific adapted materials; provision of assistive devices and adaptive tools
