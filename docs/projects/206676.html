Project Summary

UNICEF is dedicated to collaborating with government entities, the private sector, international and local non-governmental organizations to deliver a comprehensive package of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services. This collaboration aims to provide essential WASH services, items, and facilities to vulnerable displaced populations in camps and host communities as well as returnee zones, prioritizing critical services to prevent disease transmission.
This project includes ensuring access to life-saving WASH services for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) residing both in camps and host communities aiming to mitigate the impact of IDP influx on already strained WASH services in host communities, emphasizing the importance of transitioning from short-term relief to sustainable, resilient solutions that consider present and future climate impacts and support WASH in health care facilities and learning environments. The project also will work towards strengthening local capacities in operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure.
Assistance to returnees focuses on reconstructing pre-existing water and sanitation infrastructure while promoting appropriate hygiene practices. During disease outbreaks, particularly Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Cholera, UNICEF promptly responds by providing life-saving WASH services and supplies, closely coordinating efforts with the health sector.
An integral part of the strategy involves gender mainstreaming, with a special focus on Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention and risk mitigation. This emphasis is geared towards ensuring the safety and resilience of girls and women through the provision of inclusive and gender-sensitive water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Inclusion mainstreaming will include provision of access to WASH services for people with disabilities.
As WASH sector lead, UNICEF will support to identifying gaps, advocating effectively, responding promptly to critical needs, establishing robust monitoring and evaluation, and fostering knowledge management processes.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will provide access to WASH services to 541,842 IDP people displaced by conflict in the northern provinces of Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula. Access to lifesaving and life-sustaining WASH infrastructure and services will be provided for displaced populations, host communities and individuals returning to their homes. UNICEF will work in partnership with government, private sector, INGOs, and LNGOs.The project includes ensuring access to life-saving WASH services for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) residing both in camps and host communities aiming to mitigate the impact of IDP influx on already strained WASH services in host communities. Assistance to returnees focuses on reconstructing pre-existing water and sanitation infrastructure while promoting appropriate hygiene practices. Special attention will be given to at-risk groups, including women and girls and people with disabilities to ensure access to WASH services. The project will respond to emerging WASH needs due to disease outbreaks.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Ensuring access to Water supply to accommodation centres / transit camps through centralized distribution systems, including water trucking (temporary / first response for shortest possible term), management and operation of water distribution points and monitoring of water quality; Distribution of point-of-use water treatment materials / products, including training and monitoring on proper use at household level. Construction/Rehabilitation/Extension and disinfection of new and existing water points respectively in host communities, accommodation centers, returnees post conflict and reallocation areas, including support for water management committees. Installation and management of temporary sanitation and bathing facilities in accommodation centers/transit camps and / or resettlement sites; Promotion activities for self-construction of household latrines and garbage pits in resettlement sites, hosting communities and returnees’ areas (including the provision of sanitation supplies for most vulnerable households); Solid waste management (including collection, removal, disposal and drainage) in camps, host communities and returnees areas; Implement hygiene promotion activities through community engagement approach to induce behavior change; Procurement and distribution of WASH related Non-Food items, with focus on most vulnerable families /households; Provision of WASH facilities and hygiene promotion in Schools and Health centers; Mainstreaming of gender and inclusion to cover the needs of most vulnerable such as girls, women, and people with disabilities. Ensure effective WASH sector coordination.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Safety audits that aim to identify potential protection risks across gender and age groups will be conducted at the initial phases to discuss strategies for mitigation of these risks with the affected populations. The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and the principle of ‘do no harm’ is at the core of UNICEF’s mandate. For UNICEF staff and non-staff personnel, sexual exploitation and sexual abuse constitute acts of serious misconduct and are therefore grounds for disciplinary measures, including summary dismissal. For entities working with UNICEF that fail to address sexual exploitation and abuse through appropriate prevention measures, investigation and corrective measures, such failure shall constitute grounds for cessation of the cooperative arrangement with UNICEF. To ensure adequate safeguards and appropriate action related to sexual exploitation and abuse, UNICEF’s procedure involves screening, informing, monitoring, and training of partners. Using the 2010 United Nations (UN) human rights council endorsement that access to WASH is a human right, UNICEF will work with partners to ensure that all people have equal access to safe WASH services. To achieve this, UNICEF and partners will work to ensure that communal latrines are gender separated, culturally appropriate with menstrual hygiene health and disability design components and that WASH service delivery points are well lit and accessible.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Gender mainstreaming approaches will be implemented to integrate a gender and age perspective into all phases of the project to ensure that the differing needs, experiences, and roles of both men and women are recognized and addressed throughout the project lifecycle. The project will take into account specific WASH needs and challenges faced women in a community. Facilities will be designed in ways that are safe, accessible, and cater to the needs of women, girls and the elderly, addressing issues like privacy and safety concerns for women and girls, especially in facilities like toilets and bathing areas. Community engagement activities will ensure the involvement of both men and women in decision-making processes related to WASH, this will include the establishment of gender-balanced trained water committees to assist in the dissemination of appropriate hygiene practices, facility maintenance, and management. Gender and age disaggregated data will be collected to track the impact of WASH interventions on different genders and assess whether the project is meeting the needs of both men and women.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

This project will work towards ensuring that people with disabilities feel safe and are protected from discrimination when collecting water or using toilets and bathing facilities, day or night. The project will actively encourage the involvement and engagement of people with disabilities during the planning, design, and implementation stages.People with disabilities will be identified through assessments, followed by consultations to identify the barriers they face in accessing WASH services. These consultations will also aim to determine the specific adaptations required to ensure their access to WASH infrastructure and services, including latrines and water points. Engagement of people with disabilities will be prioritized at all stages when designing accessible WASH facilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNICEF takes measures to deliver its humanitarian action in a manner that minimizes harm to the environment. This includes greenhouse gas emissions, environmental pollution, toxicants and waste. The project aims to the provision of access and use to WASH services and facilities while protecting the environment from contamination. The project will promote green technologies to limit emissions of pollutants and minimize impacts of its activities. Solar water networks will be constructed minimizing CO2 emissions, generators will be used as backup to ensure continuous water supply during days with limited light, such as rainy season/monsoon. Fecal sludge management activities will be focused on reducing the negative impacts on environment and the release of pollutants to air, water and soil and minimize the risk of epidemic outbreaks.
