Project Summary

Adequate and quality nutrition is a child’s basic human right, with well-nourished children having a better chance of survival and longer-term growth, development, health and productivity. WFP Sudan aims to prevent and treat malnutrition by providing specialized nutritious foods to children from 6-59 months as well as Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PBW) among refugee, IDP and vulnerable and conflict-affected population. Geographical targeting of WFP’s treatment program as part of comprehensive Community Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) which informed by a comprehensive analysis of national nutrition survey results (such as S3M II) and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC).
The proposed response from WFP is in line with the Nutrition Sector Strategy and with the Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Strategy of Sudan. Through this project, WFP will be responding to the nutritional needs of targeted beneficiaries under outcomes identified by nutrition sector with particular focus on DO NOT HARM and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles.
During April, fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has caused tremendous human suffering and exacerbated significant pre-existing humanitarian needs amongst the civilian population in Sudan coupled with mas displacement of households which has resulted in an increase of vulnerability to malnutrition that attributed to inflations, reduced access to
food, lack of water and poor hygiene situation as well as increased risk of infections.
1. The deterioration of the nutrition situation has led to an estimated 30% increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition in hotspot areas,15% increase in IDP hosting states and 10% increase in other localities.
2. Interruption of nutrition monitoring and reporting impacting on the understanding of the evolving
nutrition situation in most parts of the country particularly in the hotpot areas.
3. The estimated target and funding requirement is for seven month from June to December 2023.

1 - Needs

The Nutrition Cluster plans to scale up maternal and young child nutrition and scale up Infant and Young Child Feeding practices among children under-fives with prioritization to new hotspot and IDPs areas. Partners will scale up early detection and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children, pregnant and Breastfeeding women in pre-existing responses. Scale up and initiate responses in new emerging hotspot areas including IDP hosting states, focusing on the youngest to prevent increased morbidity and mortality risks.WFP observed the needs of nutrition services among children from 6-59 months and PBW through its regular programme at different nutrition sites and suggested to proceed with actions to mitigate the risk of malnutrition at targeted locations. Currently WFP is aiming to address the need of 683,390 moderately malnourished individuals (480,956 Children from 6 - 59 months and 202,434 PBW) in addition to prevent malnutrition during this emergency, hence a total of 525,496 beneficiaries (Children from 6-59 months = 341,911 and other 183,585 pregnant and breastfeeding women) have been planned for emergency blanket feeding to mitigate shocks of wasting for war-displaced population. To address the needs of micronutrient deficiency which expected during emergency WFP proposed to target 348,089 children under-fives with MNPs. Based on resources and accessibility WFP also has planned to explore routine services of Food-based Prevention of Malnutrition (FBPM) for 955,113 beneficiaries using Plumpy Doz to reduce the risk of malnutrition where accessible

2 - Activities/Outputs

WFP will coordinate with key nutrition stakeholders to meet the sector strategy using different methodologies and address the critical needs of malnourished individuals through the below suggested platforms: 1. Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP): Provides treatment for children aged 6-59 months with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and acutely malnourished pregnant and Breastfeeding women (PBW). Active case finding of acutely malnourished children and Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PBW) takes place at community level. The objectives of TSFP are to: a. To prevent morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition b. To prevent MAM cases from developing SAMc. To improve birth outcomes and maternal nutritional status. 2. Blanket Feeding Programme (e-BSFP): is one of WFP’s nutrition emergency response which aims at mitigating the impact of shocks on the nutritional status of vulnerable groups (children from 6-59 months, Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women) in emergency situation. The objectives are a. Prevention of acute malnutrition through provision of Specialized Nutrition Foods to children from 6- 59 months and PBW b. Increase access to treatment services of acute malnutritionc. Improve knowledge among caretakers/mothers of targeted children on the benefits of prevention and treatment programmes.
