Project Summary

"The consortium led by IRC with members Concern Worldwide and BBC Media Action (the common service) will deliver an integrated program model to respond to the health, nutrition and protection. IRC will implement health and protection part of the project. It will directly implement protection mainstreaming, GBV response and health activities implemented by IRC partners.
The program will leverage the expertise of the existing Common Service, led by BBC Media Action, on communication and community engagement support and tools provided to each of the key focus areas of the program. The project will be focusing on providing technical support to the sector/WGs and humanitarian agencies to mainstreaming community engagement and accountability to affected populations (AAP) by build their capacities through training and mentoring as well as producing community facing information product including audio PSA, podcasts, video and graphics contents.
BBC Media Action will support the establishment and running of listening groups, information desks or other community-facing communication facilities and services by consortium partners. The services will also be available to the government organizations and the wider humanitarian community. The training and mentoring support will also cover range of diverse aspects with the humanitarian agencies, sector and working groups work on multi-hazards, gender, age and disability components. IRC and Concern Worldwide will form listening group within their existing community groups and mother care groups and facilitate discussion using the podcast that BBC Media Action will produce. Also, CWW will operate their existing information hubs and continue collecting, collating the community feedback data and share with BBC Media Action to produce “What Matters” – the humanitarian feedback bulletin.
BBC Media Action and CS partners will undertake research and develop knowledge products that help inform project partners about perceptions, concerns and priorities of residents in the camps and host communities. Based on this research, BBC Media Action will produce a wide range of communication tools, specifically developed for Rohingya and host community audiences, in the right language and about literacy limitations - BBC Media Action will also provide in-depth, targeted communication and community engagement activity in specific areas where attitudinal and social norm shifts are crucial to success. Especially in social cohesion and peaceful existence between Rohingya and Host communities, SGBV, MHPSS, there are strong attitudinal barriers to overcome. Addressing these areas will take more than simple, informational ‘messaging’ –long-term, in-depth engagement will be required, using audio or video drama combined with facilitated community discussion and dialogue. Contents on gardeninig, tree plantation is also a part of tis project to mitigate the climate impacts. The consortium will do an evaluation study to help understand the impact of the capacity strengthening, community facing information products and strategic inputs to the sector/WG/agencies among the community towards the end of 2022.
A responsive, demand-driven training and mentoring service will be delivered by BBC Media Action to consortium members and local partners, covering IPC, community engagement techniques and accountability. The training modules will be designed and regularly contextualized depending on the training participants and objectives. The AAP workshops will take pace based on a co-creation and co-facilitation from the sector/WG/agency so that their existing AAP strategy and guideline (if they have any) can be reflected properly with the minimum and standard practices around AAP. This project will also provide training to the local government staff and disaster management committee members on emergency communications and CE with support from RCRC. The further activities will be designed based on the outcome of the training."

1 - Identified Needs

"While there had been a strong increase in information access among refugees, compared with the beginning of the response in 2017, gaps remain and have been exacerbated because of the Covid 19 pandemic and the associated lockdown. Women are underserved by current information and community engagement provision, and there is a need to ensure that organizations within the response are supported to better communicate with women and understand their needs. Women’s language and communication channels differ from men's and are not yet well understood or appreciated by most agencies working in the response. (ACAPs CwC V1, J-MSNA 2020) Information needs are also not currently being well met in host communities and the gaps on access to information has increased along with ability to provide feedback and be consulted has increased after the covid 19 pandemic. Therefore, a need is felt to increase support to practitioners to communicate with host communities on these key areas. While information about basic services appears to be getting through, refugees (74%) say they are unable to access clear information on type of assistance available to the including NFI’s (59%), livelihoods (42%) and site management (33%) (What Matters) are events in Myanmar and long-term options for their and their children’s futures. (BBC Media Action What Matters). For the host community the access to information is much higher at 99%. While the MNSA data reports 93% of households feel their opinions have been taking into account research from other agencies including IOM, ODI and BBC MA paints a different picture with the community saying they are not included in the discussion in a meaningful way. Overall accountability to both the host and Rohingya community is missing. (ODI, GTS) Critical gaps exist in the provision of comprehensive reproductive and maternal health with low rates deliveries in the camp occurred in health facilities. GBV is often attributed to patriarchal norms, limited livelihood opportunities put pressure on adolescent girls to marry early or risk being trafficked. Gender-based violence additionally remains widespread. Most reported GBV includes intimate partner violence (IPV), emotional violence, physical assault and exploitation. The IRC GBV information monitoring system (GBVIMS) data analysis report from April 2020 to March 2021 shows that 56% of reported GBV cases are physical assault, 28% emotional violence, 15% denial of resources, services and opportunities and 3% rape. 95% of the cases are IPV which shows that the rates have increased in the refugee camps and host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf since the start of the pandemic.While 54% of households had acceptable food quantity consumption score, only 22% consumed food from at least four food groups recommended for good nutrition. (First 1000 Days. UNICEF South Africa 2017) Concern Worldwide Nutrition programs include nutrition specific interventions such as supplementation that directly affect nutritional status, while nutrition-sensitive interventions work with underlying factors that indirectly affect maternal and child nutrition and include agriculture and food security and gender empowerment. Barriers to accessing services for women include cultural and social norms, restricted mobility, and limited transportation. This has resulted in a preference for home deliveries at 33%, often conducted by local birth attendants in unsafe and unhygienic conditions. While there has been a rise in the use of short-acting contraceptives among the Rohingya refugee and nearby host communities in Teknaf and Ukhiya, utilization of contraception is restricted due to women’s limited decision-making power. In Cox’s Bazar, there were 485 deaths reported from April to June 2021; 239 were female, including 13 maternal deaths, 79 still births, and 55 neonatal deaths."
