Project Summary

Building on its protection by presence approach, integrated Protection-MHPSS strategy, solid coverage of several worst-affected districts and strong linkages to CCCM, Shelter/NFI, WASH and Health sectors, IOM will work towards improving the provision of and access to services for displaced, host, and returnee communities in sites and urban areas in Cabo Delgado and Nampula. With the modality of direct implementation, IOM will provide protection and MHPSS services to address protection risks and needs.
Finally, the project will focus on strengthening the protection environment through data and knowledge generation that can inform the advocacy efforts of the cluster. Also, IOM will organize protection mainstreaming activities for non-protection IOM programmes to enhance safe and dignified service provision to the most affected communities. These efforts will cover Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa Provinces.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project’s “protection by presence” approach is strongly community-based and integrates strengthening and expanding existing actions and supporting government-led protection and MHPSS services. The project aims to support the most vulnerable with integrated Protection-MHPSS activities. IOM’s direct interventions under this project cover all layers of the IASC MHPSS pyramid, with referrals to specialized services. The project focuses on strengthening the protection environment by cooperating with the partners and government in addition to the prevention and response activities such as awareness-raising, counselling and community-based MHPSS activities, and referrals to the protection services (e.g., victim assistance, police, social services, civil documentation etc.). Based on the needs and in coordination with the partners, IOM will target IDPs living in sites (relocation, temporary, host community extension, government) and within host communities as well as returnees particularly support the most vulnerable, such as elderly, women and child-headed households, victims of violence, GBV, and human trafficking and people with disabilities. Host families and communities whose coping capacities and resilience are affected by the presence of IDPs will also be targeted. Recipients of direct assistance, including victims of trafficking in persons, will be selected jointly with the government social services based on established vulnerability criteria and their individual assistance plan developed with their and the beneficiaries’ active participation. In addition to that, IOM will increase the protection and mental health capacities of other IOM programmes such as health, CCCM, WASH and Shelter/NFI. The above-mentioned activities will also be designed and implemented to prevent GBV and respond to the needs of the survivors. IOM will continue implementing awareness-raising activities to reduce GBV risks. In addition, IOM will conduct safety audits in the sites in coordination with GBV AoR and CCCM Cluster to identify risks and provide recommendations. In line with that, IOM will provide counselling and referral services for the GBV survivors within the existing referral mechanisms developed by the Cluster and AoR. IOM will target the communities with psychoeducation activities on gender equality, women’s rights, and violence with the target of behavioural change in the long term. These activities will be completed with focused socio-cultural activities to provide communities, particularly women and girls, with safe spaces to express their concerns and needs in creative ways. In order to strengthen the capacities, IOM will provide training in cooperation with the GBV AoR to the government and community focal points. The capacity-building activities will target IOM CCCM, Shelter, DTM and Health programmes to improve service provision sensitive to GBV. Similarly, IOM will continue implementing direct protection-MHPSS services for vulnerable children and their caregivers in close coordination with CP AoR partners and the national and province-level Reference Groups on Child Protection, Combating Human Trafficking and Irregular Migration (RGs) in Cabo Delgado and Nampula and other CP partners, IOM will target key child protection services, including those providing MHPSS to children, IDP children, adolescents and caregivers. These activities will include identification of the vulnerable children and referrals to the services, and awareness raising on child protection issues (e.g., FTR, grave violations, forced marriages, child safeguarding, PSEA), provision of family and community supports and focused MHPSS activities, integration of CAAFAG, and reporting of the grave violations. In order to improve children’s access to MHPSS and protection services, IOM will work with the Child Protection AoR and MHPSS TWG to strengthen capacities of child and social services in Cabo Delgado and Nampula.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM employs an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectionalconsiderations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with healthprogramming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM identifi es individual andstructural-level protection risks through outreach to IDPs via its activists and byconducting regular protection/safety audits in the districts. IOM’s community-basedactivists are trained to identify vulnerable groups, including children and, adolescentsand the elderly, refer them to available services, advocate and assist them in gainingequal access to available humanitarian aid and protection services. IOM follows strictdata protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access,not to cause further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted. IOMhas an internal mechanism (“We are all in.”), which is accessible by all stakeholders toreport SEA allegations. Also, the platform provides online trainings and refreshersessions for the staff members. IOM’s Protection-MHPSS Programme will raise thecapacity of staff involved, government counterparts, implementing partners andservice providers, including psychological counsellors, team leaders, communityactivists and integrated Protection/MHPSS/Health mobile teams, through the deliveryof PSEA trainings to ensure that they are well informed about critical values and andwill include the concept of SEA and differential power, safe referrals, as well as how toreport SEA allegations in a safe and ethical manner. Throughout projectimplementation, refresher trainings will be delivered to the staff on a regular basis toreinforce their knowledge of PSEA and reporting mechanisms. IOM will conductawareness-raising activities within the communities to ensure that the affectedpopulation is well informed about their right to free assistance and appropriatebehaviors of assistance providers, as well as confi dential and easily accessiblecomplaint and reporting channels with operators. As part of the program activities, theProtection-MHPSS team will conduct site visits and focus group discussions withwomen, girls, and persons of diverse backgrounds on how to make programming saferfor women and girls and other populations who may become vulnerable, with a focuson how to prevent SEA and report SEA complaints.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

This project aims to increase access of I DPs to the protection and MHPSS services.IOM has a multi-disciplinary core team and community fi eld workers working at thesite/district levels. The teams identify protection risks and needs and address themeither through direct service provision or referral to more specialized services. Maleand female community workers conduct door-to-door outreach to reach individuals atrisk of marginalization due to limited access to the services (e.g., elderly, persons withphysical disabilities, persons with chronic diseases) and individuals who live in distantlocations. Moreover, all community workers were selected based on their languagecapacities in cooperation with the social services to ensure that IDPs can express theirneeds, concerns, and requests in their native language. In addition, IOM cooperateswith and supports government entities to reduce the barriers in access. For example,IOM conducts joint mobile brigades with government health and social services totarget individuals with limited or no access to the facilities. In order to implement theseactivities, IOM works very closely with the community leaders and members in thesites and host community settings. The activities at site level are designed incooperation with the communities to ensure that their needs are addressed in aculturally appropriate manner.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams and community-based protection activists identify and refer peoplewith special needs, including individuals with physical and mental disabilities, toadequate services. IOM’s trained MHPSS professionals are able to provide counsellingand mitigate some mental health impacts of displacement. IOM pays special attentionto ensuring that persons with disabilities, incl. albinism, are protected from humantraffi cking, abuse and exploitation under the integrated health-protection-MHPSSactivities. Moreover, IOM Protection-MHPSS Programme cooperates with other IOMProgrammes (e.g., Shelter, WASH, CCCM, health) to mainstream disability inclusion inplanning and implementation.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project does not include any construction work or other activities withsignifi cant environmental impact. IOM’s community-based MHPSS activities strive touse environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for theenvironment is included in some awareness-raising sessions with communities. Also,the mobile teams use bicycles to travel between the sites to promote environment-friendly approaches.
