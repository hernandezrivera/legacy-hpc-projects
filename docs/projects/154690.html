Project Summary

EADE is actively involved in shelter an NFI cluster and has been closely aligned its interventions with the general and sector specific strategic objectives for 2019 Iraq Humanitarian Response Plan. This project supports the main aim of the Shelter NFI Cluster to reach highly vulnerable people and contribute to international standards of care, assistance and protection, EADE focuses on reaching the most vulnerable people as possible of affected communities. In this Project EADE is Targeting 100 War Damaged Shelters of Category II for the returning families with emergency repairs according to the Shelter Cluster guidelines as follows:
1. Identification of beneficiaries, based solely upon humanitarian need and through clearly established vulnerability criteria (CWG Vulnerability Tool)
2. Communication with communities for awareness raising on the selection of beneficiaries
3. Verification of ownership to ensure House, Land and Property (HLP)
4. Categorization of the WDS, using the Categories of War Damaged Buildings in Iraq, conducted by a qualified engineer
5. Preparation of a Bill of Quantity by qualified engineers
6. Communication with, and consent from, beneficiaries on the scope and timing of repairs prior to execution
7. Communication with, and consent from, beneficiaries on the collection of the geographical coordinates of their house for reporting purposes
8. Implementation of emergency shelter repairs
9. Monitoring during and after execution of works, and evaluation upon expiration of the liability period, run by technical experts only.
HLP guidelines will be followed as the verification of proof of ownership is an essential action required before starting a technical assessment.
Also in this project Working opportunities as the daily workers will be chosen from the area and awareness session about Child Protection and GBV. Case referrals to the relevant clusters (WASH, Protection, Education, etc.) when needed will be done
• Cluster objective 2 For highly vulnerable returnees living in war damaged shelters: emergency repairs or cash for shelter for cat 2 and 3, or provision of low cost transitional shelter for cat 3 and 4.

Needs

After the military operation started against ISIL in 2017 the escalating worsened the humanitarian conditions in Telafar which forced the civilian to leave the area now after the liberation is over many houses where damaged and a lot of vulnerable people are living in hoses that are partially damaged. Therefore, EADE has prepared a shelter response to help the vulnerable non displaced, and returnees' communities in Telafar, where needs are extremely high due to the military activates. Many people could not return to Telafar to their houses because they are damaged and missing nonfood items as they don't have enough money to repair their houses. In 2016 EADE conducted a house emergency repairs in Qayaara through IHPF fund where a 150 houses were rehabilitated and distributed NFI kits also. The humanitarian situation is reportedly severe across the villages covered by the assessment which included, Abu Maria village inhabited by returnees who are unable to return in telafar also, this area was selected by EADE for several reasons some are the fact that INGOs and UN-Agencies are targeted big cities and the small villages are not supported that much and due the face of access to these areas as EADE is a local NGOs with connection and knowledge in these areas. EADE made this assessment on the end of October, 2018 and recorded the urgent and updated needs of people in the targeted areas. The military operations in Telafar left thousands of damaged houses belongs to Returnees, non-displaced and host families. These houses are still in different level of damage after ISIL withdrawal for no ability of the families to make the required rehabilitation and no Governmental support. IDPs also displaced to such damaged houses and lived even in not completed houses to stay away as IDPs in safe neighborhoods till their completely damaged houses become rehabilitated. EADE recorded the need to support 100 families and assist them through emergency repairs for the Cat 2 houses this project will provide working opportunities for the locals as EADE will choose the labors from the area to also support them financially during the project period. Choosing the beneficiaries will be done according the CWG assessment tool and the most vulnerable people will be chosen also, EADE will conduct a CWC, Meeting with female headed households as part of the staff will be females to ensure a safe environment for women to speak freely and to apply the gender balance, elderly and people with disabilities will be also considered. Awareness sessions will be held about early child marriage, child labor and GBV. When needed there will be a case referral to relevant clusters whether it is a protection, WASH, Education, etc. need is observed by EADE Staff and management. The houses damages resulted of burning with damaging of doors, windows and walls during the military operations which led to deteriorate the humanitarian situation of housing conditions and not providing them with a protection from weather and the diffused environmental pollution in these locations. Most of the beneficiaries have not yet received the financial support from the local Government, the lack of salaries, the lack of jobs and the significant effects of the economic siege on the city during ISIL control, all that led to the economic destruction of the entire city with destroying of the financial abilities of people whom became unable to ensure good level of living for their families or to renovating or rebuilding their demolished houses. The total number of direct beneficiaries is 600 individuals =100 HH will receive emergency house repairs. Special consideration given to PWD, GBV and elderly. Strong AAP component including feedback mechanisms and CWC should be maintained during the project implementation. Strong coordination with S NFI Cluster should be activated to achieve optimum outputs for the project and working according to Shelter cluster guidelines of houses repairs for CAT II. Clear coordination with other partners providing repairs and other stabilization partners and government working on the repairs.

Activities

Detailed assessments for the houses and people in the targeted area to choose the100 houses and beneficiaries who will have daily jobs to work for the families who can't undertake rehabilitation works themselves using CWG assessment tool. Market detailed assessment will be undertaken to find out the following:Identify how many traders are selling the generic items If there is a sufficient number of traders in the marketTo find out if there are any necessary items or goods needed for the repairs are missing and to provide these items from other areas by checking a list made by the engineers who will prepare the BoQs. Rehabilitation works and the awareness sessions will start to be implanted after all assessments and beneficiaries are chosen. Provide feedback mechanisms such as focal points, phone number and complaints box Activities will be monitored by the Engineers and Field Coordinator to ensure it meets safety and quality standards as regular reports are to be shared with M&E officers and EADE management & to check with beneficiaries if the assistance provided improved their life quality specially the PWD, Elderly and Female headed households

Mitigation Measures

R1 - Difficulties in accessing the area Mitigation measures: the field staff will be selected among the (selected location) population to assure at least a minimum standard of activities even in case the will be no access. R 2 - Families are not willing to make their children attending PSS activities Mitigation measures: work closely with the community leaders and parents to help them to understand the purpose and importance of the service delivered. R 3 - Difficulties in staff selection in particular for technical positions Mitigation measures: strengthen the staff training and technical support in case the staff members hired don't have a strong expertise R 4 - The security situation in the area is getting worst Mitigation measures: Keep a good relationship and frequent contact with the local authorities. Create a bigger network of contacts in order to have more and timely information about the situation. Coordinate with other NGOs working in the same area. R 5 - Families don't allow the social workers to visit them and follow up with the children: work closely with the community leaders and parents to help them to understand the purpose and importance of the service delivered. R 6- Absence of specialized actors to refer the cases Mitigation measures: advocate at different level to allocate funds or partner to provide the missing services R 7 Sexual exploitation and abuse and Gender Based Violence Mitigation measures: the existence of female staff and regular visit of M&E teams and meeting the beneficiaries and if any GBV incidents are observed they are to be shared with the management to be shared with the relevant cluster to intervene and solve the problem.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountably to affected people The principle of Do No Harm will be fundamental to ensuring equitable access to services, recruitment, potential legitimization effects, and communication. A detailed MEAL Framework and plan will be developed for the project. Monitoring visits by EADE will be conducted at least monthly to all project sites. The outcomes of these monitoring visits will be discussed with the project team to address quality shortcomings. The system involves sharing project information with beneficiaries, creating opportunities for beneficiaries to participate in implementing the project and a mechanism for receiving and handling feedback and complaints (including a way to be able to provide anonymous feedback). This is performed either through a telephone hotline and/or a suggestion box. The accountability system will encourage information-sharing among children and their families in the targeted areas, empowering them to identify areas in which EADE can improve the quality of its programs. During program set-up, EADE will hold consultations with children and adults in targeted areas to seek input on what success would look like for the program and identify the most appropriate method for the CRM. Beneficiaries will also be engaged through active participation in identifying the most pressing needs and developing strategies to address these needs, mapping risks and ensuring feedback is mainstreamed throughout the project.
