Project Summary

HFD South Sudan’s life-saving essential health project for 2025 South Sudan HRP is aligned to South Sudan Health Cluster’s proposed strategic objectives SO1, SO2 and SO3 for the period 2024/2025 which are aligned with the HNO/ HRP South Sudan. The project will address critical humanitarian needs in selected priority Counties of Cueibet (level 5) in Lakes State and Manyo (level 4) in Upper Nile State by ensuring effective coordination, complementarity and integration of development and humanitarian nexus in all areas of collaboration with State Ministries of Health (SMoH) and their respective CHDs and with other partners in order to narrow the health care provision gaps in the most remote areas and for the most neglected groups. This will be done by proposing a range of differentiated services provision modalities (static and mobile), expected to be able to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current health care coverage at all levels of the health system and/or resulting from emergency events and therefore to respond to the essential needs of the target population, in particular of those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) make them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging. For this purpose, health related activities will be integrated with those pertaining to the protection sector, through the opening and sustained reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing health conditions and the inter- related complications (i.e. malnutrition), and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases. In this regard, the project will guarantee timely and effective prevention, containment and management of outbreaks (MPOX, Cholera, Measles, etc.). Surveillance and early warning will be strengthened, with the engagement and coordination of various stakeholders. In general, the project will promote the proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities and this will facilitate the selection and implementation of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable intervention options, focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. The project is expected to reach 67500 people in Cueibet County, and 43,000 people in Manyo County, with at least 4% of all beneficiaries being people with disability (658) in both counties.

1. Needs

The needs identified and their proposed solutions in this 2025 HRP project proposal by HFD are based on HNO 2024 issued by OCHA and the 2025 HNO Narrative issued by the South Sudan Health Cluster. Cuibet County in Lakes State and Manyo County in Upper Nile State are considered priority humanitarian setting in the 2022 health cluster severity classification at level 5 (Cuibet) and 4 (Manyo) respectively: In Cuibet County in Lakes State, people in need are the 53% (n=94461) of County population, whereas in Manyo County, people in needs are approximately 7% (n=6998) of the County population. Both Cuibet and Manyo Counties are being heavily affected by conflicts and flooding in 2024. The persisting high internal and cross-border conflicts in both Counties have hampered access to and utilization quality basic health services. The conflicts have resulted into internal displacement of people as more than 12,000 IDPs are in Cuibet and 687 IDPs in Manyo (Displacement Tracking Matrix and ISNA assessments, 2024). Matter of concern is the IDPs’ burden over host families whose resources are limited because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. This is even more concerning when considering that, due to the continuous fighting, the large majority of households are headed by women, more exposed to violence and likely to be food insecure. Despite the IDPs vulnerability, they are predisposed to diseases in the face of inadequate and inequitable health facilities distribution, and poor staffing conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies), with insufficient funding to sustain routine services, and failure to offer due preventive and curative functions. Health indicators for both Cuibet and Manyo Counties are low (2021 Annual Health Sector Performance Review Report and ISNA 2022): In Cuibet, the health indicators are low: • ANC4+ (21.3%), still birth attendance (16.6%), OPD utilization (37.7%), and Penta 3 (81.1%). In Manyo, the health indicators are also low as follows: • ANC4+(41.7%), still-birth attendance (11.9%), and OPD utilization (61.3%).

2. Project activities

As indicated in the strategic approach above, HFD’s activities are aligned to Health Cluster strategic objectives to achieve its sectorial objectives and respond to the needs illustrated above. They will be implemented in selected PHC facilities/hospital and villages, agreed with Health Cluster, after needs assessment and discussion with beneficiaries in both Counties. Priority will begiven to those areas where existing services are not sufficient to cope with care and demand, as neglected by common funding mechanisms or overloaded because of IDPs/outbreaks/flooding/violence. Activity 1: Provision of comprehensive, integrated and quality preventive and curative primary health care services at the static facility level (PHCC and PHCU). This activity is aligned with the Health Cluster Strategic Objective (SO1), and HFD focus will be on the availability of basic life-saving services – such as for prompt management common endemic infections like malaria, pneumonia and diarrheal diseases. All this will be realized by addressing the main gaps reported above as hindering services provision such as: •Hiring/rewarding/training/supervising health staff and BHWs/CHWs; • Procuring/distributing essential drugs and supplies buffer stock, complementing what provided in kind by other actors; • Replacing old/ looted equipment and repairing damaged infrastructures to restore enabling conditions for services provision; • Ensuring transport means availability/running. CHD will conduct regular supportive supervisions to six Medical Mobile sites. Activity 2: Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including epidemic control and management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of healthcare workers. This activity aligned with Health Cluster Strategic Objective 2 (SO2), and HFD will focus on outbreak prevention, detection, investigation, and integrated response. UNICEF/WHO will be advocated for regular antigens provision and cold chain maintenance to ensure sustained outbreaks prevention of vaccine-preventable diseases. Surveillance continuity/consistency will be sustained while strengthening IDSR. Key strategic interventions under this activity will includes; •Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives/iCCM. •Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population. •Review and update the emergency preparedness and response plan for malaria/measles/ cholera/meningitis and hepatitis E outbreak in these counties. • Capacity building of health staff and CHWs to address various primary health care topics including but not limited to management of common illness, management of non-communicable diseases, mhGAP, surveillance, but also provide screening services for cancers (cervical and breast cancers). Activity 3: Data-driven decision making and stakeholders engagement in both Counties (Cuibet and Manyo). Under this interventional approach, closely aligned with the Health Cluster Strategic Objective 3 (SO3), HFD will participate in weekly/bi-weekly data and performance review meetings with both the health cluster team, health authorities and prospective donor (s) and any identified challenges/gaps will be addressed in collaboration with these stakeholders and the Health Cluster team. HFD will submit monthly/quarterly 5Ws data for selected facilities to HC and/or DHIS 2 reporting. HFD will also write and submit monthly and quarterly reports to its donors. HFD will strengthen disease surveillance through completeness and timely reporting of IDSR and EWARs in identified health facilities.
