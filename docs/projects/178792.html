Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the overall efforts in Idleb and Aleppo governorates to reduce mortality
and morbidity rates and increase the resilience of the target communities by maintaining the ongoing health services within the targeted locations and facilities in order not to overburden the existing health system in the area in case of closing any of these facilities. This project aims to continue supporting two secondary health care facilities and a specialized PT center in addition to 2 thalassemia clinics and related blood banks as well as 20 PHCs (level 2) and 2 MCs for 12 months by assuming the running costs, staff salaries and providing medicines and consumables. The PHCs and MCs:
Provide services including general medicine, Pediatric, Pre- and post-natal examinations (ANC, PNC) in addition to the general SRH counseling, further services include management of NCD and communicable diseases, mental health and PSS in addition to community health and integral services including malnutrition screening, management and referral.
In BAS hospital, ICU services will be provided including cardiac, pediatric, and post-surgical intensive care cases, in addition to the ICU, internal medicine services will be provided including inpatient and outpatient consultations, in addition to trauma services and surgical procedures in several medical specialties’ including general surgery, neurosurgery, ENT, ophthalmology and orthopedic surgeries. The PT center will provide physiotherapy sessions to the people in need in addition to the PSS for them and their caregivers.
Hope hospital will provide specialized pediatric services including outpatient consultations, inpatient and neonatal care.
The blood banks and thalassemia clinics will provide the diagnosis and management of thalassemia patients in addition to the collection and distribution of blood components covering several health facilities in the targeted areas.

4 - Needs

As the Syria crisis approaches its tenth year, the health system remains heavily disrupted. While attacks on health care in 2020 fell by 67 percent compared to 2019, the COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with an economic downturn in the country, has pushed the health system to the brink. By mid-2020, more than 7.78 million people in 100 sub-districts were living below three critical emergency health standards and a localized household survey in northwest Syria found crude and under-five mortality rates were below international standards. Compared to 2020, the health sector PiN has increased three percent with the largest absolute change seen in Idleb Governorate where the PiN increased by more than 200,000 people. The health projects focus on the key vulnerable groups. According to HNO 2021, among the 12.43 million people in need of health services in 2021, 1.41 million are children U5 while 3.32 million are WRA, including 498,480 women who are expected to become pregnant in 2021. An additional 536,753 older people are likely to require health services, as well as those with early-onset NCD, which is estimated to account for 45 percent of all mortality in Syria. IDA is one of the main health partners covering the health services in Azaz, Jarablus, Afrin and Bab districts where the available services are covering a population of 1.5 m people with various types of health services including general medicine, Physical rehabilitation, pediatric medicine, community health and SRH services.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. To provide IDPs and host communities with high-quality and free primary health services in 20 PHCs and 2 MCs by covering the running costs and providing medicines and consumables. 2. Introduction of a quality management framework, training and coaching (on the job) of health personnel based on current needs. 3. Collection of health and epidemiological data for the Early Warning and Response Network (EWARN) and activation of emergency plans for epidemics. 4. Provide essential secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral. 5. provide antenatal care. 6. Provide specialized health care services (blood bank and thalassemia services) in Azaz and Al Atareb sub-districts. 7. Run one ICU department in M. Wassim Maaz Hospital in A'zaz district for by covering the staff salaries, medication, consumables and running costs needed to operate. 8. Cover the salaries of medical staff and running costs of the internal department in M.W.Maazhospital. 9. Support the staff salaries and running cost of 1 Physiotherapy center in A'zaz district. 10. support Pediatric department in Hope hospital (Ghandura hospital). The MEAL team will conduct frequent field visits to collect data and monitor the activities implementation using checklists, observation and interviews with beneficiaries and staff and share notes with project management for corrective action and follow-up.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Raising awareness through messages about PSEA topics in addition to having a complaint mechanism in place and sensitizing beneficiaries on how to use it. Also, service providers will be periodically trained on such topics.Risk 2: Risk: safe closure of facilities and protecting data. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Electronic data and information is properly protected and backed up according to IDA SOPs - IDA has developed its own exit strategy guidance to exits in emergencies.Risk 3: Spread of COVID-19 in the project facilities.(Likelihood: high, impact: high)Mitigation: COVID-19 mitigation procedures will be in place during the project and will include social distance between beneficiaries and the regulation of the patients flow in a way that keeps all of them safe where the crowding will not be allowed at all at the entry of the facility or within the waiting hall or at the clinics. The patient's companions will not be allowed to enter and at least there will be10 minutes between each patient and another when they are registered by the data entry. In addition to triage and isolation tents at the entry of each facility, where all beneficiaries will be examined and measured temperature before entering the facilities. In case of any suspected positive case, it will be isolated in the isolation tent for further investigations by the clinicians to double-check if they meet the definition criteria or not and then referred to COVID 19 management hospitals in coordination with all health actors in the area as agreed by WHO for COVID 19 response plan. In addition to the continuation of COVID and hygiene awareness.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:• The ever-changing security situation may badly affect the response and resulting in a mass influx of new IDPs including children and children with disabilities.(likelihood: weak, impact: high)Mitigation:• Safety and security measures and emergency plans will be set and considered before and during the implementation period. Project activities will be designed flexibly to be modified appropriately with the changeable security situation. Implementation sites could be located to safer places as needed in coordination with the Health Cluster.Risk 2: * the UN cross-border resolution is not extended(Likelihood: mid, impact: high)Mitigation: Intensive advocacy, in addition to coordination with the international NGOs to secure medicine and medical consumables.Risk 3:• The local authorities decide to prevent IDA from implementing the project or part of it.(likelihood: weak, impact: mid)Mitigation:• Intensive advocacy, negotiation, and coordination with local councils, stakeholders.Risk 4:• The lack of well-qualified specialties in the targeted locations(likelihood: mid, impact: high)Mitigation: IDA will communicate with the health directorates to ensure that the required specialties are recruitedRisk 5:• Corruption and abuse use of cash(Likelihood: mid, impact: high)Mitigation: IDA will assure that all accounting and financial management practices are in line with IDA Financial Instructions. All procurement and expenses reported under the project will be subject to control and appropriate segregation of duties between Program and System team members.
