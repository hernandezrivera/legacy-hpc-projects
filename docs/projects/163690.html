Project Summary

Libya is and will continue to be a destination and transit country for a large number of refugees and asylum-seekers moving in the context of mixed migration flows and who are perceived by the Libyan authorities as illegal migrants who should either be sent back to their countries of their origin or resettled to third countries. As a result of this restrictive policy and the negative public attitudes that they are subjected to, refugees and asylum seekers are exposed to a wide range of protection risks including arbitrary arrest and detention, police harassment, abuse and exploitation., in addition to facing serious difficulties in meeting their basic needs and those of their family members. Under these circumstances, UNHCR, together with its partners, will continue to provide them with a wide range of life-saving protection and assistance services across various sectors of intervention such as protection, in-kind shelter and NFI support, financial assistance, registration and documentation, public health, while redoubling efforts aimed at securing the release of refugees and asylum-seekers being held in detention and finding durable solutions out of Libya through humanitarian evacuation and direct resettlement to third countries for the most vulnerable of them who will be carefully identified on the basis of pre-established eligibility criteria both from within the urban refugee population and those kept in detention

Associated response plan

Libya Humanitarian Response Plan 2020

Plan fields

1 - Needs

Refugees, asylum seekers and migrants are in need of urgent health care upon arrival at disembarkation points as the Libyan coast guard does not have a medical team. Urgent healthcare assessment is provided through UNHCR supported mobile medical team to cover this gap. Limited access of refugees and asylum seekers in urban settings to primary healthcare facilitates due to limited resource in these facilities makes private clinics the only available choice which is not a feasible due to low income. Among the consequences of the limited access is purchasing medicines directly from the pharmacy without a proper medical consultation which will adversely affect their health.Access to secondary health facilities is limited due to scarce resources and low bed capacity. Refugees and asylum seekers in detention centers have limited access to healthcare as the services are provided through international organization mobile medical teams. Access can be affected by security constraints in the area or access permission process through DCIM. Referral system is not in place and secondary health facilities have limited bed capacity. GDF residents are in need of primary healthcare service inside the GDF as they don't have freedom of movement to nearby primary health care facilities in addition to limited services at these facilities. IDPs with physical disabilities are suffering from lack of physiotherapy services due to limited specialized centers

2 - Activities

The program will address the needs of the refugees, migrants, IDS, non-displaced and asylum seekers though multi-sectoral activities according to the needs

4 - Protection risk analysis

1-Security situation degrades thereby impeding access to the area of implementations.2-Risks of SGBV. 3-Risk of exploitation.4-Sudden movement of patient of chronic medical condition, pregnancy case and TB patients out of detention centers.5-Detention risk of pregnant refugees and asylum in urban when seeking baby delivery service in some governmental facilities.6-Unavailability of empty bed in governmental secondary health facility for critical medical cases.

5 - Mitigation measures

1-Maintain good security networks. Collecting information and advocate through clusters system for access to closed areas.2-Staff are trained on the guiding principles of SGBV and survivor centered health response to improve skills and positive attitudes for providing support and services to SGBV survivors.3-Staff are trained on the guiding principles on preventing exploitation and signed code of conduct.4-Necessary medical information would be shared with relevant healthcare provider including NCDC for TB to prevent any treatment gap.5-Referred cases by UNHCR is done by an ambulance with a medical escort who can explain the vulnerable situation of refugees and asylum seekers. In case of self-referral, the medical team will communicate with health facility as soon as possible.6-After confirming no empty beds in the governmental facilities, referral to private health facilities would be considered.
