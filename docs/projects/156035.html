Project Summary

In 2019, UNRWA will continue to provide critical protection assistance to Palestine refugees, including to those who are displaced and those returning to their habitual residence in Syria. The Agency will seek to improve the protection of Palestine refugees in Syria through various interventions. Mainstreaming protection within UNRWA services will remain a priority. Corresponding to needs, protection trainings, including on disability inclusion, GBV, PSEA, addressing violence against children, will continue to be delivered to a wide range of staff and volunteers. In addition, UNRWA will continue to build the capacity of programmes to strengthen case management and referral processes for high-risk protection cases, including unaccompanied/separated Palestine refugee children, persons with disabilities, older persons and those affected by violence, GBV, abuse and neglect. Dedicated programmatic responses will be implemented through regular service-delivery and stand-alone initiatives to support the needs of vulnerable populations including to address psychosocial distress. Areas most affected by conflict-related violence as well as displaced and returning populations will be prioritized. Legal support services will continue to be offered to women experiencing GBV through assistance to obtain civil documentation and referrals to external partners. Mine risk awareness campaigns will continue throughout the country targeting students and other community members, with a specific focus on areas of spontaneous returns.
Protection monitoring, reporting and advocacy will continue to be prioritised through the participation of UNRWA in global protection mechanisms and the international human rights system (IHRS), and through interaction with relevant stakeholders, including with the protection sector of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) in Syria. In parallel, UNRWA will focus specifically on monitoring the return of Palestine refugees displaced within and outside Syria.

Needs

In 2019, Palestine refugees will continue to rely heavily on UNRWA to meet their basic needs and enjoy a measure of protection. Palestine refugees remain one of ‎the most vulnerable groups inside Syria, with over 90 per cent living below the poverty line and almost 60% being displaced at least once during the conflict with ‎many suffering multiple displacements. Palestine refugee camps and other residential areas were, in many cases, battle grounds and one third of all housing for ‎Palestine refugees has been heavily damaged or completely destroyed (mainly in Dera’a camp, Yarmouk camp and Ein El Tal camp), resulting in an ‎extended displacement for many. Protection concerns for Palestine refugees remain severe and displacement for many protracted.‎ The lingering effects of armed conflict have had a devastating impact on the Palestine refugee community. The fracturing of traditional support structures results ‎in individuals, families and communities turning to harmful coping mechanisms such as child labour and child marriage. Protection risks and vulnerabilities ‎identified in 2018 are generally anticipated to continue through 2019. These include but are not limited to psychosocial trauma and disorders, particularly among ‎children, adolescents and other vulnerable groups; increased rates of GBV, substance abuse, sexual exploitation and abuse, and exposure to crime; exclusion and ‎discrimination, particularly in regard to people with disabilities, older persons, women, girls and boys. The 2019 protection audit indicates that there remains a ‎need for ongoing sensitization and training of UNRWA staff on protection issues, ongoing needs for beneficiary participation and inclusion of the most vulnerable, ‎improvements in quality of UNRWA service delivery in ensuring safety and dignity, access to UNRWA installations and the need for a more efficient case ‎management system.‎ The humanitarian situation in Syria remains challenging and the conditions for Palestine refugees to return in safety and dignity are not yet in place for those ‎displaced both internally within Syria and externally to neighboring countries. Returns have been occurring throughout the conflict, but numbers to date have ‎been relatively low. However, the changing nature of the conflict in Syria has seen an increase in the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) returning to ‎their habitual residence within Syria and has raised the prospect of more significant returns of those who have been displaced outside the country (in 2017 and 2018, the return of about 2,000 Palestine refugees from outside the country was observed). Palestine refugees may face additional protection risks when returning to Syria due to their legal status. It is expected that Housing, Land and Property (HLP) ‎and Civil Documentation (CD) needs will increase in 2019 as returns increase and access to areas is eased. Many of those who have returned to date are suffering ‎a secondary displacement, unable to return to their habitual residence, they often move in with extended family where space is already limited.‎

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There are some risks that are beyond the project’s control. The most important of which are the following:‎A)‎ Funding risks: UNRWA relies completely on generous donors funding towards its HRP to provide humanitarian and other service to Palestine refugees in Syria. In ‎January 2018, the Government of the United States decided to halt funding to UNRWA core programs and humanitarian assistance. However, with donors’ ‎remarkable response in face of unprecedented financial challenges, UNRWA was able to continue its core services towards vulnerable Palestine refugees through ‎‎2018. However, the funding shortfall translated into a reduced amount of cash assistance provided to Palestine refugees in Syria, and in a reduced number of ‎distribution rounds. The implementation of the project activities is completely contingent upon receipt of donors’ funds towards the HRP. If financial challenges ‎continue to emerge in 2019, UNRWA as a mitigation measure will be forced to review and reduce protection services provided to Palestine refugees. ‎B)‎ Tension and geopolitics in Syria might affect the implementation of the project in terms of increasing the number of vulnerable Palestine refugees and access to ‎the new accessible areas. UNRWA will continue lobbying with partners and international community to advocate for Palestine refugees and ensure smooth access ‎as well as restoration of UNRWA key service in new accessible areas. ‎
