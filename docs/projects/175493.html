Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at strengthening the resilience of 12,000 most vulnerable IDPs households (60,000 people) headed by women through the distribution of laying hens to increase their food self-production capacity reducing the dependency from food aid and ensuring a source of animal protein especially for the high number of children living inside transit/resettlement centres and with host families.
Additionally the project aims to support up to up to 1,500 artisanal fisher folk households (7,500 people) through the provision of e-vouchers to purchase fishing gear, lost or damaged after displacement.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will provide urgent livelihoods assistance to 60,000 (12,000 HH) most vulnerable women head of IDPs households and host communities affected by insecurity Cabo Delgado, Nampula and 1500 fishermen and their families (5000 people).The proposed intervention aims to provide the beneficiary IDPs households the necessary conditions and skills to restore and improve their chicken production. FAO and its Implementing Partners will also promote the construction of chicken coops (capoeiras) with local material in collaboration with Implementing Partners and extension staff of SDAEs. Chicken distribution will be complemented with an effective control of Newcastle disease contributing to increase the production and productivity of their flock and decrease the bird mortality.The inclusion of host communities as beneficiaries of the intervention will contribute to the improvement of the fragile food security status (IPC phase 3 and above) of host communities as well as help to mitigate social tensions between host communities and IDPs. Among IDPs, priority will be given to women headed households, large families with children under 5 years old, elderly and other disadvantaged groups. Targeting and distribution activities will be carried out in coordination and close collaboration with WFP and IOM ensuring better identification of beneficiaries and efficient use of resources.Artisanal fisher folk households of costal area of Cabo Delgado and Nampula will also receive a e-voucher to purchase fishing gear, lost or damaged after displacement, boats repairing material and cool boxes.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

FAO will identify most vulnerable women head of household among the IDPs located in the districts of Pemba, Mecufi, Metuge, Chiure and Montepuez in Cabo Delgado and Meconta in Nampula Province.The Bushveld Chickens, a dual-purpose breed (meat and eggs), will be imported from Southern African region and grown for at least 2 months in aviaries made available by DPAP (Direção Provincial de Agricultura e Pesca) before distribution to beneficiaries.The distribution of chickens will be completed with poultry feed during acclimatization for at least a month until beneficiaries start looking for alternative source of feed or scavenging Implementing partners will train beneficiaries on basic care on poultry farming (husbandry, feeding, health care). FAO Implementing Partner will also promote the construction of chicken coops (capoeiras) with local material before the distribution in collaboration with extension staff of SDAEs.FAO will select and train 30 community vaccinators for each targeted districts on Newcastle disease control and, in close collaboration with local veterinary authorities, will facilitate 2 vaccination campaigns against Newcastle disease (July and November 2021). Fishermen of the northern districts of Palma, Mocimboa da Praia, Macomia, Quissanga and Ibo have moved to Metuge, Pemba and Mecufi in Cabo Delgado while others are installed in Nampula in the costal districts of Nacala Memba and Mussuril. The beneficiaries of e-voucher for fishing gears will be identified in the accessible costal area of these districts.They will have the choice to select among an assortment of pre-identified inputs (fishing gears and boat repairing material) approved by FAO and Technical Services of Ministry of Fishery through electronic vouchers (e-voucher) valued at approximately USD 400. The fishing gears (trawl nets, lines, hooks, gill nets) will be compliant with national law and regulations. Fishermen will also receive cool boxes to store and transport the fish from the beach to the markets.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

As part of the UN's strategy to address SEA, and sexual harassment, measures are in place to prevent, detect, report and take action against personnel, who commit these inexcusable and abhorrent acts. This includes but are not limited to: i) advocate at the senior leadership of UNCT on strict adherence and zero tolerance to SEA; ii) ensure completion of mandatory training on SEA by all staff; iii) establish mechanisms for address SEA matters; iv) create an enabling working environment for staff members; FAO has a provincial focal point for SEA in all provinces and staff will be provided with practical guidance on how to respond to disclosures of sexual harassment. The above measures will be extended to service providers. If appropriate, specific training and advocacy awareness raising sessions to service providers will be conducted.The project will contribute to gender equality and women’s empowerment by ensuring that inequalities are not perpetuated during project implementation. Community consultation will always include women and other disadvantages groups’ representation. The project will prioritize as beneficiaries (at least 50%), women’s headed households, large families with children under 5 years old, elderly and other disadvantaged groups. It will also, take into consideration women’s workload and their caregiving responsibilities when designing capacity building trainings and access to input distributions and other activities. Sensitization sessions for Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment (GEWE) and GBV Preventions will be conducted during the life cycle of the project

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

FAO will work closely with the implementing partner(s) and SDAEs using participatory approaches to identify the most vulnerable IDPs and will sensitize the communities and beneficiaries on gender and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to ensure their full participation and integration in project activities. This includes the common UN complaint feedback mechanism in Cabo Delgado, with the toll-free green line (Linha Verde). To improve the qualitative information taking into account community perception and satisfaction, a set of indicators for measuring the progress of the project will be discussed with beneficiaries and included in the monitoring system. The e-voucher modality implemented by FAOMZ between 2014 and 2019 has been positively evaluated by beneficiaries and by the government of Mozambique as a tool that ensures transparency, better addresses beneficiary needs while contributing to strengthening the local economy through the involvement of local dealers.FAO has consulted host communities’ leaders and members and explained that they will also benefit from the project input and poultry distribution to avoid any conflicts with IDPs. FAO implementing partners have a solid and long presence in the rural communities and have supported beneficiaries with longer term development projects, which is key for the good relations with local communities and sensitization for the adequate hosting of IDPs.

6 - Disability Inclusion

The project doesn’t specifically target people with disability although families with disabled people will be considered as priority during the beneficiaries selection.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The increased number of IDPs arriving in the southern districts of Cabo Delgado is creating an additional pressure on the already fragile natural resources of the province. IDPs and local communities rely on firewood collection to provide fuel for cooking.FAO will conduct an assessment on the impact of the increased population in the zones of resettlement of IDPs and will recommend alternative and innovative solutions to minimize the pressure on natural resources such as production and use of fuel-efficient cookstoves, alternative fuel and support to sustainable management of wood fuel resources (tree nurseries, community woodlots, agroforestry, etc.).
