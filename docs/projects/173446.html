Project Summary

This project proposal is informed by the Food Security & Livelihoods Sector Humanitarian Need Overview HNO 2021, and it is designed in line with the FSL Sector Humanitarian Response Plan HRP 2021, WASH sector HNO 2020, recent assessments in Reifi Wad Elhilaiw locality, official data analysis provided by Kassala state government, and data provided by consultation with community key informants also an integral part of this proposal.
This project proposal is a one-year multi-sectoral approach project composed of FSL & WASH interventions, aims to strengthen the resilience of the most vulnerable host communities in Reifi Wad Elhilaiw locality of Kassala state, shocked by protracted crises/disasters of floods, affected by disease outbreaks, conflicts, and stressed by the Tigray refugees influx. The proposal interventions will target 13,430 individuals, approximately (2,686) HHs in need, to provide and enable the PiN access to the basic FSL & WASH services in order to mitigate the adverse impact on food security and livelihoods of the most vulnerable households in the targeted locality. Selection criteria will focus on strengthening the resilience of vulnerable individuals, households, and residents of Reifi Wad Elhilaiw host communities. The target communities are mainly agro-pastoralists, smallholder traders, and fishery folks. and the beneficiaries will include female/child-headed households, food-insecure households with more than 6 members, HHs with malnourished children, HHs with limited assets, women with poor access to and control over productive resources and social services, men and youth who are unable to access employment opportunities due to lack of skills and capital, HHs headed by persons with disabilities & special needs, and ethnic minorities who are marginalized from social services economic resources and justice systems. Criteria for beneficiary selection will be socialized with the communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns and/or complaints about the selection process (exclusion/inclusion).

1 - Needs

Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rates. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population.An estimated 7.1 million people face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and need urgent action to save lives. This represents a 22 percent increase from 5.8M compared to the same period last year (IPC analysis 2019). Around one million of the 7.2 million food-insecure people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and around 6 million people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). An additional 17 million people are estimated to be under stress (IPC Phase 2), and any additional shocks like floods, inflation, and poverty can push them to severe levels of hunger.Some localities in areas affected by flooding have shifted to higher levels of food insecurity compared to June 2020 projection; five localities shifted from minimal IPC phase 1 to phase 2, while 10 localities shifted from stress (IPC Phase 2) to crisis levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). Few localities reported that 5-10 percent of the affected population has shifted to emergency levels of food insecurity (phase 4). Destruction of homes and displacement led to further disruption of food security systems and access to markets for vulnerable population. The floods are further deepening already existing vulnerabilities due to disease outbreaks such as COVID-19, conflicts, inflation, and poverty. Refer to FSL - HNO 2021 Sector narrative document.

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project proposal is composed of two sectors interventions, the FSL sector activities will be the following:1) Provide agricultural support for 5000 individuals.2) Provide livestock support for 300 individuals.3) Conduct One comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment (CFSVA).4) Conduct One crop and Food Supply Assessment Mission (CFSAM) + Mid-Season Assessment.5) Conduct One rapid Assessment for Agriculture and Livelihoods.6) Provision of CAHWs training and Kits for 50 individuals. These activities will be in close coordination with the line ministries and target community.While the WASH sector activities include the following:1) Build capacities, provide training, to enhance WASH of affected communities, for 30 individuals.2) Do (60) hygiene campaigns in communal set ups (villages, schools, health facilities, markets and with media.3) Construction of (30) hand washing facilities in communal set ups (communal toilets in camps, schools, markets, public places and health facilities.4) Provision of Jerry can for (2000) HH.5) Provision of soap for (2000) HH.6) Construction of (20) institutional (Health/nutrition) centres latrines (3 stances).7) Rehabilitation of (50) emergency shared communal latrines (multiple households, villages, schools, health facilities, markets).8) Provision of Personal Hygiene Materials for (2000) women and girls.
