Project Summary

South Sudan has gone through vicious cycles of conflict, leading to trauma affecting whole communities. Provision of Comprehensive Case Managements can provide opportunities for individuals and communities to recreate themselves with appropriate psychosocial healing as a component of reconstruction (Wakahiu. J, August 2016. Breaking the Vicious Cycle of Conflict in South Sudan – A Call to Action; United Nations development Programme; South Sudan Country Office.)
It is reported that approximately 61percent of the affected population in the country are children of which 1.9 million boys and girls face acute and severe protection needs. Children have been displaced for several years, with boys and girls being exposed to threats of armed recruitment, PSS distress, family separation, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and sexual and physical violence (UNOCHA. Humanitarian Needs Overview. 2018. Juba. South Sudan). CPSG Strategic Response Plan 2018, reports that approximately 1 million children require psychosocial support with increasingly negative coping mechanisms. In the conflict most people from Central Equatoria escaped Juba. Many unaccompanied children and their parents are now exposed to unstructured hawking and street-begging for survival. The severe economic distress and the breakdown in the rule of law have cumulatively worsened the trauma. Children are extremely vulnerable to violence, exploitation and abuse. They need individual case management, alternative care solutions and families reunifications.
During the conflict, Melut and Renk have provided avenues of escape to Sudan. The conflict has resulted in high levels of family separation.
Some children have witnessed killings, destruction of homes, and losses of families and friends; issues that have severely affected their psycho-social well-being and mental health They and their caregivers daily experience difficulties in accessing food, water, education, livelihoods and recreation.
In Juba, MTT has an on-going caseload of over 583 unaccompanied, separated, and missing children, with226 girls and 357 boys. Some children have witnessed killings, destruction of homes, and losses of families and friends; issues that have severely affected their psycho-social well-being and mental health They and their caregivers daily experience difficulties in accessing food, water, education, livelihoods and recreation. The October 2018 revitalized peace agreement has however brought optimism to communities who had escaped, with a steady inflow of returnees to two towns.
MTT will identify, monitor and report grave violations; separated or unaccompanied children and other child protection concerns. This programme intends to provide comprehensive case management in Juba, Melut and Renk. We will refer affected children to necessary services such as emergency healthcare, risk mitigation, coordination and advocacy. MTT will administer psycho-social support to vulnerable children—including Psychological First Aid as well as other forms of non-formal psycho-social support. Our Child Friendly Space (CFS) will be centers for recreation. Conflict-affected and displaced children are at risk of becoming involved in the worst forms of child labour, including child trafficking, hazardous work and begging in the streets. Foster caregivers will be trained on child protection procedures at the CFSs in the community. This is because children outside of parental care are more at risk of exploitation. Capacities of communities will be increased to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns. Gender and age are embedded in planning and implementation. Boys, girls, women and men will participate in all decision-making organs to ensure maximum input from all groups. Adolescent girls will be provided with dignity kits and encouraged to go to school. Feedback mechanisms are embedded for project flexibility. Nearby primary schools will be identified as partners.

1 - Needs

The outbreak of conflict on 8thJuy2016 re-opened the wounds that had not healed from the December 2013 conflict as well as decades of conflict in South Sudan. Individuals and communities with the trauma need to recreate themselves through appropriate psycho-social healing which is a key component of reconstruction. Many unaccompanied children in Juba, Central Equatoria, have been exposed to negative coping mechanisms such as street-begging and unstructured hawking. The severe economic distress, loss of income and the breakdown in law and order have cumulatively added and worsened the trauma. This project aims at assisting boys, girls, women and men caught up in the vicious cycle of violence and its consequences in the capital city. The revitalized peace agreement has also brought optimism resulting in uncoordinated, but steady inflow of returnees to Upper Nile cities of Melut and Renk, the previous routes of escape to Sudan. Approximately, 1 million children are believed to require psycho-social support. As per CPSG Strategic Response Plan 2018, children who have been displaced for several years and still continue to encounter the same or similar situations, have adopted increasingly negative coping mechanisms. Of the returnees, approximately 61percent are children who face acute and severe protection needs. Some of them have been seriously affected by witnessing the killings of their relatives, friends and destruction of their homes. The project will assist them and their caregivers with the administration of PSS. It intends to address both FTR and PSS concerns with due considerations to gender. MTT will continue and improve on our current case load of over 583 unaccompanied, separated, and missing children made up of 226 girls and 357 boys. The children are extremely vulnerable to all kind of violence, exploitation and abuse and require individual case management, alternative care solutions and support to be reunified with their families or primary caregivers. The project will identify, monitor and report urgent child protection concerns (grave violations; separated or unaccompanied children; etc.) as well as refer affected children to necessary services such as emergency healthcare, identification, documentation and immediate tracing of unaccompanied minors, emergency alternative care for unaccompanied children, and provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA)/Psycho-social Support (PSS), risk mitigation, coordination and advocacy. The project will administer psycho-social support to vulnerable children—including Psychological First Aid as well as other forms of non-formal psycho-social support and refer to other clusters other livelihood needs of food, water, education and livelihoods. We will establish at least one Child Friendly Space (CFS) to be centers of operation. MTT will increase the level of protection and care for children by training foster caregivers within the CFSs. This is especially necessary to children outside of parental care who are more at risk of exploitation. We will strengthen and establish community-based child protection mechanisms, and raise awareness about existing referral pathways. For maximum input, both genders will be duly represented in all decision-making organs. Girls will be encouraged to go to school from where they will receive dignity kits. Feedback mechanisms are embedded for project flexibility. While abiding by the "do-no- harm" principle; schools, hospitals and religious institutions will be invited to become partners in planning, implementation and evaluation.

2 - Activities

Using community support, Child Protection Officers will identify, register and document 1,000 children in need of assistance using standard FTR forms, and enter them into CPIMS+ and share with SCI and UNICEF on Weekly basis. This will be among a community based initiative (CBI) of 50 people. Provide Interim family care services (using comprehensive health case assessment) with necessary cases being referred. Provide food and non-food items; clothes, sandals, soap, mats, bed sheets and water containers to the most vulnerable unaccompanied children Establish and strengthen referral pathway and support services for children placed in interim care and other UASC with special needs by coordinating with health, WASH, nutrition, GBV and education humanitarian workers within the counties. Conduct Temporary care monitoring and follow up with support visits according using FTR tools such as targeted case management (TCM) forms for each visit/child, referring children as needs will arise. Recruit, train and orientate new CP Officers, Case workers, PSS Officers, Facilitators on case identification and registration, assessment, case planning, implementing case plans, follow up and review, and case closure. Tools to be applied will be lectures, group discussions and case studies and case study narratives Conduct refresher CPiE training to Child protection Officers, Caseworkers, PSS Officers, Facilitators; establish and community based child protection networks (CBCPN). The training curriculum will include: • Introduction of CPiE• Case management Principles • Handling of Separated and Unaccompanied children• Psycho-social care and support • Prevention from sexual violence.• Protection of early marriagesConduct training on psychological first aid (PFA) to children and care givers in the community.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project will adhere to all the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child as articulated in Articles 1 to 54. Through the project, gender and age has been given due consideration to ensure the needs of all groups of people targeted are put in focus. Beneficiary selection will consider the needs of the most vulnerable especially boys and girls and those with special needs. Women and men will have representation on decision-making organs with focus groups settings established to ensure maximum input from all groups. All gender groups will be encouraged to participate in every component of the project with the dynamics of the gender roles put into consideration. Resource allocation will ensure equal participation of girls. The project will seek to emphasize the need for girl-child education, with hygiene kits being availed to them. Feedback mechanisms will be put in place to provide project flexibility.Environmental Marker:MTT will enhance the making of a more attractive environment by teaching children to clearing any debris like plastic food wrappers to the dustbin. In order to build resilience in communities, MTT will ensure that natural resources are managed in a sustainable manner. Taken into consideration, will the need to keep peace between the hosts and IDPs. HIV/AIDS: MTT will incorporate HIV messaging into all programmatic activities during project implementation. This will be done in collaboration with health cluster partners who will provide fliers on health promotion messages. MTT will seek to reduce stigma associated with HIV/AIDS and parents or relatives who are HIV+ will be linked with the health cluster to obtain assistance. Trainings will be oriented on the risks associated with sexual exploitation of communities. MTT will facilitate beneficiaries (particularly girls) to report cases of gender-based violence and / or sexual harassment from relatives or strangers, which will be documented and referred back to the line cluster partners.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Perspectives of the targeted people will be respected; more so, those of the children to receive and make decisions on information on issues that affect them. Since the break out of conflict in 2013 and consequent one in 2016, the three counties have had an increase in IDPs being absorbed by host communities. Juba, Melut and Renk have become places of refuge to those fleeing from their war torn counties. IDPs and communities staying outside the UN protection of civilian (PoC) compounds have formed community information networks who interact with our case workers on all decisions that affect them. Inter-agency assessments had indicated significant number of conflict-related family separation cases that required assistance. MTT will continue handling cases as they arise using the communication networks already established. This project is intended to alleviate the impact of the conflict on children from the IDP communities, hosts and returnees through the provision of targeted pyscho-social support, family tracing and reunification services targeting children and caregivers. It aims to rebuild an ability to reconnect and cope positively. MTT will pass Lifesaving Messages to prevent further separation and promote good parenting. Communities will be sensitized on child rights, protection, unexploded ordinances of war, prevention of separation and psychosocial support through public awareness sessions and community discussions (focus group discussion).

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. Community based psychosocial support is an integral part of the activities of the project. All Staff and community volunteers will be trained on Community-based PSS, Psychological first Aid, Child Protection (prevention and response) and techniques to interact and interview boys and girls.When CFSs are to be established or continued, their implementation and work-plan will be aligned to the national guidelines which favour transition to durable community-based practices, in order to meet quality and strategic requirements For the holistic development and benefit of the children, MTT will cooperate with nearby schools, religious or other community leaders for them to avail spaces repair or modify or allow construction of a CFS nearby for use of the children. MTT will agree on a timetable with the schools, religious organisations or community leaders. MTT will develop a CFS weekly work-plan, appropriate for each targeted age group and is gender sensitive. For a more durable solution, locally available resources and materials will be utilized including traditional games and handcraft activities. The work-plan takes into consideration inputs from the targeted beneficiaries, is reviewed on a regular basis (at least every quarter), and is visible at the CFS. A work-plan for the selected CBPSS methodology will be developed and will follow different sessions/topics/steps of the adopted tool. MTT will split the contents of the methodology into multiple sessions. The number of sessions will slightly vary, based on the needs of the targeted group.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. Community based psychosocial support is an integral part of the activities of the project. For the holistic development and benefit of the children, MTT will liaise with the Ministries of Education, Health through nearby schools, clinics, religious or other community leaders for them to avail spaces repair or modify or allow construction of a CFS nearby for use of the children. MTT will agree on a timetable with the schools, religious organisations or community leaders.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. Girls will receive dignity kits. The project will coordinate with WASH humanitarian actors on need for hygienic solid and liquid waste disposal. Caretakers will be trained to be aware of the risks of letting children wandering outside their camps. Children will be taught on friendly ways of solving conflicts, to desist from "fighting games", and violence. Adults will be advised not to expose children to weapons. To protect children from physical danger, children will be kept away from deep water ponds,drainage channels and dogs. (Internews, July-August report. 2019. Juba).

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The security of the vulnerable target group will be enhanced by the programme. The targeted boys and girls are within various age groups (0-5, 6-12, 13-17) having unique needs according to their age groups. For sustainability and increased security of the children, skills will be transferred to community leaders and volunteers on basic case management cycle, children interviewing skills, safeguarding, tracing and CBPSS and Psychological First Aid. Within the IDP camps, skills will be enhanced, re-organized and strengthened for community based support and referral networks.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Team leaders, caseworkers and animators will be selected from the communities. They will be made aware of the project objectives signed by members of staff working directly with children. A list containing names of members will be openly available. Views of community members will be aired in the monthly coordination meetings attended by child protection workers at state and national level (CPWG, CPSC, Protection Cluster, FTR WG, and PSS-TF). Children and/or adolescents from across all ages and dis (abilities) will be targeted and leadership selected from among them. Caregivers will learn about Community-based PSS, Psychological first Aid, Child Protection (prevention, identification and response) and techniques to interact and interview boys and girls. CBPSS methodology will be adopted targeting children and/or adolescents, and community members. A CFS weekly work-plan, appropriate for each targeted age group (0-5, 6-12, and between 13 and 18), gender-sensitive, will be designed with consultation of all community members, whose input will be included. It shall be reviewed regularly, and be displayed openly at the CFS and be shared with other stakeholders. The contents will be split into multiple sessions, each taking roughly 3 hours conducted regularly for continuity and effectiveness. Schedules will be based on needs of targeted groups and will align with school hours. PSS items will be available to the children, based on timetable registered attendees. The register will include all necessary details. It will be availed to donors as required, present at the CFS, or during a CBPSS session. MTT will produce weekly, monthly and quarterly reports on the progress of programme. A joint monitoring of project activities will be undertaken as per monitoring plan to note progress made and identify gaps in implementation. Monitoring will be an ongoing and usually internal process of data collection, focused on inputs and outputs.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Apart from using primary sources of direct feedback, secondary sources will be used. Family members, caretakers and other influential community members such as local authorities, religious leaders, teachers and peer group representatives will be used as transmitters of feedback messages from the most vulnerable members of the community. Follow up will follow through a combination of home visits, office visits and visits at child friendly spaces, phone calls and other methods depending on the needs of the child. Children will be given opportunities to express their needs and opinions privately. The purpose of the follow-up will be to continue assessing risks to the child and ensure the case plan still addresses appropriate concerns in an effective manner.
