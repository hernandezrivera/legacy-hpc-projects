Project Summary

The integrated Food Security phase classification, September -November 2023 for Kapoeta region current Analysis Period (September – November 2023) IPC 3 worse acute food insecurity, and this is also projected in the Period (December 2023 – March 2024) IPC 3 and the situation is further projected to worsen in the (April – July 2024) IPC4. Causal factors such as economic shocks, extended dry spells and erratic rainfall, low agriculture production, spillover effects of the Sudan conflict, and years of asset depletion due to conflicts. The additional burden of malnutrition is being reported in the same period attributed to high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, high child morbidity, poor child feeding, and caring practices, and gender-based violence among others. According to initial rapid needs assessment conducted by RAAH (2023), and AVSI (2023), Kapoeta East County continues to experience food insecurity challenges. The key drivers include: erratic rainfall pattern, poor crop yields, reduced pasture due to lack of water for animals and some pockets of insecurity. This has contributed to low farm yields (crops and animal production), under nutrition for the most vulnerable households, worsening water and sanitation related diseases such as diarrhea. If this situation is not addressed, the Kapoeta community is likely to see a degeneration in human health, hunger, malnutrition and even death. Access to quality seeds and tools is only limited to humanitarian support structures (FAO mainly). With the dire funding challenges South Sudan is experiencing, other resilient small holder livelihoods empowerment capabilities and humanitarian FSL support services could realize resilience efforts in these communities. RAAH now proposes to reach out the most vulnerable households (women, child headed households, persons with disabilities among others) with FSL integrated food systems strengthening mechanisms supported by climate smart interventions and market support systems in order to address for multi-faceted food insecurity challenges in Kapoeta Region. RAAH targets to reach 6,500 individuals (1083 HH) with lifesaving and resilient integrated FSL services in 5 counties of Kapoeta East, Eastern Equatoria state.

2. Project activities

RAAH will enhance and sustain emergency food production in 5 payams of Kapoeta East County (Narus, Loolim, Napotpot, Newsite and Kauto) through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock kit and all the activities work will embed elements of peace building and resilience. The activities will include:  Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits;  Nutrition vouchers (plus poultry & shoat distribution; Seed fairs. Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) & where feasible greater use of cash Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers  Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g. RVF;  Livelihood support to returnee/IDPs households Provision of timely food security information
