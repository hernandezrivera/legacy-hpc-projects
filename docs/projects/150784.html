Project Summary

In collaboration with stakeholders in Gaza, UNOPS will support the installation of solar
energy solutions to ten prioritized schools in vulnerable communities. This comes as a response to
the energy crisis in Gaza that is affecting the provision of essential and critical services to
people including their access to adequate learning environments. The intervention will improve learning and teaching in the targeted schools.

Needs

In the Gaza Strip, ten years of blockade, combined with three escalations of hostilities, have resulted in repeated damage and destruction to an already fragile education infrastructure..The severe electricity shortages in Gaza cause an average of 19-20 hours of blackout daily. The supply of electricity from Egypt, previously suspended due to technical malfunctioning, was not restored, and the Gaza Power Plant (GPP) was entirely shut down for 67 days (March-July), due to the lack of funds to renew its fuel reserves. Some 70 per cent of UNRWA schools and 63 per cent of those run by the Ministry ofEducation already operate on a double or triple shift system. This approach has reduced instruction time to about four hours a day, limiting the time available to reinforce learning, support slow learners, remedial education programmes, and preventing co- and extracurricular activities. According to the Ministry of Education, all schools suffer severe shortages in electricity thereby restricting the study time of both girls and boys and their ability to concentrate and learn and negatively impacts their right and ability to access education at school and at home. The use of generators has proven to be a costly solution that is also still dependent on fuel which continues to be scarce in Gaza. Therefore, a short-term solution is urgently required to mitigate the effects of the chronic electricity shortages to public schools, as represented by the use of solar energy solutions to ensure an adequate teaching and learning environment.

Activities

UNOPS has over 20 years of experience implementing and supporting humanitarian, peacebuilding and development projects around the world, including in some of the most challenging environments. Since the opening of its first office in Jerusalem in 2006, UNOPS has significantly strengthened its operations in Palestine and currently directly employs over 100 personnel. UNOPS is working with relevant stakeholders towards supporting the installation of alternative energy sources / solar systems to ensure critical health, WASH and education services in Gaza through both humanitarian assistance and long-term development support. UNOPS is also targeting 125 vulnerable households for providing them with renewable energy sources through the installation of PV panels. A similar approach will be utilized for installation of solar energy in the selected public schools. Through this action, and in collaboration with the Ministry of Education and Higher Education, UNOPS will target some of the most vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip, by supporting the installation of solar energy sources for marginalized public schools. UNOPS will employ the following selection criteria:1- schools suffering from a sequence of electricity outages.2- central schools that serve the educational process in main MOE events and meetings3- double shift schools Moreover, the targeted schools are located in different governorates, and serve all student levels (primary, middle , and secondary). The selected schools will include boys schools, girls schools and co-ed schools). UNOPS will undertake the following activities:• Conduct joint site assessments with the relevant stakeholders to confirm prioritization.• Procure and install PV systems• Ensure operations of the PV and train beneficiaries on the operation of the system and simple maintenance

Indicators

UNOPS will maintain a robust monitoring and evaluation system that continuously assesses the progress of the project and the achievement of the planned results. This will include regular meetings with key stakeholders. UNOPS project manager and field officers will maintain oversight of the project through regular monitoring that includes field visits, spot checks, as well as focus group discussions with the targeted schools including female and male teachers, and girls and boys attending the schools. Progress will be assessed against the following main indicators which will be disaggregated by sex, age, and location:- # of schools with solar panels (dis-aggregated by girls / boys schools)- # of students and # of teachers benefiting from the provision of material support namely the installation of solar energy solutions (dis-aggregated by female/ male; girls and boys)
