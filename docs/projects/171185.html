Project Summary

The project addresses the primary problem of "Limited access to community- based integrated MHPSS services by affected women, girls and their families amidst COVID-19 pandemic." It responds to time critical needs identified in needs assessment conducted on 22-30/9/2020. Overall objective is "Improved mental health and psychosocial wellbeing and resilience of the most vulnerable women >18-59 and >59 years and adolescent girls 14-17 years and their families based on MHPSS guidelines in emergency settings." It will be achieved “only if” outcomes are realized: 1. Enhanced capacity building of front-line workers from MHPSS service providers; 2. Improved emotional, mental and social well-being; and enhanced resilience of the most vulnerable women and adolescent girls and their families; and 3. Increased sensitization and responsiveness of local and national duty bearers towards improved MHPSS support services for most vulnerable women and adolescent and their families. The project targets directly 9365 beneficiaries (3695 women, 2140 men, 1775 girls and 1755 boys, out of them 480 with disabilities) identified based on vulnerability criteria: women/ men18-59 and >59 years & adolescent girls/ boys 14-17 years including with disabilities, GBV survivors, GBV survivors with cancer, chronically ill, pregnant/lactating women, IDPs, living below poverty line, women small farmers and FHHs according to their location, status, protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from Gaza North (Qarya Badaya, Izbat Beit Hanoun, As Siaf, Jabalia camp) and Gaza (Gaza City- Turkman, Mughraqa, Shati camp, Juhor Dik) and ARAs as they have high number of cases with MHPSS disorders based on data analysis of AISHA emergency hotline services.
AISHA is well suited to implement the project as it manages a licensed specialized mental health clinic, competent specialized staff and sound experience in providing MHPSS across Gaza Strip.
The project links to HRP Strategic Objective 1, Protection Cluster Objective 2, eligible actions, and standard activities/ indicators. It relates also to updated Gender Alert and 2021 HNO.
Activities are: I) capacity building for MHPSS actors on self-care and safety, remote counselling and
case management; IASC guidelines on MHPSS, PSEA, 4Ws to effectively& safely collect,
store, analyze and share data to ensure that MHPSS needs met without gaps or duplication & advance MHPSS programming and advocacy; and supervision for AISHA staff; II) MHPSS services applying mixed methods of in-person and remote working modality while applying COVID-19 safety measures and respecting confidentiality/ privacy and informed consent. MHPSS services cover structured individual PSS and awareness raising to 600 women, 300 girls & 300 boys; group PSS to 100 women, specialized mental health to 75 women & 25 girls/ 20 boys; case management services to 120 women, 40 girls & 40 boys; yoga to 50 women & 15 girls; family therapy to 100 women; recreational kits to 50 girls/ 50 boys’ households; III) community engagement, accountability & advocacy like 1 radio announcement, 4 press releases, 5 community meetings, online leaflet, 1 fact sheet, 2 position papers, feedback/ complaints and response mechanism, prevention of SEA, systemizing and documenting lessons learned and good practices, 5 panel hearing sessions & advocacy and lobbying campaign.
AISHA ensures a human rights framework, strategic/programmatic relevance, gender and age difference;
mainstreaming protection in COVID-19 context via prioritizing safety and dignity and avoid doing harm; meaningful access to services particularly for beneficiaries with disabilities; high level of staff professionalism; respecting ethical considerations of privacy and confidentiality; accountability, participation & empowerment; COVID-19 safety measures; environmentally neutral; and cost effectiveness.

1 - Needs

HNO (2021) stated that since the start of COVID-19, there have been 44 attempted suicides involving children. 198,000 children in Gaza are in need of structured protection and psychosocial support due to risk of severe and moderate mental health conditions, while 36,000 children require individual case management, and 274,000 adults, including caregivers, are in need of various MHPSS services.Finally, As a result of the crisis caused by Covid-19, the rates of violence increased notably. Aisha conducted online survey “Impact of COVID-19 Health Crisis on Psychosocial Conditions of Women in the Gaza Strip” on 1370 people, the results show that the highest percentage (21.1%) of respondents exposed to bullying, while 14% of them were subjected to extortion and exploitation, and the lowest percentage 0.7% respondents exposed directly to rape. While the results related to the types of violence against women show that 69.9%, 60.7%, 30.2%, 28.9%,22.9% of respondent exposed to economic, psychological, social, verbal and physical violence respectively.AISHA conducted specific needs assessment on 22-30/09/2020 using the following methods: 1)Phone calls with 100 women 18-59 and>59 years (chronically ill like cancer, diabetic and hypertensive; internally displaced; women in reproductive age and pregnant/lactating women; female-headed households including widows and divorced/ suspended; out of them 25 are with disabilities). 2)Phone calls with 50 adolescent girls and boys 14- 17 years. 3)Phone calls with 20 men18-59 and>59 years. 4)Phone calls with 50 women and girls via AISHA’s helpline calls asking support. 5) 5 WhatsApp groups for 100 women GBV survivors and in need for MHPSS support. 6) 4 Zoom meetings targeting frontline MHPSS specialists like psychiatrist, case managers, psychologists, psychotherapists, social specialists, lawyers & health workers. AISHA concluded participants’ differentiated needs in alignment with HNO 2021 and Gender Alert to be addressed by the project.All participants expressed their grave concern on COVID-19 wide spread within local communities, people are at increased risk of mental health and psychosocial problems and strict lockdowns, while households face difficulties in meeting their basic needs. MHPSS specialists & management staff of quarantine centers expressed their needs for capacity building training on: 1) self-care & integrated security while working on remote MHPSS activities from home to manage stress and support better job performance & overall well-being; 2) providing remote MHPSS support including communication skills, ethical considerations, preparing beneficiary; engagement with primary caregivers; reducing barriers experienced by PwDs; working online with groups . Women and girls explained that they: are subjected to psychological, physical, sexual, social and economic violence; have increase in their mental health and psychosocial problems (worry about family, need for information, distress, anxiety, worry about health, boredom, loss of privacy, parenting children, trauma, fear of stigma, suicidal crises, family, marital conflicts); lack awareness on their rights, positive coping mechanisms & MHPSS services; deprived of their legal rights (alimony, child watching/ custody, divorce); lack of access to reproductive health and family planning resulting in unwanted pregnancy and risky deliveries; lack of treatment and medication for chronically ill and survivors of cancer; lack of access to shelter services; lack of hygiene materials while they are at increased risk to COVID-19 infection; and suffer from extreme poverty and inability to meet their physical basic needs. They expressed their main problem as “Limited access to community- based integrated MHPSS services”. They requested AISHA to meet their critical needs including: Structured individual/group PSS, Family therapy, Yoga exercises, Specialized mental health treatment, Awareness raising and information on their rights, positive coping mechanisms & MHPSS services, Case management services including: structured individual psychosocial counselling, specialized mental health services, family therapy, social services , legal counselling, legal representation, emergency sexual and reproductive health and family planning services, health services for chronic illnesses (hypertension, diabetes, kidney failure); diagnosis, treatment & rehabilitation to survivors of cancer; referral to Hayat shelter services; cash assistance to purchase basic needs; dignity/hygiene kits ; assistive mobile & hearing tools. Raising their voices to duty bearers on their critical needs, Safety and security and Recreational kitsAISHA launched its hotline/helpline 1st of September 2020, since that time increasing number of calls from women and children from home isolation and quarantine centers reporting and reflecting women and girl’ needs. From 1st to 30th of September 2020, Aisha received 210 calls from 171 people (139 women and 32 men) aged from 16 to 65 years including (2) Mandatory Home Quarantine, (165) Home Quarantine, (4) Health Isolation Center. While the number doubled in October where Aisha received 201 call till mid of October 2020, the majority from Gaza 26%, from North 24%, Rafah 20%,Types and number of calls was: 1) COVID-19: 6 calls about Information on COVID 19 and protection measures, call on Social stigma of COVID 19 exposure or suspicion, discrimination to reach for basic human services where 150 cases cannot reach to basic needs including psychological, health and economic those provided with psychological services, 50 Complain about imposition of home quarantine from official parties. 2) Protection cases: 35 Psychological Violence, 15 Physical Violence, 3 Sexual Violence, 31 Worry and psychological disorders, 50 requested Relief aids , 35 Deprivation from resources; Cases cannot go to work and get their needs because of COVID 19, 35 Carelessness.

2 - Activities

AISHA will implement the project activities ensuring the following: mainstream protection of beneficiaries in COVID-19 context through prioritizing safety and dignity and do no harm; professionalism of AISHA staff in delivering MHPSS services, ethical considerations of confidentiality, privacy and informed consent; meaningful access to MHPSS services particularly for persons with disabilities; 10% of target group are PWDs using adopted incisive approach and manual via remote and In-person interventions; accountability, participation & empowerment; and applying protection against sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment guidelines developed with UN WOMEN support. AISHA Facilities are safe for both beneficiaries and staff via cleaning & sterilization, staff training in hygiene measures, using masks, measuring temperatures, physical distancing & isolating suspected cases in a specific room. - Conduct 10- hour REMOTE training on Well-being, Self-Care and Integrated Security for 100 (60F, 40M) front line workers from MHPSS service providers including AISHA. - Conduct remote 12- hour capacity building training for 100 (60F, 40M) front line workers from MHPSS service providers on best practices of remote counselling and case management across all MHPSS responses. - Conduct remote 12- hour training for 100 (60F, 40M) front line workers from MHPSS actors including AISHA on IASC Guidelines on "Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings". - Conduct 6-hour training for 40 (20F, 20M) humanitarian staff (non-protection specialists) who received training in protection mainstreaming. - Conduct remote 6-hour training on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures indicated in UN Secretary-General’s SEA Bulletin ST/SGB/2003/13 for 100 (60 women and 40 men of front-line workers from MHPSS service providers including AISHA. - Use 4Ws and effectively and safely collect, store, analyze and share data from the provision of MHPSS services. - Provide remote monthly supervision and psychosocial support for 20 AISHA frontline MHPSS workers. - Provide remote and in-person structured individual psychosocial support to 600 women and 600 children (300 boys and 300 girls) survivors of cancer, chronic disease, with disabilities who have psychological reactions to COVID-19 crisis to prevent development of mental health problems following Aisha protocol for remote counseling and Hawwa Zahra manual for MHPSS in emergencies that adopted to inclusive approach for PwDs. - Provide remote group psychological support sessions to 100 women at risk of developing mental health problems in accordance with Hawa Al Zahra Manual for MHPSS in emergencies (including 10% WWDs),. - Provide specialized mental health in-person services including psychiatric assessment, psychotherapy and medications to 75 women, 25 girls and 20 boys (including 10% PwDs) - Provide integrated case management services to 200 most vulnerable women and children (girls and boys including 10% PwDs) with MHPSS problems to coordinate and complement the mental health service. - Carry out therapeutic yoga exercises for 13 groups for 50 women, and 15 girls (including 10% PwDs), while applying COVID-19 protective measures including social/physical distancing and cleaning and disinfection of yoga halls. - Conduct remote and in-person awareness raising sessions for 600 women and 600 children (300 girls and 300 boys) including 10% GwDs, WWDs and mothers of GwDs. - Broadcast 1 radio announcement in local radio station and 4 press releases to explain the project to local communities. - Organize (5) REMOTE community meetings at project start involving (25) girls and (25) boys and (25) women and (25) men) to discuss the project (including 10% PwDs). - Publish and disseminate online information pamphlet directly to 2000 women and 2000 men and 2000 children (1000 girls and 1000 boys) about MHPSS services using AISHA Facebook page. - Elaboration and dissemination of (1) fact sheet and 2 position papers using AISHA Facebook page to stakeholders including safely and ethically compiled dis-aggregated data to document MHPSS gaps to be shared with the affected communities and other relevant stakeholders, to improve the community based integrated MHPSS response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions. - Undertake periodical monitoring and coordination with MHPSS humanitarian key actors at national level as well as with MHPSS stakeholders working in Gaza. Document and systematize good practices and lessons learned from the community engagement mechanisms used by AISHA, and produce a briefing note identifying key recommendations for community-based integrated MHPSS intervention. - Undertake a built-in feedback and complaints mechanism handling made to AISHA by the vulnerable women and girls and provide appropriate remedies that are safe and accessible for victims and PwDs. - Conduct pre- and post-evaluation for project activities and undertake end of project external evaluation using in-Person and REMOTE modalities. - Organize 5 remote panel hearing sessions linking between duty bearers (representatives of governmental related bodies like Ministry of Health and Ministry of Social Affairs) with most vulnerable women and girls - Organize (1) digital advocacy and lobbying campaign to raise the MHPSS critical humanitarian needs/gaps of women and girls including with disabilities, with chronic diseases, with cancer and survivors of GBV, ensuring access to information for all kind of disability like sign language interpretation. - Conduct remote and In-person family therapy sessions for 100 women families according to the security situation and tailored intervention plan. - Prevent and respond to SEA at AISHA and CBOs and service/goods’ staff level vis-a-vis beneficiaries and affected communities through receiving 9- hour REMOTE training on PSEA for 20 women and 20 men including PWDs.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

- AISHA applies PSEA manual and code of ethics and conduct.- AISHA has infrastructure adaptations including fitting ramps and railings to facilities so that all PwDs can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. - The location of AISHA’s facilities and the routes to them are safe.- AISHA provides WWDs and GWDs assistive devices and sign language interpretation services to ensure ful access of direct beneficiares to services.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

- AISHA advances access approach that is consistent with the humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity).- AISHA follows the guidelines in its "Risk management plan including risk analysis and risk mitigation" and "Security and Safety Manual" to protect the lives of their staff during emergencies. - AISHA provides MHPSS services at an adequate time when all women and girls and their families can realistically access them. - AISHA facilitates access to MHPSS services for persons with reduced mobility (persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) through making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. - AISHA facilitates access to MHPSS services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) through providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. - AISHA will raise awareness of target women and girls about their rights and MHPSS services. - AISHA monitors access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators (# of individuals accessing MHPSS services) by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g. persons with disabilities). - AISHA uses their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in ARAs to MHPSS services to leave no one behind. - AISHA adapts activities in accordance with hygiene and safety measures. - AISHA involves grassroots CBOs and local communities in these localities.
