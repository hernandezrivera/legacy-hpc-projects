Project Summary

Through ACF ongoing implementation of relief and development projects in White Nile State, ACF has observed significant gaps across various sectors, and most urgently in nutrition. Therefore, ACF will provide nutrition services including: MUAC tests1, the disbursal of Vitamin A, folic acid, and RUTF2 for moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) patients, and nutrition education; health services including vaccinations, vaccine and communicable disease education especially diarrhea and water born diseases, and, health education. This multi-faceted approach to addressing childhood malnutrition will lead to increased awareness about the scope of the problem in Sudan, the importance of proper nutrition, health care, and personal sanitation, and means to malnutrition prevention. At the same time, the proposed intervention will nurture a greater commitment to reducing the rate of childhood malnutrition for future generations by increasing local knowledge about nutrition, health and hygiene.

1 - Needs

In Sudan, levels of acute malnutrition remain above the WHO emergency threshold in 11 out of 18 States. It is estimated that 2.5 million children under five are suffering from malnutrition, 700,000 of them from severe acute malnutrition. One million individuals are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4), and around 4.8 million individuals are in crisis (IPC Phase 3). The 30th August 179,000 people were affected by heavy rains and flooding across 15 states in Sudan. Worst affected area was White Nile state, more specifically UM RIMTA locality with nearly 66,500 people who were impacted by flooding. In Um Rimta locality according to the last S3MII prevalence of MAM (WHZ between -3 and -2) is: 5,43 (IC 1,85-8,35) and the prevalence of SAM (WHZ < -3): 0,59 (IC 0,09-1,54). In White Nile State, the food insecurity rate is 11.6 (Phase 3) and 6.2% (phase 4).This data may be aggravated by the flooding emergency as at least 34,500 homes were destroyed or damaged and WASH and health facilities were impacted. Additionally, thousands of livestock were killed which is likely to affect livelihoods and food security, especially of poor households. In the area of intervention there is a total population of 16.698 with 167 PLW and 2.838 U5 children. According to the prevalence reported in S3MII and with a coverage 80% the expected caseload is considered to be 60 for SAM and 320 for MAM. In the targeted area of intervention there are not health facilities supporting the nutrition treatment (OTPs or SCs). The population is having difficulties to get the service due to: Shortage of nutrition service in the SMOH health facility (limited resources in term of HR and support services). Difficulties in the transportation (shortage of puplic transportation, shortage of fuel, and very bad road condition).No existing NGOs suporting the nutrition service in the locality. SMOH having difficulties to deploy staff due to the limited resource (no accomodation, transportation, and salary/allowances). The SMOH have one nutrition officer and one register in ALSOFI rural hospital conducting OTP activities, but is only limited to those who visited the hospital or refered by the doctor. Transportation remain big challenge for the villages in the west of the locality. In UM rimta locality there is no SC for the complicated cases and these cases are refered either to Khartoum or EL DEUIM SC, due to the distance and cost the patients cannot afford to travel to follow-up the child further treatment. Action Aganinst Hunger’s in-depth knowledge of Sudan and existing presence in White Nile State will ensure a rapid and successful launch to project activities. In conjunction with ongoing operations, AAH will utilize current resources, including solid relationships with actors in the field as CAFA, that is our local partner in the current flooding emergency response intervention and will be our implementig partner in this intervention.
