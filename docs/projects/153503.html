Project Summary

This proposed program will provide accelerated learning Programme (ALP) and early childhood development (ECD) as well as life skills for youths in Wau and Jur River county of Western Bar-el-Ghazal state and Fashoda County of Upper Nile state. War child Canada through the funding from European Union had been providing accelerated learning Programme (ALP) and life skills activities for children and youths in Wau & Jur River, Western Bar-El-Ghazal and Malakal, Upper Nile from 1st Jan 2016 to 30th Sept 2018 reaching 6,000 learners. Since this funding came to an end by end of September 2018, especially over 2,000 ALP Learners in Wau & Jur River County had been affected and WCC could not continue to provide the same support to ALP Schools therein. However for Malakal PoCs and Town UNICEF 2018 PCA has covered the gap effective from September 2018 which will run for 2 years including the secondary schools.
In addition to the gaps left by EU project above and in Upper Nile in particular, War Child Canada received a written letter from the government in Fashoda County i.e. the current Fashoda state’s ministry of Education, requesting WCC to go in and provide ALP education for over age children and youths whose regular education had been interrupted by the 5 years of conflict that broke out in December 2013, leading to 1.8 million people having to become internally displaced and over 2 million refugees have fled to neighboring countries. Approximately half of these affected populations are children and young people.
Through this proposed programme, War Child Canada will support the continuity of education for ALP learners to complete the four (4) cycle of the programme as well get enrolled into Secondary education or Vocational schools. War Child Canada has also identified a need for quality early childhood development (ECD) in targeted areas. As young children are the most vulnerable group in emergencies, ECD programs provide protection and a sense a normalcy, and children who attend ECD have a better chance of completing their basic education. War Child Canada therefore sees support to vulnerable children to attend good quality, inclusive ECD services as essential.
This programme will build on the successes and lessons learned from War Child Canada’s current UNICEF-funded Provision of Secondary Education and Peacebuilding project in 2018 respectively as well as War Child Canada’s two previous UNICEF-funded projects in South Sudan. It also seeks to strengthen cooperation with local leaders, UNICEF, and other humanitarian agencies running education programming to bring about positive change in the lives of youth and community members.

Needs

During our needs assessment in Wau, Jur River, and Malakal, the following needs were identified through observation and discussion with various community groups: There are an increasing number of children in schools with limited number of classrooms and teachers to adequately provide the minimum requirements for achieving learning objectives. The existing schools structures and Temporary Learning Spaces are old and require significant repairs to be considered safe learning environments for children and youths. There are significant unreported cases of sexual and gender based violence in schools affecting girls and boys within the schools and communities. Many children aged (3-6) are idle in the communities due to having no access to Early Childhood Development (ECD). Most of the existing teachers within the schools are not formally trained and lack the proper training to provide quality educational services to children and youth. This is compounded by the significant lack of learning materials and inadequate furniture in schools. Currently there are no skills for computer literacy and numeracy being taught in the schools and therefore children and youth lack any formal digital access and education. There is a lack of extra-curricular activities for children & youth due to no recreational materials and supervision during after school times. Poor record keeping in county education offices and in schools is a significant challenge resulting in low quality data and monitoring within the schools on attendance, grading, and other administrative information. Some schools observed lack Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) and even those that have them, the PTAs/SMCs are not aware, or are unclear, of their roles and responsibility within the schools and greater community towards providing quality education. The schools have a lack of emphasis on livelihoods and proper nutrition practices for children and youth with no school gardening activities or school feeding programs many children lack dietary diversity throughout the school week. Through an integrated approach War Child Canada aims to provide quality educational services by a holistic approach to improving the educational service provision, including improving the Protection and SGBV referral mechanism within the schools, WASH in schools, and an emphasis on adequate dietary diversity through school gardening and nutritional awareness.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

War Child Canada has a Gender Equality Policy, which includes efforts to mainstream gender equality throughout all programs to ensure the needs of male and female beneficiaries are met. As a standard practice within War Child Canada, all data collected is disaggregated by sex and age to ensure the project targets are met. War Child Canada mainstreams gender and child protection across all programming and within the organization itself, ensuring that interventions and policies consider equitable treatment of women and men, and that minimum standards for child protection are met. War Child Canada will promote the equitable participation of women, men, girls and boys by ensuring that activities are at times and locations that are neutral and accessible to everyone. War Child Canada seeks to promote equity and non-discrimination of the affected population, and aims to maximize fairness in the availability and accessibility of services and psychosocial support among affected populations, across gender, age groups, language groups, ethnic groups and localities, according to identified needs. This programme will be equitable and inclusive, with equal participation of boys and girls and young men and women in targeted areas. Members of all three major ethnic groups as well as minority groups will be targeted for participation. All of War Child Canada’s initiatives are governed by the “Do No Harm” approach, analyzing potential conflict drivers and sources of tension amongst communities and beneficiaries. This ensures that, not only do initiatives not exacerbate existing conflicts or tensions but that they will not create new ones. For all teaching and learning, safer locations will be selected to ensure the free participation of all students in learning. Special attention will be paid to ensure that female students and teachers and caregivers feel safe accessing the spaces.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes Complaints mechanisms can increase accountability to aid beneficiaries by providing them with the opportunities to provide feedback on the quality and quantity of the services they receive as well as to complain about potential wrongdoing.WCC will have local context specific mechanisms, taking into account issues such as cultural norms and values, level of literacy, phone coverage, and social patterns, among others. To address all of these issues, a broad consensus that involves beneficiaries in the design of the complaints mechanism will be done to develop appropriate culturally-sensitive and context-specific responses that identify and address the various barriers to reporting. Irrespective of the forms, procedures and channels for handling complaints, the mechanism will be transparent, independent, accountable, accessible, safe and easy to use.
