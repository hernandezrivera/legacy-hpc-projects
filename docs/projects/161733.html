Project Summary

By providing regular, reliable and predictable monitoring of the multi-sectorial needs and conditions of non-displaced populations, IDPs and returnees in both accessible and hard to reach areas of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States this project aims to inform the planning and delivery of humanitarian response to crisis-affected populations in the BAY states. Proposed activities are complementary in nature, draw on strong collaboration with coordination mechanisms and sectors as conducted in the last two years, and entail an annual i) Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), providing a strong evidence base on multi-sectoral humanitarian needs of affected populations in the BAY States and informing the 2021 HNO. This is complemented by ii) regular Humanitarian Situation Monitoring through a monthly Hard-to-Reach assessment, monitoring access to basic services and humanitarian needs in inaccessible areas as the situation evolves. Data collected from the H2R assessment also aims to inform the bi-annual Cadre Harmonisé analysis, in particular in relation to the food security and livelihood situation of populations remaining in inaccessible areas. Cognizant of the fluid context, REACH will also provide iii) rapid assessment capacity, aiming to inform rapid response mechanisms and actors responding to newly displaced populations, whilst iv) planned intentions surveys will help contribute to informing evidence-based decision-making and programming in relations to durable solutions. Building on these multi-sector assessment activities, sector specific assessments aim to provide dedicated support to the WASH sector through iii) WASH infrastructure mapping in 4 selected locations of WASH priority needs. In sum, by providing granular data, timely information and in-depth analysis this project directly feeds into aid response and decision-making.
To implement this project, REACH will continue to work in close partnership with OCHA and the established Sector coordination mechanisms, including the WASH and FSL Sectors, and other platforms, to ensure that data and findings are shared as widely as possible, thus contributing to an informed, and effective multi-sectoral response to meet the most urgent needs of conflict-affected populations. Findings from above mentioned assessments will be shared in regular information products such as Factsheets, Situation Overviews, reports and maps, as well as through presentations at sector and coordination meetings, whilst ensuring sector buy-in into assessment tools and analysis, with REACH products benefitting the wider humanitarian community working on the NE response.

1 - Needs

Following ten years of conflict across the Greater Lake Chad Region, the population in North East Nigeria continues to experience significant humanitarian needs: 7.1 million people in Borno, Adamawa, and, Yobe (BAY) States are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019, according to the 2019 HNO. As a result of access constraints rendering sections of Borno state inaccessible, shifting context dynamics, as well as limited assessment capacity of operational actors, currently the NE response faces the following primary information gaps :1) comparable data across all relevant sectors, crisis-affected areas and population groups to inform humanitarian planning 2) data on the humanitarian situation and access to basic services in inaccessible areas to understand the situation of populations remaining in these areas3) data on the most immediate needs of newly displaced populations to inform rapid response mechanisms4) data on intentions of displaced populations to inform durable solutions programming and decision-making 5) updated infrastructure mapping geo-location data for the WASH sector to help prioritize and monitor infrastructure installation and repair Building on two successfully run MSNAs in 2018 and 2019 and ongoing H2R monitoring since 2018, REACH aims to continue to contribute to filling these information gaps, and extend to new areas of interest to response actors, in particular through intentions surveys and updated WASH infra mapping.

2 - Activities

To address information gap 1 outlined above, REACH proposes a MSNA for 2020, which will be the third exercise of this kind to inform the HNO process for the subsequent year, in particular to assist with sector PiN establishment. The MSNA will be designed and conducted in close coordination with OCHA and the Sector Partners, allowing for robust comparison of humanitarian needs and trends of conflict affected populations in North-East Nigeria, based on primary data gathered through streamlined methodology, indicator overview, and systematized sector review. Methodology include: representatively sampled multi-cluster data collection at the HH level of conflict affected populations (IDPS, returnees and non-displaced). The indicators and questionnaire will be established in coordination with sector leads. The sampling frame will be calculated using DMT figures on specific locations and concentrations of IDPs and other sources of population figures (VTS etc.), to yield findings representative to the LGA level with a 10% margin of error and 95% level of confidence. The surveys will be conducted using a questionnaire administered by REACH mixed-gendered teams using Open Data Kit (ODK) technology on Android-based hand-held devices. Findings will be analysed in a joint analysis session with partners, and proposed outputs include MSNA dataset, state level + LGA level Factsheets, maps as well as a comprehensive MSNA report. ii) To inform on the humanitarian situation in inaccessible areas (information gap 2), REACH will continue to conduct its monthly Hard-to-Reach Assessment. Information collected includes demographics of persons in hard-to-reach areas, as well as to identify their needs, access to services and intentions to move. REACH has been conducting this assessment since November 2018. Using its Area of Knowledge (AoK) methodology, REACH remotely monitors the situation in hard-to-reach areas through monthly multi-sector interviews in accessible Local Government Area (LGA) capitals with newly arrived IDPs as KIs. Selected KIs are purposively sampled and are queried about settlement-wide circumstances in hard-to-reach areas, rather than their individual experiences. Findings are only reported on LGAs where at least 5% of all settlements in the respective LGA have been assessed. The findings presented are indicative of broader trends and are not statistically generalisable. Findings are represented in monthly sector Factsheets and bi-monthly Situation Overviews. Given the sensitivity of the data collected no information is shared at settlement level, and dissemination of information, whilst data protection policies in place ensure restricted access to the data collected. iii) To inform on the needs of newly arrived populations reaching accessible LGA capitals, REACH proposes to continue to conduct rapid assessments, providing a multi-sector initial needs overview informing rapid response actors. Selection of tools will be dependent on the context, but likely draw on the existing REACH rapid assessment survey HH tool, supplemented with KIIs and FGDs. Findings are shared in rapid assessment briefs. iv) To inform ongoing durable solutions programming, through 2 HH survey intentions surveys, REACH aims to provide a stronger evidence base on populations’ movement intentions and push and pull factors impacting decision-making. This will contribute to ensuring programming is based on voluntary, safe and dignified principles and the intentions of crisis-affected populations are at the center of decision-making. v) To assist the WASH sector in establishing an updated WASH infra geo-location dataset and assess functionality and status of existing WASH infra, REACH proposes a WASH infra sweep in 4 selected locations. These will be selected by the WASH sector in collaboration with REACH, with outputs including a geo-location database and WASH infra maps.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As part of the 2019 MSNA, REACH has conducted AAP focused data collection, with a pilot conducted in sharing findings with community members. Similar mechanisms will be put in place for 2020 MSNA. As above, data produced from this activity will potentially provide sensitive population-level information that is both localized and actionable. As part of ACTED and IMPACT’s data protection policies, specific attention is paid to personally identifiable information; that is all information that can be used on its own or with other information to identify, contact, or locate a single person, or to identify an individual in context. In addition, all research conducted in the framework of REACH applies IMPACT’s research data protection SOPs. These have been built to achieve the following three main goals: • Minimization of personally identifiable information: Ensure we collect and store as little personally identifiable information as possible. • Limited, controlled storage and internal sharing of personally identifiable information: Minimize the number of devices and servers holding personally identifiable information, by limiting the number of access points it passes through. • Personal ownership and accountability: Assign formalized and limited access rights for all datasets that contain personally identifiable information, to specific individuals. They then hold formal accountability to protect the personally identifiable information; including ensuring that dataset storage devices are held in a protected space as far as possible. To support these aims: • All structured data collection tools are deployed through IMPACT's centralized accounts which have controlled access. Datasets cannot be accessed through the data collection tools, i.e. cannot be accessed in the field.
