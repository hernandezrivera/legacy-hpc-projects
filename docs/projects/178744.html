Project Summary

The project aims to assist /35000/ of the affected people include (7000: men, 7000: women, 10500: boys and 10500: girls) in NW Syria as following:
1. Provision /1200000/ liters of heating fuel (diesel) for /5000/ households (25000 individuals include 5000: men, 5000: women, 7500: boys and 7500: girls), where each household will get 240 liters of diesel during the winter season.
2. Rehabilitation /2000/ shelters for /2000/ households (10000 individuals include 2000: men, 2000: women, 3000: boys and 3000: girls) depending (HLP) and Shelter guideline.
Activity 1 is relevant to Sector Objective 1, while activity 2 is relevant to Sector Objective 2.

4 - Needs

Based on HNAP & NWS S/NFI CLUSTER, about shelter conditions in northwest Syria, 17% of IDP shelters across NWS are reportedly damaged. Female-headed households are more likely to be unable to afford repairs than male-headed households. According to the HNO 2021, the Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. in North-west Syria, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services. Adequate shelter with appropriate hygiene facilities is critical for people’s living standards, physical health, mental well-being, and protection needs. so, in the Atareb sub-district, the shelter severity is 4 (Extreme), and the proportion of the damaged shelter is 57%, and the main adequacy issue was the lack of electricity with 73%, and lack of insulation from heat, cold, and rain was 40%. in Maaret Tamsrin sub-district the shelter severity is 5 (catastrophic), and the percent of the damaged shelter is 17%, and the main adequacy issue was the lack of electricity with 78% and overcrowding 50%.Based on HNO 2021, Improvements in NFI access were short-lived as nearly one million people were displaced in North-west Syria, accompanied by the impact from COVID-19 and the deteriorating economic conditions. Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving, especially during winter when vulnerable families are unable to access basic items to keep them warm, such as heating fuel, blankets, and winter clothes. in Maaret Tamsrin, the NFI severity is 4 (Extreme) and the main NFI needs is winter heaters and fuel with 77%, and in Qourqeena the NFI severity is 3 (severe) and the main NFI needs is the winter fuel and heaters with 50%.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Provision /1200000/liters of heating fuel-diesel (1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel): SRD will coordinate with SNFI cluster and local councils as well as will conduct the veriﬁcation visits to select the most vulnerable /5000/ households (25000 individuals), where each household will get 240 liters of diesel in three rounds, priority will be given to IDPs. SRD will procure heating fuel from local markets inside Syria depending on open tender.MEAL team will conduct a PDM survey in each distribution location after 15 days from each round of distribution, the collected data will be used to develop the response and correct the mistakes. Also, random visits to beneficiary houses will be conducted to confirm that they are using the fuel.Indicators: No. of people whose heating fuel needs are met. % of IDPs households from total beneﬁciary households. % of distributions have female staff present. 2. Repairing /2000/ houses for /2000/ households (10000 individuals) depending (HLP) and Shelter guidelines (2.1.1 Rehabilitation of damaged houses): The ﬁeld team will select the houses that meet the HLP requirements and the vulnerability criteria, then technical assessment of houses will be conduct to prepare the BoQs and select the contractors.SRD teams are responsible for Quality Assurance QA, by monitoring the detailed descriptions of quality assurance procedures for material and tools and the implementation of works found in the BoQ.Indicators: No. of people assisted with durable shelter solutions. % of shelter interventions that incorporate requisite HLP components, analysis, and/or activities. % of shelter interventions that consider privacy for women and girls.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees.Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigating Measure:• Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. • Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. • Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs• Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be• Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process2. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion)Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigating Measure:• SRD ensure that their staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct.• SRD developed a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly.• SRD has a complaint mechanism in place.3. Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigating Measure:• Provision of gender segregated/appropriate privacy in mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit.• Provision of gender segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings.• Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities.• Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions.• Extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All contractors and SRD staff will sign on the PSEA and Safe Guarding Code of Conduct with their contracts. The awareness session will be conducted by the outreach team to understand all issues related to protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse, GBV prevention, and mitigation guidelines, humanitarian principles, and the do no harm principle. All proposed activities will be delivered in adherence to the principles of humanitarian neutrality and ensure equal access to all beneficiaries in need without discrimination. Practical measures have been implemented, such as training staff on protection core principles and appointing focal points for at-risk women and children in addition to signing a code of conduct (CoC) and training on PSEA. SRD enhanced these procedures and created awareness training for all staff regarding CoCs, PSEA, and Child Protection. SRD improves overall PSEA & CS, ensures that all of HQs and field staff have fully understood PSEA and child guarding policy and signed Code of Conduct. SRD will continue to enhance PSEA reporting channels and respond to the complaints related to the SEA in a structured manner.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Provision heating fuel-diesel:Risks and Assumptions:1.1 Diesel fuel available in local markets is of poor quality, and its combustion, when used in heating, causes the emission of toxic and harmful gases. Mitigating Measures:• Contracting with reputable, well-known suppliers in local markets.• Our ﬁeld team will conduct periodic and surprise tests prior to purchase. 1.2 The indirect purchase of conﬂict associated resources as diesel fuel might contribute to conﬂict.Mitigating Measures:• Conducting (Anti-Terrorism) check for suppliers before contracting them.• Monitor the lines of supply of the fuel and track the source down and ensure fuel is not procured from parties involved in the conﬂict 2. Rehabilitation shelter units: 2.1 The Risks and Assumptions: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from the property owner and/or exposing beneﬁciaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated.Mitigating Measures:• Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster• Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims.2.2 Shelter activities will not contribute to transmission and increase cases of COVID-19Risk: Direct contact between project teams and frequent visits to beneficiaries' homes can increase cases of COFEDMitigating Measures:• SRD staff should understand the risks of COVID-19 introduction and propagation in the site, be trained and monitored on self-protection measures. Avoid direct physical contact and shaking hands with others. • SRD outreach team will deliver awareness messages about protection from COVID-19 during the house visits.
