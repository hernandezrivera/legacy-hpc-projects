Project Summary

As the Syria crisis enters its 13th year, the population continues to live in a perpetual state of emergency. The conflict has had an extensive toll on public infrastructure, leading communities to revert to unsafe and costly alternative water sources with devastating consequences on household economy and public health. In Dar’a, 61% of people have reported insufficient access to water, while Deir-ez- Zor has recorded a high WASH severity rate affected by both water scarcity and disease outbreaks. The cost of living has soared in the target areas, with the price of food and other essentials more than tripling over the past year. Widening income-expenditure gaps have led to a rise of harmful coping mechanisms, fuelling protection risks and cycles of vulnerability. Both areas remain hotspots for ongoing hostilities and EO contamination, combined with protracted displacement and worsening socio-economic conditions which continue to significantly affect physical, mental, and social wellbeing. Dar’a has experienced an increase in security incidents in the form of protests and civil unrest, coinciding with a dire economic situation that continues to hinder people's ability to meet their basic needs, worsened by the end of government fuel subsidies in August 2023. In late 2023, Deir-ez-Zor witnessed the most significant escalation of hostilities since 2019 where tribal and ethnic tensions led to heavy ground clashes and shelling, with thousands of people forcibly displaced across the Euphrates river into government-controlled areas (OCHA Flash Update, September 2023). As male family members often remained on the Kurdish side to look after their homes, women and children make up the majority of those displaced, resulting in a rise of female-headed households who often experience disproportionate needs and increased household vulnerability. Despite widespread needs across sectors and enduring displacement, government-controlled areas of Deir-ez-Zor remain critically underserved, with few INGOs sustaining operations in 2024.

Against this backdrop, DRC will holistically address the immediate basic needs and protection risks of crisis-affected communities in underserved areas of Deir ez-Zor and Dar’a, Syria. Damaged water infrastructures that are indispensable for communities’ survival will be rehabilitated to restore access to a safe and regular supply of water. Within the same areas, multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) will be provided to enable households with severe socio-economic vulnerabilities to meet their basic needs and reduce reliance on harmful coping strategies (note: reflected separately under MPCA sector). DRC’s protection response will prevent, mitigate, and respond to protection threats through tailored services, including child protection case management (CM), psycho-social support (PSS), and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE). With the current economic and water crises intrinsically interlinked with protection risks, improving access to protection services will be complementary and mutually reinforcing.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Military activity observed throughout 2023 in both Dar’a and Deir-ez-Zor governorates point to a high probability for hostilities to either resume or escalate in the coming year – one of the reasons driving displacement and humanitarian needs. Dar’a has been witnessing a new wave of violence since the second quarter of 2023, exposing a security vacuum and social divisions in the governorate (draft HNO 2024). Dar’a has also experienced an increase in security incidents in the form of protests and civil unrest, coinciding with a dire economic situation that continues to hinder people's ability to meet their basic needs.Therefore, DRC proposes the following interventions in both governorates:WASH: DRC will rehabilitate damaged water infrastructure in Dar’a and Deir ez-Zor that are essential to the survival and recovery of communities and have the potential to serve large segments of the population. he infrastructure (e.g., water networks, pump stations, boreholes) will be selected at the full proposal stage based on pre-defined criteria, prioritizing existing water sources that are not functional but can ensure sufficient long-term provision of water of a minimum of 20 litres/person/day after rehabilitation, in line with Sphere standards.Protection: Through child protection CM services, individuals (aged <18) will be supported to address identified protection risks that are time-sensitive and require immediate action through assistance that will prevent, reduce, or mitigate exposure to risks/harm and improve their sense of safety and dignity. This assistance does not seek to address basic needs covered by other sectors, but rather specific protection issues that are resolvable through an appropriate protection response. Clients will be identified through a combination of self-referrals, identification by DRC PSS facilitators, internal referrals from trained DRC sectoral teams, and referrals from actors operating in the same areas. Eligibility for CM depends on a clearly identified protection risk or other risk affecting the individual’s safety, dignity, and/or well-being (e.g., violence, bullying, stigma, exclusion, mental/psychological health risks, discrimination, etc.), and should not be cyclical to avoid creating dependency. PSS interventions will support affected women, men, girls, and boys to plan for their own recovery and prevent long-lasting psychological and social effects. These interventions will aim to assist children and adults in processing distressing feelings in a non-confrontational way and providing opportunities for participants to enhance their emotional, social, and practical skillsets. PSS Officers will provide training on psychological first aid (PFA) and basic counselling concepts to all case workers, to ensure they do not cause emotional harm while handling the cases and are able to identify urgent needs for psycho-social assistance. DRC will also directly deliver EORE to at-risk groups through trained DRC staff.
