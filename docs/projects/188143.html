Project Summary

The FLASH project has been designed to offer a package of integrated services to provide life-saving assistance at a household, specifically targeting the most vulnerable including female headed households, people with disabilities as well as young boys and girls. Potential areas have been selected based FAS severity scales, gap analysis and presence of highly vulnerable populations (such as FHH, IDPs and limited economic opportunities). The most vulnerable households within these communities will be selected to receive food assistance while those who had previous businesses in food or agricultural related businesses will be eligible for livelihood assistance. ADRA will use the modality of in-kind grants for livelihood activities and food vouchers (physical or electronic).
ADRA will target the areas for food security and livelihoods where other complimentary activities are being implemented, such as WASH, shelter and education.

4 - Needs

The governorates of Damascus, Rural Damascus and Homs were selected for intervention based on the high population size, high levels of food insecurity, a high percentage of PIN, especially FHH and PwD, a high percentage of districts with FAS severity levels of 3 or 4, a high number of IDPs and significant level of damages experienced during the conflict. In Rural Damascus, food insecurity in Kisweh affected 47% with additional 14% per cent being at a risk. 22% of HHs are FHH and 27% are dependent on food assistance for covering their nutritional needs. In Ghizlaniyyeh, Duma, 77% of the population spend a severe share of their expenditure on food while 36% were receiving food assistance which presented their main food source.In Homs, Mahin, 60% are food insecure. The FCS reached an acceptable level for only 12% of the population, forcing many borrow money to cover their needs which resulted in 61% of people having outstanding debts. In Hasyaa, 92% of the population are categorized as severe in the HH FES which shows that the clear majority of the people suffer under an extreme economic vulnerability, resulting in 60% who live with food insecurity. In Shin, food insecurity effects 86% of the population with 89% of the population not able to reach an acceptable diversity and frequency of food consumption. Adult women and men are more severely impacted as they frequently engage in adverse coping mechanisms to ensure that boys and girls have enough food to eat (FSA and HNO 2022

5 - Implementation of activities

Potential beneficiaries will be identified through SARC and then verified to ensure that they meet the selection criteria of the project, in line with FAS recommendations. Where possible, ADRA will identify female HH members to receive vouchers as women are generally responsible for food preparation in the household allowing a greater degree of control over food purchases. Each household will receive a monthly voucher, based on FAS guidelines estimated to cover a minimum of 1,500 Kcal p/p/p/d of family food rations (approximately 70% of daily requirements) for a period of 8 months which will allow the targeted HHs an opportunity to return to their previous livelihood or income generating activities and for communities to reinforce newly reestablished market linkages. Items will be identified for redemption under the voucher and will include items in the basic food parcel, as well as additional, nutritionally valuable commodities such as fresh fruit and vegetables, dairy, canned fish and other culturally acceptable items, designed to meet the nutritional needs of children, adolescents, adults, older adults, people with disabilities, as well as male and female. ADRA will follow the sector (as well as CWG) recommendation for the setting of the food voucher amount and livelihood grants. Livelihoods focused on the food chain will be prioritized and beneficiaries selected from the food assistance list and who have had prior experience with their proposed livelihood.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA Syria will ensure that the project works closely with all stakeholders, following a ‘do no harm’ approach. However, there is a risk that the food/assistance will not be shared equally among family members, or that beneficiary households will be put under pressure to share the food redeemed through the vouchers or in-kind assistance provided with other families. There is also a risk of SEA from project staff or stakeholders of the project for beneficiaries to gain access to the assistance, or that beneficiaries will face violence or discrimination during the distribution or for being a project beneficiary. Project staff may face undue pressure to add households to the beneficiary list that do not meet the required selection criteria. In all cases outlined above, ADRA will maintain strong community connections with multiple stakeholders, clearly outlining the aim, purpose and selection criteria of the project to all concerned. Tools will be developed to ensure the impartial selection of beneficiaries and awareness raising will take place among the stakeholders, including beneficiaries themselves about their rights and ways in which to report potential abuse or discrimination. ADRA staff will be trained in protection mitigation measures and humanitarian principals and extensive monitoring will take place to identify and mitigate any potential risks. Feedback will be provided by children, adolescents, adults, older adults and people with disabilities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

There is a risk of SEA from project staff or stakeholders of the project for beneficiaries to gain access to the assistance, or that beneficiaries will face violence or discrimination during the distribution or for being a project beneficiary. PSEA will be specifically addressed through a number of mechanisms. All project staff and volunteers will be required to sign the ADRA Syria Code of Conduct and Safeguarding which meets international standards and clearly outlines the behavior expected from staff and volunteers, including for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and includes mandatory reporting of any suspected cases. In addition, staff are required to undertake compulsory training in PSEA. ADRA also has a fully established complaint mechanism which allows beneficiaries and stakeholders to report any concerns, complaints or feedback anonymously if desired and such sensitive complaints are referred directly to the ADRA Syria Protection Officer. ADRA will ensure a gender balanced team and as much as possible conduct all activities with both male and female staff.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Contextual risk: Although the security situation in target areas relatively stable, it is possible that conflict could reignite. ADRA will monitor the security situation of each area throughout the project lifecycle. ADRA will also provide basic services in many highly vulnerable areas which may place staff, beneficiaries or stakeholders at risk. ADRA will monitor the situation on the ground though UNDSS, local staff and local stakeholders to minimize the risk of harm to staff and beneficiaries. ADRA staff will be trained on security concerns to help prevent incidents. Risk of fraud or misuse of project funds: ADRA Syria has board approved financial management, Anti-Fraud and procurement policies in place, that also include whistle-blowing, conflict of interest and anti-corruption policies. Project risks: The delay or difficulty in obtaining the necessary approvals needed to implement the project activities is a key project risk. ADRA will maintain regular contact with the relevant ministries and will encourage active involvement by all stakeholders in each stage of the project. ADRA will have a beneficiary feedback mechanism in place under this program to ensure appropriate accountability and prevent harm to beneficiaries. ADRA already has an established presence in targeted locations with reliable contractors available to undertake activities.
