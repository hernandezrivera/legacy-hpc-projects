Project Summary

International Medical Corps (IMC) is a US-registered independent affiliate organization of International Medical Corps UK (IMC UK), with which IMC UK shares the same name and charitable objectives and mission. IMC UK and IMC work together to deliver assistance programs in an accountable and effective manner in pursuit of their commonly-held charitable objectives. IMC UK will engage IMC to implement its Programmes in the field, with IMC UK oversight, according to the terms and conditions of any agreement that results from this proposal and the terms of the parties’ administrative service agreement. IMC maintains a branch office in Croatia, IMC Split that provides administrative and operational support to IMC UK and to the programmes on the ground, including but not limited to financial management, procurement management/international procurements, and logistics.
International Medical Corps proposed response plan will support delivery of integrated primary health care services backed by outreach activities for provision of services to vulnerable segments in hard to reach areas which aims at directly serving IDPs and conflict affected populations with health, , mobile medical teams, mental health and a contingent medical supplies system for emergencies.
Through the proposed 2019 response plan , IMC will implement Integrated Health Care services, including primary health care, comprehensive reproductive health service, mental health services, EPI and secondary health care are strengthened and available to vulnerable populations, referral system is reinforced and rape survivors have access to CMR and psychological first aid and that epidemic-prone diseases are detected on time and responded to immediately in Juba PoC, Wau PoC ,Malakal PoC and their catchment areas while in Nyal IMC will provide mobile clinic services.
The primary objective of the proposed project is to improve access to basic curative and preventive health care services to internally-displaced and resident populations in the post-conflict area.
In the area of intervention International Medical Corps will reach out to 140,441 people, of which 70,782 male and 69,619 female. 29,493Children under 5 years will be reached by the project.

Needs

International Medical Corps carried out health and nutrition program review and analysis of the needs in the targeted sites in September and October 2018 with the aim of better understanding the priority health and nutrition needs of conflict affected populations in four geographic areas including Juba PoC, Malakal PoC, Wau PoC and Nyal/Panyijaar, considered as locations in which humanitarian assistance is most needed. With daily movements of people seeking refuge in safe havens due to the on-going fighting among armed groups, the needs of the internally displaced populations continue to change as the demographics shift. Below is a summary of key findings: a) Acute shortage of trained health care professionals residing in operational sites and the necessity to continue staff capacity building activities. At least 50% of IMC health staff (nurse and midwives’ assistants) did not obtain certificates, diplomas or degrees from an accredited health care training institute.b) Consultations rate increased by 6% (8,887 more) in midyear consultations rate as compared to 2017 (mid-year consultations 65,036 in 2017 and 73923 in 2018). This translates into the need for continued service delivery for affected population. c) There is underutilization of reproductive health services particularly family planning service across the sites. This is evidenced by the mid-year progress against indicators in the current award locations (target 3788, achieved 526). d) IMC acknowledged from a routine health facility assessments and consultative meetings with stakeholders the following need: Nyal:a. Minor Rehabilitation of the PHCC including the maternity ward The prevalence of common mental disorders can double in humanitarian crises to up to 20% according to WHO estimates, while people with severe mental disorders are especially vulnerable. In South Sudan, there is a severe shortage of qualified mental health professionals and a continued need to build capacity of general health care providers to address mental health. According to the recent joint assessment report of December 2017 by mental health partners in Malakal, there is a continued concern of increasing mental health problems in all sites specifically suicidal cases among the population particularly Malakal. The major contributing factors include unemployment, alcohol and drug abuse and family issues.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Community consultation has been a key aspect of the design of this proposed project with community leaders being consulted during the ongoing projects as well as during the assessments. IMC will continue to work closely with the community, building the capacity of community health committees to encourage and increase participation. If community participation decreases during the project implementation period IMC will and advice from community leaders to seek alternative ways of engaging the community. Consultation meetings involves community leaders, religious groups, community Health committees , target groups (Adult) and other key stakeholders in the target locations.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IMC is having a community based feedback and complaint mechanism, where, community leaders, beneficiaries and stakeholders can use various means to provide feedback and launch complaints. All the complaints and feedback have been looked after by designated staff members and feedback has been provided for all the actions taken to adapt the feedback or resolve the conflict. The mechanism system uses all the available means like suggestion box, QAAP committees, a direct communication to staff member at office or site, and a direct global communication line to provide maximum access to the community for providing feedback or launch a complaint. IMC ensures to keep the system unbiased and uninfluenced by any person or organization

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

IMC conducts studies like bottleneck analysis, and SQUEAC surveys to assess the accessibility of the beneficiaries to the service delivery points and produce the recommendations to optimize the uptake of the services being provided. Additionally measures access through routine monitoring systems

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

IMC works closely with local authorities and other humanitarian actors to get security information regarding safety and access to the health facilities . Health workers reaches the facilities early, provides services to ensure clients are able to travel back home early enough. In case of insecurity, leading to suspension of the activities beneficiaries are informed through their community leaders, the same is done on return of services. In case of long distance to sites, IMC ensures community based services by establishing either nearby sites or mobile sites. The measures IMC ensures that the service delivery points are child friendly, easy to access, and away from any conflicting zone like military compounds or operational areas.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

This project will prioritize safety and dignity of beneficiaries mainly women and girls through linking them to the provision of psych-social and case management services, group psycho-social activities at the women centres management and support by IMC GBV team. In addition this project will provide access to assistance and services without discrimination of sex or age and also ensuring that most vulnerable groups and groups with special needs are reached and assisted with appropriate confidentiality. Through advocacy, empowerment of targeted groups as well as use of community based committees, this project will enhance population’s ability to reclaim their rights. Increased awareness on consequences of health problems , referral and treatment will enhance utilization of available services for women, girls and boys affected by conflict. All beneficiaries' information and records are kept confidential and are not shared with irrelevant person or organization.
