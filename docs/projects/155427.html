Project Summary

UNFPA's response will target 87,231 most vulnerable women and girls in need of protection from GBV in six targeted districts, namely Bindura, Chegutu, Mudzi, UMP, Tsholotsho and Mangwe, including women and girls, persons with disability, IDPs and key populations.
The project interventions aim at providing risk analysis, service capacity mapping and capacity strengthening, with a particular focus on health response – clinical management of rape; community sensitization and advocacy on GBV prevention; strengthening referral mechanisms for the existing multi-sectorial GBV services (Health, PSS, Safety and legal) and timely disseminating life-saving information about the availability and accessibility of GBV services; distributing NFIs in the form of Dignity kits as a GBV mitigation strategy; Building Capacity of both GBV specialized and non-specialized actors on GBViE prevention, mitigation and response and GBV interventions mainstreaming across all sectors.

Needs

More than 434,360 Zimbabwean women and girls will require some form of protection from Gender Based violence in its various forms (Sexual, physical, emotional, Sexual exploitation and abuse) during the 2019 drought crisis. Protection threats continue to intensify in scale and scope while the population is exposed to degenerating food insecurity. The drought-triggered internal migration generates an increased number of female-headed households in the affected rural areas – in those cases where men migrate to urban areas in search of alternative income sources; or an increased migration of young female from rural areas to urban areas, when the female members of the households are sent by the families to urban areas in search of alternative income sources. In both cases, the limited access to resources contributes to the increase of negative coping mechanisms, such as transactional sex (Sex for food) and child marriage, when the affected communities identify lobola (bride price) as an alternative income source to the agriculture sector revenue. The risk of exposure to domestic violence and intimate partner violence are also recorded on the increase as consequence of the exacerbated tension in the homestead, provoked by crop damages and income losses. The reduced capacity to sustain school fees costs also contributes to an increased children’s drop out from school. An increase in teenage pregnancy is a common consequence of child marriage and school drop outs increased rates, with further negative – and often fatal - consequences for the Sexual and reproductive health of young women and girls. Ongoing operations within other sectors active in the emergency preparedness and response phases also contribute to GBV if programmes are not implemented taking into account the Do-No Harm principle and the core concepts of GBV mainstreaming.
