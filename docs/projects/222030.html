Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services for conflict-affected populations in Yobe State, Northeast Nigeria, encompassing internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities. With a total target population of 34,585 individuals, the intervention focuses on addressing the critical WASH needs resulting from the ongoing humanitarian crisis.
The intervention will provide safe drinking water at the community and household levels by constructing and rehabilitating water supply systems, including boreholes, handpumps, and water networks. To ensure sustainability, the project will support the maintenance and repair of existing water systems through replacement of key components, chlorination activities, and distribution of water treatment solutions.
To safeguard public health and dignity, gender-segregated sanitation facilities such as latrines, showers, and handwashing stations will be constructed and maintained. Vector control measures will be integrated through solid waste and fecal sludge management systems. Emergency desludging and decommissioning of institutional latrines will further mitigate health risks in densely populated areas.
Hygiene promotion activities will focus on raising awareness of critical behaviors such as proper handwashing, household water treatment, and menstrual hygiene management (MHM). Additionally, hygiene kits, sanitation kits, and cholera-prevention supplies will be distributed to address immediate needs and strengthen community resilience to disease outbreaks.
This project aligns with global humanitarian standards and leverages community input to deliver culturally sensitive and inclusive WASH services. By addressing urgent needs and building resilient systems, the intervention aims to improve health, dignity, and living conditions for the most vulnerable populations in Yobe State.

1 - Needs

The project addresses critical WASH needs for conflict-affected populations in Yobe State, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities. These needs were identified through assessments highlighting gaps in access to safe water, sanitation facilities, and hygiene services, which pose significant risks to public health, safety, and dignity. 1. Access to Safe Water:Many communities lack reliable access to safe drinking water due to damaged or insufficient water infrastructure, such as boreholes and handpumps. This exposes residents, especially women and children, to long travel distances, safety risks, and waterborne diseases. The project will rehabilitate and construct water systems, ensuring sustained access to safe water for drinking, cooking, and personal hygiene. 2. Sanitation and Hygiene Facilities:The absence of adequate, gender-segregated sanitation facilities, such as latrines and handwashing stations, compromises privacy and dignity, particularly for women and girls. Poor sanitation has also contributed to health risks, including outbreaks of waterborne and vector-borne diseases. The project will construct and maintain these facilities while addressing vector control through solid waste and fecal sludge management. 3. Maintenance and Sustainability of WASH Infrastructure:Existing water and sanitation systems suffer from neglect, lack of spare parts, and insufficient operational funding. The project will provide maintenance and repair services, including replacing parts for solar and electric pumps, and ensure the sustainability of water systems through fuel provision and chlorination activities. 4. Hygiene Awareness and Supplies:Low awareness of hygiene practices, coupled with limited access to essential hygiene supplies, exacerbates the spread of diseases and reduces community resilience. Women and girls also face challenges with menstrual hygiene management (MHM) due to inadequate supplies and facilities. The project will deliver targeted hygiene promotion campaigns and distribute hygiene kits, sanitation kits, and MHM kits to meet these needs. 5. Protection and Inclusion Gaps:Vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities, the elderly, and female-headed households, face barriers to accessing WASH services. This project will ensure that WASH infrastructure and services are inclusive, culturally appropriate, and accessible, enabling equitable benefits for all. By addressing these needs, the project aims to improve health, dignity, and living conditions for 34,585 individuals in Yobe State, strengthening their resilience and capacity to recover from the ongoing humanitarian crisis.

2 - Activities

Provision of safe drinking water at community and/or household level, through new construction and rehabilitation of water supply systems including handpumps, boreholes rehabilitation or construction, water systems and networks rehabilitation. Ensuring sustained access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene through maintenance/repair and replacement of water system parts (including Handpumps, Solar Systems, Generator Systems, and Electric pumps), operation and maintenance of existing water systems, batch chlorination, bucket chlorination at the points of use, water treatment tablets and provision of fuel for water supply. Provision of gender-segregated sanitation facilities and services (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) to protect the health and dignity of the affected population also supporting vector control campaigns through development of solid waste and fecal sludge management systems. Desludging of emergency and institutional latrines as well as related decommissioning of sanitation facilities Affected people are reached with community-informed hygiene messages promoting hygienic attitudes, behaviors and practices, including, household water treatment, proper handwashing with soap at critical times and Menstrual Hygiene Management. Provision and prepositioning of WASH NFIs to the people in need (hygiene kits, replenishment hygiene kits, WASH cholera kits, WASH Nutrition kits, sanitation kits and MHM kits).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project integrates protection mainstreaming to ensure safety, dignity, and equitable access for all affected populations. Gender-segregated and accessible sanitation facilities will be constructed to protect the privacy and security of women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Water collection points will be strategically located in safe and accessible areas to reduce the risk of violence, particularly for women and children. Inclusive WASH committees and community engagement sessions will involve diverse groups, including youth, the elderly, and marginalized individuals, ensuring their active participation in planning, implementation, and monitoring. Feedback mechanisms, such as suggestion boxes, hotlines, and community meetings, will allow beneficiaries to voice concerns and shape interventions. Hygiene promotion campaigns will address culturally sensitive issues like menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and challenge gender stereotypes while tailoring messages to different literacy levels. To prevent harm, vulnerability assessments will inform risk mitigation strategies, and staff will receive training on protection principles, gender sensitivity, and safeguarding protocols. Monitoring and evaluation will include disaggregated data and protection indicators to measure outcomes and ensure the project remains inclusive and impactful. These modalities collectively uphold humanitarian standards and promote the well-being and resilience of affected communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish inclusive and accessible mechanisms to ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, including women, children, persons with disabilities, the elderly, and marginalized groups. Community feedback will be solicited through multiple channels to accommodate different needs and preferences, ensuring broad participation and accessibility. Dedicated community meetings will be held in safe and neutral locations, with facilitators trained in gender sensitivity and cultural awareness to encourage open dialogue. Focus group discussions will be organized specifically for vulnerable groups, such as women, persons with disabilities, and the elderly, to provide them with a platform to express their unique concerns and suggestions. Anonymous feedback channels, such as suggestion boxes and confidential surveys, will be strategically placed in accessible and frequently visited locations to protect the privacy of respondents. Additionally, a community feedback hotline will be established, enabling individuals to share concerns or suggestions without the need for physical presence. Local facilitators and WASH committee members will be trained to proactively engage vulnerable groups during project activities and gather informal feedback. Periodic monitoring visits by project staff will include one-on-one interactions with community members to identify gaps and collect feedback directly. All feedback will be documented, reviewed, and incorporated into adaptive project planning to ensure continuous improvement. Regular community updates will be provided to demonstrate how feedback has informed changes, reinforcing trust and accountability. This inclusive approach will ensure that the voices of the most vulnerable are heard, respected, and acted upon throughout the project.
