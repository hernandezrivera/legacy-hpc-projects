Project Summary

In Gaza, health related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to health care due to a combination of chronically depleting medical supplies, insufficient human resources, and imposed access restrictions, including for patients referred outside of Gaza. This all has been exacerbated by the most recent crisis of COVID-19 and how this issue has shifted the focus of donors neglecting people with NCDs and even trauma patients.
Moreover, the lack of medical supplies, including diagnostic and therapeutic, alongside limited referral mechanisms, are the primary reasons for interruption in the treatment of chronic diseases that often get neglected in light of the newly emerging disasters in Gaza.
Untreated non-communicable diseases lead to serious complications such as renal failure, disability, and amputations and may lead to death at the end, not to forget the social and economic burden on patients and their families.
In Gaza, interrupted treatment is common factor for cancer and with 58% of medicines depleted by end of Sep 2020. Those patients are considered to be at highest risk of complications and death.
The same can be stated about PHC patients who are usually compromised of chronic diseases patients and other categories such as children with respiratory issues and diarrhea. The shortages of drugs for this category reached a stunning 63% level.
No matter how much organizations intervene, CDS still reports almost the same levels of shortages in the first 9 months of 2020 with averages of 44%% for drugs and 31% for disposables.
To add, MoH does not have a strategic stock that could be prepositioned for acute emergencies in Gaza. When crises hit Gaza, the MoH faces great shortages of drugs and disposables, especially those related to trauma. That is why it is extremely important to preposition drugs and disposables to be released as soon as possible when the situation dictates.
Consequently, MOH is still in dire need of drugs and disposables to be able to save the life of Palestinian patients in Gaza.
MAP is planning to purchase a list of essential drugs and disposables to be delivered to Central Drug Stores and prepositioned in order to ensure the access of trauma and NCDs patients to the treatment and medical care they need even in time of emergency. The project is aiming to help mitigate avoidable mortality and morbidity among the trauma and non-trauma patients. Moreover, the project will be contributing to the current emergency response projects that MAP is implementing in coordination with the health cluster and stakeholders and will be in line with the strategic objective 2 and strategic objective 3; and health cluster strategic objective #1 and #2
MAP will make sure that all the items are provided to all patients attending MOH facilities regardless their gender and age. Also, MAP is going to ensure data protection of the patients if any by using a specific consent form that should be read and signed by the patients being interviewed for the purpose of writing a success story or getting a feedback.

1 - Needs

For more than 13 years now, the health system in Gaza has been suffering from the direct effects of the blockade which has been described by the UN as an “illegal blockade”. The consequences of this have been felt heavily by the patients and year by year, the numbers of people in need are increasing. Specifically, this year after the emergence of COVID-19 and the discovery of the first indigenous cases in the community in August 2020. As per the HNO for 2021, there are: • 15,000 trauma casualties that are still facing morbidities and long-term disabilities• 98,000 acute emergency cases facing mortality and morbidity due to limitations of resources• 60,000 NCD patients at risk (these are only 10% of all NCD cases)• And almost 91,000 NCD patients at risk with high mortality and morbidity ranging from cancer patients to hemodialysis to cardiac surgery patients, diabetic elderly patients and many moreThe COVID-19 pandemic in Gaza exacerbated all the above issues via:• The closure of most PHCs which in turn affected the service delivery for some NCD patients• The closure of borders stopping the of referral cases, due to limitation of resources, to outside of Gaza except for the urgent ones• Shifting the funding towards the COVID-19 crisis and forgetting the above categoriesTo go deeper into the analysis, in September 2020, 47% out of the essential drugs list, were reported at zero-stock levels, and these include 63% shortages in PHC drugs, 58% shortages in oncology and hematology drugs, 55% shortages in MCH drugs, 51% shortages in immune-system drugs, 27% shortages in hemodialysis drugs and last but not least 27% shortages in surgical and ICU related drugs. Additionally, 30% out of the essential medical disposables were reported at zero-stock levels including: cardio-vascular disposables at 65%, hemodialysis disposables at 35%, and 21% shortages in surgery and ICD related disposables. This is considered to be truly staggering keeping in mind that the health system is already collapsing in the wake of the now 13-year blockade, the deepening intra-Palestinian political divide, the energy crisis, inconsistent and reduced payment of public sector staff and last but not least, the COVID-19 pandemic that is hitting the already weakened body of Gaza’s health system hardly.Please note that in addition to the above, the MoH cannot have a strategic stock of prepositioned drugs, disposables and other medical supplies due to the chronic emergency situation and the aforementioned levels of shortages. This calls for INGOs and all relevant stakeholders in the health system to help in strengthening the capacity of the health system via increasing the preparedness to emergencies via the stockpiling of medical supplies. MAP has been one of the very few organizations in Gaza with prepositioned stock of drugs and disposables ready for release any minute now. These lists, like any other lists we procure for the MoH, are coordinated with the CDS, ICD and the health cluster to ensure that MAP is filling critical gaps in the health system.Subsequently, MAP is seeking to procure more items of essential drugs and disposables to continue supporting MOH and to ensure the availability of drugs and medical supplies for non-trauma and trauma cases regardless of their gender or age. Striking a balance of supporting NCD patients and trauma ones is crucial at this stage not to further exacerbate the suffering of any patient, especially with COVID-19 pandemic striking Gaza hard.

2 - Activities

- To procure a list of essential drugs items used for NCD and trauma cases- To procure a list of disposables used for NCD and trauma cases- To procure and preposition drugs and disposables items in coordination with MOH to be released during crisis times. Please note that MAP has a very adaptive and flexible procurement system that is intended to facilitate work and speed up the delivery of these urgently needed items. We take into consideration, when awarding, the speedy delivery, quality of the items, brands suggested, and prices that are fluctuating day-by-day amid the COVID-19 pandemic. Accountability:MAP has recently been active in terms of applying CHS standards as we are a member of the CHS alliance and recently finished an assessment on how to improve things further at our offices globally. MAP has a dedicated officer that follows up on accountability issues with the beneficiaries. In this case too, CDS can come back to MAP with any complaints or comments about the drugs and disposables provided to take actions with suppliers. In addition, MAP applies rules and regulations in the contracts signed to make sure that the MoH and CDS are benefiting to the maximum limits in terms of coverage of test costs, and the provision of free samples for analysis. In addition, MAP tends to procure items with minimum 75% of it's shelf life is still valid. To ensure the safety of the propositioned items, MAP has an open contract with Al Awda hospital to store MAP’s medical items in good conditions and also tend to release the propositioned items at least six month before its' expiry date if the items were not released earlier for emergency response(though it's rarely happening). To add, the CDS collaborates and participates with MAP in every step of the project from the design phase (identifying needs) to implementation (technical committees, selection, etc). MAP includes the feedback of beneficiaries as a routine part of our project monitoring and we continually learn from this feedback to improve project design and our effectiveness.

Safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults. MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS).They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns.
