Project Summary

Right from the start of the Malteser International (MI) program in Nigeria in July 2017 we succeeded in building up own structures and closely coordinating with
established local actors (UN, INGO, NNGO and Government actors).The measures implemented by MI were seamlessly integrated into the existing overall
structure of other actors in North Nigeria and gaps in the implementation of WASH related activities could be addressed by our program. While some INGOs
focused only on the construction of new infrastructure, Malteser International was able to close supply gaps efficiently and effectively by rehabilitating existing but
dysfunctional WASH structures. Both camp situations and off camp situations were addressed by working with host communities and informal IDP (Internally
Displaced People) settlements. One of the measures was the successful implementation of hygiene promotion through an integrated community and household
approach. During the household visits, of which many were woman led, it was found that many of the IDPs struggle with mental health issues due to the
experiences by living in a context of violent conflict. Especially woman and children have experienced violence in many forms when fleeing their home
communities. Often being traumatized, these people are struggling with the day to day life, with some of the woman having to provide care for several children. But
also sexual abuse in camp situations or domestic violence within families are an issue, often underestimated and not accounted for adequately. Children frequently
show signs of violent behavior as they are trying to compensate negative experiences, to which there is often also no adequate response. Suicidal tendencies,
marginalization and drifting off into extremism are frequent effects when traumas go unnoticed.
To address this dire need, Malteser International is planning the establishment of a Mental Health / Psycho-social Support Mobil (MH/PSS) Unit. The MH/PSS Unit
will consist of one Psychotherapist, one Psychotherapist Assistant, one Administration Officer and one Driver, plus vehicle. It will be linked to the MI Hygiene
Promoters’ community work, working with host communities and in IDP settlements. The hygiene promoters will be linked with the specialists for mental health and
able to refer people who are showing signs of trauma. Depending on need and acceptance, the MH/PSS Unit will treat people individually and in group sessions,
focusing on woman and children in both, host communities (e.g. with high levels of domestic violence) and IDPs settlements. For hardship cases and to continue
support, the team will establish a referral system to established Mental Health Treatment facilities in Maiduguri, Borno State. The MH/PSS will be initiated by
identification of trauma through the MI hygiene promoters who will receive a training to facilitate the identification of trauma cases. The offer of support will be
communicated in our target communities to enable beneficiaries to contact the MH/PSS team discretely. A high level of discretion is important since in many
communities mental health treatment is still stigmatized. Contact details of the MH/PSS Unit will be provided to other national and international actors working in
the area, to enable them to make use of the MH/PSS Unit for potential trauma cases that are being identified.
The measures will be addressing newly arrived people in neglected settlements in Jere and Bama (e.g.Pulka), overcrowded camps in MMC (e.g. Garba Buzu) and
not yet identified hard to reach areas.

Needs

Malteser International has been working in Borno State since July 2017, providing Water and Sanitation facilities as well as conducting Hygiene Promotionmeasures. One of our activities was the distribution of NFI / Hygiene Kits, including dignity sets. In the course of implementing our program, MI conducted oneinitial technical assessment (May 2017), intended to design the WASH Program. During further implementation, detailed technical assessments and socioeconomicassessments have been conducted in all target communities to create bill of quantities for the infrastructure components as well as householdinformation for the design of the hygiene and health promotion components.For the assessments MI is conducting a community approach by involving the beneficiaries in all stages of project implementation. We have established andtechnically trained Water Committees as well as hygiene promotion volunteers, which are members of the target communities. Implementing hygiene promotionactivities on household level made it possible to identify the need for Mental Health / Psycho-social Support for the target audience. It allowed for detailed and veryvaluable information of the beneficiaries that was made available to MI staff in a trustful relationship, thus providing the base for the proposed intervention.
