Project Summary

"Since influx in 2017, Save the Children has been providing a quality and integrated health service and has become one of the most trusted health partners to the affected beneficiaries. SCI will deliver an inclusive, gender responsive & comprehensive healthcare services through 3 HP and 2 PHCC over the JRP period in 5 camps of Ukhiya and Teknaf to ensure equitable access to essential healthcare services for Rohingya and host communities including persons with disability and gender diverse population (LGBTQI). As per localization strategy, some health intervention will be covered by the local implementing partners PHD, CDD, Bandhu Social Welfare Society and Nari Maitree. Project will ensure sex segregated areas/rooms and presence of sufficient female staff. Health facilities will deliver primary health care service with special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health (SRMNCAH); MHPSS; rehabilitation including need based assistive device provision and noncommunicable diseases in line with the minimum package of essential Health services (MPEHS). Along with regular health consultation, PHCC will offer 24/7 emergency care IPD service, laboratory; PMTCT; BEmONC, CEmONC service & SGBV services (CMR, IPV and Domestic Violence). All health facility will offer integrated services with Nutrition (IYCF-E & C-MAMI), Rehabilitation, WASH, Early childhood development and Child Protection by ensuring coordination and referral in between, ensuring a child friendly corner considering “Do no harm policy”. To ensure IPC measure, safe disposal practice, a dedicated IPC focal will work closely with facility level IPC committee and provide necessary guidance aligned with IPC WG and MoH. The recently closed SCI SARI-ITC structure will be repurposed as CEmONC facility, and 10 beds will remain stand by to response any communicable disease outbreak. Clinical services will be complemented with health promotion and community engagement activities through a robust community health worker (CHWs). Engagement of women, community, and religious leaders to raise awareness on health and identify gender-responsive actions at health facility and arrange the necessary intervention to address those. To cultivate ownership and resilience, the project will support existing and establish peer support groups and committees for adolescent girls, boys, people with disabilities, and those with diverse SOGIESC. Training will encompass life skills, including health education, nutrition, gender and inclusion, DRR, MHPSS, and behavioral competencies for self-reliance & community resilience. All staff and volunteers will be trained on gender, GBV, Protection, PSEAH, CP, child safeguarding. 3 MMT aligned with EPR Working Group will be providing services in any emergency health response following any natural or manmade disaster. All health facilities will be accessible and environment friendly for all age group and for PWD by ensuring ramp, disable friendly latrine, triage area, signage, lighting, and skilled workforce to prioritize and manage geriatric population and PWD.
SCI has a robust MEAL system and according to the Program’s Logical Framework, develop a MEAL plan including specific indicators, data disaggregated by SADD etc. MEAL team will apply IPTT, Quality Benchmark, Medical incident reporting (MIR) and SCI Global Medical Team recommended Clinical Standard scorecard in all the health facility to monitor the service quality. Multiple channels such as the toll-free hotline, help desks & online feedback response mechanism (FRM) are using to receive complaints and feedback. SCI gender focal person will ensure Implementation of gender action plan and monitoring of activities in health indicators. S/he will maintain coordination with GiHA and health partners to share update on gender responsive health developments, including needs, gaps, challenges which helps to attain ideal gender-sensitive practices in health response."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"In the 6th year of the Rohingya influx, Bangladesh is hosting around 1 million Rohingya refugees (half of them are children), largely depending on humanitarian aid agencies. To cover their overall health needs, 77 Health partners are providing essential healthcare services from 139 Health facilities including HP, PHCC, CEmONC Centers and field hospitals. Additionally, Upazilla Health Complexes and Sadar Hospital play a pivotal role in managing cases with critical needs. According to Health sector monthly bulletins of 2023, Presently, a surge in communicable diseases (AWD, cholera, Dengue, and hepatitis) is observed in camps. In August 2023, a peak in dengue outbreak saw 12,229 cases, with 11% hospitalized and a 0.1% fatality rate for Dengue. Due to the high prevalence of scabies (39.9%) and OPD caseload, preparations are underway for mass drug administration starting November 2023 in a phased approach. From January to August 2023, the Health sector recorded 3.8 million curative consultations, a 7% increase from 2022, where in September, with 25% reduction from August 2023 a total of 415,898 OPD Consultations were recorded however, it is too early to associate whether this reduction due to rollout of health cards. The Crude Death Rate among refugees was 2.1 per 1000 people (1,946 deaths). Maternal and Child-related mortalities constitute 20% of total deaths (2015), with Infant Mortality at 25.2 per 1,000 live births and under-5 mortality at 28.4 per 1,000 live births. Maternal Mortality Rate is estimated at nearly 300 per 100,000 live births. According SRH WQ Q2 bulletin, a total of 7,363 facility-based deliveries was conducted where approximately 4.7% were conducted through caesarian section. Out of these facility-based deliveries, 71.53% were deliveries from the Rohingya community. Within the facilities, 97.2% of the births resulted in live births, while 2.82% were classified as stillbirths. The number of mothers attending ANC 4 or more visits rising from 6,060 during the first quarter to 6,371 in the Q2, representing a significant growth of 5.13%. The number of PNC visits declined 5.92% from the 1st quarter to the 2nd. Currently, despite of extended need, the number of field or secondary level hospital with caesarean section is inadequate. Nearly half of children under 2 years old (41% refugees, 38% host community) were born at home without medical assistance (REACH BGD MSNA). NGO clinics were the most common birth facilities for refugee children (46%). Although the majority in both population groups could access healthcare, many faced barriers: host community cited costs (29%) and distance (20%), while refugees reported overcrowded services (45%) or unavailable treatment (36%). SCI MSNA-2022, findings underscore, over half of the participants demanded SRHR services, including safe delivery, Ante Natal care (ANC), Post Natal Care (PNC), Family Planning (FP), and Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services. The Children Consultation report-2022 highlights a shortage of essential medicines at health facilities and inadequate health services, particularly in nutrition and adolescent girls' menstrual hygiene management/reproductive health.Despite of numerous initiatives from Govt and Health sector, burden of NCDs also rising. Increased burden of communicable and NCDs prevalence claims the strengthening of lab services in camp level. Since influx, several extreme weather conditions, fire incidents were experienced which demands appropriate EPR plan in Health facility level to minimize the massive loss of human life and other resources. There is gap of integrated health services leading beneficiary sufferings in terms of movement from facility, service availability and accessibility. All the facility can integrate the nutrition, rehabilitation, and Early Childhood Development (ECD) services can also plan to support the education and child protection thematic area under the health services. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Rohingya refugee crisis is a protracted crisis with 100% aid dependency. SCI has always been in a neutral position to share all the suggestion and recommendations and it remained same during the rationalization exercise. Anticipating the funding status of 2024, need assessment, suggestions & recommendations from beneficiaries; CIC; Sector and rationalization result, SCI has phased out one health post from Jan,23 and planning to phase out three Health posts operation from Jan,24. SCI will further concentrate on establishing specialized health service (CEmONC) where discussion is underway with the donor and converting 1 HP of camp-15 to PHCC in end of 2023. Considering the phaseout and modification, SCI continues to coordinate with CiCs, and other actors to ensure needs and gaps are timely and effectively addressed. SCI has established a good coordination mechanism within and outside the camps, engaging all the major stakeholders who are closely involved with Rohingya and Host community operation. Stakeholders like, RRRC & its Heath coordinators, CICs, Site Management, UNO, Local Chairman, UHFPO, CS, Camp Health focals and beneficiaries have acknowledged & appreciated SCI's contribution for their vital presence during any emergencies and that reflects a healthy relationship between SCI with all the stakeholders. SCI's approach to sustainability is focused on strengthening community-based mechanisms that support Rohingya and host communities including Person with disability and gender diverse population to access to essential integrated health services. The outcome of the health facility rationalization exercise initiated by the health sector might have an impact on the health-seeking behavior of beneficiary whereas SCI is focusing on adequate and sensible planning by proper coordination and collaboration to minimize the unwanted impact. Team also emphasizing on inter-sectoral approach to assure the attainment of the sectoral objectives with expected outcome duly.

SCI mainstream the protection theme by adapting all the protection principles from the beginning of the project and adolescents, PWD, Pregnant & lactating mother and diverse populations identified as vulnerable groups with the identification of potential threat for them. Health facilities will be accessible and environment friendly for all age group and for PWD by ensuring ramp, disable friendly latrine, triage area, signage, lighting, and skilled workforce. Community strength and coping strategy also assess to ensure participation of all age and gender groups. Analyze the identified need, risk, and strengths, all the interventions will plan with necessary resource mobilization and capacity enhancement initiatives. Project objectives and indicators will set prioritizing the vulnerable group including PWD & LGBTQI. Peer support groups for adolescent girls, boys, people with disabilities, and gender diverse population and their capacity will be enhanced on health education, nutrition, gender and inclusion, disaster risk reduction (DRR), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and behavioral competencies for self-reliance. Accessibility of all the interventions with no harm and accountability of service providers will be secured by the technical supervision and sufficient implications of monitoring process. Empowerment and participations of programme participants will be ensured by the applying multiple channels for complaint Feedback Response and Reporting Mechanism. All the staffs and volunteers will be trained and equipped on contextualized protection issues and humanitarian principles. To ensure appropriate referral, service mapping will be there in each facility and relevant stakeholders will be communicated to enhance protection, avoid duplication, and address risks in a collaborative manner.

3.3 - Age & Disability

According to UNHCR, among near 1 million population in Rohingya camp, around 52% are children and about 4% are older aged population. Moreover, around 1% of total Rohingya population seeks disability support from UNHCR. According to REACH BGD Factsheet (May 2021), In Camp, the prevalence of person with disabilities (aged 2 and above) was 12%. About 17% & 35% of HH reportedly had at least one older person and at least one People with Disabilities respectively. Overall, more than half the person with disabilities had reportedly not received any assistive devices despite needing them. Moreover, risk of NCDs among the older aged and their complication affect their quality of life and health seeking behavior. SCI ensures the measures to tailor their specific vulnerabilities and developmental needs, to enable them to enjoy their rights in the existing circumstances of critical health, safety, and security. For the enjoyment of health-related rights of Rohingya children SCI has providing comprehensive emergency assistance and health care to safeguard them through healthcare services, healthcare procedures and related conditions which has work together effectively. Childrens are provided with EPI vaccination, IYCF-E & CMAMI services, need based general health consolation whereas older population receive need based clinical care along with lab facilities, NCD medication, therapeutic services, and preventive awareness messages. All the health facility underwent disability audit by disability focused agency and all the recommendations were addressed accordingly within the recommended time. SCI has the provision of screen and identify the person with disabilities, provide the therapeutic services and need based assistive devices. Additionally, disability support community has been formed and to build self-reliance they will be capacitate on life skills, including health education, nutrition, gender and inclusion, disaster risk reduction DRR, MHPSS, and behavioral competencies.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Beneficiaries of all categories are actively involved in the initial phase to end of the project. SCI MEAL team routinely conduct the need assessment, consultation, base line, midline and end line survey to identify the need and to recommend the required services based on the report. As latest example, SCI MEAL team conducted JMSNA in 2022 and Children Consultation in same year. This year another JMSNA is undertaking in the different geographical location of Cox’s Bazar & Bandarban. Under the MEAL activity, project will ensure proactive and reactive collection of complaints & feedback and follow-up in similar way. In practice, accountability covers participation of children, women and all other community population get regular, timely and accessible information and they share their complaints & feedback in convenient way. Building staff & volunteers competencies for accountable programming also make sure during this project period. Multiple channels to collect feedback like Community meetings, exit interviews, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation, child friendly mechanism and post distribution monitoring mechanisms are practicing that are accessible to even older persons and people with disabilities, as a gender & disability inclusive platform for community members to raise their complaints in a safe and dignified manner. Gender sensitivity will be ensured during feedback collection (Exp. Male will interview male and female will interview female etc.). Received Feedback and Complaints are processed, analyzed, and referred to relevant sectors on a weekly basis & as required. This helps to focus on the areas of improvement and ensuring participatory approach from the beginning to the end of project.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

SCI is an active member in the ISCG PSEAH Network, a coordination platform of humanitarian aid agencies in Cox's Bazar and shares capacity with the platform members. Apart from this mainstream PSEAH SCI promotes PSEAH awareness, prevention, reporting, and response. So, to do this, SCI contributes to staff awareness through trainings (Staff and volunteer inductions within 7 days of joining, 100% staff and volunteer refresher once in a year, PSEAH mandates in the staff meetings, developing learning materials (posters, flyers, videos, Safeguarding Pocketbooks, Volunteer Flipchart etc.), contributing to community capacity by establishing Community Champions etc. All the health facilities are posted with printed IEC material on PSEAH Also, SCI supports safer recruitment practices (background check, volunteer vetting, PSEAH statements in job advertisements), PSEAH risk identification and mitigation measures in programme deliveries. SCI have a zero-tolerance approach to any PSEAH concern and confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms are available. Organizations take the necessary actions after receiving a SEAH complaint that are in line with the SOP for the SEAH Complaint Referral in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh along with the survivor centered approach. There is a dedicated team for conducting safeguarding investigations. SCI also contributes to create partner capacity in conducting safeguarding investigations.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Since Rohingya influx, Cox’ Bazar has experienced several cyclone and massive fire incident with loss of life and other resources. Considering the context, SCI has developed EPR plan for each health facilities including the stock of medicine and other medical supplies. SCI MMTs, staff, volunteers, and partners receive training on emergency preparedness and response (EPR), basic first aid (BFA), and psychological first aid (PFA) to be able to respond during disasters and to provide emergency management support during and after an emergency. Additionally, SCI focus on Socializing risk-informed business continuity plans for SCI and partner facilities focusing on cyclone, flash flood, earthquakes, and fire hazards to staff and volunteers. Periodic assessment of SCI and partner facilities conducted based on vulnerability to hazards and develop and update business continuity plans to maintain services during and after disasters. Coherence with Health sector and CHW WG, DRR awareness messages for especially for vulnerable groups like children, PLwDs, and diverse SOGIESC to enhance their understanding and preparedness during disasters. Continue communication with the SMS team during any disaster to foster the support exchange. In all health infrastructure, treated bamboo were used as a construction material which creates less adverse effect on the environment by using less bamboo. Also, SCI is Maintaining Climate-resilient healthcare infrastructure and environment friendly programme activities through implementing low-carbon solutions Such as use of solar systems, waste reduction & proper waste management and water supply systems results of strengthening structural resilience through durable and adaptive approaches. SCI have introduced vertical tree plantation in health facility premises with the use of waste plastic bottle. Tree plantation activity in all Health facility premises is continuing to reduce the risk of infrastructural damage during any disaster.
