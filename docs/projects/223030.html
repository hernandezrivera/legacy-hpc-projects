Project Summary

This project will prevent and mitigate water, sanitation and hygiene related disease burdens of 15,750 individuals living in affected communities across Mafa, Konduga and Bade Local Government Areas of Borno and Yobe States, prioritizing improved access to sustained water, sanitation and hygiene services either through new constructions or rehabilitation of safe water sources, maintenance of existing systems, extension of water networks to underserved areas where permissible and regular water quality testing and monitoring to assess the safety of both water sources, ensuring water is free from contamination.
Targeted interventions to prevent and mitigate the dangers of open defecation by promoting the use of sanitary facilities through behavioural change approaches and community-led initiatives will be implemented. New household latrines and bathing shelters will be constructed and existing ones rehabilitated where permissible. Handwashing stations will be installed at constructed and rehabilitated facilities. Sanitation kits will be provided to ensure sanitation facilities are always kept clean. Solid waste management system will be established to promote safe disposal of household waste.
Affected communities will be empowered the to actively participate in water management and sanitation efforts through hygiene promotion campaigns that will focus on “small doable actions’ tailored to local contexts, ensuring they are feasible and impactful. These campaigns will be complemented by the distribution of hygiene and menstrual hygiene management kits through vouchers.
The project acknowledges that vulnerabilities vary hence particular emphasis will be placed on the most vulnerable groups such as children, women, the elderly, and persons with disabilities.
This project aims to leverage on JDF’s knowledge, expertise, reach/access, and resources, benefiting from combined and varied strengths of the government and other partners toward the shared goal of improving living conditions and promoting personal and public well-being of affected populations in Mafa, Konduga Bade LGAs of Borno and Yobe States through sustained access to improved WASH services

1 - Needs

After fourteen (14) years, the protracted humanitarian situation in North East (NE) Nigeria remains dire as insecurity remains fluid and complex. In 2024, the humanitarian crisis in Nigeria is driven by conflict and displacement, food insecurity and malnutrition, disease outbreaks like cholera, natural disasters including floods, camp closures in Maiduguri, relocations and resettlements. As a result, 7.9 million people are need of life-saving humanitarian response (GHNO, November,2024). Borno alone hosts approximately 85% of the displaced population, with Adamawa and Yobe accommodating 7.7% and 7.2%, respectivelyThe impact of the crisis is aggravated by recurrent hostilities, expanding global humanitarian needs, rising operational costs and commodity prices and high inflation. Displaced populations and returnees rely heavily on the limited resources of host communities, resulting in constrained access to essential Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services. Recurring floods worsen these challenges by elevating the risk of waterborne disease outbreaks like cholera and increasing the likelihood of further displacements. According to the WASH sector HNRP draft 2025, 5.2 million people remain in need of emergency water, sanitation and hygiene services across 24 high priority LGAs across the most affected states of Borno (12), Adamawa (7) and Yobe (5) with a severity classification of 4-Extreme.On 9th September 2024, the Alau dam located about 20km in Konduga LGA breached its banks spilling 112 million cubic metres of water (Reported total capacity) that flooded major parts of Maiduguri including Gwange, The University of Maiduguri Teaching Hospital, Bama Road, Shehu’s Palace, and communities in Mafa and Konduga LGAs. In Mafa LGA even before the September 9th floods in the state, 984 households, 4,035 individuals in Mafa rural communities including Simari, Zanari, Malakyariri and the GSS IDP camps were affected by flood. The WASH sector estimates thar 65, 598 internally displaced persons, returnees and their hosting communities require improved wash services.Communities in Konduga located by the banks of the Alau dam including Musakaleri, Kalaeri and Bulturi communities were also affected by the flood as over 50 shelters and hundred hectares farmlands washed way by the rampaging water.154,289 People are in Need (PiN) of emergency WASH services as estimated by the WASH sector.In Bade, 57,086 IDPs and host community members remain in need of critical WASH services following widespread cholera in the stateAffected populations now find themselves in deplorable conditions without sufficient safeguards and further exposed to heightened protection risks. Several houses, businesses and sources of livelihoods have been destroyed, aggravating the protracted and complex humanitarian situation in the state. According to an Aljazeera report, the Borno state government is projecting 3-5 years of adequate funding to repair the damage caused by the flood.Identified WASH gaps in the targeted locations of Konduga, Mafa, and Bade LGAs in Borno and Yobe States include, the prevalence of open defecation occasioned by occurrence of acute watery diarrhea/cholera incidences this is evident as Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states have all declared cholera outbreaks in 2024. Limited access to safe/improved water sources, poor access to sanitation facilities characterized by use of unimproved latrines, low levels of knowledge in good hygiene practices and where knowledge exist, there us a gap in practice and sustainability of the WASH infrastructures, lack of access to hygiene items including menstrual hygiene items and loss of livelihoods. Immediate funding and coordinated interventions are crucial to supporting displaced populations to address critical WASH needs, mitigate disease outbreaks and build resilience against future shocks.

2 - Activities

The response will improve and sustain access to WASH services to 15,750 men, women, girls, and boys including IDPs, returnees, and their host communities in Mafa, Konduga and Bade LGAs of Borno and Yobe States respectively, prioritizing: Improved access to safe water through rehabilitation/repairs of strategic water facilities, operation, and maintenance of water facilities, water quality testing analysis, chlorination and regular monitoring of Free Residual Chlorine(FRC) at the water points and house hold levels. Sanitations: In order to increase sustainable access to improved household sanitation at scale in target locations, JDF will adopt market-based programming to close the huge sanitation access gaps aimed at promoting a community-driven process. Specific sanitation activities will include construction and rehabilitation of emergency latrines and bath shelters where permissible, drainage and canal emptying, solid waste management Hygiene: Hygiene promotion activities will include door-to-door visits, focused group discussions with a focus on menstrual hygiene management, mass/speaker campaigns, and the use of Information, Education, and Communication materials/activities targeting behaviour change. Hygiene promotional activities will be carried out by trained male and female community-based hygiene promoters including persons with disabilities. Hygiene promotional activities will be supported by the provision of hygiene items that will include menstrual hygiene management items to adolescent girls, Pregnant and lactating women.JDF will identify and train a Case Area Target Intervention (CATI) team as anticipatory measures to diseases outbreaks like acute watery diarrhea, cholera ad other infectious WASH related disease. Cholera kits will be prepositioned for use by the CATI team on a need basis.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To strengthen accountability to the affected populations, JDF will be implementing a component of her Community-Based Accountability and Feedback System (CoBAFS). JDF will at the inception of the project, open a project file with the community leaders (Bulama, Lawan) and will collectively agree on the modality of tracking the project progress as in line with the design submitted. The file will contain relevant project documents and will be updated monthly with activity reports. A key component of JDFs community and accountability feedback system is the establishment of the CoBAC. The CoBAC with support from JDF will organize monthly Project Feedback and quality implementation review meeting. These meetings will be held in the presence of community stakeholders, community members, implementing sector, AAP/CEAL WG, Government sector representatives present in the community/camp. The CoBAC will give feedback on different areas of the organizations response in the community including implementation timeline, quality of delivery, collaboration, and coordination etc, after which JDF will be graded. JDF will then have a feedback meeting with the CoBAC to analyze the feedback and identify key areas for improvement. The CoBAC will be a 15-man committee which will be made up of five 3man sub-committees made up of representatives from Community/religious leaders (3), Men (3), Women (3), PWDs (3), and Youth (3). Who will independently monitor projects and give feedback to the CoBAC.
