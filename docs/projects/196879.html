Project Summary

RRI is a German NGO with two programs in the country that has rehabilitated more than 6,300 houses and built more than 11,500 L-shaped shelters to UNHCR standards in Iraq since 2009. in Syria has not yet built many shelters due to the MOLA permit, which has been a problem for us in the past. However, a new framework created by RSRP's professional shelter team (including senior international and local staff) together with Syria Trust for Development STD that will ensure that the permit will be issued within the timeframe set out in the HRP 2022-2023. According to the memorandum of understanding, Syria Trust is RSRP’s facilitator and local partner within Syria that facilitate the approval of the projects on the ground. It is worth mentioning that according to the technical assessment conducted in 2018, STD has carried out the rehabilitation of damaged houses in DeirEzzor in collaboration with the RSRP. The main objective of the project is to provide access to basic NFI and shelter services over 2023 in order to bring about a more profound change for 83,585IDPs, returnees, and overburdened vulnerable host communities and 15000 households in the severely neglected sub-districts of Rural Damascus,Homs,Hama,Arraqqa,Dar’a and Aleppo Governorates, interventions and target as following:
Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing adherence to shelter sector technical guidelines for 8500 in Qudsiya (500), At Tall(500), Sahnaya(500), Eastern Kwaires(750), Raqama(1000), Alqusayr(500),As Saan(500),Sabka(750),Dar’a(750),Mzeireb(750),Izra(1250),Hrak(750).
Distribution of 15010 core winter and NFI kits for 75,085 in Qudsiya (12500), At Tall(10000), Sahnaya(7500), Eastern Kwaires(5000), Raqama(4155), Alqusayr(7150),AsSaan(1930),Sabka(4350),Dar’a(7500),Mzeireb(5000),Izra(5000),Hrak(5000).
Worth to mention that, The compositions of core NFI kits aligned with NFI sector guidelines and recommendations.
The consequences of the conflict and the outbreak of Covid-19 have been particularly severe, transforming many parts of the various governorates into hard-to-reach areas where people are suffering from a lack of basic needs due to the enormous destruction and cut-off access to markets, the loss of their livelihoods and income, the increase in the cost of basic products, and the sharp drop in the value of the currency. Displaced people, returnees, and the overburdened host communities face enormous difficulties in providing basic needs for their families and ensuring survival. Following an assessment of the NFI and shelter gap, as well as the needs and timeline for intervention, all vulnerable groups and genders are considered, including men, women, boys, and girls. In addition, the number of kits, the components of the kits, and the target locations were determined, as well as the number of female HHs, vulnerable elderly, persons with disabilities, HHs living in overcrowded spaces, and HHs without income sources. It is worth noting that the project is in line with the HNO2023 and the sector specific targets (SO1 and SO3) and priorities:
Strategic Objective 1: Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs.
Strategic Objective 3: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities. The intervention areas may be increased according to the increase in the areas that will be under control (GoS), As well, RSRP is implementing the project directly only.

4 - Implementation of activities

The RSRP's responses are in coordination with the sector's response strategy, following the needs-based approach,identification of PINs under HNO2023 at the sub-district level, and compliance with the strategic objectives(SO1&SO3).The RSRP proposes the activities through direct implementation only, following the main pillars to achieve the proposed actions in coordination with MoLA, STD, sector and other relevant stakeholders. -The recruitment processes.-Registration through the local community and social media. Identification of83585 beneficiaries by KOBO according to the vulnerability criteria of female HHs,vulnerable elderly, persons with disabilities,HHs(living in overcrowded conditions and without income sources).-Identification of 1400 houses in need of rehabilitation by KOBO through vulnerability assessment considering female HHs,vulnerable elderly,persons with disabilities,HHs(living in overcrowded conditions and without income sources).-Procurement process.-Distribution of the15010kits through home visits, collection of tokens and signing of the receipt form by the beneficiary. One month after the completion of the project, RSRP will conduct PDM through questionnaire/KOBO to assess beneficiary access, taking into account all safety measures.-Rehabilitation of1400 selected damaged houses.-Regular field visits will be conducted to ensure quality. The handover process will be carried out by our field staff in coordination with local authorities and according to UN/RRI.
