Project Summary

Health systems in South Sudan have been impacted by prolonged conflict, natural hazards, epidemics including COVID-19 pandemic. Reliance on humanitarian assistance due to inadequate health financing by the government (<1.9% allocation of the 2021 national budget allocation), inequitable distribution of functional health facility, human resources for health gaps and access constraints due to unprecedented flooding, frequent subnational conflict and runaway inflation contribute to challenges in meeting the health needs of underserved populations in Rumbek East (Lakes State), Aweil East (Northern Bahr El Ghazal State), Koch, Mayendit, Panyijar & Rubkona Counties (Unity State).
The International Rescue Committee (IRC) proposes to respond to the immediate lifesaving health care needs of 340,728 people in need (71,579 Men, 79,095 Women, 93,220 Boys and 96,833 Girls with the inclusion of the elderly and persons living with disability. This project will provide essential outpatient care (communicable and non-communicable diseases), child health care, safe motherhood, Sexual and reproductive health, mental health care, gender-based violence response and emergency preparedness and response. The project will provide support to 12 static health facilities (2 PHCCs each in Aweil East, Rubkona, Panyijar, Koch; 1 PHCC each in Kapoeta South, Aweil South, Rumbek East, & Mayendit), 10 Rapid Response mobile (RRM) outreach posts (2 RRMs each in Panyijar, Mayendit, Koch, and Kapoeta South; 1 RRM each in Aweil South & Aweil East) and strengthen community health services by deploying 80 Boma Health Workers each in the 8 targeted Counties (39% of the BHI gap coverage).
Support to static health facilities will engage communities and County Health Departments (CHD) to map out underserved populations through assessments of intersectoral needs and prioritization of displaced populations without access to health services. The project will support the rehabilitation of structures, qualified staffing, pharmaceuticals supplies and commodities, health management information systems and reporting. Integration of services will be a priority of the project through multi-sector collaboration with food security and livelihoods, nutrition and protection mainstreaming. Providers will have capacity strengthening on provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures. The flexible approach deployed by the RRMs including a rapid deployment approach ensures that these teams can provide immediate response within the first 72 hours of an emergency. The IRC will staff, resource, and facilitate early deployment of the RRM teams to locations pre-identified by the community and CHDs factoring the health priorities of affected populations.
Coordination through engagement with local duty bearers and partners, engagement with national and subnational coordination mechanisms will be an integral component of the response. The project will consider the perspective of the affected people through IRC’s Client Responsiveness Measurement Framework using evidence generated during implementation to amplify the voices of the affected people on services provision and decision making. Disability inclusive client responsiveness will be prioritized through engagement of organizations of persons with disability where these already exist and by direct data collection at health service points using the Washington Short Set of questions on disability. Joint support supervision will be an integral part of the intervention to provide mentorship and coaching to health staff. The project will mainstream COVID-19 risk mitigation measures across all service delivery channels to protect clients and the service providers.

1 - Needs

South Sudan faces one of the largest displacement and humanitarian crises globally resulting in access constraints that impact basic services delivery to vulnerable people. Limited access to integrated, high-quality health services by communities has been further compounded by frequent conflicts, natural hazards, food insecurity and epidemics including the COVID-19 pandemic.545,445 persons (62470 Returnees, 126850 IDP's and 206420 host communities) in the targeted counties need health services including 81817 PLWD's . Displacement of populations due to subnational conflicts, floods, and food insecurity often leads to disrupted access to health services. Overcrowding as populations seek shelter and safety resulting in public health problems like increases in cases of Acute watery Diarrhea (AWD). Only 10% of households have access to toilets (worse in Greater Upper Nile regions) with open defecation the norm across the Country. Currently, 20 Counties are classified as cholera hotspots at risk of new outbreaks. Confirmed Measles and Rubella outbreaks in 15 counties in 2022 have been reported in the context of low immunization coverage (Penta 3 coverage at 49% nationally). Hepatitis E Virus outbreaks continues in Bentiu IDP settlement (Case Fatality Rate 0.76%).Health services functionality is low with around 8% of health facilities providing the full package of the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services. 12% of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns, increasing to more than 40% of households feeling unsafe while collecting water in areas with frequent conflicts. Insecurity also affects access to health services as access corridors are impacted or facilities closed during acts of violence. Women and girls, especially in IDP settings, are disproportionately affected. Only 59% of the population reportedly access health services within 1 hour. Under 5 mortality rate is high at 96.2 per 1000 live births. 75% of all child deaths are from malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia. This could be significantly reduced by a robust community health system, however, 58% of Bomas in the country still don’t have Boma Health Workers. Access to health care by persons with functional difficulty is quite low (Lowest at 20.2% in Greater Pibor Administrative Area). Barriers and facilitators assessments in the health sector found that providers attitudes varied according to the type of disability; persons with physical impairments faced less discrimination than persons with hearing or intellectual impairments. Attacks on health care have continued into 2022 with health workers being shot at and killed in some instances, health facilities looted or destroyed amid population displacement.Quality of health services provided varies widely across the country. An analysis of service delivery bottlenecks identified only about 39.8% of approved midwifery positions in supported health facilities as filled based on maximum staffing norms, while 14.8% of mother of children 0-11 months received at least 4 antenatal care (ANC) visits during the last pregnancy. Stock outs of essential supplies including pharmaceutical supplies, infection prevention and control supplies, and immunization supplies affect the continuum of care and basic services provision. COVID-19 and logistics challenges due to floods have impacted supply side dynamics. Uptake of Family Planning services remains quite low despite high teenage pregnancy rates (30% among girls 15-19 years). Contraceptive prevalence rate is low at 5% for modern methods. These contributes to the high maternal mortality rate of 789 per 100,000 live births. COVID-19 prevention and response need to be mainstreamed across health services delivery, with COVID-19 adaptations to safe delivery of health services prioritized. Sudan Virus Disease (SVD) outbreak in neighboring Uganda poses an additional threat of transmission into South Sudan across priority Counties.

2 - Project activities

The proposed project seeks to improve the health status of vulnerable populations in need across 6 Counties in South Sudan (Aweil East, Rumbek East, Koch, Mayendit, Panyijar and Rubkona Counties), targeting 212573 persons(84540 Men, 91683 Women, 19727 Boys and 16623 Girls. To improve equitable access to lifesaving health care services the project will support 2 (PHCC's) each in Aweil East, Rubkona, Panyijar, Koch and a PHCC each in Rumbek East and Mayendit. Additionally, 2 Rapid Response Mobile (RRM) Teams each will be supported in Panyijar, Mayendit, Koch and 1 RRM in Aweil East Counties. The project will also seek to improve the Boma Health initiative (BHI) coverage by 480 Boma Health Workers (BHWs), 80 BHWs each per County) to provide community level health care services.The IRC will recruit qualified health care providers in the PHCCs and RRM sites, providing 24-hour services. The facilities will provide both preventive and curative health services for communicable diseases linked with community level demand creation services through tailored health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion messaging. BHWs will be selected from community members (preferably women) and trained on screening and diagnoses for common illnesses affecting children under 5 years (malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhoea). They will be trained to screen for malnutrition, test for malaria using rapid tests, and refer severe cases for case management at the nearest health facilities. The BHWs will also be trained on contraception counselling to women at household level and strengthening referrals for Long-acting methods to health facilities. Providers will have access to capacity strengthening on mental health GAP action program (mhGAP), clinical management of rape (CMR) and Integrated management of newborn & childhood illnesses (IMNCI). Pharmaceuticals and medical commodities provision, and leveraging on pipelines for Immunization supplies, contraceptives, and other supplies will ensure that facilities are fully equipped to provide quality health services. Treatment of Non communicable diseases, mental health and trauma clients will be provided while strengthening referral linkages to existing secondary care facilities. Facilities will be supported to provide all Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (BEmONC) signal functions to ensure safe motherhood services, postnatal care are availed. Post abortion care services will be provided at supported health facilities, and by referral strengthening from community and outreach sites.The project will actively seek opportunities for multi-sector integrated approaches to strengthen linkages and promote synergies particularly for nutrition, immunization, food security and livelihoods, GBV and child protection.The project will ensure timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases through a strengthened community system, coordination with local authorities, boma health committees and health facility in charges. Samples will be collected and sent through the WHO leads to the national laboratory for timely diagnosis. Response activities will be coordinated, looping in actors (national and subnational) to ensure zero duplication of efforts.The project will enhance resilience and promote humanitarian development linkages through engaging communities as partners in the delivery of health care. Community members will be selected to join the facility health committees to strengthen accountability. Technical and operational oversight through monthly support supervision visits to health facilities will be conducted jointly by IRC and CHD teams to ensure quality health services provision. Coordination through regular community meetings will be inbuilt into the project to actively seek community feedback for timely intervention and learning.

3 - Project indicators

Success will be measured using the IRC’s IMPACT standards which identifies a set of monitoring actions across the project life cycle. The project will dedicate funds to recruit monitoring and evaluation staff that will be charged with setting up project M&E systems and check points to track project implementation progress and to document learning. The project will be implemented in line with the overall strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Needs Overview and health cluster strategic objectives to ensure that activities align with the overall prioritized needs of affected populations. The indicators tracked will be in line with the Health Cluster priorities and will be disaggregated as much as possible by age, sex and disability. This will further inform access and utilization of health services by vulnerable groups in the community. The project will report on indicators that contribute towards the following broad objectives; 1) Improve equitable access to lifesaving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, 2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and 3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Data will be collected at the health facilities and service delivery points and reported on a regular basis to the County Health Department through the District Health information Software (DHIS 2.0). This will support county level planning and data driven decision making for the health sector. Community level data will be collected at the community and summary sheets shared with the nearest health facility for submission to the CHD for entry into DHIS 2.0. Weekly information on disease surveillance and reporting will be collected from the health facilities for notifiable illnesses and submitted through the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Reporting System (IDSR) or Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS) for all health facilities in the project. Regular data review meetings will be held at the service delivery and field office levels where project staff and partners will review data on all indicators and use the data to make critical decisions about how to best deliver on the project outcomes and make necessary adjustments. Project cycle meetings will be held on a monthly basis to discuss performance across service delivery sites, share experiences and learnings and make high-level evidence-based decisions geared towards strengthening the project delivery mechanisms and success pathways. IRC will build in a client responsiveness framework into all projects across all stages of the project lifecycle to ensure that our projects respond to the needs of clients and actively input these into project progress reviews through an iterative approach to support continuous improvements.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IRC will collect, analyze and use feedback from beneficiaries regardless of their age, nationality, gender, residency status, education status, disability status and other diversity factors. IRC will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to disaggregate feedback and complaints by disability status. The IRC’s feedback channels will disaggregate feedback and complaints by gender, age, nationality, residency status and disability status to enable us to understand the needs and perspectives of different clients, including the most vulnerable members in the communities we serve. IRC will pay special attention to the most vulnerable and gather their feedback through key informant interviews, focus group discussions and community meetings. The community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements include persons with special needs to ensure their needs are into account.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project beneficiaries and stakeholders, including the Boma Health Committees, Women groups, Youth Groups and Organization of Persons with Disability will be informed on the scope of the project, timelines, key decision gates and community accountability mechanisms and systems during project opening meetings, project review and coordination meetings, stakeholder and community forums, information boards and using IEC materials. IRC has established community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements who will inform community members on the available client feedback and complaints mechanisms that they can use to provide feedback about project assistance. The community-led feedback management committees will also follow up with IRC on actions taken on the feedback and complaints provided to ensure that the feedback and complaints received from clients are included in adaptive program management. As a client-centered organization, the IRC has a well-structured client responsiveness and accountability system in place where clients’ complaints and feedbacks are collected, analyzed, and used to inform program decision making. We will use both the proactive and reactive feedback channels to solicit client feedback that will guide project implementation and strategies for improvement. This feedback will be used to inform decision-making and adapt interventions to achieve better outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priority needs and preferences.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Client safeguarding leads across IRC sites ensure the dissemination of information on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to all IRC staff monthly. All new project staff will be onboarded and trained on safeguarding, including PSEA. The trained project staff will be required to support the roll out of PSEA and safeguarding information to the clients. Through the project, the IRC guidelines for survivor centered approach to reporting safeguarding and misconduct will be used at service delivery points. Clients will be widely informed of their rights, safeguarding measures and avenues for “raising concern” through confidential IRC channels that are managed by independent vendors. The client feedback mechanisms and systems set up across service points and at the community will serve as an avenue for clients to raise concerns around safeguarding, and PSEA. Monthly meetings conducted with key community stakeholders will be used to create awareness on safeguarding and PSEA, in local languages and accord clients an opportunity for concerns around PSEA to be voices with all key stakeholders in the delivery of health services.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Experience and research has shown that if beneficiaries do not know what has become of their feedback, then they will lose trust in the feedback process. At the IRC, we respond to beneficiaries’ feedback by asking them how we should respond, selecting the appropriate channels, identifying the timing for the response, planning and allocating resources, recording and keeping track of client feedback, and monitoring and reviewing our feedback mechanisms. Complaints and feedback will be analyzed and presented during weekly field site meetings and monthly project cycle meetings where practical actions to address them will put in place with clear timelines and responsibility allocated. These actions will then be followed up in subsequent meetings to ensure they are implemented and to measure the results. Key actions to adapt project assistance will include, but not limited to, making programmatic changes on assistance delivery, changing policy, provision of goods and services, providing information on available assistance, providing linkages with other projects, providing external referrals and including the needs of clients in project funding proposals.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will work jointly with local community leaders, CHD officers and the Boma health committees to identify service delivery points based on needs and priorities. Site selection will ensure client safety and map out safety corridors to minimize the risks of clients, particularly women and girls, being exposed to violence as they seek health services. The clinic hours will be from sunup to late in the evening to accord clients access to services at timelines that ensure maximum safety. The project will work with protection actors to mainstream referral pathways including access to justice and rule of law for any perpetrators of violence. Coordination with health cluster, health actors and protection actors will mitigate any potential risks of exposure to violence in project locations. Local authorities will be engaged in joint planning of RRM movement plans into the hard-to-reach locations to provide security guarantees while the project staff will be provided with IRC and donor visibility, facilitated transportation and communication devices to always ensure access to their locations. Evacuation and conflict sensitivity standard operations procedures will be in use and updated to reflect the changing contexts.The project teams will work closely with protection actors to map out existing referral pathways for clients in need of protection services. These pathways will be widely shared with the community, local leaders, and health providers. The health facilities will be supported to provide CMR services by availing trained providers and equipment for service provision. Linkages to psychosocial support (PSS) and legal services will be provided through the referral systems. In locations with potential explosive hazards, the IRC will leverage existing pathways for referrals to health providers capable of providing secondary health care services including trauma management and care through the County Health Departments. The project will rehabilitate health facility service points to include ramps to facilitate easy access. Triage of clients will include use of the Washington Short set of questions on disability to ensure that clients with disability have access to services and are adequately supported by providers to benefit from the health services. Affirmative action in selection of community members to attend community leadership meetings, facility committee meetings and other fora will ensure that women, Persons with disabilities are given an opportunity to participate and even lead meetings. Complaints desks and other community feedback mechanisms channels will be placed in easily accessible locations to allow access for all community members. Local authority interference will result in skewed allocation of project resources and ultimately affect best use of resource consideration. The project will work with all stakeholders from the design to project close out to ensure that they have a clear understanding and contribution to the development and success of the project. This will mitigate the challenges and ensure peaceful co-existence of communities benefiting from the project. The project will hold community level dialogues at startup to collect the views of all community members with wide representations of vulnerable groups in a transparent manner to offset any personal interests that may seek to subvert the project goals.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will support health facilities in highland areas inhabited by displaced populations during the rainy season. This will ensure continued services delivery for flood affected populations and limit disruptions to care for clients on continued follow up for health services. Medical and pharmaceutical suplies will be pre-positioned in higher grounds before the onset of the rainy season to ensure continued services provision even when access roads are completely cut off. The Community health services delivery through the Boma Health initiative will ensure continued provision of health services in affected locations by Boma Health Workers selected from community membership and residing within the community. This will significantly contribute to reduction of under 5 years old morbidity and mortality.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project locations across the 8 Counties will have health providers trained and capable of providing Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services thus closing the continuum of care for GBV survivors. These clients will be supported to receive clinical management within 72 hours of the violence perpetrated against them and linked with appropriate referral pathways for further management.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

By improving the overall health status of populations in the project location this will improve the demographic dividend and the years lived with disability for affected populations. This in turn is expected to contribute to productivity of affected populations in economic recovery efforts in the community.
