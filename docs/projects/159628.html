Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2018 – 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan strategic objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”. It is also in line with the Health Cluster second strategic objective: “Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building”; and WASH Cluster first strategic objective: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and suﬃcient WASH services with dignity for the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank”. This proposition is based on ACTED assessments, extensive consultations with the Ministry of Health in Hebron and other key stakeholders including OCHA’s Head of Office in South and the partner HWC (Health Work Committees), a Palestinian non-profit organization with extensive experience in providing health services in H2, and follows the recommendations of both Health and WASH clusters. The project will aim to ensure the quality of WASH facilities within health centers in the old city of Hebron, H2 through WASH facilities rehabilitation and WASH and Protection training of staff. It will also aim to provide health services for people in marginalized communities of Hebron H2 and raise awareness on hygiene practices among women. The needs assessed in terms of lack of health services in H2 community have been already identified in 2019 and confirmed as still valid and unmet in 2020 by the Health Work Committees. New needs, which have been identified and assessed by ACTED between August and September 2019, have seriously emerged in terms of rehabilitation of WASH facilities inside the target health centers in H2 area in Hebron.

1 - Needs

H2 area of Hebron, with a population of over 33,000 Palestinians under Israeli control, is one the most affected area in the West Bank by settlement activities and access restrictions. Palestinians living in H2 live in highly stressed environment, facing multiple challenges in terms of restriction of access to services and violence by Israeli forces and settlers. Among the most pressing problems, there is the access to safe health facilities. OCHA assessment in H2 (2019) showed that only 36% of HH members receive regular medical rehabilitation treatment. People with disabilities or elderly are particularly impacted with restricted access transportation to medical centers. In September 2019, ACTED, in coordination with the Ministry of Health, conducted visits to 6 clinics inside the H2 area. By adopting the indicators assessment of the WASH FIT Tool created by WHO and UNICEF, ACTED conducted a preliminary assessment in 4 health centers in order to assess their WASH conditions. The assessment demonstrated severe emergency needs in terms of sanitation inside the following clinics: - Al-Karantina clinic; the main health center serving H2 residents, with the highest number of daily patients, 10 toilets are in urgent needs of full rehabilitation, including the sanitation network. The water cistern is in critical condition, and need full rehabilitation, as it is the main water source due to shortage of water. Also, 6 other water tanks need to be provided; - Al-Haram center; 3 toilet units are in need full rehabilitation, including the sanitation network; - Tal Rmida clinic; there is a need to solve the issue of the moisture which makes rots on the walls exposing staff and patients to health risks and needs in rehabilitation of the sewage network; - Masharqa clinic; 2 toilets units need full rehabilitation. In the centers, facilities are not adapted for PWDs and hygiene promotion materials are not visible and understandable at key places. ACTED will coordinate with the MoH to gather and analyze gender and age disaggregated data of the patients of the target clinics. Partners recognized that there are still unmet needs in terms of hygiene and health promotion in H2. The promotion of safe and inclusive access to essential services and practices, especially for women, is of vital importance in H2 area. According to the needs assessment published by OCHA in April 2019, 81% of Palestinian living within H2 cannot access their house without going through a checkpoint, and 89% of them cannot access them with a vehicle, 69% have experienced settlers’ violence since October 2015, which consists mainly in intimidation and threats (79%), physical assault (48%) and stoning (33%). As a consequence, health services are hard to reach for many Palestinians within H2. The movement restriction and continuous harassment and violence is discouraging people to go for health centers and doctors. The first concerns regarding access to health care is crossing checkpoints (72%), cost of health services (52%), cost of transportation (41%) and settlers harassment on roads (30%). In the multi-cluster H2 HH survey (2018), 6% of the interviewed HHs (280) have family members with disabilities (5.1% according to PCBS) and 36% of them have disable family members who need to go health centers on a regular basis. An estimated average of 1,500 girls and 1,500 boys in Area C, H2 and Hebron, are acutely vulnerable and are at risk of life-threatening conditions and developmental delays and disabilities. Women accessibility to essential health services is a critical challenge in H2 hard-to-reach areas. Women in Palestine are still being exposed to GBV and 56% of women of the OCHA sample of H2 do not know where to receive GBV related services; 37% of married women have exposed to one or more forms of GBV. Of those, 58.6% have been exposed to emotional violence, 55.1% to financial violence, 54.8% to social violence, 23.5% to physical violence and 11.8% to sexual violence.

2 - Activities

Activity1-Technical assessment. Building on preliminary BoQs, produced by MoH engineers and ACTED supervisor, a technical assessment will be conducted at the first stage of the project in collaboration with the MoH to develop a detailed plan of rehabilitation. The clinics were selected jointly with the local authorities based on the preliminary assessment. Protection Officer will ensure that specific needs of vulnerable categories will be taken in account (PWD, women, children and elder people). Activity 2-Rehabilitation of WASH facilities and PWD adaption. ACTED will rehabilitate sanitation facilities in 4 Health Centers to ensure they are functional and in line with global and SPHERE standards. While the nature of the rehabilitation works will be determined on a case-by-case basis, based on the BoQs, rehabilitation is expected to include: the supply and rehabilitation of toilets and lockers to ensure privacy and reduce protection risks (separate sanitation units for staff and patients, female and male), adaptation measures for PWD and elderly people (adequate access to toilets, entrances and corridors leading to the toilets; provision of assistive devices for PWDs' improved access), hand-washing stations. ACTED will perform minor non-structural rehabilitation when needed, such as sealing or repairing holes and cracks in walls, maintenance of roofs, ceilings and windows, electricity network. By addressing the WASH needs of key health institutions, this intervention contributes to improve the environment in which such clinics operate, thus resulting in a positive impact for the community. The rehabilitation works will be conducted by contractors selected through ACTED’s standard competitive tender. Activity3-Capacity-building of health centers workers. A total of 12 health workers (6 women and 6 men) of the 4 clinics will benefit from a 2-days training in WASH and Protection. The training will ensure that relevant staff is aware of protection concerns and well equipped to maintain the WASH facilities, ensure that men, women, boys, girls, elder and PWDs have equitable access to water and sanitation facilities, facilities are well operated and maintained, and hygiene good practices messages are properly delivered. Activity4-NFI/hygiene kits distribution to 200 vulnerable HHs.HHs will be selected during the awareness-raising sessions, targeting criteria will be based on differentiated needs of all HHs members (HWC methodology used in previous projects in H2). In order to maximize the impact at community level, a specific focus will be given to hygiene promotion. ACTED and HWC will distribute hygiene kits to ensure safe and correct hygiene practices at the hh and personal level. A quick and transparent procurement process will be launched by ACTED to purchase the kits, which will include long-term and consumable items appropriate in terms of gender, age and disability to promote dignity, respect and safety of all HH members. Activity5-Hygiene/health awareness sessions for women. HWC will conduct 200 hygiene/health awareness sessions to raise awareness about healthy practices and healthy lifestyles (5 sessions/week for 40 weeks). The sessions will include, amongst other subjects, health-related risks, GBV, SHR, chronic diseases, sanitation and hygienic facilities, bathing and personal hygiene. Around 12 women will participate to each session. The sessions will include group and individual sessions and counselling tackling GBV issues. Activity 6-Open medical days. HWC will conduct 2 open medical days to provide health and chronic disease services to most marginalized neighborhoods of the H2 area in Hebron. The open days will be conducted in prohibited and restricted areas where accessibility as well as availability is minimal and residents in these areas (6,920 individuals) have very diﬃcult times trying to access health services. Each session should target approximately 50 people, with a minimum ratio of 70% of women.

3 - Indicators

In addition to the standard indicators requested by the clusters, ACTED will also monitor the following indicators: % PwD (disaggregated by sex and age) reporting less concerns about accessibility to clinics after the intervention; % of PWDs (disaggregated by sex and age) reporting an improved feeling of safety and dignity at clinics WASH facilities by the end of the project; % of PWDs (disaggregated by sex and age) reporting that delivered humanitarian assistance is approppriate and satisfactory. The target of these indicators will be 85%. As means of verification ACTED will use post-intervention monitoring activity and satisfaction survey. Further information will be collected through sessions where beneficiaries (disable men, women, boys, girls and elder) testimonies will be captured in a concise report on how and to what extent the intervention improved their accessibility to clinics and their feeling of safety and dignity at clinics WaSH facilities. The following indicators will be also measured: # of hygiene and health awareness sessions (group and individual) conducted to raise awareness about healthy practices and healthy lifestyles (Target: 200; Means of verification: activities report, On-Site Training Observation Sheets, attendance sheets); % of women and girls benefited by the open medical days (Target: 70%; Means of Verification: activities report and records, On-Site Observation Sheets); % of health workers who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the capacity-building training (Target: 90, Means of Verification: Pre-Training Assessment (PTA), Post Training Evaluation (PTE)); % of HHs (disaggregated by sex and age) reporting that the hygiene kit is appropriate and satisfying (Target: 80, Means of verification: satisfaction surveys with a representative sample of the HHs received hygiene kits): 100 individuals (30 men, 70 women) benefiting from the services provided by the open medical days in the marginalized areas. Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant and disaggregated data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. AME activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed sex and age disaggregated data on beneﬁciaries and to measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (SADD). Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. ACTED Protection Officer will ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level to guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety, dignity and privacy of each beneficiary. Through ACTED’s Protection Officer, ACTED’s AME will also oversee and ensure transparency in the confidential complaint response mechanism, duly reported to ACTED management when relevant, and then properly addressed.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ensured through ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including oPT. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. All beneﬁciaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in conﬁdentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will provided with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, ﬁeld paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms features several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by ACTED Protection Oﬃcer to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries and, when preferred, conﬁdentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of ACTED projects. Beneficiaries’ satisfaction is continuously monitored by ACTED’s AME Unit, that regularly visits and interviews beneficiaries regarding ongoing activities. The AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

During this project implementation, ACTED will make every effort to ensure safe and secure environment for the targeted beneﬁciaries. The do-no-harm approach will be monitored by ACTED Protection Oﬃcer that will be in charge of providing the most secure environment for all stakeholders involved in the project. A particular focus will be laid on vulnerable communities, as a serious attention will be given to gender-based violence issues and people with disabilities' protection. In order to avoid exposing people to threatening and dangerous situations, ACTED and HWC will pay a strong attention to providing the aid in the right place and time. H2 in Hebron is a stressful and oppressive place for the Palestinian community. The Humanitarian Situation in the Area H2 of Hebron City ﬁndings assessment displayed that soldiers’ and settlers’ harassment, along with physical assaults and stoning are the most frequent types of violence that Palestinian have to face on a daily basis. To ensure the safe access of our activities for beneﬁciaries, ACTED and HWC will manage an access to the hard-to-reach areas. As a consequence, people won’t have to endure long travels, several checkpoints, or long exposure to get the health services provided by this project. ACTED and HWV will meet relevant authorities and coordinate to facilitate movement of materials and staff in communities subjected to closure. In order to facilitate access and movement of humanitarian workers and materials, the security situation is monitored on an ongoing basis by partners, in close coordination with AIDA and UNDSS. ACTED has provided ample incident, programming-based and policy evidence to expose these practices; and will continue to do so under this action. It has also acquired considerable experience working in this environment and mainstreamed risk analyses and mitigation measures in its standard operating procedures. Beneficiaries are systematically informed about the risk entailed by the planned activities and available recourse should problems arise. Their consent is, likewise, systematically solicited. This proactive approach to risk mitigation will ensure that beneficiaries are able to access assistance. HWC will also manage to deliver health service at home for disabled people, elders, or severely vulnerable people who don’t want to be threatened by the settlers or the military. The WASH rehabilitation activities will also integrate a protection approach. Apart from technical rehabilitation, ACTED will ensure that locks, hard walls, light, separated toilets and PWD adaption measures will be included in the BoQ. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ﬁrstly ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including Palestine. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of the activities. The beneﬁciaries will be informed on CRM, and encouraged to report any issues or concerns. Several channels will be available to beneﬁciaries to ﬁle a complaint or raises concerns: through ACTED dedicated phone line open 24/7 (incl. via SMS/WhatsApp), dedicated email address, complaints box (within ACTED oﬃces), AME monitoring visits. All beneﬁciaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in conﬁdentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will be provided with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. ACTED Protection Oﬃcer will ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component in the design, implementation and monitoring or ACTED projects. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED internal security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

ACTED and HWC will ensure that the project will take into consideration all vulnerable groups in this framework. During all the phases of the project cycle management, ACTED will gather, analyze and report sex and gender disaggregated data for all the implemented activities. ACTED and HWC also ensure the impartial access to aid through the needs-based approach of this project which was built out of different assessments (internal and external). Firstly, as the WASH facilities rehabilitation will assure to offer good quality services to everyone, they will also provide separate toilets for staff and patients of the clinics and for men and women. Training of the health centers' staff will ensure access to and quality of WASH facilities, thus mitiganting risks such a poor maintainaince or repair, inadequate hygiene practices, unsufficient privacy and unsafe use of the facilities. All the measures related to increased access of people with disabilities to WASH facilities inside the clinics (separate toilets, access to doors, corridors and entrances, height of toilets seats and washing taps, support bars etc) will be prioritized in the rehabilation works. Furthermore, as ones of the main constraints for the Palestinian community in Hebron H2 to access health services are movements limitation (crossing checkpoints – 72%) and cost of health services (52%), according to OCHA’s 2019 assessment in H2, ACTED and HWC will propose health activities accessible for all people in need in areas where the access issues are the most important. Providing these services in hard-to-reach areas will allow disabled people, elders, and every member of the Palestinian community to access the site without/with few exposure or checkpoint crossing. Finally, the project will give a particular focus on women, as the health and hygiene awareness raising sessions (target: 2,400) will be dedicated to women, in order to provide them services, advice, guidance and sensitization on topics rarely addressed to women. As women have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children, inadequate facilities and practices for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanism. Targeted and invidual sessions will be conducted with women and girls on GBV, SHR and chronic diseases. A case management and referral mechanism will be implemented for the hardship cases which will be referred to other organizations working in the area. Individual support, covering individual sessions, counseling, legal and medical aid, willl be ensured. ACTED and HWC will ensure accessibility for PWDs to the awareness sessions and the open medical days by choosing appropriate locations and addressing whatever barrier prevents access to the facilities. Strong coordination with local stakeholders, other humanitarian actors working in the area and local auhtorithies will strenghten the protective environment and enable to address immediate protection concerns. The open medical days will pay a strong attention on gender, as a minimum of 70% of attendants will be women (total target: 100). All the households members will be consulted regarding their preferred type of items to be included in the kits. The kits will be composed based on the household size and compotision, taking into account the needs of children, boys, girls, elder people and people with disabilities.
