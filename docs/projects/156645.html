Project Summary

The project aims to increase safe and equitable access to formal and non formal education for crisis affected children and youth (aged 5 17 years) and improve the quality of formal and non formal education for children and youth (aged 5 17 years) within a protective environment. The project will be implemented through ADRA Syria education team, closely in coordination MoE and related DoEs ensuring the protection of all beneficiaries through a ‘do not harm’ approach. ADRA Syria through this project will improve access to formal and non-formal education and learning opportunities for girls and boys by creating safe and protective environments for 15,500 children who have been affected by the crisis. This will be done through education interventions within the governorates of Rural Damascus, Quneitra, Homs, Aleppo, Dara’a and Deir Alzour. ADRA Syria through the project will enhance the formal and non – formal education level for the children, boys and girls, in the same rehabilitated schools. This will include a holistic program with psycho-social, familial and educational support, encouraging the engagement of mothers and fathers in educational activities.
ADRA will coordinate with key stakeholders to ensure the most vulnerable boys and girls are identified in the target locations, and ADRA will conduct comprehensive needs assessments in the areas identified to locate OOSC and students with priority educational needs.

Needs

The protracted nature of the Syrian crisis has had a devastating effect upon the educational psychosocial wellbeing of boys and girls across Syria. Estimates state that over 2.1 million boys, girls and adults remain out of school in Syria for reasons which include lack of access to educational facilities, child labour, and early marriage, especially for young and adolescent girls. In addition, approximately 1.3 million children (with approximately equal numbers of boys and girls) are at risk of dropping out, and 5.85 million school-aged children and about 245,000 education personnel female and male (mostly adult and older adult women) are in need of education assistance inside Syria. The provision of education services inside Syria is limited. (HNO 2019). In ADRA’s recent educational assessment in eastern Aleppo city (Merjeh, Salheen and Sakhour), more than 1,000 OOSC were identified (approximately 500 boys and 500 girls), with estimates suggesting the real figure is much higher. The OOSC range in age from 6 to17 years old, including married adolescents with up to 6 children. All have been out of school from between 2 to 9 years, 75% have never been to school, resulting in approximately 85% of the children being assessed as illiterate. Beneficiary feedback identified the main reasons for being out of school as the commitment to family responsibilities, the lack of support for female education, a lack of documentation and severe poverty. The complex educational needs of OOSC are compounded by high infrastructural damage, widespread psychological trauma and malnutrition amongst school-aged children. In December 2018, ADRA also identified high numbers of OOSC in Western Ghouta in areas of high IDP population such as Sahnaya (population: 750,000 individuals) and Jdeidat Artooz (population: 60,000 individuals), which are hosting communities with IDPs composing up to 60% of the population, and up to 35% (7,000 children) of the children are out of school due to lack of educational facilities and poverty. The infrastructural damage in conflict-affected areas also results in inadequate and overcrowded education facilities with the average number of students per classroom reaching 60 (Ghasm, Dara’a) or 80 (Salheen, eastern Aleppo city), and children sitting on the floor in classrooms without doors and windows. In other areas where there are no functional schools such as in Aftaress, East Ghouta, or where there is a shortage of schools such as Zabadani (Rural Damascus), students walk 2-4 kms per day along dangerous roads to attend alternative facilities if they attend at all. ADRA also identified high needs of PwDs in educational facilities, which are often ill-equipped to accommodate students as well as teachers with mobility challenges. While many PwDs are unable to attend at all, those who do must be accompanied by a parent to access WASH facilities. With the rise in the number of children with disabilities in governorates such as Quneitra and Dara’a due to UXO-related injuries, there is an increased need for disability awareness both in the communities and amongst teachers. Further to the conflict-related physical impairments, ADRA field staff estimate that 90% of children attending school require additional PSS and educational support.

Implementation of activities

Rehabilitation of damaged schools o This intervention will ensure equal access for boys and girls, including gender segregated WASH facilities, and children with disabilities, including accessibility to classrooms and WASH facilities. The intervention will include repair of doors, windows, open spaces, painting, electrical systems, access ramps, hand rails and the installation of western style toilets to facilitate access to WASH facilities for those with mobility issues. • Provision of non-formal education programs, such as catch up classes, remedial classes, literacy and numeracy classes and summer programs to at risk boys and girls• Provision of Self Learning Program (SLP) for out of school young and adolescent boys and girls, including children with disabilities • Provision of non formal vocational education opportunities for male and female adolescents• Provision of learning materials• Provision of school kits• Capacity building for male and female teachers and education personnel on topics including active learning, classroom management and psychological support ADRA Syria has good experience in teacher capacity building where it managed to conduct around 30 training sessions for both female and male teachers, benefiting over 900 teachers, during the past two years. Feedback from the training showed it had a good effect on renewing the passion and energy among the teachers in schools. In addition, the distribution of school kits, learning and recreational materials is fundamental to the education response in the suggested areas. Throughout all stages of the project, ADRA will receive feedback from potential and actual project beneficiaries including boys, girls, adolescent boys and girls as well as men and women and people with disabilities. ADRA will elicit feedback through multiple means, including direct communication with project beneficiaries, constant follow-up with key stakeholders and anonymously via the established ADRA Syria complaints mechanism. This feedback is used to plan project activities, undertake adjustments to ongoing activities, as well as help measure the impact of the activities.

Protection Risk Analysis

The selection of teachers and children to participate in activities or training and learning may also create tensions from those who were not selected, placing staff or beneficiaries at risk. ADRA will work closely with the MoE to select teachers from a variety of schools in areas of high need and according to certain criteria. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes).Distribution of learning material and school kits might create tension between host community members and IDPs, especially in that most of the targeted communities include a large number of IDPs which could place beneficiaries or staff at risk. Thus, ADRA Syria will create certain criteria for distribution, depending on the vulnerability level and need of the beneficiaries which will include both IDPs and host community beneficiaries. (Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes).Another risk could happen, beneficiaries placed at risk due to project interventions to mitigate these potential interventions will be discussed with key stakeholders and beneficiaries and follow education sector guidelines to ensure a standardized response across all humanitarian responders and that no harm will inadvertently come to project beneficiaries (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several potential risks have been identified that may impact the success of the project.One of the major risks identified is that of the changing security situation. To mitigate this risk, ADRA Syria will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. ADRA will also work closely with the MoE to choose locations which have been stable for some time and unlikely be directly impacted by renewed conflict. If needed, ADRA will negotiate with the donor to confirm the flexibility to change the project locations as required. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). On the other hand, as the proposed project is mainly community-based level, and targets mostly children, it is unlikely to create protection risks of project beneficiaries. However, these is a slight risk that by reopening schools, children will be exposed to protection concerns either on the way to and from schools. To mitigate this possibility, ADRA will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the children when attending school. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities and security approvals to access project areas in a timely manner from relevant authorities. To mitigate this ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the Ministry of Education and the Education sector in Damascus (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes).The lack and difficulty in securing supplies and materials, ADRA's experience in Syria in a similar field has resulted in the identification of potential contractors and staff and thus is unlikely to impact project activities. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).One of the risks could happen that local staff do not have the existing capacity or skills needed to implement the project. To mitigate this risk, ADRA Syria Staff will continue to receive relevant training, including in the area of humanitarian principals to ensure that they are qualified to undertake the tasks assigned to them (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).Also, another risk is that the procurement of supplies and materials could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest, to mitigate this ADRA Syria has developed and follows a clear procurement policy under which all purchases and contracting takes place. The policy includes a clear segregation of duties, levels of authorization, and conflict of interest declaration to help prevent fraud, corruption and conflict of interest. This process is followed, supervised by the Operations Manager and Country Director and documented at all stages of the process. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).
