Project Summary

The project will provide case management, S/GBV response, MHPSS and socioeconomic reintegration services to unaccompanied and separated children including S/GBV survivors and children formerly associated with armed groups and armed forces and their caregivers.
According to the HRP 2023, (Child Protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military offensive leading to the high influx of people from NSAG, inflation, extreme poverty rates and relocations of IDPs, etc. Priority needs include case management services for victims of abuse, neglect, and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labour, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities.)
The Northeast Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview HNO indicates also 2.1 million people will need child protection services in 2023 with 13 LGAs in Adamawa, 21 in Borno, and 14 in Yobe having the highest severity ranking of child protection needs due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety nets, causing 1.3 million children to need child protection services. The project will contribute to the HRP target of reaching 1.5m people in need (1,050,000 children, 53% girls and 450,000 caregivers, 53% women) by supporting boys, girls, adolescents, youths and caregivers to re-establish a sense of safety and normality through:
I.) Social Economic Reintegration for Children Associated with Armed Groups.
II.) Case management/GBV services.
III.) Mental Health and Psychosocial Support.

1 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO the protection needs in the North-East of Nigeria include case management services for victims of abuse, neglect, and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labor, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities.At the core of these needs is a prevalence of abuse, neglect and violence against children, with life threatening experiences which impact the immediate and long-term development of children. Most abuses are reported to be perpetrated by family members or relatives involved in the lives of children, further aggravating the psychological effects of the experience especially survivors of sexual violence.Mental health challenges and psychological distress among children and adults remain unattended, and on the rise due to prolonged exposure to economic hardship, stress, and traumatic experiences. Many children are living with this challenge in unsupportive family systems due to poor relationships and lack of skills by caregivers to manage the situation.

2 - Activities

The project will address the highlighted needs through the following activities which are aligned to the CP AoR strategic objectives 1 and 2:1. Children at risk (boys and girls) including adolescents, children with disabilities accesspreventive and comprehensive quality response services in a timely manner: 1.1. SC will provide social and economic reintegration services for children formerly associated with armed groups and their caregivers to promote safe reintegration into their communities. This will be facilitated through a community-based approach to reintegration where support for CAAFAG and adolescents within the community helps to reduce stigmatization and tensions by promoting equity in the delivery of assistance. Caregivers will be trained in business and financial literacy and subsequently empowered with income generating activity IGA start-up kits. Adolescent children/youths will be enrolled in skill acquisition programs of choice to acquire marketable handy skills. 1.2. SC will provide age-appropriate, comprehensive multi-sectoral services to survivors of SGBV, separated, unaccompanied and other at-risk children including CAAG through case management process based on selection criteria, and best interest assessments. SC will explore referral options complex cases with limited services options. Caseworkers will support GBV survivors and CAAFAGs by providing PSS services and dignity kits in addition to the case management process.1.3. SC will provide focussed support sessions in the community targeting the stigmatized (CAAG, SGBV, etc) and families. SC will also provide Psychoeducation for children and caregivers on psychosocial distress, using the SCI parenting without violence PWV common approach sessions.
