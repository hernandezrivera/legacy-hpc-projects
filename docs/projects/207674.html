Project Summary

The IRC’s proposed project will employ a twin track approach to ensure the protection of and response to child abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation. IRC’s proposed project will consult the clients, and ensure services to vulnerable children, adolescents, and their caregivers through inclusive response and prevention programs in camps -1E, 1W, 6, 8E, and 25 and host communities of Teknaf and Ukhia Upazila Rohingya refugee camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The IRC targets children who have experienced violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation and or those at risk. The project is aligned with IRC’s global strategy, the country strategic action plan (SAP) 2022-2024, and objective 4 of the 2024 JRP. IRC will ensure the project is delivered in accordance with sector objectives and enhance the capacity of partners as an active member of SAG, PSEA network, leads the CP-GBV case management technical working group. The project is premised on a social-ecological model to cover the different protective layers. Inclusive community-based child protection structures (50 F and 50 M) including persons with disabilities will be established and or strengthened, conduct community child protection risk mapping, and develop risk mitigation plans. Support systems strengthening through training and capacity-building schemes for government and other stakeholders. Mainstreams protection, gender, equality, diversity, and inclusion (GEDI) as it has a fully-fledged sector as well as PSEA, and safeguarding in every activity. In addition, mainstream EPR, and conduct multi-sector gender-sensitive assessments on protection risks associated with climate change. IRC has established AAP through the complaints and response feedback, child-friendly feedback mechanism, and inclusion of PWD is imperative in IRC’s project to ensure equal access to services. promote social cohesion and peaceful existence within the refugee community and between refugees and host communities. Provide technical and handholding support to the implementing partners to implement IRC’s flagship programs in camps and host communities. Establish Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) by engaging in age, gender-specific, and culturally-appropriate structured or non-structured psychosocial support (PSS) and life skill programs. Thus, children will cope with and heal from trauma; and participate in recreational activities to regain normalcy. Within the SHLS, responsive case management services will be provided to children who have experienced or are at risk of harm by well-trained and supervised caseworkers. IRC will work with the Department of Social Service (DSS), UNICEF and other protection actors in Ukhiya and Teknaf host community to strengthen case management activity, and support the child welfare board through functioning referral mechanism, collective support to reduce protection risk and vulnerability of children in collaborative way and without overlapping. IRC’s structured curriculum SAFE (Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies) for boys and girls aged 10 to 18 years to increase their safety and participation, strengthen family and peer support, and equip them with positive coping strategies, and support building resilience. Equip caregivers with positive parenting skills to improve social skills to be able to support a nurturing environment for children. L2L will target both boys and girls 10-18 years with civic engagement, particularly on issues related to leadership, social cohesion, advocacy, conflict resolution, and peacebuilding. awareness-raising activity is designed based on the child protection needs identified through CPIMS+ data, and regular child protection situation monitoring in camp and host community. Establish and strengthen children’s clubs where children from host communities and refugees get trained on their rights, receive PSS, participate in structured and non-structured PSS activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya, the world’s largest stateless population, have experienced decades of persecution, systematic discrimination, and targeted violence by successive Myanmar authorities. This has resulted in the mass displacement of over 965, 000 Rohingyas to Bangladesh. A 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (JMSNA) by the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) highlights protection concerns as a top priority in the refugee camps (48%), along with lack of cooking/sleeping space (30%), and low access to services (17%). The 2022 Child Protection Sector Assessment found high levels of child labour (64%), neglect (59%), child marriage (57%) and trafficking (32%). Among the host community, IRC found in 2023 that 63% of children had experienced natural disasters including cyclones (37%), floods (28%), and earthquakes (16%); resulting in psychological distress (24%), loss of education materials (21%), going hungry (19%), limited access to shelter (16%), separation from family (9%), physical injury (8%), and physical and/or sexual abuse (1%). Additionally, 48% of respondents reported that the Rohingya community is at protection risk of crime and violence according to MSNA 2023. This revealed that parents use negative coping mechanisms e.g., child labor and child marriage because of negative coping strategies. According to IRC’s Child Rights Situation Analysis, 2023 , host community, in Cox’s Bazar, 63% of the children had experienced natural disasters including cyclones (37%), floods (28%), and earthquakes (16%); resulting in psychological distress (24%), loss of education materials (21%), going hungry (19%), limited access to shelter (16%), separation from family (9%), physical injury (8%), and physical and/or sexual abuse (1%). According to the Child labor study, IRC, September 2022, 22% of boys and 5% of girls were involved in paid employment in the host community, whereas 15.6% of boys and 1.5% of girls worked in the Rohingya community. In both communities, limited family income is the primary reason for sending children to paid work. Moreover, in both communities, the prevalence of dropouts rises with age. Around 40% of host children (15 to 17 yrs.) and 70% of Rohingya of the same age group were not attending school or madrasa. Limited opportunities for adolescents especially to ensure meaningful skill development resulting to boys and girls engaging in negative coping mechanisms (criminal groups, substance abuse, etc). There is limited access to national protection systems including response (case management) and prevention services. The current level of interventions for out of school adolescents are inadequate to fully empower them with skills. Limited child participation, this is so because children are less visible, valued, and their voices not hard. According to Child rights situation analysis September 2023, 47.34% of the respondents reported not having the opportunity to share their views on issues which affect their lives. This category comprises 18.97% of boys and a higher percentage (28.37%) of girls. The findings underscore the importance of promoting inclusivity and active participation in decision-making processes, particularly for girls, who face greater challenges in accessing platforms to voice their opinions. According to the study a significant percentage (41.37%) of children encountered barrier of adults not deeming it necessary for children to express their opinions (16.9% are boys, while a higher percentage of 24.47% are girls, indicating a concerning gender disparity in the perception of the importance of children's voices); another significant obstacle is the perception of children being treated as immature to form opinions, impacting 25.88% of the respondents, 17.44% of the reported that many children lack the capacity to form opinions, and 15.31% revealed negative attitude towards children's participation.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The proposed project is aligned with rationalization principles including principle-3 adopt an integrated approach, principle-6 Ensure transparency of information, and principle-4 Ensure predictability and continuity. The IRC played a fundamental role in helping the sector shape the rationalization process, active involvement of SAG, CPSS finalized rationalization mapping having two agencies in one camp. Final rationalization mapping was shared with appealing partners and SAG. IRC child protection covered camps (1E, 1W, 5, 8E, 14 18, 22 and 25) and two host community locations in 2023. Following CPSS final rationalization mapping, IRC left camps 5, 14, 18, and 22 gradually following rationalization guidelines and continuing business in Camp 1E, 1W, 6, 8E & 25. During the rationalization period, IRC prioritized integrated protection (GP, CP, GBV) along with other sectors Health, SRH, DRR, and Education already are in place. IRC’s rationalization is to ensure service integration and reduce service gaps and costs with quality maintenance. IRC has good presence, acceptance, and coordination with the government, and CP area of responsibility (AOR) including UN, INGO, NNGO, and CBOs since 2017. The IRC will participate in and support the inter-sectoral camp-based coordination and camp-level coordination platforms and maintain good relations with the Government (in particular, with CiCs). Coordination and collaboration will be highly maintained between CP and other service providers. IRC will build the capacity of implementing partners including women-led organizations/local organizations to improve their capacity in delivering services to refugees. Following the fund-raising strategy, IRC will work to ensure the stability of program operation. IRC has operated its program in the same camps for multiple years and this strategy will be followed in 2024 and onwards.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The proposed project will ensure protection mainstreaming by incorporating protection core principles prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm, meaningful access, accountability, participation, and empowerment for child protection interventions. The IRC will build the capacity of partners on protection mainstreaming issues to ensure clients’ basic rights, easy access to services for diverse clients, and ensure participation and functioning accountability mechanisms in workplaces and which will ensure staff's sensitivity to do no harm and to ensure community's participation to empower them to have control over their own life. The IRC will review each substantive activity to identify child protection risks so that its prevented, and or mitigated, enhance rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming that considers age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness throughout the project. ensure integration within the protection (CP, GP, and GBV) and with other sectors of IRC and partners. Particular attention should be dedicated to all mechanisms to protect refugees from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation by humanitarian workers. A continuous assessment on the project activities, services, plans, monitoring, and evaluation will be conducted with protection mainstreaming lenses to identify existing limitations and gaps of the program and effective plan to overcome identified limitations (Protection Mainstreaming Action Plan- PMAP). Regular review on the Action Plan will help the program to identify the progress, changes, new opportunities, possibilities of intentional and unintentional harm etc. and take necessary actions to minimize the negative impact of the program, maximize positive impact and mitigate the limitations and gaps including possibility of causing harm through program cycle and activities.

3.3 - Age & Disability

IRC will mainstream age and disability issues throughout the project cycle and diverse people including PWD, will be engaged throughout the project. IRC has a strong presence in PWG and ADWG, and good relations with other specialized organizations. Through the project, a service referral is to ensure at a sector, working group, partnership, and camp level. All the tools and formats of IRC to be reviewed through the gender-diverse lens and ensure that considerations relating to diversity and inclusion are appropriately included, and workers are to be trained on gender, equality, diversity, and inclusion (GEDI). The protection mainstreaming will help to identify the risks for the diverse population, and service delivery to ensure according to the diverse population. The integrated programming approach will contribute to expediting cross-referrals and will allow for assistance to be tailored to the individual needs of beneficiaries.The IRC will analyze the likely risks and needs of the clients in terms of age and disability. A risk matrix with strategies to prevent, minimize, and or mitigate the consequences of the risks will be developed. The services are designed to address specific needs persons above 40 are supported with multiple diagnoses to have a comprehensive assessment, children aged 0 to 5 are taken care of with the highest level of sensitivity, and adolescent boys and girls are provided with separate content and services being aware of the issue of cultural sensitivity. All the interventions for children and adolescents will be contextualized and customized considering the different age groups of children. Also, children and adults with disabilities will be considered within the project activities. Diverse groups will be considered whether it is recruitment of staff or selection of project staff and volunteers.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IRC has a well-set up feedback mechanism channel including hotline, helpdesk, suggestion box, community feedback mechanism to ensure accountability to the affected population. IRC will ensure that the feedback and accountability mechanisms through child friendly complaints tools, feedback mechanism and follow CPSS AAP guideline. CP will promote the Complaints and feedback mechanism among children, parents, caregivers, and community people through establishing and integrating feedback mechanism in all program activity considering the Core Humanitarian Standards and organizational policy. IRC will review partner CFRM policy, build the capacity of partners on accountability mechanisms, PSEA; child safeguarding, and strengthen child participation techniques at different levels of the project stage. IRC will continue group discussions with the beneficiaries, protection monitoring, visit door to door to collect feedback and complaints from the community, and respond to mitigate child protection concerns. IRC developed child-friendly feedback tools and techniques to ensure all ages and gender-diverse children’s accountability and informed community people about their rights to protect, service delivery, information sharing, and feedback mechanisms channel to improve service quality.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The IRC is committed to mainstreaming Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within the proposed project by establishing a comprehensive framework guided by IRC’s global guideline, policy, the Core Humanitarian Standards, PSEAH Index, and the SoP for SEA complaint Referral in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh. IRC has dedicated PSEA Focal Points and will work closely with the network. IRC will strengthen the capacity of frontline workers to address PSEA and will ensure that the relevant staff will be trained on confidentiality policies and “do no harm” approach. IRC and its partner will undertake communication and awareness-raising efforts to ensure that safeguarding and PSEA policies are well-understood by all staff, incentive workers, volunteers, and beneficiaries. IRC will make sure that the awareness materials including key PSEA messages, and the available complaint feedback mechanisms are visible to beneficiaries. IRC deal Child safeguarding with highest organizational priority, this falls under mandatory reporting protocol. Both preventive and response mechanism are in place to ensures safeguarding awareness (robust sessions with staff/incentive worker who directly contact with clients on regular basis), reporting in-terms of clear flowchart and anonymous reporting process in every facility where IRC operate, response includes immediate support to the survivor including functioning referral pathways. Child safeguarding risks are regularly assessed, and mitigation strategies are designed throughout the project cycle management. IRC promotes child safety and security while deal with PSEA issue and main child protection principle. The proposed project will also ensure that information and reporting channels are safe and accessible for all beneficiaries and staff regardless of gender, age, or other protected characteristics and will ensure high-quality investigations adhering to core principles.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In line with the Child Protection sub-sector, Disaster Risk Management (DRM) will be integrated into Child Protection Intervention and all its operations, specifically addressing the vulnerabilities and protection risks for children during the pre-, during, and post-disaster periods. The Community-based child protection focal points and volunteers will receive basic refresher training on child protection in emergencies, disaster preparedness and response, rapid child protection assessment, and urgent response, and PFA and participate in the simulation drills on multi-hazard. The community-led disaster preparedness and response plan will be developed based on community risk assessment for child protection prevention and response, including identification of focal points for, inter alia, temporary alternative care, psychosocial first aid (PFA), prevention and mitigation of violence, neighborhood watch mechanisms to mitigate risks of trafficking, kidnapping, and/or abduction as well as dissemination of audio messaging related to child protection risks. In addition, community-led disaster risk communication with the community will be made considering seasonal hazards and risks of this context using IEC materials and IVR to mitigate the risk of family separation, preventing long-term family separation and supporting rapid family reunification within the displaced community and the area of relocation. Including identification of community-level focal points and/or standby caregivers.As part of the protection in the emergency response unit, mobile teams will also be strengthened with technical training and prepositioning of essential equipment to support separated and unaccompanied children, as well as children injured or subjected to violence or abuse in the context of a disaster. Periodical facility risk assessment will also be conducted to take mitigation measures and response pre and post disaster period.
