Project Summary

Planned project:
The project target 3200 families with regular (monthly) food assistance through two appropriate modalities:
A- Cash for food: GAN will target 1600 most vulnerable families in village and camps with cash for food, the cash intervention is feasible in these camps as they all have access to different main markets in the targeted communities, eight rounds of cash for food will be distributed, each round consists of 20 days for distribution and 10 days for validation and PDM, 60$ will be allocated for each family as recommended by FSL cluster and CWG, the data gained form the FSL cluster indicate this areas as high priority ranking and no partner plan to target the same vulnerable and underserved camps, according to the needs assessment, the first priority is food, the proposed modality is a cash for food, as it is considered one of the most effective modality since the markets are available near camps.
B- E-voucher for Food: GAN will target 1600 most vulnerable families in the village with food voucher with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food for 8 months for most vulnerable groups, beneficiaries could replace the monthly top-up assistance (60$ per family) with the main food items from the markets contracted with GAN and will be able to buy among 40 specified items with restricted amounts that allowed us to fulfill the calories min standard set by the WFP.
Monthly market assessment in terms of availability of the items, prices, and accessibility will be conducted to ensure the local markets are functioning and all beneficiaries have good and equal accessibility to the different goods.

4 - Needs

13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance, with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. More than 90% of the population is now estimated to live below the poverty line.The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities in a context where 50% of the work-age population is now estimated unemployed.In particular, there was a decrease in employment of 18.2 percent for males and 19.7 percent for females. Most enterprises lost significant revenue ranging from 40 percent to 100 percent, and about 13 percent had to close their operations.While hostility-induced displacement in early 2020 generated additional needs amongst the population in Syria for internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities.Facing deteriorated living standards, families are increasingly adopting harmful coping mechanisms. Seventy-one percent of households and 75 % of female-headed households have taken on more debt since August 2019.28% of families now adopt ‘crisis’ or ‘emergency’ food-related coping strategies, including withdrawing children from school to have them work instead, selling property, migrating due to lack of food, and early child marriage. Protection Sector, January 2021Debts and credits are a common coping strategy with 52.9% of households having taken on debt since displacement with an average amount of debt per household as SYP 701,443. The primary reason for taking on debt was to buy food.In addition to visiting the camps and meeting with camp managers and local councils to determine the best response method.

5 - Implementation of activities

Monthly food assistance for the assessed food insecure people: 1- Food voucher: The project will provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 1600 HHs. The project will provide food assistance by restricted voucher modality (e-voucher) with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food by a monthly 60$ which will last for 8 months with a total of 480$ for each HH. 2- Cash for food: The project will provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 1600 HHs, in the camps The project implemented through cash for food modality using (e-voucher) regular access to food by providing a monthly 60$ per family which will last for 8 months. We will use beneficiary selection criteria that prioritize vulnerable HHs, including female or youth-headed households, those with elderly or disabled family members, and households with more than six persons They will be able to use the e-voucher card immediately after the orientation sessions. The beneficiary will be able to buy with 60$ his/her needs among 40 specified food items with restricted amounts that are allowed in the e-voucher card to fulfill the calorie standard set by the WFP. GAN chose to implement through e-voucher system based on many reasons, such as saving the dignity of beneficiaries, as to prevent food basket selling which is frequently happened and GAN implemented and used this modality in 2019 and 2020 and the beneficiary satisfaction rate was 98%

GAN try to build the capacity of individuals to protect themselves and recover from acts of GBV, by encouraging and promoting equal participation of men and women in all community activities and through providing equal opportunities of employment, and this project will have female staff members to deal with sensitive cases, the communication through (WhatsApp channels) between our female beneficiaries will be conducted through the female staff and the distribution points will be the safe and accessible places, availability of electricity and ventilation and toilets for males separated from females.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at distribution points. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of sexual exploitation or abuse. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account.Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the distribution points.The distribution points will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:- The MTA using the e-voucher modality are available and functioning.- Markets are functioning properly.- Adequate presence of vendors to support the voucher program- Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries- COVID context is continuedRisk:1: the risk of that one money transfer service is responsible for transferring the cash amount:Likelihood: low, Impact: high.GAN has a prior fundamental condition for a money transfer agent: the payment is conducted after distribution.2: COVID-19 spread in target communities:Mitigation: GAN team takes all necessary measures to avoid the transmission and spread of the disease infection, such as wearing a face mask, gloves, providing hand sanitizers, maintaining social space spacing, and measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the distribution point, GAN will use the latest version of recommendations in regards of distributing NFI under the cotext of COVID-193: Lockdown and curfew in the targeted communities due to the COVID-19: Mitigation: In case that a curfew is imposed, there will be permitted from the local authorities for humanitarian workers to work and movement, and GAN will change the cash assistance with in-kind assistance and will deliver assistance to the beneficiaries' homes (Door to door)4: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high.Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on a regular basis by the program team
