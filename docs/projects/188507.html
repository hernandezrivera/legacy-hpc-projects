Project Summary

The purpose will be achieved through working in both the Monthly Food Assistance and MPCA sectors. Through providing food security assistance, emergency food assistance, and multipurpose cash assistance, the intervention will achieve the purpose of improving food security and enabling current and newly displaced HHs and vulnerable communities to simultaneously meet their immediate multifaceted food and non-food needs with dignity and flexibility. This will further support the overall intervention goal by addressing life-saving needs and promoting resilience.
SR will enable 9,000 vulnerable individuals (1,500 HHs) to meet their basic food needs by providing Monthly Cash distribution (cash in hand) over a period of 8 months. Each HH will receive $65 per month, reflecting a total planned cash distribution (after 8 months) of $780,000 USD. Due to the deteriorating status for both IDPs and host communities, SR will target both groups. To ensure that the most vulnerable HHs selected, SR will conduct 100% door-to-door registration and verification and select the HHs according to the SR vulnerability scoring system. Thereafter, SR will undertake secondary verification of a random sample of 10% of the selected HHs.
Also, SR will provide 9,000 displaced individuals (1,500 HHs) across the Harim district with an MPCA 130 $ one-off gran, reflecting a total planned cash distribution of $195,000. Through MPCA, beneficiaries will have the flexibility to choose the items that meet their basic emergency food and non-food needs with dignity from local markets. MPCA to these 1,500 HHs will entail a one-off direct distribution (cash in hand) of 130 USD as a Cash WG recent recommended amount where any changes in the design and the exact MPCA value will be reconfirmed based on monthly REACH updates and reflected in REACH price monitoring reports.
MPCA will be provided to the most vulnerable HHs in the Idleb governorate, Harim district, it will be distributed according to a process of registration and verification, using a scoring system.

4 - Needs

A Household Economic Profile undertook by SR, Dec 2021. Aiming to establish a representation of household economic status and needs, and to get a very comprehensive intervention that will solve the issues that face people in NWS. The findings were:• The economic vulnerability of HHs impacts their ability to meet basic needs. Indeed, HHs which have already spent their savings or incurred large debts to meet basic needs such as food potentially risk being unable to absorb economic shocks, such as loss of a livelihood source.• More households reported using strategies limiting the intake of food than relying on support from others, there is a need to provide distribution of food vouchers/ food assistance for vulnerable households especially for families who lost their source of income due to the economic situation and/or to C-19.Gaps exist in meeting the increased Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) food needs in NWS, and in responding to HH preferences for cash to purchase the food items they need. SR’s Rapid Needs Assessment in Harim district found that only 20% of respondents reported cash assistance being provided in their communities during the last month. In addition to cash being identified as the most urgent food need (90 % of respondents), Needs Assessment findings in camps indicated that IDPs identify cash as a first priority needs across all sectors.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

SR will do many actions to mainstream protection and gender across all project stages as following: SR conducted a rapid needs assessment by using Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with males and females separately as well as the most vulnerable groups such as disables and PLW to ensure the program design meets the exact need for beneficiaries to avoid doing harm in the implementation stages. Female staff will be hired for distribution/redemption and MEAL to ensure equal access for all beneficiary categoriesincluding females headed of household, girls and widowed. Beneficiaries will be clearly informed that all distributed value-based vouchers are free and that cash distributed is provided with no obligations on their end. Options for home delivery of the needed food commodities for vouchers which will be redeemed on behalf of vulnerable persons (e.g., PWD who cannot access the markets, elderly, etc.) confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries will be clearly informed where to refer or report incidents or violations. The day, time, and location of thedistribution of the vouchers will be discussed with different community members and leaders to determine the safest most accessible location to allow all individuals including women, children, and the elderly to access the service with dignity and without exposure to further risk of harm; moreover, the list of contracted markets, allowed food commodities, and the redemption


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risks: 1-SEA risk against beneficiaries especially women 2-GBV Risk against beneficiaries 3-Not equal access for services from marginalized groups such as disabled and women (Bias and discrimination in distributions process) Mitigating Measures: All staff and vendors will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA. PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the distribution site, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp). All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously. Female staff will always be present at the distribution/ redemption places andinvolved in the distribution process All markets populated by beneficiaries will have access to educational posters/banners for the appropriate use. Ensuring safe and equal access to inclusive, accessible, and non-discriminatory list of markets to get their food assistance. Protection cases including GBV will be referred to the respective focal point NGOs in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational R: A-Markets are not functioning which may affect the voucher redemption, Likelihood: M, Impact: HB-Exchange rate fluctuations between USD and TRY and SYP which affects the price in NWS, Likelihood: H, Impact: MC-Lockdown due to Spread of C19 pandemic in NWS. Likelihood: M, Impact: H.Mitigation measures: 1-Regular price monitoring and markets assessment to ensure this intervention will not cause harm to the local markets 2- Monitor REACH Report for SMEB price monitoring 3- All voucher value will be loaded in USD and all contracts with service providers will be signed in USD 4- SR will apply precautionary measures which are in the FSL guidance about COVID-19 as well as WHO recommendations for staff and contractors in terms of hygiene practices, use PPE, and maintain physical distance for safety. Security R: A-General daily security threats including robbery and looting Likelihood: M, Impact: MB- Macro level conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, shifting alliances, border closure, and chemical attack. Likelihood: H, Impact: H.Mitigation measures: 1. SR will use vouchers modality so the looting will not an issue 2. Following SR safety and security guidelines and established safety protocols. 3. If the project location is no longer accessible, SR has plan B to shift the allocated quantity to other locations
