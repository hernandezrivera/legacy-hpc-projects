Project Summary

After more than two decades of civil conflict in Somalia the situation has been worsened by conflict, Droughts and floods and the resulting collapse of the country’s institutions. The conflict in Somalia is largely driven by poverty and inter-clan contests in a society that is founded on a deep clan-based culture with a strong pastoral tradition. Lack of a functioning centralized government for the whole country remains a major challenge. The entire country is burdened by the ravages of conflict and entrenched poverty.
The need for CCCM interventions in Mudug & Galgaduud Somalia continues to grow due to the lack of adequate access to CCCM facilities exacerbated by the continuous arrival of, Refugees, returnees, new IDPs, and Host Communities. As per the Joint flood assessment done by UNHCR, OCHA & partners on flood the GU rains resulted in flooding in this localities, Qardho, Ba, aadweyne and Hobyo Districts which are among Puntland &Galmudug Districts. Massive fire breaks out in Ceel Duaale grazing area Hobyo which resulted death and loss of properties and Migration. COVID-19 restrictions, including the suspension of international and domestic flights. Therefore, the situation has been worsened by increased IDP evictions in Puntland & Galmudug forcing vulnerable IDPs to settle in areas with limited CCCM services putting them at risk of related diseases. Thus, there are no solid waste collection systems in most camps with many IDP settlements often overwhelmed by uncollected garbage posing a major public health risk. Poor access to safe drinking water and lack of adequate sanitation facilities coupled with poor hygienic practices are major threats for the survival and development of children in Somalia especially among IDP children who suffer the brunt of diarrheal diseases and malnutrition. Large portions of the population are at persistent risk of Covid 19 Epidemics. The desperate shortage of cccm services has further been exacerbated by seasonal droughts and floods leaving the vulnerable households with limited affordable options. Poor sanitary conditions in densely populated and congested settlements characterized by lack of latrines, open defecation, on site human waste disposal combined with overflowing filled up pit latrines has led to direct fecal contamination of water points. The current status of sanitation in camps has affected negatively the privacy and dignity of women and girls, who mostly wait until dark to defecate in the bush which increases the risk of exposure to gender based violence, in particular, sexual violence.
WISE has been on the front line when it comes to responding to the urgent emergency needs of affected displaced persons as well as address protection related issues in hard to reach areas of Mudug region and Galgaduud regions. With constant displacements due to the aforementioned details newly displaced IDPs are not able to carry their household items. As a result, they continue to be in need of emergency assistance, including basic household assets, and emergency shelter that can lead to quick settlement and coping in the newly settled environment
The overall goal of this Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Project is to ensure equitable access to services and protection for displaced persons living in camps and camp-like settings, in Mudug & Galgaduud regions of Somalia so as to improve their quality of life and dignity during displacement, and advocate for solutions while preparing them for life after displacement. The main objective is to enhance the protective environment of refugees, IDPs and Host Communities in Puntland & Galmudug Somalia with emphasis on comprehensive solutions, social integration/access to basic services, self-reliance, and special needs, and create improved mutual trust and confidence among communities through implementation of peaceful co-existence. Enhance protection of refugees, asylum seekers, IDP’s and Host Communities living in the region.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Most households in somalia depend on cash to some extent, when aid is given in kind, households are often forced to seek cash in ways that may be detrimental to longer-term livelihoods, for example selling productive assets, or giving children to the care of relatives. Evidence shows that cash transfers reduce the negative coping strategies used by beneficiaries. stimulation of the local markets, cash-based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

This project aims to increase the effectiveness and efficiency of programme responses by promoting dignity and boosting the local economy through CBI to areas in mudug regions that are prone to conflict (subsequent interclan fighting and IS attacks and natural disasters (i.e. recurrent floods, locust invasion, drought etc.) thus our interventions will boost impacts that causing a negative effect on local markets. This project will also help enhance the community (IDPs, Returnees, Refugees and the local host community) coping mechanisms by giving the new avenues to support existing market systems and structures.
