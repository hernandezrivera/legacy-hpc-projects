Project Summary

The proposed response aims to specifically support Objective 1 of the Health Sector under Beirut Explosions flash appeal – namely Support reconstruction of the Central Drug Warehouse and rehabilitation of damaged health facilities to enable and maintain functionality and service delivery via the provision of essential equipment and materials to enable resumption of the functionality of one of the medical services at the hospital of Karantina. Bearing in mind scale of destruction and funds required (more than 9MLN USD to cover costs of different components according to WHO Post Disaster Rapid Hospital Assessment) ACTED is appealing for 500,000 USD to contribute partially to the restoration of the service/s in the hospital, based on ongoing funding negotiations between ACTED and decentralized French authorities interested in providing this specific support.
With an established presence in the underserved neighbourhoods of Greater Beirut since 2013, ACTED is a well-positioned actor to support this intervention, both as a first-line responder to the port explosions in the provision of essential services as well as an INGO embedded in the urban Beirut communities for more than 15 years. ACTED has conducted several multi-sectorial (WASH, Shelter, Protection, MHPSS, Livelihoods) interventions in these areas which have granted the organization an extensive knowledge of key stakeholders in this complex urban environment. This allowed ACTED to immediately start-up operations and deliver on-the-spot emergency relief in the aftermath of the disaster while ensuring ongoing coordination with local and international actors active in the response. In the immediate aftermath of the explosion, ACTED closely coordinated with the Shelter sector, including through contributing to the data collection for the Shelter and Multi-Sectoral needs assessment led by LRC in the affected areas of Beirut and distribution of more than 1064 core relief items (including sealing off kits, dignity and baby kits) and removing over 1232 tons of debris from the streets and HHs of Beirut.

1 - Needs

At least 200 people were killed and 5,000 wounded following two explosions that erupted in Lebanon’s capital on Tuesday, August 4th at 6 pm. Hundreds have been reported missing in the aftermath of the blast, raising fears that the death toll will rise. The second explosion’s shock wave raced from the site of the blasts, Beirut’s industrial waterfront, into the city’s densely populated residential neighbourhoods and shopping districts downtown. Dozens of nearby buildings collapsed or were severely damaged, destroying more than 1000 families’ homes, and leaving more than 300,000 people displaced. The blast came at a sensitive time for Lebanon, which is struggling through a severe economic and public health crisis. Since October 2019, Lebanon has faced dramatic political and financial challenges which have culminated in an unprecedented economic recession, further compounding existing vulnerabilities among the country’s population – Lebanese and refugees alike. Against this backdrop, the COVID-19 outbreak and related containment measures have largely affected all population. The number of COVID-19 hospitalizations has increased sharply since the beginning of July and pressure on both public and private hospitals is an all-time high. On August 4th, before the explosion, Lebanon’s main coronavirus hospital announced being on the verge of reaching full capacity in its critical care ward. Karantina hospital - one of the major medical centres in Beirut suffered from severe damages to the extent that none of its units, old or new, are currently operational or safe. The old and new buildings are approximately 80% and 30% destroyed respectively (while the new building is not yet operational nor equipped). The damages include collapsed walls and partitions, cracks in multiple beams and columns, rusted steel reinforcements, severe damage to windows, doors, water tanks, and the wastewater system. Moreover, the electrical supply for the old building is damaged and the new one is still unconnected, therefore most equipment could not be tested, although much of it is visibly broken and covered by sand, plaster, or glass. The Neonatal unit has also been destroyed. The immediate priorities identified by the assessment team are the transferral of equipment to a safe place, testing of the equipment, removal/demolition of the old building, and the completion of the new building to replace it. The gravity of the disaster and the pre-existing crisis conditions call for immediate action aimed at restoring essential medical services for vulnerable residents who lost their homes and who lack means and resources to address the essential needs.

2 - Activities

1: Provision of essential equipment and materials to support the restoration of medical service at the hospital of Karantina to support estimated 4,638 individuals (2,180 women and 2,458 men of which)ACTED will provide essential equipment and materials to enable the resumption of one of the medical services offered by the hospital. This will be done in close collaboration with the Ministry of Public Health, WHO, the hospital director and any other health partner supporting the hospital to ensure that support for this service is well coordinated with the overall plan for the restoration of medical services and other governmental and non-governmental actions undertaken in response to the destruction of the hospital.
