Project Summary

The project includes a comprehensive household-based food security approach by increasing the technical skills for climate supported food production and increasing the income so that they meet their food requirement. The project will be implemented both in the host community and in Rohingya camps.
The household-based approach will analyse current trends of income and agricultural production of each household and will facilitate a development plan for strengthening food security at the household level. Based on the plan, alternative agro or non-agro-based income-generating activities will be started. The project will include 80% of the female member or young women. The project will contribute to starting additional income sources in each household by engaging female household members.
The project will adopt two steps income-earning and food security plan. Firstly, the project will engage the target people for quick income earning and food production from the kitchen garden and rearing of poultry birds. This will ensure quick income and nutritional foods at the household level. Secondly, the project will develop a business plan for each household for long-term income generation activities either in the agricultural sector or in the non-agricultural sector. The project will include a strong contextual analysis for sustainable food security at the household level.
Each of the target people will receive training in climate-sensitive food production (chicken, duck, goat rearing, and homestead garden). They will also receive training on ‘starting and improving your business (SIYB). The business capital will be transferred to each household to purchase productive assets for climate-sensitive food production. A community-based purchase committee will be formed to improve the negotiation and bargaining capacity of the target people. As the project will select 80% female members, therefore, this involvement with the purchase committee will improve their access to the market. The project will emphasize the plantation along with kitchen gardening and will coordinate with the EETWG.
The project will increase the market linkages to purchase inputs and sell the products in a coordinated way. The periodic meeting will be organized between the market actors and the target people to have access of them at the agro-based value chain. A risk mitigation plan will be developed for the security of food production. The target people in the host community will be linked with the MFIs for further capital for a better food security situation and the sustainability of the intervention. The regular meeting will be organized with the Government Extension Department ( Agriculture, Fisheries, Livestock) to improve the relationship between government officials and target people so that the target people receive technical support beyond the project period. The project will conduct a fortnightly session on the rights issues, early warning, and recovery system.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Both the Rohingya and the host community need support for food security because they are under double pressure with the post-COVID situation and price hikes due to Russia-Ukraine War in the country. From the past, the people in the Rohingya camps were consuming inadequate food. The people in the host community had a similar situation with inadequate food consumption. The Rohingya Influx vulnerability assessment report (June 2022) showed that ‘More than one-third of Rohingya households (36 %) and one-fourth of host community households (25 %) borrowed food or relied on support from friends or relatives.’ Therefore, both community needs support for quick income generation so that they may purchase food with the increased income. The report also showed that overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly high since 2019 among Rohingya households. The latest findings showed that 95 % of all Rohingya households are moderate to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, similar to 2020 (96%) and 2019 (94 %). These results reflect the slow economic recovery of an already fragile population with neither income sources nor livelihood opportunities. Overall vulnerability in the host community has shown an upward trend since 2017 and has remained high since 2020, with 52 % of the population moderately to highly vulnerable in 2021 compared to 51 % in 2020. The main drivers were economic contraction and decline in economic activity across most sectors causing reduced income opportunities and market volatility during the COVID-19 lockdown in a population highly dependent on daily wage labour.Both communities were also suffering from the low quality of food where they could not meet the nutritional requirements. The suffering is higher in households with more than 3 children under 15 years old or the older population. Therefore, food security support is required among the extremely poor people in the host community and the camps. There is a lack of diversification in the production and consumption of food among the people in the host community. They have also lacking for plan-wise food production at the household level. Due to the geo-location, the fisheries sector could be an option for income and food security which is in the trap of the middleman and the extremely poor people are not a full benefit. Both the Rohingya nor the host community people have no productive capital that can be used for starting and improving businesses. On the other hand, they do not have training in the technical aspects of business that may contribute to climate-sensitive food production as well as income from non-agro-based earnings. People in the host community and Rohingya camps are vulnerable to disaster due to geolocation. They lave lacking for a coping mechanism to survive the disaster.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is built based on integrated approaches with social cohesion and education. Plan International formed youth clubs in the host community and inside the camps. Regular awareness sessions including leadership development-related activities are implemented. Plan International is implementing learning centers inside the camps and arranging classes for the dropout adolescents and youth in youth clubs in the host community. To avoid overlapping with other organizations, Plan International will implement the project in the selected camps. Plan International is taking part in the camp-level coordination with other organizations. Plan International Bangladesh is actively participating in different sectors/WG like food security, livelihood & skills development, SRHR, and Education, etc, and coordinating with other stakeholders during the implementation of the projects and program development. Before the start of any project, Plan International arranges meetings with the NGOs in the locality or camps to share the objectives and avoid overlapping. Plan is continually working to ensure multi-year funding which has already started in Teknaf. Therefore, the proposed project is fully aligned with the rationalization plan.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

As a global child rights organization, protection is one of the priority issues in every project of Plan International. For the staff, training on protection is mandatory. The project included specific activities in the areas of protection. Before, every event like a meeting with the government extension offices will be assessed in the areas of safeguarding. Plan International Bangladesh has the tools for the protection of the target people from any harmful situation.

3.2 - Gender

The project will work for agency building of girls and young women by bringing them under a group and disseminating information and capacity development. A family-based approach is used in the project including the use of specific tools that ensure the participation of the girls and young women in a decision-making process at the household level. The project will include 80% of young women so that they have regular income to meet the food security crisis as well as control over resources. The project ensured men's engagement and diversification for gender transformation where role sharing will be encouraged. Plan International has comprehensive gender marker tools which included agency of the girls/young women, diversity, enabling environment, male engagement etc. which will be used in the project annually. Based on the report, activities will be reviewed to ensure gender transformation. Plan International is also working nationally on the area of decent working and fear of violence. T

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

As per Plan International's policy, girls and boys below 18 years are treated as children. Plan follows strict child protection mechanisms in developing and implementing all the projects. All the staff in Plan International including partner NGOs are trained in safeguarding. Plan arranges periodic reviews on the safeguarding among the staff. However, the protection-related activities are equally applicable to external stakeholders, visitors and consultants.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will follow diversity to include persons with disability and excluded people. It is expected that 8% of Persons with Disability will be included in the project. Again the project will select the most vulnerable members of the population by arranging PRA sessions and wealth ranking. The women headed and member with Persons with Disability in households will get priority.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by Plan International Bangladesh. Plan International inform people in the camps and the host community about their rights and entitlements, and participation in decisions that affect them. The courtyard session is a platform in the project where the target people express their opinion which has been included in the design of the project. The income empowers the youth, especially girls and young women and creates space for control over resources. Awareness raising on disaster preparedness is part of the project. The project ensures services from the government offices for better resilience.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the DRM and climate change issues are embedded in the project. The target people are oriented on the preparation and recovery mechanism from the courtyard sessions fortnightly. As a risk mitigation plan, the target people are linked with the MFIs for savings and loans so that they may use it as further capital after any disaster. The project encourages environment-friendly technologies for agricultural production, specifically for vegetable production at the household level. The target people will be trained in the preparation of compost also. As a global humanitarian organization, Plan International has a well-equipped DRR response team who are ready to respond immediately after any disaster. There are 2 warehouses of Plan equipped with Non-food items. Plan is responding to the victims of Sitrang in Teknaf in 2022. The project included plantation activities along with the kitchen garden as a long-term impact on climate change.
