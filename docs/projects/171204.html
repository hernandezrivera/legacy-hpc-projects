Project Summary

The continuation of protracted crises in Somalia, now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, Floods and
droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. Approximately,
4.6 million Somalis are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation
services of the affected population in many districts, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health
and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities. This spike in numbers compared to 2020
is mainly exacerbated by the situation developed as a result of Covid-19 and its rapid transmission. Lack
of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD),
leading to malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of 25.3% in
children below 5 years of age. It is also a cause of relapse (ADD) when treated patients and children
returning to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited.According to JMCNA, 53% of the IDPs face challenges in terms of access to WASH services in Somalia (53% as highest pertaining to access to soap and handwashing facilities). Furthermore, issues related to access to WASH
facilities for vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities, elderly, women, boys and girls is an
ongoing challenge. It requires availability of reliable data, extensive consultations and community engagement on planning and design while considering unique needs of each group.
To meet the most urgent identified WASH needs in the targeted areas WRRS proposes the following WASH Humanitarian Response Plan activities.

Mitigation Measures

WRRS will ensure the risks to beneficiaries are reduced as humanly possible by ensuring strong community consultative, engagement approaches from bottom up at all steps of project implementation. WRRS will give particular attention will be given to people with disabilities, elderly people, minority groups, unaccompanied children, women and children in designing, positioning, beneficiaries selection process to ensure their views, opinions are listen to and incoporated in project designing and planning. In addition WRRS will Coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases. WRRS will ensure WASH staff and committees should be representative of all groups within the community(e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should be given protection mainstreaming training.As part of transparent accountability frame work actions will WRRS will be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them.WRRS has qualified M&E and protection staffs are experience in Setting up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions. WRRS will ensure provision of information to the affected communities through various communication means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels eg. door-to-door, poster, radio,social media, use of pictograms).This will be done cross -sectoral community awareness on how WRRs works during project implementation to ensure community knowledge on their rights, participation project ownership and sustainability of humanitarian interventions offered,promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygiene kits, feedback box at WASH facilities, phone lines. To mitigate issue of risks of explosive and hazard contamination in project targeted area WRRS will ensure WASH facilities and distribution sites are far from military settlements, arm conflict bases sites, do distribution where possible at household level or cash modalities to reduce risk exposure to targeted benficiaries. Will also ensure proper storage and use of chemicals and chlorine by training people dealing with chemical have health and safety training in handling this chemicals. Locate WASH facilities at central locations within 500m are from settlement, ensure all WASH communal facilities sites are installed with lighting or targeted household do have access to solar lamps or othe portable lighting modes to risk risk of GBV, all WASH infrastructure construction and designs meet WASH cluster guidelines, protection mainstreaming and disability mainstreaming . WRRS will ensure WASH staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. will ensure also all staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). In addition will rotate staff engaged in distribution of hygiene kits and other WASH items for distribution to mitigate exploitation of beneficiaries. in order to address issue of improper use of beneficiaries data WRRS will keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use -apply effective data protection measures. In order to address to issue of land tenure or disputes WRRS will closely work with local authority, protection actors and advocate for land allocation to IDPs.
