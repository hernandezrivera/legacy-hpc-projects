Project Summary

Provide an emergency humanitarian mine action response for effective community resilience leading to the peaceful settlement of civilians in Gao, Kidal, Menaka, and Timbuktu, by assisting the population vulnerable to the threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war. MAG will deliver risk education on the threat of mines, explosive remnants of war and improvised explosive devices and of Small Arms and Light Weapons to women, girls, boys and men, community leaders, religious leaders, women associations, youth groups, drivers, internally displaced people (IDPs) and nomadic groups. MAG will also sensitize teachers, journalists and radio presenters to disseminate the security messages and to develop the capacity of key community actors to help protect the population. MAG will conduct non-technical survey to identify contaminated areas and facilitate clearance activities. In addition, given the rise of intercommunity clashes, MAG proposes to work on conflict prevention measures. Finally, MAG aims to reinforce the victim referral system, which is currently under development, to facilitate the access to the required assistance for the victims of mines and explosive remnants of war and their families, as many victims may live far from the locations where they can get support.

Needs

The socio-political and security context in Northern Mali is marked by the resurgence of inter-community conflicts, by the increase of asymmetric attacks by radical armed groups against national and international forces, and by the intensification of military operations. Civilian populations are exposed to the threat of injury and death because of the contamination by explosive remnants of war, mines and improvised explosive devices. Although civilian are not the direct target, they account for 27% of victims of explosive hazards incidents recorded between 2013 and 2018, and 46.3% of victims recorded between January and September 2018.The efforts of humanitarian mine actors have contributed to reduce the number of victims of explosive remnants of war from 55 victims recorded in 2015 to 15 recorded from January to September 2018. However, the explosive threat to the populations persists. The number of civilian casualties related to explosive devices such as improvised explosive devices and mines has increased significantly over the last five years (26 civilian casualties recorded in 2013, compared to 71 recorded in 2017, and 171 from January to September 2018). The contamination by explosive devices also restricts humanitarian access to vulnerable populations and access to livelihoods for people. Finally, due to the insecurity, there are high levels of civilian gun ownership in Northern Mali and therefore the civilian population is also exposed to incidents caused by involuntary discharge and unsafe storage of small arms.

Activities

• Training and accreditation for locally recruited Community Liaison (CL) teams.• Liaison and promotion of partnerships with national organisations. • Focus group discussions with community leaders, religious leaders, women’s associations, youth groups, drivers’ associations, etc. to identify the needs and priorities of the affected communities.• Non-technical survey to identify the scale and scope of Explosive Remnant of War (ERW) contamination.• Risk education sessions by locally recruited CL Officers to reduce community members’ risk of death and injury from mines, ERW and Small Arms and Light Weapons.• Teachers’ training on risk education messaging in school environments.• Training of community focal points who will carry out risk education sessions and report accidents.• Radio public service announcements on RE messages and call-in radio shows to raise mine risk awareness using radio presenters who speak local languages.• Community meetings and workshops on peace and reconciliation (through a partner, to be identified).• Establishment or reinforcement of Peace Committees• Referral system to support the victims, including family members of civilians injured or killed by mines/ERW• Ongoing data collection to improve need assessments and to adapt activities taking into account specific needs of women, girls, boys and men.• Risk education session surveys with a sample of the beneficiaries to measure impact. Surveys will capture samples of beneficiaries’ levels prior to any risk education delivery, immediately after risk education delivery and again approximately six months following risk education delivery.• Regular supervision visits, including quality assurance.
