Project Summary

This project is intended to improve the emergency preparedness and response capacity of MA’AN Development Center through the physical stockpiling of urgent non-food items. It includes the pre-positioning of NFI kits to respond to the urgent needs of the affected population at the onset of an emergency considering their age and gender requirements. The NFI kits to be procured and pre-positioned within this project will serve affected people during different emergencies including conflicts, weather extreme, and Covid-19 pandemic.
This project comes in line with HRP strategic objective (3): “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, the protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. It contributes to enhancing emergency response arrangements for possible man-made and natural hazards including the Covid-19 pandemic by promoting the access of vulnerable households and individuals to NFIs at the onset and during the emergency.
The project design is informed by the findings and recommendations of the Shelter/NFIs Cluster HNO for 2021. According to the HNO 2021, there is an increasing need for the provision of the necessary NFIs and disinfection materials to quarantine centers and the families at home quarantine as a result of Covid-19 outbreak in the Gaza Strip. In addition, the unstable and deteriorating situation in the Gaza Strip may lead to a new devastating escalation and the displacement of large numbers of the population, estimated at 600,000 (Shelter/NFI Cluster HNO 2021). Further, the HNO 2021 shows that some 235,000 people in the Gaza Strip are at risk of flooding during winter as the majority of people in need are living in poor shelter conditions. This project looks in-depth at the needs of affected people during the aforementioned emergencies and proposes a set of activities that respond to those needs considering the specific needs of the most vulnerable groups including elderlies, people with disabilities, and children.
In addition, this project focuses on specific on increasing the capacity of hosting communities in Gaza and North Gaza governorates to provide for adequate and gender-sensitive responses to the need of IDPs during possible future escalations of conflict. A set of NFI kits including bedding sets and sealing-off kits in addition to dignity and hygiene kits for the different gender and age groups including women, men, and children will be pre-positioned at 6 warehouses owned by MA’AN and it's 5 partnering CBOs in Gaza and North Gaza governorates. A set of hosting communities will be targeted in Gaza and the Northern Governorates including Southern Rimal, Beach Camp, Sheikh Radwan, Al Nasser Area, Jabalia Al Nazleh, and Western Zaitoon Area. MA’AN capitalizes on its community-based emergency response structure to support the IDPs in hosting communities and others in the PA-Led designated emergency shelters to access their needs of life-saving NFIs during emergency events.
This project will target a total of 9,353 individuals including 2,580 men, 2,520 women, 2,152 boys, and 2,101 girls. Out of the total target of the project, 20 persons with disabilities will be supported. The targeted individuals will have access to their needs of shelter NFIs at the onset of an emergency. In addition, the project will target a total of 200 community members (emergency first responders), including 120 men and 80 women, with training on the mainstreaming of the humanitarian standards and protection principles in emergency responses. Further, a total of 400 individuals (200 men, and 200 women) will be targeted with awareness-raising sessions on child protection, prevention of sexual abuse, GBV-related issues, and self-protection measures during emergencies.

1 - Needs

The design of this project was informed by the results of the recent shelter vulnerability assessment conducted by MoPWH in January 2020, which concludes that some 235,000 people in the Gaza Strip are at constant risks of flooding during the rainy season particularly the people living in ground floors and houses located in low depression areas near the valleys or adjacent to Sewage Water pumping stations. The assessment has also revealed that the instability in the Gaza Strip may lead to a new devastating escalation and the displacement of large numbers of the population, estimated at 600,000,. This coincides with the increase in cases affected by the pandemic, and in both scenarios, the shelter and isolation centers need sufficient quantities of NFIs to meet different needs. MA’AN has conducted an assessment in August 2020 targeting 40 families who were supported with NFIs during February 2019 as they were affected by the harsh weather conditions and flooding. The assessment included 2 focus group discussions with 40 persons (25 men and 15 women) representing the targeted families to collect feedback about the implemented response and to listen to their concerns related to the coming winter season. The assessment has revealed that 100% families are still vulnerable to the potential flooding and harsh weather conditions. Further, the assessment has revealed that all families will be in a dire need to be supported with NFIs including mattresses, pillows, and blankets at the onset of the respective emergency. In addition, MA'AN had conducted an assessment in September 2020 to study the experience of hosting communities in war 2014 and examine their needs in relation to access to emergency needs of non-food items. This assessment was guided by the findings of MA'AN’s review for its experience in the last two years concerning the stockpiling of emergency NFIs, and responding to emergencies in coordination with the shelter cluster and the national emergency response structures. The assessment involved the partner CBOs and hosting communities through a set of consultations. This besides focus groups discussions with 3 groups of hosting communities from Gaza and North Gaza governorates which were identified by an assessment conduct by MA’AN Development Center and We-World GVC in 2019. The assessment has involved 60 members (37 men and 23 women) of the hosting communities- some of them are emergency first responders. The FDGs were intended to examine their experiences in the past wars (2008, 2012, and 2014) as hosting communities. In addition, MA’AN has conducted consultations with the shelter cluster to address the gaps in place and to provide recommendations regarding the working methods. Overall, it was found that hosting communities and IDPs have experienced critical difficulties accessing non-food items and shelter assistance due to the lack of accessible and safe NFI warehouses which undermined their protection. In addition, the assessment has revealed that an essential part of the NFI assistance that was provided to hosting communities and IDPs was at a low-quality level and was not responsive to the diverse needs of the different age and gender groups. During the years 2019 and 2020, MA'AN has managed to distribute almost 51% of the NFI stockpiles people affected by the harsh weather storms in 2019 and the crisis of Covid-19. The distributed NFIs included 727 Hygiene Kit, 716 Dignity Kit, 567 Mats (2.7x3.6m), 3303 Mattresses, 4573 Pillows, 3515 Blankets, and 155 pieces of Nylon. The assessment has also revealed the following concerns and recommendations:• The need to adapt the kits (packaging model) to be more suitable for individuals' needs (instead of family base kits) with considering the special needs of women, children, and men. It is needed to enhance the efficiency of the Warehouses Management Protocols and Plans.• There is a need to address more effectively the special needs of PWD and elderly people.• It is needed to enhance the warehousing capacity while applying good practices ensuring safety and hygiene.• The emergency program that has been implemented by MA'AN and the five partners CBOs should provide dignity, access, participation, and safety for all people affected by disasters and crises. It is needed to provide clear measures on how to mainstream these four principles based on a consideration of gender, age, disability, and other diversity factors. This should address protection, gender, and inclusion concerns.• The Bedding kits should consider the issues related to different weather and seasons, and in particular for the blankets.The NFI kits to be pre-positioned within this project include individual hygiene kits for men, women and children, individual dignity kits for men, women, and children, and infant kits. The content of these kits will contribute to promoting the protection of the affected people during emergencies as they allow them to maintain adequate hygiene practices and mitigate the risk of infection by Covid-19. In addition, the NFI kits will include family bedding sets, family sealing-off kits, cleaning and disinfection kits for hosting families, kits for people with disability, and hygiene and dignity kits for elderlies. The sealing off kit will be for the use of the hosting families in order to provide for the adaptation of the hosting space addressing the concerns of the overcrowding conditions, GBV and child protection concerns, and to provide for more protective and safe hosting space addressing the house vulnerabilities to the weather conditions (winter-related risks). The Bedding Sets, hygiene kits, and dignity kits will be for the use of the IDPs or affected people by Covid-19 and harsh weather conditions (families and individuals) to address their emergency needs while helping women, girls, PWDs, and elderlies maintain their dignity and protection.

2 - Activities

The project activities focus on replenishing the existing stockpiles of NFIs at the warehouse of MA’AN Development Center and its 5 partnering CBOs which are located in Gaza and North Gaza governorates in order to promote the capacity of MA’AN and its partnering CBOs to undertake effective, timely, and gender-sensitive emergency response at the onset of an emergency, including possible future escalations of conflict, harsh weather conditions, and COVID-19 pandemic. During 2019 and 2020, MA’AN has carried many emergency responses addressing the needs of people affected by harsh weather conditions in the rainy season of 2019 and the outbreak of Covid-19 inside the Gaza Strip. Almost 51% of the NFI stockpiles were distributed, which influenced MA'AN’s current level of shelter NFI stocks. As a result, and in light of the shelter/NFIs cluster HNO for 2020, MA’AN expects that the current stock of NFIs may not be sufficient to response effectively in the future emergencies. The project activities are designed to address the findings of the assessments conducted by MA’AN Development Center. The proposed activities will support for the safe and accessible pre-positioning of an additional quantities of NFI stockpiles that will be allocated for meeting the urgent non-food needs of affected households and individuals in Gaza and North Gaza governorates during emergency events. The project will also promote the stock management capacity of the partner CBOs through a set of capacity building activities that focus on strengthening the stockpiling and warehouse management systems and protocols in place to allow for more safe, efficient and responsive emergency response. The project’s main activities include: 1) Revisit the contents of the NFI kits in consultation with the shelter cluster. 2) Update MA'AN partner CBOs about the project plan and the arrangements in place. This will include a kick-off meeting that will be conducted with each of the partner CBOs to orient them about the project and arrange for the implementation of the project activities including the stockpiling of the additional quantities of NFI kits at their warehouse. 3) Conduct PSEA training for the project staff. The training will include an introduction to PSEA and the SOPs adopted by MAAN on how to report SEA cases. 4) Procure and preposition NFI and emergency shelter kits at the warehouses of MA’AN partner CBOs. This includes elaborating the BoQs of the kits in terms of technical specifications based on consultations with the Shelter Cluster. 5) Conduct maintenance for the warehouses of MA’AN and partner CBOs to improve the stockpiling conditions and promote the safety measures. 6) Equip the warehouses with the necessary supplies and materials to ensure adequate and safe stockpiling of the NFI kits. 7) Update the contingency plans of the partner CBOs to include issues such as managing the complaints and feedback of the affected population including IDPs and hosting communities during emergency events, how to assess the needs of the affected population at the onset of an emergency, and set out plans for emergency response, and the gender-sensitive and safe distribution of NFI assistance on the affected population during emergency time. 8) Re-designing the layout of the warehouses and developing a stockpiling plan for the new stocks of NFIs to be pre-positioned at MAAN warehouses and CBO warehouses. 9) Provide training to the emergency teams in the partner CBOs on managing emergency response during crisis and risk reduction. The training will address issues such as how to mainstream them while addressing the needs of IDPs and hosting communities, and how to assess the needs of the IDPs and the hosting communities in emergencies. 10) Conduct set of awareness sessions in the targeted hosting communities to enhance the community preparedness capacities, addressing 1. child protection, preventing and reporting sexual abuse, GBV related issues (how to address, how to deal with, and how to prevent), and referral pathways of complaints of SEA, 2. the mapping of the GBV and child protection services providers, 3. the existing national emergency preparedness and response systems, 4. The self-recovery through community-based mechanisms and how to address privacy concerns while mainstreaming the protection and GBV related concerns in the adaptation of the shelter, 5. orientation about protection measures such as explosive remnants of war (ERW), and first aid skills. This activity is linked to indicator 13.1. 11) Conduct training for emergency first responders “community members” on the mainstreaming of the humanitarian standards and protection principles in emergency responses, with particular focus on issues related to GBV, prevention of sexual abuse, and child protection. This activity is related to indicator 11.1. As for Covid-19 protective measures and considerations, MA’AN will ensure the application of all necessary pre-cautionary hygiene and protection measures and practices to maintain the safety and protection of its staff, supplies, and the project beneficiaries during the implementation of the project activities. As for the pre-positioning of the NFI stockpiles, MA’AN will develop a protocol for supplying the materials to the warehouses including the provision of hygiene and self-protection tools and hygiene material. The protocol will also include SOPs for the vendors regarding the packaging and supply of the kits. In addition, MA’AN will apply all protection guidelines as instructed by the WHO and the Ministry of Health. MA’AN will also conduct training for the project staff on the mainstreaming of the protection principles related to Covid-19 and the PPE at the beginning of the project. Moreover, MA’AN will develop SOPs for the distribution of the NFI kits on project beneficiaries. The SOPs will include the provision of hygiene materials, safe transportation for the staff, and measures to avoid crowding.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

In the last three years, MA’AN has been working on developing an accountable, accessible, and gender-sensitive complaint system. The system offers different means to address complaints or feedback in writing, verbally, via phone or SMS, directly to the responsible person/field monitors from MA'AN, and verbally and directly to MA’AN offices. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

As part of its work on enhancing a gender-sensitive emergency response system, MA'AN has an updated contingency plan and emergency SOPs that include policies, procedures and work practices sensitive to gender issues and child protection concerns. MA'AN's humanitarian team and all emergency structures including the partner CBOs, community-based emergency response teams, and volunteers are well trained on these policies and practices and are capacitated with the knowledge and resources to carry out a gender-sensitive humanitarian response and address all gender issues and IHL violations during an emergency event properly. The CBOs and volunteers who are involved in managing the project activities will be well oriented about the MA’AN contingency plan and related emergency SOPs. In addition, they will receive training on the protection principles and how to mainstream while addressing the needs of IDPs and Hosting communities. During the project, the emergency first responders “community members” will receive training on the mainstreaming of the humanitarian standards and protection principles in emergency responses, with particular focus on GBV and child protection related concerns. The training and orientation for CBOs, volunteers, and emergency first responders will ensure a particular focus on GBV detection and referral and will ensure linkage to child protection referral networks. From another perspective, this project seeks to enhance the protection of IDPs in the hosting communities in Gaza and North Gaza governorates through providing NFI kits that respond effectively to the emergency needs of both IDPs and the hosting families. The identified NFI kits in this project take into consideration the concerns and specific needs of all IDPs, particularly women and children who are more likely to be exposed to GBV during an emergency event. In addition, it includes NFI kits for the use of the hosting families to enhance their preparedness for safe and protective hosting conditions through providing for the adaptation of the hosting spaces, addressing the concerns of the overcrowding conditions, GBV, and child protection concerns.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In coordination with the partner CBOs, a set of sessions will be conducted in the targeted communities to ensure that they are well-oriented about the project scope and the intended support. The sessions will include orientation about MA’AN complaint system and mechanisms. The project includes to develop contingency plans for the partner CBOs and building the capacity of the CBOs teams addressing clear mechanisms to ensure equal and impartial access to aid. A set of accountability mechanisms will be ensured in place. In coordination with the partner CBOs, a set of sessions will be conducted in the targeted communities to ensure that they are well-oriented about the project scope and the intended support. The sessions will include orientation about MA’AN complaint system and mechanisms. Accountability to the targeted communities will be encouraged and ensured through a gender-sensitive complaint and feedback mechanism that involves direct and regular communication between MA'AN and the final beneficiaries to allow affected persons to communicate their concerns and complaints on regular basis. Specific attention will be given to the complaints and feedback mechanism in order to increase the ownership of beneficiaries over the project, engage them in addressing the expected risks and challenges, and give them sufficient spaces to participate in the decision making and project evaluation process of the project.
