Project Summary

Johanniter is proposing an intervention focused on access to primary health care through curative, preventive, & promotional health care in general & maternal & child health in particular.
It will specifically support the following:
-Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) provision at four health facilities
- Functionality of the four health facilities with the provision of medical & non-medical supplies, (equipment, drugs) & capacity building of the MoH staff on BEmONC (Basic Emergency Obstetric Neonatal care), CMR (Clinical management of rape) & referral pathway, IPC (Infection-Prevention & control), EPI (expended program of Immunization)-cold chain & management of the program, IDSR (Integrated diseases surveillance & response).
-Provision of free primary health care for all IDPs/returnees & Host communities (specifically to those under five years, Pregnant & lactating women).
- Treatment of medical complications associated with the severe acute malnutrition specifically for those under five years at the Naivasha PoC/Inpatient therapeutic feeding program (& prevention through awareness focusing on Family MUAC screening, nutritious food-Balance diet, exclusive breastfeeding, etc in all the health-supported facilities).
-Health promotion of the EPI activities, & diseases surveillance for the potential outbreaks
- Awareness raising on prevention of GBV (Gender Based violence) & provision 1st aid psychosocial support
-Provision of incentives to the seconded staff from the SMoH & volunteers while ensuring easy access to primary health care & secondary via referral health services
The project targets 56,596 people in areas serviced by 4 health facilities including camps in Wau North and South; Kangi & Kuarjena PHC in Jur River Counties.
The action will target children under five years of age, pregnant & lactating women, vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities, survivors of GBV, & minority groups such as orphans, widows & young mothers. This project will compliment other projects currently being implemented in the same region by Johanniter & other actors.
- Johanniter International Assistance with funding from other sources is currently implementing activities in FSL, WASH, protection, Community Case Management, Nutrition sectors in Jur River & Wau counties.
Johanniter will collaborate & coordinate with other actors in the health sector such as:
- IOM in Primary health care support to some health facilities in PoCs.
- Islamic Relief in Primary health care service in Bagari PHCC, Kpaile/Bazia & Beseliya PHCCs.
The project will be implemented in partnership with the State Ministry of Health, the county health department, the Health Centers (CS), & local health committees.
The expected results in the intermediate & long term will be:
-The health facilities are equipped & functioning with reliable supplies of essential medicines & consumables during the project implementation period.
-MoH staff with capacity to ensure the provision of the quality health care services.
- Enhanced referral service for the under 5 years & PLW
- Increased early access to health service of the GBV survivors through increased awareness on GBV prevention & referral pathways.
- Earlier detection & response to outbreaks
The proposed action is in line with the guidelines & objectives of the South Sudan Health Cluster on life-saving interventions in areas where emergency thresholds are alarming, so as to reduce morbidity & excess mortality of people affected by the crisis. As well as cover, immediate needs whilst simultaneously embed durable solutions in the design of humanitarian interventions by addressing people's vulnerabilities & building their resilience.
The response will be conflict-sensitive, & it will consider community feedback & the different protection needs of women, men, girls, & boys & disable people affected by the crisis.

1 - Needs

The reality on the ground has shown that without support from external partners (humanitarian), it is practicably difficult for health facilities to continue providing quality primary healthcare services in an area where the majority (around 70-80% of south Sudanese) is leaving below 1$/day.A baseline survey conducted by the Johanniter in Jur River through funding from the German Federal Ministry for Economic affairs (BMZ) showed that: - The under-five crude mortality rate for Kangi Payam (Jur River) was four deaths out of 10,000 habitats from 2021 to the beginning of 2022;- The GAM rate (based on MUAC screening) is 4.2%, - The Minimum Acceptable Diet is 6.7% among children aged from 6-23 months in Jur River (and 11.8% in Wau) and the exclusively breastfeeding is 45.6%. This shows that both 0-5 months and 6-23 months old children are not getting enough nutritious foods for their growth. - 4.7 cases per 10,000 individuals (Among adolescents under 18 years) diagnosed with the Sexually transmitted disease during the consultation.- Cases of GBV/SGBV are still being reported (for example, the Johanniter up to 30 August 2022, reported 64 cases and 16 out of the 64 were from Jur River). 80% of which got holistic support, but still to cope with the stress, which requires long-term psychosocial support that Johanniter is supporting through the women and girl-friendly spaces. According to the assessment report of UNOCHA (30 AGUST 2022), due to Flooding in Jur River, the affected areas have 11 082 households with an estimated population of 57,367 in the 5 Payams. People's livelihoods have been affected, businesses have been disrupted, vegetables and crops have been destroyed, and some submerged. As a result of the flooding there is noted increase in water borne diseases for the Under 5, PLW, Malnourished children based on reports from the nutrition centers and ICCM sites. The Humanitarian Needs Overview South Sudan (issued in Feb 2022) shows that around 100-200 000 (including 46 000 IDPS) were estimated in need humanitarian support in Jur river and Wau counties. The Insecurity due to the presence of the OAG in the area result in low production of the household nutritious food consequently leading to HH Food insecurity and Malnutrition. Johanniter International Assistance has continued to support the affected communities as part of her contribution and its donors (BMZ, GFFO, ADH, UNICEF, and WFP) to alleviate human suffering and build quick resilience.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Identifying the most vulnerable has been done or is ongoing, and we will ensure it is completed beforehand where it still needs to be completed. In most cases, we will diversify the methodology with emphasis on the Help desk, community meetings, local focal points, and sometimes random visits to their home. Collaboration with other Johanniter team members funded by the other project will be essential to ensure linkage and extensive feedback data collection is done. While JIA is collecting feedback especially during focus group discussion from the beneficiaries, the participants will be segregated according to gender, age and vulnarabilities. In documentation of the complaints and feeback, JIA will dissagregated data according to age, gender and vulnerabilities so that when act upon the issues, the most vulnerable’s voice is also heard.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Johanniter is committed to the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), ensuring beneficiary participation in programming throughout the project and guarding their safety and dignity while implementing all the activities. With technical support from HQ Safeguarding Focal Point and with consultation from the target community, JIA will setup up a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CRFM). JIA will use different channels such as help desk, focus group discussions and community meetings (including inception meetings) with the beneficiaries. CRFM will enable beneficiaries to access all information needed concerning JIA and submit feedback and complaints regarding all services. The input collected regarding program implementation will be entered into a database, analyzed, and shared with the Field Manager (Field Manager), SPM (Senior Program Manager), and PMEAL (Planning, Monitoring-Evaluation-Accountability and learning) Coordinator (and if necessary, with the HoM) on a bi-weekly basis to address the complaints as soon as possible. The sensitive complaints from the beneficiaries, such as PSEA, will be treated with high confidentiality, as per the JIA Safeguding principle (survivor-focused approach). This feedback will be utilized to apply lessons learned from the implementation and guide future decision-making. In addition, JIA has set up a reporting mechanism for any potential cases of fraud, bribery, or sexual exploitation and abuse, both from or towards any JIA staff. At the HQ level, JIA has a particular commitment to fight against those behaviors, which is part of the Code of Conduct (CoC) training regularly organized for staff (national and international). All JIA staff and consultants must understand and sign the CoC and their contracts. Johanniter will attempt to either mediate with the parties involved, adjust programming if necessary, or engage with the relevant stakeholders on behalf of the communities. Johanniter has a strong acceptance, so it will be easy to engage with local stakeholders in advocacy when necessary. All staff, including seconded staff from the SMoH (State Ministry of Health) and Gender, will be trained in the areas of protection/PSEA and accountability that are valued in practice.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

_ Johanniter has recently updated his code of conduct, the Protection of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment Policy (PSEAH) and Safeguarding and Protecting Children and Adolescents Policy. By the end of the 2022, all Johanniter current staff will be sensitized on them through a formal training. All new staff will also be orientated on the policies and signed before started. The 3 documents are mandatory part (annexes) of any contract with external providers, HR/Hiring process and MoU with the local stakeholders. They also part of the briefing during the onboarding and mandatory. It is planned to include the thematic in each of the awareness campaign that Johanniter will be having during the project implementation. JIA will share the three polices to the beneficiaries through meetings and community sensitization activities. Beneficiaries will be informed on the reporting channels, and JIA will commit to investigate on any reported complaint related to PSEA and provide feedback as per JIA policies.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries' feedback will be collected through different channels including the help desk, community meetings, and local focal points (trusted by the community) among stakeholders at the local level. They will be compiled in a database managed by the PMEAL department (low and medium sensitive) and HOM/HQ (highly sensitive. Based on the type of complaints, it is planned that feedback should be provided (immediately, 48-72H, weekly, monthly, after investigation). PMEAL department will held meetings with program to discuss the feedback and complaint from the beneficiaries and agreed on the action plans. The action plan will be used to adjust the implementation of the project. The typology of the complaints will be used, independently, to inform our current programming (internal management, access, Information/awareness, advocacy and coordination, protection/prevention, coordination, new need)
