Project Summary

Background of the intervention:
Some 1.3 million people, including 650,000 children/youths, are now in need of some form of humanitarian assistance in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, as the security situation and living conditions continue to deteriorate. Around 450,000 of these people, half of whom are youths, are Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Youths and their families are suffering amidst and fleeing armed violence, attacks on their homes and schools, abduction, sexual violence and recruitment and use of youths into armed groups. For many over aged girls and boys, it has been 6 years since they last stepped foot in a classroom. Due to the prevailing ban on education by non-state armed groups and attacks on education, 54 percent of schools remain closed during the 2022-2023 academic year and 36% of schools in 2023-2024 academic year representing over 3650 schools which are still currently shutdown. This has forced many children and youths to join non-state arm groups (NSAG), get into drugs, violence, and juvenile delinquency with high crime wave.
The crises in the NWSW regions have led to forced rural urban migration hence a high rate of IDPs in urban areas. When these people move from their comfort zones to start a new life style in town, it becomes challenging especially when they cannot make ends meet. With the life stressors and frustrations some of them turn to drugs and substance abuse as a coping mechanism.
As a result of exposure to peer pressure, broken homes, internal displacements, most of these groups of young people cited above got into drugs and substance abuse as a means of getting rid of depression. Their situation is exacerbated by the fact that no MHPSS is available to cater for their needs. As a result of this, CES intends to strengthen Mental Health and Psychosocial response on youths into drugs and substance abuse to reduce violence and enhance protection in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon.
This project intends to provide MHPSS services to 10,000 youths across the 13 divisions of the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon.
Specific Objectives
Establishment of 13 Mental Health clinics in all the divisions of the NW and SW Regions of Cameroon
Provide case management/ MHPSS services to 10,000 identified youths involved in drugs and substance abuse
Carry out sensitization campaigns on the effects of drugs in all the 13 divisions of the NW and SW Regions

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The violence in these two regions are at its peak due to the high consumption and abuse of drugs and substances by most youths. This has led to violence in schools, communities, within peers, teachers, family members and others. To curb this, CES intends to create 13 health clinics at the divisional level. Within this facility, youths will find a safe space where they can be treated and their mental health restored. The issue of drugs and substances abuses have resulted to so many Mental Health and Psychosocial (MHPSS) issues. Case management and MHPSS services shall be provided to 10,000 youths in drugs and substance abuse. It is expected that, at the end of all the group and individual counseling sessions the clients will be mentally healthy and more focused to contribute to the development of their communities. Communications strategies and sensitization campaigns put in place shall go a long way to reduce the negative effects of drugs in the communities and enhance prevention of drugs consumption especially for most-at-risk youths. Sensitization will address the impacts caused by drugs and substance abuse and will contribute in promoting social cohesion. Two sensitization campaigns and distribution of visual materials on the effects of drugs and substance abuse in all divisions. Key messages of materials shall be developed, printed and distributed in schools.
