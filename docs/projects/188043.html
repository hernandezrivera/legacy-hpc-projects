Project Summary

This project aims at supporting food-insecure families in several areas in Rural Damascus where the severity scale is 3 and 4, and where the severity label is extreme in some of them. the project will support 7000 families (35000 individuals) in 2022 and 7000 families (35000 individuals) in 2023 with food baskets on monthly basis and will support the income-generating activities for 100 individuals in 2022 and 200 in 2023.
Income generating activities will be selected according to needs of each group and taking into consideration the previous experience. some examples of planned vocational trainings are: dairy industry, Detergents and soap making that can make benefit from oils and natural ingredients, kitchen garden, Carpentry, pluming,...and other kinds depending of surveys and market assessment. Targeted areas are: Duma, Harasta, Ghizlaniyyeh, Az-zabdani, Dimas, Ein Elfijeh, Markaz Darayya, Maliha, Kafr Batna, Arbin, and Qudsaiya.
AlTamayoz has a very good experience in GFA through a partnership with WFP since 2014 that proves its high-quality level of service provision. AlTamayoz will use the same system in Rural Damascus for registration, validation, and monitoring of activities as its system has proved its efficacy and accuracy during the last years of GFA activities implementation.
Project activities will assure that people with specific needs (PLW, children, elderly, and people with disability) will be part of the targeted population.
Project activities will be linked with other activities implemented by Altamayoz or any other NGOs working in the same area and under different sectors, for example, the health services, especially related to reproductive ones, provided by Altamayoz in partnership with UNFPA can be provided to female headed households that are targeted by food assistance, in addition, the gynecologist in RH clinic can support pregnant and lactating women targeted by CBT activities supported by WFP. Moreover, WGSS (women and girls safe space) in Harasta running by Altamayoz in partnership with UNFPA will support women under this project with GBV response and prevention activities. and as for education linkage activities, families whom their children dropped out of school, as an attempt to secure some income, will be targeted in this project and will be provided with food baskets, also, adolescent will be targeted with vocational training.

4 - Needs

According to the Global Humanitarian Overview 2022 an estimated 14 million people are still in need, representing 64,5% of Syrian population, with a severity index of5.0. Throughout 2021 the macroeconomic situation has continued to deteriorate severely,with currency depreciation, price rising and job loss. These problems affected additional segments of the populations, pushing them in humanitarian needs. In 2021, 12.4 million people were estimated to be food insecure in Syria; the highest number ever recordedBased on HNO 2022 Food/nutrition and Livelihood support have been estimated to most urgent needs suffered by people, with corresponding percentages of 86% and 85%. The increase of Food insecure population formed 20 to 87% of the total population in need in different areas in Rural Damascus. In RD 22% of HHs are FHH and 27% are dependent on food assistance. As from AlTAmayoz presence and work in Rural Damascus, the increased needs for food assistance are highly reported, there is a noticeable increase in Malnutrition levels among children and pregnant and lactating women, this was observed in many areas in targeted areas in Rural Damascus. The percentage of children suffering from sever acute malnutrition increased by about 25% by the end of 202.1 in 3 locations (Harasta, Duma, Hamoura ).Adult women and men are more severely impacted as they frequently engage in adverse coping mechanisms to ensure that boys and girls have enough food to eat (FSA and HNO 2022

5 - Implementation of activities

AlTamayoz has centers in many areas in Rural Damascus that provides different kinds of services. Most of them will be used as distribution points, and as places for vocational training to support vulnerable families. AlTamayoz is a WFP partner that provides GFA, essential data of families who benefited from this program is uploaded on the governorate website, like all other beneficiaries from other partners, so no duplication can be found in providing this kind of assistance.Selection criteria will follow FAS criteria and will consider both eligibility (socio-economic status, nutrition/protection referrals) and vulnerability criteria (household demographic status, residency status – displacements, returnees), in addition to other factors like number of family members, presence of one or more persons with disability, and if the family is a woman headed, and if it has one or more elderly person.Food items procurement will follow market assessment and will be in line with AlTamayoz procurement policy which is approved and considered by many UN agencies.Livelihood activities will include provision of vocational training and tool kits to the households (male and female) according to specific criteria that takes into consideration previous experience, availability of suitable place, degree of vulnerability, and gender. Activities will be monitored through regular field visits, surveys, and feedback mechanism that includes 2M&E officers, complain box, Face to Face complain.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of GBV when women are supported with food basket or vocational training: Likelihood: medium. impact: mediumMitigating measures: improved training on GBV first-line response, increased recruitment of female staff, Conducting awareness-raising sessions to mainstream GBV aspects and GBV prevention, Food distribution will maintain women dignity and well-being, - All staff members have to sign a code of conduct that includes GBV related aspects, staff members have to sign a code of conduct that includes PSEA related aspectsRisks associated with distribution locations: Likelihood: medium. impact: mediumMitigating measures: places are chosen to assure that affected communities are not exposed to potential hostilities and other protection risks.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: COVID-19 related restrictions and lockdowns Likelihood: High. impact: highMitigating measure: Applying the social distancing, providing distribution points with hygiene materials and supporting the staff with protection equipment (masks, hygiene hand gel, gloves,..) Risk: Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities and access project areas in a timely manner Likelihood: Low. impact: mediumMitigating measure: maintain strong communication with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals Risk: Financial risks related toincrease materials and services prices Likelihood: medium. impact: high this will be mitigated by considering this increase while preparing the proposal budget, conducting market survey and try to get fixed prices from vendors, trying to do the procurement as a chain so the financial load will be distributed through the whole project period.Risk: tension between HHs in the community due to supporting limited number of families, Likelihood: medium. impact: high. mitigating measures: explain selection criteria to the community, Meetings will be held between project management, beneficiaries and field monitors to assure that project is on the right track and to observe any deviations or risk and ways of mitigations.Risk: difficulty in delivering food baskets to Rural areas: Likelihood: medium. impact: medium. mitigating measures: obtaining the needed approvals on time, procuring basket component
