Project Summary

In response to the WASH infrastructure requirement in North-West Syria (NWS) of significant support and substantial investments and the fact that 47 percent of the population in NWS are relying on alternative to piped water and often unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs (38 percent in mid-2020), Space of Peace and its Implementing Partner (IP) WATAN are aiming to enhance the long-term WASH infrastructure and ensuring sustainability of water stations in Ariha community. To this aim the planned intervention will respond to the needs of 56,476 individuals (11,953 men, 13,250 women, 14,600 boys, and 16,673 girls) -36 percent of them are IDPs- through the following strategies:
Rehabilitate three water stations in Ariha community ensuring the functionality of the water network. Ensure the three water stations are connected to the public power grid for sustainability. Secure low-cost power source by installing solar power systems in the three stations. In addition to a standby generating power source in all stations. Gradually descending operation and maintenance phase for five months. As well as construction of sewer line to connect Sado neighborhood to sewage system.
A cost recovery system will be established in the community by supporting the O&M cost for 5 months with descending coverage (100 percent for the first month, then 80 percent, 60 percent, 40 percent, and 20 percent by the fifth month) to ensure a smooth exit strategy. afterward, Space of Peace will conduct end-line survey for the water station using the tool by WASH cluster.
The proposed intervention will include the establishment of WASH user’s committees at the community level. Space of Peace will encourage women and adolescent girls to be part of those committees. The purpose of establishing the WASH user’s committees is representing the vulnerable and marginalized groups in decision making at the community level and empowering them to raise their voices. In the other hand the WASH user’s committees will be the core for Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) modality that will be used in control the spread of COVID-19 at the community level and hygiene awareness as well.
Additionally, Space of Peace will implement a safety audit questionnaire on a quarterly basis to inform recommendations across intervention for improving the security of women and girls in the targeted community and measure general improvement conditions over time. The recommended actions based on the safety audit data should be interpreted as initial engagement points for strengthening gender-based violence (GBV) response and prevention. Space of Peace is running a GBV community centers in Ariha. The GBV center will provide protection consultation in this area.
The proposed intervention will mainstream protection principles throughout the project cycle, men, women, boys, and girls will be consulted to ensure that they are served equitably and consider the specific needs of these groups.
Female staff will be hired as community mobilizer and hygiene promoters, and protection specialist will be consulted in Gaziantep office to manage the hygiene promotion, COVID 19 prevention, and implement the safety audit of the GBV during the implementation of the proposed intervention. The female hygiene promoters will take the lead in promoting hygiene awareness among communities in target location.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The intervention have identified several protection risks (in-line with WASH cluster protection risk analysis framework) and set a strategy to mitigate them:-Unequal distribution of servicesinadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Mitigation:Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organise joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.-Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities.MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.Mitigation:Promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. -Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals.Mitigation:Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with chemicals wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. SOP will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA Policy, and will conduct induction sessions for new staff to ensure that they fully understand the content of CoC and PSEA policy. Strict disciplinary measures will be applied on any breach of the CoC. Affected population will be consulted on the convenient reporting channels to ensure effective and timely response for any compliance. SOP is a member of PSEA network (Adopting network's CoC and Policy) and regularly coordinating on the relevant issues and disseminating the central hotline numbers during the provided activities along with the organizational own reporting channels and hotlines. SOP periodically assessing the targeted communities on the suitable reporting channels for them and feed the information into threatening the existing CRM and deploying new channels when needed.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: The security situation remains stable and the center remains accessible. The acceptance of SOP and its center by the local community and the local authorities is sustained. The need for GBV programmed in the area persists. Risks 1:military escalation erupts in the targeted location mitigation: we will train our staff on emergency preparedness and GBV closure SOPs. the situation will be monitored closely with the field staff and the local authorities. Risk 2:the congeniality of the GBV is breached. mitigation: we will put in place strict disciplinary measures and train the staff on the code of conduct. Our staff have been working in this center for several years and they are aware of the sensitivity of GBV cases. - Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; -Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities . -Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards (sufficient number of water points with adequate quality etc.) and closely monitor these aspects during implementation;
