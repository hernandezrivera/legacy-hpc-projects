Project Summary

This proposed project will address emergency shelter and non-food items needs of conflict affected vulnerable populations both displaced and in secondary displacement. The project will target 5000 HHs or 30,000 individuals both IDPs and host communities, disaggregated as follows (Men 3,600, Women 7,200, Boys 8,400 and Girls 10,800) for emergency shelter and NFIs activities response in Warrap (Tonj North and East) and Lakes (Rumbek East, Rumbek North, Rumbek Center, Cueibet, Wulu, Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial) most vulnerable populations in need (after an assessment). About 56% of the target population will receive Gift In-Kind assistance which will be prepositioned from the Cluster Core Pipeline. After assessment has been done, and an analysis report submitted through the SFP to the Cluster at the National level, a pipeline request will be submitted. After an approval, items will be transported to respective CCOSS storage facilities and subsequent distributions. CCOSS will coordinate with other cluster partners in respective locations for effective response.
Despite the advent of the revitalized peace agreement and the promise of better times to come, the cumulative effects of the conflict have translated to sustained poverty and persistent humanitarian and protection needs for more than 7 million people in South Sudan. Warrap and Lakes states have continued to remain one of the most difficult locations especially for humanitarian agencies to operate. This is due to drivers and multipliers of crisis have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal violence (IVC), ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, flooding, economic instability and insecure access to food and livelihoods. Some places such as Yirol East have been categorized under Catastrophe IPC 5, making communities here more susceptible to famine.
CCOSS will also ensure that there is proper coordination especially in locations where there is more than one implementing partner. County cluster meetings will be held weekly to ensure proper coordination, to avoid double counting and as well reduce duplication of efforts. CCOSS is a key member of other clusters working within the same locality.
CCOSS will use humanitarian hubs for coordination meetings and other related humanitarian activities. CCOSS will be working with other partners who are operating in the same areas or locations on the issues affecting vulnerable populations in hard to reached areas through mobile response in collaboration with other partners on the ground. CCOSS will also work with other partners on area based approaches through open forum during cluster meetings where partners would highlight activities being implemented and locations of operation to avoid duplication activities. This would be achieved through coordination of the cluster and support of CCCM, coordinating the activities of partners to avoid overlaps of same activities in one location.
General Operation & other direct costs include costs associated with providing direct management & other support functions at the Main office in Juba. This includes management staff salaries, communication, stationary, rents etc. The Indirect project costs include bank charges, staff recruitment & servicing costs, auditing and consultancy costs.

1 - Needs

Internal displacement continued in Warrap State and Lakes State throughout 2019, primarily due to inter-communal violence, and flash floods in Warrap that led to massive displacements of more than 300,000 people. According to REACH assessment, Warrap had proportions of assessed settlements reporting a presence of internally displaced persons (IDPs) at 31%, majority of whom were primarily newer. Displacement in Warrap State and Jur River County left many IDPs without access to essential non-food-items (NFIs). Among the IDP population, women and children will remain especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. A small number of host community members do not have sufficient emergency shelter or non-food items (ES/NFIs) as they have used their already limited resources to provide for the displaced. People’s ES/NFI needs are primarily linked to conflict induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. While people outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall ES/NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacements. Displacement in Warrap State and Jur River County at the end of the first quarter left many IDPs without access to essential NFIs. Across the proportion of assessed settlements with IDPs in Warrap State, jerry cans (29%), plastic sheets (22%), and blankets (12%) were reported as the main NFIs needed by IDPs. Similarly, NFIs required by IDPs in assessed settlements in Jur River included jerry cans (21%), plastic sheets (21%), soap (14%), and blankets (14%).(REACH Assessment – Situation Overview Greater Bahr El Ghazal 2019) In Lakes, in the past year, several episodes of inter-communal violence and unreliable and or heavy rains have sustained large-scale humanitarian needs in Rumbek North, Rumbek East, Cuiebet, Awerial, Yirol East and Yirol West Counties in Lakes region. Underscoring the scale of the humanitarian crisis in the region, the latest 2019 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projected that populations in these three counties would face emergency levels of food insecurity (IPC phase 4). In August 2019, Yirol East of former Lakes State has an estimated 10,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Acute food insecurity persists, driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality. CCOSS will focus its interventions on communities in crisis and emergency in all Counties of Lakes State (Rumbek Center, Rumbek East, Rumbek North, Cueibet, Wulu, Awerial, Yirol East and Yirol West). The beneficiaries will be populations in need and will be targeted using the cluster targeting criteria: • Households with children under 5; • Pregnant or lactating women (PLW); • Unaccompanied elderly (>60 years); • Persons living with disability (PLWD); • Female headed households; • Child headed households and/or unaccompanied minors; • Chronically ill people; • Persons with no effective community linkages. CCOSS will use participatory approaches to identify the needs of women, men, boys and girls. In this proposed project, barriers to participation of women and other vulnerable groups will be discussed and taken into account in both assessment and implementation stages. Gender and age considerations will be included in needs assessments prior to implementation. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle.

2 - Activities

1) Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation and promote accountability to the affected populations. This will be through focus group discussions; setting up complaint and feedback mechanism (feedback desks in the community) prior to project implementation; formation of committees comprising of 7 women and 5 men. This will enhance the community’s dignity while increasing their ability to respond to new shock and build on existing skills. 2) Provide assistance with emergency shelter (Restricted Voucher modality) to 750 HHs (4,500 individuals) IDPs and or affected populations who have not received any form of response in Lakes. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment and a market assessment. This activity will protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks as well as enhancing access to other basic services3) Provide assistance with live saving NFIs (Restricted Voucher modality) to 750 HHs (4,500 individuals) IDPs and or affected populations who have not received any form of response in Lakes. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment and a market assessment. This activity will protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks as well as enhancing access to other basic services4) Provide assistance with emergency shelter gift in-kind (GIK) to 23,208 IDPs and or affected populations (Men: 2785, Women: 5570, Boys: 6498, Girls: 8355) who have not received any form of response in Warrap and Lakes. The emergency shelter will include plastic sheets. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment. This activity will protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks as well as enhancing access to other basic services 5) Provide assistance with live saving NFIs gift in-kind (GIK) to 23,208 IDPs and or affected populations (Men: 2785, Women: 5570, Boys: 6498, Girls: 8355) who have not received any form of response in Warrap and Lakes. The lifesaving NFIs will include blankets, mosquito nets, sleeping mats and kitchen sets. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment. This activity will contribute to better health of the IDPs, enhance nutrition, protection and better hygiene 6) Shelter upgrades gift in-kind (GIK) to 2,292 host community members (men 356, women 790 boys 466 and girls 680) whose shelter requires upgrade. The shelter upgrade materials will include plastic sheets. A need and verification assessment will be conducted first to ensure proper targeting. This activity will help improve the living conditions, safety and dignity of the host communities. 7) Monitoring of quality service provision – PDM will be conducted two weeks after the distribution is finalized. The format for the PDM will include conducting focus group discussions, random sampling for interviews. This activity will ensure fast, lifesaving response to the right people, enhances ability to use S/NFIs for supporting results bases health, protection, livelihood and hygiene needs and reducing overlap to reach the truly vulnerable. 8) Mainstream HLP AAP/CCE and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response through conducting conflict analysis, sensitizing local chiefs on women rights especially about land, avoiding manipulation of distribution of S/NFIs by remaining neutral and practicing “do-no-harm” principle. This activity will reduce harm to communities and build resilience by strengthening existing systems and community dynamics. 9) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response – this will be done through conducting gender safety audits, ensuring that distributions are done in an open space, during the day, and close to the camps. This will ensure gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating the chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project will ensure that the beneficiaries rights are legally recognized and promoted through the following actions; 1) training of local chiefs or the land rights for women and displaced persons; 2) empowering the community on land rights; 3) establishing referral pathway for GBV survivors and ensuring perpetrators are reported; 4) training staff on rights of beneficiaries including enforcing code of conduct and PSEA

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project will target displaced populations who have been affected due to conflict or natural calamities. The village committees and project staff will ensure that the most vulnerable populations are targeted and identified to benefit from the project. The affected populations will therefore access the project by directly benefiting from gift in-kind or through the restricted voucher modality.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

CCOSS will train community volunteers on shelter establishment and shelter maintenance. These group of volunteers will play a critical role in future after project close-out. CCOSS will also ensure that HLP issues are addressed through the established community systems such as local chiefs. These chiefs will be trained and equipped with information on the rights and needs of women and other at-risk groups related to protecting and securing land rights

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to the national objectives (Land Act 2009) which among other things allows women to own and inherit land and property. The inter-community violence in some cases ends up destroying property and displacement of populations. The destruction plays out in a context marked by an unclear legal framework on land, under-resourced institutions and the lack of a systematic, transparent process for land registration and allocation. This project therefore will closely work with some of the established structures at the local level such as County Land Authority or Payam Land Council (where applicable). There is need to however advocate for formulation of inclusive laws and policies on HLP to benefit all marginalized groups

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

CCOSS considers it has a moral obligation to ensure the environment is protected throughout the project cycle. Efforts will be put in place to promote activities that will have minimal or no environmental degradation. This includes ensuring all distribution sites are properly cleaned after all distributions, there less haphazard cutting of poles, and that the community is educated on how to protect and manage the environment

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CCOSS will put in place measures geared towards sustainability of this project right from its design throughout the implementation. Prior to project initiation, CCOSS will conduct meetings in the community with beneficiaries to explain to them about the project period during community engagement sessions, to adequately prepare the affected population about the project duration. Towards the end of the project, CCOSS, through community engagement activities, will emphasize the following to the community:• Explain to the community what knowledge on shelter design and establishment volunteers have and how the community members can continue benefiting from their experience after the end of the project.• The training of relief committees, the construction of Shelters, distribution of life-saving non-food items, which also mean more sustainable assistance that will stay with the families and communities, thus the program outcomes will continue to have effect beyond the implementation period.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaint /feedback mechanism will be agreed upon and established prior to commencement of project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. The community participation in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the post distribution will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of community volunteers to help in distribution among others. After the completion of distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, CCOSS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints Complaint and Feedback desks will be set in strategic places in the community. Some of these desks will be manned by some community members who will assist other community members about the issues they want to raise. Illiterate community members will be assisted with those who are able to read and right by documenting their complaints and feedback. Households with members with disabilities will be visited and their complaints and feedback received. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle.
