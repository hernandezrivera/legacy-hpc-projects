Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to meet the lifesaving food requirements of the most vulnerable, food and nutrition insecure people in South Sudan, including IDPs living in Rubkona ex-POC and Malakal POC, recent returnees from the Sudan crisis and protracted and new caseload of refugees (from Sudan crisis). A second objective is to ensure that boys and girls in the most marginalised and acute food insecure areas have access to quality education and receive safe, nutritious school meals to improve school attendance and eventually their education outcomes. Finally, WFP will provide an integrated package of health and nutrition support to malnourished children under 2, pregnant women and girls and new mothers living in these same areas where access to
WFP will provide food assistance to the most food insecure people in South Sudan. The Assistance will be either conditional (ACL) or unconditional (GFA) and can be in form of in-kind commodities, CBT or a hybrid depending on the market functionality and/or capacity support to restore and strengthen resilience capacities. At the field level, food assistance will be overseen by the WFP field offices and implemented by cooperating partners. WFP provides food assistance and common services through a comprehensive and collaborative humanitarian intervention by deploying mobile teams, supported by cooperating partners to reach populations facing the most urgent needs in the most inaccessible areas of South Sudan, subject to the availability of resources.
Complementary to food or cash-based transfers, WFP will seek to offer value-added solutions (light resilience- building activities, peace/conflict resolution training also called GFD+), in response to needs and priorities identified by the community. Through GFD+, WFP together with FAO and their cooperating partners and other stakeholders will provide unconditional opportunities, identified through a community-based, people-centric participatory approach to targeted beneficiaries. GFD+ adds value to emergency food assistance with a focus on solutions that address the root causes of food and nutrition insecurity and better equip beneficiaries to fight hunger.
The GFD+ activity type, duration, frequency, and participation will be catered to specific local sites and may range from agricultural training, or sensitisation on peaceful resolution of conflicts, nutrition education, dyke rehabilitation and communal asset management, income-generating activities which lead to women's economic empowerment. Duration will be linked to duration of GFA assistance in all locations. WFP will also implement more comprehensive resilience building activities - Asset Creation and Livelihoods (ACL) program - to support vulnerable communities and households to improve their food security, restore their livelihoods, strengthen their resilience to shocks, and become self-reliant over time, while contributing to peace through strengthened social cohesion and community structures and collaboration with local authorities and peace actors.

1. Needs

Since independence South Sudan has faced prolonged armed conflict interspersed with subnational violence, economic shocks and consecutive years of extreme flooding and localized drought. Poor transport and communication infrastructure perpetuates marginalization and inequitable access to social and economic opportunities. Humanitarian needs remain alarmingly high, far exceeding available resources. Furthermore, humanitarian access is constrained by protracted insecurity, threats and violence against humanitarian personnel and assets, operational disruptions, and extremely poor infrastructure. According to an UN Common Country Analysis (CCA) for South Sudan, approximately 74 percent of the population is under 30. Limited livelihood and employment opportunities drive high youth unemployment rates, estimated at 29 percent in 2019. This leads to disengagement from school, workplace exploitation and the recruitment of young men into armed groups, undermining social cohesion and prospects for peace. Wasting prevalence remains concerning at >10 percent, while stunting is high at 31 percent. This is driven by deteriorating food insecurity, poor diet, disease and limited access to health and nutrition services. Suboptimal maternal, infant and young feeding practices and harmful sociocultural practices are also contributing factors. According to the recently released IPC results, the severe food security crisis in South Sudan is expected to continue with 7.7 million people (57 percent of the population) projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity during the height of the 2025 lean season (IPC), compared to 7.1 million people during the same period in 2024. 44 out of 79 counties (56 percent) and 252,000 returnees are expected to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity during the lean season (April to July). In 2025, due to reduced funding, WFP plans to respond to these needs through the provision of relief assistance to close to 2.8 million people (352K under ACL, 202k IDPs, 2,044mil LSR and 228k Sudan crisis returnees) living in counties classified as IPC Phase 4 during the April to July lean season (This excludes 448k refugees who will also receive GFD support). This includes 140,320 MT of food and USD 50 million in cash-based transfers. In counties with people facing IPC Phase 5 Catastrophic levels of food insecurity, WFP will provide 70 percent rations to prevent famine, while all other targeted locations will receive 50 percent rations. This is to ensure the maximum reach of WFP’s reduced resources in the most affected and vulnerable communities. In addition to GFD, vulnerable PBW and children <2 years of age in these counties will also receive specialized nutritious foods

2. Project activities

WFP response will provide this lifesaving food and nutrition assistance by prioritizing communities facing the highest levels of food insecurity and malnutrition using three modalities: in-kind food, cash-based transfers (CBT) and a hybrid food basket depending on the local context and market functionality. This approach is aligned to the humanitarian-development-peace nexus where WFP promotes a “do no harm” approach. WFP promotes livelihoods following community identified priorities while meeting immediate needs of the vulnerable communities. Household targeting is based on vulnerability criteria.These vulnerability criteria are agreed upon by the participating communities and they help identify the most vulnerable households for food assistance. Geographic targeting at county and payam level is guided by Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) results, gender and protection analysis, and other assessments. Due to resource constraints, assistance is only provided to households facing the most severe levels of food insecurity. WFP plans to target 147,377 school children in these same areas of high acute food insecurity with safe and nutritious school meals to support learning outcomes, keep girls in schools and reduce child and forced marriage and early pregnancy. In 2025, WFP plans to reach 1,229,407 individuals with 15,637 mt of food under its malnutrition prevention activities. Under this activity, WFP will provide an integrated package of health and nutrition support to malnourished children under 2, pregnant women and girls and new mothers. These activities will include blanket supplementary feeding, SBC, counsellingUnder the ACL program, WFP will support 60,000 vulnerable households to strengthen their agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods to enhance resilience capacities and reduce humanitarian needs in the face of future shocks. ACL will be present in 24 counties with repeated occurrence of IPC 4. In beneficiary targeting, WFP will prioritize communities and households previously assisted by GFD to support a transition from humanitarian assistance to resilience support. Other vulnerable households may be targeted so that that resilience building achieves scale at targeted communities to make sustained impact. To facilitate crop and livestock production, WFP will support targeted communities to restore ecosystem services (e.g., soil nutrients, vegetation cover and water retention) through nature-based solutions and improve access to water through the construction and rehabilitation of small-scale infrastructure with low-environmental risk (e.g., shallow wells). WFP will coordinate with the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security (MAFS) and FAO so that targeted households will access to agricultural inputs (climate-resilience crops and varieties) and advisory services. Market-based approaches (e.g., vouchers redeemed at seed fairs and on the local market) will be pursued to develop the private sector, where appropriate. WFP will support vegetable production, especially amongst women for improved household diets and income generation. In agro-pastoral and pastoral areas, WFP will coordinate with the Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries, FAO and other partners so that targeted households will access animal health services and improve livelihoods including through increased milk production. For increased access to and protection of villages, production areas, social services and markets, WFP will support targeted communities in the construction and rehabilitation of access roads, drainage channels and dykes. Moreover, households will be supported with knowledge and skills development in conflict resolution, nutrition, financial management, and income-generating activities. WFP will provide food or cash transfers to targeted households to avoid food gaps during the peak of the lean season (between 3 to 6 months).
