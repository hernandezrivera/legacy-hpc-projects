Project Summary

With the aim of reducing the barriers that persons with disabilities face and promoting their inclusion and equal participation in society, also identifying and prioritizing those who are most at risk of being left behind applying the DGA approach, the INGO intervenes at different levels through the operation of the IASC Guidelines on the Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action:
- at an individual level, the INGO focuses on providing tailored and personalized support through an inclusive case management (ICM) service with the aim to identifying people with disabilities more at risk of exclusion, increasing their level of access to services through a referral mechanism, contribute to their empowerment and protection. ICM starts with the holistic assessment of the person’s situation, risks and needs and with referral to services (including lifesaving and life-sustaining humanitarian services) and continue with referral follow up and, when needed, with personalized accompaniment to remove the barriers met during the process of accessing the service. For most marginalized beneficiaries, ICM also aims to increase empowerment, self-determination, participation and capacities for self-protection to reach an increased protection and social inclusion in the person’s household and community. This include the development of a Personalized Action Plan where the case worker and the supported person co-define concrete actions to achieve a specific final goal to be achieved for the end the personalized intervention and a number of social work sessions with the person and her/his family to increase awareness and contribute to their empowerment. It may also include provision of adaptations to home environment and tools of daily living to improve the person with disability capacity to live independently.
- at community level, through the active participation and involvement of persons with disabilities in what is called the "Ambassadors Program" with the aim of increasing the representation of people with disabilities in society, promoting their rights conducting awareness sessions, increasing the knowledge towards disability, then reducing negative attitudes, beliefs and stigmatization;
- at the services level, efforts are made to improve the inclusiveness of services through increase awareness and knowledge building of operational and managerial staff on disability-related issues, strength the capacity building of the staff on how to support and include persons with disabilities in their services, improve the accessibility of services provided, with a specific focus on protection, health and basic needs services;
- at the cluster level, INGO supports the rights of people with disabilities and works for their inclusion and equal participation in the humanitarian response. The INGO also co-lead the ITWG within the Protection Cluster.
The MEAL framework in HI's humanitarian intervention in Syria is integral for effective project planning and implementation. Operating under HI's guidelines, the MEAL department ensures oversight at mission and hub levels. At the Whole of Syria level, a dedicated manager coordinates M&E activities with technical units and staff. MEAL hubs collaborate to design performance measurement frameworks. Field agents use diverse methods for monitoring, including observations and stakeholder interviews. Data collection includes quantitative and qualitative approaches, with FIE sheets for health indicators. Feedback mechanisms ensure continuous assessment of service quality. Accountability is maintained through inclusive feedback and response mechanisms. Confidentiality and data protection are prioritized, with individual case files securely stored and restricted access, safeguarding beneficiary privacy.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs:In Syria, 16.7 million individuals requiring humanitarian assistance. Among these, 24% of individuals are persons with disabilities. HNO report highlights the need for protection services for 15.3 million people, with conflict-affected populations facing challenges in accessing humanitarian aid and services. PwDs face unique challenges, intensified by displacement, stigma, and social isolation. Accessibility issues, negative attitudes, and protection risks underscore the imperative for targeted support, including improved facilities, attitudinal change, and inclusive opportunities for persons with disabilities in the communityImplementation of activities:The INGO focuses on enhancing the inclusion of persons with disabilities in humanitarian response using a twin-track approach that involves providing technical support to services and personalized assistance to vulnerable individuals. On one hand, assessments and removal of barriers who prevent the access to services and capacity building for humanitarian actors on Disability, Gender, and Age approach. On one other hand, provision of personalized social support IAR (Identification-Assessment-Referral) or Case Management services to high-risk individuals. Protection Risk Analysis:INGO employs a low-visibility strategy to safeguard staff identities. In the event of an attack, the Duty of Care policy is activated, providing staff with PSS to prevent burnout. Despite no heightened risk in activities, the organization applies a comprehensive PSEAH policy for staff and partners, ensuring accountability through investigation and awareness campaigns. Risks related to PwDs are managed through staff training, secure facility closure, partner assessments, and ongoing feedback collection. Cholera prevention measures, established during COVID-19, remain in place.PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy:The INGO is committed to combating sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Robust policies and a Code of Conduct are in place, with mandatory and refresher training. Recruitment includes background checks, and PSEAH policies apply to staff, partners, and accompanying individuals. Information sessions for beneficiaries, complaint mechanisms, and gender balance in staff address cultural sensitivity. Any partner involved in abuse faces case follow-up and termination unless a drastic behavioral change is ensured.Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures:INGO identifies and mitigates risks effectively: strategic access planning counters border closures, positive relations address acceptance issues, and community engagement surveys measure satisfaction. Funding risks are minimized through donor communication and adaptive program planning. Logistic challenges are handled by assessing partners, coordinating with INGOs, and maintaining contingency stock. Cash-related mismanagement is prevented through individual beneficiary monitoring and adherence to FRM and anti-fraud policies.
