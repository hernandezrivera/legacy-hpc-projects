Project Summary

Somalia is faced with limited development and recurring climatic shocks giving rise to high level of people in need, insecurity or conflict affect access by humanitarian actors. Nearly seven in ten Somalis live on less than USD 1.90 per day indicative of poverty rate. Poverty is serious for households in rural settlement areas and internally displaced persons in camps (blogs.worldbank.org). It is estimated that a total of 3.5 million out of the 15.8m people face acute food insecurity with IPC 3 and above until December 2021. Approximately 1.2 million children under 5 are likely to be acute and a quarter million severely malnourished (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021)
WADA would facilitate access to food for people facing acute food insecurity and protect livelihoods to communities in rural areas affected by droughts causing water scarcity, crop failure, low households food consumptions, malnutrition, diseases and death. The result of locust swarms and effects on vegetation and pasture for livestock and crops have resulted low crop yield and poor animal body mass. Drought and insecurity caused displacement and migration of people making up the districts targeted. People have nothing to depend on in terms of nutrition and building resilience to climatic changes and conflicts.
The project locations both urban and rural livelihood areas are highly affected but at different level. There are so many poor households without immediate support, and their food insecurity situation as currently faced and is highly probable to continue into the future according to expert estimates . FAO-WFP early warning report (July, 2021) recommends priorities for emergency response and short-term protective interventions in the regions and specified districts for interventions.
The district of Buhodle urban and rural livelihood areas are mostly affected food insecurity situation. Currently the drought has become more severe because of the dwindling economic capacity of the local communities, leading to increased food scarcity, augmented morbidity and high levels of acute malnutrition (rapid assessment by project team). The district impassable and poor road access is adding to the hardships. Buhodle was among the areas that was severely affected by droughts since 2017. There is less humanitarian attention because of existing conflicts and insecurity. Chronic pest infestations and the low capacity of farmers to invest in farm inputs has prevented more substantial improvement in terms of annual farming yields to below average compared to 2020 in the region of Togdeer Buhodle included (FEWSNET update Sept, 2021). There are almost 20 IDP sites in Buhodle District- and two camps at Shangale and Balidocol.
There is large IDPs and returnees in Kismayo that keeps increasing since last two years. Currently the drought has become more severe because of the dwindling economic capacity of the local community households.
The project shall be targeting poor returnees, IDPs and residents for UCT and conditional-restoring communal water sources and building community resilience and durable assets. Vulnerable Persons like the disables, the elderly, child-headed households, the sick, and minority groups-ethnics, would beneficiaries. 950 HHs or 5700 beneficiaries would benefit from the project with a 60USD monthly transfers for 4 month period per household with reference to WFP and FEWS NET 2021guidelines. Besides Cash transfer provides greater choice to beneficiaries and foster dignity in receipt of assistance (ec.europe.eu).
Conditional cash transfers activities will engage household members to de-silt water catchment/source resource facilities (6 dams and water pans) from silts and sediments filling up and bush-clearing on trunk and feeder roads leading to Buhodle and kismayo town. Four hundred Households are expected to benefit from both the conditional and unconditional cash transfers (200 hundred water source de-silting with their home tool and 100 bush clearing).

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Beneficiaries are in hardship and poverty. They are weak, displaced and would need immediate cash to buy not only food but other needs for the households and the children or anything else that they need on a regular basis. Cash transfer promotes empowerment, and responsibility and self esteem to beneficiaries. It helps beneficiaries integrate with the local economy. Research shows Cash transfers helps Households engage in small business for income. There is less corruption with cash transfers where direct transfers to beneficiary mobile numbers. Besides cash transfers, provides a high level of transparency, ensures accountability that demonstrate the donors that funds is sent to the people who need it. Cash transfers allow people to spend in their communities. The small businesses, which could lead to more jobs, encourages entrepreneurship and jobs availability in the target locations. cash transfer, also allows people to invest in their own work and supplement their incomes.The project will use participative approach when selecting beneficiaries to avoid social jealousy. We shall coordinated with other agencies in the area on the transfer rate . The target beneficiaries, neighborhood leaders and government will have full knowledge of the principle and agree to selection criteria, villages and water facilities to be improved with CfW activities. All the opinion makers will agree and have agreed with WADA on the modalities of selection and payment methods. WADA has worked in the area six years ago building some of the water catchment facilities and now need serious de-silting of gravel, sands and tree debris. The cash program is an immediate recourse to the dire need newly displaced households joining the villages and the funds would of help for them to integrate into communities they are joining. The local market has enough cereals and vegetable oils, fruits and nuts in good supply because of its location and imported products in the shelves of many stores. According to beneficiaries, there are staple foods stocks in local markets and only that they have no income, assets to trade with and saving to access some of the foods they would wish to have for their families. Cash transfers proposed is a strategy that is totally favorable with the letter and spirit of the cashlearning.org guide.Beneficiaries have already expressed their preference for cash to enable them purchase the specific food items and other needs especially that their needs are many at this point in time while their nutritional options are limited. Women and vulnerable persons have expressed their preference in this regard. There are no immediate concern about the risk of cash not being spent on the intended purpose by those in need of it.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

A rapid market and need assessments survey would be conducted to assess, as well as interviews and focus groups with communities, local government, and NGOs on the suitability of the cash transfer over in kind. Our own assessments have already established that food items are in local markets are available. Moreover, the project team, would carry out, quarterly market surveys to know food availability and price trends and further carry out price monitoring of key goods two weeks before cash redemption and two week after cash redemption on any arbitrary price increase noted from the vendors and beneficiaries. We shall, work with government and local business communities to determine how they could help minimize fluctuations and ensure there is consistent supply of affordable food in the area. Besides we shall engage beneficiaries to practice saving and invest in income generating activities.The project location markets are large and mature enough to supply the needed food rations without the sensitivity of demand pressure on any one item. Further, we have spoken with the business merchants who have reported low market ‘no money this days’. They have welcomed the idea of cash grants to less fortunate and jobless household youth. They have dispelled any fear of price increase on essential goods. They have said their prices are not increased arbitrary and have never changed as a result of improved demand from their customers noting that honesty, just and fair price of all essential goods is what the faiths demands them to do in both boom and bust cycle.
