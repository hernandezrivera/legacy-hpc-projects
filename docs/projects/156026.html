Project Summary

UOSSM is proposing to provide women and girls safe space project in the community, culturally-appropriate and tailored to the context aiming to reduce risks and prevent further harm during acute emergency by providing an area where women and girls can:
• Socialize and re-build their social networks
• Receive social support
• Acquire contextually relevant skills
• Access safe and non-stigmatizing GBV response services
• have a safe entry point for services and a place to access information.

Needs

According to HNO 2019 In Syria which UOSSM participated in Kafrnobol by holding FGDs with adults and adolescent boys and girls and KIIs , GBV sub cluster analyzed these collected data, and the results were part of the HNO in Syria .Denial of resources experienced by women and girls Assessments have indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. The most affected population with the mentioned issues were women, adolescent girls, girls, widows, divorcees, persons with disabilities, elderly women. The length of the crisis and deep-rooted social norms, in conjunction with the mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalizing this violence, resulting in the detrimental psycho social well being and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, at distribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is a concern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psycho social stress and limits their movements. Additionally, various means of technology—e.g. texting and social media—are used to sexually harass and facilitate early/forced marriages. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately curtails their rights, with widows who have experienced divorce and widowhood and adolescent girls disproportionately impacted. The shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence means that survivors often do not talk about violence when it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family. Girls are reportedly being married younger, with early/forced marriage- sometimes involving girls of 10 or 11 years old - framed as a protective coping strategy. The socio-economic situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty are ultimately leading to women and girls to be at higher risks of all forms of violence including sexual exploitation and abuse. Adolescent girls and young girls can experience sexual harassment and violence in situations such as when working on the land/ farm, in shops, and in instances when affected by child recruitment. Household level gender roles continue to change with negative implications within families and communities. Although women have more responsibility, this does not infer authority. Women have taken on additional roles traditionally associated with men, including as heads of household and principal breadwinners for their families. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families as they juggle commitments within the home and workplace. Consequently, they are exposed to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at their workplace. Where GBV services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with women and girls’ safe spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training).

Implementation of activities

In order to provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services,including case management,psycho social support and reinforce referral pathway UOSSM will establish WGSS in Kafrnbol and two mobile teams(one of male and one of female) in order to reach most vulnerable categories in the remote areas.UOSSM will provide the following activities:Provide case management, focused psychosocial (PM+) and referral services to GBV survivors through two mobile protection teams and one WGSS,the design of the safe space, the activities and services it offers, and the discussions it organizes should prioritize the safety and confidentiality of women and girls accessing the center.Any case files,documentation of services, and client data kept at the center should be properly secured. All the services will be survivors cantered and in line with the case management SOPs developed by the GBV sub cluster. the case workers will utilize the localized service mapping to provide safe referral pathways for survivors when needed. individual counselling including PM+ will be provided to survivors by trained and supervised case workers.Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV,with a particular focus on most at risks groups (for ex.adolescent girls and female headed households,and divorced and widowed women and girls)The center will be women and girl-led and offer an inclusive and empowering environment for them. Women and girls will be included in project planning, implementation,and monitoring and evaluation of the space to ensure relevance and ownership.While the centre will be a space meant for and run by women and girls, its sustainability will require the input and support of many stakeholders. Husbands, parents,and community leaders have a lot of influence over the ability of women and girls to participate in programmes. It is, therefore, essential to understand the perspectives of these individuals while setting up a safe space, and to mobilize community support for the WGSS so that women and girls are able to safely participate in all activities by utilizing outreach activities to engage men and adolescent boys in GBV awareness raising and community mobilization. The project will maintain balance between structured activities, services, and times to socialize. Activities and approaches will be culturally and age appropriate as the needs and interests are bound to be different.The project will take into consideration, the special needs of women and girls living with disabilities.Recreational activities will be implemented in the center including Sewing, make-up, hair dressing, computer literacy, language, crochet,painting, drawing led by women and girls with resources procured by the UOSSM.Life skills sessions for women and adolescent girls customized and age-appropriate will be held in the center.Day care services will be provided in the center to increase access to center for women with young children.Awareness raising activities will be held with consultation with women and adolescent girls utilizing the GBV awareness tool kits developed by the GBV sub cluster.two mobile teams one female and one male will implement outreach activities where the female team will conduct home visits to offer access women and girls whose movement may be restricted in some way. Meanwhile,the male team will conduct outreach activities aiming to engagement with community structures,religious and community leaders to prevent GBV by dissemination adapted GBV messages included in the GBV awareness tool kits developed by the GBV sub cluster and to inform community members about activities and services.The M&E Plan aims to achieve the following objectives:•Monitor the changes in the numbers of Beneficiaries of the project per month. •Monitor and assess the quality of services provided. •Monitor various programs efforts, to the extent possible, link such program efforts to outcomes, and thus assess the effects and impacts of program interventions.

Service mapping including those that can provide survivor-centered services and with coordination with GBV sub cluster and other GBV actors.2. Ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.Activity: Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activitiesprotection risks: 1. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging 2. Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment 3. Women and adolescent girls with child-bearing responsibilities might have difficulty accessing services in the WGSSLikelihood: mediumMitigation measures:1. Community engagement from the onset of the project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders 2. Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities 5. Equip all WGSS with child-care services 6. Provide transportation support for persons with disability

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks that we may face in the projects:- The threat of poor security situation and in the areas. Assumption: The team will receive training in PFA, staff care.UOSSM will mitigate the risk by daily communication with the security and safety team, following security advisories, recommendations, updating contingency plans, provide PFA to the new IDPs and activate the mobile teams. Conduct consultations with women and adolescent girls on the appropriate timing for the activities and inform them with any change in the implementation approach resulted from the security situation. Exit strategy will be put in place with communication with community members and other service providers with revision regularly based on the changes happening in the field -The risk of disruption of activities due to change of dominant forces on the groundThe activities of the project will be locally closed at the level of the communities benefiting from the services of the project and inform (local councils, .) about the time of closing the project.Activation of safe evacuation and destruction of documents and posters.Activation the Exit strategy when needed with close consultation with the beneficiaries.the lessons learned and the challenges that have been faced with the implementation of the project will be shared with the concerned managements for future use in designing of similar projects.
