Project Summary

Purpose:
The project aims at strengthening SBGV prevention and risk mitigation interventions, increasing the accessibility of integrated services, and improving the quality of SGBV response services to Rohingya refugees, minority Hindu refugees, and the affected host community populations in Cox’s Bazar district.
Interventions & Target:
The interventions focus on increasing meaningful participation and community engagement through sustainable, innovative and adapted approaches, while engaging with key government authorities, SGBV and non-SGBV actors in preventive, risk mitigation and response measures.
• Prevention will be strengthened through proven and adapted community activism methodologies (incl. SASA! Together), sensitization and awareness-raising on SGBV, as well as life-saving information on available services. The engagement of men and boys (as informed through meaningful consultations with women and girls) along with the empowerment of women and girls, will be prioritized.
• Meaningful participation, community consultation and engagement will be pursued through the reciprocal research methodology developed by the University of New South Wales (Global Compact for Refugees – Refugee Women and Girls’ Project) in participatory assessments at all stages of SGBV programming (design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). Protection as well as age, gender and diversity mainstreaming (AGDM) efforts will be further enhanced.
• Pursuant to UNHCR Bangladesh’s Action Plan on SGBV Mainstreaming 2020, the revised IASC Guidelines on Integrating GBV Interventions in Humanitarian Action will be progressively mainstreamed across all sectors within UNHCR and its partners’ programmes. This would be in complementarity to the GBV SS’ efforts.
• A robust component of the intervention will address the need for building capacities through on-the-job coaching, mentoring, needs-based/prioritized training of trainers and step-down training of UNHCR staff, partner organizations, government authorities and the affected communities on SGBV prevention, risk mitigation and response. This is aimed at improving the quality of case management, timely and effective referrals to appropriate, holistic and integrated services (including medical, psychosocial support, legal and security, livelihoods, sexual and reproductive health, and other services), as needed and wished - in accordance with the survivor-centred approach and the best interests of the child (in cases involving child survivors of SGBV).
• With the bulk of existing services focusing on women and girls, the project will also include the enhancement and/or expansion of entry points where male survivors, LGBTI survivors of SGBV, as well other diverse and/or marginalized groups (including persons with disability), will find meaningful, equal and impartial access (including differentiated access as needed) to specialized support and services in a safe, confidential and dignified manner.
• Integrated services and assistance provided within the existing 38 community and counselling facilities managed by UNHCR’s partners - including 15 Women & Girls-Friendly Spaces - will be strengthened based on monitoring and evaluation reports, the feedback received from clients, and the results of the GBV Sub-Sector’s safety audits. Community-based safe shelters established in complementarity to a partner-run safe shelter, will be accessible to SGBV survivors in need of refuge.
• Ensure the integration of SGBV interventions in emergency preparedness & response plans, and disaster risk reduction initiatives
The modalities of implementation will be directly through UNHCR SGBV specialized staff, qualified and skilled partners, and through community-led/supported groups whose capacities have been built over the past years and will be progressively enhanced.

Objectives

The objectives of this project commensurate with several of the Protection Sector’s and GBV Sub-Sector’s objectives. It predominantly targets Protection Objectives 2, 5 and 6, but is not limited as such. Protection Objective 2: Promote a community-based approach to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the ultimate aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthen the resilience of affected communities, and place communities at the center of the response in line with AAP principles. Protection Objective 5: Improve access to quality survivor-centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowering women, girls and survivors of GBV in Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host community sites. Protection Objective 6: Promote an integrated and multi-sectoral Protection, Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) approach

Needs

52% of the total refugee population are women and girls (UNHCR Operational Dashboard: Indicators Monitoring, Aug. 2019). Many were exposed to widespread and severe forms of conflict related sexual violence (CRSV) in Myanmar before & during flight. Evidence points to men and boys experiencing CRSV (incl. male genital violence and forced witnessing) but there is dearth of entry points and services specially designed to support them (‘It’s Happening To Our Men As Well – Sexual Violence Against Rohingya Men & Boys’ by Women Refugee Commission, Nov. 2018). This report highlights similar gaping issues for persons of diverse sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, or sex characteristics. Furthermore, a small but often overlooked Hindu minority community congregated in Hindupara, Cox’s Bazar, which also fled the violence, receive limited SGBV programming. According to the GBV Information Management System reports, intimate partner violence is the dominant form of SGBV reported in Cox’s Bazar. Domestic violence is seen as the norm (ACF, Save the Children & Oxfam in, “Refugee Response Gender Analysis - Recognizing and Responding to Gender Inequalities” - Aug. 2018). According to this analysis, a significant number of participants reported an increase in domestic violence, both in the host and Rohingya communities. Unemployment amongst men and financial pressures while displaced, were key factors. Participatory assessments conducted by UNHCR and its partners in 2017, 2018 and 2019 confirm that SGBV continues to affect Rohingya refugees pre and post-2017 influx. It remains one of the main protection risks within the community. Many survivors of trauma experienced prior to and during flight, continue to suffer in silence, while the cycle of violence continues in Bangladesh. Stigma, which tend to be attached to female victims, make it very hard for survivors to speak out. Multiple reports confirm the inherent power imbalance within the family, and the perpetuation of violence against women and girls at home. This imbalance of power and acceptance stem from certain harmful cultural and religious practices observed by the community even whilst in Myanmar. According to UNHCR’s Population Statistics (Oct. 2019), 3% of refugees are elderly; of the 31% of families identified with at least one protection vulnerability, 4% have a disability and 2% are caregivers of elderly persons. While access to services have improved through outreach, barriers to accessing services for persons with specific needs – especially women – prevail due to site topography, lack of appropriate communication modalities and interventions, which worsen during monsoon and cyclone seasons. These reflect the compounded risks faced by certain segments of the refugee community. The national Violence Against Women Survey conducted by the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics in 2015 reveals an alarmingly high incidence of SGBV amongst the host community. Structured gender inequality and deep-rooted patriarchal socio-cultural norms shape perceptions and behaviour that condone and accept acts of violence against women. UNHCR commissioned SRHR research studies in 2018 & 2019 reveal that persons with diverse sexual orientation and gender identity (LGBTI) from the affected communities in Cox’s Bazar district, are vulnerable to SGBV and have limited access to specialized services. Despite national legislation protecting women and girls, SGBV survivors face severe challenges in accessing the justice system. Refugees faced additional hurdles when wanting to lodge a legal complaint/ initiate police investigation as they need prior authorization from camp authorities. Gender inequality perpetuated by harmful socio-cultural norms and beliefs is prevalent amongst law enforcement agencies and the judicial system - all of which impede access to justice and perpetuate impunity. Given its immediate and potentially life-threatening consequences, addressing SGBV is a priority.

Activities or outputs

• Support ethical & sustainable community mobilization initiatives and community-based mechanisms which contribute to SGBV prevention, risk mitigation and response: Adapt and progressively roll out “SASA! Together” to address the core driver of violence against women -imbalance of power between women and men, girls and boys; Support specialized community outreach teams trained on SGBV to take proactive steps to lead or participate in SGBV prevention activities while supporting safe referrals and providing accompanying support to SGBV survivors to specialized response services; Expand the number of community-based safe shelters to provide immediate support to persons at risk of SGBV or with physical protection needs as they are often the first line of responders; Employ the reciprocal research methodology developed by UNSW to facilitate meaningful participation by women, girls, men, boys, and other diverse groups; Promote men & boys engagement (as informed by women and girls) in order to enhance gender equality, challenge gender stereotypes and reduce the risk of SGBV; Promote women’s engagement in camp/community-leadership structures by supporting women leadership, empowerment and mentorship; Support informal learning for women and the education for girls as an essential protection response; Develop access for women, particularly women-headed households, to vocational skills training, and livelihood opportunities by linking with key state actors such as the Ministry of Women and Children’s Affairs and promote social cohesion through marketing between host community and refugees. • Mainstream SGBV across all sectors: Progressively integrate the revised IASC Guidelines on Integrating GBV across all sectors pursuant to UNHCR Bangladesh’s Action Plan on SGBV Mainstreaming 2020; Expand inter-sectoral linkages such as the ongoing production, procurement and distribution of hygiene kits to women of reproductive age & the continued support of local production by refugee women under a partner-run vocational skills/livelihood programme.• Improve the quality of case management & promote holistic integrated services: Conduct regular multi-functional safety audits, participatory assessments, and satisfaction surveys to monitor the quality of services; Build capacities through on-the-job coaching, mentoring, needs-based/prioritized training of trainers and step-down training of UNHCR staff, partner organizations, government authorities and the affected communities on SGBV prevention, risk mitigation and response including PSEA; Strengthen the quality of case management, timely and effective referrals to appropriate, holistic and integrated (incl. multi-sectoral) services in accordance with the survivor-centred approach and the best interests of the child; Expand entry points including differentiated access and specialized services to ensure equal and impartial access by men, boys, and other marginalized groups including LGBTI, survival sex workers, and persons with disability. • Step-up the services provided in 15 Women Friendly Spaces (WFS), 23 other integrated, multi-purpose and/or counselling centres in SGBV related services, partner-run and community-based safe shelters, shelter home services for women and girls who are at higher risk of HIV/AIDS and STI, as well as the establishment of a new WFS in Nayapara Registered Camp to reduce the service gap.• Mainstream SBGV in emergency preparedness & response, and disaster risk reduction: Provide specialized capacity building for partners and affected populations, contributory stocks of 229,410 dignity kits, targeted awareness raising sessions, safety audits for disaster prone areas, enhanced case management and follow up for SGBV survivors living in zones at heightened risk.• Support the coordination mechanisms including the GBV Sub-Sector on the standardization of approaches, preventive and qualitative responses, and information management.
