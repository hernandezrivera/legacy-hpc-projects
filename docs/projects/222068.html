Project Summary

South Sudan, Unity State, Mayendit county continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions attributed to public health gaps, climatic shocks, Sudan crisis, economic crisis, endemic violence, conflicts that have pessimistic impact 2 people’s livelihoods hampering access to education & wash & health services thus negatively impacting the nutrition situation with more children acutely malnourished. HNRP 2024 estimated 9 million people including refugees in critical need with 7.1 M requiring food assistance while 1.6 children are at risk of acute malnutrition.
According to September 2025 – July 2025 IPC, projections 6.1 M & 1.71M pple will likely experience phase 3 & 4 respectively with 31,000 returnees at IPC 5 catastrophe btwn December 2024 – March 2025.With the arrival of lean season April – July 2025 food security situation is expected to deteriorate with 7.69 M people at IP3, 2.53 M at IPC 4 & 63,000 at IPC 5 while 44 counties & 252,000 returnees likely to face phase 4.July 2024- June 2025 notes nutrition situation deterioration with 2.1 M children 6-59 months (SAM;650,00 & MAM 1.43 M) & 1.11 million PBW suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition with Unity among the 5 states with 67% of the burden reflecting to a 20.4 increase of acute malnutrition compared to 1.66 M in 2023 attributed to high disease prevalence, poor IYCF practices, health gaps, food insecurity & WASH gaps. December 2024-March 2025 cites onset of food security deterioration with its 64.7% population(unity) at IPC 3 which 204,000 people at Emergency IPC 4.It worsens in April -July 2025 with 71.6 % of state population at Crisis IPC 3, which 255,000 people are likely to be in Emergency IPC 4 with 5 of its counties including Mayardit at IPC 4 acute food insecurity.
Through 2025 HRP, UNIDOR seeks to ensure equitable access & utilization of timely, qualitative & quantitative lifesaving services to children 6-59 months, PBW & people with disabilities through provision of integrated nutrition services reaching at least 80% of SAM, 75% MAM & 60% PBW in mayendit county targeting total of 68,780 beneficiaries.Through management & treatment of 6,369 & 7,797 boys & girls with SAM &MAM respectively & 6,727 PBW through OPT/TSFP static sites & outreaches per the CO2
In line with the CO1, 20,430 will be reached with MIYCN counselling (13,620 caregivers of children 0-23 months & 6,810 PLW).32,348 & 28,943 boys & girls will receive Vitamin A & Deworming respectively while 14,539 adolescent girls targeted for folic supplementation. Capacity strengthening to 4,865 PBW/caregivers, 401 CNVs/BHWs & 81 HWs on Family MUAC & MIYCN respectively with 235 MTGs formed in prevention of malnutrition with an estimated budget of $2,840,00. UNIDOR will enhance the component of surveillance through continuous program monitoring that will include Mass MUAC screening, SMART surveys & supporting in FNSMS rounds per CO3.
Also, UNIDOR will strengthen integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening, EPI &GMP distribution of LLITN), WASH (provision of safe water, soap, education on hygiene & sanitation), FSL on kitchen gardening & cooking demonstration & ECD through Education,Protection (referral pathways & GBV safety audits & feedback mechanism establishment of CFM, complaint desks, & community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback per CO4.
Proper & timely coordination & collaboration with MOH/SMOH/CHD will be obliged.UNIDOR will also ensure direct provision of services revitalizing the broken referral pathways either to local structures or to humanitarian partners. In addition, UNIDOR has built strong linkages with SMOH & CHD providing capacity building, Job training/mentorship &effective collaboration at all levels to improve quality programing & strengthen the government capacity for transaction purposes UNIDOR will also work with government actors i.e. the local authorities, CHD,commissioner, Governor ..etc.. to carb Aid diversion

1. Needs

Mayendit County continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions attributed to public health hitches with high disease burden, climatic shocks, economic crisis, endemic violence, conflicts, access constraints & operational interference that have pessimistic impact to people’s livelihoods hampering access to education & water, sanitation & hygiene & health services thus negatively impacting the nutrition situation with more children acutely malnourished. The situation deteriorated further in the lean season April – July 2024 with heavy rainfalls and controlled water release from Lake Victoria resulting in flooding with 3,659 HH in 158 villages affected by floods. Influx of returnees from Sudan after the war in April 2023 with an estimated 14 % returnees and 2 % refugee of the total population further caused distress to the already affected households (August 2024 IRNA). REACH/IRC March 2023 SMART survey notes GAM of 20.5% (16.8-24.7, 95% CI) above the WHO emergency threshold while 2024 PIN cites GAM prevalence of 21.2% (SAM 4.4%,MAM,16.8% ,PLW-AMN19.6) and at severity 4According to the IPC AMN October 2024- March 2025, Mayendit is among the 7 counties of Unity state at IPC AMN Phase 4 with acute malnutrition deterioration projected in the period January-March 2025.April – July 2025 IPC projection analysis affirms, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate in unity with 803,000 people likely to be in crisis IPC 3 or worse food insecurity, & 255,00 people likely to be emergency IPC 4 with Mayardit being among the 5 counties classified at emergency phase IPC4. 80% of its population is currently in need of humanitarian assistance with a 65% and 95% PHNHA in the 1st and second projection respectively with the most at-risk being women, girls, children & the elderly faced with large food gaps and likely starvation if humanitarian food assistance is not provided. The floods have affected the crop harvest, and subsequently resulted in a high cereal deficit for the 2023/2024 consumption year with the 2023 production able to meet 72.3 of the country cereal need. In addition, the current nutrition situation has multi-dimensional aggravating factors, such as severe food insecurity, in adequate child feeding practices, high child morbidity, low health services, the occurrence of concurrent malnutrition aggravating factors as increasing food insecurity, poor access to WASH & health services & climatic shocks such as recurrent flooding/dry spells are likely to impact negatively nutrition outcomes. Its worthy noting that, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions, currency devaluation, reduced market access and functionality played a role in the deterioration of food security situation leading to increased malnutrition status of the most vulnerable children U5 boys & girls, adolescents & adult & PLWs with women carrying out many roles, affecting negatively the amount of time they are able to spend feeding and caring for their children, which consequently increases children’s risk of malnutrition. UNIDOR in collaboration with other Humanitarian actors seek to ensure continued nutrition support through 12 OTP/TSFP sites,4 outreaches and 1 SC to the affected populations factoring in promotion of equity & access in participation of men, women, boys and girls in need assessment. UNIDOR is Education, FSL, WASH, CP & Peace partner with a well-established base hence the advantage to deliver multi-system approach to reduce all foams of malnutrition.

2. Project activities

Conduct community nutritional assessments for acute malnutrition through community screening and referral to the respective programs. • Strengthening Family MUAC to caregivers and PBW in HH with children U5. • Provide micro nutrient supplementation to children, adolescents, and women • Community awareness on Growth monitoring and promotion with the GMP charts. • MIYCN counselling and behaviors change messaging to mothers/caregivers of children 0-59 months and pregnant women through mother support groups, individual and group counselling at the community and facility • Provide timely and quality treatment to boys and girls U5 and PBW with malnutrition at the TSFP/OTP/SC. • Malaria testing and treatment for all SAM children U5 at and MAM U5 symptomatic and treatment of diarrhea cases in collaboration with health. • Capacity strengthening, Job training and knowledge sharing to nutrition and health workers,CNVS/BHWs and the CHD staff on CMAM, MIYCN integrated with the preventive package and reporting. • Provision of preventive packages (Soaps, water purifiers & treated mosquito nets • Integration of ECD in the OTP/TSFP sites for stimulation by ensuring availability of Child friendly spaces with age-appropriate toys • Set up kitchen demonstration gardens and conduct cooking demonstrations to impact on dietary diversification with school feeding programs to improve on optimal nutrition services. • Collaboration with Protection to ensure mainstreaming of GBV referral pathways and support on GBV safety audits at the nutrition facilities. • Strengthen Accountability to affected populations trough community engagements activities at all phases of the project. • Commemoration of Nutrition related global events. • Conduct Mass MUAC screening, SMART surveys and supporting in FSNMS to enhance nutrition surveillance. • Conduct routine and quarterly joint supportive supervision in coordination and collaboration with CHD/SMOH on job training/mentorship to improve the quality of programing and strengthening the state capacity for transaction purposes
