Project Summary

Somalia continues to witness conflict, violence, human rights violations, and disasters, which trigger repeated waves of displacement. The IDPs often move to the urban ercenters and reside in informal crowded settlements, with inadequate access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. The displacement situation continues to exemplify the need for emergency shelter and Non-Food Items (S-NFI) needs in Somalia, to improve their living conditions. The provision of emergency shelter remains high among newly displaced IDPs in informal settlements, as they lack alternative shelter materials, with their dignity and protection at risk. Women, children, the elderly, child- & female-headed households (CHHH and FHHH), the physically and mentally disabled, people living in conflict zones, and marginalized clans, are mostly exposed to the highest risks and are consistently socially excluded. Many IDPs Settlements lack access to basic household items or materials for proper shelter and remain vulnerable. Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) and its implementing partners in Somalia (IPs) seek to improve the living conditions of the newly displaced, protracted IDPs, refugees/asylum seekers or returnees. The most vulnerable IDP households in areas of new or recent arrivals will be prioritized for emergency shelters and NFI kits. The project intends to deliver the rehabilitation of community infrastructure like improvement of access routes within the settlements through Cash for Work activities (CFW) to provide these households with temporary employment to cover their urgent basic needs. Shelter and NFI kits shall be distributed through the modality of flexible vouchers where the list of eligible items for redemption is not fixed but gives the flexibility to prioritize individual items. and DKH and IPs aim to provide standard cluster recommended emergency shelter and NFI kits to IDPs affected by conflict and natural disasters in response to their emergency needs. The project will be implemented in Banadir, Gedo, Hiraan, Galgaduud, and Mudug regions, targeting a total of 3000 households (18,000 persons) in 55 IDP settlements. The project will incorporate gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs assessments and response recommendations. The Shelter and NFI voucher includes core relief items for daily household use. Shelter items like plastic sheeting, timber, tools, nails, binding wire, door-frame, door-blade with hinges, latch and padlock, curtains for privacy protection, mats, and mosquito nets. The list of items for NFI includes pots, pan, and household utensils and also hygiene and other WASH items such as jerry cans and solar lamps. The flexible voucher is limited by the fixed amount in USD that cannot be overspent. It is an unconditional and restricted voucher that will be handed out to new IDPs (including newly evicted IDPs) and the beneficiaries will have the choice to prioritize items to choose from the list. The IPs will conduct PDM and PCM to ensure accountability, appropriateness of materials and effectiveness of distribution. In addition, DKH and IPs will support establishment and strengthening of complaints response mechanism (CRM). DKH and the IPs will ensure gender and age awareness including the disaggregation of data and the targeting of specific vulnerable categories which are; FHHH, CHHH, persons living with disabilities, marginalized, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly persons’ gender-based needs in the household. The intervention will also be implemented while mainstreaming protection as a core component in the targeted IDP camps. The beneficiary selection criteria applied will focus on the most vulnerable crisis-affected IDPs and non-IDPs.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will use cash for work for the shelter and NFI kits through both conditional and unconditional vouchers which will be redeemed from the local vendors who will be identified by the project to supply the items. DKH uses unconditional but restricted voucher system where the beneficiaries will have the flexibility to redeem on household items of choice from the list of items approved by the cluster for emergency kits. This will enhance decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they are able to make an independent choice, with the flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need, including quantity, but guided by the list allowed by the Shelter and NFI Cluster. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of the cash into the local markets, especially in the Somalia context where purchasing power among the vulnerable is low.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect in the market functionality.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation I: Beneficiary Selection1.The project will ensure the location, distance and access routes to distribution points minimize the safety risks for displaced women and girls, in addition to identifying vendors closer to the beneficiaries’locations to avoid exposure to risks of sexual exploitation of women and girls and safety concerns due to long-distance walking to distribution sites. 2.In collaboration with the protection actors, the project will advocate for measures to prevent and respond to sexual violence arising due to crowded shelter conditions. 3.The project will enhance the capacity of community Shelter/NFI workers and committees to monitor, report and refer protection concerns in accordance with the standard operating procedure. 4.DKH will ensure the transfer modality and the timing of distribution will minimize safety risks for women and girls. Redeemable vouchers and avoiding exchange of physical cash, conducting distribution during the day hours give the women and girls ample time to reach home before nightfall. Beneficiaries with disabilities will nominate a HH representative to act as an alternate where the beneficiary can’t make it to the distribution point or Cash for work activities 5.The project will consider gender balance among distribution teams/committees to ensure enough women available as contact points for women & girls in the community. Mitigation II: 1)The project will ensure a selected location for registration/ distribution services is safe with enough space for orderly registration/distribution. Central locations will be identified to enable beneficiaries to travel conveniently and safely or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. 2.The registration/distribution centres will observe culturally accepted values; adopting separate queuing areas for male & female and guarantee female staff present to serve them 3.The project will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent & respond to physical violence arising during registration/distribution; an accessible, confidential and well understood CRM will be established with the beneficiaries to allow them to register their complaints. 4.The project will ensure shelter/NFI staff, IPs and volunteers working with the targeted beneficiaries understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct on their commitment to humanitarian standards and uphold beneficiaries rights Mitigation III: CRM1.DKH will establish and strengthen beneficiary CRM within the settlements and beneficiaries informed about it, with confidentiality enhanced. 2.DKH will ensure that there will be a variety of channels for provision of feedback on the performance of the project, especially on the quality and service delivery3.DKH will conduct PDMs after every distribution with beneficiaries where their views on the selection, distribution process, quality of items from the suppliers and usefulness of the items will be sourght and corrective measures taken4.The project will promote public participation where the beneficiaries will be engaged in public for a, including clear information sharing to ensure people are aware of the existing CRM in the areas of residenceMitigation IV: Land disputes1.DKH in collaboration with other humanitarian will work closely with authorities to ensure that IDPs are given land for a period of time to avoid being evicted2. DKH will also endeavour to support the implementation of National IDP policy, including the National Evictions Guidelines designed to ensure that any evictions are carried out in a planned and legal way that protects the rights of displaced people3. DKH and IPs with other protection partners will advocate for durable solutions through the allocation of permanent land to the protracted IDPs4. Regular engagement with humanitarian protection monitors and actors to help prevent rights violations such as evictions that occur because of an increase in land values

Please provide further explanation

The project is designed with a view to ensuring emergency lifesaving support, especially for the vulnerable internally displaced person and host communities. The project enhances coordination mechanisms within the settlements and also with external authorities to ensure that the people affected are able to access services, and further enhance stability. The project strengthens community structures, and promote community participation in the identification of beneficiaries, while at the same time taking into consideration in decision-making, ensuring ownership, training on various measures at the camp level and these actions will also contribute to empowering of the affected populations in decision-making. The project also strengthens beneficiary access to information, thereby strengthening their ability to contribute to the decision-making processes for the project delivery in the settlements.
