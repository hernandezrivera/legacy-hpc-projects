Project Summary

Community Action Organization (CAO) proposes the 2024 HRP health project to provide quality and comprehensive healthcare services to conflict-affected populations in Fangak and Ayod counties to prevent and manage medical and health-related threats and enhance the health systems at lower levels. CAO’s proposed HRP health response is well aligned with Health Cluster and the 2024 HRP objectives and plans to improve access to and utilization of essential health care services to reduce excess morbidity and mortality while saving lives, reducing health risks, and enhancing coping and recovery among 30,000 vulnerable populations (IDPs, Returnees and host populations).
CAO will use an approach consistent with the health cluster to address health-related challenges by working with the Jonglei State Ministry of Health (SMOH) and two CHDs to support them in health leadership, capacity building, health program implementation, monitoring, and supervision. CAO will coordinate with two CHDs to recruit qualified and technical health personnel to work on the frontline within health facilities and communities. Through CAO’s technical health teams, the frontline health personnel will be provided with routine supportive supervision, job training, and health mentorship, including capacity-building training on IMNCI, BEmONC, HIV/AIDS, IPC, family planning, CMR, EPI, PMTCT, and drug and laboratory management.
To ensure a constant pipeline supply chain, CAO will maintain a robust coordination with WHO, UNFPA, and UNICEF to ensure that medicines and other essential medical supplies are provided and propositioned in time within the health facilities to provide emergency health services. CAO also aims to support the health systems infrastructure by conducting rehabilitation of the existing 3 HFs in new Fangak and 2 HFs in Ayod. The project will enhance community health referral systems and safe motherhood packages so that ANC and PNC services are readily available and provided in all PHCCs by trained health personnel.
Further, CAO will enhance integrated mobile outreach health services to ensure that health services are provided to all people in need in hard-to-reach locations while ensuring that no woman or child dies at birth. Communication in the referrals, especially between referral centers and pathways, will be effective by providing ambulances and mobile phones to health workers in charge in Fangak and Ayod counties. PHCCs will be equipped to provide PMTCT and HIV/ART services. CAO aims to reach 30,000 children U5s with HF and community consultations and 30,000 with Vitamin A supplements to U5s. 9,000 children U1 will be reached with DPT3 vaccination and 11,000 U1s with measles vaccination. Fifteen thousand seven hundred children U5 and 17,000 PLW will be provided with integrated nutrition services, and 5,000 women will be provided with safe family planning counseling. At the community level, social behavior change communication will be strengthened through community awareness raising, mainly on health service availability for increased uptake and utilization using IEC materials, community radio channels, community engagement, and stakeholder meetings. CAO shall coordinate with UNFPA and WHO to provide promotional materials for ANC, PNC, and HF deliveries like mama kits, Pads/MHM kits, and t-shirts; and Penta 3 to motivate the target population to access health services. CAO will strengthen the Boma Health Initiative component to link cases in the community to the health facilities for easy access to health services among hard-to-reach populations while ensuring timely prevention and responses to clinical cases, ensuring that CMR services are provided to the victims at the community level. CAO will ensure the integration of emergency health interventions through coordination with other partners within the five famine clusters: Nutrition, FSL, WASH, Protection, and education.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, the worst health indicator is the under-five mortality rate, with 98/1,000 live births (over 40% of deaths in Under-five years’ children occurred during the first month of life due to high risks associated with delivery and post-delivery period for both mothers and their new-born), infant mortality rates (IMR) in South Sudan is at 63/1,000 live births, neonatal mortality rate(NMR) with 40/1,000 live births, an Age-adjusted death rate of 23/100,000 population (UNIGME, 2021) have been attributed to poor access to health services due to an acute health workforce shortage, inadequate health infrastructure and poor health service utilization (SS WHO, 2022). As of October 2023, the South Sudan Health Cluster had estimated 6,324,453 people in need of emergency lifesaving health interventions ( including Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) by 2024 (SS HC, Oct 2023). The WHO reference data indicates that about 75% of deaths in under-five are caused by Malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia. Maternal mortality ratio(MMR) with 1,150 per 100,000 live births, the highest percentage in the world (MMEIG, 2017), with leading causes of maternal mortality as PPH at 16%, obstructed/prolonged labor at 13.3% and Postpartum Haemorrhage with 12.9% (MOH, 2013). ANC at 39% of women during pregnancy, institutionalized delivery at 12.3% as well, and skilled birth attendance is low at 12.3%. Access to BEmONC is shallow; teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, the contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6%, with modern techniques at 5%, and the unmet need for family planning is estimated at 29.7% for 2020 (FP,2022). According to ISNA 2023, the limiting factors to access and utilization of care services were lack of medicines, staff and equipment transportation cost, insecurity, floods, long distance to health facilities, and high cost of healthcare requiring mobility costs, especially by the IDPs, returnees and host communities. Jonglei State, including the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA), has the lowest EPI coverage owing to unstable security and the effects of floods on access to the needy population. Fangak and Ayod counties present with some of the worst health outcomes due to conflicts and floods limiting access to essential health services, safe water, and sanitation facilities. The 2022 SMART survey capturing mortality data in Fangak revealed a GAM rate of 17.5% (ACF-USA, 2022). According to the current IPC report, many households in Fangak and Ayod are in IPC phase 4 and 5. EPI coverage Fangak remains one of the lowest in the county, with an average of 53% and 27% coverage for Penta 3 (Week 38-EPI Coverage Report, August 2023). The two counties had a malaria outbreak reported in 2023. Further, Hepatitis E cases significantly increased to over 3,473 points with 26 deaths and maintained an epidemic threshold in 2023 (SS HNO 2023). Fangak had a declaration of the HEV outbreak in September 2023. In Ayod County, 14 HFs are supported by UNICEF/World Bank, while 17 are not supported/non-functional, yet the area is flooded (UNICEF/World Bank Review-Ayod CHD, 2023). With the increased number of returnees, IDPs, and vulnerable populations, the CHD has limited capacity to scale up emergency healthcare interventions to cope with the enormous lifesaving health needs. According to HMIS data, Malaria, AWD & ARI remain the significant causes of morbidity & mortality (SS IDSR week 40, 2023). The effects of acute food insecurity in Fangak and Ayod counties, with many households in IPC phase 4 and 5, places many vulnerable populations (children U5, PLWs, and elderly at risk of high disease commodities and increasing the risk of mortality among U5s associated with high food and acute malnutrition. Through this project, CAO will improve access to emergency healthcare interventions for 30,000 people in hard-to-reach communities in Fangak and Ayod counties.

2. Project activities

Objective 1: Improve equitable access to lifesaving essential quality health care services, including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations. Output 1: 30,000 vulnerable populations have improved access to lifesaving healthcare services, including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations. Activities:Act. 1.1: Implement mobile and outreach clinical activities in hard-to-reach locations.Act. 1.2: Provide immunizations against vaccine-preventable diseases at mobile and static facilities; enhance the capacity of health workers on EPI and COVID-19 vaccination. Act. 1.3: Conduct capacity building of health workers on BeMONC and provide BeMONC services in our primary health centers. Act 1.4: Provide and integrate MHPSS services in all health facilities, including mobile health outreach.Act 1.5: Provide lifesaving health care services, including management of non-communicable diseases; build the capacity of health workers to provide lifesaving health care services in hard-to-reach areas. Act 1.6: Conduct integration and provide services for SGBV, including CMR, in all health facilities targeted for this response and enhance referral to WGFSS and safe houses in Ayod and Fangak countiesAct 1.7: Provide technical and operational assistance to health facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services.Act 1.8: Provide community health services using Boma Health Initiatives and strengthen the linkage with facility-based services; Act 1.9: Provide services for disabled people.Act 1.10 Conduct and Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) in Ayod and Fangak, where the CAO is operational.Act 1.11: Provide psychosocial support and psychological first aid for survivors of rape Act 1.12: Provide M & E tools for mobile and static health facilities. Act 1.13: Clinical management of rape and associated sexual infections Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases. Output 2: 30,000 vulnerable populations have reduced morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases in Fangak and Ayod counties Activities Act 2.1: Enhance IPC in our health facilities through regular assessment using scorecards, provision of supplies and protocols, continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities, and refresher training to prevent disease outbreaks of COVID-19 Act 2.2: Give PMTCT & advance HIV/AIDs management and care Act 2.3: Distribute LLIN/mosquito nets in hard-to-reach locations. Act 2.4: Conduct community sensitization and disseminate IEC materials, including those for COVID-19Act 2.5: Conduct community consultative meetings to identify the needs of affected populations and strengthen feedback mechanisms to ensure beneficiaries provide feedback on the project. Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Output 3: 30,000 vulnerable populations are linked, and their resilience is enhanced and promoted through health system recovery and coping mechanisms. ActivitiesAct 3.1: Conduct MUAC screening U5s and PLWs to identify MAM and SAM cases and refer to TSFP/OTP sites, respectivelyAct 3.2: Provide SAM children with medical complications management in PHCC's primary healthcare centers. Act 3.3: Support the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA) on Health Care and report the attacks regularly.Act 3.4: Coordinate with core pipeline managers to provide emergency kits to support our mobile and static facilities.Act 3.5: Participate in the training on use of essential health commodities
