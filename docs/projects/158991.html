Project Summary

The project is designed to provide urgent support to protect the livelihoods of the small scale farmers' owners of open farms, small-scale poultry breeders and provide rehabilitation work and renewable power supply system for water wells and agricultural ponds based on BBB approach to enhance resilience of agricultural sector.
The project will target 250 of dunums of open farms for 125 small-scale farmers (80% males and 20%females) in order restore production and functionality by providing either full and/or partial land rehabilitation, fencing, piping and/or irrigation system, seeds and
seedlings distribution based on the individual needs assessment of each farm.
3 Agricultural wells will be rehabilitated for the sake of rural communities; 90 farmers " MoA indicates that available Agri wells need rehabilitation to the north of GS to provide irrigation water to 30 farmers as average). Three wells * 30 farmers = 90 beneficiaries in average". The wells will be supported with renewable power supply system (20 Kw each) to increase its functionality and durability to pump water during day time which will improve farmers access to water resources for irrigation purposes.
The project will also rehabilitate 50 affected old agricultural ponds (concrete, plastering and plastic) as per the scoping study to examine available water ponds in GS (PARC/DKH Jul 2018).
The project will also provide rehabilitation works for 50 of small-scale poultry farms (95% males and 5% females) and provide them with renewable power supply system (1.2 kw each) in order to make available the lighting, watering and ventilation required for the chicks. This activity likewise other interventions will be closely coordinated with the MoA.
Gender analysis:
Women in agriculture in Palestine are particularly disadvantaged, mainly due to their limited power and overall share within the agricultural system. Despite this it is to not be neglected that women constitute an unregistered yet significant portion of workers in this sector. This is in particular due to restricted mobility, and limited technical training in the sector offered to women.
Gender bias in agricultural labour is rampant; in addition to a huge gap in remuneration (both wage and self-employment income), female labour is often unpaid and precarious. Nevertheless, farming represents a crucial coping strategy for Palestinian households at times of crisis, playing a key role in promoting food and nutrition security within a context characterized by pervasive risks, where more than one quarter of the population is food insecure.
It is also important to mention that labour market participation of women is very low (19.3 percent compared to 71.6 percent of males) – and significant generational differences (youth unemployment 50 percent above the average). In addition, due to the limited power shortage and increased households and financial pressure, women are more overloaded with home chores, cooking, cleaning, and teaching and so on. it is more difficult for women to participate in the public life and /or other family businesses like farming unless necessary.
PARC, therefore, and based on own experience, considers the share of female owned agribusinesses is in the range of 7-8%. For the sake of this project PARC will be using the Gender Analysis tool to improve its performance and understanding of male, female and youth roles and responsibilities, shares and influence in the agricultural process.
Protection standards and core humanitarian standards will be fully respected. PARC staff was introduced to the protection standards and was well introduced to the Humanitarian core standards. Good steps forward were documented to develop the staff performance and reporting. Still PARC continues to support and equip the staff and community with awareness and tools to improve the quality of work and be able to correct, intervene and report properly against protection issues in the field.

1 - Needs

Increasing attention has to be given to Small-scale farmers ( male-female) for being categorized as one of the most marginalized social groups and affected of food insecurity( UNWOMAN Gender Alert. 2019), especially those whose access to land and agricultural inputs have been affected by the barrier, mobility restrictions and the blockade. As per latest updates of MoA/Oct. 2018, 15,000 dunum of open field are still in need for land rehabilitation interventions after the war 2014. Land rehabilitation in all governorates of the Gaza strip was set top humanitarian priorities for the FSS & the MoA "HRP 2019". As per the needs assessment of PARC for the Organizational plan and latest scoping survey (PARC/Jul.2019), land rehabilitation is still urgent and is high priority in the north governorates (1500 dunums) and Gaza governorate (2000 dunums). *Power crisis as well continues to affect farmers ability to sustain their small scale business: The continued energy crisis, though relative improvement, still imposes an additional burden on farmers who already suffer from an increase in agricultural input costs accompanied by low purchasing power by households due to falling economic situation, salary cuts and shortfall in Aid provided to Palestine. Reported in the PARC scoping survey Jul.2019. *agricultural ponds: The bad quality of water in some areas, and power crisis to pump up water for irrigation purposes along with the drought challenges have decreased farmers' resilience and increased farmers' vulnerability to food insecurity and poverty. This has also obliged farmers to resort to negative coping mechanism like selling the land and/or abandoning farming as profession, (PARC& DKH 2016). PARC has identified 2700 Agri ponds in the GS with different functionality conditions (PARC and DKH 2018). The scoping survey (PARC Jul. 2019) has indicated that agricultural ponds continue to be a high priority. Moreover and based on past experience of PARC 2013-2018 water drought resilience program DKH and Christian Aid, installing of renewable power supply system to water ponds, give the farmers ability to control the irrigation process/timing, as well as to provide assistance to surrounding farmers' community to maximise the beneficiary & enhance collaboration and social bonds. *Agricultural wells: MoA has set the rehabilitation of agricultural wells as a 2nd high priority for the HRP 2019. Based on the priority intervention matrix of MoA, rehabilitation of agricultural wells in still high in the north and Gaza govern-orate where 35 and 48 agricultural wells are still in need for rehabilitation. As well, agricultural wells have been set as high priority intervention for PARC organisational plan 2018-2019. *Poultry farmers: 700 broiler poultry farms are at risk due to the electricity shortage, as per last updates of MoA, )Oct. 2018(. As well, due to the deterioration of the economic situation in the past three years, most of animal farms were sold. This is also due to the fact that farmers are unable to sustain the costs of the business and have accumulated debts that they cannot repay, & agro-suppliers are not providing assets/agr-input on credit anymore. As per the scoping survey done by PARC in July 2019; 5 workshops and 10 FGDs in the 5 governorates, youth with age average (15-20) and adult females (>40) and farmer males were strongly participating. - Due to the fact that land ownership is mainly for men in GS, male farmers focused on the land rehabilitation in the north and GS governorate. - Male farmers focused on the need to get connected to sustainable water resources and provision of agricultural inputs. - Female farmers expressed the need for cash for works programs. They expressed their willingness to work in the field in the harvesting & packaging activities in olive and other crops harvesting times. Female farmers focused agricultural IGAs for sheep & poultry.

It is important to raise here, that female farmers or business owners are not to be neglected; and that PARC is taking important steps to address their needs and rights. PARC continue to visit and provide awareness to farming female; visits were paid for multiple purposes including awareness regarding the best water resource management practices and farm management at the farm level and more importantly learning about their role/responsibilities in their families and in managing their farms. PARC is using a specialized tool developed only for that purpose. It was learned that, while women who own farmland benefit from their ownership, for the most part their level of engagement in managing the farm is limited especially in issues that bring them in touch with men such as suppliers, retailers, wholesalers, etc. Further, the role of women in the farming activity itself is most noticed during the harvesting periods because it requires an increase in labour in a limited timeframe. For activities such as diagnosing diseases that require less labour intensity is identified. Getting old or suffering from diseases make women retire from most of their roles in the farm, whereas inheritance make women become more active in the agricultural sector. Gender mainstreaming will be ensured along the project by: (1)Firstly, prioritize women heads of households in agreement with the selection criteria. (2) Women CBOs will be increasingly consulted to enhance the share of women owned farms; (3)PARC will follow up closely with women wives of the farmers at the family level to make sure of the how the project affected the family members from gender and age perspective.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PARC is a based-right organization that committed to the humanitarian core standards, humanitarian principles and code of conduct. The safety and dignity of population are considered in all project’s interventions. PARC continuously provide orientation to the new staff about its polices, rules and standard, and will do so with the team of this project and with the project stakeholders to ensure the best respect of people and their dignity. Moreover, beneficiaries’ data will be secured and the access to it will be limited to the responsible staff and the use of it will be for specific purposes that explained to the beneficiaries before collection. further more, PARC also implement protection measures for the intervention themselves as per the risk analysis conducted, for example, protection fence and shades covers should be installed around and above the agri ponds to protect kids from falling in the ponds.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to target the most vulnerable affected groups in the community including women, elderly and People With Disabilities PWDs. Through PARC experience, active engagement for the PWDs was achieved through contacting specialized ORGs with PWDs to assist to engage and encourage them as breadwinners and supporters to their households. PARC will make sure to engage the different age and gender groups in the project different phases by conducting continuous interviews, meetings and FGDs with the targeted communities. PARC will make sure that selection process is done in an accountable manner so that to select the most vulnerable and eligible groups. PARC will make sure the safe and fair access of its beneficiaries to the services, particularity in this project, the PARC staff and selected suppliers will install the service at the farmers' lands.
