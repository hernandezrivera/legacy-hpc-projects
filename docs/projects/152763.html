Project Summary

The project will reach the most vulnerable 270,000 people (97,200 girls, 64,800 women, 48,600 boys and 59,400 men) which corresponds to 45,000 HH in Nassir 9500HH, Maiwut 8500HH, Akobo 7500HH, Leer 10,000HH and Fangak 9500HH. We shall contribute to FSL cluster objective 2 and 4 by providing production inputs (crop kits, fast growing vegetable kits and fishing kits). This will improve food production and provision of fishing kits will contribute to better nutrition at the HH level and in other cases improve HH income due to the sale of excess fish, this will take place from January 2019 to December 2019. We shall also provide unconditional cash assistance in line with FSL cluster objective 1 lifesaving food assistance, this response plan is aimed at an effort to prevent an even more severe food shortage as well as strengthening the coping capacities of the vulnerable host community and IDPs. Nile Hope shall liaise with FAO to get in kind support for the crop fishing and vegetable kits for the emergency responses but will procure for the other ongoing development projects
The production inputs will be given to 39,000 HH (23,400 Female HH and 15,600 Male HH) 7500 in Akobo,10,000 HH in Leer,7500HH in Fangak,6500 HH in Maiwut and 9500 HH in Nassir ,which corresponds to 234,000 beneficiaries vulnerable Host Communities 60% and IDPs 40% and unconditional cash assistance to 6000 HH (2000 HH in Maiwut,2000HH in Nassir and 2000HH in Fangak) which corresponds to 36000 beneficiaries. Special focus will be given to people with disability to make sure they are not left out. Capacity building training will be conducted on improved crop, vegetable and fishing practices and continuous practical sessions in the farms to selected recipient beneficiaries at the Payam level as TOTs to the rest of the Community for sustainability purposes targeting 10,000 people (6000 women and 4000 men). The overall project objective is improved food security and livelihoods at house hold level for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition

Needs

The crisis in South Sudan is marked by alarming levels of hunger, 4.4 million people facing emergency levels of acute severe food insecurity, (WFP Situation report #231), 2.1 million acutely malnourished women and children (HCT) & 1.76 million IDPs. Akobo has witnessed high influx of IDPs from the surrounding counties of Nyirol & Uror where insecurity and conflict pushed most people to flee while others moved in search for water and pasture for their livestock, it is classified in IPC 3 (IPC Sep, 2018) and likely to experience severe food shortage due to poor harvest & the high number of IDPs giving pressure to the host community. NH aims at preventing food shortage by providing production inputs (fishing, crop &vegetable kits), also with the ongoing support from EU shall continue building their resilience by supporting producer groups through capacity building training on improved production, post harvest loses, VSLAs, value addition and marketing. Leer is one of the most affected county in Unity State, September 2018 IPC putting it in IPC 4 ,it is at the center of the conflict, 3 payams being controlled by government while all other payams controlled by the IO. The community resorted to seek safety in swampy areas commonly known as Toch . The swampy areas are controlled by the opposition. In the midst of all the challenges, fishing &vegetable production is a good opportunity. The County is affected by rising food insecurity mostly a result of the insecurity, deepening economic crisis & depleted food stocks from insufficient household production. NH will continue supporting vulnerable HH in Leer County in 2019 with fishing, vegetable and crop kits, this is aimed at enhancing food production at HH level .Fangak is classified in IPC 4 (IPC, Sep 2018). The community there is vulnerable &It is hosting IDPs fleeing fighting In Unity state &also from Pigi County. There is also decreased access to food and disruptions in the supply chain due to the fighting between government & IO forces over control of the Tonga market which supplies the Fangak. NH has an ongoing unconditional cash programing in partnership with DCA & in 2019 plans to continue supporting the vulnerable HH in Fangak with provision of unconditional cash assistance & provision of production inputs (crop, fishing & vegetable kits). Maiwut is classified in IPC 4 according to Sep IPC report, it is known to be the headquarters of IO. The community has experienced recent shock due to displacement which left them very vulnerable as a result of the conflict between the warring parties over control of the area & is also hosting IDPs from Longechuk &Nassir. The population is in dire need of immediate humanitarian support. We have an ongoing Unconditional cash project in partnership with DCA and intends to continue with the same throughout 2019 to ensure that the vulnerable community has access to food &other basic needs. We will also provide production inputs (crop, vegetable& fishing kits) to enhance food production. Nasir is classified in IPC 3 (IPC Sep 2018) and remains as a high priority location ,the protracted conflict between the warring parties has subjected the population to multiple displacements in search of safety, some areas are controlled by the government and some by the opposition, which makes it very unsafe and unpredictable for the civil population .The food security situation remains dire as the community is not able to stay in one place long enough to farm consistently or engage in livestock production as well as engage in other livelihood activities, the number of IDPs keeps increasing putting too much pressure on the community. This has subjected the people to great suffering& limited options to better their livelihoods.).NH has ongoing unconditional cash assistance support with DCA & SSHF project, we intend to continue supporting the area in 2019 through unconditional cash assistance and provision of production inputs (crop, vegetable and fishing kits)

Activities

• Establish and train beneficiary’s selection committees (BSC), cash distribution committees (CDC) and project steering committees (PSC)• Create Synergies with Nile Hopes protection department and other stakeholders to enhance safe access to the distribution sites and other project sites for the beneficiaries and staff• Provide and conduct fair distribution of quality production inputs to 234,000 which corresponds with 39,000 HH (60% Female headed HH and 40% Male headed HH) vulnerable IDPS 40% and Host Community 60% by May 2018 for crops kits and January to December 2019 for fishing &vegetable kits in Akobo 7500 HH, Fangak 7500 HH,Leer 10,000HH,Nasir 7500HH and Maiwut 6500 HH• Farm level support on improved food and fish production, post harvest loses, VSLAs, value addition and marketing to 500 producer groups (20 members each) vulnerable IDPS 40% and Host Community 60% .This are TOTs selected from the beneficiaries of production inputs in Akobo 3000 people (150 groups), Fangak 1500 people (75 groups), Leer 2000 people (100 groups), Nassir 2000 people (100 groups) and Maiwut 1500 people (75 groups)• Provide Unconditional cash assistance to 36,000 people (6000HH) in 2000 HH Maiwut, 2000 in Nassir and 2000 HH in Fangak Counties• Monitoring and evaluation of the project to measure progress and impact of the project in all the project areas

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

We will conduct inception workshop to inform the target community about the project and their entitlement as well as the role of each stake holder,in the same meeting we will constitute project steering committees (PSCs),cash distribution committees (CDCs) and beneficiary selection committees (BSCs) that will work closely with NH program team at the mobilisation stage and through the implementation

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The context varies in different locations depending on the environment and the social dynamics .We will ensure the messages or communication disseminated are tailored to that specific location with consideration of both genders and age groups

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

We have an established safeguarding taskforce in Nile Hope to ensure all the issue of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse (SHEA) are prevented .We carry out safe guarding monthly sessions to ensure all our staff are on board and are observing the safeguarding policy that includes also the code of conduct which is annexed to staff contracts and emphasizes on zero tolerance on any sexual misconduct. We also ensure we advocate for gender balance as early as at recruiting level of staff and of during the formation of project steering committees aiming at 50% female where possible, this is aimed at creating safer space for our women beneficiaries to raise any safe guarding issue that may arise . We also work closely with our gender department to broadcast safe guarding localised messages at distribution sites as well use the existing referral pathways in case any cases of sexual abuses are reported at the community level

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultation included all the stake holders in the community to ensure it is participatory and representative (women, youth, men, community based structures e.g self-help groups, producer groups and producer associations) as well as people with special needs. This was done during consultative meetings, focus group discussion and assessments. All the views are considered in the response plan

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Nile Hope has a feedback and complaint mechanism system which consists of suggestion box, stakeholder’s project review meetings, complaint desk at the field level and a dedicated staff focal point based at the field and Juba level. We also have 2 trained people (male and female) tasked at the project steering committees to handle any complaint that may come up at the community level and escalate to Nile Hope focal point at the field level any issue that they are unable to handle. Also during distribution we always put up a complaint desk to handle any issue that may arise during the distribution.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community is an active part of the project. Nile Hope endeavours to engage with them through the locally recruited staff (local knowledge), local leaders, community representatives and relevant ministries at the county level. This is done via regular stake holders meeting as well as through consultative sessions with the locally constituted project steering committees, beneficiary selection committees and cash distribution committees. The MEAL team in Nile Hope also conducts joint monitoring with the selected stake holders and the project steering committees. This is aimed enhancing community ownership and participation

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The risk analysis has been conducted and the a risk management matrix developed to guide the implementation of the project, measures are put in place to mitigate the risks that can affect the beneficiaries and staff

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All the 5 target counties are in control of opposition in some part and some the government. This makes access a challenge and pauses risks for staff .Nassir,Fangak,Leer,Akobo are supported by UNHAS regular flights, Maiwut at the moment is accessed through Nassir County as it is not cleared by UNDSS for UNHAS to fly in. In 4 Counties except Leer we access the far payams using NH speed boat and for Leer we use canoes to access the water catchment areas. We have established physical presence in all the 5 Counties making it possible for us to reach the vulnerable people through our locally recruited staff who have local knowledge and can easily access most of the locations by foot.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Both genders are considered and 60% assigned to women beneficiaries as they are the most vulnerable. At the decision making level we have also ensured that women are well represented for example at the project steering committees, cash distribution committees and beneficiary selection committees. At staff recruitment level 40% is women to ensure we provide safer space for our women beneficiaries as they mostly feel comfortable working with their women counterparts. Distribution sites are also chosen in consultation with community representatives to ensure they are safe and accessible and will not pause any threat to our women and children as they go to access the inputs
