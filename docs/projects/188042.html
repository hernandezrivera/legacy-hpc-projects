Project Summary

The project aims to support the activity of a CEmONC created by Medina Association in January 2015 in North-western Syria and operated by the association since this date. Through the work of a staff of 55 workers, the facility, which is composed of:
-A mother and child hospital (Name: Medina Mother and Child Hospital, HeRAMS Code G0700140)
-And a mobile clinic (Name: Nezar Mobile Clinic, HeRAMS Code B0200164),
offers a comprehensive service including antenatal and postnatal consultations, deliveries, c-sections, family planning, psychological support, dental cares during pregnancy, and a mobile clinic providing field consultations to pregnant women, mothers and children. The activity is covering a large sector without any other CEmONC, hosting about 200 000 people, including residents and IDPs. We are a member of the WHO health cluster and UNFPA SRH group, and we are in touch with local health administration, in order to bring a coordinated response to women, mothers and children, victims of the conflict.

4 - Needs

2022 is marking the eleventh year of war in Syria. With the aim to bring a response to the healthcare needs of mothers and children, which in war context are harshly exposed to violence, insecurity, and deprivation of care, a mother and child facility was created by Medina Association in 2015. Firstly based in Aleppo suburbs, the facility had to move to Western rural Aleppo district after an aerial attack. Then, in February 2020, front the advance of governmental forces, we had to move to Northern Idleb district, where the place of settlement, Kelly, was selected by coordinating with WHO health cluster leaders, and UNFPA leaders in Gaziantep. There was no CEmONC in this sector, only a BEmONC and PHCs, despite the sector is hosting about 200 000 persons, including residents and IDPs. The number of IDPs had strongly increased in the last months of 2019, due to heavy forced displacements of population. Moreover, the villages and camps to be visited by our mobile clinic were selected with the same coordination, including two places in Western rural Aleppo, Daret Azza sub-district.In 2021, our CEmONC offered 45 630 consultations, ANC and PNC visits, and realized 3854 deliveries including 716 c-sections. 629 neonates benefited incubator, and 2 110 children were admitted in the paediatric ward. The mobile clinic provided 24 328 gynecological consultations, ANC and PNC visits, and 15 324 paediatric consultations. We are informing case management focal point if needed (protection).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Movements of people, from the place they stay to the facility (risk of attack, shelling, or violences);Measures: the mobile clinic is going to visit target people in their villages or camps (for example antenatal consultations); we are offering an ambulance service to transport pregnant women to the maternity before childbirth.2-Movements of the mobile clinic staff (risk of attack, shelling, or violences); Measures: each day, before the staff circulation, the field coordinator establishes a contact with the head of the village or the camp to be reached, and they check the ongoing situation together. In case of any doubt, the consultation is cancelled.3-Aerial attack of the facility, death of patient and workers.Measures: the place is known from the population and the local administration, but it is installed in an usual house, with a few details permitting to know what is the activity inside.4-Medical incidents (with drugs, medical consumables) leading to infection, inefficiency of the treatment.Measures: a large part of drugs and consumable are provided by UNFPA and drug supply line. The field coordinator, who is a pharmacist, is checking every drug and consumable from other sources.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

- Sexual exploitation of female workers- Sexual abuse of female workersMeasures: every worker, male or female, is briefed by the program coordinator about this risk, their consequences for the victim and the author; there are some referents inside the staff, including a doctor; these referents received GBV training. In case of some related facts, the field coordinator would lead an inquiry, then final decisions would be taken by him and the head of project in France.- Sexual abuse of patientsNo patients stay alone with one staff member. In case something would be noticed, the other staff members have to report to referents (GV trained) or to the field coordinator. the field coordinator would lead an inquiry, then final decisions would be taken by him and the head of project in France.A chart, mentioning PSEA, is existing, and each job contract is including an item about PSEA.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Aerial attack or attack by an armed group; discretion of the facility and of the workers as preventing this risk. Some medical equipment is protected in a warehouse, outside of the facility, in order to be able to have residual activity in case of destruction of equipment.2-Closure of the border, leading to a lack of drugs and consumable. Measures: diversification of the sources. Cooperation with other facilities in case of shortage of some essential drug or consumable.3-Closure of the border, leading to the impossibility to transfer patients to Turkey. Measures: the facility has increased its capacity of care; in some particular cases, a transfer to another hospital in the sector could be a solution.4-Exceeding the capacities of the facility: the staff is dimensioned in link with the volume of the activity. In fact, we had to add one evening outpatient consultation, due to the decrease of the capacities of the BEmONC of the sector. Any other exceeding would lead to coordination with WHO health cluster, UNFPA, and the field NGOs.
