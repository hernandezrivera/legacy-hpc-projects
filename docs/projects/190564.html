Project Summary

The different assessment findings and situational updates of different regions in Ethiopia indicated that the food insecurity situation severely affected. The crop production and productivity become deteriorated and the livestock and crop sectors as well lack of animal health service and feed shortage. Almost in all targeted woredas of the regions the veterinary services are out of function due to lack of veterinary drugs and equipment’s.
Drought and conflict impacted the community through the depletion of assets such as disruption of crop harvesting season, looting and destruction of household properties. Farmers lost their animals, lost matured crop in the field, living houses destructed, and alternative livelihood assets destroyed due to the conflict. In addition to the private properties, service providing government offices such as veterinary clinics were destroyed during the conflict. Reports showed malnutrition rates remained critically high in all regions in February and March 2022. Further deterioration is expected as the delayed Belg rain disrupts the planting season.
Though this humanitarian assistance is essential to save life and to protect the livelihood of the crises affected communities. For smallholder farmers who rely on subsistence farming, timely assistance will allow them to continue to grow their own food, secure income, take advantage of local economic opportunities and make them rely on less humanitarian and government assistance. GOAL sees that the recovery intervention of agriculture is indisputable in which the effect such activities will improve food and nutrition security of HHs and at the production phase it will boost the local market as well as increases income and employment opportunity.
Therefore, unless urgent recovery actions taken the HHs will face chronic food insecurity and remain waiting external food aid. This is unachievable and undesirable. Thus, the restoration of affected populations agriculture and other off farm livelihoods is unquestionable. Therefore, the recovery of livestock resource would have a multiple effect such as improved nutrition, income, employment, and boost the crop production. GOAL Ethiopia has geographical presence in regions of the target woredas where Nutrition, WASH, Health, and protection sector projects actively implemented with integration to complement each other. GOAL has strong and good partnership with the local communities and with the existing governance system.
The affected community will be supported by crop seeds including vegetables, pulses, cereals, MPC labeled for agricultural inputs, and livestock services to ensure adequate animal fodder and veterinary services. Cash will be used as the preferred modality to provide this agricultural support to help farmers/pastoralist/agropastoral to purchase agricultural/livestock inputs like livestock feed and water based on the functionality of local markets.
GOAL has operations established across the targeted woredas in other thematic interventions, and office and logistical capacity already established. By leveraging existing presence and staff we can ensure a cost-effective response. GOAL has strong targeting, verification, and accountability mechanisms institutionalized across all our responses. We have been a key partner with various donors, including EHF on agriculture interventions. We have long been working with vulnerable groups all over the country and have extensive experience with cash, seed, and agriculture interventions. Proposed interventions under this project are in line with the EHF allocation strategy and priority as well as they are need based and the selected interventions under this project are responsive to the prioritized activities of the agriculture sectoral plans.
GOAL will consider environment and climate changes in its intervention so that natural resources will be protected from any unintended consequences.

1. - Projects activities

The project will address 45,000 most vulnerable HHs with emergency seed (cereals, pulses and vegetables) MPC, animal health, forage seeds and feed, egg laying poultry and extension works and capacity development activities. The major activities proposed for this project are: 1.1. Provide emergency seed support for poor small holder farmers GOAL will procure and distribute emergency seeds for 19,807 prioritized households from conflict and drought-affected communities in the target woredas of the regions. Seeds will be procured from registered and certified suppliers (e.g., Ethiopian Seed Enterprise (and regional branches), seed producer cooperatives, unions) in line with the Ethiopian regulations. Each government supplier from whom GOAL will procure the seeds will be certified according to the necessary quality standards. GOAL will ensure that this is part of the essential criteria for supplier selection during the procurement process. In the case of insufficient seed stock in the formal seed suppliers or sectors, GOAL will print and provide cash vouchers to the target beneficiary households. 5000 households will receive cereal seed with an average of 50 Kg per households while 4000 households will be provided with pulses (beans and peas) with an average of 10kg per households. 4107 households will receive different varieties of vegetable seeds like cabbage, carrot, and onion. 6700 households will be supported with multipurpose cash assistance of 40 USD per households. 1.2. Provide support of government veterinary clinics with vaccines, drugs, and basic equipmentGOAL will procure and provide veterinary drugs, vaccine and equipment’s and provide them to the Woreda Animal Production and Veterinary Service office in the targeted woredas where the subsequent distribution of drugs will be done to targeted public veterinary clinics. GOAL will ensure livestock owners in the targeted woredas in which 17674 hhs have free access to livestock services and drugs for their animals. Besides, GOAL will regularly monitor the utilization of livestock drugs and ensure the functionality of the supply chain. GOAL will advocate for subsidized/free community service and will monitor the implementation of the proposed activities, given the financial inability of poor livestock owners because of the crisis.1.2. Provide support for smallholder farmers on improved forage productionGOAL will purchase appropriate seeds of fast-growing forage 6000 kg (alfalfa, Rhodes grass, pigeon pea. Vetch and cow pea) and provides to 7522 HHs to cultivated in their backyard. These are highland and lowland forage shrubs and grasses that would be used for all types of livestock. 1.3. Provide egg laying poultry to women headed HHs GOAL will purchase and provide support to most vulnerable 3000 HHs (priority to Female headed and Child headed Households) with improved egg laying chicken. Each target HH will receive 10 chickens with 3-month poultry feed 1.4 Provide capacity building training for 180 government agricultural extension workers on pre- and post-harvest handling disease surveillance and reporting system in the selected target woredas.GOAL will provide capacity building training for 300 model farmers about pre harvest farming and post-harvest handling. This training will also be conducted for DAs who will be engaged at kebele level extension service provision and the DA will also cascade the training to the wider farmers. Woreda and zone animal health experts will also receive training on disease surveillances and reporting system.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project is designed reviewing the rapid need assessment reports conducted participating communities at the Woreda level. The project implementation and monitoring process will also be ensured to involve community participation through their male and female community representatives GOAL Ethiopia has institutionalized accountability across all levels, ensuring accountability towards our project participants and partners through participation, feedback, transparency, information sharing, appropriate activities that respond to the needs, and appropriate use of resources. Engagement with local communities is crucial to a successful programme. GOAL ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. GOAL considers it of paramount importance that beneficiaries have access to information about their entitlements, have access to GOAL’s Code of Conduct and are able to provide feedback on programs. During any community activities discussions are held in advance with the affected community, project participants, community leaders and authorities. Information is provided about the project, the Donor, targeting criteria and entitlements, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, and ways to contact the organization with feedback, requests for information, and grievances. In every steps of project implementation, the relevant key stakeholders at all levels will be informed and engaged with the response. Information is shared with government authorities at all levels on a regular basis; joint supervision and monitoring activities take place regularly to the sites. GOAL is a member of Core Humanitarian Standards Alliance. A Community Response Mechanism (aka Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism) will be established for all locations for stakeholders, including beneficiaries, to be able to provide complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries will be informed of this mechanism during activities, entitlement posters will be printed and displayed at relevant locations. The CRM is designed for direct and indirect project participants, community members, local and national government, partners, and GOAL staff. Core principles of GOAL’s CRM are confidentiality, accessibility, safety, and transparency. GOAL has dedicated third party (independent from operations) in-country resources in place to manage the CRM. All staff are trained on accountability measures, including the CRM, and comprehensive guidelines and SOPs are in place. Monitoring visits will also address accountability questions to establish the level of knowledge of the beneficiaries of the selection criteria, their entitlements, and GOAL’s Code of Conduct. GOAL is committed to a having a culture of evidence-based learning and accountability.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

As part of the CRM, information is shared with communities during interventions (such as seed and MPC distributions) about the project. Community members and project participants are provided with clear information about how to contact GOAL to provide feedback, request information, make complaints or express grievances. Contact details are provided for field staff and the Regional Manager and the Country Director. In addition, details of a dedicated “Call me Back” phone number and e-mail address in GOAL’s Head Office in Addis are provided. During distributions, representatives (male and female, and literate if possible) are selected by the community as ‘Community Feedback Members’. The community can also make contact directly. All grievances are dealt with in a serious and confidential manner and shared with GOAL Ethiopia’s Head Office in Addis. The most serious complaints are dealt with directly by the in-country management team. Representatives from communities will be engaged as part of the CRM to provide feedback or request clarification on the project from GOAL staff and will be fully engaged throughout the project activities.
