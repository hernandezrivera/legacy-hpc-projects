Project Summary

The goal of this project is to improve protection of the most vulnerable in Iraq by providing operational updates on at risk populations, specifically assessing IDP movements—both new displacements and return intentions and patterns, district profiling, and contextualising the feasibility of IDP returns reintegrating into their villages of origin. This analysis will be conducted through the lens of durable solutions and will seek to understand the situational context for IDPs comparative to host communities and their villages of origin. This analysis will be operationalized and disseminated through regular reports, briefings, and bilateral meetings as needed and requested by operational actors. The geographic scope will span across Fifteen districts in Five governorates.

Needs

In support of future durable solutions initiatives and ongoing operational activities supporting IDPs, iMMAP, on behalf of the National Protection Cluster, will conductcommunity level assessments and district profiling in Anbar, Ninewa, Diyala, Salah Al-Din and Kirkuk provinces. The primary focus of these district profiles centers on identifying security dynamics, political and social structures, socioeconomic structures and monitoring key atmospherics that have the greatest influence on the social fabric influencing the IDP return landscape. An established mechanism for monitoring local integration and social cohesion is currently absent, and there are significant information gaps regarding nuanced conditions for area of origin returns among the IDP population. iMMAP aims to transition research and analysis expertise and building off the momentum of iMMAP’s Syria Population Monitoring Initiative (S-PMI), district profiles and stakeholder mapping, to fill these information gaps and strengthen the proposed durable solutions framework. iMMAP’s community level enumerators team will promptly work to establish a current picture of the scope and scale of conditions affecting return-related vulnerability and provide risk information to IDPs, returnees, and host communities about local integration and relocation to another part of the country. Special attention will be given to people deemed most vulnerable including children and women by advocating for community level assessments to be carried out immediately in their areas to protect the safety movements of IDPs.

Activities

Raw and analysed village-level population movement data (i.e. # of residents, # of IDPs, # of IDP returns, length of stay, intention to move, origin, destination) will be published monthly following each data collection round accompanied by a narrative analyzing return conditions. Monthly submission flexibility for granular validation and contextualisation of large scale and unique displacements and/or return movements between each data set publication. District profiles will be updated and published in anagreed upon format every 3 months. Thus, planned activities entail:1) Reporting on population movements, including new displacements and IDP return movements, and humanitarian conditions at the village level.2) Developing accurate and timely reporting on new displacements and IDP return movements and community perceptions. 3) Facilitating the dissemination of in-depth contextual information via district profiles highlighting the economic, political, social, and resource-based conditions and the implications of those conditions for durable solutions and the most vulnerable. 4)Developing a nuanced understanding of the return population in the region and theconditions, perceptions, intentions, and impact of their return within the community/area of origin. 5) Monitoring key atmospherics that have the greatest influence on the returnlandscape.6) Developing an understanding of local conflict dynamics, areas of influence, tribal dynamics and the local governance environment. COMPONENT ONE: Monthly Population Monitoring and Context Report FOCUS: The reintegration of a population influenced by years of conflict in Iraq will define the humanitarian conditions, social cohesion balance, reconciliation, and communal peace and stability of the future.Understanding these dynamics is essential to understanding the landscape, needs, conditions and potential points of conflict that will develop. Research and analysis will seek to understand the current perceptions, intentions, information streams, socioeconomic factors, and prominent push and pull dynamics of IDP returns at a sufficient level of detail, and as to inform social cohesion preparedness. METHODOLOGY: Will take place in two parts as follows: Part A: Integration Conditions (Statistically Representative Household Surveys) Following an assessment of existing knowledge databases, information networks, and collection modalities, iMMAP will collect qualitative data at the household level (as access allows) to be statistically representative and comparable across time, location, and population group (IDPs, IDP-returnees, nondisplaced residents). Household surveys will be complemented with monthly KIs with and local administrative bodies at the village level to monitor population drains and influxes and verified through secondary networks and incident monitoring mechanisms. Part B: Contextualising Returns within Social Dynamics (KI, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), secondary data review and open source) In addition, iMMAP’s I-PMI will work with partners to schedule and conduct qualitative key informant interviews and focus group discussions (as relevant) to understand the more qualitative aspects of conditions, considerations, and social structures of returns that directly impact intentions and reintegration. DELIVERABLE: The deliverables under this component will be defined and adjusted according to the operational context and information needs as follow: 1) Regular monthly data set and report analysing and contextualising the survey findings and general situational updates; 2) Flash updates on dynamic situations requiring more frequent reporting. iMMAP will consult with operational partners and the protection cluster members to establish a trigger for flash updates as needed. COMPONENT TWO: Quarterly District Profiles FOCUS / METHODOLOGY: iMMAP will assess the availability of secondary data and open source media and merge these information networks with primary data collection to fill relevant information gaps. The I-PMI will utilize the access and networks developed under component one to identify additional KIs relevant to understanding the situation around political, tribal, ethnic, sectarian, economic, and conflict dynamics. In addition to using data and analysis regarding return perceptions and interviews conducted, the team will seek to assist contextualising strategic social cohesion specific interventions. DELIVERABLE: This component will produce Quarterly District Profiles. iMMAP will identify and analyze key local and regional variables, namely political, tribal, ethnic, sectarian, economic, and conflict dynamics that influence the decision-making processes around IDP returns and protracted displacement. These reports summarize the I-PMI’s findings into succinct and holistic overviews for each profiled district.

Protection Risk Analysis

Targeted beneficiaries: Female: 175,000 Male: 325,000 This project will directly target affected communities and IDP returnees through field enumerator teams, who will have access to the focus areas and will conduct in-person interviews. Operating in a complex conflict environment requires keen security as well as social awareness and corresponding protocols to responsibly ensure both diverse and representative coverage of the affected population as well as duty of care for the enumerators. The proposed areas of action for this initiative are all reflective of Iraq’s diverse ethnosectarian landscape and kinetic security environment, with each proposed area of action featuring different social and security dynamics affecting returns. Sunni-majority Anbar Governorate and Hawija District in Kirkuk Governorate were some of the first areas in Iraq to fall under IS control in 2014. Since the GoI recapture of the governorate in a series of large-scale military operations, inter-communal tensions stemming from alleged former tribal affiliations or past cooperation with IS inhibit the returns of IDPs to their areas of origin. Moreover, large-scale destruction of domiciles and ordnance contamination in major urban centres such as Ramadi, Fallujah, Hit, Haditha, and Qaim are also inhibiting factors. Tal Afar and Sinjar are defined as ‘Disputed Territories’ between the Kurdish Region of Iraq (KRI) and Federal Iraq by Article 140 of the Iraqi Constitution. Given the military advance of GoI affiliated forces (including sectarian PMUs) into Disputed Territories formerly controlled by the KRI in October 2017 and subsequent accusations of ethnic and sectarian cleansing, these areas have been focal points for inhibited IDP returns. The ethno-sectarian makeup of Sinjar has also given rise to intercommunal tensions and corresponding multiplicity of ethnic militias that inhibit returns. The added complication that the area is also a stronghold of the PKK-affiliated YBS in Iraq has drawn Turkey’s military attention to the area.

Mitigation Measures

Duty of Care:- Field Staff: iMMAP has multiple years of experience in managing the safety and security as well as the reliability of its PMI network in Syria. In this experience, iMMAP has proven a robust commitment to and its own internal policies covering the personal safety of enumerators and other KI networks that places the security and personal judgement of the source first. - Do No Harm: Further, in compliance with the principles of ‘do no harm’, iMMAP utilises a highly experienced field operations team, and a vetted and pre-existing network in Iraq. iMMAP is well-networked within the NGO fora, partner organisations, coordination actors and stakeholder communities, which provide a robust network for information sharing and triangulation and will utilize these resources to reinforce other in-country sources. Accessibility Considerations: - Area of control: iMMAP has access to multiple districts in Iraq though its Mine Action and other related programmes and has the capacity to increase its footprint and area of operation to other locations in which current programming is limited. Due to duty of care (DoC) protocols and threats to enumerator safety concerns. Given current conditions, iMMAP does not anticipate a total or near total loss of access due to changes in control or challenges to the operational environment in the next six months. - Security: iMMAP closely tracks and monitors security incidents and shifting control lines. In the case of clashes, looming volatility, or specific targeting of staff, the field team will be instructed not to travel to the area of contest. Depending on the intensity of conflict, or level of threat to field staff, enumerators will collect data remotely in order to maintain information access while mitigating risk to staff. - Political conflict: Due to the relative unknowns and changing dynamics in terms of areas of influence (AoI) at the local / District level, it may – in some cases – prove difficult to obtain specific information from qualified, experienced key informants (KIs) at the local level. In such cases, enumerators will turn to their alternative networks to obtain and verify information. For villages experiencing shifts in governance dynamics and challenges to control/influence due to returns of IDPs, iMMAP enumerators, in most cases, will still be able to access the village, either physically or remotely. Such instances will be addressed on a case to case basis, with a priority for field team safety. - Diverse Sampling: iMMAP will utilise national staff including males and females with specific expertise and backgrounds in the enumerator teams, allowing for access to a diversity of households and KIs during data collection. Additionally, iMMAP will develop and deliver tailored training, both in-person and remotely in English and Arabic to partners interested in receiving support on humanitarian information management to ensure data quality. Emphasis will be on questionnaire design, assessment methodology, data collection, data authentication and data verification with additional training capacity on whistleblowing policy as well as Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Assault (PSEA). Capacity strengthening will also be grounded in practical exercises and follow-up mentoring, as needed. Risk Mitigation Analysis for Partners: Furthermore, the project will have the added benefit of conducting rolling analysis on the situational context and protection concerns. This analysis will be widely disseminated via a multitude of avenues and fora to better inform humanitarian partners and aid in their mitigation of risk. Having such a dedicated mechanism also allows for the harmonising of tools, surveys, and analysis, which allows for the collection of data that is comparable across organisations and villages in Iraq. IMMAP will support partners by providing programmatically relevant, timely, and reliable raw and analysed village-level data and contextual analysis.

How is your project accountable to affected population

iMMAP is committed to the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) IASC policies and operational framework and guidelines. iMMAP research and analysis programming is directly implemented at field level and accountable to affected populations.iMMAP is committed to incorporating the principals meeting endorsed five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into the policies and operational guidelines and to promoting them with operational partners, within Humanitarian Country Teams and amongst cluster members.These commitments are:Leadership/GovernanceTransparencyFeedback and ComplaintsParticipationDesign Monitoring and EvaluationBuilding on previous targeting and selection criteria, iMMAP, in close coordination with the national protection cluster (NPC) partners will conduct and design an in-depth desk review of existing data and information as well as best-practices for vulnerability criteria in similar settings. This according to the accountability to affected populations (AAP) and operational framework objectives.iMMAP proposes a draft vulnerability-based targeting criteria methodology to identify potential key information gaps impeding current research and analysis and AAP supporting program implementation. Pending successful completion of said assessment, draft vulnerability-based AAP sensitive targeting criteria recommendations will be finalized based on assessment findings.During the above-mentioned process, iMMAP will work closely with NPC partners to ensure that the assessments and analysis meet specific targeting goals and works off the lessons learned from previous assessments to best inform and communicate with affected populations. Refining current tools and methodologies should bolster aid and targeting modalities looking into 2019 and based upon objective number three of the AAP framework (to ensure accountability to affected populations and effectively integrate within systems for planning needs assessments and response).As such, and keeping in mind duty of care, potential assessment fatigue, and the collection of sensitive information within the community, the below tools are suggested:PMI Data collection consists of village level information sources, (both key informant and household interviews) using conflict sensitive questionnaires via a KoBo mobile primary data collection tool when applicable, secondary sources, media and open source. PMI Contextual Analysis. The purpose of area-based contextual analysis is to provide responders with a holistic and comprehensive understanding of the areas in which their interventions occur. Geographic contextual analysis utilizes a systems-based approach to explore and analyze the most significant functions and dynamics within an area, based upon the following key factors: security, economy, governance (politics), and general conflict events that enable better targeting of interventions for the needs of the most vulnerable.Development/Harmonization of transparent and AAP sensitive Data Collection Tools and MethodologyiMMAP, in close coordination with the NPC and incorporating objective numbers four and five of the AAP framework, will design agreed upon or harmonized questionnaires and methodologies that all contributing partners can use if amenable to collect and submit population monitoring and protection environment related data. This effort takes into consideration the information gathered in a partner capacity assessment and to strike a balance between sector information needs, the reality on the ground in terms of humanitarian and partners’ existing data collection practices and risks, geographic reach and monitoring, and evaluating current and future interventions.
