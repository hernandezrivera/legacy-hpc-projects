Project Summary

The Overall Objective of the intervention is to strengthen protection and resilience of vulnerable migrants and migrants at risk, refugees and host communities (including IDPs and returnees) in Libya. This action builds upon the previous programming of EU Trust Fund for Africa (T05- EUTF-NOA-LY-03) and the EIDHR-funded intervention (NEAR-TS/2018/391-272) and further seeks to respond to the specific needs of GBV survivors while trying to reduce and mitigate the GBV risks faced by women, girls, boys and men in Misrata.

1 - Needs

Currently, TB is diagnosed and treated in only 5 Specialty TB Hospitals: Abo-Setaa Hospital and Misrata Hospital in the Western Region, Shahat Hospital and Al-Koafia Hospital in the Eastern Region, and Sebha Hospital in the Southern Region. NCDC/NTP is currently supported by WHO and MSF, but there is a need for more support to expand the technical and operational capacity in term of decentralisation of TB Care and to expand isolation capacity and specialized health Information Systems (TB registers). MSF-France currently manages a high capacity TB hospital in Misrata and is actively increasing its capacity to deal with the spectre of a COVID-19 outbreak in Libya. However, Tripoli remains woefully ill equipped to handle the high numbers of TB patients present in Libya’s largest urban area. The NCDC and the MoH have also reached out to IMC for assistance in preparing for potential outbreaks of COVID-19 in the Tripoli metropolitan area. To fill this need, IMC proposes to work closely with the NCDC to provide TB diagnosis and treatment in 2 primary healthcare centers (PHCS) in Tripoli (Gargarish PHC and Abshosha PHC). Both of these facilities are in areas frequented by migrants and refugees, the most at-risk populations for TB in Libya. IMC will also be equipping six isolation rooms in a hospital to be selected by the NCDC. These isolation rooms will also increase the NCDC’s capacity to handle outbreaks of COVID-19, because at present there is severely limited isolation capacity in Tripoli. Over the past 5 years International Medical Corps (IMC) has had a very effective open channel with NCDC in terms of identification, follow-up, and medication provision mainly to migrants TB patients in Detention Centres and in UNHCR-supported Community Day Centre. IMC receives support and guidance from the NCDC focal point related to steps of patient follow-up, medication provision, dose adjustment, and alternative management plans, while simultaneously providing the NCDC TB focal point with information and updates on all of the TB cases under our supervision at all the remote DCs we provide services. On many occasions, IMC has collaborated with the NCDC in TB screening campaigns in both remote and urban areas. Through these activities, large numbers of migrants have been diagnosed with TB and treated by IMC under NCDC guidance. In the urban areas, migrants and vulnerable Libyans live in extremely poor conditions; they are particularly marginalized, due to their lack of access to basic services, which drive them into situations of isolation, lack of hope, and radicalization, exposing them to the risk of being forced or coerced into joining armed groups or militias . Therefore, the outbreak of conflict and deteriorated instability within the country have caused new displacements among Libyans and non-Libyans who have been forced to move from their homes, with highly exposure to serious protection and health risks, and challenges to access basic goods and commodities.Finding proper housing for refugees and migrants is extremely challenging with the vast majority of migrants and refugees experiencing difficulties to afford safe, dignified and private settings. Moreover, refugees and migrants displaced due to the hostilities in and out Tripoli, including those released or escaped from official DCs, have mainly found refuge with fellow migrants and refugees in communities, adding to the burden of the hosts, and increasing the need for financial and shelter assistance.

2 - Activities

IMC will increase awareness among the general public, as well as clinicians at primary and secondary heath care levels on subjects related to TB detection and management. Awareness activities will be implemented in migrant communities through CHWs living in the areas close to the two supported PHCs in Gargarish and Abshosha neighborhoods of Tripoli. Health personnel will be reached through regular visits to health facilities in which IMC doctors and nurses will work directly with healthcare professionals working in the national healthcare system. IMC will rehabilitate and furnish 6 isolation rooms in Tripoli for patients with Open TB and MDR-TB in close cooperation with the NCDC’s NTP. These rooms will follow WHO standards, and will be equipped with all necessary medical equipment. The renovation of TB isolation centres will help establish a referral pathway for secondary health care in case of need. Identified Open TB and MDR-TB patients will be referred to the renovated TB isolation rooms in Tripoli. To properly set up the referral pathway, IMC will recruit one TB medical doctor, two nurses, two lab technicians, and one medical coordinator (TB specialist) who will lead TB treatment trainings. IMC will train the health personnel working within the supported health facilities on the proper management and treatment of TB patients. All training and treatment activities will follow the national TB treatment protocol. Moreover, the TB medical doctor, nurses, and lab technicians will lead, monitor and support the operations of the supported health facilities. IMC’s psychiatrist will also refer patients in need of continued psychiatric care to Misrata Emergency Hospital when needed. This referral component will ensure that the project is able to offer a quality mental health intervention which is multi-layered and integrated along the IASC spectrum for MHPSS in emergency settings.
