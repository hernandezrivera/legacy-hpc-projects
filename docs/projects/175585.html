Project Summary

To address the immediate risks exposed by insecurity to displaced children in the districts of Ancuabe, Montepuez and Chuire Street Child will provide MHPSS through community based activities alongside case management, training of foster carers for UASC and IGA grants for the most vulnerable families, providing independence to respond to needs and prevent risks to children.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

To address the immediate risks exposed to displaced children and in response to the above issues and priorities identified, Kulima, with the support of Street Child will target 3750 children and 700 caregivers in the districts of Ancuabe, Montepuez and Chuire. 3000 children will receive MHPSS through community based activities, with measures put in place for remote facilitation if COVID-19 restrictions continue. This will include contact via telephone for one to one sessions, alongside provision of tools for recreational activities that can be completed at home between caregiver and child, this includes storybooks relating to COVID-19. 750 children will receive case management services with a focus on UASC, child headed households, victims of GBV and those identified through psychological first aid as needing additional support. 400 of the most vulnerable families will be identified to receive support in developing IGA’s, providing independence to respond to needs and prevent risks to children. In addition to this 300 foster carers will receive training on alternative care, including positive parenting practices and child rights, to address the needs of USAC. Wider community engagement will also take place to build local knowledge on child protection risks and strengthen protection mechanisms.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

• MHPSS activities for 5000 children. Trained facilitators will deliver activities focused on knowledge of how to deal with stressful and traumatic situations alongside building life skills in areas such as leadership, problem solving and decision making. Facilitators will also deliver psychological first aid, assessing needs and concerns of children and referring them for case management services as required. • Provision of Case Management Services for 750 children. Case workers and government social workers will be trained in delivery of services, with mapping of available services and referral to be made at baseline. Target children for case management services will be UASC, child headed households, victims of GBV and those identified through psychological first aid as needing additional support. • 300 foster carer trained. Training on alternative care, including positive parenting practices and child rights, to address the needs of USAC and reduction in use of institutional care facilities. • IGA’s for 400 Most Vulnerable Caregivers. Caregivers will receive a one off grant to start a small IGA, with provision of workshops to build capacity to improve business and financial management. Continued mentoring will be provided to support the IGA to grow alongside activation of a 12 week savings scheme. The overall aim of the IGA’s is to provide families with a more stable sustainable income in order to improve the overall wellbeing of the household and build resilience to minimise the impact of life crises. • Strengthening of Community Mechanisms. Community advocacy and awareness raising will be conducted to build local knowledge on child rights and child protection risks to strengthen community child protection mechanisms.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

All staff working on this project, including implementing partners, are required to have received training on safeguarding and child protection and adhere to organisation policy prior to conducting any field visits or contact with beneficiaries. In turn beneficiaries will have access to a complaints and feedback mechanism, with promotion of the ‘linha verde’ line. Street Child has extensive experience of delivering child protection in emergency projects, working in protracted crises through operations in DRC, Afghanistan, Cameroon and North East Nigeria. Facilitators of case management and PSS and PFA will be trained to a high standard, and align with the do no harm principle. Available services and referral mechanisms will be identified prior to the project starting. The project components and activities will be delivered through coordination with community leaders and engagement with community ensuring transparencey and accountability, with a partner focal point identified for complaints and feedback.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

In May 2020, Street Child conducted a rapid needs assessments, including 4 districts of Cabo Delgado, Metuge, Montepuez, Ancuabe and Mecufi, incorporating host communities. The results showed that 28% of respondents did not not know of any available protection service they had access to, while only 6% reported being aware of toll free hotelines, such as Linda Verde and 10% of having access to case management services. In addition to this, only 2% reported any provision of psychosocial support services. Initial community engagement with target areas will take place to introduce the projects. Community leaders and key community members will also be consulted to finalise the vulnerability criteria for selection of beneficiaries for the project. Each community will also identify a preferred complaints and reporting mechanism that will be located in each project area, either through face to face reporting or a complaints box that will be checked on a weekly basis. Baseline assessments will also include a mapping of available service and referral mechanisms in the areas.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Children and caregivers with disabilities will be identified through baseline assessments, engagement with district departments and key communities members. The baseline will be developed modelled on the Washington group tool, to ensure that necessary data to captured for equitable access to support. All beneficiaries will be selected through a vulnerability criteria ensuring that the most vulnerable and marginalised are targeted as part of this project.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

In the short term caregivers receiving an IGA grant will be encouraged to start up viable businesses that are not detrimental to the environment, such as coal and firewood. Business training will also focus on incorporating disaster risk reduction strategies into development of business plans, and the promotion of resilience and sustainability. In the long term, improved household income impacts on positive child wellbeing and access and retention within education. This in turn increase opportunities of the child and increases chances of breaking the generational poverty cycle, reducing child exposure to negative environmental hazards and educating households on reducing environmental impacts, such as polluted water and risks caused by natural diasters.
