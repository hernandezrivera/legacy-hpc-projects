Project Summary

UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency, is responsible for ensuring the Protection of the refugees. Therefore, Protection is at the centre of all activities. For addressing the needs of the Rohingya refugee and the host community, the project will have three dimensions- 1. skills development for the Refugee youths for preparing them for repatriation and reintegration; 2. creating some provision for wage employment while staying in Bangladesh, and 3. household food & nutrition security, and self-reliance of the poorest and vulnerable households of both the host and refugee communities.
For the skills development, around 1600 youths and adolescents (15 to 24) will receive technical skills training with a recognition certificate to allow them to engage in technical works after their repatriation. The project will promote self-reliance of the host and refugee communities with selected youth female beneficiaries who will receive skill training on the targeted trade and will be engaged with wage-earning opportunities, e.g. jute bag production, cloth making, mask, and sanitary pad. Under household food and nutritional security, the targeted households will get support for food production in their homestead with innovative multi-layer technologies. The activity will cover climate-smart vegetable and fruit production in the homestead with low irrigation and climate-sensitive off-farm interventions.
In both communities, more than 50% of the targeted beneficiaries will be female. The project will work for empowering the women through engaging them in skills development, production, wage-earning and developing market linkage. Women empowerment will reduce the risk of being victims of GBV incidents and increase their decision-making participation. Overall, the project will raise awareness of the local institutions and communities for improving social cohesion. The project will integrate with other sectors of UNHCR to ensure gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, and Protection. The project does not contain any direct DRR and environmental conservation activities, but it will coordinate with Field and Site Management (DRR inclusion) and Energy and Environment Units for better integration. The trades and interventions will be promoted for addressing climate-smart and green growth.
UNHCR emphasises the need for synergy between livelihoods and other sectors to bolster livelihood interventions and support the host community. Also, the key is the collaboration with other UN agencies, the World Bank, the Asian Development Bank, and stakeholders (national and international NGOs) to ensure effective use of resources and support advocacy. The refugee crisis represents multiple challenges that require a comprehensive solutions strategy. While efforts to create conditions in Myanmar for safe, dignified and sustainable return will continue, their humanitarian needs, rights and well-being must be met in Bangladesh. It will also be essential to address the needs of host communities. UNHCR will continue dialogue with the Government of Bangladesh on durable solutions and will take a comprehensive and balanced approach that benefits both refugees and host communities. To address the Complaint, Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), UNHCR has a separate entity to capture feedback, complaints, and suggestions as part of the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP). The project will adapt learning from the community and improve the intervention.

1 - Identified Needs

The refugee population in camps remains highly reliant on humanitarian assistance with limited access to self-reliance activities (J-MSNA 2021). Only 30% of the refugee population have access to self-reliance activities lower than the 2020 (41%). The impact of the COVID 19 pandemic and associated preventative measures reversed the trend of food security/livelihoods observed in the 2020 J-MSNA. In the host community, J-MSNA revealed that Food Consumption Scores deteriorated compared to 2020, and 70% of the households adopted livelihoods-based coping strategies, such as reducing essential non-food expenditures, selling productive assets, means of transport, jewellery/gold or household assets. In addition, Cox's Bazar District remains one of the poorest in Bangladesh, making it highly susceptible to environmental and economic shocks such as cyclones, monsoons, and COVID-19/ health pandemics. GoB states that 17% of people live below the poverty line across Cox's Bazar on average, and 57% of poor individuals do not receive safety net support (GoB, December 2019). Shortages and unavailability of agricultural inputs due to supply chain disruptions and movement restrictions amid the lockdown lead to a hike in the cost of inputs and shortages of labour (FAO, June 2020).Due to the COVID-19 restriction, 37% of the host community households reported access to food and income-generating activities among their top 3 priority needs. While 72% of households in the camps claimed unmet needs in Food security/livelihoods. Households without access to self-reliance activities have higher unmet needs than those who have access. They were more likely to have worse food consumption scores and livelihoods-based coping strategies. The scenario is worse for the female-headed households than the male-headed households.
