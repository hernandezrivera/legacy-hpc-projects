Project Summary

UNESCO proposed action aims to reduce the vulnerability of young people and women affected by the crisis, through supporting the creation and protection of jobs, small businesses for a better recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic. Women and youth are most at risk of coronavirus infection due to the types of economic activities that occupy them on a daily basis. The aim is to help them reinforce their socio-economic resilience, boost life skills and competencies and to access and seize job viable opportunities in sectors that provide them with income by also enabling them to combact the spread of the pandemic. In Cameroon, the spread of COVID-19, has critically exacerbated structural socio-economic vulnerabilities and crisis impacts on communities and vulnerable groups, in particular women and youth, along the three major crises – and particularly the Far North region, seriously impacted by the Boko Haram crisis in and around the Lake Chad Basin. Such disruption affects all core aspects of societal fabrics and economic life and has immediate effects on gender inequalities, in terms of livelihood, lifeskills and economic opportunities, especially for those women who remain heads of households, often with little access to education. The effects of the pandemic are also already visible in terms of steady growing of violence in households and communities, gender-based violence (GBV) and early pregnancy, further reducing the ability of these women to cope with the socio-economic consequences of the pandemic.
The project further integrates innovative components on literacy, life-skills, entrepreneurship, advocacy and Community Violence Reduction (CVR), and engagement mechanisms to support social cohesion and COVID-19 life-saving messages. The project interventions will also capitalize on the achievement of UNESCO launched initiatives with regard to peace and stabilization, among others: the UNESCO-CEIDES preliminary survey in the Far North on the status of 83 TVET community training centers (31 SAR/SM under MINEFOP and 54 CMPJ centers under MINJEC, March 2020), the reinforcement of digital learning platforms and TVET training for women & youth, based on tailored programmes; Lifeskills training/awareness for vulnerable youth and women (including bioethics and Ethics of Science and Technology and COVID19 prevention messages). Improved access to digital skills and training on self employment/on line jobs platform and economic empowerment will be provided to enable the most vulnerable women and young people to benefit from new jobs opportunities, income from their activities, in most viable cooperatives and small businesses, during and after the pandemic; Making and popularizing programs/messages to strengthen community cohesion, promote peaceful coexistence and initiate early warning systems on a community level.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon continues to be negatively impacted by the Boko Haram violence in the Lake Chad Basin. Various reports indicate an estimated 527,000 persons have been displaced as a result of the conflict and violence and continue to face significant trauma and protection risks. In the Far North, the Boko Haram insurgency, compounded by chronic vulnerability, the growing impact of climate change, and COVID-19 have left more than 1.2 million people in need of urgent assistance, with the majority being women, adolescent girls and children (HNO 2020). Beginning of 2019, the Far North region saw a sharpupsurge in violence and the ongoing hostilities have displaced 527,000 people (112,000 refugees, 6 297,000 IDPs and 117,000 returnees and continue to steadily push more people to flee their villages (HNO Report 2020). The number of IDPs increased from 228,000 in October 2018 to 297,000 in December 2019. While girls and women are particularly vulnerable due to the combined effect of gender discrimination and socioeconomic vulnerability caused by the crisis, leading to specific threats against them (in particular gender-based violence), boys and men are more exposed to arbitrary arrests, forced recruitment and physical violence. Both genders therefore live with psychosocial trauma related to violence, including sexual violence and rape. In the Far North, population movement has resulted in an increased demand on basic social services and sustainable livelihoods. Returning populations require economic assistance to facilitate their reintegration. The highest concentration of people in need of ER interventions appears to be in Logone and Chari in the Far North region (344 136 people), followed by Mayo Sava in the Far North region (246 943 people), Mayo Tsanaga (256 236 people). Initial analysis of the collected data shows that the need for jobs and emergency employment is most severe in Far North region, where almost nine out of ten people are in need of jobs, followed by the North region, Est region and Adamaoua. Conclusively, the Far North continues to face recovery challenges that include: (1) sustained and enhanced service delivery and basic community infrastructure; (2) creation and stabilisation of basic livelihoods and social protection for socio-economic recovery; (3) promotion of social cohesion and communities’ engagement to strengthen the resilience of affected people and mitigate the effects of the protracted crisis.
