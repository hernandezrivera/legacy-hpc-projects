Project Summary

Due to a lack of access, transparency, the difficulties to monitor, and given that, according to international law, individuals under detention in a country must be provided with the means to live with dignity by the government of this country, WFP, along with the partners of the Food Security Sector, took the decision not to intervene in detention in order not to legitimize detention and provide assistance blindly where access and monitoring is impossible.
Consequently, alternative forms of assistance to vulnerable migrants have been developed and this project, targeting food insecure migrants outside detention centers, has been designed to support the food security and nutrition status of migrants while preserving them from any form of exposure, stigmatization and discriminations, ensuring their protection and promoting their inclusion within the local communities.
WFP and IOM have built their functioning partnership since November 2019 to provide food assistance to vulnerable migrants in Libya based on analyzed data. The most food insecure non-detained migrants were identified in close partnership with IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Unit and Migrant Resource and Response Mechanism (MRRM) Unit. Based on the built success, under the 2022 HRP - Food Security Sector, WFP is looking to continuously work with IOM to maximise the impacts, outputs and outcomes of the food assistance project for vulnerable non-detained migrant individuals in urban settings.
COVID-19 precautionary measures such as masks, disinfection, social distancing, temperature checks will be systematically applied to the distribution.
To enhance the quality and the impact of the assistance, building on the successful collaboration between IOM and WFP, including WFP-IOM joint-publication on Migrant Food Security in Libya, WFP looks to strengthen the collaboration with IOM in a joint analysis of IOM's face-to-face flow monitoring surveys on food security WFP’s web-based Migration Pulse surveys. Over half of migrants reported having been worried about not having enough food to eat. Results from face-to-face interviews showed that a third of migrants were found to have poor and borderline food consumption, compared to 12 per cent of Libyan citizens. Similarly, web-based surveys found that 33 per cent of migrants and 12 per cent of citizens reported having to compromise their food consumption by eating only one meal. Food coping strategies were adopted by 57 per cent of migrants due to lack of food or means to buy food. Most concerningly, one in four migrants reported having gone a whole day without eating in the past month.
