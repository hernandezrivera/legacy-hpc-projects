Project Summary

Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are some of the main shocks that drive displacement and largely affect at least 2.9 million internally displaced persons in Somalia. The majority of displaced people have self-settled in over 2,400 sub-standard and unplanned IDP sites in urban, semi-urban and rural areas across the country as reported humanitarian Programme Cycle 2022 issued October 2021. Over 56 per cent of all evictions occurred within the Kahda and Daynile districts in Banadir which accommodate some 1,808 IDP sites or 848,760 IDPs.290 Furthermore, 11 per cent of households in IDP settlements (17 per cent of Banadir IDPs) believe that they are currently at risk of being evicted from their shelters.291 Inaccessibility to land tenure security continues to function as a primary obstacle preventing IDPs from establishing permanent settlements. This project will establish capacity of the community focal points and understanding of the basic principles of CCCM and support conflict management, risk mitigations, site management, and de-congestion. a 3 days' training will be organized for 60 ( 30 Daynile, 30 Kaxda) participants from the IDP camps in Kahda and Daynile districts of the Banadir region. SCC will work closely with the camp management committees to improve the quality of life, safety, and dignity of displaced people through coordination activities. At the site level, coordination mechanisms will be developed/strengthened involving local authorities, active partners, CMCs in the project sites. SCC will conduct 4 quarterly coordination meetings that each will attend for 30 participants from the camp management committee, local authority, and active implementing partners in Kada.
The total participants for the coordination meetings will be 120 persons. In the meetings, the participants will discuss and identify humanitarian gaps, mobilize resources, respond and minimize overlaps if there is any. SCC will implement CCCM project for 24 IDP sites in Kahda and Daynile districts of Banadir region with over 30,000HHs as per the latest detailed site assessment (DSA), targeting a largely undeserved area. Approximately 36000 IDPs (6000 households) will benefit from this proposed intervention, in addition to the community working in these sites including local authorities, clusters, and other NGOs. These numbers might vary due to external factors, which might attract more IDPs, excessive flooding, or conflict with the landowners or the authorities, which may cause IDPs to flee to other areas. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys, and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age, and diversity dis-segregated data. SCC will implement this intervention in the Kahda district of Banadir region targeting 6000 HHS. Kaxda District is one of the IDP major hubs in Mogadishu that hosts the largest number of IDPs. Throughout the project, the needs of women, girls, men, and boys of all ages will be addressed throughout the planning, implementation and monitoring, and evaluation of the activities. Through regular site monitoring, needs and gaps will be reported and shared with partners and all stakeholders. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to contend with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. Through this project, SCC will also address the current and newly identified needs by setting up camp coordination structures at the national and, where necessary, regional or district levels, facilitating and supporting the establishment of effective coordination and management in IDP sites while respecting national context and authorities. Currently, SCC is implementing CCCM program with SHF Somalia in Kaxda targeting 16000 (60% women) providing CCCM services.

1. - Projects activities

This project will establish capacity of the community focal points and understanding of the basic principles of CCCM and support conflict management, risk mitigations, site management, and decongestion. a 3 days' training will be organized for 60 ( 30 Daynile, 30 Kaxda) participants from the IDP camps in Kahda and Daynile districts of Banadir region. SCC will work closely with the camp management committees to improve the quality of life, safety, and dignity of displaced people through coordination activities. At the site level, coordination mechanisms will be developed/strengthened involving local authorities, active partners, CMCs in the project sites. SCC will conduct 4 quarterly coordination meetings that each will attend for 30 participants from the camp management committee, local authority, and active implementing partners in Kada and Daynile. The total participants for the coordination meetings will be 120 persons. In the meetings, the participants will discuss and identify humanitarian gaps, mobilize resources, respond and minimize overlaps if there is any. SCC will train 16 (8 Daynile, 8 Kaxda) site mobilizers on core principles of protection and the humanitarian context in Somalia, awareness-raising skills, eviction monitoring skills, and complaint feedback mechanism and referral pathway for three days. SCC will also 4 days’ Workshop for implementing partners (IPs) and local authorities (LAs) in Kahda and Daynile will be conducted. The core training subjects will be included: basic principles of CCCM, humanitarian principles, GBV risk mitigations, and Complaint feedback mechanism, and referral pathways. 30 participants will be invited from two locations. SCC will engage the trained mobilizers to monitor evictions, conduct behavior change awareness-raising sessions, coordinate field-level complaint mechanisms and provide emergency referrals. The engaged community mobilizers will closely work with the CMCs and direct report to the CFM focal point and project officer. The CfW workers will receive 3 days' training in improving the site's infrastructures, cleanliness, garbage collection, and waste management, and delivery of services to improve living conditions within the IDP sites. Training will include personal safety, a basic concept on personal safety to be respected while implementing activities and basic site planning principles, and expansion of the sites. Throughout project implementation, SCC will conduct quarterly safety audit exercises in Kaxda and Daynile districts targeting 24 IDP sites and produce a report summarizing the findings that will be shared with partners and the cluster. The safety audit will be conducted through a mixed methodology of observations and key informant interviews, FGDs using CCCM safety audit tools. The results of the safety audit will be presented through workshops to the partners. A 4 days' workshop on fire prevention to minimize the protection risks and ensure safety in the project sites will be conducted. 44 participants from various sites in Kahda and Daynile districts will participate in the training on fire prevention and life-saving skills. the aim of this training is to establish fire wardens to minimize the fire risks and save lives. SCC will establish 26 complaint feedback mechanisms (CFMs) for 24 IDP site levels. SCC ensures its functionality and accessibility to people with disabilities. SCC will also establish a complaint box in each site, Stickers for the complaint procedures will be printed and disseminated across the project sites and SCC will put in place a toll-free hotline number and operationalize. CFM awareness-raising sessions will be reached to 1000 members from the 20 sites (each 50). Means of verification, Monthly CFM report. 3 days’ workshop on safety audit will be conducted for 18 enumerators (9 women and 9 men) on CCCM data collection tool, method of reporting, and presentation to data for partners and local authorities

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

As mitigation measures of risk analysis, SCC will ensure CCCM community engagement of all groups as a key requirement in the response mobilization phase and ensuring that marginalized groups are by default among the prioritized groups. CCCM cluster proposed also to include clear strategies for reaching marginalized communities in all the program partnership documents with implementing partners. The project will be inclusive consultations with other partners in the target locations and different groups of households, mentioned reinforced complaint and feedback mechanisms to identify any situation of exclusion, and to condition implementation on equal and direct access to all communities. This is particularly relevant to IDP setups where informal settlement managers often control access to communities and influence the beneficiary selection process.
