Project Summary

The proposed project aims to upgrade 2 governmental schools (Basher Al Raess Secondary girl school and Al Qahera elementary girl school) in Gaza city, to serve as Design Emergency Shelter (DES) for people fleeing their homes due to security concerns or due to a lack of services in their original residence. After the emergency phase, the schools will be converted into Collective Centers, if needed. The total number of IDPs (internal displaced people) that can be hosted in the two governmental schools is estimated to be 1,564 (415 girls, 432 boys, 355 women and 362 men).
The intervention will contribute to achieve the Objective 3 of the HRP for the Shelter cluster: Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased
The main activities and outputs of the proposed project will be as follows:
• The WASH components of the intervention are designed to ensure access to clean and safe water, sanitation and solid waste facilities in each shelter which will include - but not be limited to: rehabilitation of existing sanitation facilities, establishment of additional sanitation & hygiene facilities; construction of water wells and brackish water desalination plants with a capacity off 10 to 20 m3/day; procuring and deploying standby generators, domestic and drinking water storage tanks, solid waste containers, and laundry facilities. Toilets and showers will be provided in sufficient numbers for both females and males.
• The shelter components will include maintenance works for the existing buildings, lightning, external ramps, extinguishers, installation of laminated film on windows for safety, and fabricated temporary partitioning to ensure privacy for women and girls.
• The food security sector intervention will include provision of stoves and protected gas cylinders to allow for preparation of light meals for babies and children.
The targeted schools were prioritized according to agreed-upon criteria, including but not limited to, hosting capacity, budget and geographical distribution. The final outputs (scope of work, budget, design & project time frame) were presented to the ICCG and shared with stakeholders, including PWA, CMWU, MoSA & MoEHE. The total number of beneficiaries is estimated to be 22,500 IDPs in the 32 schools with an average of 703 IDPs per school.

Needs

Due to the 2014 conflict, almost 485,000 people were internally displaced. During the conflict, some of 285,000 IDPs were hosted in 93 UNRWA schools and around 48,000 IDPs in 24 PA schools (excluding informal shelters). However, Gaza lacks well-equipped and safe formal shelters. Based on the review of the 2014 intervention through the Emergency Operations Center, and further lessons learned through discussions by the Gaza inter-cluster coordination group (ICCG) and all clusters, a number of alternative shelter solutions have been explored. The conclusion was – in coordination with MoEHE and MoSA – that schools are the most feasible option for IDPs in Gaza.The project will address the needs through hard and soft component as follows:1- Hard component: identify and upgrade schools to be used as DES.2- Soft component: develop sector-specific standard operating procedures (SoPs) & management tools for the operation of DES in a gender sensitive manner, integrating GBV prevention & protection standards and provide training to shelter managers. Engagement with IDPs (men and women) in the shelter management and decision-making to ensure equal access to humanitarian assistance, registration and access to information (most of the soft component activities are under implementation).The action aims at upgrading 2 governmental schools to serve as DES during emergencies, targeting people fleeing their homes due to security concerns or due to a lack of services in their original residence. After the emergency phase, the schools will be converted into Collective Centers, if needed. HNO 2018 addresses the needs of IDPs as a priority, including safe shelters to guarantee enhanced protection during a crisis and meeting their basic humanitarian needs.The proposed interventions will ensure the mainstreaming of gender principles during all phases, since according to UNFPA it is well established that the impact of displacement is highly gendered, for example in terms of security, mobility and economic engagement, and in terms of women’s roles as caregivers to their families and communities,.The project idea has been built on the needs of IDPs to have safe and sufficient place during emergencies, with all the needed requirements, like access to WASH and shelter facilities, especially for women and children, and keep their health and hygiene condition safe. The interventions will also support people to have the needed amount of water for hygiene tasks as well as for drinking and cooking purposes, supporting women who are the main responsible of the mentioned tasks. Supporting the shelter with sufficient number of toilets and showers for males and females, including with the needed privacy, will ensure the good hygiene conditions, especially protecting vulnerable children from diseases. It’s important to mention that these shelters usually receive a lot of people in the emergency situation, so any lack of hygiene levels, will put all the people in risk due to the possibility of the spread of epidemics.Led by OCHA/IDP working group in cooperation with UNRWA, MoSD, MoEHE and the relevant clusters, selection criteria were set and an initial list of 65 schools was presented by MoEHE. Out of this list, 32 schools were selected, based on five additional criteria Location, Capacity, Access, Existing facilities/infrastructure and Security/safety.A multi-sectorial technical task force (TTF), including Oxfam, AAH, NRC, PUI, UNICEF, GVC, UNDP and others, in cooperation with the relevant authorities assessed each school in April-May 2016. Activities

The proposed activity, in line with the HRP priorities in the shelter sector, is the Preparedness and upgrading of DESIn close coordination among the partners of the TTF (ACF, Oxfam, UNICEF, PUI, NRC, GVC and UNDP), the proposed intervention will cover WASH, Shelter, food security, and energy components for 2 schools to meet the needs as outlined in the assessment by OCHA/IDP working group, clusters and humanitarian agencies (Humanity and Inclusion - Handicap international, Islamic Relief, GVC, NRC, Oxfam, ACF and PUI) in cooperation with UN agencies (UNRWA, UNICEF, UN-Women, UNDP and WFP) who provided technical advice to ensure that the design of the DES guarantees the safety of displaced children, boys, girls, women and men.• The WASH components of the intervention are designed to ensure access to clean and safe water, sanitation and solid waste facilities in each shelter which will include, but not be limited to: rehabilitation of existing sanitation facilities, establishment of additional sanitation and hygiene facilities; construction of water wells and brackish water desalination plants with a capacity off 10 to 20 m3/day; procuring and deploying standby generators, domestic and drinking water storage tanks, solid waste containers, and laundry facilities. Toilets and showers will be provided in sufficient numbers for both females and males.• The shelter components will include maintenance works for the existing buildings, lightning, external ramps, extinguishers, installation of laminated film on windows for safety, and fabricated temporary partitioning to ensure privacy for women and girls.• The food security sector intervention will include provision of stoves and protected gas cylinders to allow for preparation of light meals for babies and children. All shelter key equipment and hardware, including backup generators, will be protected by security fencing for children safety and to protect against unauthorized access. Harsh climate conditions during summer and winter will be addressed during the upgrading as much as applicable. GVC is proposing to rehabilitate 2 governmental schools (Basher Al Raess Secondary girl school and Al Qahera elementary girl school). The total number of beneficiaries in the two governmental schools is estimated at 1,564 IDPs with an average of 782 IDPs per school.Regarding the accountability, implementing organizations will maintain ongoing discussions with all concerned stakeholders, including but not limited to potential beneficiary households, the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), the Palestinian Civil Defense, and Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE), as well as the relevant water authorities, in order to ensure that the proposed activities are implemented properly taking into consideration the real needs of the affected population. Consultations are also held with the management teams of the designated emergency shelters. Furthermore, each organization will implement its own feedback and complaint mechanism to collect any complaints or suggestions regarding the implemented activities. All implementing partners are considering communication with community elements in the upgrading process. Partners met with the affected people as a result of the recent conflict in different occasions and kept them updated through official meetings with their representative who attended different bilateral meetings or in the IDP WG meetings lead by OCHA. Neighboring communities adjacent to the DES will be consulted and their concerns will be addressed during the design and upgrading of DES. In addition, partner’s agencies received CwC training carried out by BBC Media Action in coordination with OCHA. The training helped implementing agencies to develop/improve communication strategies and tools during the project phase. Finally, a feedback mechanism is assured which enhances the accountability to beneficiaries.

Indicators

The action will allow to achieve the below indicators:2 schools prepared as DES2 of governmental schools with adequate WASH facilities in DES 1,564 individuals able to be sheltered at DES according to agreed standards (415 girls, 432 boys, 355 women and 362 men). As part of the monitoring system, specific assessments are designed to capture indicators related to the shelter sector, which will be collected giving consideration of the impact on women, children, people with disabilities and the elderly. After the completion of the intervention, an overall evaluation will be conducted, including quantitative data collection (Post Specific surveys) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups (women/girls and men/boys) focusing on Displacements Safety / Dignity and Integrity. The new shelters will ensure: an improvement of the privacy of all family members, especially women and girls, children and elderlies and for vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities and families headed by women). The separation of spaces for boys and girls will allow increased sense of privacy and dignity for men and women. At the beginning of the project, each organization will develop its monitoring and evaluation plan to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, in coordination with the ICCG, IDP working group and clusters. This plan will describe the entire M&E system for the project. Moreover, it will include roles and responsibilities, what forms and tools will be used, and how the data will flow through the organization. In order to follow up on the progress of activities, regular field monitoring visits will be conducted by M&E officers on a weekly basis to follow up on the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. The Monitoring and Evaluation officer will issue a written report summarizing the main findings after each monitoring visit.Interim and final report including updates on the progress of activities and the achievement levels of different indicators will be submitted by the implementing organization to the donor on agreed dates.
