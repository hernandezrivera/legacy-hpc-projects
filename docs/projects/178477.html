Project Summary

The project contributes to sector objectives SO1 and SO2 by providing equitable access for girls' and boys' quality learning opportunities in both primary and secondary school levels in formal and non-formal education settings within a protective environment. Children’s educational needs are being addressed through different projects, locations, and approaches, with flexible education service models (physical, blended, and distance) to improve accessibility and ensure the continuity of education. Violet aims to achieve the overall change by improving student resilience and learning outcomes – specifically, improved resilience for up to 12,772 children (at least 6,396 girls) and 307 CwDs.
• Throughout the implementation, Violet will ensure the children, parents, and communities from the HC, IDPs, and returnee's groups activities and supporting children’s education and wellbeing. Specific attention will be put to enabling girls to participate in education, e.g., through awareness-raising and ensuring access and education plans for children with difficulties—the project targeted areas identified with high needs due to many displaced people communities return.
• Basic access to education will be ensured through rehabilitate the existed classroom in schools and the scope of rehab is windows, doors, walls, floor, and schools’ yards. WASH facilities will also be rehabilitated making sure that the facilities are clean, healthy, both gender (males, females) have easy access to it and adapting it with the disabilities.
• Implement comprehensive health and hygiene protocols and awareness-raising on COVID-19 particularly.
• provision of basic learning materials, whiteboards. Moreover, covering school operational costs to secure the functioning of schools and to create safe and child-friendly environments.
• Non-formal education in the summer program along with formal education will be provided by the evaluation of children's levels to help plan for the effective educational intervention based on the evaluation outcomes, and capture the intervention impact. accordingly, the provision of formal education with complementary remedial and catch-up classes ends by re-entering the students into formal education after a short-term intervention.
• Besides, the quality of education will be improved through contextualized and targeted teacher training and further support to the professional development of teachers (child-centered and protective pedagogy, active learning, life skills, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies (EiE), and national/INEE minimum standards (MS)), with the collaboration of teachers, school management, and relevant education authorities.
• Monthly incentives will be provided to all educational personnel and other staff in both formal and non-formal education facilities.
• Refer vulnerable children to available services in the working area

4 - Needs

Based on HNO for Syria 2021, an estimated 13.4 million people need humanitarian education assistance. 6.9 M (51% female, 97% children, 15% CWDs) need education, according to Multi-Sector Needs Assessment in 2020, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out is almost certain to increase. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible, or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Also, the fluxes of IDPs put additional pressure on the access of public services particularly in affected host communities in North Syria. classes are overcrowded and over one million children required to attend double-shift schools. This reduces the teaching-learning hours per day, adversely affecting learning outcomes. Teaching capacity remains overstretched. Over 140,000 teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts. This shortage, as well as irregular and underpayment of qualified teachers also limits the quality and provision of education. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage for girls and the worst forms of child labor. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war-related psychological trauma for children, their families, and education personnel. the rehab of schools is still a challenge that contributes effectively to increase safe access to schools.

5 - Implementation of activities

Violet aims to implement the following activities to respond to the dire educational needs: • Recruitment and provide monthly incentives, along with the Capacity building to the education workers, and support Teacher in monthly Learning Circles (TLCs).• Enroll students provide formal non-formal education (remedial and catch-up classes.• Provide monthly running costs with essential supplies, teaching and learning materials, hygiene, water supply, winterization, sanitation services.• Provide internet charges to vulnerable beneficiaries and school staff in all locations for distance and blended learning • Perform light rehabilitation and maintenance to schools. • Implement comprehensive health, hygiene, risk mitigation measures protocols, and awareness-raising. • Conduct awareness sessions to parents and children on child protection and about Covid-19.• Promote child safeguarding in learning facilities to prevent harm and abuse to children. Service mapping and referral pathways.• Conduct school safety planning sessions and develop/update School Safety Plans and risk mitigation; provide Safety Equipment (fire extinguisher and first aid kit). • Refer vulnerable children to available service in the working area • Community involvement has been considered an important aspect of the project. In this regard, Violet is carrying out Specific school activities target community participation (Parents Teacher Associations (PTAs), Back to Learning Campaigns (BTL), Awareness-raising Sessions) to get their opinions on the project activities to ensure their participation.• The MEAL team conducts a PDM after each kit distribution, establishes an active accountability mechanism. Conduct Focus Group Discussion FGDs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Specific vulnerable groups/individuals are excluded from accessing education and psychological support. Mitigation: CRM mechanisms, participatory approach, and consultation with local stakeholders, conducting community events that help to understand excluded groups especially vulnerable out-of-school displaced children. In addition, Conflict, Sensitive teacher training.• Additional threats to children’s well-being, such as child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups... Violet addresses these wherever possible through holistic approaches to targeting vulnerable communities. Mitigation: Wherever possible Violet integrates FSL programming carried out in the same target areas through the FSL cluster. Programs include Food Vouchers, Cash for Work, Food Basket distributions, and CP program.• Behavior of educational personnel threatening children’s well-being and protection – Mitigation: to ensure accountability and quality, mentors and center staff sign a Code of Conduct (CoC) and participate in a workshop detailing CoC requirements as well-child protection best practices. Training will be also conducted on all educational and Violet staff PSEA policies. Any breach of the CoC will be investigated and result in the disciplinary projects up to and including suspension from the project without pay, and as necessary, termination.• Overstretched education physical infrastructure posing a threat to children’s safety – Mitigation: Violet plans to conduct necessary rehabilitation, which will take into consideration gender, age, and disability. All schools' rehabilitation is conducted in line with INEE standards and schools/learning centers are supported to develop their emergency and safety manuals.

VIOLET trained 450 staff members and 200 Volunteers and built their capacity on how to prevent and respond to SEA concerns and issues. ​Violet Protection and HR staff had attended a ToT for PSEA investigations and reporting and are capable to handle any investigation or PSEA concern, also Violet had recruited 2 PSEA focal points one in Turkey and one in Syria.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk; Inaccessibility to target areas due to insecurity, large-scale military offensives, or change in territorial control with safety and security of staff/beneficiaries being compromised. Mitigation; Violet would triangulate information with available sources, use rigorous monitoring mechanism, suspend; relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; shift program resources to other areas; switch to distance learning, distribution the SLP materials. 2. Risk; Insufficient stipends continue to result in teachers and education personnel absenteeism due to additional employment and/or leaving their jobs to seek higher-paying work.Mitigation; A standardized and harmonized stipend scale that ensures adequate remuneration should reduce turnover, absenteeism, support teachers by other sectors and resources. 3. Risk: COVID 19 cases are still increasing in the targeted location, all project stakeholders and activities will be affected, and the staff will face many obstacles to conduct face-to-face activities.Mitigation: Protection Violet staff will take into consideration all Donor, Partner, Clusters, and Local Authority's recommendations in link with WHO and Health sector guidelines. The number of beneficiaries per session will be decreased, also using the online modality to conduct the activities after discussion and coordination with donor and partner. Violet covers the internet charge to families.4. Risk; students dropping out of schools. M. motivate students through PSS and recreational activities, awareness-raising for children and parents, support with Edu supplies.
