Project Summary

Coalition for Humanity (CH) is proposing this general protection project to meet the immediate protection needs of vulnerable households comprising of women, girls, adolescent children, boys and people with special needs (PSN). The project will build on the strong protection foundation CH has and further strengthen its activities to support people with limited access to lifesaving humanitarian needs in the targeted counties. Overall, a total of 8,000 individuals or 1330 HHs will directly benefit from the project (3,200 women, 2,800 girls, 1,200 men, boys: 800. 15% of the targeted beneficiaries will be people living with disabilities (PWDs)
The project will target vulnerable communities with critical protection needs in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Guit,Pariang and Abienhom of the former Unity state. The project targets Host communities, IDPs and Returnees.
Further breakdown of the beneficiaries based on actual needs will be done at the time of implementation ensuring that priority goes to those beneficiaries with the highest needs.
The project is designed to meet the strategic objective of the humanitarian response plan (HRP 2020) and the Protection cluster objectives as outlined below:
Our project is in line with CL10/CO1 which is to “Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas” and CL10/CO3: that is aimed at “Preventing and to mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience”. The project will further strengthen one of our core activities over the last years as outlined in CL10/CO4: Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response.
We also provide support to range of other projects within Coalition for Humanity such as emergency shelter and Non-food items, health, WASH, Food Security and livelihoods by integrating protection through mainstreaming across various programmes. We also work with other partners and clusters to share our knowledge in protection within the humanitarian context. We closely collaborate and support partners such as Concern worldwide in their Shelter and Non-food items projects to ensure there is strong protection mainstreaming in various project within the counties listed in this project.
The project will meet the immediate lifesaving intervention of the beneficiaries as evidenced in many reports. The humanitarian situation in South Sudan generally and former Unity State in articulately remain dire. The population depends on humanitarian assistance for daily sustenance and therefore this project is of highest priority. These facts are collaborated by our internal CH assessments report and partners as well as reports in IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) placing1, 420,189 as Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) as of March 2019.
The REACH reports describe the situation in South Sudan general and Unity State in particular as a complex humanitarian crisis, restricting humanitarian access and hindering the flow of information required by aid partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to populations in need. More than 50% of the population interviewed report protection concerns such as insecurity lack of access to various, insecurity for girls and women as major concerns. The IPC report released in September has painted very desperate humanitarian situation, which is projected up till the first quarter of 2020. There is likely to be 6.21 million people in need of assistance, which is higher than last year figures. (55.4%) of that requires urgent humanitarian assistance.

1 - Needs

South Sudan in general has been going through multiple crises and former Unity State in particular was worse hit. From the devastating civil conflict that started in December 2013 to the multiple internal communal conflicts, revenge killings, cattle raiding, serious gender basis violence including rape, sexual assault, high level of diseases outbreaks and malnutrition due to broken health system to lack of food and no food markets. The protection environment remains one of the worse when it comes to humanitarian outlook. Most of the population in the former Unity State are either in Protection of civilian sites (POCS) or internally displaced camps in hard to reach areas for safety or outside in the neigbouring countries. While the ceasefire that was declared in August 2018 has largely held, there is no guarantee that peace shall prevail with multiple delays in formation of unity government. Either way, even if all things go smoothly in term of peace implementation, local population shall be faced by multiple challenges when returning to their former homesteads and wanted to rebuild their lives. Therefore humanitarian assistance shall remain the only option in 2020.According to the IPC reports, more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million population in needs are in phase 4 and 5, which is a worrying situation. This information is higher than the projection for 2019. The data above has been collaborated by our internal CH assessment reports and partners in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Pariang, Abiemhnom. According to reports prepared by the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) the number of registered IDPs is at 1, 420,189 (March 2019). Furthermore, the REACH reports describe the situation in South Sudan generally and Unity State in particular as a “complex humanitarian crisis, restricting humanitarian access and hindering the flow of information required by aid partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to populations in need”. More than 50% of the population interviewed reported protection concerns such as insecurity, lack of access to various services, Gender basis violence and insecurity for girls and women as major concerns.These reports confirm dire humanitarian needs and urgent support to the most affected population with limited humanitarian assistance especially for those outside the POCs. From the protection monitoring and assessments by CH both in Panyijiar and Leer Counties, which are entry, points (Tayar and Adok ports) for returnees, over 1000HHs Returnees were recorded in which there is resettlement issue of returnees evolving coupled with high protection risks among IDPs, Returnees and Host communities. Food aid is constrained, shelters and NFIs, WASH become limited while health concern become life threatening as they were rendered non functional due to conflict across most the counties being targeted in this project. A recent Youth Engagement assessment conducted by CH in southern Unity Counties of Leer,Mayendit and Panyijiar confirm disputes over girls has continuously caused killing among boys/youth and this is insinuating by the high involvement of youth in carrying guns. Revenge and rampant killings are the worst fears of the community. CH assessment report 30th Aug-12th Sept, 2019)Besides, the implementations of peace deal where deployment of troops in cantonment sites or barracks near community is heightening tension especially in locations targeted in this project in former Unity state. Examples of these locations include Mirmir in Mayendit, Dingding in Leer. In this case, CH will work closely with partners like UNMISS, Cluster and other protection partners and non-protection partners to ensure potential protection risks mitigation, prioritizing the protection of the civilians and the dignity and rights of women and girls reserved.

2 - Activities

Activities and outcomes.Activity 1.1.0: Establish 6 multi-purpose youth and community friendly spaces targeting 2,000 youth in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Guit,Pariang and Abiemnhom county to offer recreational activities, counseling and rehabilitation to reduce trauma and improve healing from stress PSD.Activity 1.1.1: Capacity building training to 36 PSS facilitators and social workers in the 6 Communities friendly centers in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Guit,Koch, Pariang and Abiemnhom counties Activity 1.1.2: Awareness creation and advocacy on protection risks mitigation, peacebuilding and conflict mitigation, GBV and other bad cultural practices to 30,000 community members in the targeted counties of Leer,Panyijiar,Guit,Pariang and Abiemnhom.Activity 1.1.3: Conduct 36 Protection monthly monitoring and 100 protection assessment weekly reports to document protection concerns and issues for immediate advocacy within CH, PC and other partners including recommendations/ referral pathways.Activity 1.1.4: Support and build capacity of 150 local leaders including women leaders, Police and local administrators to strengthen community based protection mechanisms. This includes discouraging bad cultural norms which fuel domestic violence and biased against women and girls. Activity 1.1.5: Document and support 300 female households and children headed households) IDPs, host communities and returnees by advocating and improving their access to legal and rights to land acquisition and land ownership in line with prevailing legal framework (HLP) Activity 1.1.6: Strengthen internal institutional policies, standards and practices within CH to drive action and accountability on Protection, GBV and Gender equality in humanitarian service delivery as linked to our commitment to Call to Action on Protection from GBVie.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Coalition for Humanity as a humanitarian organization is cognizant of the rights of our beneficiaries and the environment we operate. We recognize and advocate for the human rights in general and especially for vulnerable women, girls and children. We are guided by DO NOT HARM principle across the board and ensure that our staff, consultants and volunteers are guided by our strict code of conduct which do not condone any abuse of authority and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. As part of these efforts we ensure that our beneficiaries are brought on board in all intervention that we do by consulting them and taking their views into consideration. By this they all feel valued, right recognized and become part and parcel of everything that will happen within the communities. We advocate on behalf of our beneficiaries with different actors both government and other oppositions to help reach to the vulnerable households for assistance that they greatly need.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project targets internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities without access to basic service. Priority benefits are channeled to the most affected communities with limited access to other support.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project is linked to the community system with loose connection to government system, as it is emergency humanitarian response only. We however, cooperate with the local authorities, different categories of beneficiaries such as women, girls, boys and people wit special needs to incorporate their views from time to time and part and parcel of the entire response cycle. The project primarily meets the immediate life-saving need of women and girls but tries to provide training and advocacy and awareness to change the bad mindset especially against women and girls.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with Humanitarian response plan, which is national in nature. However, since this is an emergency response, government participation except in provision of security, coordination and facilitation is quiet limited. The project primary beneficiaries include IDPs, returnees and host communities with limited attention to refugees. The project is aligned with Protection objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

CH takes all necessary precautions to ensure that our activities are conducted in manner that does not bring more harm to the affected population. Therefore to do that, we under thorough risk analysis before any intervention to ensure the environment in which we provide humanitarian service is safe for our beneficiaries. While the priority of this project is immediate and emergency, we strive to ensure that it has lasting effects on the community to change their lives in the long term. We work with established project management committees (PMC) and local leaders and beneficiaries to ensure that they views are incorporated and inform the way the project is implemented. This result in maximum benefits to them.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project designed include and the participation of the community. Depending on the project duration, the community are able to secure the sustainability of the ongoing project and take it up for further support to the vulnerable members.The project will build on our emergency work in most of the targeted locationswhere we have in place trained members, Extension workers and other localleaders.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Coalition for Humanity take seriously the risks raised above and as it has done before, we will first consult with affected communities/beneficiaries to continuously map out risks and concerns affecting every different category of people for intervention by coalition for humanity, local authorities and other partners including relevant clusters. Secondly, our planning will be done in manner that risks, perceived or known are identified and measures taken to address them. For instants, distributions are to be done in places or areas where women and girls are not likely to get exposed to related or protection or GBV concerns. Disabled or people with special needs (PSN) needed to be considered when it comes to location of assistance sites, distances traveled, arrangement at the site that is friendly and support everyone according to the condition including lactating and pregnant mothers etc. We take proper risk analysis or assessments of risk before any intervention is carried out to ensure safety of the beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

As a practice, our project staffs carry out periodic focus group discussions and key informant interviews with separate groups (women, girls, boys and men) to find out how they feel about our service and how they would like the project to improve them. Separate FGD for women and girls and one boys and women will be conducted. Some sensitive information where groups might not be comfortable to share will further be obtained through key informant interviews organized privately by our respective staff. In order to make everyone comfortable and free to speak, a thorough mobilization will be carried to explain the background of the exercise so that people will be free to speak and share their experiences without fear of being victimized. The beneficiaries might be illiterate in term of English but some might able to communicate in other languages such as Nuer, Dinka and Arabic. We would encourage those to provide feedback in the language they are comfortable with as we can understand or translate the feedback later on.
