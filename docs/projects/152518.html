DDG will deploy 5 highly mobile Multi Task Teams (MTTs), together with 5 embedded Risk Education (RE) teams to respond to urgent threats to physical safety, dignity, freedom of movement and access to humanitarian assistance within communities. The objectives of the project are to:
1. Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs of the most vulnerable (including women, children, elderly, disabled and survivors of sexual violence in conflict) in hard-to-reach areas.
2. Enhance prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience
3. Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations
Tasks which will support the humanitarian response in the country will be prioritised through the following key activities:
• Non-technical survey (NTS) and technical survey (TS) – to target and prioritise clearance according to humanitarian need, survey will be conducted using a mixture of key informant interviews and household questionnaires held with the local population and authorities. NTS will determine the likely locations and density of ERW contamination in target locations, whilst assessing the impact of these hazards on vulnerable populations in any given location. TS (using detectors) will be used upon discovery of an area with a high concentration of explosive hazards.
• Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) and battle area clearance (BAC) – once NTS has determined the nature of the threat, technical capacities within the teams will be deployed to remove and destroy identified hazards.
• MRE – all teams will contain a capacity to deliver risk messaging raising awareness about how to recognise dangerous items, as well as associated safe practices that should be followed, and high risk behaviour that should be avoided in relation to mines and explosive remnants of war.
The activities outlined in this document will primarily target 90,000 IDPs and host community members living across Upper Nile, Unity and Western Equatoria States, including in hard-to-reach locations where mine/ERW contamination remains substantial. DDG will target these populations with RE, improving their knowledge of the explosive hazards they can encounter, and at the same time collect evidence for clearance tasks. RE will be delivered based on analysis of specific needs of women, men, boys and girls. A total of 17,325 women, 14,175 men, 32,175 girls and 26,325 boys will be targeted by this intervention. Furthermore DDG will, through Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), will identify the major service delivery points which will be a primary target for clearance responses.
This project has broadly been designed to directly benefit the 2019 multisector response in South Sudan. The intervention will be delivered as a protection mainstreaming effort, designed to target survey, clearance and RE towards areas to which population movements are expected, as well as ensuring beneficiaries are able to safely access water points, schools, health facilities and other socio-economic assets being supported / rehabilitated by agencies on the ground. In doing so, DDG will ensure that beneficiaries of other partners are not at undue risk when accessing services.

Needs

Deployment of a flexible Mine Action capacity is urgently required to address multiple challenges presented by South Sudan’s ERW problem, which can be characterised as follows: • As of September 2018, there were 322 hazardous areas / HAs (locations where ERW have been found) and 212 minefields posing a threat to the safety and free movement of civilians across South Sudan. Throughout 2018 to date, 1,706 HAs have been addressed, and a further 117 minefields (IMSMA Monthly Report, September 2018). As ground fighting and shelling have been central features of sustained conflict since 2013, these figures are likely to represent a fraction of South Sudan's entire ERW problem at the national scale. • 44 people have been killed or injured as a result of ERW accidents in 2018 to date. Whilst the majority of these victims are often young boys (45%, IMSMA), people of all ages and gender have been gravely affected by explosive hazards in 2018. For this reason, DDG has ensured a gender balance among those benefitting from its MRE programming in 2018. This approach will strengthen coping mechanisms across all sections of ERW affected communities, whether potential victims or their relatives. • Some of the worst affected areas in recent months are unable to benefit from Mine Action activities. Upper Nile and Unity States have seen some of the most intense periods of fighting since December 2013; conversely, the security situation and resulting limitations on movement mean that mine action support to these areas has been limited. DDG will launch a roving emergency response capacity able to rapidly deploy to where ERW poses a grave threat to protection concerns. • Displaced populations remain particularly vulnerable to the ERW threat: internally displaced people (IDPs) are particularly vulnerable to hazards, travelling through unfamiliar areas with limited knowledge of dangerous locations while seeking refuge. Working with the Protection Team under Danish Refugee Council (DRC) and other humanitarian agencies, DDG will target these populations through mapping and prioritization systems designed to target vulnerability. The mobile structure of DDG’s teams will enable timely, targeted responses. Existing DRC-DDG infrastructure in Aburoc, Bentiu, Leer, Mayom, Juba, Maban and Malakal will additionally allow DDG to quickly deploy across Protection Cluster priority locations, by using these sites as base camp locations or supply points where relevant.

Activities

1. Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas 1.1: Desk research and liaison with partner organisations, identifying areas in crisis that are likely to be heavily affected by ERW restricting access to humanitarian services and critical basic services / community assets 1.2: Deploy MTTs to identified emergency / crisis locations, to conduct non-technical survey (NTS), identifying and mapping immediate threats to physical security and humanitarian access posed by HAs1.3: Transit routes / infrastructure are surveyed through NTS by DDG’s technical team, facilitating the timely, effective and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance by other actors1.4: Conduct meetings with humanitarian agencies to prioritise locations in terms of humanitarian access / service delivery, including warehouses, compounds, distribution sites and/or schools.1.5: Conduct refresher training for DDG staff on NTS, technical survey / TS (using detectors) and clearance methodologies. NTS training includes strategies designed to ensure participation and active engagement from women and children, recognising the obstacles presented by societal norms, and gender / age-specific daily routines and movement patterns1.6: Conduct EOD or BAC to remove and destroy dangerous items (single MTT) 2. Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

Approximately 5,000 (2,400 women; 2,100 men, 250 boys, 250 girls) people have been consulted over the months of September and October 2018. This consultation took place during regular monitoring visits, FGDs with men, women, boys, and girls, protection assessments conducted on mobile missions, PSN identifications, community based protection networks, etc. In general, DRC consults communities about their needs, the risks they face, their displacement history, their intentions, their safety situation, as well as identifies specialised protection risks (SGBV, child protection and mine action). These consultations take place on a daily basis, and are captured in DRC’s monitoring reports, protection assessments, context snapshots, monthly reports, and camp management satisfaction surveys and information products. DRC engages with communities on a daily basis to understand their needs, concerns, and analyse the protection environment. This project design is based on these discussions and observations within the communities, including separate discussion with women, men, boys, and girls, persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, and community leaders.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community engagement is integrated into the project through the following elements: Feedback and complaints: Follow-up data collected routinely as part of the Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL) cycle will allow space to determine beneficiary satisfaction with project activities and the manner in which they were conducted, including distinct perspectives held by women, men, boys and girls. Leadership/governance: NTS / community liaison activities have been incorporated as a core support function for this project, to ensure that channels for beneficiary dialogue and feedback are used as the basis for prioritising technical activities and target populations, in addition to being the principal mechanism for collecting data to monitor and evaluate project activities. Participation: All staff conducting NTS will be trained in strategies ensuring that less represented groups (e.g. women / children / people with disabilities) can also participate in DDG’s activities. This is partly driven by the recognition that full participation will enable DDG to gather more comprehensive and accurate information regarding the impact of ERW contamination and its clearance. Transparency: Throughout the project, community engagement will always begin by disseminating information on DDG’s objectives and services offered via county/payam officials and traditional leaders at the village level. Where demolition of items may be required, communities will be consulted and duly informed of these plans to ensure this process is conducted in a manner that is sensitive to views of nearby residents.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, this intervention will target populations based on an analysis of the threats posed by mines/ERW to specific population groups. DDG will ensure that its teams systematically prioritise the most urgent humanitarian threats at any given point in the project. At the Juba level, DDG draws on several layers of mapping data to prioritise survey, clearance and RE, including planned service delivery locations (UNOCHA), recent perception data regarding possible contamination (REACH), and exact locations of items cleared in 2017/18 (UNMAS / IMSMA). Of great concern is the risk of death or injury involving ERW as a result of increased population movement as civilians move to access humanitarian assistance at key service delivery points. Priority will be given to hazards that obstruct the safe access of displaced populations to water, schools and other socio economic assets. RE will be delivered based on analysis of specific needs of women, men, boys and girls. Furthermore, DDG will, through Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), identify the major service delivery points which will be a primary target for clearance responses.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, DDG’s mine action programming ensures that all affected populations can comfortably participate in survey and RE activities. To this end, the following strategies are employed: 1) Gender-balanced RE teams; 2) Timing and planning of NTS and RE sessions to account for the distinct schedules and movements of different demographic groups, including women, children, and IDPs; 3) Gender- and age- sensitive prioritisation of EOD tasks. DDG ensures that the RE and NTS is carried out by teams with both male and female staff, and that all staff conducting NTS and RE are trained in strategies (eg. timing/location of sessions, gender balanced teams) ensuring that less represented groups can also participate in DDG activities and inform programming. KIIs are scheduled at appropriate times and locations (eg. DDG has previous experience that afternoon sessions are appropriate for inclusion of women, when lunch has been cooked and dinner is not being prepared yet) to ensure inclusion of the perspectives of women, men, boys and girls.
