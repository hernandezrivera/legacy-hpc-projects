Project Summary

In light of the acute needs outlined above, PIN proposes an integrated, multi-sector intervention to support the most vulnerable in Northern Syria to meet their basic needs in the aftermath of rapid onset crises, improve their food security, and (re)establish livelihoods. Interventions will be informed by detailed needs, market, and protection risk assessments, with geographical and household (HH) targeting based on the severity of needs and HH economic analysis. PIN will provide life-saving and life-sustaining cash-based assistance in support of basic needs and food security, drawing on extensive experience in cash-based assistance, food security and livelihoods to maximize response impact and mitigate risks.
First-line emergency response activities (mainly distributions of RTERs) are anticipated to be largely focussed on Idlib and Western Aleppo governorates; however, PIN will retain capacity to launch rapid responses in NES as required. Activities will be underpinned by emergency preparedness and response plans developed in coordination with relevant clusters, working groups, and partners – including the Cash Working Group (CWG), Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), WASH, S/NFI and CCCM clusters – to promote timely, efficient and joined-up responses that seek to maximize geographic and sectoral coverage and minimize gaps. Result 2 will focus upon assisting vulnerable, conflict-affected households to meet their basic food needs and (re)establish livelihoods. Activities will target the most vulnerable households in communities chronically affected by the adverse impacts of the conflict, using common assistance modalities and selection criteria, as established in the FSL and Early Recovery Clusters and CWG. Under this result PIN will plan to reach beneficiaries with monthly food assistance (food baskets, food vouchers), agriculture vouchers and farmer field schools and bakery support.
PIN does not target based on status, including both Internally Displaced People (IDPs) and host community members meeting eligibility and vulnerability criteria, to avoid creating tensions within and across communities. Selection criteria have been elaborated in consultation with the affected communities, as well as based on evidence from previous project monitoring and community feedback obtained through the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and include low income families from the following groups: Female-headed households (HH); Child-headed HH; Elderly-headed HH; HH with disabled and chronically ill members; HH with high dependency ratio; Unemployed male and female youth; Poor farming HH. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities.
PIN aims to provide most needed assistance to such groups through: employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct need, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritise inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border, to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of projects.

4 - Needs

Based on the HNO draft (2020), the FS situation in 2020 is expected to further worsen and the most vulnerable in conflict-affected communities will continue to experience a deterioration in their food security, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity.The ongoing displacement and the chronic impacts of the conflict have resulted in high levels of food insecurity. REACH’s most recent FSL Factsheet for NWS (Sept 19) indicated that 100% of communities assessed reported challenges in accessing food, with the top three reasons cited as: lack of resources to purchase food; high food prices; and decreases in local food production. Prevalence of resort to negative coping strategies was extremely high, with 80% of key informants reporting HH borrowing money from family/friends, and an extremely worrying 56% reporting sending children to work/beg (56%) as coping strategies for a lack of income. PIN data confirm these trends, with 96% of respondents of recent assessments citing food needs amongst their top three priorities. Just 11.6% of the most vulnerable HH were found to be food secure, whilst 41% exhibited food insecurity. Very high levels of resort to negative coping strategies were observed, with an average score of 50.9. PIN surveys indicate that just 16% of the most vulnerable HH have ‘acceptable’ FCS, decreasing to 11% when disaggregated for female-headed households only – 46% of whom were food insecure. HH were also found to exhibit high resort to negative coping strategies, with an average score of 30.5 across those surveyed. The most vulnerable were found to include female, elderly, people with disabilities and child-headed HH.

5 - Implementation of activities

Under Result 1 PIN will focus on First Line Emergency Responses (FLER) in support of vulnerable HH through distribution of Ready to Eat Rations (RTERs). RTER kits are designed in line with Sphere standards and meet FSL cluster recommendations for short-term food assistance. The projected 5-day RTER basket contains 10 commodities, weighing 29.2 kg and providing 2,119 kcal per person per day for a HH size of 6. To ensure an integrated multisectoral response, the distribution of RTERs is usually paired with the provision of MPCA and distribution of hygiene kits. Result 2 will focus on assisting vulnerable households to meet their basic food needs. PIN anticipates the following activities: 1/ distribution of monthly food vouchers, equivalent to at least 1,550/person/day in line with FSL cluster Minimum Standards for severely vulnerable HHs to meet 70-78% of their food needs. Food vouchers are set in Syrian Pounds to not damage local markets by injecting large quantities of foreign currency, thereby potentially undermining the local economy); 2/ distribution of monthly food baskets - the projected food baskets will be designed to meet 70-78% of the food Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) in order to meet the needs of the severely food insecure HHs; 3/ Distribution of vouchers for agriculture assets and Farmer Field Schools - PIN will support farmers throughout both the winter and summer agricultural seasons, assisting them with packages tailored to each season in line with Agriculture Working Group guidelines; a lead farmers approach will be utilized, to build local capacity for agricultural extension, enhancing the resilience and autonomy of rural farming communities 4/Support to bakeries (PIN will also provide support to functional bakeries in target areas in the form of flour and yeast donation and coverage of bakery running costs).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments, focusing on cash and other assistance modalities. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)Risk: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)MM: needs-based targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs; advertisement in targeted public places; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the local councils.;100% verifications of the beneficiaries; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, wide advertisements and communication channels for PIN’s CFRM, 2)R: intergroup tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs.3) R:PSEA 3)MM: Trainings of all staff; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection related questions, Clear process to swiftly address PSEA case, Signs are posted throughout project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: Internal distribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with Security and Access teams for timing and place of distributions, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered waiting areas are selected, with seating (where possible); Different queues for men/women, PIN home delivers assistance to beneficiaries with mobility/other difficulties if needed; Food rations are grouped into smaller, lighter boxes to facilitate easier transportation. 5)R: Distribution staff do not treat beneficiaries with respect, 5)MM: Screening and vetting of all new first-line field PIN staff, including reference checks; Training on staff on key institutional policies (CoC and PSEA/Safeguarding policy).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming. (A6/R6): PIN is able to retain registration in NES under the HAO, in Erbil under the KRI Administration and central Iraqi authorities, and in Turkey; (MM6): If PIN’s registration or visas in cross-border response hubs, PIN would adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal.
