Project Summary

Throughout 2019, the security situation in Darfur continued to improve, prompting families displaced within Sudan and refugees from Chad and the Central African Republic (CAR) to return to their areas of origin. IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Zero Report, estimated more than 400,000 returnees across all ﬁve Darfur states between 2010 and September 2019. From July to September 2019, DTM conducted 39,506 registrations across six-states in Sudan, out of which 34,507 (87%) were returnees. DTM reports continue to indicate an increase in returnee registrations over IDPs registrations, which is indicative of the fact that the returnee movement is on the rise, a trend that is expected to continue steadily throughout 2020. IOM’s DTM return registration disaggregates vulnerable groups, to include youth (between 5-17-year old), women who are either pregnant or lactating mothers and people with disabilities, - groups who face numerous threats such as lack of adequate housing, high unemployment levels, and the lack of income generating activities to sustain their livelihoods, as well as limited access to basic health /maternal service and protection concerns.
IOM in Sudan adopts an approach to durable solutions focused on a progressive approach to resolve displacement situations. This includes the identiﬁcation and mitigation/reduction of potential risks and negative coping mechanisms and the reinforcement of mobility opportunities to transition towards self-reliance and increased resilience. Across the four programmatic areas of intervention: protection, safety and security; adequate standard of living; sustainable livelihoods and employment; and inclusive governance, IOM will therefore aim to continue supporting communities in return areas by increasing the self-reliance and resilience of returnees and affected local communities who remained behind to support transition towards durable solutions through sustainable reintegration, social stability development. Reintegration and social stability support will be carried out through the identification of community needs and the impact the returnees’ movement can have on the community as a whole. The project will therefore apply a flexible and community-based approach to respond to humanitarian needs and to ensure that socio-economic needs and basic services of communities are met based on priorities identified directly by the community through a Community Needs Identification Workshop. Under HRP this project ﬁts under Objective 2 – “Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services” and ensures to the UNDAF and IOMs programmes.
This project is in line with IOM’s Progressive Resolution of Displacement Situations Framework and in support of the implementation of programming along the Humanitarian-Development Nexus by foster the resilience of displaced persons, migrants and affected communities to transition towards durable solutions. IOM Sudan’s Transition and Recovery Unit will deliver the project drawing on its extensive experience implementing locally-driven and contextualized longer-term community-led activities that empower affected populations ownership over addressing the conditions of their displacement and restore their ability to make informed mobility decisions and reduce their vulnerability.

1 - Needs

As the security situation in Sudan improves, IDP may choose to return to their places of origin, however, the lack of basic services, livelihood opportunities and security in their places of origin limit their capacity to return or achieve durable solutions when they return. IOM plans to create favorable conditions for sustainable reintegration in areas of return through an integrated, multi-sectoral response that includes immediate assistance (basic services) and longer term assistance (socio-economic development) for returnees across Darfur. Based on IOM's experience in supporting the sustainable return of IDPs and refugee returnees across a number of rural villages in Darfur, this project will continue to assess the speciﬁc needs of the returned populations across various localities through community-led initiatives and will aim to implement activities that will contribute towards durable solutions through the provision of adequate standards of living, including at a minimum access to adequate food, water, housing, health care and basic education*. Specifically the project will support ); (a) Rehabilitation of basic services (WASH, health and education services); (b) Organization of market orientated vocational trainings to improve food security, livelihood and employment opportunities for vulnerable community members and returnees; (c) Promotion of capacity building for local CBOs/CSOs and institutions. Throughout the project, durable solutions principles will be followed which will include the direct involvement of community member to increase ownership of the planned interventions through the establishment of community based committees, including elements like the support of a WASH-tariff system via community-led water management committees. This project will also beneﬁt from the results of IOM's previous and ongoing experience in implementing projects supporting durable solutions, where for example, in North Darfur, in the village of Sarafaya, more than 19,000 returnees returned within a year (only 17 individuals were residing in the village before the start of the project). IOM will also work in close collaboration with local actors (CBOs and CSOs) and institutions (local authorities and line ministries) to improve the sustainability of the services to contribute towards re-establishing trust and improve governance at the local level. Under HRP this project ﬁts under Objective 2 – “Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services” and ensures links with IOM’s activities and UNDAF. Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment: Special needs of women, men, boys and girls will be identified through needs assessment and community workshops/interviews and will be factored into the planned activities. Sustainability and Resilience: this will include community and government engagement in the planning and implementation of this intervention and capacity building training sessions to support local authorities and beneﬁciaries to have necessary skills to support self reliance. *Approximately 1,461,000 people with education needs (80 per cent are children between 4 and 17 years old, and 30 per cent are adults including, youth). The facilities available are limited and can't ensure equitable access to education for all children and youth, school supplies remain low or limited, and the average enrollment for basic school is at 40 per cent. Furthermore, school fees can be unaffordable for many families and children may drop out of school to contribute to the family income. This multi-sectoral integrated project will aim to increased equitable access to services and improve socio-economic conditions for communities.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1: Community led workshops and initiatives (including community-committees, water, waste and resource management committees) develop community action plans and contribute to the selection of the activities to be implemented; 2: Water and sanitation infrastructures are rehabilitated, and hygiene awareness activities are promoted at sites of return in line with SPHERE Standards. 3: Health facilities are rehabilitated and clinics are supported to provide Minimum Basic Health Package to returnees and the host community. 4: Facilitating training of water technicians (female and male) and health workers to support the provision and maintenance of basic services. 5: Vulnerable Returnees and host community members are provided with market oriented vocational and essential livelihood trainings based on community priorities. 6: Cash based support will be provided to the most vulnerable returnee households to foster self-reliance. 7: Education facilities, including teachers’ accommodations are rehabilitated or constructed; 8. Capacity building activities will be designed for local authorities, CBOs and CSOs to improve the sustainability of the activities

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Disasters and environmental degradation threaten to hamper progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and durable solutions, especially for those countries left furthest behind. Climate change is expected to trigger growing population movements within and across borders. IOM aims to build the resilience of communities to risk factors and expected changes in their natural environment, while contributing to sustainable development of livelihood opportunities; and support efforts to strengthen the interaction with responsibility holders (national and local authorities) and develop their capacities. IOM is working on establishing integrated water management systems and will also continue working on establishing sources of renewable energy (solar energy) and improving local practices to reduce the environmental footprint and waste of natural resources. Livelihood and agricultural training will continue to focus on improving the targeted beneﬁciaries' knowledge of agricultural practices so that they are able to better respond to climate shocks and replace the negative coping strategies currently used and that contribute to ongoing land degradation and desertiﬁcation (e.g. ﬁrewood collection). IOM will consult with UNEP/ISCG on the community environmental action plan tools and conduct area impact assessments before implementation of any supports on rehabilitation of basic service facilities such as water and sanitation facilities.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Women and girls tend to be identiﬁed more frequently as victims of traﬃcking for sexual exploitation and forced labour. Protection threats such as harassment and rape targeted to women and girls are still reported in Darfur. To address these issues, IOM will continue to provide direct assistance through psycho-social support, counseling and medical assistance to mobile populations in vulnerable situations and make efforts to raise awareness among all community members of the risk and danger of irregular migration, human right and protection. The project will plan to deliver community-based health education, awareness raining activities will not be limited to promotion of health, hygiene and prevention of infection diseases, and nutrition but also address issues of irregular migration, limited basic rights and special needs and concerns faced by women and girls in vulnerable situations. Such activities will encourage participation of local community, relevant stakeholders, people with disabilities and women’s representatives to foster an inclusive environment for all community members affected by violence, displacement and migration.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase - registration of returnees, immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. The proposed project is built around a community-centred and participatory approach to ensure that the communities themselves are agents, enablers and drivers of their own resilience, recovery and development. Accountability will also be promoted through capacity development activities for relevant government stakeholders, community leaders and leaders from women and youth groups, etc. Furthermore, through the establishment of the community committees, community members will be able to provide their feedback and voice their concerns in regards to the activities implemented and corrective measures will be employed whenever needed throughout the implementation of the project.
