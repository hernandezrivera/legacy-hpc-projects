Project Summary

The project will contribute to ensuring that all women, men, girls, and boys (including all age groups and persons with disabilities) in the Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host communities have access to WASH services through the following three areas of intervention in 7 of the camps under IOM's Area of Responsibility (AoR) :
Improved water services: Small-scale solar-powered piped water networks will be installed in the newly developed areas for the relocated families to provide safe water to the beneficiaries in camp 20 extensions. IOM will continue the operation and maintenance (O&M) and upgrade the existing deep tube wells in IOM AoR Camps and water networks in camps 09, 10, 11, 12, 18, 20, and 20 extensions. In newly developed valley areas and for the replacement of non-repairable deep tube wells, some deep tube wells will be installed in camps and host communities for ensuring better access to the most vulnerable people in need. Two existing solar water supply systems in camp 20 extension will be upgraded for smooth operation and supply required water and to reduce fuel consumption. IOM will procure, replenish, and replace the emergency stocks for cyclones, fires, floods, or other emergency responses.
Access to Sanitation: To ensure dignified and safe access to the latrine facilities, IOM will continue to upgrade the existing poor functional latrine facilities with gender-inclusive and disability-related features in consultation with the community. IOM will provide assistive devices (commode chair, commode chair with potty, and urinal bottles for males and females) to people with disabilities or physical impairments to ensure their specific needs based on the assessment. It will continue the durable household latrine construction work to cover the sanitation gaps at the targeted host community villages in Ukhiya. Decentralized Wastewater Treatment Systems (DEWATS) will be built and the upgrade of lime-based fecal sludge treatment plants to the DEWATS system to meet the coverage gaps. In addition, IOM will continue to build more small-scale Intermediate Sludge Transfer Systems (ISTS) connecting with the fecal sludge management (FSM) units to reduce the sludge transfer cost. IOM will continue to work with the community to ensure the appropriate waste segregation at the source and keep the surroundings clean. IOM will upgrade the existing material recovery facilities (MRF) to increase their capacity. The complete chain of the solid waste management (SWM) monitoring system will be continued and strengthened at all levels.
Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Critical supplies of hygiene items (soap, water containers, Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) materials, etc.) will be provided, with community education and mobilization activities targeting all age groups, genders, and persons with specific WASH needs at the community level. IOM will distribute WASH NFIs through GDT distribution system in all IOM camps to better assist with hygiene kits for people in need. MHM kits will be provided to the reproductive age group (10-55 years) on a six-monthly basis, based on the field assessment and needs across all the IOM-managed camps. The community will be furthermore engaged in O&M of WASH facilities, waste segregation, and prevention of mass transmissible, and waterborne diseases like AWD, Scabies, etc. Real-time service monitoring and community feedback mechanisms will be continued and strengthened throughout the project period.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Water Services: In IOM’s Area of Responsibility (AoR) there are a total of 44 water networks, with 68% of the beneficiary population using tap-stands as their primary source of drinking water, although equitable access has not been assessed and assured. According to the 2024 ISNA, 89% of Rohingya refugees have enough water to meet their household needs. Water quality surveillance conducted by IOM found that a total of 62% of tap stands were functional, 34% were functional but with various critical issues and repair needs, and 4% completely non-functional (ISNA 2024). Many of the water networks do not comply with sector standards on free residual chlorine (FRC) presence at distribution points. Frequent repair and maintenance of handpumps is necessary to ensure adequate water supplies for meeting the daily needs of the targeted beneficiaries. Fire outbreaks in Camp 9 and Camp 11 in 2023 and 2024 respectively showed that a rapid provision of water supply or a viable source of drinking water with supplemented Aquatab distribution is necessary in the aftermath of such emergencies. Sanitation: Latrine coverage at a camp level provides access at 1:20 in line with sphere standards, but block-level data shows that at least 3 of the 7 camps (9, 10, 11) are unable to implement the necessary minimum ratio due to the high population density. Latrine functionality is monitored on an ongoing basis, with current functionality rate of 96% on average. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 86% of households reporting private bathing space in the shelters, but often these spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11% not connected to any form of drainage (ISNA 2024). Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. Pilot programmes have been instigated to install women-friendly spaces and WASH blocks but are challenged by space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. A continual concern regarding sanitation is the coverage of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements, or scale-up. Solid Waste Management (SMW) remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of refugee households are disposing of waste in the designated area, with 27% of household reporting garbage near their shelters. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Community engagement strategies will focus on changing identified high-risk behaviors through participatory approaches and capacity building, such as safe water usage and solid waste containment to reduce risk of waste entering the surrounding environment of communities. Strengthened hygiene practice monitoring and follow up, focusing on vulnerable people through increased cross sector linkages with Health & Education with epidemiological evidence of WASH-related diseases, will guide the response for meeting critical supply needs such as soap, water containers and menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits in a timely manner and in sufficient quantity. Based on Wash Severity Classification (WSC) report 2022, 26% of households in the host community reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs. This requires installation of deep hand pumps to address the drop in water table and improve host community access to water supply. As conveyed to the upazila administration by the host community, there is a need for improved sanitation facilities. The most common sanitation facility for host communities is a pit latrine with slab. Approximately 20% of households do not have access to these facilities and there is a need for further installation of pit latrines.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Incorporating a protection lens is fundamental to IOM’s operations, ensuring all programs are designed with attention to protection risks and potential violations. In Cox’s Bazar, IOM prioritizes protection as a cornerstone of its humanitarian response, integrating activities that safeguard the rights of crisis-affected individuals. The focus is on empowering communities, building resilience, and preserving dignity by reducing risks and enhancing safety and participation for all. The 'Do No Harm' principle guides all IOM activities, with capacity-building sessions for community volunteers, advocates, and support groups enhancing access to information and informed decision-making. In the specific context of WASH interventions, going out to crowded distribution centers is always a challenge for women, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Considering the difficulties to access the centralized distribution centers, IOM will continue distributing all the NFI WASH items using GDT system to meet the exact needs. To reduce GBV and protection risks, IOM WASH will ensure WASH staff, IP volunteers, and hygiene promoters are trained on gender sensitivity, Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and SEA referral pathways to make an appropriate response. Following consultations with users, particularly women and girls, the IP will ensure gender marking on all WASH facilities and install privacy fencing to provide secure and dignified access. Additionally, IOM will construct disability-friendly latrine and bathing facilities wherever needs are identified.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM maintains a Zero Tolerance Policy toward sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), binding all staff, implementing partners, contractors, vendors, and service providers to high standards of behavior at all times. SEA against affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result in disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution.A dedicated PSEA Officer supports ongoing efforts to combat SEA, ensuring adherence to PSEA principles. IOM continues to roll out PSEA training and Training of Trainers sessions for PSEA Champions, aiming to equip all IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers with essential knowledge of PSEA guidelines and mandatory reporting systems.IOM will facilitate refresher training on PSEA for all personnel involved in the WASH service delivery. During the project’s initial implementation phase, SEA-related risks are screened, and mitigation measures are identified to enhance safe programming for affected populations. IEC materials are developed to raise awareness among communities about their rights to free assistance, reporting mechanisms, and available referral services. The PSEA Officer, in collaboration with the program team, conducts regular consultations and focus group discussions with beneficiaries to improve awareness and address concerns about SEA.IOM has an online reporting platform, WEAREALLIN.IOM.INT, for reporting SEA incidents. Recognizing potential access barriers, IOM has also established alternative complaint and feedback mechanisms to receive SEA-related concerns from beneficiaries and community members. IOM’s specialized investigation team ensures follow-up on SEA complaints, with access to protection and support services for survivors provided in coordination with its MPHSS-Protection team, the PSEA network, and existing GBV and Child Protection referral pathways."

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM will upgrade 2 existing water supply systems in camp 20 extension to increase the capacity and to reduce fuel consumption and at the same time reduce the negative impact on the environment. Solid waste management is one of the big issues in camps to minimize negative impacts on the environment and keep the camp environment clean and healthy for the camp dwellers. Implementing partners will continue operating material recovery facility (MRF) to manage solid wastes. This willl involve continioung to collect wastes from households and transfer them to the MRF site; organic wastes will be converted into compost, inorganic waste will be sent to sanitary landfill, and recyclable wastes to scrap dealers. Compost will be distributed to camp dwellers and host community for homestead gardening or vegetation. IOM will continue testing influent and effluent from faecal sludge treatment facilities (FSTP) and will take corrective action to meet the standards.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

IOM will continue updating the emergency preparedness and response plan for rohingya refugees and host populations. 4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Rohingya Refugee volunteers are engaged in all segments of WASH activities in camps. This includes managing the operations of solar water supply systems, handpump repairing and maintenance, regular maintenance of latrine and bathing facilities. In terms of sanitation volunteers are engaged in house-to-house solid waste collection, segregation and compost production including distribution for kitchen gardening. Volunteers also support hygiene awareness and NFI distribution management through community engagement activities. IOM service delivery approach contributes significantly to skills development and building the self-reliance of the refugees, enabling them to operate WASH services confidently with minimum supervision. The community engagement approach used builds the capacities of the refugees to manage and take ownership of WASH facilities and maintain hygiene practices.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

In host communities, IOM will support equitable access to quality WASH services aligned with national development plans. WASH vulnerability criteria (e.g. lack of improved water and sanitation) will also be taken into consideration when targeting neighbouring communities, both directly and indirectly impacted as a result of hosting Rohingya refugees/FDMNs.
