Project Summary

The project focuses on raising awareness about explosive ordnance risk education to enhance safety and security for vulnerable groups in Malakal, Upper Nile State. With a significant number of returnees, the Community Action Organization (CAO) plans to effectively communicate the dangers associated with explosive ordnance in priority counties. The initiative aims to educate host communities, internally displaced persons, and returnees from conflict in Sudan about the risks of landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) in South Sudan.
Key activities will include clearing and marking contaminated areas to support humanitarian efforts and facilitate safe settlement for IDPs and refugees. The project targets 20,000 children and adults who have been affected by conflict, promoting increased awareness of explosive ordnance risks among this at-risk population. By partnering with local authorities, CAO will seek to maximize the project's impact, ultimately contributing to a safer and more secure environment for the community.

1. Needs

The ongoing conflicts in regions like Jonglei and Upper Nile have significantly heightened the threats posed by landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW), resulting in the displacement of tens of thousands of individuals within South Sudan and across its borders. The situation has been exacerbated by the conflict in Sudan, which has further undermined economic conditions in South Sudan, particularly affecting northern states reliant on imports from Sudan where essential commodity prices are rising sharply.In response to these critical challenges, the Community Action Organization (CAO) is actively engaged in supporting children, adolescents, and adults through various initiatives. Their efforts range from clearing and marking hazardous areas to ensure safe humanitarian assistance to facilitating the resettlement of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugees. CAO places great importance on raising awareness about the risks associated with explosive ordnance, especially among vulnerable populations.To mitigate protection risks, CAO adopts a comprehensive strategy focused on community support, enhancing youth centers, and establishing psychosocial support systems at the community level. They also have a referral system in place for landmine victims. Furthermore, the organization champions mine risk education in schools, child-friendly spaces, and the broader community, promoting the formation of school clubs and child help desks to spread crucial information regarding landmines, unexploded ordnance (UXO), and ERW.

2. Project activities

The following activities are proposed for the Mine Risk Education (MRE) program: 1. Implement educational initiatives for children in schools to promote MRE awareness.2. Provide comprehensive training for educators on MRE guidelines.3. Increase awareness of MRE in community centers affiliated with educational institutions.4. Foster understanding of MRE among adults within the community.5. Organize awareness sessions for children, adults, and adolescents conducted by the MRE program staff.
