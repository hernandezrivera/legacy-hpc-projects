Project Summary

The proposed WASH project for 2024 will contribute to the achievement of HRP 2023 objectives through triple nexus approach. The project will be achieved by increasing and improving access with sustainable use of gender sensitive and safe emergency WASH services such as water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion sessions, ensuring that conflict affected populations receive immediate and sufficient assistance according to their needs. The project will respond to WASH needs in IDP camps and communities as well as in health facilities and schools. It will ensure free and equitable access to safe drinking water and latrine in secure places, enhance their knowledge on WASH practices, prevent the spread of water borne diseases, reduce prevalence of diarrhea and ultimately also lead towards improved nutrition security. This will be achieved through a combination of interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities, risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), Social behavioral change (SBC), formation and training of gender balance WASHCOMs with 40-50% female and building capacity of government authorities to effectively provide lifesaving assistance and have improved capacity to meet the immediate needs of newly displaced, diseases affected population and returning populations. Throughout the course of the project, there will be active participation of the stakeholders and affected persons including women and marginalize groups in the design and implementation to ensure the sustainability of the interventions while protection needs of women and girls will be identified and integrated into our programming.

1 - Needs

HRP 2023 indicated that 2.4 million people were in acute need. In Borno State, 2.9 million people are in need while in Yobe State, 2.0 million people were in need. The Niger crisis affected the WASH infrastructure in border States of Borno and Yobe State. Majority of the new arrivals are Returnees (originally Nigerians who were initially displaced into Niger republic but now had to return back into Damasak town due to the rising unrest in Niger Republic. WASH sector identified a total of 5,060,583 people in need of WASH services with 3,052,274 people targeted in the BAY states. About 75% of persons have been reached with WASH services in Borno State with operations and maintenance still been a huge challenge. As such, access to safe and portable water sources for drinking and WASH purposes has remained a challenge across the states with at least 50% of HHs having poor WASH facilities. Even if there have been some significant improvement in the BAY States, incidences of water borne diseases (Cholera and Diarrhea) still remained a challenge in several rural and peri-urban communities. It is imperative that state authorities and partners intensify their efforts to improve water quality and hygiene awareness creation. A significant amount of investment is required at various points along the water supply chain, sanitation facilities and hygiene awareness creation, both in rural and urban areas, to improve access and quality of WASH services.

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Improved protected access to safe water supply for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno states. In close coordination with the Government, RRT, RRM working group and the WASH sector-working group, the response will be deployed, integrating protection concerns and adopting a gender sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls. Populations will be provided with access to a safe water supply (water trucking /quick repair of water points with chlorination, new drilling and rehabilitation of boreholes with solar pumping system/hand pumps) that prioritizes first distribution to vulnerable groups (women, children and elderly people). Water quality testing including free residual chlorine will be tested for all water supply to ensure the quality of the water. The IDP camps will be supported with operation and maintenance of water points to ensure continues safe water supply. In communities, WASHCOM will be formed and trained with 40-50% of female in the committee to ensure the needs of the women are considered during the implementation and LGA level revolving fund mechanism will be establish to improve the sustainability of water points. Activity 02: Improved safe access to sanitation service for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Multi-unit communal gender separated latrines will be constructed and rehabilitated in IDP camps in safe places with light to ensure the safe access. Male and female Hygiene and sanitation volunteers will be trained and supported to monitor the cleaning and maintenance of latrines. Solid waste management will be conducted through distribution of waste bins, trainings and coordinating with BOSEPA for waste collection. In communities where returnees, host communities and IDPs have been displaced for extended periods, the project will focus on household latrines. Following a transect walk focused on the negative aspects of open defecation, the Community Lead Total Sanitation approach will be launched. The approach will ensure that community mobilizers and project staff integrate gender-related dimensions to ensure women and girl’s participation while developing community action plans. Beneficiaries will commit their own resources, including locally available materials to construct latrines. Local artisans and skilled technicians will be trained to build standard structurally sound latrines. The design will ensure that pour flush/VIP latrines for health facilities and schools are accessible to people with disabilities and take into consideration the needs of women, children and elderly. The structure will include ramps, be child-friendly, have hand railings, be lockable, gender segregated and have containers for menstrual hygiene management. Activity 03: Improved safe access to hygiene practices within affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Hygiene promotion activities will be delivered with different approaches in emergency phases and resilience phases. In emergency, rapid hygiene session conducted by hygiene promotes and volunteers through group session and HH sessions. WASH EM approach will be implemented to improve the hand washing practices. In resilience building phase the beneficiaries will be reached through mix approaches of PHAST, CHAST, CLTS and sanitation marketing to ensure sustainability of the practices. BABY WASH approaches will be implemented together with health and nutrition staff members to improve the WASH practices and prevent the malnutrition. In addition to that the use of IECs and other communications materials will be adopted in sensitizing the beneficiaries through house-to-house visits, FGDs, campaigns to be conducted by a trained and equipped gender balance community hygiene volunteers and CHIPs. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The safety of beneficiaries will be prioritised in the delivery of WASH infrastructures such as water points, latrines and showers. With open defecation being practiced of both women and men in target area. Following an environmental analysis, ACF will prioritise the construction of latrines that are proximate to households and with adequate lighting. As 87% of women and girls are partially or totally responsible for collecting water, water points will be located in places convenient for women and girls. ACF will work to increase women’s voice and leadership by working with the state and community structures. We would ensure women are active participants in project decision-making and monitoring by conducting household surveys.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACF has a well-established accountability system with a feedback and complaint response mechanism within the MEAL Department, which aims at improving the quality of interventions and promoting Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) commitment on AAP, and ACF’s goal of institutionalizing the Mission's Accountability Framework (Including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) in all functions and operations alongside system level cohesion, coordination and learning. To ensure accountability to affected population in this project, ACF will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all aspects of this project to provide a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication to the affected populations. These channels will facilitate reception of feedback and complaints and to inform affected populations about procedures and processes that affect them so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices. ACF will capitalize on the result of Humanitarian Accountability Assessment (HAA) to determine the existing systems and structures of complaints referral as well as to determine the communities’ preference in terms of channels to put in place to enable them provide feedback/complaints relating to the intervention, while maintaining do-no-harm principle. Through the established channels, ACF will ensure that all formal and informal communications from persons of concern (both positive and negative) are analyzed to inform protection, assistance and solutions to the programming, and corrective action is taken as appropriate. Furthermore, ACF will ensure constant sensitization of the affected population on the established channels and how to use them for earing their views and opinion regarding the intervention.
