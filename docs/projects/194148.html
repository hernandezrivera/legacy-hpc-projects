Project Summary

4. South Kordofan Context
South Kordofan has witnessed bloody conflicts. Women, who have nothing to do with triggering conflicts, stand to pay the bill of all conflicts. Their rights to life are violated, they are subjected to gender-based violence. As a result of the war in south Kordofan extending from the year 1984 (through the ceasefire of 2002 and the resumption of fighting on 06.06.2011), the region has not started to recover from the impact of the conflict when the south Kordofan protocol collapsed. There is no doubt that this long period of war has negatively influenced all aspects of economic, social and political life. It is found that the fighting had direct impact on women. Women suffered from displacement, loss of spouses, and disruption of normal life and loss of schooling opportunities, being rendered single mothers to eke out a living for households and up to the violation of their own rights.
I in the city of Dalanj, the displaced occupy the outskirts of the city, taking refuge in many neighborhoods like el Safaa, el Widay, Gadra, Kajang, Imtidad el Turug and el Taoamat. Similarly, the town of Kaloagi (downtown, el jabak el Ahmar, It’s noted that these neighborhoods had a number of basic-education (primary) schools for girls and the majority of school goers of these schools are girls from displaced households. As a result of the situation of their displacement, they are subjected to violence: psychological, sexual and physical abuse and, thus are more likely to drop out of schooling. Some are exposed to premature pregnancy and childbirth and become child mothers or end up working as food and tea sellers, or work as domestic maids or other menial occupations. This project seeks to civic-educate girls and women in areas of displacement on the outskirts of Dalanj - in southeast, north and west Dalanj, on their rights, and how to protect themselves against GBV.
Women and girls, long subjected to violations of their rights and lacking sufficient awareness, especially as they were affected by the war, need to build a solid platform of awareness of their rights and how to support these rights and how to report abuse.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CfPS adopts a rights-based approach to conflict prevention. this approach sees poverty as a major driver of conflict. the way people in rural areas pursue farming and animal husbandry is backward. clearing land for farming and overgrazing by herders both negatively impact the natural environment. the environment, natural resources and climate change have been included in the geographical and humanitarian context analysis, as well as in project design, proposed activities and budgets (including mitigation measures to minimize impacts and enhance benefits)
