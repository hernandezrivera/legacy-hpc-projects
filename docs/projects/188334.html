Project Summary

The project is planned to encourage cohesion and recovery by reducing shelter vulnerabilities of residents and IDPs for camps by reducing the adverse effect of fire incidents on the IDP's living in informal settlements in Aleppo and Idleb governorate during 2022 and 2023 which will be more beneficial and sustainable to achieve the goals.
2000 IDPs will benefit from the proposed fire response intervention in the 20 targeted camps. Also, 100 IDPs will benefit from the technical capacity building for fire prevention.
All the IDPs will also be benefitted from the formed emergency intervention groups.
The project will cover the enhancement of the capacity of the camps' representatives in the targeted informal settlements to prevent, mitigate and respond to the fire incidents by conducting FGDs and awareness-raising sessions with the camp management and beneficiaries, taking into practice and account the COVID-19 preventive measures during the awareness-raising sessions and FGD's along with developing fire warden systems in the targeted camps. Additionally, the project will ensure the provision of fire extinguishers and fire blankets to enhance the capacity of the people on firefighting by providing them with the needed materials in the targeted informal settlements in case of any fire incident occurring by Constructing fire units in camps and equipping them with recommended tools and equipment by Fire TF.
The main activities during a two-year period (2022-2023):
-Forming Fire Warden Representatives committees FWRs with female representatives.
Provide capacity building and ToT for fire prevention for community leaders and committee members.
- Establishing 30 equipped fire points and identifying the emergency procedures for any possible incident with setting up a fire response.
- Distributing fire blankets
-Conducting 50 fire prevention training sessions.
- Awareness-raising campaign for fire prevention for 20,000 IDPs in the camps through training for HHs and camps committee taking into account all the CCCM, SNFI, and WASH clusters recommendations to ensure physical distancing and COVID-19 related measures.
Regarding vulnerability criteria, the activities will be for all the IDPs inside the targeted camps; however, the selection criteria for the committees will be focused on: (after coordination with the CCCM cluster)
1- Community acceptance from the sector in the camp.
2- Age between 25 to 45 years.
3- The ability to act as a focal point for any issue related to the camp and assist in resolving disputes.
4- The ability to liaise between IDPs and humanitarian service providers;
5- Ensure effective information dissemination among site residents, empower IDPs, and encourage participation in decision-making.
All the mentioned activities are a top priority under CCCM. Forming FWRs committees and establishing fire points with Awareness-raising will be prioritized if HRP won't be fully funded.

4 - Needs

According to the (HNO 2022/MSNA 2021) for Syria estimates, 13.4 million people require humanitarian assistance, of which the highest number (2.7 million) are located in Aleppo governorate. An estimated 6.7 million IDPs are in need, many of whom live in protracted displacement, while years of conflict have increased demand amongst host community members. COVID-19 has further undermined the ability of conflict-affected host and IDP households to meet basic needs. According to the MSNA 2021, fewer households live in damagedapartments/buildings (20%) compared to the MSNA 2020 (28%). However, families reportedly living in last resort sites more than doubled from about 3% (MSNA 2020) to about 7% (MSNA 2021).According to the CCCM 2022/ ISIMM and incidents reports, As of December 2021, the CCCM database includes 1,398 IDP sites that host 1,694,712 IDPs or 321,085 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 45,997 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs.During 2021, CCCM manages to list 368 new IDP sites and Inactivate 304 sites due to the merging of sites, as well as the depopulation and subsequent closure of some sites.During the same month, CCCM Cluster reported incidents in 446 IDP sites. Of these, 269 incidents were flooding, 110 incidents were wind and 67 incidents were fires. These incidents resulted in one death and 18 injury cases, as well as the destruction of 4,682 tents.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

This project was designed in a manner that enables all gender and age groups, in addition to PwD to access the project services. Rahma Worldwide will reflect the age and gender of Project beneficiaries in the beneficiary lists. A referral system will be in place in coordination with Protection Cluster to refer any GBV case and provide needed support. Moreover, a feedback and complaint mechanism (complaint boxes, WA number, email) will be established and shared with all Project beneficiaries to ensure Rahma can receive any Project-related complaint, including PSEA and CSG related complaints. Rahma will train all project staff on its protection policy, including the child safeguarding policy and the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse policy, and when Rahma will conduct the training to the camp managers, it will include PSEA training and CSG. - Rahma will mainstream PSEA policy including training of Rahma `s personnel in addition to signing Rahma protection policy and COC by all of Rahma personnel, volunteers, partners, suppliers, services providers, and all relevant persons. - Activating the complaint mechanism including protection incidents and penalty procedures. - Rahma will assign a PSEA focal point to ensure the implementation of all protection principals, prevention any abuse, and to conduct the protection training and awareness-raising sessions related PSEA and CSG topics, with coordination and supervising of Rahma Protection Program.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigations: 1- Overlapping, losing some locations due to other NGOs intervention, loss of access. - will perform multilevel coordination (Cluster, Field) - coordinated with all the stakeholder during the design phase 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety - Ensure validation of the documents during the project - communicate clearly with all the stakeholders - Involved the camps management committees,LCs,KIs in the process in case of any conflict - Rahma Worldwide will be monitoring all updates on the ground, and if we noticed that any of the beneficiaries are displaced, we will change the selected beneficiaries and choose other beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the same location. 4: Increased number of cases infected with COVID - Training Rahma Worldwide teams about best practices, including social distance, sterilization and wearing face masks. - Providing teams with PPE kits (gloves, sterilization and face masks). - Rahma Worldwide distribution teams will organize beneficiaries and make the social distance. 5- Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the eld staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse, and reporting via established reporting mechanisms of any concerns or suspicions regarding sexual exploitation and abuse, and investigation if necessary (hotline)
