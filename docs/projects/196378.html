Project Summary

GFA: WFP will provide life-saving food assistance to the entire Rohingya refugee population through General Food Assistance. Beneficiaries will receive entitlements that can be redeemed from WFP-contracted local retailers across the camps. E-vouchers provide more flexibility, dignity and greater dietary diversity for the refugees while also creating market linkages with the host community and the wider Bangladeshi economy. To ensure an effective and efficient emergency intervention, WFP will preposition enough food to rapidly respond to the needs of approximately 30,000 households should they be displaced by monsoon, cyclone or conflict. In addition to providing large-scale food assistance, WFP will make sure to address the needs of the most vulnerable households through promoting income generating activities in the camps and host communities. WFP will provide complementary food vouchers to 250,000 extremely vulnerable individuals through Fresh Food Corners.
DRR: WFP will support vulnerable Bangladeshi communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf by enhancing their capacities to manage natural resources, which are being heavily affected by climate change. Approximately 45,000 beneficiaries (18-59 years old) will be targeted, with a strong emphasis on including women - particularly from female-headed households - and people with disabilities. Participants will engage in activities to support natural resource management and disaster risk reduction (e.g., roadside tree plantation, care, and maintenance; watershed management via channel excavation; improvement of drainage systems).
SR: 30,000 vulnerable individuals from vulnerable households will be provided with agricultural training and inputs to improve diverse food consumption. The Self-Reliance programmes targets the most vulnerable individuals who are often overlooked for other income-generating opportunities (e.g., PWDs & youth)
Livelihood: Through multi-year livelihoods model, the project aims to improve vulnerable women’s food security and nutritional status in the Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar.
The programme provides a combined package of monthly subsistence allowance (MSA) with complementary skills development focused on food production, delivered through Self-Helf Groups (SHGs). The programme works with women as an entry point to transform communities, through empowering them socially and economically and helping them become entrepreneurs. The programme also supports social cohesion and community integration for households who have been impacted directly or indirectly by the Rohingya influx, COVID-19 pandemic, and recent global food crisis.
•A monthly subsistence allowance of BDT 1,050 (approximately USD 12)
•Technical training to improve production techniques, bring down the cost of production, and increase profit margins
•Additional financial literacy training and negotiation training, social and behavioral change communication sessions, and modules on gender-based-violence (GBV), human rights, disaster risk reduction, hygiene, and health
•Activities related to market linkages, including aggregation centres for on-farm production and ESFN shop for off-farm production.
School Feeding: WFP School Feeding Programme aims to improve children’s learning by addressing the root causes of hunger and micronutrient deficiencies that can have consequences on their cognitive growth. In the camps, WFP will provide a packet of 50gms of micronutrient fortified biscuits to children enrolled in learning centres every school day. In the host community, WFP will provide a packet 75gms of micronutrient fortified biscuits to pre-primary and primary school children in Government schools, approved “madrassas” (Islamic schools) and NGO-run schools every school day. These locally-produced fortified biscuits are fortified with 16 vitamins and minerals, and provide approximately half of the daily micronutrient requirements for a child between 5 and 11 years.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Five years from the start of the refugee influx, there are now more than 930,000 refugees residing in 33 camps and the island of Bhasan Char. In both the Rohingya camps and Bangladeshi communities, the post-pandemic early recovery stage has been interrupted by the impacts of the global food crisis, which have led to rising costs not only of food, but of all other essential commodities, including fuel. Overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly high since 2019 among Rohingya households. WFP’s Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-V) showed that 95 percent of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, similar to 2020 (96 percent) and 2019 (94 percent), with a gradual increase from 2017 (80 percent). The worsened situation reflects the slow economic recovery of an already fragile population with neither income sources nor livelihood opportunities. Although livelihoods and skill-based programmes have resumed, the WFP Market Monitor published in June 2022 demonstrates that purchasing power is being eroded as the cost of essential commodities and services continue to increase faster than the value of assistance. A global food crisis fuelled by conflict, climate shocks and the COVID-19 pandemic has grown; the ripple effects of the war in Ukraine are driving up prices of food, fuel and fertilizer, putting vulnerable populations at risk. Since the global food crisis escalated in March 2022, driven by the war in Ukraine, the annual inflation rate in Bangladesh shot up to 7.4 percent in May 2022. This marked the highest inflation rate since May 2014, underpinned by soaring prices of food. While the effects of the global food crisis on malnutrition are not yet fully known, it is anticipated that without timely interventions that cushion families from the increase in food commodity prices, vulnerable households will spend a higher proportion of their entitlement on energy-dense cereals to cope with the increase in food commodity prices at the expense of micronutrient-rich fresh fruits, vegetables, animal proteins and other nutritious diets. This means that many refugees and vulnerable Bangladeshi households will not meet their nutritional needs, which will increase the risk of malnutrition, including micronutrient deficiencies and their consequences. Children under 5, adolescent girls, and pregnant or breastfeeding women are particularly vulnerable to food security deteriorations given their higher nutritional needs, making any nutrient deficiencies even more severe and causing long-lasting consequences on their own health and productivity, as well as their offspring. Overall vulnerability in the Bangladeshi community has also shown an upward trend since 2017 and has remained high since 2020, with 52 percent of the population moderately to highly vulnerable in 2021 up from 51 percent in 2020. The main drivers of continued vulnerability were economic contractions during COVID-19 lockdowns and a decline in economic activity across most sectors, causing reduced income opportunities and market volatility in a population highly dependent on daily wage labour. Further, as average daily wages are not increasing with rising costs, the most vulnerable are disproportionately bearing the brunt of increased prices. In addition, Cox's Bazar has one of the lowest enrolment and completion rates in schools across Bangladesh. Low income rates, exacerbated by the global food crisis, put pressure on families to take their children out of school for financial reasons. Furthermore, a significant number of Government primary schools do not have proper functioning WASH facilities and learning materials, hindering the learning experiences of children. Considering that half of the refugee population make up school-aged children (UNHCR, November 2022), it is critical to provide nutritional incentives to sustain regular attendance.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Overall, WFP is reviewing all costs to improve rationalization of its operations including consolidating outlets where possible, reviewing food costs and supply chain optimization. Blanket Food Assistance will cover the entire population of the Rohingya refugees with a single pipeline. Extremely vulnerable populations (approximately 250,000) will receive complementary food vouchers through Fresh Food Corners located in the same e-voucher outlets. Where possible, the outlets and the SCOPE/Building Blocks system will be used by other agencies for soap and hygiene kit distribution. Under the livelihood programme, graduates will focus on business growth by: 1) linking to local supply chains for agricultural produce; and 2) connecting producers to markets for non-agricultural products. To ensure the businesses’ sustainability and profitability, market linkage initiatives are promoted. Farm production is channeled mainly through aggregation centres, while off-farm production is supported with an EFSN beneficiaries’ shop in Cox’s Bazar. WFP will work with FAO and other FSS partners to consolidate and reduce the number of aggregation centers. The aggregation centres link the Bangladeshi livelihood participants to local markets and WFP-contracted retailers of e-voucher outlets and Fresh Food Corners; playing a key role in enhancing social cohesion between both communities and increasing the localization of the WFP’s food supply chain. WFP school feeding programme is implemented in all 33 camps in Cox's Bazar and in the surrounding sub-districts in the host community also as the sole provider. WFP and partner NGOs maintain close coordination with the sector partners, CiC and RRRC offices in the camps, and with Districts and Upazila Education office and community including parents, school management committees (SMC). WFP has been implementing the school feeding programme for 20 years and plans to continue the support in coming years.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

WFP incorporates protection principles in designing and developing programme interventions in Cox’s Bazar to ensure that all activities target the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. WFP carried out various assessments and analysis; selection for interventions and beneficiaries is informed by vulnerability monitoring and classifications, based on REVA and FSOM findings. These updates and assessments on vulnerable groups help programmes adjust their target groups while maintaining documentation on the impact of programmes on vulnerable populations. WFP has followed PSEA guidelines and inter agency referral pathways to report sexual exploitation and abuse, GBV and other forms of violence. Onsite protection monitoring, protection referral and follow-ups further complement protection assessments.

3.2 - Gender

WFP prioritises gender equality as a central feature of its policies, operations, and commitments at the global, regional, and country level. WFP has three key objectives to address the gender inequalities that continue to exacerbate hunger and nutrition around the globe: 1. Achieve equitable access to and control over food security and nutrition; 2. Address the root causes of gender inequalities that affect food security and nutrition; 3. Advance the economic empowerment of women and girls in food security and nutrition. To achieve these objectives, WFP addresses gender equality and women’s empowerment through a twin-track approach. Firstly, gender is mainstreamed into WFP’s strategies, guidelines, training, and tools, as well as into project design, implementation, and evaluation. Secondly, WFP carries out gender-targeted actions and activities.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Recognizing that children are often more vulnerable to protection risks, and often lack the means/opportunities to seek support when needed, WFP is committed to integrating protection strategies and actions into its programmes to reduce exposure to risk and to support a more protective environment where children can grow and learn. WFP has clear guidelines and never engages child labor in its activities and strictly monitors the participation process in the implementation process. WFP will provide orientation sessions and awareness raising for staff and cooperating partners. Child protection focal points remain present during the distribution of entitlements to prevent possible safeguard-related issues. WFP provides additional top-up food assistance support to child headed households to meet their basic food and nutrition. Assistive and flexible nutrition services are provided to both parents and children with disabilities.

3.4 - Diversity

WFP works to streamline gender, disability inclusion, elderly and other diversified people (including LGBTQI) to support the participation of all beneficiaries across its programmatic interventions, and to ensure their rights and safety from the diversity perspective. Trainings and capacity building exercises are conducted with WFP staff and cooperating partners on basic concepts, frameworks, and programmatic applications of diversity approaches. For meaningful engagement, a number of communication materials are in use to ensure adequate awareness raising of the frontline workers. Distribution sites have segregated waiting areas, toilet facilities, and help desks managed by at least one female staff member/volunteer. At nutrition sites, priority is given to pregnant and nursing women with disabilities, and caregivers of children with disabilities. Porter services are provided for vulnerable households.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

WFP re-committed to ensuring accountability to affected populations (AAP) through adoption of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (C-AAP). WFP exercises active processes and actions that meaningfully place affected populations at the center of humanitarian response with the aim of providing assistance that meets people’s needs, elevates existing capacities and dignity, and helps strengthen long-term resilience and independence from humanitarian assistance. WFP’s priorities and approach to AAP is outlined in the Protection and Accountability Policy (2020), elaborated in the Community Engagement for Accountability Strategy (2021 – 2026) and mutually reinforces the Gender Policy (2022) and the Disability Inclusion Roadmap (2020). WFP has established a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which includes a Hotline, help desk, and complaint boxes, for receiving feedback and complaints from the targeted populations.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project has DRR elements and environmental issues, and climate change mitigation measures. There are reforestation activities including land stabilization in the whole camp to mitigate the impact of the heat wave, landslides, siltation, and desertification. It will create a positive impact on forests and wildlife, ecosystems, and natural components (air, soil, and water) of the environment. Drainage cleaning activity will help to manage the healthy watershed.
