Project Summary

The Federal Ministry of Education Sector Strategic Plan (ESSP) 2018-2020’s access and retention to education remains a challenge with only 23% of children accessing education opportunities in Central South Somalia. Nearly, 77% of children continue to be out of schools, including 54% of primary school aged children in rural areas and 45.9% in urban areas. The primary GER for IDPs is only 16.6%, compared to a national GER of 32%. The JMCNA in Lower Shabelle for June-August 2021 outlook identified main causes to be conflict and natural disasters including drought and seasonal flooding. Top three challenges faced by IDP girls to access education including protection issues and rights of CWDs are actual conflict/ fear of conflict in community or surrounding area at 26%, real conflict in surrounding areas at 23%, drought at 17%, early marriages and boys preferrance to girls. Recent flooding has displaced 11,000 households in 21 villages (30% women, 28% children,7% people living with disability,12% elderly while 23% men) in Lower Shabelle including Marka, Qoryoole and Wanla Weyne districts. The report confirmed 2,800 households (HHs) displaced around Marka district and 5,000 HHs displaced in Wanleweyn districts, respectively.
Reasons why children in Marka, Qoryoole and Wanla Weyne stopped attending school since the outbreak of covid-19 were closure of schools at 77%, lack of transportation to school especially for physically disabled learners at 44% and parents preference for children at 24%. The most commonly reported preferred needs for learning are basic learning materials at 74%, text books at 62% and other materials like uniform at 33%.
On school WASH; 67% of school WASH facilities were found to have been damaged by the May 2021 floods either through structural damage or via water infiltration/spillage. 82% of the displaced learners don’t have a latrine and are practicing open defecation thus raising the risks of AWD and other hygiene issues. Most learners and teachers decried their inability to practice proper hand washing due to lack of soap and clean water.
On access to safe drinking water; 60% of the population depend on river water for domestic use. 72% of the assessed schools show learners carrying water to school. Recent drought and subsequent failures in rain has left most schools without safe drinking water with only 21% of the learners able to access water albeit not good for consumption (AYUUB assessment Jan 2021). 75% of the schools in displaced settings have no clean water and are fetching water from stagnant water or from surface runoff.
On protection, most commonly reported areas in the community where girls/female do not feel safe are on their way to school at 47%, water points at 22% while 15% of girls feel unsafe at toilets. Girls expressed fear of ridicule by boys when visibly seen to be attending to their periods. Other protection needs noted included trainings for teachers and CECs on GBV related issues, PSS and referrals for MHPSS for learners exhibiting trauma.
The proposed interventions will improve access to basic primary education targeting 14749 most vulnerable children (7,400girls, 2,200 children with disability, 8,850 children in IDP settlement and 5,899 children from poor host communities. The interventions will include: Back to school campaign integrated with Covid-19 prevention messaging; School based GBV activities for children; Teachers and CECs training on GBV relevant to education programming; Support for adolescent girls with menstrual hygiene kits; Rehabilitation of classrooms with ramp and handrails for learners with disabilities; Improve School WASH facilities such as construction/rehabilitation of gender friendly latrines, handwashing facilities, basic hygiene and sanitation supplies; Provision of safe water and improved hygiene promotion; Teachers and CECs training on school based GBV, pedagogy, school governance, psychosocial skills and Establishing/Engaging Child to Child clubs.
