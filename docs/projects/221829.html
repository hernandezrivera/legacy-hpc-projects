Project Summary

South Sudan faces a severe food insecurity crisis, with areas like Jonglei (Bor, Canal/Pigi, Ayod, Fangak), Lakes (Cuiebet, Rumbek North), Central Equatoria (Terekeka), and Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East, North) classified as Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Crisis (IPC phase 3). Vulnerable households, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and returnees are most affected, struggling with critical food and nutrition shortages.
The crisis stems from escalating violence that has caused loss of life, displacement, and livelihood disruptions. Years of severe flooding have submerged farmland, decimated livestock, and impeded aid delivery. Economic shocks, including currency depreciation and high food prices, have further reduced household purchasing power. Climate-related shocks, such as flooding and dry spells, along with crop and livestock pests and diseases, have impacted production. Disruptions to multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance and the ongoing conflict in Sudan exacerbate the crisis. Combined, these factors have led to low agricultural output, prolonged asset depletion, and weakened coping capacities, leaving millions in urgent need of assistance.
In response to this, Community Organization for Development and Empowerment will aim at improving food availability and enhancing the resilience of vulnerable communities against future threats associated with large-scale displacements. Our approach will focus on promoting sustainable farming practices designed to mitigate climate change impacts. This includes implementing agroforestry systems to improve soil health, reduce erosion, and capture carbon dioxide. By encouraging intercropping, it will diversify crops, minimizing risks associated with pests and extreme weather, while enhancing soil fertility. We will provide training for contact farmers and farmer groups in conservation agriculture techniques, enabling them to maximize yields while minimizing costs. This training will also cover value chain development, equipping farmers to better process their produce and explore new market opportunities. CODE will facilitate the establishment of community food banks, promote gunny bag gardening, and support kitchen gardens to bolster food security in vulnerable households. These initiatives will empower families to produce and store their food, ensuring they have access to essential nutrition. In collaboration with other partners, CODE will distribute early-maturing vegetable seeds and agricultural tools to farmers in targeted areas, with the aim of restoring food security and expanding nutritional diversity within communities. Recognizing the importance of fish as a protein source, we will support local fishing initiatives, improving production capacity and diversifying food sources. We will distribute fuel-efficient stoves and briquettes to reduce cooking costs and improve household energy efficiency, which is crucial for vulnerable families. We will provide direct food assistance to help alleviate the burdens faced by vulnerable groups, including the elderly, women, girls, individuals with disabilities, and child-headed households, ensuring they have access to sufficient nutrition. Our project will also be providing psychosocial support and safeguarding against gender-based violence and abuse, creating a safer environment for all community members.
Throughout the project, CODE’s MEAL teams will conduct regular field visits to produce results, ensure accountability to the communities we serve. Our holistic approach will aim not only to address immediate food security needs but also to empower communities to build a sustainable future, enhancing their resilience to ongoing challenges.

1. Needs

According to IPC (September 2024 - July 2025 projection), South Sudan's food insecurity is projected to remain critical, driven by frequent climate shocks (flooding, dry spells), economic crises (currency depreciation, high food prices), and worsening income opportunities. Factors like waterborne diseases, crop and animal pests, and conflict further strain livelihoods. Poor market functionality, degraded roads during the rainy season, and the ongoing Sudan conflict have led to disrupted supply chains and increased pressure on host communities in the north due to a continued influx of returnees and refugees.From September to November 2024, 11 counties and 126,000 returnees (20%) are classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), 62 counties as Phase 3 (Crisis), and 6 counties as Phase 2 (Stressed). In the first projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 4.33 million people (32 percent of the population analysed) are likely to face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), 1.71 million people (13 percent of the population analysed) will likely face IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). There are 17 counties and an estimated 220,000 (35 percent) returnees who will likely be in Phase 4, 52 counties who will likely be in Phase 3, and 10 counties who will likely be in IPC Phase 2 (Stressed).Based on the analysis of key assumptions for the lean season and projected levels of Humanitarian Food Assistance (HFA), 2.53 million people (19 percent of the population analysed) are in Phase 4, and 5.09 million people (38 percent of the population analysed) are in Phase 3.During this period, 44 counties and an estimated 252,000 returnees (40 percent) are likely to face Phase 4, 32 counties are expected to be in Phase 3, and three will likely be in Phase 2. Severe acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains critical, requiring an urgent scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian aid to save lives and protect livelihoods. Priority must focus on counties with high populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), as well as support for returning South Sudanese. In response to this crisis, Community Organization for Development and Empowerment (CODE) aims to implement the project to improve food availability and enhance community resilience. The project will include promoting climate-smart agriculture practices, such as agroforestry, intercropping, and conservation agriculture. Community Organization for Development and Empowerment will also work on value chain addition to increase crop production efficiently. Farmers' groups are being organized to develop community food banks, gunny bag gardening, and kitchen gardens to enhance food security. Community Organization for Development and Empowerment will collaborate with partners to provide inputs like early maturing vegetable seeds and tools to farmers, aiming to restore food security and nutritional diversification. Additionally, CODE will support the pastoral communities with fuel-efficient stoves, animal health, and food assistance to alleviate food expenses. The organization will also address the needs of vulnerable groups, including the elderly, women, girls, people with disabilities, and child-headed households, by providing psychosocial support and protection against gender-based violence. During project implementation, CODE’S MEAL teams will conduct field visits and progress reports in various locations to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of their interventions. These areas include Kapoeta East, Cuibet, Pibor, Fangak, Uror, Twic East, Canal/Pigi, Malakal, Luakpiny/Nasir, and Panyikang.

2. Project activities

• Identification, registration, and Verification of 25,000 vulnerable HHs amongst IDPs, returnees and host communities as primary beneficiaries of the project in the project locations in consultation with other stakeholders like the community leaders and the local authorities. • Distribution of vegetable kits to 3,000 HH vulnerable (1 standard Kits - 180g/HH) -based on FAO technical standards in the areas of Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East, North and South).; Rapid post distribution monitoring conducted for the household receiving kits. • Rapid training of 3,000 beneficiaries on vegetable farming in Jonglei, Lakes, Unity, and Eastern Equatoria states; Support Contact farmer’s feed disseminate key production information and submit reports. • Training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and community-based extension service providers (CBESP). • Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support. • Ox Ploughs & training. • Maintain support to small scale subsistence producers in locations with less agricultural potential and include animal health support. This should also include emergency nutrition, especially during the lean season. • 150 Self-selected producer groups formed as a platform for transferring improved agricultural technologies and making stronger market linkages. Agricultural training modules include social cohesion, gender, and conflict and nutrition sensitivity. • Recruitment of 30 Field Extension Agents (FEAs) equipped with modern agricultural education and skills. • Training of Community Based trainers (CBTs) as TOT on VSLAs. • Cascade training of community beneficiary groups on VSLAs. • Provide flood adaptive seeds and approaches (rice, floating gardens), tools and fishing inputs to farmers. • Pre-position grain and seed protection kits (hermetic bags, tilted buckets, grain bag etc.). • Monitoring of small enterprises and support for small startups to strengthen markets. • Support sensitization for 600 Pregnant, breastfeeding mothers and caregivers living with under 5 to address barriers to good nutrition during the monthly feedback meetings. • The project will support vulnerable households with food supplies and severely malnourished will be linked for/supported with food by prescription. • Setting up of 15 mobile Growth Monitoring out post, cooking demonstration and education on complementary feeding using commonly available foods. • Sensitize and Link youth, women and adult GBV survivors on Development of IGA’s. • Scale-up provision of humanitarian assistance (food and Non-food items) to counties in Crisis (IPC Phase 4 and 5). • Adopt revised global guidance on population based assessments so that real time data is available on the nutrition situation. • Conduct assessment and review existing early action protocol (EAP) • Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support. • Good Agronomic practices (e.g. farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management etc). • Monitoring and Evaluation, this activity will be undertaken throughout the project. The MEAL team will conduct field visits throughout the project to evaluate the progress. Cross-cutting Activities In this project, CODE will work closely with local community leaders, authorities, women, and people with disabilities, and displaced community leaders to conduct assessments. The aim is to gather valuable insights and ensure that the voices of these community members are heard and considered. Additionally, the project places a strong emphasis on mainstreaming Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention and implementing the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures. This is essential to protect vulnerable individuals from all forms of abuse at every stage of the response. The project aims to expand and enhance access to essential services, including clean water, sanitation, and healthcare services, all year round. This will contribute to the well-being and overall quality of life for the communities in need.
