Project Summary

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023 (draft), approximately 5.9 million people including 3.75 million children (2.2 million girls and 1.55 million boys) and 2.1 million women are in dire need of nutrition assistance in Syria, an increase from 5.5 million people in 2022Syria is faced with a triple burden of malnutrition- high chronic malnutrition depicted by high prevalence of stunting, increasing levels of acute malnutrition and emergency levels of anemia among children and women of reproductive age. Aggravating factors promoting malnutrition include food insecurity, elevated food prices, devaluation of the Syrian pound, scarce and unsafe water use, disease spread and loss of household income.About 277,403 children aged below 5 years suffer from acute malnutrition. The number of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases among children aged 6-59 months increased by 45.6 per cent - from 51,013 in 2022 to 74,296 in 2023. Of the 277,403 children, about 203,106 will suffer from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in 2023- a similar trend depicted in 2022.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

SR will do many actions to mainstream protection and gender across all project stages as following: • SR conducted a rapid needs assessment by using Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with males and females separately as well as the most vulnerable groups such as disabled and PLW to ensure the program design meets the exact need for beneficiaries to avoid doing harm in the implementation stages.• Female staff will be hired for the nutrition team, to recognize and address gender and power dynamics and to ensure that people do not experience coercion, discrimination, or violence/mistreatment/disrespect/abuse in receiving nutrition services.• Ensuring that services and supplies are affordable or free, accessible to all without any discrimination.• The nutrition facilities should be designed in a way that ensures the confidentiality and privacy of each beneficiary especially for women and girls.• Safeguarding environment should be considered to prevent harm and reduce the risk of abuse or neglect to adults and children. • Confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries will be clearly informed where to refer or report incidents or violations.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1-Sexual exploitation and abuse risk against beneficiaries especially women and girls.2-Gender-Based Violence Risk against beneficiaries.Mitigating Measures:• All staff will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA.• PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the location of the project, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp).• All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies to ensure full adherence to the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously.• Ensuring that services and supplies are affordable, free, accessible to all of beneficiaries without any discrimination or exploitation. • Establishing internal SEA reporting and investigation mechanism, with ensuring that all protection cases, including GBV and PSEA will be referred to the respective focal point in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security is the major and high risk across Syria. The security situation remains unstable in NWS. There might be a high risk of closure of borders due to recent political developments.likelihood: High, İmpact: high. Mitigation: SR owns a warehouse in its Idleb to store life-saving drugs and other essentials for use as a backup in case of any security incident. Disruption in medical supplies: Due to the situation in Syria supplies of essentials, particularly drugs can be affected due to closure of borders. It poses a medium risk.2.Risk: spread of epidemic diseases such as Covid-19 disease and Cholera.Likelihood: high, Impact: medium.Mitigation measures: SR will apply precautionary measures about COVID-19 as well as WHO recommendations for staff and contractors in terms of hygiene practices, using PPE, and maintain physical distance for safety. In addition to raising awareness towards Cholera, and updating the staff about all the measures according to cholera taskforce.
