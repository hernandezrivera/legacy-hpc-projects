Project Summary

Through this project, Violet organization aims to target the IDPs inhabitting the camps in Idleb and Aleppo governorate with multi-sectorial services as following:
1 - graveling 11 camps; 5 in Idlib governorate (2 in Aldana and 3 in Salqin) (9200 individuals ) and 6 in North of Aleppo (Azaz and Jarablus) (34850 individuals).
2 - Ground insulation for 7 camps (1940 tents) by compact gravel (10950 individuals )
3 - Tents replacement in Harim and JesrAlshogor Districts (15000 individuals ).
4- Provision of fire safety training IDPs in 17 camps (10 camps in northern Aleppo and 7 camps in Idleb governorate) as well as equipping these camps with the necessary safety means.
throughout the project implementation, Violet will ensure the timely reporting for the CCCM cluster and other humanitarian actors through its IDP tracking team. additionally, the Gender Action Plan matrix will be conducted for all activities of projects, from designing, recruitment, assessment conducting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation to put standard plan to make sure the projects meet the minimum standard of gender and protection mainstreaming. Project teams will be gender balanced and trained on gender awareness. This includes removing barriers that inhibit the recruitment of women to field teams in particular. Furthermore, Violet will ensure the beneficiaries participates in the project, especially women and adolescents to take their suggestions and discuss their needs to make sure the assistants provided suitable for them. Lastly, All activities will require the completion of a participation plan (e.g. accountability mechanisms), in addition to protection risk assessment, monitoring and modification as part of the project cycle."

Needs

According to HNO 2019, the northwest of Syria has witnessed more than 1 million population movements in 2018, many of these movements were for IDPs who had been displaced multiple times. On average, more than 96,000 monthly population movements compared to 82,000 in 2017 and 40,000 in 2016 in north-west Syria. The main causes of these displacements were renewed military offensives in Idleb, Aleppo, Hama governorates (particularly in September-December 2017). Arrivals were also reported form Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. According to the CCCM reports, Only in northwest Syria, there are 322 sites, including 286 informal settlements, 10 collective centres, 22 planned camps and 4 reception centres in Aleppo, Idleb, Hama, Ar-Raqqa and Der-ez-Zor: Afrin. These sites, are hosting 426,353 IDPs (as of October 2018). Approximately 25 and 26 per cent of the population in sites, do not received adequate shelter and NFI assistance, respectively. Challenges also persist in sanitation, waste removal and water (15% of the population). the CCCM IDP tracking shows that the majority of recently displaced arriving in camps spent time in other types of sites, such as rented accommodation, host family and collective centers, before moving into IDP settlements or camps. As of the end of 2018 the overall population of IDPs in sites had reached 426,353 IDPs and the CCCM Cluster has tracked 272,193 new displacements into the camps since September 2017. A total of 73 new sites were registered with CCCM, 12 in Aleppo and 61 in Idleb governorate. A large proportion of IDP sites in Syria are informal settlements, also known as “spontaneous” or “self-established” camps. These usually consist of groups of tents or other shelters that were set up by IDPs themselves or other actors. Crucially, these sites are not managed by anyone and often have no legal claim to the land on which they were established. In these sites, the delivery of dignified protection and multisectoral assistance cannot be guaranteed by humanitarian actors, a critical issue for vulnerable groups, and particularly for women- and child-headed households. while the number of sites decreased from 346 in 2017 to 317 in 2018, this should not be interpreted as an improvement in the overall situation. On the contrary, many sites were closed due to shifting frontlines in the south of Idleb Governorate, while the overall IDP population in northwest Syria sheltering in IDP sites actually increased by 16 per cent from 362,527 to 420,103 individuals. A space availability exercise conducted between April and July identified an increase of 120 percent in the number of shelters within three communities in Dana sub-district (northern Idleb) alone. In short, IDP sites are growing larger and facing additional pressures in ensuring the delivery of life-saving assistance and basic services in line with humanitarian standards to all displaced individuals.A comparison of challenges faced by IDPs in accessing shelters against the findings of the 2018 HNO show a decrease in reports indicating rent cost or availability as a challenge (which nonetheless remain the main factors, along with substandard shelter conditions) but an increase for overcrowding, and lack of access to collective shelters as challenges in accessing shelter. according to HNO 2019, 43% of the communities reported that IDPs shelters are in bad conditions, while 31% have reported that the shelters are not safe.

Implementation of activities

Through this project, Violet organization aims to target the IDPs inhabitting the camps in Idleb and Aleppo governorate by implementing the following activities1 - graveling 11 camps; 5 in Idlib governorate (2 in Aldana and 3 in Salqin) (9200 individuals ) and 6 in North of Aleppo (Azaz and Jarablus) (34850 individuals). the gravelling will be conducted for the camps with the thickness of 15 CM. 2 - Ground insulation for 7 camps (1940 tents) by compact gravel (10950 individuals ). 3 - Tents replacement in Harim and JesrAlshogor Districts (15000 individuals ).4- Provision of fire safety training IDPs in 17 camps (10 camps in northern Aleppo and 7 camps in Idleb governorate) as well as equipping these camps with the necessary safety means. The camps' graveling and tents ground insulation activities will be conducted through contracts with service providers, while the fire safety training and ground insulation activities will be provided by Violet technical teams. Additionally, Violet will ensure all project stakeholders are aware of HLP rights. As for the HLP, Violet will make sure HLP is respected and won't proceed with the ground insulation, graveling, tents replacement activities unless getting the owners approval. To this end, Violet will ask for proof of registered HLP ownership and verify ownership claims before proceeding the activities implementation. It will also ensure signing the MoUs with the camps management and other stakeholders including the Housing Lands and Properties rights. Violet will ensure the timely reporting for the CCCM cluster and other humanitarian actors through its IDP tracking team. additionally, the Gender Action Plan matrix will be conducted for all activities of projects, from designing, recruitment, assessment conducting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation to put standard plan to make sure the projects meet the minimum standard of gender and protection mainstreaming. Project teams will be gender balanced and trained on gender awareness. This includes removing barriers that inhibit the recruitment of women to field teams in particular. Furthermore, Violet will ensure the beneficiaries participates in the project, especially women and adolescents to take their suggestions and discuss their needs to make sure the assistants provided suitable for them. Lastly, All activities will require the completion of a participation plan (e.g. accountability mechanisms), in addition to protection risk assessment, monitoring and modification as part of the project cycle."

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During the implementation of the project Violet is expecting to face some risks regarding protection including and not limited to the following: 1 - Risk: Create pull-factor and expose IDPs to physical safety risks when/(if the camps and settlements are located in unsafe areas) - Likelihood : High - Impact: High - Mitigation: Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on the camp establishment and camp support ensuring that camps are the last resort. Tents provision is the beginning of any potential camps regardless if formal or informal. Any distribution of tent should follow CCCM guidelines to minimize this risk. As ISIMM tool captures only the camp that respect the above mentioned principles, ensure that only the locations ISIMM are supported by the cluster members 2 - Risk : An increase in tensions within the camps, as space (a scarce resource) is occupied by this infrastructure and create differences between the camps in terms of services - Likelihood : High - Impact : Medium - Mitigation: Residents should be consulted before the construction of any infrastructure and be made aware of the risks that the current project was attempting to mitigate 3 -Risk: Construction contractors could create some tensions or harm based on their salary payments - Likelihood: High - Impact: Medium - Mitigation: Portion of unskilled construction workers are composed of residents. Maintenance roles on constructed facilities are clarified early to prevent labor-seeking vandalism, Strengthen and involve IDP Committees in engaging with service providers and also the residents to report to the feedback through pre-established complaint mechanisms
