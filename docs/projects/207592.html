Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to improve food security through climate-sensitive and resilience agricultural support, increase production and preservation of agricultural, livestock and fishes in the host communities, specially, emphasizing more where there are still gaps. In addition, the project aims to contribute to sustainable natural resources management through creating ecology and environment friendly surface water reservoirs for water-supply which also provides better opportunity for groundwater recharge and afforestation/tree plantation.
Besides, considering the need of improving food habit, nutritional value of the food that people take, environment protection and ecological balancing, appropriate & timely preparedness and response, the project also plans to implement awareness raising activities in the host areas engaging the communities.
The project activities will include irrigation, homestead gardening, poultry rearing with the provision of required vaccination & medicine for disease control, market linkages for the input and output markets, organizing reforestation/plantation with seedling and material support, and awareness raising on food safety, social cohesion, disaster preparedness, environment conservation. The project will consider identifying needs, gaps and challenges of different gender, age and disability throughout the project cycle.
The project will be implemented in the areas where Shushilan is already working and in close coordination with other relevant sectors and actors including the local government and the government authorities in Jalia Palong, Ratna Palong, Palong Khali and Raja Palong unions of Ukhiya Upazila and Sabrang, Teknaf, Teknaf Paurashava, Nhilla, Baharchhara and Whykong unions of Teknaf Upazila reaching directly to 10,300 people from which 57,645 people are estimated to benefit from these interventions.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Food is the first priority need among both host community (33%). Compared to the last year, 81% of host communities reported that they can afford less to access food, followed by for clean drinking water, household/cooking items, health services and/or medicine, clothing, essential nutrition services for children and pregnant women (1%), education for children, protection services, cooking fuel, income generating opportunities, information, feedback on relief items or services, and conditional cash (J-MSNA, 2023). There is gap in 44% of host community households in the provision of climate-resilient food security support (FSS Coordination meeting, 11/14/2023). The FSC SAG, appreciating aggregation centre model, emphasized on more linkage for sustainability, particularly in livestock, and fishery, BCCI for nutrition, plastic waste management and DRR during the Cox's Bazar for better nutritional conditions and ability to meet the refugee demands (FSC, 10/4/2022). Significant drop-down of ground water level due to increased extraction for refugees with lesser recharge (WASH Sector, Debriefing GTH WASH mission to Cox Bazar, 3/28/2023), lack of groundwater in Teknaf area, salinity intrusion, shared surface water with refugee camps along with drying surface water sources mostly due to deforestation and climate induced disasters such as cyclone and floods are resulting in irrigation water scarcity in many parts of the host communities.The households who received interventions from FAO (treatment group) had improved resilience capacity in 2022 compared to that of 2021, compared to those who didn't receive interventions (control group). The interventions have positive impact among all three farmer categories: marginal, large, and smallholders. Compared to 2021, Ukhiya, Tenkaf, and Rohingya camps have significantly increased resilience index. The improved resilience can be because of higher income, savings, and access to loans, increased usage of technology in agricultural production, receipt of capacity building training, and savings of crops for the lean period. However, marginal farmers and Teknaf are the least resilient. Shocks like landslides and price hike had negative impacts on the resilience capacity (FAO, RIMA, Round-2).

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Shushilan is an active member of PSEA network and reguarly participate in GBV and Child protection sectoral meetings. The project will provide training to its direct beneficiaries on the issues like gender-based violence and GBV Referral Pathway, social cohesion, females empowerment, Complaint-Feedback-Response Mechanism (CFRM), Safe-guarding, Child protection and PSEA which will guide females and males to engage in accountable practices, contribute to create a congenial social environment and relations, reduce gender-based violences, protect from abuse and exploitation and take remedial measures in case of any harmful incidences. Shushilan provides mandatory trainings such as PSEA, Safeguarding, code-of-conduct, whistle blower policies to 100% of its staff members and volunteers. Shushilan keeps all modes of communication to file complaints against its project team such as complaint box, leaflets with zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse with hotline number and email address. Shushilan has active teams and policies maintaining confidential, fair and victim-centered investigation for the complaints it receives. Besides, it follows the guidelines and SOPs of PSEA network while dealing with any of its PSEA related complaints.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will enrol around 12% Person with disabilities (PWD) or their family member as direct beneficiaries. The project will conduct gender analysis and accessibility audit in facilities and service points and ensure accessible venues and facilities for the PWD. Project will organise an initial assessment with beneficiary communities to understand barriers, challenges and solutions during project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation, ensuring inclusion of PwD and people from all sex and age groups among the target beneficiaries. In consultation with Protection and Site Management sectors, if required, the project will organize a survey to identify PWD using Washington Group Questionnaire, adapting in the context. The project will identify gaps, needs and preferences of PWD, and people of different age, sex and diversity, and addresses them through appropriate program delivery models. The project process will be designed to reduce risks of GBV such as different training venues for male and female beneficiaries, raising awareness about GBV, GBV pathways, promote positive role of boys and males for encouragement and conducive environment for females to come forward in the project activities. The project will ensure a functional confidential complaint process that considers gender, age and disability and respond to the requests for changes. The project will deliver different messages through appropriate media according to the gender analysis. The project will be implemented following participatory approach ensuring do-no-harm and protect its staff, volunteers, beneficiaries following PSEA, Safeguarding and Child Protection policies. As Cox's Bazar is prone to disasters, the project will consider possible risks and impacts, and will put efforts to reduce damages and foster early recovery minimizing possible interruption in services and support to the targeted beneficiaries.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project is developed based on the findings from the 2023 JMSNA, participatory assessments and analyses to address the priority needs. We will organize systematic and regular consultation with all relevant stakeholders throughout the project cycle to ensure that the project design, implementation and phase out will ensure optimal benefit for the beneficiaries and the wider stakeholders, bi-yearly consultation meetings to evaluate the project and use them as reference in the lessons learnt workshop at working level, at least one meeting with beneficiaries and the community to explain the selection criteria and process, to identify and address issues which might not be covered by any specific Sector or might be common to several Sectors to ensure they are covered adequately, bi-yearly perception surveys to understand community perception, and plan specific actions to strengthen our CFM, and follow a transparent beneficiary selection process and engage affected population while prioritization.We will translate JRP into accessible format and share with communities, validate priorities with community members, respecting age, gender, vulnerability, and diversity, design a coordinated plan for disseminating messages on assistance delivered, plan ahead, and actions on complaints and feedback through a bi-yearly townhalls at each camp, establish a mechanism to monitor affected population’s perception of the humanitarian response on a regular basis, and of adequacy, quality, and timely information-provision and engagement in decision-making, ensure that the communities are informed within an appropriate timeframe in case of any changes/adjustments to humanitarian services and assistance and encourage a dialogue with donors to enable them to adapt changes stemming from community feedback, and organize community dialogues with clear, accessible, and understandable information regarding any changes, and use appropriate communication channels before any change.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Shushilan is an active member of the PSEA Network in Cox's Bazar. We mainstreaming PSEA in each stage of project cycle management. As part of the recruitment process, we have ensured that 100% of the staff and volunteers have receive mandatory PSEA training which we will continue. We have and will continue having PSEA awareness materials (such as posters, fliers, and videos) including key PSEA messages, and Complaint Feedback Mechanisms in places visible to the staff members, volunteers and the beneficiaries. We have PSEA policy and PSEA committees that ensure availability of confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms. Shushilan takes the necessary actions after receiving SEA complaints that are in line with the SOP for the SEA Complaint Referral in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Shushilan has DRR/DRM and environmental management policies ensuring active pre-intervention social and environmental assessment of its proposed interventions. Shushilan is one of the major actors in coastal areas with anticipatory and post-disaster preventive and response mechanisms. The project will find out venues in consultation with the concerned humanitarian actors and government authorities as relevant to the activities. We expect that our intervention may produce some organic waste which will promote to reuse through composting or properly dispose. In case soil is taken out, we will coordinate with relevant authorities to use without harming environment and as far as possible to protect environment such as for land stabilization as appropriate. We will raise awareness about DRR, Environment and ecology together with organizing plantation/reforestation engaging the community people and adolescent children for promoting sustainable positive attitude towards environment conservation and ecological balance. We will promote homestead gardening and high-yield crop production using integrated pesticide management and no-chemical-based insecticide. Hence, the proposed interventions have no environmental risk, and promote environmental preservation. Besides, orientations will include topics such as proper disposal of wastes, Disaster risk reduction, barriers for females and prospect of their contribution in prosperity, PSEA, Gender-based violence and the GBV referral pathway, CFRM with hotline number and email address to submit complaints. Hence, the project will not only organize their works in environment-friendly way but also promote a better natural as well as social environment in the project areas.
