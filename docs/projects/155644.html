Project Summary

The situation in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon has gradually deteriorated since October 2016, with strikes causing public services to shut down and blockage of food movement. Persistent tensions and hostilities between non-State armed groups and security forces in these regions have resulted in the displacement of estimated 27,877 Cameroonians into Nigeria, and up to 437,000 people (227,760 women, 209,240 men) are estimated to have been internally displaced. The IDPs are highly food insecure and in need of the immediate humanitarian assistance. In 2019, WFP Cameroon plans to provide unconditional in-kind food assistance with full-ration to the most vulnerable 400,000 IDPs in the North-West and South-West as well as in the neighboring regions of West and Littoral for 12 months to address the urgent food needs. Food security and nutrition assessments are conducted both with face-to-face surveys and mobile surveys to accurately understand the ongoing situation and food insecurity status of the IDPs and the host communities. Regular monitoring activities will also be conducted so the data will be collected based on the performance indicators for reporting on a monthly basis.

Needs

In July 2018, the Food Security Monitoring System (FSMS) revealed that 10 percent of households in the North-West and South-West were food insecure. All households with severe food insecurity have poor food consumption, regardless of the region. In addition, almost all households that are moderately food insecure have poor or limited food consumption (98%). In the North-West and South-West, where more than 70% of the population is dependent on agriculture, insecurity causes farmers to abandon their fields or fail to meet the schedule. This should lead to the reduction of agricultural production and and increase of prices for staple foods such as maize and beans. As a result, the depletion of stocks and the deterioration of the purchasing power of households expose them to acute food insecurity crisis. In addition, The MIRA conducted in the South West region in 2018 also stated that the food security situation of IDPs including Kumba and Mamfe regions is severely deteriorated (100% of the IDPs interviewed). IDPs living with families in Kumba and Mamfe (82%) have indicated that they have no means to purchase food or support themselves and they have no source of income and no possibility to carry out livelihood activities or other income generating activities. Approximately 65% of IDPs survive with aid from neighbors or relatives or some of them borrow food. As coping strategies, around 69% of IDPs have reduced the number of meals per day. IDPs are highly vulnerable to food insecurity so they need urgent humanitarian assistance.
