Project Summary

The proposed action will target the most vulnerable people affected by or vulnerable to food insecurity to help recover or improve their livelihoods and have improved access to food sources. The intervention will address urgent food security needs while building on the long term factors that encourage vulnerabilities.
The action is a 12 months project aimed at increasing and securing the food access of 14 960 ( 8976 males and 5984 females) vulnerable household and includes the rehabilitation and restoration of agricultural producing factors which have been destroyed or damaged, both at community and private level.
Agricultural related infrastructures, such as irrigation canals and systems, farm lands, greenhouses, grain storage facilities, animal shelters and warehouses and also repair of fisheries structures, will be rehabilitated by Cash for Work schemes.
Small farming equipment will be supplied to vulnerable producers. Highly vulnerable herders will receive emergency distribution of animal feed and/or fodder with a feed ration (level) covering a minimum of three months.
Integrated technical support and training in crop and livestock production addressed to vulnerable farmers and herders and aimed at strengthening their resilience against future natural related stressing factors. The initiatives will be informed by food security and livelihood assessment (including nutrition sensitive data), carried out together with the local communities.
ACF approach intends to reinforce the resilience of rural communities by re-establishing communal and public infrastructures and, contextually, to boost domestic food production with the aim of contributing to household food security stability. The above will be pursued by focusing on integrated and sustainable approaches to food production, promoting principles of conservation agriculture adapted to climate changes matched with water saving techniques.
The proposed action will so work closely with technical stakeholders (such as the Directorate of Agriculture) at national and local level in order to identify the needs and to include them in the project implementation. ACF will also contribute to building and upgrading their capacities, with aim of restoring and/or strengthening the extension services normally provided to the rural communities.
Furthermore, during the project, a gender analysis will be conducted to identify and understand how the different roles, activities, needs, vulnerabilities and capacities of women and men of all ages are influencing the agricultural sector. The gender analysis in the livelihood and food security sector will assess: a) what the main livelihood assets in the community needs, b) who has the control of them, what are the role of women and men in the agriculture sector, c) how is the property and ownership of the land within the HH, d) what are the existing and the needed skills for men and women and what is the unpaid work divided within the HH. The food security and livelihoods assessment will also detail the seasonal activities of women, girls, boys and men in the pre production and post production cycle of each of the major local crops and livestock. Under CFW, consideration will be made to consider women in CFW participation and priority for less physically demanding jobs will be accorded.
The project will target the most affected populations, focusing on IDPs, returnees and host communities in Ninewa, Dohuk, Anbar and Salah-al Din. ACF will prioritize women and elderly headed households, people without an income source, who are moving out of closing camps, facing new or secondary displacement, youth, SGBV victims and people with disabilities. The proposed intervention will take into account their specific needs to include them in the activities.

Needs

Since 2013, Iraq was engaged in a war against the so-called Islamic State (ISIS). Iraq has lead several military campaigns to regain control of its territory, which resulted in a number of large scale population displacements. Although the Iraqi Government officially announced the end of the fight against ISIS in December 2017, the humanitarian crisis in Iraq remains one of the largest and complex in the world. The large return movements expected in 2018 did not happen, with still 2 million displaced in September 2018, and in 2019, most IDPs will likely not return until sustainable conditions are met. According to REACH assessment in Anbar in August 2018, 50% of IDPs living in camps have intention to stay in the camps in the next 12 months, and 75% of IDPs living in informal camps. In 2019, it will be necessary to respond to food security urgent needs as well as addressing underlying factors for returnees, IDPs and host communities.Over 31,000 houses have been destroyed due to ISIS conflict, in addition to hundreds of public and commercial facilities and infrastructure projects. The World Bank estimates that reconstruction could take at least ten years and will cost upwards of $80b USD. Almost half of the population requiring assistance reside in Ninewa. Returns, displacement, and secondary displacement across Iraq remains complicated with some families unable to return home – due to security factors, lack of basic services (water access, sanitation infrastructures, livelihood opportunities, destruction of houses, etc.). Other families, with perceived affiliations with IS will remain in the camps with no possibility to return to their areas of origin. According to IOM DTM as of 31 October 2018, the total number of returnees was at 4,113,660 across 1,568 locations of return. 1,866,594 IDPs remain in displacement. Ninewa (1,591,734 returnees) remains the highest governorate witnessing returns in October, followed by Anbar (1,283,292) and Salah al-Din (575,736). 94 location have no return as confirmed and verified by DTM data; 23 are in Ninewa, 39 in Salahadin, main reason for non return are security issues and block.Food security has been severely impacted by the combined effects of active conflict, large scale multi displacements and returns, political and economic insecurity, disruption of livelihoods and employment, chronic malnutrition, moderate increase in food prices, increased household expenditure on food, regularity of or access to Public Distribution System (PDS), limited agricultural production and reduced coping capacity or use of adverse coping strategies. According to the Multi Cluster Rapid Needs Assessment (MCNA) findings disseminated in September 2018, an average 37% of IDPs, returnees and host communities is in need of livelihood assistance. According to the draft HNO and HRP Framework Analysis 2019, the most affected areas are concentrated in Diyala, Ninewa, Dahuk, Anbar and Erbil governorates. Host Community is the most affected population, with around 329,000 food insecure people, then the returnees with 237,000 and refugees and IDPs with 104,000 and 92,000 people in need. Vulnerable host and IDP households still remain at risk of becoming food insecure due to increasing pressures on dwindling livelihoods, unsustainable coping strategies and protracted displacement. In areas such Ninewa, has been noticed a decrease in the % of returnees and resident household with borderline food consumption, while percentage of households who reported using food related negative coping strategies increased in all the surveyed districts by 16 percent on average in June-July compare to April 2018 .Agricultural, fishery, and livestock activities are low across all Iraq due to looting and damage caused to agricultural tools and machinery, which are difficult to replace due to inflated prices. Late or poor rains have already affected cereal production particularly in the South. Generally, the district affected by drought are those whose household reports the highest use of food based negative coping strategies. Rural livelihood and resilience activities targeting returnees and host communities in Ninewa, Salahdin, Kirkuk, Anbar, Diyala are now considered priority needs.In general, there are various gender issues and needs associated with food security programming and in Iraq; men ‘traditionally’ control the means of food production and money while women are more responsible for managing the food produced and preparation and other household items and commodities. The proposed food security response will ensure that 50% of beneficiaries are female so as to significantly foster gender equality. ACF has a gender policy (March, 2014) which seeks to increase the impact of ACF’s work through gender equality programming and a gender lens will be applied in all aspects of the MPCA project, including staffing.

Activities

Objective 1: Provide agricultural inputs or services to protect agricultural productive assets and help restore fragile livelihoods of returning or vulnerable familiesA1.1: Base-line measuring main FSL ACF (r-CSI, HDDS, MDDW) and cluster indicatorsA1.2: Community based meeting and local committee creation will help ACF to assess and decide list of infrastructures in need of rehabilitation through the CFW schemeA1.3: Cash for Work scheme implementation to rehabilitate and restore productive agricultural assets at community level. ACF will implement the scheme according to SoPs agreed at national level by the relevant clusters and as per experience developed in the last years in the country. A1.4: Provision of agricultural inputs and on the spot technical assistance to highly vulnerable household and producers will be done according to the assessment results and will target different categories of producers (farmers, herders, fishermen). The inputs will include small equipment, local seed, fertilizer (production of compost will be promoted) and any other inputs might be necessary to a full recovery of food production local system.A1.5: Animal feed and/or fodder provision will be distributed to those in need herders to cover immediate needs, and while restoring pastures areas at community levels. The intervention will be decided as per need assessed and integrating the crop production recovery which will include fodder production as well.A1.6: Distribution of animal vaccines will be done in coordination with the concerned authorities. Via the information sessions (A2.3) producers will be put in contact with the technical authorities in charge of the services, while ACF will provide for emergency cost covering. A1.7: Post Cash Distribution Monitoring carried out after cash disbursement A1.8: End-line measuring main FSL ACF (r-CSI, HDDS, MDDW) and cluster indicators Objective 2: Improve quality of the response based on evidence, capacity building and strong coordination with national stockholders.A2.1: Local community and stakeholders mobilization and coordination to create FSL assessment (including gender analysis) tools. ACF will coordinate and involve key local community stakeholders in order to tailor the type of tools used and to assess the length of the investigation. Integration of different tools will be privileged. A2.2: FSL assessment implementation: the assessment will include secondary and primary data collection and will be followed by analysis phase. A2.3: Sharing the information with local communities and stakeholders, will help ACF to triangulate information collected, to promote appropriate targeting, to empower the communities and ensure a sustainable recovery.A2.4: Information sharing sessions and trainings on sustainable agro-pastoral practices will be carried out with the participation of target groups including local relevant public authorities and will be facilitated together with local technical expert of the Directorate of Agriculture. A2.5: Awareness sessions on nutrition sensitive balanced household’s diet will be carried out at HHs levels in community based organization and addressed mainly to women. Considering the high number of organization working in the targeted districts, ACF will actively participate in Food Security cluster mechanisms at different levels (national and sub-national) in order to promote coordination and avoid duplication during the intervention. Relevant information such as Food Security Assessment results, lessons learned, best practices and challenges will be shared among the FS organization. Proposed capacity building training will be opened to other organization staff when possible. Furthermore ACF will promote a holistic approach, implementing other sectoral projects in the same areas of the FS project such as MHCP and Wash activities. This will maximize the impact of the intervention. When it will be not possible for ACF to use a holistic approach within its project, dialogue with other NGOs working in different sector will be established in order to find complementarity during the implementation of the activities.

Protection Risk Analysis

Specific protection risks identified by ACF include: 1) Security and political environment in the targeted areas deteriorates. The security threats includes possible ISIS attacks, and possible violence due to tensions between tribal and ethnic groups. While the political issues include tensions between Kurdish Region of Iraq and Federal Iraq, the militia groups running the roadblocks and the local communities and humanitarian agencies. There is also uncertainty due to the newly install Government in Federal Iraq,, as it is new and thus unproven and there are tensions between the Ministries and the Governors. 2) Reduced access to targeted population At times due to security or political issues, ACF has found itself unable to access its project sites. When there are access issues ACF coordinates with UN OCHA.There are multiple levels of lack of access:• Foreign staff cannot access the project sites, but national workers can.• Foreign and non-local national staff cannot access the project site but local staff can.• No staff has access to the project site. 3) Unexploded ordinance and mines in the targeted areas 4) Inter group tensions can occur that could have the potential for violence (IDP/refugees/host community, but also farmers/herders), along with tension between ACF & communities/beneficiaries due to targeting issues. 5) The project may impact intra-household dynamics by encouraging women participation (due to social norms and the household’s prioritization of male household members as breadwinners). This involves a risk of backlash towards women or supporters of the project in the community. 6) Sharing personal data of refugees, IDPs or others affected individuals or households with third parties, potentially putting them at risk of violence, detainment or discrimination 7) Cash distributions may present protection risks if not well organized, considering of specific needs of vulnerable groups.

Mitigation Measures

1) ACF has a Safety and Security Advisor based in Iraq to maintain abreast of all new safety and security issues as they represent themselves as well as maintaining awareness for consisting safety / security concerns. ACF’s Safety and Security Advisor is a member of the coordination team and is consulted when considering new locations / areas for projects. ACF constantly monitors the security and political environment by maintaining contacts with UN agencies and other partners (INSO, local authorities). ACF frequently revises/updates its operational security plan. If the conditions warrant, ACF may temporarily stop implementation in areas, or change areas or methodology of implementation. 2) ACF ensures access through regular and effective communication and cooperation with authorities at Governorate and community level and with other relevant actors. ACF reports access issues to UNOCHA. If needed, ACF will try to implement remote implementation through local staff, etc, in order to continue to serve the beneficiaries. 3) ACF works with UNMAS and the mine/UXO clearance agencies to train ACF and contractor staff and to ensure that work areas are cleared of mines and UXOs. When a UXO or mines is found. ACF will report it and request it be cleared. If it is in an area where a work is being implemented work will be stopped until the danger is removed. 4) ACF will integrate the recommendations developed on “conflict sensitivity approach into Project Cycle Management”. ACF conflict sensitivity lenses used in the country include meeting with community leaders and local officials to discuss and coordinate projects. ACF will have well-designed eligibility criteria and targeting based on context, community inputs, evidence and objectives of transfers; may need to modify targeting to ensure inclusion of different groups, host community, women, etc. ACF clearly explains its rationale on operational strategy to the community, local leaders and government authorities. ACF establishes and explains a Complaint and Feedback mechanism for the beneficiaries. Post distribution monitoring to include questions on social relations and “Do No Harm”. ACF ensures transparency on criteria and clear implementation guidelines. Particular attention is dedicated to prevention and mitigation of tensions occurring in areas of water scarsity where herders move into already under pressure lands in search of pastures and affecting its productivity. Community and participative problem analysis will lead to integrated solution for the two categories. 5) The project will ensure that the community understands the project and participates at all stages. ACF takes into account the complex dynamics associated with gender and roles. 6) ACF is entitled to a Data protection Policy, which is included in the implementation. For example, contract with service providers include provisions in line with data protection policy. 7) Cash distributions are organized in a safely manner and follow procedures already tested in the country. They will be tailored to the type of setting but will be done via Money Transfer Agent – Hawala namely - minimizing all possible risks. Main measures minimizing the risk are: sharing list of beneficiaries and cash with the Hawala only three days before the distribution; informing the beneficiaries about date and location of distribution only one day before; choosing location closed and protected from external eyes; avoiding the labelling with visibility plates; separating if necessary women from men for major comfort of the receivers.

How is your project accountable to affected population

For this project, ACF will have feedbacks and complaints mechanism system for each sector and activities, part of the MEAL plan. In addition to establishing a formal feedback and complaints mechanisms, the collection of feedback and complaint will also be incorporated into regular monitoring activities during implementation. For instance, routine on-site monitoring conducted by project and MEAL team will be an opportunity to ask beneficiaries questions to solicit feedback.Direct interview, formal surveys (such as post distribution monitoring, satisfaction survey) and informal ones (fields visit) will also allow the ACF team to get feedbacks from the population we aim to help and evaluate not only their satisfaction regarding to ACF services but also relevancy and quality of our actions. Feedback obtained through these mechanisms will be included in analysis and, when possible, will be used to inform programmatic decision-making and adjust the intervention. Analyzed feedback will be reported to relevant stakeholders and managers and lessons learned will feed into decision making, readjustment of project plans when possible, and as well as, future project planning.During the implementation, Action Against Hunger will also directly involve its beneficiaries in the decisions related to them: - Households and individuals will give their informed consent to benefit from the project’s activities, including Action Against Hunger’s direct services and referrals to other organizations. - Action Against Hunger’s beneficiaries will always have the right to refuse the offered services. - Action Against Hunger’s beneficiaries will have opportunities to provide feedback on the services, in particular through MEAL survey Action Against Hunger aims at building the capacity of the community. Therefore, Action Against Hunger will also involve community through the identification of areas and certain types of activities. Moreover, Action Against Hunger will work in a close collaboration with the different authorities concerned which will be also a mean to ensure the sustainability of the project. In this regard, liaison with relevant governmental departments for field assessments (joint where possible) and written authorizations will be ensured.When conducting formal and informal interviews with beneficiaries, ethical consideration such as confidentiality is always an important consideration. Therefore, ACF teams will constantly reassuring respondents who are consulted or who voluntarily gave feedback, that the information they are giving will remain confidential and will be kept within ACF. The consent of the beneficiaries will be asked prior to each interview; none beneficiaries will be forced to answer the questions. In case of any critical or sensitive protection issues that may be raised, ACF team will report the sensitive case to the identified senior management team.
