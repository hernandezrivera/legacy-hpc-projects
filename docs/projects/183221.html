Project Summary

The proposed action is a COVID-19 emergency response intervention and contributes to the allocation strategy in providing life-saving emergency assistance to vulnerable populations in Lebanon by supporting COVID-19 case management and improving community engagement in the governorate of Saida. The intervention complies with the overall strategy of the Health sector under the ERP 2021-2022, namely to provide essential short-term support to meet vulnerable people’s critical needs in terms of health care, support the response capacity of the Lebanese health system in coping with the COVID-19 emergency, and enhance assistance for migrants.
The proposal is based on assessments and visits conducted by DPNA in Saida and by ARCS in Saida Governmental Hospital (SGH) between January and June 2021, with the full support of the hospital’s staff and administration. Visits and assessments have identified key priorities and operational practices to better contribute to the overall project objective. Specifically, the project will provide SGH with medical equipment and PPEs for COVID-19 case management and will empower and protect vulnerable population of Saida through community awareness activities on preventive healthcare practices and COVID-19 immunization, including support to vaccine registration, mapping of services and referral mechanisms. In addition, the project intends to address the energy crisis and to contribute to Objective 2 under the ERP (Health) "Support health facilities to maintain operations during the ongoing emergency context" by providing SGH with a solar panels system, which will sustain critical wards and laboratories, including the dialysis center, vaccination and PCR labs.
The proposal, closely coordinated with the MoPH, UNHCR and WHO, is in line with the priorities defined by the “COVID-19 Lebanon Appeal” issued by the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs in July 2020, and contributes to the Sector objectives, insofar as it focuses on supporting public hospital through the provision of equipment and on strengthening health promotion and community outreach on relevant health topics. By supporting the hospital through medical equipment and PPEs provision, the project increases the preparedness and capacity of SGH to safely cope with new waves of COVID-19 while contributing to the smooth functioning of the remaining wards and units operating in the facility. The equipment procured for COVID-19 response will then serve the hospital’s needs in the post COVID-19 phase and will increase the quality of services for vulnerable population accessing SGH, which reflects the added value of the project in terms of sustainability. In parallel, the project will contribute to lower the COVID-19 pressure on public hospitals by supporting the immunization campaign, and diffusing information and practices on preventive healthcare. Activities under objective 3 and 4 are fully covered by LHF and will be implemented between 01/08/2021 and 31/01/2022. The activity foreseen under objective 2 is requiring extra funding and intends to be implemented between 1/02/2022 and 31/05/2022. Thus, the overall project is already funded by 57%.
The project will reach a total of 8,249 direct beneficiaries, including around 4,000 hospitalised patients, 249 hospital's employees and additional 4,000 vulnerable individuals (1000 vulnerable households) of Saida governorate.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Objective 2 “Support health facilities to maintain operations during the ongoing emergency context”. The project intends to provide SGH with a solar panels system (including UPS, ATS and inverter batteries) to sustain the functioning of priority wards and laboratories.Objective 3 “Respond to COVID-19 in Lebanon”. The project supports SGH by providing equipment to address shortages and replace old and deteriorated ones, as well as PPEs to guarantee the safety of healthcare workers and the protection of patients (BoQ approved under LHF).Objective 4 "Provide enabling, cross-cutting support to coordinated, safe, timely and quality health responses with an emphasis on vulnerable communities". The project contributes to increase information and registration for COVID-19 immunization, and information on relevant preventive healthcare topics (Mental Health, SRH, Wash and Hygiene, Nutrition) and protection through tent spots and door-to-door campaigns (Activity 4.2). The project is activating coordination tables with local organizations working with migrants in order to link migrant beneficiaries with the project activities thus to increase the impact within migrant communities of Saida. As main migrant communities in Saida are represented by Sudanese and Egyptians, IEC materials will be drafted in Arabic; other minor communities will be supported by outreach workers of local NGOs within the coordination tables. Target: 4,000 individuals from the vulnerable communities of Saida.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Gender and protection mainstreaming is integrated within the project. Project staff have been trained in Protection to ensure their capacity to mainstream protection principles. Youth and women are actively encouraged to join the community-engagement effort of the project.Volunteers’ teams are gender/age balanced, and therefore more capable of receiving and addressing specific gender protection concerns of the population.Awareness-raising activities gives a particular focus on women, as women in Lebanon have often the highest responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and children; this adds to the fact that inadequate facilities and practices for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls. A particular attention is given to SRH and access to SRH services during COVID-19, considering the little knowledge among women (UNFPA). The GBV referral mechanism put in place by the Protection Sector is implemented in case hardship cases are identified.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ARCS and DPNA apply the UNHCR AGD Policy by: integrating community engagement in all aspects of the project and recognizing the capacities and needs of the target groups; sharing information and communicate in a culturally appropriate and accessible way to target groups; ensuring that all persons of concern enjoy their rights, have access to protection, services and assistance, and can participate fully in decisions that affect them, their families and communities; establishing and maintaining effective feedback systems, using communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern; learning from continuous engagement with communities of concern and adapt interventions accordingly; Monitoring system: weekly visits by ARCS PM to SGH, to DPNA field activities and constant contact with the hospital management and DPNA coordinator; weekly coordination meetings DPNA and hospital; preparation of internal monitoring reports for all activities; Focus Groups Discussions (FGDs).
