Project Summary

Adequate hygiene practices, and access to water to practice safe hygiene is the single most efficient measure against COVID-19, but many vulnerable communities do not have access to clean water. WV will provide vulnerable remote communities with access to clean water, and promote safe hygiene practices, to support GoH in the preventing the spread of COVID-19, and safe lives. This project aims to save lives of the most vulnerable children and their families affected by COVID-19 through improved access to water, sanitation and hygiene.

1 - Needs

There is an urgent need for emergency WASH provision in COVID-19 affected areas. An estimated 750,000 people require safe water for drinking, cooking, and personal hygiene, according to OCHA. The WASH cluster has prioritized “ensuring access to safe water”, “sensitizing affected people on public health risks and cholera protection” and “ensuring adequate excreta management and elimination in affected areas” for the COVID-19 response. Handwashing with soap and water is the single most efficient measure against COVID-19, yet in Haiti, 60 percent of households having neither water nor soap at home for handwashing, 66 percent not treating water before consumption and 26 percent not having access to an improved water source (40 per cent in rural areas). In La Gonave, the situation is even worse with only 25.1%households having safe and sufficient drinking water within 30 minutes from their home (WVH LQAS, 2019). Moreover, 45%, uses water from unprotected sources (rainwater, uncaptured water sources unprotected wells, and untreated water from trucks), which exposes them to waterborne diseases. An initial post-COVID-19 Rapid Assessment conducted in April 2020 by WV in La Gonave, indicated that there were challenges in terms of adoption of certain behaviors to prevent the spread of COVID-19. Another key challenge noted was the availability of handwashing soap, and chlorine in the areas. Due to price increase, most communities in the region are more in need of soap and chlorine, because of their increased prices. Additionally, in La Gonave in particular a key issue observed was access to water due to its remoteness.WV assessments show that most water is being collected from broken water pipelines and surface water in rivers, lakes and spring water, which are at high risk of contamination. Hence, it is important to prioritize public awareness around hand and respiratory hygiene, and access to water to vulnerable households, and in key public institutions. WVI has already been responding to the WASH needs of the affected populations in La Gonave through mass sensitization, hygiene kit distribution, hand washing facilities installation in public areas. Thus far, WVI has been able to reach more than 60,940 people in La Gonave through various WASH activities; a large portion of this has been majorly through mass sensitization efforts (COVID-19 information and prevention).With the requested funds, WVI will be able to reach 5,000 more beneficiaries with essential WASH services, especially access to water in order to prevent the spread of COVID-19, as well as protect the most vulnerable communities. To implement this project, WVI will work with DINEPA, MENFP, MSPP, school leaders, church leaders, municipal authorities and other local partners for a better integration of the interventions of different actors in the field. WVI will draw on its 30+ years’ experience in La Gonave, and its WASH activities within its areas of intervention to apply the best approaches to ensure that project objectives are achieved. WVI will also respect the guidelines established by the MSPP in response to COVID-19 to ensure the safety of its staff and members of the targeted communities.

2 - Activities

Facilitate access to clean Water Supply This will target to improve safe water access for 5,000 COVID-19 affected households (25,000) in rural communities through rehabilitation of six damaged water points. 1. Waterpoint rehabilitation/ extension - WVI will support in the construction/rehabilitation or extension of water points/kiosques, especially in areas with limited water availability. Currently, in La Gonave, according to WV assessment of the zone, has 38 nonfunctioning water points, and around 24 non-functional water kiosques. WVI will coordinate with local DINEPA, and community members to rehabilitate and repair affected water points (those feasible within 3 months and most urgent). For this project, WVI will support in ensuring the functionality of 6 water collection point (taps/hand pumps/kiosques) in the two communes of La Gonave, to ensure adequate water service extension. Rehabilitated waterpoints/kiosques will benefit more than 3000 households in the targeted communities of La Gonave. 2. In addition, as community water points are usually managed by Water Point Committee (WPC). World Vision will assist community to form and train 6 WPCs, where WPC does not exist or is non-operational. Female community member will be particularly encouraged to join WPC (the ratio of 60% Male /40% Female). Training will then be provided to the newly-formed or existing WPC on proper management, utilization and maintenance of water points to ensure suitability. 3. In collaboration with TEPAC (Ministry responsible for water services at rural level), WVI will distribute water treatment inputs for water systems and water points. WV will distribute HTH chlorine (approx. 45kg per drinking water supply systems), WV will also work with the Drinking Water Supply and Sanitation Committees, as they will supervise and assure daily water treatment. In addition, WV will purchase and install a metering pump equipment to 12 water points, it will be under the supervision of WPC, to assure management and water treatment. Promotion and installation of handwashing facilities/stations. 4. WVI will promote the use of institution-level public handwashing stations. WVI will work with 50 key institutions (schools, health centers and churches) to make sure that there are handwashing facilities (including buckets, soap and disinfectants). In the case that schools open in August as planned by the Ministry of Education, WVI will emphasize the provision of key hygiene materials for schools. 5. To reinforce hand washing at critical times for most vulnerable households during COVID-19, WVI will promote installation of basic hand-washing devices within 200 households in La Gonâve, to install tippy taps or buckets with water and soap. Methods of reaching communities with the targeted messages will be done through COVID-19 safe community sensitization. Raise public awareness about hand and respiratory hygiene 6. WVI will promote understanding and awareness of COVID-19 along with hand and respiratory hygiene through context appropriate channels (e.g. sound trucks, small groups practicing social distancing, home visits, radio, social media, posters, SMS) by leveraging MSPP authorized messages. Hygiene promotion will be provided for key beneficiaries at different levels, in schools, health centers, and for households, with emphasis on practices to limit the spread of the outbreak. WVI will deliver continues messaging on COVID-19 to combat misinformation and fake news, as well as stigmatization. The activity will target 5000 people from the communes of Anse-a-Galet and Point-a-Raquette through hygiene promotion and sensitization. WV will leverage its faith leader networks in the area to support with community outreach. Relevant materials and resources will be provided to support trained community health workers to conduct hygiene promotion, targeting in the two communes of La Gonave.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WVI recognizes that the essence of responsibility lies in respecting the needs, concerns, abilities and willingness of the people we work with and in taking responsibility for our actions and decisions. A feedback and complaints mechanism will be established in close collaboration with communities. Depending on the community, feedback mechanisms will take the form of: face-to-face meetings; community summits; hotline; letter; follow-up meetings; suggestion boxes; help desk; feedback boxes. WVI works with the most vulnerable to identify critical needs and seeks to ensure their right to receive assistance and protection on the basis of their informed consent. WVI reaffirms its commitment to receive and respect their views, timelines and the relevance of our interventions. The accountability framework aims to empower communities and is based around the following standards: - Transparent sharing of information, - community consultation,- community participation and involvement in decisions and activities that impact on the community, and- the effective implementation of feedback mechanisms and efficient claims management Feedback for each mechanism listed will be treated and analyzed confidentially. This data will be analyzed, and a report will be shared with the program team leader. WV will closely monitor and follow-up with data from established feedback mechanisms considering women and youth participation.
