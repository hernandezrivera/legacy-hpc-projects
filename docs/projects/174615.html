Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security, economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target area.
NRC Cameroon’s Livelihood and Food Security (LFS) programme will target displacement-affected people based on vulnerability criteria and not their displacement status only (IDPs, returnees (former IDPs) and host communities). NRC will apply social and economic vulnerability criteria to identify vulnerable households most at risk to food insecurity. Priority will be given to female-headed households, child-headed households; households, pregnant and breastfeeding women; elderly people (60 and above); households with a person with physical or mental disability or debilitating chronic disease. For the economic vulnerability criteria, NRC will use as a reference: poor or limited food consumption score; no food stock, number of meals per day, no productive assets, and loss of income. For agricultural restoration activities, NRC will also include the following criteria: having at least one able-bodied person in the household who can farm land. NRC’s LFS programmes will also target as far as possible vulnerable youth and women for vocational training and economic empowerment.
To address the intermediate needs of affected populations NRC will use cash, e-vouchers or in-kind assistance based on the results of market analysis and cash feasibility studies. To maximise the chances of success in this activity, NRC will as far as possible couple livelihoods interventions with food assistance to meet basic food needs. Moreover, NRC will implement complementary activities such as training and awareness raising sessions to support target households. Programme activities will gradually scale down and phase out so that by the end of the projects, communities have the skills and resources needed to be more resilient to shocks and crises in the medium and long term. NRC will work in coordination with local partners such as local technical and administrative structures and will collaborate closely with them so they can provide technical support during programme implementation and continued follow-up after the programme ends.
The LFS programme will include Cash/voucher assistance, livelihoods promotion and enhancement applying a market-based approach (MBA) that refers to local traders so to improve access to income and revive/strengthen the local economy. The intervention will be geared towards supporting the most vulnerable displacement-affected households and at the same time building their capacity in food production, Livestock production and Income generating activities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since 2013, the humanitarian situation in Cameroon is compounded by three main crises: Boko Haram (BH) attacks in Lake Chad Basin in the Far North; the Central African Republic crisis in the East, Adamawa and North and the Anglophone crisis in the South West and North West regions. Social and political claims in the North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions in 2016, met with violence from security forces, escalated in 2017 in a crisis that has paralysed the two regions for several years. Since 2018 clashes between non state armed groups and the security forces have caused major displacements in the two regions and to West and Littoral regions and subsequent humanitarian needs continue to attract the attention of the international community and humanitarian actors. Over 15,780 individuals from 2,625 households were reportedly displaced in the NWSW regions in June 2020 due to ongoing violence and this number continues to increase. About 679 000 IDPs were registered in NWSW regions since the beginning of the crises. An additional 58,000 people have sought refuge in neighbouring Nigeria. The occurrence of COVID-19 pandemic in addition to the pre-existing security and displacement crises, placed further strains on the country, especially in the eight regions (out of 10 regions in the country) affected by the three humanitarian crises. To reduce the expansion of the COVID-19 pandemic Strict measures, including the closure of borders and movement restrictions were immediately introduced by the Government of Cameroon and Nigeria, these had an immediate impact on vulnerable households. Farmers were selling crops at low prices due to border closure. At the same time, purchasing power decreased leading to potential food insecurity. Most displaced people rely on agricultural activities and small businesses to survive. These activities were also affected due to decreased purchasing power and movement restrictions. According to a survey conducted by NRC in August 2020, food security appears as the first needs of men, women, girls and boys. Access to arable land is the second concern of men and women, followed by support for the implementation of small businesses. Women are mostly concerned about the increase in household expenses due to COVID and are thinking more to engage in petty trade as a palliative measure. Men are more concerned about the lack of agricultural inputs and access to land and the rise in the cost of living due to COVID-19. Overall, 2.685 039 people are in need of food security assistance in Cameroon from October to December 2020 and humanitarian needs have greatly increased since the intensification of the crisis in the NW/SW regions, An estimated 959,801people in the NW and SW regions fall within the Crisis and Emergency phases of food insecurity between October and December 2020 (CH October 2020), which shows need for urgent action to protect and revive livelihoods, reduce food consumption gaps, and reduce acute malnutrition.
