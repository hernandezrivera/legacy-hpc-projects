Project Summary

The project will provide conflict affected populations residing in areas of high humanitarian concern within Donetsk and Luhansk, GCA, with the means to cover their critical needs and protection requirements through multipurpose cash (MPC) grants, with a focus on more vulnerable groups including women headed households.

Needs

INSO data suggests a decreasing trend in the average of weekly conflict-related incidents so far in 2018 (from 236 in 2017 to 175 in Jan-June 2018), with a renewed high peak however in spring 2018 (306 incidents in one week). Ninety-four percent of the incidents take place within 5km of the contact line (44% GCA and 50% NGCA).Due to the line of contact (LoC), major economic hubs (industry and markets) are now located in the Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA). Pre-conflict market networks have been disrupted, leading to a loss of economic opportunities for the entire region, but especially for residents of areas close to the LoC (REACH ABA 2017). Given intersectional vulnerabilities, this has particularly impacted women and the elderly.The population within the 5km zone is disproportionately female (57%), retired (36%) and elderly (33%); 67% of those aged 60+ are women, 36% have a chronic illness (REACH Trends Analysis, 2018). Fifty-nine percent of HHs within 5km are headed by women, including 90% of single-parent HHs. Children make up 18% of the target group (44% girls). Eighteen percent of those 50-59 years old (ineligible for pensions) report being unemployed. Figures are similar overall for the population within 20km, with two exceptions: 60% of those aged 60+ are women, and 2% aged 50-59 years old report being unemployed.As per the analysis undertaken by ACTED for the Cash Working Group (CWG), the gap between average incomes and actual subsistence level (3,235 UAH average for the first eight months of 2018, tracked by MoSP) continues to grow. As of October 2018, this gap is estimated at 47% given an average monthly income of vulnerable population estimated at 1,700 UAH per person. Primary data from vulnerability analysis performed for ongoing ACTED projects (October 2018, MPC caseload) suggest an income gap of 49% for women-headed HHs, up to 69% for women headed HHs with children. The income gap for HHs of elderly is 38%; this increases to 40% for single elderly HHs, of whom 90% are women. Increasing income gaps hinder the ability of vulnerable populations to meet their basic needs; key informant interviews highlight prohibitive transportation, healthcare, food and NFI costs (REACH ABA 2017; ACTED program data 2017-2018).The FSL estimates that 30% of women headed HHs in GCA have poor or borderline food consumption scores (FCS). The proportion of HHs with members of pre-pension age (40-60) with poor or borderline FCS is greater than elderly HHs, at 27% vs 22% (Food Security and Socio-Economic Trend Analysis, March 2018). Latest REACH data shows that the proportion of HHs with poor and borderline FCS – highest closest to the LoC – increased from 13% in 2016 to 16% in 2018, with a peak during winter 2018 at 21%. This corresponds to market monitoring data which shows that prices for food – amongst other goods – increased by 11% between August 2017 and February 2018. Other costs – medication, fuel (coal and firewood), and hygiene – increased by a respective 1%, 26% and 10%. Notably, market monitoring shows that there are no significant bottlenecks regarding supply chains: the first barrier faced in accessing all types of goods on the market is the high prices.The project will thus provide cash assistance to individuals unable to cover their basic needs, and will target the following in doing so:- 60% women;- 37% elderly;- At least 15% children;- 10% with a disability;- 5% IDPs.

Activities & outcomes

ACTED will provide 2 rounds of MPC assistance in urban and rural areas of Donetsk and Luhansk GCA, aiming to cover 6 months of assistance for 2200 individuals (970UAH/person/month as per CWG 2018 guidance).In line with 2018 ACCESS CSI endline analysis, and the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview, targeting will focus on the 5km area (where multi-sectoral HH insecurity is highest).Given the protracted conflict and diversity of critical needs across sectors and groups, MPC remains a cost-efficient modality to provide rapid and efficient assistance to highly vulnerable populations where banks and markets are functional and accessible.The modality selection is informed by beneficiary feedback: 98% of HHs surveyed post-distribution under ACCESS reported being able to meet primary needs with MPC assistance provided. Overall, beneficiaries reported satisfaction with beneficiary assessment, selection and distribution processes, finding them transparent, suitable and accountable.ACCESS CSI endline analysis shows that MPC distribution resulted in a 30% decrease in the proportion of HHs in the highest CSI category; 70% showed a non-increasing tendency. HHs with children had the biggest decrease.Beneficiaries will be selected in coordination with CWG partners to avoid duplication and referrals will be sought from humanitarian actors. Under ACCESS, ACTED refined beneficiary assessment and selection, now based on cumulative vulnerability – taking HH status, gender and age-based indicators into account. For cases of extreme isolation or sector-specific vulnerabilities, ACTED will refer individuals to projects with in-kind modalities and/or for appropriate protection or sectorial assistance. ACTED will also facilitate the referral of eligible beneficiaries to government safety nets programs.ACTED will use the CSI methodology (revised under ACCESS and adopted as guidance by CWG partners); it includes an updated list of coping strategies to better reflect the multi-sectoral nature of HH insecurity and explore changes in HH application of coping behaviors before and after cash distribution.ACTED will consult ad minima 80% of communities targeted. The project and selection criteria will be presented in community meetings to local authorities, community representatives and volunteers. This ensures that feedback from affected populations can be internalized as necessary, and that referrals are provided from the communities themselves so that the assistance is sensitive to their needs and vulnerabilities. ACTED ensures that beneficiaries have access to Complaints and Response Mechanism, managed by an independent AME team, e.g. through posters and contact cards distribution. Community representatives are informed of internal ACTED and donor accountability processes. ACTED’s AME team conducts spot check monitoring with beneficiaries during implementation, and community focus group discussions upon assistance delivery to receive and incorporate beneficiary input.PDM includes a beneficiary satisfaction survey and is conducted after every round to assess whether negative coping strategies have been prevented and to monitor the use of cash: information and feedback collected is used to adjust subsequent programming.ACTED will use a range of cash modalities to account for beneficiaries’ additional vulnerabilities, e.g. limited mobility, including financial service providers which have home delivery systems: beneficiaries will be asked their preferred modality. ACTED will focus on:- Women headed HHs, incl. with children and especially single parents (who are poorest, with lowest FCS);- HHs of elderly, more likely to be women and face chronic illnesses or isolation;- HHs with members with disabilities (additional vulnerabilities and possible limited mobility);- HHs of pre-pension age without an income (incl. unemployed);- Men headed HHs with children (majority of children reside in men headed HHs);- IDP HHs.
