Project Summary

This is an urgent emergency response to reinforce the capacities, resilience and protection of vulnerable Palestinian communities in Area C of the West Bank from natural and manmade hazards.
The action includes the rehabilitation and upgrading of shelters and provision of capacity building and training, which will reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries to both man-made and natural disasters, weather conditions and risk of water borne diseases and health problems, by enabling them to live with adequate standards, and improve their preparedness (remedial and environmental building action)
The action aims at preventing, reducing, mitigating the threats against persons and communities and support resilience building in face of protracted threats and chronic vulnerability while responding to their needs in terms of shelter and protection, and indirectly WASH and Health.
Providing adequate shelter and protective assets will contribute to reduce and mitigate the effects, vulnerabilities and risks associated with the coercive environment in Area C, especially women, children, elderly and PWDs. Shelter rehabilitation and upgrading activities, coupled with the implementation of the community participation methodology will allow to advance in the fulfilment of the objectives of empowering communities, in terms of coping capacities, ownership and expertise, and protection response mechanisms, enhancing their resilience and capacities to respond to protracted crisis and mitigation and preparedness to environmental and man- made hazards.
The action, which is fully in line with the 2019 HRP Strategic Objective 2 and 3, complies with the minimum SPHERE standards for adequate housing, Shelter Cluster minimum agreed standards, gender, and adequate access to housing by people with disabilities. This integrated approach endeavors to ensure that communities are sufficiently protected and equipped with both tangible and intangible assets, in order to remain in their current location of choice, and thus prevent their forcible transfer.
The beneficiaries of this action are among the most vulnerable households in terms of socio-economic and protection related vulnerabilities which are connected to the physical and political-related characteristics of Area C communities, including H2 Hebron. Residents of these communities are highly affected by setter and ISF violence, lack of access to basic services, inadequate shelter, and movement restrictions among many other hindrances.
Specifically, the action will target 30 communities (8 in Hebron -including 3 in H2-Tal Al Rumeideh, Haret al Salaymeh, Wadi Al Hussien); 2 in Bethlehem; 1 in Jenin; 3 in Jericho; 1 in Ramallah; 15 in Tubas) and 1326 beneficiaries (666 adults – 334 women and 332 men, 68 elders-36 women and 33 men, 359 adolescents- 164 girls and 196 boys, 234 children-110 girls and 124 boys). GVC is present in 24 of these communities since 2015 as focal point in the WBPC and MAAN has been actively working in the rest of the communities in shelter rehabilitation over the years.
The shelter rehabilitation will target 150 HH through different techniques (rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof, rehabilitation of concrete shelter with asphalted roof with internal renovation works; provision of PVC plastic cover). In addition, GVC and MAAN will conduct capacity building to the community members on self- implemented house renovation and techniques to improve the shelter conditions, including protection from natural hazards and climate, and to ensure privacy and safety. GVC and MAAN will support the targeted communities who suffer from inadequate and overcrowded shelters, with activities and sessions to contribute to self- recovery, to reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk, and to sensitize the communities about privacy inside shelters, WASH facilities hygiene and privacy, PWD minimum living standards.

These high-priority interventions will be implemented in partnership by GVC and MAAN (already partners since 2015), in coordination with the Palestinian Authority-related bodies. The action is part of a multi-year action which aims at covering the total assessed needs (462 shelters in need of rehabilitation) in 3 years’ time. The partnership will capitalize on GVC’s and MAAN presence and experience in shelter rehabilitation as well as outreach capacity to the communities. . The action foresees the self-rehabilitation of shelters, implemented using a community implementation approach which relies on the active participation of the community to reduce protection risks and improve coping mechanisms. The action responds to communities’ needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of personal safety and dignity
The action aims to support gender and age-sensitive relief provision and gender and age-responsive protection as a means to meeting specific material and safety needs, ensure gender and age responsive assistance and equitable access to humanitarian goods and services, prevent and mitigate potential negative effects of the Action on different gender and age groups and ensure adequate participation. The action aims at responding to the differentiated vulnerabilities of all community members, considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. In this regard, analyses and strategies utilized throughout the project take special consideration of the vulnerability and needs of women, children, people with disabilities and the elderly. Based on a protection-centred approach to humanitarian responses it aims at meeting the humanitarian and protection needs of communities at risk of FT. The action is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment. It aims to reduce the beneficiaries’ exposure to forced displacement by mitigating the effects of the above-mentioned factors and to restore more dignified living conditions

Needs

As part of the Community-based Protection Approach (CPA), in the framework of the ECHO West Bank Protection Consortium, GVC has been collecting quantitative and qualitative data in the targeted communities since 2015 to collect protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities, and coping strategies. The protection analysis focuses on community engagement. The last round of assessments carried out in June 2018, highlighted the persisting difficult environment, continuously challenging protection threats, restriction of movements, and restricted access to basic service (including shelter). GVC has participated in the OCHA multi- sectorial assessment in H2 in June 2018, and the shelter assessment led by the Shelter Cluster completed in May 2018. This data, along with the joint assessment carried out by GVC and MAAN during the proposal elaboration, highlighted the following issues: presence of old shelters in urgent need of rehabilitation, including severe electrical damage, persistent dampness, and leaking and caving roofing, and many other critical needs; incapacity of the shelters to guarantee proper summer insulation and proper hygiene conditions; need for installation of doors and windows to prevent the entrance of animals, as well as to mitigate the harassments from Israeli Forces and settlers; need for privacy (especially for women who suffer from lack of privacy in their rooms and in the WASH facilities inside their homes and for boys and girls to facilitate their homework); unhealthy and unhygienic WASH conditions due to old sewage systems in their shelters; proliferation of bacteria and presence of snakes, scorpions, and centipedes into the shelter; prevention of work on the site by Israeli DCL citing security reasons. The assessments shows that the inability of the community to improve structures is due mainly to economic deprivation. Data analyses conducted by GVC show that specific groups are extremely vulnerable and exposed to these risks, particularly new couples, households lead by women, and pregnant women (pregnant women often stay outside the community for at least three months until the newborn is able to endure the harsh conditions of the inadequate shelter), as well as children, elders and PWD. Women represent a vulnerable group, given the patriarchal culture that stresses women’s role in the domestic sphere, they specifically suffer from improper and unsafe living conditions and they are more vulnerable to poverty and diseases. The same applies for children, elders and PWDs. During the design of the project the different needs of men, women, boys and girls collected though the above assessments and related with the rehabilitation of the shelters have been taken into account,. The identified activities will be further appraised through technical assessments and community consultations in line with the applicable humanitarian standards (e.g.: SHPERE) and the parameters of the Shelter Cluster, also factoring social acceptance factors to insure gender and age-appropriate solutions. Furthermore, social team will study the situation of the shelters with all family members and specially women and girls to define the main problems related to health, hygiene and women/girls privacy, and transfer this information to the technical staff, to define the suitable and accepted solution with the family members and access to PWD. These will be taken into consideration in the final design, drawings and BoQ to make sure to adapt them to the indications of the end-users. Women will be involved in designing the rehabilitation of the houses, based on the needs they expressed through the brainstorming meeting with field staff. The project will place particular attention on the vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities and families headed by women) as these are spending a great deal of time at home, according to the social customs and tradition of the selected communities.

Activities

The action will entail the provision of winterization assistance to vulnerable households at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards (e.g. winter storms, flooding) and Shelter upgrading through providing cash or materials assistance and technical training; Community capacity building and awareness raising ; Technical assessment will be conducted prior to the intervention to design interventions and formulate the MOUs, specifying the materials delivered for each HH and labor work allocated for each HH. Based on the Community Implementation’s methodology, rehabilitation/upgrading will be completed by the beneficiaries themselves, whom will establish local project committees, one in each community committees (at least 30% of the members are women), that will be in charge of the implementation of the activities. MOU signature and handover is foreseen with the local committees, which assume the responsibility of the implementation of the works. Rehabilitation of shelters, will guarantee proper ventilation, decreased water leakages, improved hygienic conditions, and privacy, in compliance with SPHERE standards. Households whose currently shelters do not comply with minimum SPHERE standards and shelter minimum standards will benefit from renovation activities which will include proper tiling, plastering and painting of the shelters, ensuring proper accessibility for people with or without disabilities, adequate insulation, ventilation, prevention of rain-water leakages, improvement of the WASH connections and facilities, replacement of doors and windows. The renovation works will include also, when needed, WASH infrastructure in the shelters, with improved health conditions and the water, sanitation and hygiene management and preparedness for the hot and cold temperature (e.g. through summer/winter isolation when relevant). GVC and MAAN will provide technical support and supervision to the shelter upgrading through weekly field visits conducted by GVC, MAAN to monitor the smooth implementation of the Intervention. Constant coordination with the Shelter Cluster, and stakeholder: Hebron Governorate (based on ongoing MOU) and Hebron Rehabilitation Committee (HRC) Palestinian DCO, HRC, OCHA, ICRC and OHCHR will be ensured. The specific technique selected for each community depends on several factors (among other, the status of land ownership) and include: the rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof: (the composite shelters consists of concrete sides with a roof composed of metal or zinc roof, polystyrene between wood and metal that functions as an insulation material. They are comprised of steel framing covered with metal sheets, insulated, and include a wooden floor framework treated with fireproof material; rehabilitation of concrete shelter with asphalted roof with internal renovation works; provision of PVC plastic cover (supporting the structure of metal bars that the community members already own). GVC and MAAN will conduct capacity building to the community members on self- implemented house renovation and techniques to improve the shelter conditions, including protection from natural hazards and climate, and to ensure privacy and safety. GVC and MAAN will support the targeted communities who suffer from inadequate and overcrowded shelters, with activities and sessions to contribute to self- recovery, to reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk, and to sensitize the communities about privacy inside shelters, WASH facilities hygiene and privacy, PWD minimum living standards (2 sessions per community). The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming targeting vulnerable families through a response to their immediate needs, while increasing their protection and resilience to manmade and natural shocks, integrating risk reduction and coping strategies to improve the provided emergency assistance and reduce the beneficiaries’ exposure to forced displacement.

Indicators

The purpose of this action is to address, by assisting families in restoring their shelters, the communities’ humanitarian and protection needs, while improving their living and health conditions and contribute to safety and dignity. Specifically, the action will lead to the achievement of the following indicators: 100% of assisted HH living in upgraded unit in accordance with minimum standards; 100 % of assisted families reported improvement in living conditions; 150 household / 1326 individuals drop in emergency caseload due to provision of durable climate protection. As part of the monitoring system, specific assessments are designed to capture indicators related to the shelter sector, which will be collected giving consideration of the impact on women, children, people with disabilities and the elderly. The interventions are guided by protection analyses. Context-adapted responses and accountability to affected populations (including gender-age sensitivity) are ensured thanks to active beneficiary engagement throughout all stages of the project cycle, starting with participatory protection assessments and response planning. The community protection approach is specifically designed to support adequate beneficiary participation and to involve groups traditionally excluded from decision making (women, children, PWDs). Specifically, the achievement of the indicators will be measured as follows: A technical assessment is conducted prior to the technical implementation to investigate thoroughly each of the community’s protection, through focus groups conducted with men and women, girls, boys, children, elders with and without disabilities (separated for males and females) to evaluate indicators (such as ventilation, number of rooms, separation between girls and boys, size and reason of seasonal movements) related to shelters conditions as baseline After the completion of the intervention, an overall evaluation will be conducted, including quantitative data collection (Post Specific surveys) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups (women/girls and men/boys) focusing on Displacements / Seasonal displacements / Family Separation / Safety / Dignity and Integrity. Finally, a feedback mechanism is assured through concrete community engagement in decision making related to intervention’s design and community implementation, which enhances the ownership of the activity by the community. The action responds to communities’ needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of personal safety, dignity and protection. The new shelters will ensure: an improvement of the privacy of all family members, especially women and girls; good ventilation and subsequent improved health conditions of all family members and especially children and elderlies to during the cold and the hot seasons; increased security and safety of all family members, especially of women and girls who will be able to carry out their duties in a safe place. The proposed shelter intervention will significantly decrease the risk of displacement for the most vulnerable Palestinian populations, especially women and children. Reduced seasonal displacement will provide protection for vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities and families headed by women) as these are the beneficiary families have members spending a great deal of time at home, according to the social customs and tradition of the selected communities. Improved shelter standards will provide environments with improved WASH standards, allowing for pregnant women to spend more time at home before and after their delivery, decreasing separation from their families and community. The separation of spaces for boys and girls will allow increased sense of privacy and dignity for men and women. In compliance with SPHERE standards the shelter rehabilitated will provide better lighting system improving the life of youth in the community.
