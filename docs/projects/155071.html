Project Summary

Save the Children Nutrition program activities are aligned with sector priorities and will contribute to the achievement of the overall nutrition sector strategic objectives one and two of the 2019 JRP. The activities are designed to complement ongoing nutrition programs in the target refugee camps with an aim to contribute to improved nutritional situation of children under 5 and pregnant and lactating mothers. These entail provision of quality services; Community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) for treatment of severe Acute Malnutrition ( SAM) through outpatient therapeutic program ( OTP)s, and Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) treatment through Therapeutic supplementary feeding program (TSFP) , Blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP) and Community management Mother At risk of malnutrition and Infants (C- MAMI)and Infant and young child feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E) interventions through skilled counselling in the IYCF-E corners and messaging through various platforms in collaboration with relevant actors and in addition to active participation in the sector coordination forums whilst also taking the chairing role for the Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) technical working group forum. The program funding will be supported by UNICEF, UNHCR for CMAM (100%) and IYCF (90%).

Needs

The SMART surveys conducted in April - May 2018 have revealed significant decline in the prevalence of Global acute malnutrition (GAM) in the makeshift camps from 19.3% observed in round 1 conducted in November-December 2017 to 12.0% in round 2, however the range of acute malnutrition prevalence levels still remain on the emergency thresholds border line. In the Nayapara registered camp acute malnutrition prevalence has remained almost unchanged with 14.3% GAM registered in round 1 to 13.6% GAM recorded in round 2. The presence of aggravating factors such as diarrhoea coupled with poor hygiene conditions and the effects of the monsoon season could further exacerbate the already poor nutrition status of the most vulnerable boys, girls and adolescent girls in all camps. Poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices are also reported in the SMART surveys, showing that only a small percentage of 6-23 month olds are reaching the recommended minimum dietary diversity (Kutupalong (9.8%), Makeshift (8.3%), and Nayapara (15.7%)). Minimum acceptable diet of 6-23 month olds is only reached for 8.8% in Kutupalong, 6.4% in Makeshift, and 15.7% in Nayapara. SCiBD in Cox Bazar also conducted an IYCF baseline survey in late December 2017 in four-refugee camps and one host community; the overall findings of IYCF practices were poor among refuges compared to host community. For instance, perceived knowledge towards the benefits of EBF is higher in host communities (40%) compared to 36% in refugees ones. The baseline revealed a knowledge gap towards the benefit of EBF for mothers. To overcome barriers of breastfeeding difficulties, the majority of women depend on advice of older and breastfeeding women rather than seeking professional help (12.4%). Out of the total 294 infants (223 refugee and 72 host) in both refugee and host communities interviewed HHs reported that infants less than 6 months consumed infant formula and milk powder, 13% and 5% respectively, in the last 24 hours.
