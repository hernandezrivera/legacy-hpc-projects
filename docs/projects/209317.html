Project Summary

In response to the need for accurate information on displacement and the identification of beneficiaries for the delivery of humanitarian aid, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) began its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program in 2004. The program aimed to support the Government of Sudan (GoS) and its humanitarian partners in establishing an accurate data collection system and providing data on displacement in areas most affected by conflict and natural disasters. The military conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that began on 15 April 2023, has led to the destruction of key infrastructure in affected areas, significant displacement and increase in morbidity and mortality, and interruption of humanitarian activities across Sudan. Despite the limitations to DTM's logistical operations due to the access and security challenges in conflict-affected states such as Khartoum and Aj Jazirah, and Darfur and Kordofan regions, DTM has been able to maintain key data collection operations and has provided humanitarians with preliminary estimates of displacement and return. Amidst a rapidly changing context, registration requests from humanitarian partners have reduced significantly over the last 5 years due to the emergence of different types of data requests from inter-agency groups and Clusters and a change in the methodology used in Sudan. In response to requests and the current security context, DTM appropriately expanded its Mobility Tracking data collection since 2023 to focus on remote and in-person key informant interviews to provide evidence-based information about the humanitarian context which other organizations rely on for their programming and anticipating beneficiaries' needs. However, DTM Sudan will still continue to routinely assess and anticipate partners' needs for humanitarian information. Should the context, beneficiaries, and humanitarian partners benefit from the information derived from registration activities, DTM Sudan will rapidly deploy registration teams to gather information for inter-agency coordination on future humanitarian interventions and support DTM's partners to identify hotspots of vulnerability and support precise targeting of assistance.
IOM has been working in close collaboration with partners in the field to establish a unified and systematic method to collect individual data on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. Registration data is captured by DTM field teams through direct individual interviews in the target locations, usually with the heads of households. All enumerators are trained on conducting interviews, engaging with female headed households, as well as safe and ethical referrals. Additionally, throughout all activities, DTM teams secure gender-responsive data collection by establishing gender mixed field teams, collecting sex and age disaggregated data, and capturing other demographic information (wealth status, ethnicity, vulnerability etc.). When collected, registration data is shared with partners by means of data sharing agreements, in line with data protection principles, and is used for beneficiary selection, vulnerability targeting, and programming.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM's DTM activities are conducted with strict adherence to data protection principles and international best practices. The exercises draw on IOM's global expertise in conducting similar activities to ensure that information is non-identifiable, stored securely and only accessible, by request, by approved partners. Furthermore, DTM is committed to collecting information on age and sex breakdown and vulnerabilities of target populations per location of assessment, which provides a foundation and insight to the demographic and specific gender nuance for protection focused programming. IOM will also use male and female enumerators from target locations possessing experience in collecting information and language skills to support data collection exercises for persons with specific needs and make key informants, IDPs and returnees more comfortable to provide information.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The basis of DTM work is interviews with key informants and conversations with displaced populations. During these conversations and interviews, informants are offered the chance to describe their experiences, needs and priorities in their own words. The insights provided by affected populations are the primary data used by DTM to inform partners and ensure that humanitarian work is accountable to the voices and needs of displaced populations. In addition, enumerators share their contact information to enable informants to give feedback on DTM processes. Enumerators are trained in referral pathways so that, if an informant is in need of assistance, enumerators are positioned and trained to support their needs through the appropriate channels. For information product users, DTM includes contact information on all products released, encouraging product users to contact DTM with feedback and suggestions. As of November 2023, DTM introduced a user feedback survey on the DTM Sudan website to gather feedback on how subscribers use DTM data, their perceptions of DTM information products and suggestions for improvement.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

DTM data relies on key informant interviews and conversations with displaced individuals to better understand their needs and priorities. This information is then analyzed at the population level and presented through DTM products for humanitarian organizations to use to ensure that the work that they are doing is meeting the needs of, and accountable to the populations they serve. As such, DTM is an inter-agency accountability mechanism for the humanitarian response, in addition to IOM's internal accountability to affected populations (AAP) mechanisms and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) standards. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA, as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to enumerators to ensure compliance.
