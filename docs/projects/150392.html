Project Summary

The project aims to support vulnerable groups of patients receiving organ transplants, children with Type I diabetes and children with PKU through the provision of life-saving medications, nutritional supplements, health and life-style education and awareness as well as conduct a scoping study to map urgent, unmet needs of these groups in Gaza Strip.
200 children with type I diabetes will receive insulin pens and glucometers and laboratory investigations. 1000 community members will receive health education sessions and material.
120 children suffering from PKU will receive laboratory investigations, gluten free flour and phenylalanine free infant and children formula. Care givers and community members will receive health promotion and psychosocial support.
At least 30 patients will be able to receive life-saving surgeries due to availability of solutions and other supplies needed for the surgery. 400 organ transplant patients will receive immunosuppressants and other lifesaving medications.
A scoping study to decide the needs of vulnerable groups in the project will be conducted with the aim or issuing a report on provided services, service providers and gaps that need to be filled.
Objective: The overall objective is to provide vital health services to vulnerable groups with the aim of improving their health and nutritional status as well as help them make necessary life-style changes to cope with their conditions.
Specific objectives:
- To improve and maintain the well-being of boys and girls suffering from Type I Diabetes mellitus and PKU.
- To diagnose new cases suffering from Type I diabetes and PKU and render them to treatment.
- To improve the lifestyle, knowledge, attitudes and practices of the community regarding diabetes.
- To ensure a proper feeding pattern of infants and children affected by PKU.
- To increase awareness regarding different types of disease for community members.
- To develop more comprehensive networking with other health providers aiming at securing a comprehensive management approach for Type I diabetes.
- To promote and advocate for PKU at the national level.
- To reduce risks faced by organ transplant patients through supplying them with needed antiviral immunosuppressants and steroids.
- To enable more patients to receive life-saving surgeries through supplying medical solutions and instruments (omni tract) needed for surgeries.
- To enable health providers to target their services towards the most vulnerable through a scoping study of urgent unmet needs of marginalized groups in this project.

Needs

The Gaza Strip has been going through a protracted health crisis caused by all surrounding circumstances including political and economic hardships. The health sector is one of the areas that are negatively affected by emergencies. The latest trauma situation is adding to the shortages facing health providers and depleting resources. According to Gaza’s Central Drug Store at the end of September, 245 essential medicines out of the total 516 essential medicines list (47%) were at less than one month’s supply and 206 essential medicines (40%) were completely depleted at the MoH store in Gaza. In addition, 253 essential disposables out of the total 853 essential disposables list (30%) were at less than one month’s supply. At times of crisis, one of the main groups that suffer due to the emergency are those suffering chronic diseases and in need of lifelong support. Attention shifts usually towards the acute crisis and less attention is paid to those not classified as urgent cases. However, matter of fact is that those groups are usually in life-threatening situations and should not be neglected. Children continue to be one of the most vulnerable groups and when this is paired with a disorder then the risk is higher. Children suffering from Type I Diabetes Mellitus and those with PKU are two of the main groups at high risk of developing serious complications if they do not receive necessary care continuously. While Diabetes prevalence in the world is at 6%, the prevalence in Palestine is at 15.3% according to reports by the MoH. Of those, 4.4% are diagnosed with Type I Diabetes. In terms of PKU, prevalence varies according to region with higher prevalence detected in rural areas. The average prevalence is at 6 per 100,000, with a total of 120 cases detected in Gaza Strip. With such a small number, the MoH does not include this group in its priorities, leaving it to NGOs to provide them with necessary services. Organ transplant patients normally receive such operations as the only treatment possible for end stage organ failure, such as liver, heart and kidney. In cases of end stage renal disease, patients can be treated through other measures. However, kidney transplants are generally accepted as the best treatment for both quality of life and cost effectiveness. According to recent numbers from Ministry of Health in 2017, there are 690 patients in Gaza who need kidney transplants. Most organ transplant operations are conducted outside Gaza, but when patients return to Gaza, they need lifelong supply of medications to maintain their health conditions. Needs assessment is always a challenge. The health sector suffers from fragmentation and inconsistency in service provision. It is important to have a clear vision of the situation in order to provide better service. The project aims at conducting a scoping study that will cover children with Type I diabetes, children with PKU and patients receiving organ transplant and those in need of such operations to decide the actual need, actual services provided and gaps of still needed interventions.

Activities

QRC will implement the project in partnership with the Ministry of Health and with Ard Elinsan Association through the following mechanisms: - Meetings with stake holders, including communities, to clarify urgent needs and approach to meeting them.- Provision of instruments and medical solutions needed in surgeries.- Provision of medications to patients through MoH and AEI.- Carrying out blood tests/investigations for children with Type I diabetes and PKU.- Carrying out health education and awareness activities.- Outreach activities - Monitoring, evaluation and reporting.
