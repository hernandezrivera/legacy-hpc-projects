Project Summary

In General Protection, the community based protection lies at the heart of the intervention since it’ll reinforce accountability towards affected people, and will ensure communities are empowered and recognized as agents of their own protection, leading to sustainable outcomes while DRC support the communities & individuals to avoid having resort to harmful coping mechanisms. DRC will ensure the meaningful access to assistance and services for repairing harms imposed by mainly disability discrimination, and the most vulnerable individuals and groups will be paid specific attention. Critical individual protection issues will be addressed through case management, referral, IPA and tailored customised support. COVID-19 awareness messages will be mainstreamed across activities. DRC will strengthen PSEA mechanisms among staff and beneficiaries, with internal and external capacity-building of field-based staff through online and in-person trainings. Therefore, to improve the AAP, DRC will outline the user-friendly, accessible feedback & reporting mechanisms and establish accessible helpdesks suggestion/complaints boxes to ensure that all interventions are safe for beneficiaries. Protection Monitoring will enable adaptability and a community-based approach to identify trends, concerns, needs and complement evidence-based advocacy. DRC will further explore and design inclusive and innovative remote protection monitoring and CM tools to improve access of those most-at-risk (women and girls, sick, elderly, persons with disabilities, larger economically vulnerable families) regarding COVID-19 limitations risks. The most vulnerable women and girls, disabled, and third gender will be particularly targeted with specialized outreach, empowerment, and participation modules. DRC will be engaged with UNO offices in Rajapalong and Palongkhali and with CiC offices, and DRC SMT will regularly interact with the District Commissioner, RRRC and other government agencies. All services will be provided using mobile delivery or be based in DRC’s existing camp & community structures, constructed according to engineering standards, and the environmental impact of the project implementation will be minimum.

1 - Identified Needs

Since January 2020 DRC Protection Monitoring sector is working in Camps 6, 8E, 8W, 10, 11 and 12 as well as Rajapalong and Palongkhali host communities. During the interventions DRC provided evidenced-based information that it is required to ensure tailored protection programming based on communities needs and concerns. According to DRC Protection Monitoring findings and Joint Multi-Sector Need Assessments data (October 2020), gaps need to be addressed in protection monitoring, community-based protection and individual case management services. On the other hand, COVID-19 increased the protection service needs for the most vulnerable population in camps and host community. Substantial reduction of humanitarian actors in the camps due to COVID19 limitations have reduced availability and access to key services such as legal aid, psychosocial support, clinical care and referrals. Besides, the safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic for everyone, but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated. According to the JMSN survey, 13% of households reported security issues since the COVID-19 outbreak, largely about theft. Households without men were mentioned to be more at risk. During the quantitative survey, protection-related concerns were raised related to robbery, kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment, rent payments, threats by local people/armed groups, Majhi's group demanding money/using violence against those reporting problems. DRC Protection monitoring since March 2020 indicates that the limitations on freedom of movement and the lack of livelihoods opportunities for Rohingya cause negative coping strategies and exacerbating risks to women and girls, including survival sex, child marriage, trafficking and reduced access to assistance items. In both the refugee and host locations, the COVID pandemic has deteriorated the protection environment for specifically women and girls. Also, GBV is increasing, including IPV, polygamy, transphobic violence, while access to support is more restricted. These findings are projecting the evidence of the needs for the continuation of DRC’s individual-centred case management programme, an and particular need to strengthen community capacity to support delivery of essential life-saving and life-sustaining services by engaging and training community-based volunteers, considering further access challenges. Coordination and information sharing remain a challenge across the camps between local, national and international field-based staff (including volunteers) who were not being trained in basic protection mainstreaming principals and disaster rapid response (DRR) mechanisms. It has direct impact on overall humanitarian response during emergency as well as non-emergency. Hence, a need of capacity building of field staff and partners in protection mainstreaming and DRR is paramount for improving the protective environment of beneficiaries. Furthermore, based on PM findings, specialised services and assistance for vulnerable groups (individuals or groups who are unable to anticipate, cope with, resist and/or recover from emergency and women, children, elderly and persons with disability), remains a challenge inside the camps and in host communities. Service provision in host communities is particularly poor as most services remain centred in the camps. This has led to increased tension between both communities. Violence, exhaustion, theft, economic and employment opportunities as well as access to basic services for host communities and/or refugees who arrived prior 2017 influx feel overlooked and demand compensation and equal support (PM team findings). Whereas the need for increased intervention to tackle social cohesion has been identified. The rising tensions within Rohingya communities in the camps has widely been overlooked. This indicates that there is a need to start strengthening the social cohesion and conflict resolution activities.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Protection Monitoring: Systematic and regular collection, verification and analysis of information and data through community-based data collection Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and direct observation where the vulnerable population will be involved directly over an extended period of time by trained protection monitoring staff. This will permit to identify protection trends, risks and concerns the refugee and the host communities are facing, for the purpose of informing effective responses, coordination and evidence-based local, national and internal advocacy efforts (in accordance to Protection Information Monitoring (PIM) guidelines), and will be used to reach out the most-vulnerable individuals both in refugee and host community. 2. Identify and reduce the protection risks and/or specific protection needs of PoC through direct GP case management and provision of individual assistance of the vulnerable population, while conducting direct systematic referrals to specialized services (internal and external). Increased caseload in case of an emergency will be accounted for at the time of events. At each step of the case management process, which is the entry point for identification of IPA cases, is has been taken in consideration to ensure that no harm comes to the vulnerable population or their family members as a result of case worker conduct, decisions made, or actions taken on behalf of the client or family, following the no harm principal. 3. Expand on DRC's current efforts and geographical training coverage of camp level staff of humanitarian actors of local, national and INGOs across different sectors in implementation zones in protection mainstreaming to support them in improving the access to, quality of and protection-sensitivity of their response towards the targeted people. 4. Identify and strengthen community-based protection (CBP) mechanisms and resilience, through targeted community-based group sensitization, capacity-building and community-led activities in Rohingya camps and Host Communities to foster self-determination, self-reliance and social cohesion among both communities. DRC CBP will tailor sensitization and capacity-building activities based on identified protection concerns by Community Protection Groups (CPG), providing a knowledge base enabling community-led projects and sensitization activities by CPG to the wider community contributing positively towards the overall protection environment within area of intervention. This will include targeted capacity-building training on COVID-19 and also natural disaster response for community volunteers and community based protection groups. 5. Identify and foster peacebuilding and conflict-resolution initiatives by establishing peacebuilding groups (PBG), specifically with the vulnerable women and youth. Linked to activity 4 outlined above, CBP teams will work with women and youth form Rohingya and Host communities by providing tailored trainings/workshop activities on conflict resolution/mediation, peacebuilding and community/based protection mechanisms. Gained knowledge will provide PBG with a firm base enabling a community driven approach through awareness initiatives aiming to foster social cohesion among their own and across communities. 6. Coordination with the protection sector through three main avenues: a) regular attendance and active participation with the Protection Working Group (PWG) b) being an active member of the PWG-Task Team (PWG-TT), c) information sharing of camp and host communities’ concerns through the dissemination of DRC monthly monitoring reports with the PWG and other relevant protection actors.
