Project Summary

The project is a part of UN Women integrated response to triple crisis affecting Niger due to the deterioration of the security situation, the sociopolitical instability following the coup, and chocks from climate changes. The project aims is to provide livelihood and protection to women and girls, both the newly displaced and those who are expected to return to their villages of origin. UN Women in Niger includes all these responses in a Nexus HDP approach. The nexus approach will address the gender-specific needs of women and girls by articulating emergency and social cohesion actions to lay the foundation for sustainable and inclusive development in displaced and returning communities. For this project: 1-Humanitarian: 75,000 new internally displaced persons and returnees to Diffa and Tillaberi, nearly 52% of whom are women and girls will benefit from provision of life saving assistance to women and girls to build their resilience, with a focus on protection and GBV 2- Peace: Through social cohesion spaces for initiatives and awareness to peacebuilding following the coup and to prevent enrolment in armed groups and foster the positive and leadership role of women in the process of security and peace in conflict zones.3- Development: The solar energy equipment will make it possible to include the achievements in sustainability beyond the urgent response, support the economic empowerment of women and girls and foster the emergence of green value chains.

1 - Besoins

Niger is facing an increasing triple crisis due to the deterioration of the security situation, the sociopolitical instability, and the chocks from climate changes which exacerbated structural vulnerabilities of women and girls who already had been living in poverty, facing various gender inequalities and lack of access to basic services. Deterioration of Humanitarian situation: Boko Haram terrorist group and non-state armed groups continue to operate in the Liptako-Gourma three-border area in the Tillaberi and Diffa region where most social and economic assets of households and communities have been destroyed by the above-mentioned terrorist and armed groups. The 2023 HRP estimates that 4.3 million people need humanitarian assistance among which 2.9 million are women and girls, 371,900 IPDs and 255,500 refugees who fled insecurity in neighboring Burkina Faso, Mali, and Nigeria. Women represent 50,3% of the total number and are most affected by insecurity.  Sociopolitical crisis and gender equality: According to the UN Women Gender Alert, based on a perception survey conducted between 23 August and 19 September 2023, with a participation of 8,933 people from the 8 regions of Niger, including 1,386 Women and girls, the military coup have dramatic impacts on the population, especially the most vulnerable groups. The economic and political sanctions that followed the coup, have put an halt on key national socio-economic programs. The crisis have increased the level of violence against women and girls. Women also no longer have the same freedom to travel to carry out their activities and there is increased difficulties in accessing basic social services.  Climate change and Humanitarian needs: In March 2023, the Harmonized Framework analysis in Niger estimated that, due to climate chocks, about 2,454,354 people are food insecure (phase 3 and +) and 398,060 people are under pressure (phase 2). From June to August 2023, it is estimated that nearly 3.3 million people will be food insecure (phase 3 and +) and 7,341,355 people under pressure (phase 2). Furthermore, the 2023 agricultural campaign was in deficit on a national scale due to the poor distribution of rain attributed to climate change effect.  Floods and foods insecurity: According to UNICEF Humanitarian Flash Update No. 5, for 20 October 2023, the Ministry of Humanitarian Action and Disaster Management (MAH/GC) registered a total of 169,598 people impacted by flooding (20,126 households), including 52 deaths. Maradi is the most affected region, with 67,548 people affected, followed by Tillaberi (31,806 people), Tahoua (24,923 people), Zinder (24,619 people), Diffa (10,266 people), Agadez (6,176 people), Dosso (4,166 people), and Niamey (94 people). Food insecurity remains widespread in Niger, particularly in areas affected by armed group activity where violence and resultant displacement restrict agricultural production and access to markets. As a result, nearly 3.3 million people across Niger likely faced Crisis—Phase 3—or worse levels of acute food insecurity during the June-to-August lean season, the period when food is most scarce, according to a March Harmonized framework analysis.
