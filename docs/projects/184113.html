5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In cooperation with other agencies, WFP contributed to the climate vulnerability assessment that examined climate vulnerability with a focus on the implications of vulnerability to climate change on the environment, energy and food security situation of targeted communities. The methodology for the assessment was based mainly on remote sensing techniques. The types of data and information used included historical records on precipitation, temperature, vegetation, land-use/land-cover (LULC), flood events and population. The idea is to use the long-term mean and coefficient of variation of the inter and intra-annual (e.g. monthly and annual) assigned climate parameters to detect the potential impact of climate risks and vulnerability. The assessment identifies the climate risks based on observed climatic trends and variability that have contributed to the vulnerability of the target areas. The analysis established that some states are prone to high rainfall variability. Vegetation productivity proxies showed a variable trend. Understanding of the vegetation dynamics is expected to help for better planning of livelihood activities to a wide array of beneficiaries in regions where the majority of the population depends on natural vegetation and agriculture to secure their livelihood. The general pattern of the LULC changes that took place is rapid increase in bareland and a reduction in natural vegetation. The increasing trend of bareland is expected to increase the risk of erosion of fertile soil from farmlands and might create hotspots of desertification. The concentration of the humanitarian population in the most populated parts of the states is expected to create high pressure on the natural resources and the surrounding environment. Based on the findings, a set of recommendations was made to be taken in project design. Specifically:In order to achieve successful forest rehabilitation programme it is important to share responsibilities in the protection of the forest and organization of the access to forest resources with the local communities . Reforestation activities should concentrate on multi-purpose tree species that meet the local demands. The multi-temporal tree species should be drought tolerant so as to stand long dry spells and rainfall variability. Encourage communities to use improved stoves and renewable energy facilities such as solar and biogas. The energy facilities should be provided with full package of training on the construction and sound use and maintenance of the facilities. Under the current variable climate conditions careful management of WASH intervention is needed that take into account the amount and quality of water supply that might diminish due to droughts.Local population should play an active role in managing and maintaining water facilities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Drawing on the organizational 2019-21 Strategy for Protection and Accountability to Affected Persons, WFP Sudan remains committed to mainstreaming these populations across all activities, encompassing gender and age considerations, through focus group discussions with diverse gender and age demographics throughout the lifecycle of the Project. Data collection and analysis for monitoring are disaggregated by age and gender, and extends unto process monitoring, direct observation and individual interviews as part of Beneficiary Contact Monitoring. WFP is an active member of the Protection Sector, as well as Gender Based Violence and Child Protection subsector working groups at country and state levels, the Protection Technical Advisory Group (TAG) under the Refugee Consultation Forum, the Counter Trafficking in Persons/Mixed Migration Working Group and the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Network. Participation in these forums enhances collaboration, coordination and learning to incorporate best practices within WFP operations. In particular, information shared by the Protection Sector, including the Gender-Based Violence and Child Protection sub-sectors, is analyzed and incorporated within WFP programming. Beyond this, WFP Sudan selects Cooperating Partners via a due diligence process; integrates clauses on gender, protection and accountability to affected persons in agreements; and monitors and enhances their capacities to protect safety, security and dignity of targeted beneficiaries. WFP and its implementing partners ensure beneficiary targeting is transparent, consistent and accepted, and communicated widely. Where lists are provided by local authorities, a rigorous reconciliation is undertaken via monitoring mechanisms to ensure no one is left behind.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

WFP Sudan ensures close involvement of beneficiaries in the design and implementation of its activities through their participation in the design and implementation of the methodologies to target/profile those most vulnerable, and providing avenues to request information, channel complaints and challenge project-specific decisions. WFP also receives feedback and collects perception of beneficiaries regarding the distribution process via distribution and post-distribution monitoring. These findings are discussed with Cooperating Partners and incorporated into design of the distribution process itself. In addition, WFP and its CPs instituted the beneficiary Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) to ensure protection and other key concerns reflected in the distribution process are received and processed in a timely manner. WFP currently has a call center, including a toll-fee 1460 Helpline, for complaints and feedback mechanism that fully centralized at the Country Office in Khartoum. Representatives are already in place, addressing concerns/complaints immediately as they receive them; or referring them to any of the thirteen focal points for each of the WFP activities when they are not in a position to respond immediately. WFP Sudan further develops and maintains communication and relationships with beneficiaries and traditional/local authorities through regular meetings, focus groups discussions and communication campaigns aimed at guaranteeing that messages regarding WFP programs and decisions are communicated accurately to all members in the community. It also takes note to ensure that beneficiaries, emerging from different vulnerable groups are adequately represented and targeted in the design and implementation, including receipt of transfers, verification and record keeping. WFP Sudan has a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism as a formalized system for receiving information from individual in communities where WFP operates. Through its established hotline and centralized call center supporting beneficiaries nationwide, WFP strengthens two-way communication with communities it serves, establishes procedures for recording, referring, taking actions and providing feedback to the complainants, and ensures implementation of its 2019-21 Strategy for Protection and Accountability to Affected Persons. WFP Sudan also relies on its own monitors and continues to deploy third-party monitors (TPM) to carry out process monitoring. They collect process monitoring data for all activities, which includes quantity/value, use, acceptability, and quality of acceptability, and quality of assistance provided, infrastructures, timeliness of assistance, etc. In addition, they also monitor cross-cutting themes such as protection, accountability to affected populations and gender, which guide programme decision-making. Serious issues are reported on a weekly basis so that WFP can quickly improve its programmatic response.
