Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to increased food security for vulnerable displaced and host community households in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. It has 2 main components Household Food Security and Bakery Support. Within the Household Food Security component NGO X targets an average of 50,999 HH/254,997 food-insecure individuals per month with food kits or value vouchers and 4500 HHs/22,500 individuals with one off ready to eat kits. The regular food assistance is to the value of 70% of the food SMEB (Survival minimum expenditure basket) or 70% of the Sphere minimum standards of 2100 kcal/p/day.
The Bakery Support component provides an approximate 664,125 individuals living in supported bakeries’ catchment area with bread at a reduced and stabilised price. This is achieved through subsidising up to 50% input (flour and yeast) of bread production thus supporting local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditional upon the selling of this bread in the local markets at a reduced and stabilised price agreed with and monitored by NGO X’s Cash and Markets, and Monitoring Teams. Approximately 32 bakeries are targeted to benefit from this assistance.
NGO X will implement the proposed project in partnership with 3 NGOs. Please note that these 3 NGOs will input into the OCHA HPC separately under this project. This decision was agreed between both parties and submissions have been coordinated to ensure there is no duplication with regards to either budget or beneficiary targets.
NGO X has secured total funding for this project. NGO X has permission to operate in all proposed locations.

4 - Needs

Ten years of conflict in Syria have devastated the economic and social fabric of the country and resulted in a protracted humanitarian crisis. Approximately 4.3 million people are in need of food security and livelihood support in NWS (FSL Cluster data). WFP reported that the number of food insecure people increased by 1.4 million in Syria from late 2019 to April 2020, the majority located in NWS. The wave of displacement of almost one million people during this time exacerbated an already dire humanitarian situation in NWS. According to REACH, of 848 assessed communities in November 2020, food was the second highest priority need among 53% of residents, while food was the number one priority among 66% of IDPs (from 820 communities). The low purchasing power of the population is set to continue with limited employment opportunities and continued depreciation of the Syrian Pound along with low levels of local food production and a heavy reliance on imported food, mainly cross border from Turkey. The COVID-19 pandemic adds additional challenges, potentially affecting vendors’ ability to restock, and hampering access to markets. The combination of these factors contributes to a highly food insecure population in NWS for the foreseeable future.The estimated (PiN) for 2021 - of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 9.8 million to 14.2 million. Similarly, subsidized bread prices have continued to increase throughout 2020. In government controlled areas and North East Syria a bundle of bread has doubled in price (now at 100 SYP/ bundle) while both size and quality of bread have decreased. (HNO 2021)

5 - Implementation of activities

The household food security component will support food security for HHs through (Cash and Voucher Assistance) CVA or via direct food kit distribution. Each household will receive 8 rounds of assistance. The bakery support will advance the following outcomes: households have access to diverse foods in adequate quantities; communities have access to bread at affordable prices; recently displaced households are able to meet their emergency food needs. The project is comprised of three components: community food security, household food security and emergency food security. NGO X plans to transition households from kits to vouchers as conditions allow, scale up its use of electronic vouchers, and continue supporting bakeries. In Idleb and Northern Aleppo, NGO X will continue to apply its household selection methodology developed using vulnerability assessments and in line with Cluster guidelines. NGO X will provide blanket distributions in IDP camps and informal settlements due to high vulnerability of the target populations. NGO X will support 32 bakeries, providing required flour and yeast inputs to ensure high-quality bread which will be made available to the bakery catchment population at an agreed affordable weight and price (up to 38% reduction of the current market bread price). NGO X will pre-position 11,300 RTE kits (adequate for approx. 4,500 households) in NGO X’s warehouses to be able to respond within 72 hours. Routine monitoring including distribution monitoring and post-distribution monitoring will be conducted for all distribution-based activities. For the bakery component, bakery feedback surveys, shopkeeper satisfaction surveys and bread beneficiary survey will be conducted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Mitigation: a) Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs b) Selection criteria are transparent, and groups are fully informed c) Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs d) Apply NGO X’s feedback mechanism e) Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process 2. Access restrictions to geo area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines would prevent the humanitarian actors providing timely emergency relief to the affected population. Mitigation: a) Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground b) Develop contingency planning c) Explore different distribution modalities 3. Method and location of distribution of cash or vouchers can put vulnerable beneficiaries at risk of robbery or extortion. Risk of cash and or voucher value diversion to non-humanitarian purposes. Mitigation: a) Distribution methodology with clear protection risks analysed and mitigated b) Arrange home deliveries c) Verified beneficiary list to prevent cash or voucher value being misused for other non-humanitarian purposes.

NGOX ensures they are accessible and safe for women and children to travel to in daylight and all distributions are undertaken in daylight. If beneficiaries cant or dont want to attend distribution points they can authorize another household member to collect their assistance for them. In cases where a beneficiary is unable to attend the distribution point, home delivery can be organised

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: IDPs influx to NGO X’s AoOMitigation: NGO X would increase the quantity of flour allocated to the supported bakeries to meet some of the additional need. R: Deteriorated security situation making access more difficult M: NGO X has established a remote monitoring mechanism, with multiple whatsapp rooms, including bakery owners, bread distributors, local councils etc. This mechanism provides NGO X with sufficient monitoring data to ensure bakeries can continue to produce subsidized bread. Assumption: NGO X & partners can maintain long term access and if access is restricted that it is temporary with the above mitigation measures are employed during a short term access issue. R: Syrian pound fluctuationM: Vouchers and cash distributions are provided in USD, which is the most stable currency in circulation which protects the transfer value provided to the beneficiaries. FGD are carried out with the local government and community representatives regularly to understand the impact of currency fluctuations.Assumption: That sufficient USD notes are in circulation in North West Syria to distribute at the scale NGO X & partners intend to. USD is allowed to be distributed by local authorities in NWS. R: COVID19 Pandemic M NGO X redesigned its food security programme to be adapted to COVID-19 and mitigate risks of transmission between staff and beneficiaries. These measures include social distancing, hand sanitation, mask wearing at distributions, training and monitoring bakeries to ensure machinery is sterilized daily, and PPE is used. Assumption: It is assumed that large scale vaccinations will not be possible in NWS in 2021 and therefore assumed that COVID19 will continue to be a risk for all of 2021.
