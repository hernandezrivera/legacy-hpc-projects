Project Summary

"The Danish Refugee Council aims to prevent and respond to Gender-Based Violence. DRC's comprehensive approach focuses on addressing the root causes of GBV while providing specialized case management services to meet the urgent needs of survivors. This includes offering non-specialized psychosocial support, engaging men and boys in accountable practices, distributing dignity kits, and making referrals to specialized support services.
The objective of DRC's work is challenging harmful attitudes and practices within communities, which often contribute to GBV. To achieve this, DRC collaborates closely with the GBV sub-sector, along with other protection sectors such as child protection and general protection. This emphasizes enhancing the skills of GBV case workers to comprehend the risks and master case management principles. DRC also provides training to non-protection staff, enabling them to effectively mainstream GBV considerations, identify GBV cases, and make appropriate referrals.
In alignment with DRC's commitment to addressing the specific needs of the population, DRC implements a range of GBV activities, each strategically designed to mitigate risks and foster a safer environment. In the target areas, GBV incidents increased in the last year, and an in-depth effort to foster inclusive environment is much needed, particularly regarding prevention and addressing root causes. DRC employs specialized case management services to meet the urgent needs of survivors, providing comprehensive support and assistance.
Comprehensive awareness-raising sessions are conducted for refugees and local communities, emphasizing the prevention and addressing of exploitation, harassment, and abuse. These sessions focus on recognizing warning signs and identifying appropriate reporting channels.DRC engages in social and behavioral change activities, aiming to challenge harmful attitudes and practices within communities that contribute to GBV. This includes initiatives to transform social norms and promote respectful and accountable behaviors. DRC is committed to enhancing the skills of GBV case workers and non-protection staff through targeted capacity-building initiatives. This includes training sessions on safeguarding responsibilities, recognition of GBV signs, and appropriate referral procedures.
DRC is deeply committed to ensuring the relevance, appropriateness, and safety of its GBV programs. To achieve this, DRC actively participates in safety audits conducted by the GBV Sub-Sector, aligning its services with the guiding principles of GBV. Through DRC's protection monitoring activities, the organization is documenting risks and producing quarterly protection monitoring reports where GBV is a key thematic area. DRC's activities are guided by an Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach to data collection, allowing for the disaggregation of information by age, gender, and diversity profiles. This approach ensures that DRC caters to the specific needs of survivors, including those with disabilities, and extends its support to marginalized communities such as the hijra community and other diverse populations.
The geographical scope of our project includes Camp 6, Camp 10, Camp 8W, Camp 8E, and Palongkhali, Rajapalong. Within these areas, our GBV team provides life-saving case management services for women, girls, men, boys, gender-diverse populations, and children with or without disabilities. To facilitate this, DRC has established safe spaces for women and girls, community centers, and case management desks. These safe spaces serve as hubs for sessions and psychosocial support, ensuring survivors have access to the help they need.
By aligning DRC's activities with the sector strategies outlined by the ISCG, DRC aims to create an impactful approach to GBV prevention and response. DRC's selection of locations is informed by a thorough analysis of GBV-SS mapping, ensuring that its interventions are strategic and targeted."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"As reported by the protection monitoring report issued by the Protection Sector in Bangladesh in 2023, several concerning trends have emerged with regards to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) within the camps. Comparing to the previous quarter, reported cases of physical assault have decreased by 2%, while instances of psychological and emotional abuse have increased by 2%.The current situation is exacerbated by various factors. There are limited opportunities for income generation and a lack of recreational options within the community. This, combined with deteriorating security conditions, has led to an alarming rise in GBV risks. Girls and women are particularly vulnerable, often targeted by organized criminal groups for forced marriages or sexual violence. These concerns were highlighted in focus group discussions.Women have resorted to marriage, both voluntarily and involuntarily, as a strategy to ensure their safety from criminal/armed groups and reduce the risk of sexual violence. The absence of proper marriage and divorce registration processes further heightens women's vulnerabilities to forced marriages and domestic violence, complicating their access to justice.Refugee women have even married unknown men in a bid to escape to neighbouring countries, such as Thailand and Malaysia, without guarantees of their safety. This exposes them to trafficking, sexual violence, and exploitation risks. Additionally, the deteriorating security situation has pushed women and girls to adopt harmful survival tactics.Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) has been a major concern, accounting for 82% of reported GBV cases, mostly within the survivors' residences. Husbands are often the perpetrators, engaging in physical assaults and withholding resources, particularly in cases of polygamy where alimony isn't provided.Incidents of GBV are prevalent at certain locations, such as water points and bathing facilities, affecting mainly adolescent girls and unmarried women. Unfortunately, underreporting is common in these cases due to the stigma associated with sexual abuse and rape. Focus group discussions indicated that reduced humanitarian aid and increased congestion within shelters contribute to higher risks of violence.The absence of a strong deterrence system for men engaging in multiple marriages perpetuates the problem. Traditional marriage arrangements by community leaders, often involving monetary transactions, have enabled men to have multiple relationships and contribute to higher rates of IPV incidents. Deep-rooted gender norms, faulty social values, and harmful beliefs continue to be underlying factors for GBV. Perpetrators of GBV are often unemployed or daily wage laborers, representing 73% of all offenders in the camp. There are instances of men engaging in illegal drug trading within the camps, further fuelling violence, especially intimate partner violence.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project aligns with the Joint Response Plan (JRP), emphasizing critical protection needs for individuals facing heightened risks. Following Rationalization Principle #3, DRC integrates General Protection, Community-Based Protection, Protection Monitoring, and Gender-Based Violence to provide a comprehensive package for all basic services. The project enhances inter-sectoral camp-based coordination through CCCM, integrating services for refugees and host communities. Preventive activities target women, girls, men, boys, and hijra communities with specific sessions for age and gender-segregated groups in both host and camp locations. Principle #4 ensures program stability, is aligned with donor-funded projects (ECHO, FCDO, SIDA, GAC). DRC's technical expertise supports partners and stakeholders in GBV through training, sensitization, and mentoring, adopting a people-centered approach that builds local capacity. Services are mobile or based in DRC’s camp and community structures, constructed with minimal environmental impact. Regular engagement with government agencies and the host community ensures alignment with rules. DRC conducts local partner mapping to expand the program with national and local partners for sustained impact.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

DRC is strategically integrating protection across various programs within the organization, including CCCM and economic recovery initiatives. This integration extends to encompass Gender-Based Violence (GBV) alongside other critical protection components such as case management, community-based protection, protection monitoring, and economic recovery efforts.The DRC protection team is dedicated to ensuring the seamless integration of these aspects into all the aforementioned sectors and at the camp level where DRC operates. This involves conducting thorough needs assessments and delivering comprehensive training sessions for both protection and non-protection staff. The goal is to enhance awareness and understanding of protection issues across diverse sectors.Furthermore, the DRC protection team is actively engaged in producing information management products tailored to the protection sector. These products serve to streamline and optimize the collection and dissemination of crucial protection-related information.To fortify the capacity of sectors at the camp level, the protection focal points receive Training of Trainers (ToT) facilitated by trained protection experts. This approach not only empowers the focal points but also contributes to a cascading effect, fostering a more knowledgeable and capable workforce within the camp-level sectors.In essence, DRC's commitment to mainstreaming protection extends beyond theoretical integration to practical implementation, leveraging assessments, training initiatives, and information management strategies to create a robust and comprehensive approach across diverse programs and at the camp level.

3.3 - Age & Disability

DRC prioritizes age and diversity to ensure effective interventions. Actively enhancing service accessibility, particularly for individuals with disabilities and minority groups, DRC seeks technical support from Handicap International (HI). HI conducts regular assessments in DRC centers, offering insights that guide DRC in refining its services. DRC Bangladesh has a dedicated action plan for disability inclusion, showcasing its commitment to creating an inclusive environment. Guided by the Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) policy, DRC ensures interventions are responsive to diverse needs. A key strategy involves continuous efforts to increase service accessibility for all age groups and genders, aligning with the broader goal of fostering a diverse-friendly environment. Central to DRC's success is the active involvement of beneficiaries, ensuring accurate program alignment with community needs. Emphasizing data collection in line with AGDM guidance, DRC stays well-informed for informed decision-making and adaptive programming.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

DRC is committed to engaging Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) principles in its protection intervention, placing a strong emphasis on the Community-Based Feedback and Mechanisms (CFM) framework. Recognizing the paramount importance of open and efficient communication, DRC will establish a robust CFM system within the camps and host communities. This system empowers refugees and host communities to voice their concerns, provide feedback, and actively participate in decision-making processes. Utilizing accessible channels like phone hotlines, community focus group discussions, and feedback boxes, DRC ensures that the voices of these communities are not only heard but also integral to shaping interventions. The CFM not only enhances transparency and accountability but also fosters a sense of ownership among community members. Regular feedback loops enable DRC to continuously adapt and reshape its protection interventions, ensuring they remain relevant and responsive to the evolving needs and concerns of the affected populations.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Having undertaken a PSEA assessment with a score of 6, DRC proactively addresses areas for improvement, exemplified by ongoing initiatives like PSEA awareness raising.DRC conducts comprehensive awareness-raising sessions for refugees and local communities, emphasizing the prevention and addressing of exploitation, harassment, and abuse, with a focus on recognizing warning signs and reporting channels. Clear and confidential reporting systems have been established, enabling individuals to express concerns without fear of retaliation. Dedicated response teams, including PSEAH focal points, ensure swift reporting to national focal points and HQ for suitable responses, including thorough investigations when necessary.Team members and focal points undergo training on safeguarding responsibilities, recognizing signs of exploitation, harassment, and abuse, and understanding necessary steps for identification. DRC has developed comprehensive PSEAH guidance and a robust code of conduct, transparently outlining expected behaviors and prohibiting any form of exploitation.Collaborating with the PSEA Network facilitated by the UN, DRC ensures compliance with international standards and meaningful information sharing. DRC's commitment to safeguarding is a fundamental principle ingrained in global operations, surpassing mere compliance. Through ongoing training, transparent communication, and proactive measures, DRC upholds the protection of all involved parties.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Disasters can disrupt essential services, including health and support services for survivors of GBV. Limited access to these services can hinder the recovery and well-being of survivors. Empowering communities, especially women and marginalized groups, in disaster preparedness and response efforts is vital. Inclusive community engagement can contribute to building resilience and reducing the risk of GBV. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) is integrated into the GBV activities implemented through community-based protection programming (general protection). Furthermore, DRC teams play an active role as part of PERU, the emergency response teams coordinated by the sector and mobilized in the event of natural hazards. This integration ensures that DRR measures are embedded within community-based protection activities, enhancing the overall resilience and preparedness of the communities served by DRC. The active participation of DRC teams in PERU showcases a proactive approach to emergency response, enabling swift and effective interventions in the face of natural disasters to mitigate their impact on the affected populations.
