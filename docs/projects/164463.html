4 - Needs

The Northeast of Syria (NES) has been the scene of hostilities and internal flight movements for almost 8 years due to the prolonged Syrian civil war and the temporary assumption of power by the terrorist organization Islamic State. Combined with the difficult climatic conditions, NES is one of the most complex operational fields for transitional aid measures. At present, 1.65 million people are dependent on aid, and the destruction of the social infrastructure, especially in former IS areas, is far-reaching. The northeast has been under the control of the so-called Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) since the end of the 15-month intensive fight against the IS, which was conducted with the support of international alliancesAs of 18 December, OCHA reported that in NES around 70,590 remain displaced from Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, and Aleppo Governorates. Displaced people have, in the majority, found shelter in local communities but also in informal settlements and collective shelters. There are 77 shelters in Al-Hasakeh Governorate. According to UNHCR, as of 30 November, more than 17,500 people have crossed into neighboring Iraq. Prior to 9 October, Northeast Syria already hosted approximately 710,000 people displaced from earlier phases of the conflict, around 91,000 of whom remain in Al Hol, Areesha, Mahmoudli, Newroz and Roj camps.In NES, according to REACH, there are currently 10 displaced person camps, two densely populated and informal settlements and a further 16 smaller informal settlements. The majority of these camps and settlements are located in the governorates of Raqqaand Hasakeh. The majority of the camps and settlements are inhabited by Syrian internally displaced persons with little prospect of return, mainly from the Deir-ez-Zor. Tthe Al-Hol camp is also home to some 10,000 refugees from the Iraqi Ninewa region.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Run 2 PHCs level 2 in Dier-Ez-Zor for 12 months. 2- support the facilities' staff with salaries and training. 3- Support the facilities with drugs and medical supplies needed. 4- support the referal pathway towards higher management facilities, as well as, EWARN and data collection. IDA does understand that there is a major risk regarding Community acceptance and careful, sustained presence is key to success in the targeted area. Thus, IDA will work to reduce their total portfolio and invest deeply in these under-served areas. IDA has applied to grant to rehabilitate 10 PHCs in both Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor in collaboration with the Germany BMZ and run 4 of these PHCs for 24 months. Thus, the proposed project aims to cover salaries and running costs for 2 PHCs in Deir-ez-Zor governorate that will be selected according to the needs assessment that IDA is planning to do in April.2020. This propsoed 2 PHCs will provide Lv2 primary health care services according to the essential health package and will coordinate closely with the health working group in NES. These 2 PHCs will provide pediatric, GP, and midwifery services for people in need, as well as, cover at least 50% of the drugs through in-kind donations from other IDA partners. 5 CHWs per PHC will work to provide public awareness for the targeted communities as recommended in the WHO new module for community management and mobilization. The contracted medical workers will get a basic package of training recommended by WHO that includes; ANC, PNC, CD, NCD, information management provided by IDA through external trainers. These 2 PHCs will coordinate its activities with the nearby health facilities to maintain the referal pathway, and refer cases for higher management. IDA will implement this project directly from its office in Iraq, and its field office in NES. IDA M&E team will apply concreted M&E plan to measure the project activities, indicator, beneficiary satisfaction and Accountability.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

IDA does understand that there is a major risk regarding Community acceptance and careful, sustained presence is key to success in the targeted area. Thus, IDA will work to reduce their total portfolio and invest deeply in these under-served areas.This project proposal has been designed on the basis of needs assessments consisting of interviews with key informants within the target population. The project staff will be hired whenever possible from the target area itself, ensuring intimate knowledge of the context and good acceptability of the staff by the community. IDA has incorporated beneficiaries' feedback into their existing feedback system, through suggestion boxes at each PHC, which are collected and reviewed by the M&E field officer on a monthly basis.The M&E Officer will obtain direct patient feedback during the monitoring process. All patients feedback will be registered in complaints tracking sheet, the tracking sheet will easily show the statues for each complaint (such as category, time cycle, decision, extra), the beneficiary will get the result of his/her complaint if possible( since the anonymous complaints are acceptable). IDA team is trained on service provision according to humanitarian principles, as well as, IDA will implement meetings with camps' management, Local Council and community leaders as well as with Health Directorate to know the affected population suggestions and needs to improve the services provided. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks related to protection mainstreamingRisk probability: MediumPrevention strategy:In addition to watch the situation on the ground, IDA will maintain a wide network with local informants on the ground and in government positions to keep up to date on developments in the conflict environment. And to avoid sparking any intercommunity conflicts, IDE will carefully choose the locations of the facilities to be of duly identified owners, to avoid implementing at any disputed premises. Although the scarcity of the skilled persons in NES, IDA will be very careful in selecting local candidates for the positions in the facilities, minorities, the tribes where the facilities run to be taken into consideration, to avoid creating any barriers for the beneficiaries to the health services. Affiliation-check is mandatory for all staff ahead of hiring. Risks: Shifting frontlines of military conflict zones result in security threats for staff and premises and potentially forced the evacuation of the PHCs. Risk probability (low/medium/high): MediumPrevention strategy:IDA has contingency plan for each facilities that includes triggers and actions based on the triggers and the severity of the contingency, where there is an evacuation procedures is ready to use for rapid evacuation of staff and equipment, temporary suspension of activities, changing project target locations or closing the facilities with exit plan based on the situation and its severity.Risks: Shifting Access restrictions for international staff of IDA and MI and subsequent restrictions regarding monitoring and support lead to the underachievement of project resultsRisk probability (low/medium/high): MediumPrevention strategy: Long-tested mechanisms for remote management at IDA in place to ensure transparency, continuous monitoring, and reporting. Innovative digital data collection (such as HIMS) and teaching approaches improve remote management and capacity building.
