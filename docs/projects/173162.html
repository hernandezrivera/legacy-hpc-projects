Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen the capacity of vulnerable children in particular girls and young people to mitigate the risk of SEA and CP; and strengthen the capacity of child protection structures to respond effectively to SEA and CP issues at community level.

1 - Activities

 Train Child Protection structures on protection related risks during humanitarian situations Work with the Ministry of Education, Health and other relevant actors to sensitise and orient health workers, teachers and other professionals on Child Protection including child-friendly communication, child safeguarding, child protection concerns safe referral practices and comprehensive sexuality education. Conduct age, gender appropriate awareness raising activities, and disseminate messages through different channels (TV, internet, radio, posters etc.) and promote safe behaviours among, children, adolescents and communities. Work with schools to ensure that protection and safety messages are delivered to parents, teachers and children in a way which limits panic and distress and encourages adherence to health messaging (i.e. posters, infographics)  Develop joint position papers, messages or/and plans highlighting child protection risks present during humaniatarian crisisand call for a response with a focus on children and families affected. Work with government and CP coordination mechanisms to develop Child Friendly Spacescomprising all key Child Protection Services and support Case follow-ups.  Collaborate with service providers (econet, netone, telecel) to develop free child protection toll free lines that can be utilised by children to quickly report Child protection concerns. Collaborate with health actors and coordinators to adapt existing assessment and monitoring tools to include child protection concerns, such as separation, psychosocial distress, etc. Utilise food/cash distribution points to raise awareness on Child Protection and safeguarding, SRHR of children,adolescents and young people during the Humanitarian crisis Work with community volunteers (children, adolescents/ youth and adults) to develop child-friendly messages, measures and strategies to share up to date information, adapt cultural practices and receive questions and feedback.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

While the project is designed to respond to the protection needs of children and adolescents in humanitarian situations, addressing those needs may have negative impacts on the environment. For example, there may be logistics-related emissions, waste and other environmental impacts along the supply chain of moving goods and services from their producers to populations affected by disasters. Recognizing this, where financially feasible, Plan will prioritize the procurement of locally-made goods and services to reduce the carbon footprint associated with transporting goods from offshore. In addition, the transportation of goods will be done using serviced trucks that are not only able to deliver bulk goods to reduce fuel consumption associated with making multiple trips. Plan will also ensure that transporters use serviced vehicles that do not emit as much fumes. • Plan will strive to procure environmentally friendly supplies. • All project activities will take note that it does not compromise environmental sustainability and will continuously assess possible impact to the environment. • Resilience for access to protection activities will liaise with EMA and Forest Commission to facilitate awareness sessions on climate variability and sustainable development given that communities will make various contributions to the project through provision of locally based materials especially during infrastructure development.
