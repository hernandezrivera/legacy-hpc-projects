Project Summary

The project will ensure provision of timely, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian intervention to 8500 IDPs to improve their well-being, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. The delivery of CCCM activities in informal sites will be conducted through the deployment of mobile/roving teams. Narrative Hub, a women-led organization, is quite conversant with CCCM cluster strategy and methodology having been trained by the cluster from 2021 to 2023. NH has recently implemented and Emergency GBV project in Nyirol,( waat payam) while implementing a transitional justice project in Twic, and according to local assessment reports and observation, CCCM support is needed. A multi-sector approach between CCCM, GBV, ES/SNFI and protection will be prioritized at the sites where needs will be commonly identified by the CCCM in coordination with ES/NFI and protection and all activities will be integrated during the implementation period to achieve the cluster set objectives in locations where needs are very highly identified. Narrative Hub has both static and mobile presence in the location providing protection services and therefore, the intervention will include services like support to GBV survivors, psychosocial support and raising awareness sessions for men, women, boys and girls, as well as mitigation measures. This will be done in the form of training. It will respond to emergency needs by empowering community and strengthening the capacities of local actors to have access to both essentials camp management service. Through CCCM Cluster support, the project will contribute significantly to service delivery in hard-to-reach areas of Twic, and Nyirol counties, coordinating multi-sectorial assistance through organizing site level coordination meetings with community members from both displaced, returnees and host communities; conducting site maintenance and service mapping activities to support minimum living standard; and protection of people with special needs in coordination with actors; and strengthening community structure by training community members on CCCM and humanitarian response. Community engagement will be a priority to the project for purposes of accountability to the affected population (AAP) and strong complaint mechanisms will be established among others.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying according to the latest IPC report, with the latest data showing that 5.83 million people (46.3 percentage of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). An estimated 35,000 people are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in Nyirol (6000) county of Jonglei State, while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). These individuals face extreme vulnerability due to the lack of viable alternatives. Self-settled, unplanned IDP sites, often overlooked and underserved, pose an even greater risk of exclusion from essential services. In 2023, 58 per cent of displacement sites (hosting 327,215 people) did not receive Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) support, resulting in limited access to coordinated services, community support, information, or site maintenance. The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023, attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, and cattle raids continued to displace people in Warrap and Jonglei. According Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FAWS NET) report of September 2023, in the northern counties bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. where some households are likely still in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in the latter two counties due to multiple years of asset and livelihood erosion exacerbated by the arrival of South Sudanese returnees and conflict-related disruption to trade flow with Sudan. The Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 16 as of 20th October 2023 by UNOCHA noted that South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families. As of 16 October, 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5 per cent of all arrivals. New arrivals continue to report harassment, intimidation, torture and extortion of money during their journey to South Sudan, including by security personnel along the route. Life-saving assistance is needed for returnees, including shelter and non-food items, food, water, sanitation facilities and protection services. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has had a significant impact on trade between Sudan and South Sudan. The reduced imports from Sudan have led to a rise in the cost of the food basket. According to market monitoring conducted by humanitarian partners, the cost of the food basket has increased significantly since the start of the Sudan crisis. In areas bordering Sudan. An IRNA assessments conducted in Twic and Nyirol counties between June and December 2022 to determine the effect of flooding and subnational conflicts on the population reveals an estimated of 6000 Population in Nyirol and 2500 in Twic has been affected. This population, particularly is in urgent need of CCCM services. A number of protection concerns were noted in the settlement areas. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls.

2. Project activities

The following activities will be implemented under the four cluster objectives:CO1: Safe and dignified access to services at site or area level• Establish/facilitate functional site management teams• Provide CCCM services to beneficiaries by coordinating humanitarian response in camps and camp-like settings, monitoring service provision at site level and advocating on needs, and working with service providers to ensure an equitable and needs-based response. • Establish/facilitate static facilities providing specific services to vulnerable groups and PWSNs to ensure protection standards through regular site monitoring, community engagement, and site development • Provide case referral through the site/area level referral mechanism• Conduct safety audits in coordination with site level protection actors• Provide flood mitigation and preparedness works• Establish/facilitate transit centers CO2: Ensure and facilitate access to two-way communication pathways for displaced population• Establish functional feedback and complaints mechanisms at the sites and ensure the process of registering and receiving complaints are accessible and known to all site residents. • Conduct awareness raising campaigns to ensure that the displaced are aware of the assistance and services available in the camp and how to access them, and ensuring that the distribution points are accessible and the mechanisms for distribution has taken into consideration groups with specific needs to minimize chances of violence, abuse and exploitation within the camps • Set up and maintain a camp information management system including sex- and age-disaggregated data collection and sharing. CO3: Strengthen community self-organization and community cohesion• Establish functional and inclusive community committees and self-governance structures• Conduct trainings for community committees, site leadership and authorities• Provide cash for work to HHs from community-based projects aimed at site development, infrastructure improvement, site risk reduction or livelihood activities• Develop joint community-based environmental management plans. CO4: Support integrated approaches to develop phase-out/transition strategies, resilience, and durable solutions• Develop phase-out/ transition strategies together with other CCCM partners• Conduct joint intention surveys supported by CCCM partners• Conduct regular comprehensive site profiling exercises.• Support/facilitate peaceful coexistence and/or durable solutions community-led projects
