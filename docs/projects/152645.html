Project Summary

This food security and livelihood project is a combined response which will run for 12 months from January to December, 2019. Main goal of the project is to strengthen food and nutrition security and resilience among food insecure HHs in former states of NBeG in South Sudan. The project directly targets 34,000 vulnerable households of primarily IDPs (10%), returnees (15%) and host community (75%). The beneficiaries will be targeted basing on vulnerability matrix and context
To achieve this, the project will support the immediate nutritional needs of vulnerable HHs through the provision of cash/vouchers transfers. Using cash transfers, HHs will access, food sufficient food to meet daily caloric and nutritional requirements. This will address the immediate impacts of food insecurity, reducing the incidence of malnutrition and malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality. Transfers will be both unconditional and conditional upon the completion of work to rehabilitate and/or construct communal assets that have the potential to enhance longer term food security and livelihoods including physical assets such as dykes and roads, as well as natural assets.
In addition to meeting basic and immediate needs, short term cash transfers will ensure HHs have the energy and time to participate in activities that enhance their ability to deal with disruption and change. By improving the availability and accessibility of these productive assets, the project will strengthen long-term food security by improving agricultural productivity, and supporting the regeneration of the natural resource base. As production increases, availability of food for consumption and/or sale increases, resulting in improved livelihoods and an increase in local, regional and national food security.
To enhance emergency food production, main season agriculture seeds & tool, fishing gears for Aweil Center, and fast maturing vegetable & crop kits shall be distributed. This project will also contribute to the reduction of pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication and also conduct Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF;
To achieve its expected impact, the project will be proactive and supportive (rather than reactive and focused on relief) in providing assistance for target communities to enhance its effectiveness. SPEDP will also do M&E, reporting and coordination with relevant stakeholders to effect complementary, maximize synergies and contribute to project sustainability. SPEDP will also use a community based participatory approach where beneficiary identification and registration process will involve community members.
Project management committees will be established and will take an active role in the beneficiary selection, monitoring implementation and also handling complaints and feedback mechanisms and monitoring of the project, this CFM will be set in place where beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries will be able to able to air out their concerns about the project. Stakeholders will be invited during the launch and all project details will be shared to foster accountability to the affected populations
SPEDPs added value is that the organization has an already established presence in NBeG since 2013 to date has implemented a number of projects since them in different thematic areas. SPEDP is currently implementing FSL SA2 project in Aweil West, FOA-SAFER Project in Aweil Center and North and so is familiar with the local settings

Needs

According to Key IPC findings of September 2018 – March 2019, it is expected that 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). An anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). These estimates are in the presence of planned humanitarian food assistances. It’s also projected that in NBeG, 510000 people will be under IPC3 whereas 281000 under IPC4 in between January to March 2019. Northern Bahr el Ghazal region are suffering a major food security and nutrition crisis that is driven by market failure and the depletion of livelihood assets, rapidly depreciating South Sudanese pound, shortages of hard currency, declining oil production and prices and dependence on imports. Large numbers of returnees from the Sudan and people displaced by conflict from other regions in South Sudan are without means of support. The continuing food and nutrition insecurity is likely to result in increased chronic malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies among nutritionally vulnerable groups(South Sudan Interim Country Strategic Plan (2018–2020). NBeG prioritization is informed by the high levels of vulnerability to climate change related shocks and stresses. The area fall within South Sudan’s Western Flood Plains agro-ecological zone, where floods and droughts are common. In July and August 2018, croplands in parts of this former state were affected by flooding and waterlogging. Based on predicted changes to climate, the frequency and severity of these disasters is likely to increase. If left unaddressed, this will exacerbate existing food insecurity, which is already at crisis levels and predicted to increase

Activities

Main Activities- Unconditional cash & voucher based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives - Main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits distribution - Fishing kits/ rapid response kits (fishing kits/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) distribution- Disease and pest surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication - Livestock vaccination & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF- Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets)/ Conditional cash transfers (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees)/ Voucher system for seed procurement;- Support value chain addition e.g. through milk & fish preservation & marketing- Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. To implement the above activities, SPEDP will employ a range of strategies including the following;Carrying out Rapid Market survey and risk analysis, this will help in determining local level capacities of commercial sector to source and provide Food to the beneficiaries.• Sensitization and selection of traders/vendors, given the location context, this will be done through less formal mobilization of vendors. Vendors sign an agreement acknowledging full understanding of the voucher process—no guarantee of sales, vendors ensures security of their own merchandise, respect for beneficiaries as a humanitarian intervention.• Set up of Community Committees for beneficiary selection, a Community based approach will be used since its effective both in terms of providing a mechanism for implementing a relief operation in a relatively short period of time, and in terms of meeting the principles of transparency, accountability and fairness. This will have a maximum of 12 members• Selection, registration and verification of beneficiaries, this will be done by the enumerators with recommendation from the community committees. Registration lists will be finalized using vulnerability and targeting criteria. • Sensitization of beneficiaries & vendors on voucher redemption process, for beneficiaries this is primarily to underline key aspects of the approach such as families being represented by the female adults, the use of the coupons• Community participatory identification of productive assets for rehabilitation• There will also be training of beneficiaries on basic agronomics through demonstration plots and modern methods of fish preservation. Lead Farmers will be identified and trained, besides, 25% of the beneficiaries will also be trained Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring exercises and post harvest surveys will be conducted to ascertain the impact of the project at household and Community level and broader economic level.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

To ensure accountability, SPEDP staffs will detail all the project components, budgets, duration and any other relevant information to the stakeholders during the inception workshop so as to show a sign of transparency, there will be a two-way communication approach during the implementation. Focus group discussions will be organized to provide feedback on matters that are not of a confidential matter. Constant communications will be made with the beneficiaries and other stakeholders about the progress of this project through reviews

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To ensure that marginalised groups are included in these communications, the project will ensure that meetings and/or workshops are held at a location and time that enables attendance, especially by women and persons living with disability. To ensure that the voices of these and other groups (including youth) are heard, the project will conduct both mixed and group-specific communication for them to gain a stronger understanding of the project and also to capture their unique concerns and interests. Female facilitators will be included to ensure women have a constructive space to provide input.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

In order that project activities accurately reflect local priorities and requirements, participatory methodologies that ensure female representation were used to ensure activities equally benefit women, girls, boys and men. To support the prioritisation of community-identified needs, the project used Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) process. SPEDP recognises it is critical to include women’s voices in these discussions, as community planning is traditionally dominated by men and may miss opportunities to build on women’s knowledge (particularly related to nutrition and childcare), or be of equal benefit to women and girls and therefore included all categories of people in these consultative meetings.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee’s to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community member (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels.The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicise and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.

Community Engagement?

Yes

SPEDP will use a Community Centred Development Approach in conducting monitoring of this project. Community volunteers will be selected with the help of local chiefs to take an active part in the PM&E activities of collecting information at agreed upon intervals on activities that are being/have been implemented, reflect and report on them. This will provide a platform for collection of beneficiary experience through the most significant change stories (MSC)

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP incorporated protection into this project to ensure interventions are safe and appropriate to the context. To ensure that this project is safe, dignified and mindful of people's varied circumstances, needs, rights and capacities, protection considerations were incorporated to uncover the linkages between food insecurity and protection risks and identify protection issues that might affect the impact of food security interventions. A protection analysis was conducted to analyze protection needs, considering risks, threats, people's vulnerability to threats, and their capacity to reduce threats to themselves. This was not done as a stand-alone exercise but rather in conjunction with the community based participatory exercise and will continue to be carried out at all stages of this project to inform project implementation. This was undertaken to understand issues like protection risks women, men, girls and boys exposed to, who is affected and how? (Men, women, boys and girls, different age groups, ethnic groups, people with special needs may be affected differently), if protection risks affect project implementation and the impact of SPEDP programmes, and how SPEDP can avoid exposing its beneficiaries to protection risks when implementing its activities

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP will arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to needs and without barriers (e.g. discrimination). Particular attention will be provided to the situation of vulnerable people who may face particular problems in accessing services, because of reduced mobility, marginalization among their communities, or other constraints. SPEDP will further reinforce procedures for the easy referral or orientation of people with special needs to the protection sector actors within the communities of operation. Social/cultural obstacles to services for GBV survivors will also be addressed during this project life cycle

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP is fully aware that individuals or households may be vulnerable to protection risks and food insecurity as a result of discriminatory structures or institutional norms that perpetuate abuse of power and therefore understands the interplay between these and their impact. Therefore, SPEDP will ensure that there is no discrimination and marginalization of individuals or groups based on gender, sexuality, ethnicity, social status, chronic illness or disability when it comes to beneficiaries’ selection. The project will also ensure that unaccompanied children, child-headed households, elderly-headed households, households with high dependency rates and no or limited income generating opportunities, women-headed households (depending on context), and households headed by the chronically ill, including persons with disabilities are included in the project as they are considered as groups particularly at risk of being exposed to protection risks related to food insecurity. The project will also ensure communities are actively involved in beneficiary selection, will conduct face to face information, question and answer sessions, and establish a complaints system to receive and pre emptively respond to issues with the potential to negatively impact community relationships. SPEDP will ensure that this project is implemented with a gender lens-women girls ,boys and men will equally be represented at all levels of implementation. SPEDP will also ensure an integrated approach in the implementation of this project, passive nutrition will be carried out in the course of this project

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

SPEDP will assess and monitor access of this project by beneficiaries by collecting disaggregated data by age, gender, and location or specific community. For some individuals, for example, older persons or persons with disabilities, cannot access the services, monitoring team will ensure that special arrangements are made to bring inputs, food or cash to them. SPEDP understands that It cannot always be assumed that friends and family will do it. Further still, the monitoring team will ensure that coordination with specialized organizations for example, Handicap International and HelpAge International is made to identify individuals with limited mobility and include them in the programme assistance

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

SPEDP will ensure safety monitoring is applied to, at, and from distribution points. In the current conflict in South Sudan, women and men have often taken high risks crossing frontlines and moving long dangerous routes in search of food and water. SPEDP engages with affected communities throughout projects implementation and considers women’s, men’s, boys’ and girls’ need and protection concerns that affect our operations.Monitoring will be looking at aspects of protection concerns that beneficiaries face coming to our site such as crossing rivers or long walking distances, where distribution location sites are set-up and ensuring meaningful access for the most vulnerable individuals and groups such as older persons, female heads of household, unaccompanied minors, and those severely sick. Further monitoring will also be looking at capacities that the local communities have that SPEDP can support to reduce and mitigate protection risks

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

SPEDP believes that protection is instrically linked to food security and livelihoods and so beneficiary self-worth, esteem and giving people choices informs SPEDP’s programming. Project monitoring will look at aspects that include but not limited to beneficiaries lining during distribution to ensure that they are not kept in the line for long and that there are shades to avoid scorching sun, latrines and water at distribution sites. This will be done to see to it that affected communities are not seen as victims but as individuals that can adopt positive coping strategies in times of crisis that we need to support and respect. This will further protect dignity in the most fundamental aspect of being able to provide assistance for oneself and one’s family
