Project Summary

Since the break out of the Syrian conﬂict more than ten years, Syria has become a battleground for many world and local powers. Armed groups were emerged and the war has left what can be described as one of the worst humanitarian crises of all time. Millions of people have been killed or forced to ﬂee from their homes. Families are struggling to survive inside Syria, or make a new home in neighboring countries.
Ten years of conﬂicts were enough to weak the Syrian citizens and create execrable humanitarian situation. The degree of resilience and positive coping mechanism of the affected population have reduced due to the protracted nature of the conﬂict. The economic and social situation in Syria continues to decline and severely affect everybody especially those who have been displaced from or returned to their homes. Statistics reveal that two-thirds of Syrians are living in extreme poverty, unable to meet basic food and non-food needs. The main reasons for poverty are loss of property and jobs, loss of access to public services, including health and clean water, and rising food prices.
According to HNO 2021, over 75 per cent of communities reported that clothing had been unaffordable or unavailable over the last 3 months. Unavailability and unaffordability of individual items are linked, indicating that limited supplies may be driving higher prices. people in Syria are resorting to harmful coping strategies such as depletion of assets and increasing debt in order to meet their needs.
It was reported that 13,587,131 people are in need for NFIs assistances in Syria, this project will contribute to provide sustainable resilience to those must in need for NFIs.
The purpose of this project is to provide winterization items to returnees people living NES and other rural areas.
If the commodities in local markets available, the distributions modality in this intervention will be through vouchers for winter items, Otherwise, it will be in-kind aid according to sector standards.
This project will target the following areas through 2022 project:
1- Rural Damascus: Al-Tall, Yabroud, and Sahnaya. these areas are with severity scale 4 and Pin 188,012 persons. through this project 3000 H.H will be targeted with winterization items.
2- Homs city: Al- Qusayr, severity scale is 3, PiN 14,302. and 1000 H.H will be supported with winterization items.
3- Idleb: Harim and salqin, severity scale is 4, PiN 175,809. and 2000 H.H will be supported with winterization items.
4- Al-Hasakeh city, severity scale is 2, PiN 79,573. 2000 H.H will be provided with winterization items.
These activities will carry on from October till end of December 2022.
As well as for 2023 project GOPA-DERD plan to target 5,000 H.H (25,000 individual) with winterization assistance.
This project will target the most venerable families by applying the following selection criteria's:
- Female headed household
- Families with pregnant women.
- Families with disabilities and elder people
- Families did not received any winterization items before.
- Families with large number of household members (more than five)
Also, IDP households who have not received any supports before are prioritized.
It is expected that at least men, women, girls and boys will be supported (assuming 5 family members per household). The amount of each transfer is 375,000 SYP.
During the life of this project, GOPA-DERD will coordinate with the community leaders, other humanitarian actors providing assistance in this area, NFIs sector through the sector meetings.
GOPA-DERD will coordinate with other actors working in the field in terms of different projects being implemented , to avoid duplication and to coordinate with other activities being implemented to target the most needed households in addition to implement joint activities in the same targeted location.

4 - Needs

One of the main sector plans is to support saving lives, alleviation of suffering and increase access to humanitarian response for vulnerable people, not to mention those with special needs, who are increasingly affected by the length of the crisis.The scale and scope of life-saving needs continue to increase. Lack of access to many affected people in need remains one of the main impediments to addressing needs, which requires increased innovation and flexibility.GOPA has its own Beneficiary Registration Management System (BRMS) that reflects the needs assessment carried out by GOPA-DERD mobile teams in all 13 out of 14 governorates in Syria. This conducted needs assessment aims to provide an overview of the situation in the areas based on beneficiary input in one-on-one interviews with beneficiaries who visit GOPA-DERD’s 56 service centers scattered across the country. GOPA's mobile team conducted needs assessment visits in these areas at the last 2 months from 2021 year in order to better identify the humanitarian needs and priorities of families affected by the crisis, so that it can design a program that better meets these needs. The needs assessment visits have a clear idea of the population dynamics and need.As well, through needs assessment GOPA-DERD was able to identify the most vulnerable families to be targeted through this project.HNO 2022 statistics shown incurring on numbers of PiN (4,909,468) compering with PiN one 2021 (4,694,262)

5 - Implementation of activities

Distribution of winterization assistance to target communities following a set vulnerability criteria GOPA-DERD will be responsible for needs assessments, beneficiary verification data based on selection criteria's mentioned in project summary.GOPA-DERD's team will conduct a market study in each target area, and based on the result the distribution modality will be decided either vouchers or in kind assistance.Since this project will target some rural areas where there is no functioning market yet, wherefore using vouchers as a modality is not suitable in this case. but when the market has the capacity to provide the winterization commodities the distribution modality will be as a vouchers.GOPA-DERD follows a participatory approach throughout the project phases (planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). The opinions and views of the various stakeholders are elicited and taken into consideration regarding the various project components and through various channels: focus group discussions, interviews, meetings, and field visits.For all components of the program, GOPA-DERD will apply its M&E Policy Framework reflecting standardized monitoring processes and criteria that will facilitate ongoing information gathering and reporting throughout the project implementation period.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All project activities will use a rights-based approach to provide a protective environment that promotes equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specification of targeted populations. Also, all our volunteers have already received a package of training including: psychological first aid, non-violent communication, protection induction, basics of SGBV, first aid, PSEA and risk management in case of any emergency, as well as other basic information that enables them to act towards any person in need with total respect and most importantly to deliver assistance while protecting the dignity of the person.GOPA-DERD will ensure the “Do No Harm” principle by carrying out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its strategy development. - In some urban and crowded areas, humanitarian and beneficiaries may face a GBV abuse and Sexual exploitation.Mitigation measure :1- GOPA-DERD will make sure that its staff is well trained on SGBV basics.2- GOPA-DERD had developed a misconduct internal policy to be fit with the humanitarian works in all sectors and locations. 3- It is very common that the children would be exposed to any kind of harassment or abuse, GOPA-DERD will ensure providing gender-segregated facilities to avoid such cases.

And Coordination will be made with the sector and SHF in order to increase or decrease the project target.Level of risk: Acceptable.Inclusion and exclusion risk through distribution activities: (Low risk)Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: Since the intended beneficiaries of this project are the most vulnerable part, the possibility of excluding vulnerable groups is not possible.Level of risk: acceptable. - Spread of Covid-19:Impact: high Mitigation measures:GOPA distribution team was trained on protection measures which include the following:- Instruct beneficiaries to maintain a distance of one meter at all points during distributions - Ensure that all beneficiaries wash their hands at the hand washing point and have body temperature checks before approaching the identity verification checkpoint. - If a beneficiary presents high temperature, they must be directed to the sheltered/covered area and referred to the relevant health authorities. - It is mandatory that all staff at the distribution site perform hand sanitation and follow general hygiene practices.Ensure that all team and beneficiaries wear a face mask during distributions process.
