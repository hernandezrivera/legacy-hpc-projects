Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance health system resilience, ensuring a rapid and effective response to health emergencies, particularly cholera outbreaks, flooding, and other infectious diseases such as diphtheria, in Bade, Nangere, and Fune LGAs of Yobe State. The project will deploy Rapid Response Teams within 72 hours of a confirmed cholera outbreak or flooding event to conduct immediate needs assessments and initiate essential health interventions. This proactive approach will help prevent the spread of diseases, mitigate public health risks, and ensure timely medical care.
A key component of the response will be the establishment of mobile health units to provide basic health services to affected populations, especially in remote or hard-to-reach areas. These units will maintain continuity of care and address immediate health needs, including cholera treatment, diphtheria management, maternal health services, and routine medical care. The project will also facilitate weekly integrated health outreach to vulnerable communities in the three LGAs to monitor health conditions and prevent potential outbreaks.
In addition, the project will ensure the availability of essential medicines for treating minor ailments and providing reproductive health care, including antenatal, labor and delivery, and postnatal care services. By pre-positioning critical resources and strengthening the capacity of local health systems, the project aims to create a more resilient health infrastructure capable of managing emergency health challenges and ensuring sustained care for vulnerable populations in Bade, Nangere, and Fune LGAs, Yobe State.

1 - Needs

According to the Yobe 2024 Floods Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment Report and the RAWYOD Flood Needs Assessment, access to healthcare services in Bade, Nangere, and Fune LGAs of Yobe State remains a significant challenge, despite general awareness of available services. Nearly half (47%) of the population is aware of healthcare services, yet key barriers such as long wait times (27%), unavailability of medicines (22%), and high costs (16%) restrict access to essential care. Women, in particular, face increased difficulties in accessing maternal health services (24%) and reproductive healthcare (11%), which further heightens the vulnerability of affected households during health emergencies, such as flooding and disease outbreaks.While 32% of respondents report no issues accessing health services for children, 43% observe psychosocial distress among children, including nightmares, sadness, and anxiety following the floods. These findings reflect the pressing need to address not only physical health challenges but also the mental health impact of flooding. In light of the ongoing threat of cholera outbreaks and other infectious diseases, such as diphtheria, these barriers expose the population to heightened health risks, underscoring the urgent need for strengthened health systems, improved access to care, and focused interventions for vulnerable populations, including women, children, and those in hard-to-reach areas, as identified in the assessments.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will consider implementing the following key activities; • Deploy, within 72 hours, a Rapid Response Teams to conduct rapid needs assessment and initiate immediate response activities in event of increased cases of confirmed Cholera outbreak in a specified location. • Set up a mobile health unit to provide basic health services to address basic health needs of the affected population and ensure health services in focal ward PHC are sustained. • Provide integrated health outreach to hard-to-reach communities and settlements, at least once in a week to catchment population of the focal PHC. • Ensure availability of essential medicines for the treatment of minor ailments and reproductive health care (ANC, Labour and Delivery, PNC).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming in the implementation of the outlined activities, the project will adopt a rights-based, inclusive approach that prioritizes the safety, dignity, and well-being of all individuals, especially vulnerable groups, during emergency responses. Rapid Response Teams will be trained to conduct gender-sensitive and inclusive assessments, ensuring that the specific needs of women, children, persons with disabilities, and marginalized groups are identified and addressed promptly during cholera outbreaks or flooding events. Mobile health units will be equipped with both female and male healthcare professionals to ensure women and girls have access to gender-sensitive healthcare services, particularly reproductive health services. Safe spaces will be provided for women during labor and delivery, ensuring their dignity and privacy. Health outreach will be conducted in a participatory manner, ensuring that community members, including women and youth, are actively involved in decision-making processes. Special efforts will be made to reach vulnerable groups, including those in hard-to-reach areas. The project will also establish mechanisms to prevent and respond to gender-based violence (GBV), ensuring safe, accessible healthcare for survivors, and will ensure that materials, such as medicine and healthcare services, are distributed equitably with particular attention given to pregnant women, children, and individuals with special needs. By embedding protection principles into every activity, the project will ensure that its interventions uphold the safety, rights, and dignity of all individuals, especially women, children, and marginalized groups.
