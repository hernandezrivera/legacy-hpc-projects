Project Summary


The ongoing insecurity since 2019 in Metekel Zone in Benishangul Gumuz Region has displaced hundreds of thousands of people, with some of the communities facing recurring displacement. The severity of the woredas is determined by the ongoing conflict, humanitarian access, number of affected populations and the limited operational presence of the cluster partners in the region. In Western Ethiopia, the presence of UAGs has led to the displacement of a huge number of people from their homes. In the Benishangul Gumuz Region, attacks by UAGs and clashes between UAGs and government security forces continued throughout the year, shrinking the humanitarian spaces. On the other hand, according to UNOCHA Flash Update No. 2, security in Metekel Zone has been gradually deteriorating since 2019, and more intensely so in recent months, prompting the federal Government of Ethiopia to declare a State of Emergency in Metekel Zone. Since July 2021, attacks by unidentified armed groups (UAGs) have cumulatively displaced 150,000 people in Bullen, Dangur, and sherkole woredas, representing over 40 per cent of the total population of Metekel and Assossa Zone, which was about 479,162 people. Currently, Metekel and assossa zone of Benishangul is the most affected zone where the displaced communities are left without basic humanitarian aid.
Majority of the IDPs are living in collective sites like schools and are without basic shelter and housing. Most vulnerable people are living in collective sites that are sub-standard for shelter. Needs for Shelter and NFI assistance remain significant, and response is still limited compared to the needs. In response to this MCMDO plan to provide assistance for beneficiaries that settled in camps, collective sites, Host communities and informal settlements. The project location are categorized as sever and catastrophic level as per the cluster priority classification for 2022 HRP. The project will be implemented in Dangur(n- 20318)and Bullen (n-14902)woreda of Metekel zone and sherkole woreda(n-6401) of assossa zone. The project targeted a total of 41621 beneficiaries out of which 4161 are returnees, 6242 are Host communities and 31218 are IDP in camp and informal settlement.
During beneficiary targeting, the shelter cluster beneficiary selection and targeting guideline will be used while the most vulnerable community group including child-headed household, the separated child in need of shelter, Children at risk, female-headed household, elders, and people with underlying chronic illness, a large family will get the priority. The project will strictly follow and adhere COVID-19 during registration, and distribution operation procedure to mitigate the outbreak. . Post-distribution monitoring assessment will also be conducted for both ESNFI and Cash distribution using shelter cluster standard tools. the proposed response is in line with ESNFI Cluster objective 1 (Contribute to the protection of IDPs by providing life-saving ES/NFIs to improve their living conditions and the capacity for self-recovery) and objective 2 (Ensure that displaced affected people have adequate protection, safety, dignity, well-being and equitable access to shelter solutions). The project proposes to implement the following activities in three location-woredas. Sherkole: NFI and SRK, Dangur: NFI and SRK and Bullen, ESNFI, cash for shelter, NFI
• Provision of in-kind or cash for emergency shelter assistance for physical protection and to reduce overcrowding
• Provision of Emergency Shelter Repair Kit to HHs whose houses are completely damaged
• Provision of Non-food items to reduce the likelihood of health and protection consequences
• Provision of in-kind or cash for emergency shelter assistance for physical protection and to reduce overcrowding
• Provision of ESNFIs to the persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Response are designed considering the real needs at the community level. MCMDO conducts quick assessments to identify the scope and types of humanitarian responses needs. The assessment is a joint assessment which involves the local government, sector offices, the community representatives, affected communities and other key stakeholders. MCMDO assessment teams use standardized templates to capture the existing response needs. Key informant interviews, Focus Group discussions, document review and site visits are some of the basic tools used to capture the key information to design the response. MCMDO also conducts a discussion before the design of the response with the sector offices to discuss and agree on the strategies, activities and targeting. Using these inputs, MCMDO compiles the information and produces assessment reports which are used as a sole information to design the responses for resource mobilization. As resources are committed by donors, MCMDO submit the response to the regional or zonal stakeholders for appraisal. The stakeholders review and sign the project agreement as the project is designed jointly and reflects the primary needs of the affected communities. The response is aligned and harmonized with the existing government and other actors plans to avoid duplication of efforts and realize complementarity to meet the wider need of the communities. MCMDO advocates the stakeholders and the community to contribute resources including resources in cash, in kind, technical support or other forms of contributions. This helps the response to be sustainable and the beneficiaries and the stakeholders to ensure the ownership of the response outcomes. During the implementation of the projects, the stakeholders, community representatives and other actors engaged during the design stage will be part and parcel of the implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of the response. MCMDO invites the government counter parts to periodically review the performance and achievements of the response, provide feedbacks and capitalize the best practices. The beneficiaries and community representatives lead the implementation of the activities at community level. They are engaged in Beneficiaries selection & verification, delivery of response/support, modalities, efficiency, effectiveness and relevance of the response. Moreover, MCMDO will design and implement compliant and feedback mechanisms at grassroots levels which helps to ensure the accountability to the affected Populations (AAP). Such systems will be reviewed periodically and assess their effectiveness as well as relevance to the targeted communities. Thus, the targeted beneficiaries and the local stakeholders will make meaningful participations and lead the response to contribute for the realization of the planned objectives and achieve wider impacts on the lives of the beneficiaries.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Accountability to affected people (AAP) is one of MCMDO,s quality standards reflected in programs design and implementation through active engagement and participation of the community throughout the project cycle. MCMDO has engaged the targeted beneficiaries and local actors in all stages of the project. The project will establish robust feedback mechanisms to get feedback, complaints and other information from the community beginningMCMDO will establish a complaints and feedback response mechanism (CRM) for community feedback on the ES-NFI- distributions. All targeted households will be provided with information on the project, their entitlements, and modalities for collection of NFIs, as well as ways to make contact with MCMDO and provide feedback through the CRM or by other means. Male and female representatives from the targeted community will be selected to take part on a community feedback committee during the distributions. In addition, information regarding MCMDOs will be provided to all stakeholders for all project activities (including but not limited to Woreda and kebele government, community members, contractors, health workers and community representatives involved in the training), during meetings, and information will be displayed in MCMDO office.. The target groups in need of special support or protection include the elderly, women, and people with disabilities, as they are marginalized or generally more disadvantaged within society and may therefore develop less resistance to crises such as displacement and food insecurity. Women are largely excluded from decision-making processes due to cultural norms and have very few economic. They are often also the main caregivers of children and other family members needing care (sick, people with disabilities, elderly). In addition, elderly people and people with disabilities are particularly affected by various shocks and conflicts as they are also rarely involved in decision-making processes and their special needs remain invisible. For these reasons, Vulnerability criteria will be established and agreed on with the local authorities and target communities (such as pregnant and lactating women, female-headed and youth-headed households, households with elderly or disabled members, or large family size). Finally, the inclusion of these especially vulnerable target groups (women, people with disabilities, infants/children and elderly people) in the project will be first ensured by a transparent beneficiary selection procedure to ensure that they are involved in the decisions on project support from the very beginning.
