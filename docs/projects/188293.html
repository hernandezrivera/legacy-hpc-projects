Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, World Vision Syria Response (WVSR) will target some of the most vulnerable families in Idleb, Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa through multi-purpose cash assistance including but not limited to: HHs with PLW and members at high risk of malnutrition, HH with PwD and chronic illness members, GBV, Child labor, Child marriage among others. WV will aim at supporting families to meet their most urgent Survival needs through one-off multi-purpose cash assistance (MPC) of 100 USD based on the harmonized MPC guidance. WVSR follows CWG recommendation in the design and implementation of CVA in the targeted area. The frequency of distribution, targeting, and currency used will be shaped by coordinating and aligning together with other humanitarian actors active in the targeted area of implementation as well as engaging with the community and in involving them in decision-making around programme design and frequency of MPC distribution.

WVSR tends to provide cash and voucher assistance (CVA) as part of its emergency aid for the acute needs of the IDPs and host communities. CVA is intended to reduce the negative impacts created by sudden onset displacement and enable flexibility and adaptability to meet survival needs, especially for IDP populations of high mobility.
As per the recommendation of the lessons learned WV conducted about former CVA intervention in Syria, MPC has been found as the best modality to deliver emergency assistance for people with acute needs. The humanitarian community acknowledges that MPC is the best form of relief assistance where markets are functioning, accessible to beneficiaries and a reliable cash transfer mechanisms exist.
MPC emphasizes crisis-affected households’ ability to prioritize their own needs, upholding their dignity. CVA also has the additional benefit of supporting local markets and boosting the local economy.
In terms of market functionality in NWS, based on the last Syria market monitoring exercise conducted by Reach December 2021, and the regular mentoring activities WV and its implementing partners conduct to mentoring the availability and affordability of the main items. The local markets have been exceptionally resilient, accessible, and responsive throughout the crisis and are expected to remain so in the near future. The primary risks on the economy stems from the high inflation of the Syrian Pound and Turkish Lira but this weakness of local currencies is mitigated via (a) the use of U.S. Dollars in programming and (b) the use of e-value vouchers, which has the further benefit of reducing the pressure on US Dollar liquidity needs.
Within this operational environment, WV identifies the need to use the hardest currency available in our distributions so that it preserves its purchasing power for beneficiaries. However, following area-based programming, a joint decision between the humanitarian actors on currency to be made based on cost-effectiveness and cost-efficiency and involving the local community at the area level in developing the targeting/prioritization to avoid local tension.
To ensure robust accountability towards recipients and donors, WV cash assistance will rely on its digital platform Last Mile Mobile Solution (LMMS) and LMMS E-cards to ensure accurate management of beneficiaries' data and will contract a trusted financial service provider (hawala) for the hard-cash provision in distribution points accessible and safe for both recipients and staff.
WV MEAL department will monitor local markets pre- and post-project implementation to ensure the functionality of local markets as well as being present during the distribution to ensure regularity of operations and will conduct a post-distribution monitoring PDM exercise to assess the use of cash. For the PDM exercise, WVSR adheres to the usage of the harmonized PDM tool developed by the CWG. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be in place and will allow WV to promptly address recipients feedback.

4 - Needs

The impact of the prolonged crisis on people, services and access during 2020 has led to a 21% increase in the number of Syrians in need of humanitarian assistance, from 11 million in 2020 to 13.4 million in 2021Living standards for the vast majority of the population have substantially worsened in the past year, with 82% of Syrians and 91% of female-headed households self-reporting a significant deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs since August 2019, due mainly to price increases and loss of income. Across different population groups, income insufficiency is generally highest amongst IDPs, with IDPs living in Idleb, Al-Hasakeh/Ar-Raqqa which are the first, third and fourth highest cities hosting IDPS respectively reporting above-average figures65% of households indicate that they are currently not able to meet the basic needs of their families in a self-sustained manner, with the population in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa reporting particularly high limitations of 86-100%. At the beginning of Y21, people in Syria prioritize access to food as their number one need, followed by LH and income support, and NFI and shelter assistanceWV intends to utilize MPC as a first-line response addressing the survival needs of the affected population across the different sectorial needs.WV will use secondary assessments data in addition to internal market assessment\feasibility studies conducted in the targeted locations to identify the best modality/mechanism of the distribution

5 - Implementation of activities

Within this operational environment, WV identifies the need of using the hardest currency available (U.S. Dollars) in our distributions so that it preserves its purchasing power for beneficiaries. However, following area-based programming, a joint decision between the humanitarian actors on currency to be made based on cost-effectiveness and cost-efficiency and involving the local community at the area level in developing the targeting/prioritization to avoid local tension. As per the MPC guidance, each family will be entitled to 100$ to ensure their ability to meet their most urgent survival needs. The transfer value is calculated based on the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket SMEB (coordinated at the CWG level). WV will conduct Distribution Monitoring (DM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) activities for each round, carried out by the MEL team (PDM approx. 3-4 weeks after each round). WV will utilize the harmonized PDM tool developed by the CWG for the PDM exercise. Further, community-level evaluation exercises take place to get their perceptions on the CVA intervention and to capture any growing risks to WV field staff and consequential access issues. Further, WV will follow its internal CVA SOP which clarifies all steps needed to implement MPC intervention with detailed roles and responsibilities delineations of each department/ implementing partner (if any).

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WV adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA. And it is committed responsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men – are treated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WV field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which is led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WV field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle. Furthermore, WV has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WV uses a standard online platform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WV will prioritize having a harmonized risk mitigation approach based on the CVA risk mitigation matrix developed by the task force for NWS in which WV played a key role for its developmentOne of the key risks to consider as part of the MPC programming is the lack of harmonization of the currency used in the assistance distribution which have a negative impact and local currency, by encouraging devaluation, and the absence of a unified WV modality could create tensions within communities where difference currencies are being used. WV will make sure to utilize the hardest currency available in our distributions so that it preserves its purchasing power for beneficiaries. Also, to follow the Cash Working Group recommendation WV will actively participate at the area level a joint decision between the humanitarian actors on currency to be made based on cost-effectiveness and cost-efficiency and involving the local community at the area level in developing the targeting/prioritization to avoid local tensionThe distribution of cash, or all types of CVA, is selected against by donors or prohibited by local authorities.
