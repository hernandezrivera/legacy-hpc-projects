Project Summary

The emergency Education project will be majorly tailored to provide Education in emergency (EIE) services to the vulnerable Children of IDP, Returnees and Host communities who are affected by crisis in Malakal, Pigi, Fangak, Pibor, Bor, Akobo, Aweil North and TWIC counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei, Northen Bahr-el-gazal and Warrap states, Targeting 16839 beneficiaries( 8083 B and 8756 G) of which 15% will be children with disability(CWD) through provision of lifesaving Education in Emergencies services including Back to school and sensitization campaigns to increase enrolment of children and adolescents (3-17yrs) to PPE, Primary and Secondary classes; Enrolment of pupils in Formal and Non formal education, Establishment/ Rehabilitation of Temporary learning spaces, Construction of gender sensitive WASH facilities, Capacity building of Teachers on EiE, Distribution of learning kits, procurement and distribution of learning and recreational material for children and youth, Establishment and orientation of PTA/SMC including training, and offering deworming health services to targeted learners.
In addition, this project will directly target teachers with 40% as female teachers across the targeted locations, PTA/SMC members and Education officials in the States and Counties. While this project purposes to primarily focus on the above-mentioned school age children, SAADO will also target children (age 3 - 5 years) with early childhood education interventions. The following key areas will be the focus of this project: Access to education and retention of boys and girls including out of school children, IDPs, Returnees, children with disabilities and teenage mothers is enhanced: Barriers to accessing education, such as lack of learning materials, inadequate learning spaces, WASH facilities will be reduced through provision of learning materials, renovation/construction of TLS, provision of WASH facilities, and provision of assistive learning materials for CWDs through partnership with concern institutions and organizations; The quality of teaching and learning is sustained through the capacity of teachers to adapt teaching to challenging conditions, psychosocial support for children, provision of teaching and learning materials for learners and teachers; Children are safe in schools and can be able to build resilience and positive coping mechanisms to face shocks related to conflict, crisis and disasters, and responding to security and safety, and violence against children within school environment; Improved capacity and systems of the Ministry of Education and community-based structures for service delivery, quality education and leadership strengthened. Through this project Prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse will be prioritize during the implementation. Teacher, PTA, Project staffs and pupil will receive training on PSEA. For Accountability to affected population (AAP), a committee will be established in each county where all the complaint from the beneficiaries will be channels through them to the partner. To strengthen Synergies with other sector, SAADO will work closely with other partners implementing project in different sectors including Nutrition, WASH, Health, GBV & Protection and FSL.
Generally, through this project a strong MEAL system will be put in place to monitor report, Community feedback, document, and share evident lessons. The project is also designed to ensure maximum impact is achieved through the proposed interventions besides ensuring accountability to the affected population. The estimated cost for this education in emergency project will be 1,118,104.94 USD. The main overall objective of these project is to increase access to, and quality for education in emergency, targeting vulnerable pupil (boys and Girls) in the targeted areas that are hard-to-reach

1. Needs

As evidenced from various assessments and analysis, the current and evolving needs in relation to education services in South Sudan, which identified as existing educational challenges and inequities that have been exacerbated by broader social-economic hardships according to HNO,2023. There are at least over 800,000 vulnerable children with severe needs in South Sudan. Insecurity and recurring inter-communal violence are the main impediment to access to education services for children and youth. These two factors have had significant impact on school-to-school functionality, resulted in high dropout rates and contributed largely on compromising the quality of education in the country. Report indicates, In the wake of persistent fragility and conflict, at least 2.2 million school-aged children are estimated to be out of school with thousands more at risk of dropping out. Many schools have been closed, submerged, or destroyed due to floods according to Education briefing note, (UNICEF South Sudan, Oct 2022). Furthermore, barriers are even extreme for the marginalized groups in the country. For girls, forced marriages and other negative traditional practices have had a significant impact on enrolment, retention, and transition to secondary school for the girls. Children with disabilities face more worse challenges in relation to access to education services. Stigma from the community, lack of accessible learning materials, school infrastructure that are not responsive to their needs and lack of trained teachers are just among some of the barriers. At the school level, the barriers include lack of clean water and WASH facilities, inadequate learning spaces, low teacher /student ratio, and lack of trained teachers. Barriers are amplified for the adolescent girls and teenage mothers. Lack of menstrual health and hygiene and washrooms for girls in some schools has resulted in irregular attendance or even drop out of girls from schools. Children in IDP camps are finding it very difficult to gain access to education services due to several social-economic reasons. In addition, the fees associated with education is the greatest barrier to access to education. Most of them are likely to miss out on education due to these challenges.

2. Project activities

This project is meant to improve access to, and quality of education in emergency to 16839 pupils (8083B & 8756 G) in the targeted counties of Malakal, Pigi, Duk, Pibor, Bor, Akobo, Aweil North and TWIC through the following plan activities • Conduct back to school, community mobilizations and sensitizations campaigns to encourage enrolment of children and adolescents (3-17yrs) to PPE, Primary and Secondary classes. • Support targeted school and pupils' enrolment in Formal (ECD, Primary and Secondary and Non formal accelerated learning Programme (ALP) reaching 16839 pupils (8083B & 8756G)• Establishment/rehabilitation of 34 temporary learning spaces and classrooms in targeted counties in line with INEE-Minimum standards for education in emergencies.• Construction of 168 stance of gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with MoGEI/education cluster standard• Training of the 72(29F:43M) teachers on EiE- education in emergency and Pedagogical skills including PSEA• Procurement and distribution of 51 ECD Kits to the targeted schools • Procure and distribute 46 learning and recreational material for children and youth (6-17 years)• Establishment and orientation of 119 PTAs in the targeted schools to ensure retention and sustainability• Training of School 119 Management committee (SMC), PTA and education authorities on school Management, Governance, climate change, Disaster Risks Reduction (DRR) and PSEA• Offering deworming health services in 17 selected learning centres targeting 16839 pupils (8083B & 8756 G) in Malakal, Pigi, Duk, Pibor, Bor, Akobo, Aweil North and TWIC Counties.
