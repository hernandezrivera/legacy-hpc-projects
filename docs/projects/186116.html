Project Summary

The proposed project’s objective is to enable conflict-affected populations to meet their basic needs and provide vulnerable individuals with support when they experience harm, while promoting a transition towards early recovery and resilience-strengthening in the medium term. This strategy is aligned with the signature outcomes of the IRC Strategic Action Plan.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Active violence in the North-West and South-West regions has seen about 742,253 individuals displaced from the area . Humanitarian needs, compounded by structural under-development, continue to be wide-ranging for an estimated 2.3 million in need in the NW/SW, an 80% increase compared to 2019 . The ongoing violence perpetrated by armed actors, combined with pre-existing vulnerabilities, endemic poverty , and cultural/traditional norms, presents significant risks, especially displaced women and girls. Between January and August 2021, 7,534 cases of gender-based violence (GBV) were recorded in the GBVIMS by UNFPA in the three regions, with 1,164 (15%) reported by IRC, along with other human rights violations (e.g., theft, physical violence, kidnapping, looting of properties) which displacement increases vulnerability to. An increase in protection incidents affecting children was also reported in 2021 as the security situation continued to deteriorate. This, combined with already precarious socio-economic conditions faced by many households living in conflict-affected areas limits children and youth’s access to social services, education and livelihood opportunities and further undermines their safety and well-being . Crisis-affected populations in the NW, SW regions of Cameroon continue to face acute problems related to access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) facilities. According to the HRP 2021, a shortage of safe drinking water in rural areas leads people to rely on water from streams, rivers, and unprotected wells for drinking and domestic use. According to the MSNA of August 2021, 52.7% of of the population collect water from an unimproved water source (unprotected springs, surface water, open unprotected wall) and 31% of villages are more than 30 minutes away from their closest water sources. In addition, open defecation or construction of unsafe latrines is a common practice in most rural settings; the main issues to access sanitation facilities are facilities not hygienic and lack of latrines. Cameroon is also affected by COVID-19, with 107,148 confirmed cases and 1,804 deaths as of November 2021 according to the government's N104 sitrep. Only 5.4% of the population is vaccinated, which makes the population vulnerable to the multiple variants of COVID-19 that could occur. Food security and populations’ capacities to meet their basic needs as well generating income is severely impacted by insecurity, ongoing conflict and COVID 19; and the NW/SW regions are expected to be in crisis phase (IPC 3) between October 2021 and March 2022 due to higher prices in the market and early depletion of crops, and FN region in stressed phase (IPC2). The ongoing crisis in the Far North and NW/SW continue to erode the protective environment and directly affect men, women, boys, and girls, particularly the most vulnerable, including IDPs, refugees, returnees and host communities.
