Project Summary

Needs:
Humanitarian exigencies in NWS have reached a perilous zenith, necessitating outlined project's pivotal interventions. 2024 HNO report delineates a dire scenario with 15.3 million individuals in desperate need, arising from basic service failure, cholera outbreaks, and climatic adversities 1. Catastrophic earthquakes in February 2023 wrought havoc, with over 7.2 million affected in Aleppo and Idleb alone, emphasizing the need for robust Protection Intervention 2
Escalating hostilities, evidenced by intense airstrikes and bombardments, further exacerbate the humanitarian landscape, underscoring the need for comprehensive protection services. 
Based on RNA conducted recently by IYD in proposed areas, recommendations are: 
Prioritize GBV prevention, emergency readiness, and survivor help. Enhance child safety with focused facilities, aid for unaccompanied youths, therapeutic care, and learning support. Enforce protective measures, legal support, essential documentation, and information access for vulnerable individuals.
Intervention, and Implementation of Activities:
This project is a beacon of hope for improved access to superior protection services for those affected by the ongoing conflict, focusing on widows’ camps, EQs reception centers, and frontline areas in NWS. Leveraging its ongoing GBV initiatives funded by SCHF, IYD will sustain GBV services across six WGSSs in five sub-districts within Idleb and Aleppo.
Expanding its protective embrace, IYD will enhance access to protection, legal aid, and child protection services by maintaining operations in two Reception Centers in Idleb and inaugurating two additional Community Centers in Idleb and Aleppo. Additionally, community-based activities will receive a boost through 11 mobile teams across eight sub-districts, including frontline areas in Idlib and Aleppo. IYD’s spectrum of services includes:
Comprehensive case management for GBV survivors.
Focused PSS for GBV survivors.
Distribution of dignity kits.
Recreational activities non-structured.
Child protection (CP) case management.
Structured PSS group-based sessions for children and youth. Parenting programmes sessions.
An array of legal services encompassing counseling, informative sessions, and HLP safeguarding initiatives.
Community-driven prevention sessions on protective measures and risks related to child protection and GBV, supplemented by life skills training and Young Mother Club sessions.
Explosive ordinance risk education ( MA).
Structured psychosocial support (PSS) for children alongside parenting skills workshops for adults.
Grassroots initiatives featuring committees for women and children, fostering community-based protective mechanisms.
Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) for GBV survivors, CP cases, and legal aid beneficiaries.
Diligent protection monitoring, CP Situation Assessment, and safety audits.
Special attention is considered to ensure protection mainstreaming, gender equality, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Accountability to the Affected Population, and the inclusion of People with Disabilities (PwDs) from the designing to the closing process.
Expert staff will be hired in a timely manner to efficiently and effectively satisfy the emergency response within the determined project timeline. Continuous technical and admin supervision by the HQ project team will be ensured, and the relevant guidelines, SOPs, and toolkits of the Protection Cluster/GBV & CP sub-clusters will be adhered to. 
The project is planned for 12 months, and it targets 52,571 (M:12,814, W:18,230, B:8947, G:12,580).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis:Risk1: Individuals, especially women may find challenges in accessing GBV&CP activities due to social stigma.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation: staff will be well-trained to be sensitive to BNF suffering, treat them with respect/dignity, and avoid creating more harm. Project staff will avoid creating false hopes and unrealistic expectations about what protection and assistance the projects offer.Risk 2: Families prevent their children from accessing these activities.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation: Project staff will target families with trust-building initiatives and avoid culturally sensitive topicsRisk 3: BNFs want support in front of judicial mechanisms.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: The team explains that legal services provide information and assistance without dealing with judicial mechanisms and only refer to lawyers.IYD's PSEA Strategy:1. Prevention:1.1. Training: IYD holds mandatory induction and refresher trainings for all employees and related personnel on the IYD’s PSEA policy and procedures.1.2 Awareness raising of beneficiaries to ensure that they are aware of the IYD’s zero-tolerance policy on SEA and how to report allegations.2. Response:2.1. Reporting: IYD has safe, confidential, and accessible mechanisms and procedures for personnel, beneficiaries, and communities, including children, to report SEA allegations.2.2. Investigation: IYD has a process for investigation of SEA allegations in place and shall properly and without delay conduct an investigation of SEA by IYD's employees or related personnel. Also, IYD has measures to protect those reporting violations(whistleblower policy, anonymous reporting).2.3. Victim assistance: IYD has a system to promptly refer SEA survivors to available services based on their needs and consent ( GBV services inside IYD's WGSSs, for example). Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating Measures:Assumption: Community-led committees are very interested in project activities. Risk: Community key members/leaders want to receive incentives for their initiatives and efforts.Mitigation: In the first meeting with the committees project staff explain that the initiative support will only cover the in-kind materials and supplies for implementing the initiatives, and no incentives will be provided at any level because it`s a voluntary effort by community members themselves. IYD could refer them to any livelihood or CFW interventions in the same area.Assumption: The target population accepts and engages in provided legal services.• Risks related to the location of service provision (safety of movements for beneficiary, contamination from remnants of war, proximity with sensitive buildings). Mitigation measures: choice of locations done in close consultation and agreement with the community, conduction of safety audits.
