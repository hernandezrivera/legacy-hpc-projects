Project Summary

Despite the recent gains made through the collective humanitarian and Government efforts, malnutrition remains a challenge for the Children and Women of Somalia. The projections of recent FSNAU assessments reveal that there is a dire need to gear the humanitarian efforts towards addressing the risk of malnutrition and similarly the projections done earlier in 2020 showed that malnutrition levels were above emergency threshold level in most of the parts of the country and correspondingly the national median GAM prevalence in children was reported to be around 13.8% and this translates to 0.96 million boys and girls. Out of this 162,000 are affected by life threatening severely acute malnutrition while around 800,000 are moderately acute malnourished and at the same time the programmatic data shows that of the total admissions, 54% are girls. In addition to children under five years of age, a further 571,159 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance.
In 2020 Somalia have major shocks that great affected service delivery and livelihoods these included infestation of desert locust, flash floods and Corona Virus Pandemic. According to FSNAU 2020 post Gu report COVID 19 pandemic had an adverse effect on the service delivery whereby access of essential health and nutrition were compromised. However, with the adoption of the revised guidelines, the critical lifesaving nutrition services were resumed on time. The impacts of this triple threat not only led to aggravation of the shocks but also rendered the vulnerable groups food insecure, decreasing purchasing power and morbidity due to water borne diseases and low immunization services. Similarly, as a result of floods there were reported displacements that further exposed the population to poor livelihood conditions. The main contributory factors, therefore, include the high number of children effected by acute watery diarrhea, malaria and the concurrent reported low coverage of measles vaccination and vitamin A supplementation
In light of the persistent high rates of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) coupled with the recent floods and desert locust upsurge there is need to continue delivery of lifesaving nutrition interventions targeting vulnerable populations in IDP camps and hard to reach communities. Mogadishu remains a priority since it is hosting larger number of IDPs as well as, Galgaduud and South Mudug regions where many pastoral communities lost their livelihoods and are in vulnerable state.
The project targets a total of 30,771 children 6-59 months with lifesaving nutrition interventions; 8161 severely malnourished children with appropriate treatment facilities in 6 Mobile, 6 Static OTP facilities with those manifesting complicated SAM admitted for treatment in the nearest stabilization centers and 25,215 pregnant and lactating women.
In districts where CISP is the primary or the secondary IP, the intervention foreseen will be implemented using mobile OTPs facilities linked to existing static health facilities for better coverage. CISP is working with Education and Protection programmes in Lower Shabelle in the proposed areas.CISP will coordinate with the nutrition cluster to ensure that there is no duplication while ensuring maximum impact of interventions.

Mitigation Measures

To mitigate this CISP will prevent exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential, both in terms of its economy and its level of governance. Social Inclusion will be promoted through: •When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, gender and disability • Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC). • Training Health staff on do no harmThe proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely precarious conditions with only limited community support. This project will support the MOH to identify the staff with optimum qualifications and experiences taking into account the different capacities and needs of men and women. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed. CISP will prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing any unintended negative effects of its intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and Psycho-social risks. CISP will ensure people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.
