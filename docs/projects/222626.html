Project Summary

The project aims to improve food security and enhance the food production of vulnerable populations in the targeted counties. This will be achieved by providing Cash-Based Food Assistance to address the food needs of vulnerable communities during the lean season and providing timely and appropriate complementary vegetable, crop and fishing kits coupled with training on improved climate-resilient agronomic and fishing practices. In line with the South Sudan FSL cluster HRP objectives and priorities, NPA will support vulnerable populations in IPC 4 and 5 locations. Specifically, NPA will target Canal/Pigi and Fangak, Counties of Jonglei State; Panyikang and Malakal Counties of Upper Nile State and Kapoeta North county of Eastern Equatoria State.
NPA seeks to support 46,896 HHs (281,381individuals) by providing timely and appropriate complementary vegetable, crop and fishing kits. Some kits will be provided by FAO (core pipeline) and NPA partnership projects, while others will be procured and distributed directly by NPA to enhance food production. NPA will build the capacity of the HHs supported with the kits through training on group cohesion and group dynamics, improved climate-resilient agronomic and fishing practices, post-harvest processing, value addition and marketing. A Training of Trainers (ToT) approach will be utilized whereby lead farmers and Lead fisher folk will be trained by NPA. Through farmer field schools, they will share their knowledge and skills with other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The kits provided through this project and the knowledge shared through the training are expected to improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target households, thereby contributing to food security and enhancing climate-resilient sustainable food production.
Cash-based food assistance will target 1,500 HHs (9,000 individuals) in Kapoeta North County in Upper Nile State and 400 HHs (2,400 individuals) in Fangak and Canal / Pigi Counties in Jonglei State and 200 HHs (1,200 individuals) in Panyikang and Malakal counties in Upper Nile State to improve access and consumption of diversified food. The planned interventions are aligned with the FSL cluster objectives 1 and 2. They will provide emergency assistance and livelihood support to save lives and avert the total collapse of livelihoods in the target counties. NPA will integrate humanitarian, development-oriented and conflict-sensitivity activities in this project to enhance the resilience of vulnerable populations in line with the Triple Nexus. Therefore, 2,125 vulnerable households will be supported with small-scale cash grants to establish Income Generating Activities. The target HHs will be sensitized to set VSLAs for easy loan access and encourage saving mentality among the VSLA group members. The IGAs will contribute to increasing and diversifying income and livelihood opportunities while building the resilience of the target households and enhancing financial empowerment.
NPA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach, which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMCs) to lead the targeting of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria and targeting guidelines. Vulnerable households facing severe food insecurity, including female-headed households, child-headed households. people living with disabilities, chronically ill-headed households and the elderly, will be targeted in this project. NPA will ensure meaningful engagement of the target communities throughout the project life-cycle through holding regular meetings with the PMCs and community members and conducting Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project. NPA will ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the implementation of this project and the design of future projects.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks such as drought, flooding, conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the continuous conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation and putting additional pressure on an already fragile situation in South Sudan. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity and classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal county (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. However, in the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, the situation is expected to improve slightly as an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above.In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate further with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties of Jonglei State and Panyikang and Malakal counties of Upper Nile State, experience perennial flooding fuelled by climate change and have intensified vulnerability and increased the risk of food insecurity, malnutrition, and SGBV against vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls. Due to drought, flash flooding, conflict and insecurity, communities in Kapoeta North county have been experiencing acute food insecurity and malnutrition. NPA monitoring reports reveals that poverty induced by recurrent widespread loss of productive assets caused by many years of conflict and flooding continues to be one of the major underlying causes of vulnerability to food insecurity for women, men, boys and girls. Furthermore, revenge killings, the proliferation of small arms and light weapons, and the lack of infrastructure continue to affect Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Panyikang, Malakal, Kapoeta North county. Flood spot check assessment report conducted in Fangak county in August 2024 revealed that houses have been submerged in water forcing residents to abandon their homes and move to higher grounds with limited/no fertile lands for cultivation and visibly high state of food insecurity as people are surviving on wild food and leaves. Many families have been internally displaced from the arable lands due to conflicts, drought and floods, resulting in widespread food insecurity and loss of livelihoods. Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Kapoeta North and Panyikang counties are classified under the emergency food insecurity class (IPC level 4) while Malakal county of Upper Nile State have been classified under catastrophe (IPC level 5). The increased food insecurity and loss of livelihoods have affected household income levels and intensified domestic tension, which in most cases is mitigated by resorting to harmful coping mechanisms to ensure the family's survival. These include reducing food portions and the number of meals per day, limiting food to older people to feed the children, and resorting to less preferred wild foods/leaves. Child labour, early/forced marriage, commercial sex work and unplanned selling of productive assets are also reported in the mentioned counties.

2. Project activities

Activity 1- Project launch and community mobilization: NPA will meet with project staff, community representatives and other key stakeholders to discuss the project objectives, implementation strategies approach and plan, distribution plans, etc. Activity 2- Formation and training of Project Management Committees (PMCs): NPA will facilitate the formation of Boma- level PMCs. Each PMC will comprise 10 (5 women, 5 men) members who will be trained and empowered to be a voice to and for the targeted beneficiaries and participate in beneficiary selection and monitoring of the project implementation. Activity 3- Beneficiary selection and sensitization on the project activities: Under the guidance of NPA based on selection criteria and targeting guidelines, the PMCs will identify and register the beneficiaries targeting food-insecure households with a focus on women/child/chronically ill-headed households, People Living with Disabilities (PLWD), widows, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, orphaned etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards the vulnerable. The selected beneficiaries will be sensitized on the project implementation modalities, entitlements, duration of assistance, etc. At least 60% of the target HHs will be female-headed. Activity 4- Provision of Cash-Based Food Assistance to 1,500 severely or highly vulnerable households to meet their immediate food needs during the lean season: The cash-based food assistance values will be based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) as established by the South Sudan JMMI at the time of the transfer coordinated by REACH South Sudan. Cash transfer will be done by a Financial Service Provider (FSP) who will disburse cash according to the schedule provided by NPA and report to NPA upon completion of the activities. Activity 5- Distribution of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits: NPA will distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum, Groundnuts and Cowpea), vegetable kits and tools (Amaranth, Collard, Jew’s Mallow (kudura), Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, mono-filament and spools of twine) to the 111,148 targeted households. FAO (core pipeline) will provide some of the kits, while the rest will be procured and distributed directly by NPA to enhance food production. Activity 6- Formation and training of farmers and fisherfolk groups: NPA will encourage and facilitate agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided to 3,700 model farmers and fisherfolk. The trained farmers and fisherfolk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisherfolk in their communities. Activity 7- Provision of cash grants to 150 Income Generating Activity (IGA): The groups will be trained on business plan development, business management, budgeting, loan management and supported with cash grants for investment in business enterprises. The amount of cash grants to be disbursed will be informed by the nature and type of businesses based on their business plans. Activity 8- Monitoring and reporting: NPA will conduct routine monitoring activities to gather project progress data to inform the project management team and subsequent programming and submit the required reports. NPA will also conduct PDM after every distribution of cash, vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits to ascertain the appropriateness of the response. Outcome monitoring will be done periodically to assess the changes in the target communities due to the project. Feedback from the beneficiaries, lessons learnt and best practices will be used to inform subsequent project activities and design future projects. Activity 9- Markets, traders and price monitoring: This will be conducted at the end of each month following each cash distribution cycle to establish changes in market accessibility, availability of commodities, traders restocking capacities, and trends in market
