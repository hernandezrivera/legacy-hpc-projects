Project Summary

CDD and CBM have taken different service strategies to extend the services for the Rohingya Refugees in the Camps and the Host Community outside the camps. In the Rohingya Refugee Camp, CDD-CBM are providing services through ‘One Stop Integrated Health & Rehab Service Centre for all’ and ‘Home Based Rehabilitation Teams’. In the host community we are extending services through ‘Host Community Mobile Camps’

Objectives

Rohingyas and host communities in Ukhiya with and without disabilities have improved health through accessible, comprehensive health care and rehabilitation services and children with and without disabilities in Balukhali camp have accessed safe learning and recreational activities closer to their dwelling

Needs

A rapid assessment was conducted by CDD and ASB on situation of people with disability in the refugee camp. It identified that persons with disabilities are mostly not accessing or not reached by service providers. Persons with disabilities and older persons face discrimination and barriers in the camps, including from service providers. Most older people and people with disabilities were not aware of specialised health services, including opportunities for accessing assistive devices to meet their needs. The persons with disabilities and older people hardly had access to services in Myanmar.CBM and CDD are working together to address along with the implementation of the activities in the camp, namely: ● General limited awareness on the importance of, how to collect disability data and identify persons with disabilities and older persons within the response;● Actors are waiting for more information and technical support on collecting disability data through The Washington Group Short Set of Questions on Disability ;● Few services are accessible to persons with functioning limitation, such as toilets, water sources, access around camps and distance to health and distribution points; this directly impacts person with disabilities and older persons ability to meet their needs;● People with disabilities are not aware of specialized health services, including opportunities for accessing assistive devices to meet their needs;● Persons with disabilities face discrimination and barriers in the camps, including from service providers; The latest data from multi-sectoral needs assessment don in July 2018 by UNHCR with REACH show that 44% of households have at least one person with disability or chronic illness

Activities or outputs

Rohingyas and host communities have received basic health care, rehabilitation services and mental health support (PSS) through on-site, mobile and home base services or appropriate refer system On-site: the health and rehabilitation center in camp 18 (established last December 2017) is providing a comprensive spectrum of services, using a olistic approach to be able to create a "One Stop Integrated Health & Rehab Service Centre". In the centre CDD team provides basic medical services, pysiotherapy, occupational teraphy, ear and hear assessment and PSS cunselling. In addition every quoter, there will be a fitting area where a team from CDD HQ will take the measurement of those who need artificial limbs.Mobile clinic: same services are provided in the host communities true a mobile unit that move in different location in consultation with the local authorities, that suggest where ther is more needs.Home Based Services: the home based teams (5 in total working in camp 12, 18, 19, 20 and Ukhia Raja Palong) are composed by pysiotherapists, occupational therapists and teraphy assistants. The PT and OT provide the first assessment and define the treatment needed and then the therapy assistants will follow up regularly the different patients identified. Those teams are targeting those who are not able to reach the helath and rehabilitation center and the mobile clinic. The PT and OT also teach to parents or caregivers excercise to be done in a daily base to improve the condition of the beneficiaries. Assistive Devices will be provided ah hoc bases and depends on the needs of the beneficiaries. A strong referral system is esthablish to esnure the beneficiaries in need of more specialized services are supported and accompanied to other centers.
