Project Summary

Pibor, Raga, Tonj North and Rumbek East Counties are among the hard to reach counties in South Sudan as stipulated by the UNOCHA and FSL Cluster with demanding FSL needs particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. The effect of years of conflict, displacement, flooding, disease outbreaks, weak health systems, effect of COVID-19 pandemic and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis published in December 2020. This includes 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers who need FSL support in 2021. This project aims at enabling a total of 12,000 HHs (60,000 individual) the most vulnerable families amongst IDPs, returnees and host communities in Pibor,Raga,Tonj North and Rumbek East Counties in South Sudan. The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is likely to continue to worsen in 2021, driven by cumulative effects of years of recurring conflict and violence, further aggravated by subsequent shocks. Continued macroeconomic crisis, the low preparedness capacity of national and state authorities, COVID-19 pandemic, depleted asset, relatively lower oil prices, and consecutive years of flooding are expected to further exacerbate existing vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of people, while pushing more people downward spiral into crisis and reliance on humanitarian assistance. Humanitarian food assistance must be scaled up immediately to save lives and prevent total collapse of livelihoods in locations where populations were classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4). The Food security and Livelihood condition is also demanding in these Counties with critical needs in access to food security to the IDPs, returnees and the host community. PCO intends to scale up food security and livelihood services to these vulnerable through an integrated approach in Nutrition, Health and GBV sectors. The enhancing food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support will be addressed through distribution of the inputs and fishing kits, and crop seeds, agricultural tools and vegetable Kits, training of farmers on integrated pest management, post- harvest and good storage, training of fishermen on fish handling procedure, processing, preservation methods and marketing, training of Community Animal Health Workers on livestock vaccination and treatment, Training farmers on reduction on post-harvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies. Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks will be done through formation and training of community village savings and loaning schemes and micro finance. Training & capacity building both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production through use of farmer/agro-pastoral field schools and demonstration sites, income generation on business skills and marketing, vocational training,- Support value chain addition through milk & fish preservation & marketing, Conduct agronomic training and post-harvest management, Mainstream and ensure Accountability to Affected Populations by instituting at least four beneficiary feedback centres in targeted Counties, Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring.
Peace Corps South Sudan intends to contribute to reduction of prevalence of Food Insecurity by the end of 2021, through localization agenda and work with other organization to achieve this. The overall purposes of the response will be life-saving and contributes to reduction of acute food insecurity related morbidity and mortality within the stated 4 Counties of South Sudan. The project is to ensure recruited staff are well trained on the SEA policy and COVID-19 prevention measures.

1 - Needs

The high levels of acute food insecurity continue to be driven by the cumulative effects of national and sub-national conflict including gender based violence, Economic crisis/rise in commodity prices, heavy reliance on unpredictable and poor rainfall performances, associated population displacements, Flooding and prolonged years of asset depletion. These factors have contributed to insufficient crop production, disruption of livelihoods and impacted households’ access to other food sources, such as wild foods, fish, and livestock products. The on-going economic crisis has significantly reduced households’ purchasing power, prolonged dry spells at critical stages of crop growth, flooding, crop pests and diseases, Livestock have been looted, the recent influx of spontaneous refugee returnees from the neighboring countries, and the returning of IDPs to their original residential areas are demanding food, humanitarian access challenges, chronic water borne diseases and malnutrition (FSNMS 24 report). The affected populations in the targeted Counties are increasingly adopting negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestock’s. These results to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. Livelihoods have been decimated by the frequent crisis with limited access to land or assets, limited investments in market- based employment opportunities, ever deepening poverty, vulnerability, and continues displacement due to conflict involving a significant degree of revenge preventing the affected IDPs, returnees and communities from cultivating their lands.According to OCHA, a total of 34 counties (out of 79) in the country have been affected. Conflict- and flood-affected areas of Jonglei, Warrap, and Lakes where crop and livestock losses are highest and market access is low, Emergency (IPC Phase 5) or emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes have been noted. Moreover, during the lean season of January-April 2021, Pibor county has been classified as IPC phase 5 food insecure in the latest (IPC AMN) Due to multiple challenges within South Sudan, the situation might remain the same or improve depending on humanitarian response. The Current Proxy GAM rate of Malnutrition in Warrap (Tonj East 15.6%; Tonj North 15.3%); Lakes (Rumbek East 23%; and Pibor 15%) which are all ≥ than 15% W.H.O threshold. Generally, the government is supposed to take responsibility for the basic services within the country; however, with economic instability within the country due to effect of conflict has led to the government struggle with funds to address the food security gaps. This project aims at enabling the community increase food and livestock production to save life and reduce dependency on food and agriculture input to strengthen household ability to absorb shocks to conflict affected population of Pibor, Rumbek East, Raja and Tonj north Counties in South Sudan.

2 - Project activities

The key activities which peace corps Intend to address the current severe food insecurity situation in the targeted areas are:-Rapid need assessment,-Community mobilization and sensitization awareness campaigns in Raga,Pibor, Rumbek East and Tonj north Counties of Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Lake and Warrap States to introduce the projects communities,- Identification, selection, registration and verification of vulnerable beneficiaries in the targeted counties,- Request and prepositioning of agriculture inputs and fishing kits from FAO Core Pipeline, - Transporting of the inputs and fishing kits, and distribution of crop seeds, agricultural tools and vegetable Kits, -distribution fishing kits, -training of farmers on integrated pest management, post- harvest and good storage,- training of fishermen on fish handling procedure, processing, preservation methods and marketing,- training of Community Animal Health Workers on livestock vaccination and treatment. -Training farmers on reduction on postharvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies (e.g. hermetic storage bags),-Diversification & restoration of livelihoods, -Formation and training of community village savings and loaning schemes and micro finance. -Training & capacity building both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production through use of farmer/agro-pastoral field schools and demonstration plots, income generation on business skills and marketing, -vocational training,- Support value chain addition through milk & fish preservation & marketing in the targeted locations,- Conduct agronomic training and post-harvest management,- Mainstream and ensure Accountability to Affected Populations by instituting at least four beneficiary feedback centres in targeted Counties, Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse issues to all the beneficiaries. Women and girls will be consulted through FGDs on the best site considering safety and accessibility before rehabilitating water sources and latrines so as to reduce their exposure to GBV and other forms of abuse when visiting these sites. In addition, the rehabilitated latrines will be gender disaggregated to avoid men and women meeting while protecting their privacy and exposing them to sexual exploitation and abuse. The agricultural Extension Workers and Community Animal health Workers will be trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse avoid exposing the beneficiaries to a lot of harm. PCO will train FSL staffs on cross-cutting issues such as GBV and protection to provided sensitization to the beneficiaries on GBV and other related abuses and ways to minimize their occurrence so as not to cause more harm to the beneficiaries. Distribution and training of adolescent girls on usage of menstrual hygiene kits will also be done by a PCO female staff so that the girls feel free and hence also protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life cycle of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee comprises of diverse representatives such as beneficiaries representative, Community agricultural Extension worker, Community Animal Health Workers, Local authority representatives, Community Elders with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and work plans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project will include community engagement in monitoring and evaluation throughout the response cycle. The community through their representatives and the local leaders will also be invited to conduct independent monitoring and evaluation on regular basis. The findings will be shared to the stakeholders upon their request for verification and the details of the community members and representatives that participated in the monitoring and evaluation exercise will be noted which can be used for reference and authenticating the findings.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

PCO sets up a feedback and complains mechanism at the project location among the beneficiaries, at Juba level, a committee composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedback directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiaries advise and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. PCO is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. PCO is a very active national NGO on GBV in the three States and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. PCO, together with other agencies will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools PCO will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. PCO staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. PCO is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

PCO will emphasize on skills training to beneficiaries and the need for durable solutions within the communities we serve. The beneficiaries will be taught basic life skills/IGAs so that they have skills and knowledge to general some income for themselves including farming. PCO will establish local implementation committees, and equip them with leadership skills which will also act as local development catalysts which the project comes to an end as part of resilience building. PCO will ensure that local communities change their attitudes through community awareness campaigns such that the narrative is changed from 'we cannot do it on our own' to 'we have the capacity to do it'.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

All projects activities will be implemented with a protection lens, ensuring that safety of women and girls, boys and men and taken into consideration through safety audits without compromising on quality and time. PCO will through a multi-sectoral approach and joint missions with other agencies and clusters to negate negative livelihood coping strategies, which expose beneficiaries to additional risks, and providing them with safer ways to meet their basic needs. As a usual practice, PCO, as a protection partners, will work in conjunction with protection teams on the ground, ensuring meaningful information on protection risks and concerns in a specific area are properly identified and conflict sensitivities are taken into consideration through a do-no-harm approach.
