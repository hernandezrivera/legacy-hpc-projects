Project Summary

Health Management BD Foundation (HMBD) will deliver comprehensive health care services over a 12-month period in our existing health posts in Camps (Camp 2w, Camp 13) of Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar to ensure that Rohingya refugees have access to essential primary healthcare services through OPD, Maternal health, Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT), Family Planning (FP), Palliative and NCD Care, Oral Hygiene and Dental Care Services Emergency and Referral in line with the Ministry of Health and Health Sector recommended minimum package of essential health services. This intervention aims to increase safe, dignified and meaningful access and utilization of quality comprehensive healthcare services. All health facilities are staffed by coordinator, doctors, midwives, clinic aides and community health workers (CHWs). We are providing above healthcare services to the FDMN regularly and few (NCD, Palliative care, Oral hygiene & Dental Care Services) in small scale. So, we decided to provide all services in full-fledged form from January, 2022 which depends on funds availability. Furthermore, HMBD has decided to introduce EMR (Electronic Medical Record) to keep medical records of the patients.
The project contributes to the improvement of the public health situation in the Rohingya Refugee camps by addressing gaps in access to quality service regarding Palliative and NCD Care. According to WHO, Palliative care is an approach that improves the quality of life of patients and their families who are facing problems associated with life-threatening illness. Palliative care is a crucial part of integrated, people-centered health services. Relieving serious health-related suffering, be it physical, psychological, social, or spiritual, is a global ethical responsibility. Each year, an estimated 40 million people are in need of palliative care; 78% of them people live in low- and middle-income countries. On the other hand, NCDs, such as cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes and chronic respiratory disease, are a major source of preventable illness, disability and mortality worldwide. Moreover, providing Palliative care services will focus on relief from the symptoms and stress of a serious illness.
Oral diseases pose a major health burden for many countries and affect people throughout their lifetime, causing pain, discomfort, disfigurement and even death. These diseases share common risk factors with other major non-communicable diseases. Health Management BD Foundation (HMBD) is working for the Rohingya Refugees since September, 2017 on health, education, protection etc. We organized 6 dental camps in 2021 at our camp-13 health post. We treated around 400 dental patients. Later, we found lot of cases of all ages facing severe dental problems which need further treatment. There are limited dental facilities in the Rohingya Refugee Camps. So, we decided, HMBD will deliver Oral Hygiene and Dental Care Services over a 12-month period in Camp-13 and Camp-2w of Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar from January 2022. Besides, this project will operate School Health Program for host communities. Major goals are to provide periodic health checkup, oral hygiene and dental care services for school going students, distribute menstrual hygiene kits and conduct awareness programs among adolescent school going girls. We will also provide training to Govt. Health Facilitators and awareness programs among the influential community people on Palliative and NCD Care. We have a MMT team to work during any disaster which is engaged with MMT working group, named HMBD-1 as surge team. The team trained on MMT and having regular contact with WHO-MMTWG and IOM.

1 - Identified Needs

The key health needs for the Rohingyas are mostly prevention and treatment of communicable, non-communicable diseases, antenatal and post natal care services, emergency obstetric care services, reproductive and child health. However, these population faces increased susceptibility from underlying conditions, limited access to comprehensive health services, poor health seeking behaviors & unhygienic living conditions. On top of that, the arrival of a pandemic to the camp setting created additional challenges to service provision, past one year everything got slowed. According to J-MSNA 2021, household satisfaction with assistance since the COVID-19 outbreak decreased most for assistance on education, shelter, food, site management, health care and nutrition. Overall health outcomes have been negatively affected by the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak on health seeking behavior, with the proportion of individuals reported sick enough to require medical treatment having dropped from 35% in 2019 to 9% in 2020. Crucial services such as sexual, reproductive and maternal & child health services have been reduced. On the other hand, global need for palliative care will continue to grow as a result of the ageing of populations and the rising burden of NCDs and some communicable diseases. Rohingya refugees are unknown to NCDs and likely unaware about the treatment. On top of that, taking care of palliative patients is beyond imagination. Palliative care is required for a wide range of diseases. The majority of adults in need of palliative care have chronic diseases such as cardiovascular diseases, cancer, stroke, chronic respiratory diseases, paralyzed and complicated diabetes. Pain is the most frequent and serious symptom prevalent in palliative care patients. Besides, while working in Rohingya Refugee camp for past four years, HMBD found out the number of Palliative care service in Rohingya camp is very rare. Furthermore, oral health is the general condition of the mouth, mouth cavity, teeth and associated structures. In perspective of Rohingya Refugee Camp, dental facilities are very limited. Among 34 camps only 3 to 5 dental units are available and support the whole camp. This is not good enough for over one million Refugees. Moreover, the Rohingyas have limited idea about basic dental hygiene, basic awareness, maintenance of oral hygiene and other oral diseases. That’s why HMBD Foundation tries to implement dental services. Moreover, we will introduce the EMR (Electronic Medical Record) system in this project. By this, we can easily track the patient’s medical history, prescription, test results and medications. If any emergency situation creates for a patient, it can alert the physician at right time. For any kind of disaster our MMT is ready for response. After fire incident at camp 9, 8E and 8W, HMBD deployed 2 teams for camp 9 and 8E and provided service for fire affected victims. Later on team engaged with MMTWG name HMBD-1 as surge team. The team trained on MMT and having regular contact with WHO-MMTWG and IOM.
