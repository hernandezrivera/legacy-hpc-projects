Project Summary

The overall objective of the proposal is to increase school enrolment, attendance and retention among out of school children and children at high risk of drop out by removing monetary barriers to school enrolment and increasing household spending on children needs.
The project will support 15,000 extremely vulnerable children (as defined through specific and coordinated vulnerability assessments at household level), approximately an equal number of boys and girls. It is anticipated that 53 per cent of project beneficiaries will be Internally Displaced People (IDPs), 43 per cent will be returnees and 3% will come from the host community. Indirect beneficiaries include the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MoLSA) in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq (KRI) and its Directorates who will benefit from the cash transfer, which will include elements of institutional and technical capacity development.
The project intends to build on and enhance existing cross-referral mechanisms with Government-led social and child protection systems for affected children or caregivers who may require case management support. This element of the project will be undertaken in collaboration with UNICEF Child Protection programme.
Expected results include: Increasing school enrolment and retention of out of school children and children at risk of drop-out; decreases in negative coping strategies such as child labour and taking children out of school; Government systems strengthening by establishing cross referrals and social care service provision.

Needs

As per recent estimates 22.5% of the population Iraq are poor, with 6.5 million Iraqis in need, including 3.3 million children (under age 18) and 3.2 million women continue to need some form of humanitarian assistance and protection. More than 2 million people remained internally displaced, with the three governorates of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq (KRI) hosting half of the currently-displaced population. In addition, more than 240,000 Syrian refugees have sought shelter in the KRI after fleeing conflict in their home country; many have been living as refugees for more than six years. Returnees and internally displaced populations (IDP) are very vulnerable facing daily challenges in accessing services and maintaining livelihoods. Although oil prices have increased since 2016 and the conflict against the armed group Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) is almost over, the country’s situation is still marked by high multidimensional vulnerabilities. With the retake of Mosul in July 2017 many IDPs returned to their houses, and high return rates have continued through into 2018. The poverty level amongst the displaced population is considered to be relatively higher than those who are not displaced, and humanitarian organisations estimated that around 50 per cent of the displaced are children under the age of 18. The poverty among IDPs had increased by 15 points (from 23 per cent to 38 per cent) (Government of Iraq, National Poverty Reduction Strategy) which means four in every ten IDPs are poor. The poverty rate also increased in the conflict affected governorates, reaching 41 per cent of the population. Children are disproportionately affected by conflict and economic downturn, which exacerbated deprivations in education, health, nutrition, and child protection indicators. A recent survey by the Ministry of Planning (excluding KRG) reveals that multidimension poverty in Iraq is 14 per cent overall, but up to 29 per cent in conflict-affected governorates. Two main factors drive deprivations of IDP children: inadequacy, or lack of, of education and child protection services; and economic and social barriers hampering children accessing these services. Failing to address drivers of child deprivation will result in partial or lack of fulfilment of children’s rights and in the persistence of deprivation among the most vulnerable. To address this, UNICEF is partnering with government institutions and Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) to implement an Emergency Cash for Education Transfer programme for the 2018-2019 school year focusing on children affected by humanitarian situations, to support their educational needs. Findings from a 2018 UNICEF Post Distribution Monitoring analysis conducted for 30 per cent of the reached population of UNICEF’s 2017-2018 cash for education project in the governorates of Anbar, Dahuk and Erbil revealed that impact of cash intervention for children at risk of dropout was very close for boys and girls with the exception of Dohuk. For children who were out of school, a 38 per cent increase in enrolment for boys was noted, compared to only 26 per cent enrolment for girls. In Fallujah, enrolment figures increased for both boys and girls, but were slightly higher for boys (67 per cent) as compared to girls (60 per cent). For Erbil, the enrolment rate for out of school children was 54.3 per cent for boys and 51.7 per cent for girls. This shows that impact of the cash intervention on the education outcomes for both girls and boys were very close with slight improvements for boys. Based on these findings the project will target equal numbers of boys and girls for the school year of 2019/2020. The project will also support case identification and referrals for the reached children and caregivers who are identified as requiring additional support. Female headed households, households with people with disabilities and chronic illness, out of school children, child labour are considered for case identification, assessment and possible referrals in collaboration with UNICEF child protection section.

Activities

a. Household assessment for about 20,000 households with education-focused questionnaire to collect socioeconomic data of returnee and IDP households living in Anbar, Dahuk, and Ninewa governorates. The data will be collected through ‘KOBO’ software using tablets that register GPS coordinates of interviewed households . b. Registration of selected beneficiary households with the Finance Service Provider (FSP) to enable households to receive UNICEF support using a ‘mobile money’ transfer modality. c. Transferring cash for education support to selected households through the FSP via a network of cash agents (points of sale) with sufficient liquidity. d. Establishment of Complaint and Redress Mechanism to solve operational issues and improve social accountability increasing project participants’ trust and involvement. e. Identifying households in need of case management support (issues may include out of school children [OOSC], female-headed households, children with disability, households with members suffering from chronic illness, among others). Case management support will be facilitated to address each case’s specific needs, and will likely include referral to specialized service providers (will be undertaken by government social workers in collaboration with the UNICEF Child Protection Programme). f. Implementing Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) after each cash transfer for 30 per cent of the targeted caseload through random sampling. The PDM will support provision of an accurate picture of cash utilization for children with specific focus on spending on education needs and impact of cash on school enrolment and retentions Indicators # of boys and girls who are at risk of dropout and retained in school # of out of school boys and girls who are out of school and enrolled to school # of individuals identified for case management support # of individuals referred to services

Protection Risk Analysis

Some of the children may have protection concerns that will affect their enrollment and retention in schools. Also, some adults who have protection issues may directly or indirectly affect education outcomes for children.

Mitigation Measures

Risk mitigation measures include; • Identification of children and adults who may require case management and cross referral services due to their vulnerabilities during beneficiary assessment phase, where additional vulnerability criteria such as out of school children, child labour, female headed household, people with disabilities and chronic illness will be applied. Although the scope of the cash assistance stops at the identification stage, every effort towards promoting referral pathways will be made• Establishment of complaint mechanism, continues awareness raising endeavors to educate beneficiaries about project objectives, continuous monitoring and coordination to mitigate and prevent risks.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The project will establish direct interaction with beneficiaries through social workers of the partner organizations by raising their understanding of the project objectives, and providing additional specialised assistance in addition to cash transfers such as psychosocial support and referrals. The intervention will improve social accountability by establishing redress mechanisms which work in parallel levels; the partner organizations; MOLSA/Erbil, DOLSA/Dohuk and Mercy Hands will have dedicated staff and hotlines to receive feedback and complains; the ‘mobile money’ transfer service provider will also have a hotline system to receive complaints that relate to payments; and enumerators and social workers will have direct interaction with beneficiaries throughout the project cycle to raise awareness and receive/address complaints and seek their feedback on the impact of cash on children after each round of cash delivery.
