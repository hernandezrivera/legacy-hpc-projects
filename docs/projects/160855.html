Project Summary

This project aims at improving learning performance and health of schoolchildren, through improved sanitation facilities and hygiene education. WASH in Schools is a pathway to healthier schools and better performing children. The project will complement FCA’s ongoing education support, and help children fulfil their right to health, education and participation. One of the major challenge in Mingkaman IDP schools is lack of latrines. Some schools have toilets, but in disrepair and without hand-washing facilities or soap, others have none. Poor sanitation and inappropriate hygiene behavior is unfavorable to health of children who spend long hours in school. The physical environment and cleanliness of a school can significantly affect the health and well-being of children . FCA is currently establishing learning spaces & rehabilitating classrooms, training teachers, paying incentives and building capacity of PTAs and county education staff. The proposed WASH component will not only promote hygiene but also increase access to quality education.
Output 1: Increased enrolment and retention in target IDP schools in Mingkaman, Awerial County.
FCA will establish/rehabilitate gender-segregated latrines, hand-washing stands & provide sanitation facilities in targeted schools. Learning environments equipped with latrines & hand-wash facilities allow children to learn in a safe & healthy environment, and have the opportunity to practice appropriate hygiene habits taught in class. In addition, WASH facilities encourage hygienic behavior, such as using a toilet and washing hands. Experience has demonstrated that enrolment and retention rates are high in schools equipped with WASH facilities.
Output 2: Enhanced awareness on WASH practices by integrating life skills education, focusing on key hygiene behaviors.
FCA will focus on life skills-based hygiene education to enable children to learn about water and sanitation related behavior. As a result, change in children’s hygiene behavior will stimulate their positive influence on hygiene practices in homes and the wider community. The project will establish school hygiene clubs to support messaging and awareness on safe use of latrines. This will improve children’s health and resistance to infectious diseases that would keep them from attending school, indirectly contributing to educational achievement by increasing school attendance and retention.

1 - Needs

Since conflict erupted in South Sudan in December 2013, four million people have faced displacement, with 1.47 million remaining displaced within the country and close to 2.2 million people crossed into neighboring countries bringing the total population of refugees from South Sudan to unprecedented levels. The implementation of the revitalized Peace Process has witnessed a relative decline in insecurity, providing an enabling environment for service delivery. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), some 2,784,276 girls and boys between 3 & 18 years of age in conflict-affected areas are estimated to not have access to pre-school, primary and secondary education in 2019. In addition, some 42,902 teaching personnel and members of school management committees are in need of humanitarian assistance. According to results of the last biometric verification conducted by ACTED and WFP in Awerial County, Mingkaman is still hosting 69,567 IDPs, and over 18, 087 (26%) of this population is school-age children. Finn Church Aid has supported education in Awerial County since 2014, and has gained experience, acceptance and recognition in the county. FCA is also implementing education projects in Fangak and Pibor with different donors including UNICEF, ECHO, EU DEVCO and Ministry for Foreign Affairs of Finland (MFA). In both Jonglei and lake state, FCA has built over 45 learning spaces through partnership with UNICEF and ECHO; in addition, FCA constructed five (5) blocks of permanent classrooms in Pibor and Gumuruk with funding from Finnish MFA. FCA is currently supporting 10 schools in Mingkaman through PCA with UNICEF, the project involves establishment of learning spaces, rehabilitation of 10 classrooms, distribution of 200 desks, payment of teacher incentives, admin/logistical support to county education department and teacher training components. According to FCA’s recent monitoring reports, of the 10 schools supported, only two schools (St. Paul Girls & Mingkaman P/S) has latrines in good condition. Marik, Panhial & Padar primary schools have no latrines, open defecation is visible around the environment of these schools. At Roordit and Mat Academy, latrine blocks for girls collapsed, forcing them to share the existing latrines with boys. There is need to establish latrines & provide hygiene education in the targeted schools to enable conducive learning, thus contributing to increased enrollment & retention FCA has established strong operational capacity and amicable relations with other sector partners, communities & local authorities in locations of implementation. Most importantly, FCA has ongoing & anticipated EiE projects until the end of 2020 in these areas. FCA has previously integrated WASH components in education projects in Mingkaman, Pibor and Fangak County, through establishment of gender-segregated latrines and installation of water harvesting systems aimed at improving the health & learning performance of school-aged children. Experience gathered in recent years has showed that enrolment and retention rate in schools are very difficult to maintain. Usually when children are old enough, parents prefer to engage them in child labor to contribute to family food requirements. In addition, children are involved in pastoral practices including participation in cattle rustling. The potential multiplier effect of this project is that children are healthy and able to learn, and increase in educational achievement, school attendance and retention. Improved hygiene in schools will lead to better health and better resistance to infectious diseases and illnesses that would keep children from attending school. This intervention will complement other trainings covered by EiE activities such as life-saving messages in peace education, child protection, girls’ education, GBV, early marriage, HIV/AIDS in school. FCA will encourage schoolchildren to disseminate similar messages to their households and the wider community.

2 - Activities

1. Establishment of gender segregated latrines, including hand-wash stands: Finn Church Aid will establish 80 dis-aggregated latrine stances (8 stances per school, 4 for girls and 4 for boys in each of the 10 schools targeted), for example in Marik and Pan-Nhial primary school where there is none, and rehabilitate 20 latrine blocks of 40 stances, based on need, for example in Roordit and Mat Academy where latrines have collapsed. Latrine designs will take inclusiveness in consideration, and situated in close proximity to the schools, have sufficient capacity for the number of children, sized appropriately and simple to use. Environmental sustainability will be an integral part of the design, implementation, operation and maintenance of facilities and the accompanying hygiene education component. 2. Provide fully integrated life skills education, focusing on key hygiene behaviors for schoolchildren, using participatory teaching techniques: FCA will build capacity of 10 existing hygiene clubs in the targeted schools, to act as focal points for hygiene related campaigns. To sustain the initiative, FCA will roll out training for 100 teachers, 20 county education staff, 110 PTAs and 50 SMCs to engage actively in planning, implementation, operation and maintenance of WASH facilities in schools. The outcome of training is that schoolchildren, teachers and SGBs have positive influence on hygiene practices in school environments, their homes and the wider community. Involving School Governing Bodies SGBs promotes a sense of ownership, which is a prerequisite for sustainability. In addition, FCA will also encourage school committees to take important roles in monitoring the impact of WASH in their schools and responding appropriately to improve children’s health. 3. Create awareness and sensitize families and the wider community: FCA will conduct six (6) awareness raising and sensitization sessions in the targeted school communities. Community mobilization and sensitization will extend the impact of life skills development beyond schools to the whole community. If parents and the community understand, the importance of appropriate hygienic behavior, long-term effects will result.The project will provide systematic awareness on hygiene-related risks to families and the wider community to ensure children apply their knowledge at home. Experience has shown that children are promoters of their newly acquired hygiene skills and can potentially be effective agents of change within their homes and communities. If messaging and practices are consistent with the cultural environment, children’s advocacy can lead to better hygiene practices in homes and communities .FCA will implement these activities in close coordination with WASH actors in the area, such as HELP, RUWASA and the relevant County Department. FCA is an active member of the Education Cluster and has over 6 years of experience and expertise in Education in Emergencies work in the region. FCA has established good working relations with local actors through ongoing projects, and we are in good position to leverage this experience for implementation of WASH in schools in Awerial County.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

FCA will build capacity of existing hygiene clubs in targeted schools, to act as focal points for hygiene related campaigns. To sustain the initiative, FCA will train teachers, County education and health staff, Parents Teachers Association members PTAs and school management committees SMCs, and actively engage them in planning, implementation, operation and maintenance of facilities. Involving PTAs, SMCs and relevant county departments promotes a sense of ownership, which is a prerequisite for sustainability. In addition, FCA will also encourage school management committees to take important roles in monitoring the impact of WASH in their schools and responding appropriately to improve children’s health.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Capacity building for school leadership and school improvement is embedded in South Sudan General education strategic plan (2017 - 2022). In this project, FCA will work closely with relevant county departments at the local level to improve children’s education and health by creating an environment conducive for implementing, operating and maintaining WASH in Schools programs.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Protection, environmental sustainability will be an integral part of the design, implementation, operation and maintenance of facilities and the accompanying hygiene education component. FCA will design WASH facilities with caution, aiming at preventing harm and promoting care of the environment. The project will sensitize children on environmental issues in the school setting, and as much as possible, avoid negative environmental effects or degradation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

FCA will promote a sense of ownership by actively engaging PTAs and SMCs , which is a prerequisite for sustainability. In addition, as an exist and handover strategy, FCA will collaborate with relevant County level departments and other partners in Mingkaman to advocate for initiation of a planning process to address issues such as long-term stability. The objectives of this planning will include identifying capacity needs and defining roles for capacity building; additionally mapping out potential local human resources within project stakeholders for operation, maintenance and monitoring of WASH in schools programs.
