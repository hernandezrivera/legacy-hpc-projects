Project Summary

Education is a fundamental right of every person, one that all children including those caught in natural and human made emergencies, must be able to access. Because of this, the project shall improve provision of equitable access to safe and protective education services for emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys), to communities affected by conflict and floods in Upper Nile, Warrap, and Unity States. This will be done through implementation of project objectives such as; (i) Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 866,000 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) by 2023 in the priority counties. (ii) Improve the quality of education services at all levels and protect the wellbeing of all children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of crisis on learning environments in the priority counties. (iii) Strengthen the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities in priority counties. UNKEA will establish 10 learning spaces, rehabilitate 10 classrooms, establish 10 gender segregated latrines, rehabilitate 10 latrines, distribute learner and recreational kits to 14,011 learners, provide training to 400 teachers on pedagogy and MHPSS, 400 PTA/SMC and 54 members of county education department. This project targets learners 14,446 (6,349 females), which 9390 (3756 females) from the host communities, 2889 internally displaced persons (1156 females) and 2167 (867 females) returnees in targeted counties within the mentioned states. 21,299 (8520 females) indirect beneficiaries will benefit from this interventions in the aforementioned states. With a budget of $ 915,272 of which 20% resource contribution will come from the communities benefiting from the response. UNKEA will use integrated approaches in activities implementation, that is, continuous partnership with other sectoral partners to integrate nutrition, GBV, health and protection in schools to be supported. UNKEA will continuously work in collaboration with beneficiaries of all groups, community leaders, county education department, state and national clusters and line ministries for better service delivery and sustainability of this project.

1 - Needs

South Sudan context is very challenging due to climate change which brought about floods, communal conflicts which led to mass displacement, destruction of livelihoods and property of people. These hindered provision of services to affected people and particularly access to education to these locations. Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. Active conflict and regular communal violence with destruction and looting of classrooms and learning materials throughout 2021 led to school closures in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Warrap and Unity. Learners cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. COVID-19 set an unprecedented challenges leading to closure of schools, early and unwanted pregnancies to school girls, and interruption of EiE responses in such conflict prone and flood affected areas. In 2021, over 61.3% of non-functional schools are from rural and hard to reach areas and those affected by disasters, with teachers and learners fleeing these areas. In the context of these challenges, in addition to others, South Sudan’s education system has struggled to deliver accessible and quality education to children and youth and is characterized by poor learning outcomes and high rates of dropout and out of school children and youth (Education Needs Assessment - ENA 2021). Maiwut county has total of 47 schools (46 primary and 1 secondary school). As a result of the 2017 conflict in the area, only 14 schools are currently functional (all primary) with support solely from NGOs. A lot of children have been isolated from education services and thus have to walk long distances in search of these few functional schools (ADRA joint IRNA 2019). The area was seriously hit and mostly affected by the flooding water from overflowing from Rivers Sobat and Gilo. This has been worsened by the heavy rainfall between July and September 2021. But the flood impact remained serious (HNA IRNA 2021). These challenges created serious gaps in education interventions and increased the requirement for substantial interventions to reduce the educational gap that children had to endure. There is a dire need of improving education infrastructure, school WASH facilities, providing essential learning materials, teachers and PTA/SMC. Delayed or lack of payment of teachers’ salaries has led to serious teacher absenteeism, which eventually led to children dropping out of school. In order to mitigate these gaps, the project will improve education service delivery and improve safe learning environments. The response will integrate WASH in school to keep children in classroom and ensuring their safety and well-being, with clean and secure separate toilets for girls and boys, hand-washing services available to learners and teachers. This project will incorporate and complement life-saving components such as psychosocial support, referral mechanisms, gender-sensitive WASH facilities to enhance secure learning spaces. Therefore, the organization will actively work with other inter-agency aiming at providing quality integrated service approaches to quality education, gender and other crosscutting issues such disabilities, child protection, marginalized children, girl-child education and those with ethnic disadvantage. The response will strengthen SMCs, PTAs and community leaders in order to protect and reduce the vulnerability of school children, particularly girls -against gender-based violence, while increasing opportunities for marginalized persons to withstand shocks and stresses through sensitization. Response interventions will demonstrate sensitivity to protection through project designs, inter-sectoral approaches that mitigate protection risks through regular monitoring of education project implementation and assessing predicaments that school children are facing in the schools and school environment, including in the community. Apart from educational gaps, other sectoral needs widened too.

2 - Project activities

The project activities shall include the alignment of sectoral objectives such as:(i) Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 866,000 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys). Under this objective the project shall implement;(a) 12 sensitizations or back to learning campaigns to boost enrollment of children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, (b) establishment of 10 temporary learning spaces, which are inclusive of disability access (c) rehabilitation of 10 existing classrooms, (d) establishment of 10 latrines, with inclusive of disability access (e) rehabilitation of 10 gender segregated latrines, (f) provision of 80 handwashing facilities and COVID 19 items for boys, girls and teachers (ii) Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments. Under this, the specific activities such as; (a) provision of learner and recreational kits to 14011 (5,604 females) to ECD, primary and secondary school learners and teachers in school,(b) train 400 teachers (160 females) on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children, (c) train and update 400 teachers and learners (160 females) on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition, (d) provide dignity kits and hygiene management sessions to 200 girls (e) provision of assistive devices to 69 learners (27 females) (f) establish and inducting school social advocacy clubs at school level for both boys and girls(g) Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to 234 (94 females) youth and adolescents. (iii) Strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities. Specific activities will be;(a) Train 400 (160 females) SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change, (b) Support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases/COVID 19 Pandemic through training school communities and providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials, and,(c) training of 54 ((22 females) county education officials.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All feedback received from participants through different means will be analyzed and use to make informed decision in consultation with the beneficiaries, community leaders and project management team. Beneficiary feedback will be used for improving activities implementation to ensure that the project is in the right direction and in order for beneficiaries to hold organization account. So, this needs to be embedded into organizational processes. UNKEA to better understand all crisis affected people and pay attention to the most marginalized and vulnerable segments of the population are being adequately engaged, such as children, girls, people with disabilities, marginalized children and forcefully recruited in to army. Beneficiaries feedback is useful mechanisms during the implementation to provide feedback and suggestions. UNKEA will use the beneficiaries’ feedback throughout the programming cycle to make adjustments and adaptations to activities and services. It will too use such feedback information from beneficiaries as evidence base in reporting to the donor and stakeholders or local authority.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This project proposes two-way approach; community engagement focusing on consultations, one-on-one discussions and active engagement with community leaders to inform communities about existing risks for the people with disabilities, elderly, illiterate, women and girls, to ensuring all messages are accessible to women and girls and take into account the cultural and religious context and gender norms and roles. The outcomes of these consultations, meetings and discussions with people having disabilities, illiterate, women and girls are used to guide and making informed decisions for this project implementation strategies to ensure that these groups of people are inclusive in response. UNKEA efforts to work with other partners should continue to employ variety of community approaches and trusted communication channels to reach, influence, include, enable and engage communities with accessible, accurate, easily understood and trusted information about all communicable diseases and COVID-19. It will be important to ensure that these channels are reaching people with disabilities, illiterate, elderly women and men, as well as their family members living within school communities to provide feedback on project activities implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project interventions focus on providing safe and protective access to education services to conflict and flood affected communities. So, beneficiaries safeguarding is principally considered in project design, planning and implementation strategies. UNKEA has zero tolerant Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, which every employee must adhere to when working with vulnerable communities and children. This policy is shared to empower local communities, beneficiaries, children, stakeholders and most vulnerable persons. The primary beneficiaries of this project are children who are affected by conflicts and at risk of, or currently suffering in situations of abuse, exploitation, neglect, discrimination or other forms of violence within families or communities, then UNKEA will use internal and external models developed to provide a comprehensive framework for child protection issues at the community level and to establish an evidence base for advocacy efforts at community, state and national levels. UNKEA will use a systems strengthening approach which emphasizes prevention, protection and response, coordination between sectors and integrated responses that can benefit and protect all children in and out of school compound. That is to say, cases of sexual exploitation and abuse will be safely monitored, evaluated and referred to specialized partners to provide the victims with relevant services and support. This project will incorporate the learning and experience from numerous community-based child protection, adapting rich experiences and practices from inside and outside the organization. In order to uphold ‘Do No Harm’ to all persons engaged through the project, subjected to sign a code of conduct to protect children, boys and girls from abuse. This will apply to staff, education personnel, contractors, and school/community structures that shall take part in any of the project activities. UNKEA does referral of children who are victims to child protection specialized agencies operating in the same area of project implementation. This is to enhance safeguarding of children and other vulnerable groups serve by this project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Social protection has always been one of the cores of welfare and development initiative by UNKEA throughout her humanitarian response both in emergencies and development projects. UNKEA swill start activities implementation by engaging communities through inception meetings to share project information for beneficiaries knowledge and awareness. UNKEA provides beneficiaries with essential information at each of stage of project design, planning and development regarding their assistance, which in turn are fundamental to improving the effectiveness of assistance efforts. Her commitment to learning from and including the voices of recipients in an effort to improve the quality of future programming and to engage them in decisions that directly affect their lives. This recognition has resulted in a great deal of rhetoric about effective participation and inclusion of recipients and some concerted efforts to put these commitments into practice. The project will ensure establishing of help desk in each school for the learners and community members raise their concerns and provided appropriate feedback to them. This is to share information with beneficiaries and vice versa. In platforms such meetings with stakeholders and beneficiaries information will be shared to both sides and feedbacks are provided for to benefit beneficiaries. Also information will be shared to beneficiaries particularly children through school social advocacy clubs established at school levels to ensure information sharing and children engagement in project implementation process.
