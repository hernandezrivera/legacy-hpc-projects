Project Summary

Save the Children proposes this livelihood based emergency project in response to the ongoing conflict and its associated impacts in eight counties, which are known for track records of severe food insecurity and malnutrition in three States in South Sudan. The proposed action aims at saving lives, addressing the immediate and medium-term needs and improving access to improved agricultures and livestock services, therefore, improving the food and nutrition security of the conflict affected communities and increasing their resilience to famine and disaster risks and shocks. The project targets primarily conflict affected children and their families in the internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities.
In response to the ongoing food insecurity crisis, Save the Children proposes a livelihood based emergency project in response to the ongoing conflict and its associated impacts in eleven counties, in four States of South Sudan. The proposed action aims at saving lives, addressing the immediate and medium-term needs and improving access to improved agricultures and livestock services, therefore improving the food and nutrition security of the conflict affected communities and increasing their resilience to famine and disaster risks and shocks mainly intercommunal violence and flooding.
The project targets primarily conflict and flood affected children and their families in the internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities. The key interventions proposed through this action are food assistances, cash and voucher based assistances as part of rapid responses; and agricultural and fishing inputs provisions and productions with training packages on improved agronomic practices including improved kitchen gardening; and livestock vaccinations and treatment services against diseases greatest economic importance.
The design of the proposed project is informed by findings of the multi-partner and multi-sectoral assessments, security and risk analysis and recommendations which have been conducted and released in the last six months.
The proposed emergency response is designed in line with Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and to contribute to the HRP 2022 Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO2 and SO3). The Action aims at reaching out to 348,000 people (at least 60% female), which fall under IPC 5, 4 and 3 in four States of South Sudan, more specifically Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil East), Jonglei (Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South), and Eastern Equatoria (Lafon, Budi, Torit, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Magwi) and Warrap (Tonj East).

1 - Needs

Compounded by complex and interlinked causes, the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is exacerbated by armed and inter communal conflict, annual floods over the past four years. Mass displacement, high levels of food insecurity and disease outbreaks including COVID-19 have triggered an escalating humanitarian and protection crisis. The Humanitarian Needs Overview projection shows over 8.9 million people (71.8%) including refugees are expected to be affected by the humanitarian crisis in the lean season of the year (May to July 2022). The FSL sectoral analysis highlights 8.3 million people (24% women; 54% children, 15% with disabilities) are expected to face severe food insecurity in several counties in the lean season of 2022. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighbouring countries. The same report indicates that 1.3 million children (6-59 months) are acutely malnourished; and 676,000 Pregnant or lactating women acutely malnourished. The FSL Cluster in its FSNMS Sector Analysis (February 2022) also indicates the severity levels in 2022 by counties: 31 counties of extreme or severe concern; 36 very high or high concern and 11 counties low concern. The main drivers of the crisis were categorized into two: i) covariate climatic, conflict & economic shocks such as floods and dry spells, high food price rises, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence; ii) household level idiosyncratic stressors such as death of bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services. In December 2021, community level conflict erupted between Dinka Malual from Aweil East and Misseria from Sudan in the adjacent border areas. Incidents of similar cases were reported recently in some of the proposed locations such as Tonj East, Bor South between Murle and Dinka ethnic communities), and competition for grazing and farming lands in Magwi county). The incidents have caused displacement of thousands of people, loss of livelihood assets (crop and livestock), lack of basic services. Analysis of Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives (JMMI, January 2022) and WFP/VAM (November 2021) shows the increased trend of market prices of basic commodities (food and non-food) in target locations. An intersection of the crisis of community conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 pandemic, and flooding have exacerbated the food insecurity, malnutrition and the already extreme levels of GBV and other protection mainly sexual abuses in women and girls in target locations. The different sources highlight that women, girls, boys and people with disabilities are among the most vulnerable due to those shocks. In response to HRP 2022, Save the Children proposes to support 12 counties, specifically Aweil East; Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South; Lafon, Budi, Torit, Magwi, Kapoeta (East, North South); Tonj East. The proposed counties are selected based on FSL Cluster IPC's compatible acute food insecurity analysis for HNO 2022 (Dec 2021 - May 2022) that fall under IPC 5, 4 & 3; SCI’s operational presence and experiences in the areas, good partnership and potential integration with ongoing multi-sectoral programs in the areas. Therefore, it's against this backdrop that Save the Children proposes this Action in response to the humanitarian crisis through implementing life-saving, short term emergency and recovery livelihood based interventions in the target locations. The proposed emergency response is also developed in line with Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO 2 and SO3) of HRP 2022, minimum guidelines, priorities, and considering the minimum standards where relevant. Starting from 01 January 2022, the proposed action will be implemented for 12 months in the target 12 counties by targeting a total of 348,000 food insecure people in IPC 5, 4 and 3 locations.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Some of the project locations have functional markets. Save the Children is already implementing CVA interventions in some of the locations. Cash transfers are relatively cheaper than in kind assistances in terms of staffing required, logistics, and associated costs. Final decision will be made in consultation with target beneficiaries on their preferences on assistance modality. In most cases, target beneficiaries prefer cash instead of in kind because of the flexibility to use cash to spend in expenditures that are highly needed by the households. Cash/voucher can also be delivered in a timely and cost-effective assistance tocrisis-affected populations, while at the same time supporting the localeconomy.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In line with the standard of operations (SOP), Save the Children will assess risk and market feasibility assessment for cash transfer programs in target locations. Using the standard format that is developed by the Cash Working Group, market prices for food and non-food items will be collected and analysed to determine the minimum expenditure basket for foods. Markets with sustainable flow of market commodities and relatively stable prices will be selected for cash transfer. Market fact sheet from JMMI or monthly bulletin from WFP/VAM will also be used during the design of cash transfer projects.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, an “accountability report” will be generated for all complaints received and will seek solutions to those complaints from the relevant staff. The project staff will also be required toprepare a monthly response plan by locations and cases reported, the specific actions taken to address the issues raised from the previous report. The regular analysis and use of the complaints and feedback from target communities will be included into the project for actions that include adapting, redesigning some of the activities in line with and resourcing with budget and staff and monitoring of their applicability in the remaining periods of the project.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Project team will adhere to inclusivity principle throughout the project implementation so as to hear from all gender groups inclusive of partners with disability, audio, video or sign language will be paid attention to read and address beneficiaries untold stories or concerns expressed in the form of body language as well as strengthening outreach awareness to reach vulnerable groups to ensure feedback and gather their views regarding the project assistance. The team also ensures to bridge the language gaps for illiterate beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA messages are part of the information package shared and disseminated to the project beneficiaries during the consultations and information sharing sessions throughout the project implementation. PSEA messages shall be disseminated and beneficiaries are encouraged to report with immediate effect any form of such acts during the project implementation in such a confidential fashion as possible through the established feedback & complaint mechanisms.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Save the Children will conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities and explore ways of strengthening or setting up joint complain and feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability and community participation throughout the project period. Feedbacks from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, post-distribution monitoring, post-harvest assessments and meetings with beneficiaries. The targetgroups will be represented from girls, boys, women and men, people with disabilities, together with community and government leaders and community structures from onset of implementation, to ensure communities are well informed about the project and getting assistances considering their safety and consultation with identified individual needs and vulnerabilities. Any complaints from the beneficiaries about the project activities will be collected and feedback will be given by the Boma level project management committees. In addition to existing experiences, we will work with thecommunities to determine a most appropriate and suitable complaint response mechanism (CRM) in order to facilitate beneficiary complaint/feedback collection, complaints handling and response mechanism. We will continue to build capacities of staff as well as communities to ensure they are aware of their rights and entitlements.Issues raised could be discussed during the monthly coordination meeting for jointactions where due necessary. Feedbacks from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, post-distribution monitoring, post-harvest assessments and meetings with beneficiaries. Services providing team will carry out regular group discussions and key informant interviews with different key stakeholders (women, girls, boys and men).

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Informed by SC's multi-sector gender analysis (2020), Save the Children will prioritize interventions that address the root causes of food insecurity so as to address GBV issues. The emergency of livelihood support will enhance food production which will help to address food insecurity which exacerbates GBV issues encountered and psychosocial stress due to food insecurity. The Livelihood team will work with child protection, safeguarding and mental health and psychosocial Officers to implement interventions that are integrated in addressing PSEA and GBV issues.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Informed by environmental and social impact assessments, the proposed project will contribute to reducing the impact of flooding including reducing displacements by 30% through implementation of actions that include community managed physical structures constructions (dykes), strengthening water drainages, awareness on early warning information analysis and communications. This could be more materialized through community participation in labour works such as cash for work schemes and natural resource management plans.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Save the Children will use an inclusive approach in the implementation of the project.The beneficiaries of the project will be the most vulnerable people, those fallingin category IPC 4 and 5, of the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC). Beneficiaries will include Internally Displaced Population (IPD), host communities, refugees, and community vulnerable groups’ representatives (female headed household, households with malnourished children, single mothers, widows, elders, handicapped or chronically ill, IDP, refugee, youth at risk). The reduction in the caseloads can be done through implementing livelihood based interventions that are integrated with other sector, which will help in maximizing impact of interventions.
