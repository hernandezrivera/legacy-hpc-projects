Project Summary

NRC Bangladesh in 2019 established its adolescents and youth programme in refugee camps, through 26 community-based learning facilities and it is currently constructing 5 adolescent and youth centres (3 of which are two-storey buildings), in coordination with SMSD and Education sector, which will be fully operational in 2020. Capitalising on lessons learned from its direct implementation during the second half of 2019, and through the knowledge gained with its local partner, COAST Trust, NRC will scale-up its geographical reach and provide access to quality learning opportunities to 7,660 adolescents and youth. This will entail the recruitment and training of an overall 800 among learning facilitators, host community para-teachers and youth club leaders.
NRC has already supported Education Sector’s partners through master training in the past 2 years, especially on BLP, and DRM. NRC intends to continue its support in the Education Sector Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan (EPP) and in the event of an emergency, NRC staff will be made available to inter-agency emergency response, to support needs assessments, information dissemination (during the week after the weather event). In 2019, NRC developed a life skills framework, contextualized to the Rohingya response in Bangladesh. In partnership with UNICEF; NRC has planned to develop a curriculum for technical/occupational skills based on the "Skill Development Framework for Adolescent and Youth of Displaced People From Myanmar in Bangladesh", (correspondent to a non-academic level V - GIEP/former LCFA) developed by the Sector to address the needs of adolescents and youth with little to no level of literacy. The curriculum development will engage partners already working in life and technical skills, and will involve consultation with the Youth Working Group. For both life and technical skills, in 2020, NRC will offer life skills and technical skills training for facilitators and will open the possibility to Education Sector partners to benefit from this capacity building initiative.
The community engagement, third essential element of this project is at the core of all NRC’s youth interventions. In 2020 NRC will work on community engagement by establishing committees to run and monitor learning centers in the camps, and by supporting the existing committees through advanced training on community participation and ownership.
NRC’s adolescents and youth programme, especially in its life skills’ component, is designed through a participatory process. Life Skills Education aims at contributing to resilience, enhancing empowerment of youth and social development. In Bangladesh, NRC has adapted its life skills modules to this response, as the modalities in which the outcomes are pursued vary depending on the target group, its age, gender, and context. Life skills training also entails a full participation from youth, during its delivery, as it is not mere transfer of knowledge, but a process that demands from participants a commitment to reflect on themselves, their society and to develop an active, positive behavior towards life and its challenges. In this effort of being participatory as much as possible, the programme will take into account not only the differing needs among age groups and gender, but also will try to collect and provide material support, at all stages, the voices of the most marginalized, such as people with physical and mental impairments, through continuous training of facilitators on inclusiveness, and through the establishment of center management committees and with NRC’s complaint feedback mechanism.
Besides being an active member of the Education Sector, NRC is part of the NGO Platform through its base in Dhaka, and continuously liaises with the NGO Bureau and the UNO office in Ukhiya. NRC´s education staff on the ground participates in all relevant coordination meetings at camp level.

Needs

Almost 915,000 Rohingya refugees currently reside in Cox’s Bazar District, Bangladesh with the majority having fled from Rakhine State, Myanmar since the most recent outbreak of violence on 25 August 2017. Two years on, living conditions in the camps remain sub-standard, with high levels of congestion in a location prone to landslides, flooding and water logging. Lack of access to adequate shelter, education, WASH, healthcare and other basic services remains a critical gap.Access to education and learning opportunities: The approximately 485,000 Rohingya children now residing in Cox’s Bazar are almost solely reliant on the national and international non-governmental organisations for the provision of education services. As highlighted in REACH’s education needs assessment earlier this year, services are available primarily to children aged 3-14, with minimal educational opportunities currently available to adolescents and youth aged 15 and over. In the Education Sector’s gap analysis, it emerges that more than 49,500 adolescents from 15 to 18 years old and more than 120,000 youth (19-24 years old) do not have access to any kind of learning opportunities. From MSNA (October 2019), 2% of 15 - 18 aged and 0% of 19 - 24 aged females have access to TLCs whereas male are 13% and 18% respectively accessing TLCs. According to REACH’s among the reasons for the low attendance rate of this age group reported for the by boys is the perceived non-relevance of the subjects and material taught and gender norms for girls. Since adolescents and youth also support their families through work either at home or outside, also caregivers often do not see the importance of education.Restricted capacity in the response affecting the quality of service: Education partners working in the response are facing the obstacles of language, level of education among the Rohingya community and space constraints in the camps. The lack of sufficient number of people qualified enough to work as facilitators among the Rohingya population continues to slow down the efforts to provide quality and inclusive education. The language issue is another aspect affecting the quality of the education response: due to the scarcity of qualified Rohingya facilitators, some learning centres mostly rely on Bangladeshi facilitators, who speak Chittagonian, which, while close to the language spoken by the Rohingya community, is not the same. As pointed out by Translators without Borders, this often leads to miscommunication, and poses obstacles between communities and service providers. Impact on host community education: Prior the crisis, education in the host community of Cox’s Bazar district was already lagging behind the country average in terms of learning outcomes and quality at both primary and secondary levels. The influx heavily affected the local economy, human resources and education system, from which formal school teachers left in order to take up work in NGOs. According to USAID’s needs assessment (Oct 2018) the perceptions in the host community is that the humanitarian focus on the Rohingya is unfairly overlooking them and at the costs of the influx they must bear. All this considered, NRC is looking into an increasingly integrated programming, with a focus on resilience that take into account all communities’ needs and perceptions.

Activities or outputs

Objective 1 - Access: In continuity with NRC’s Adolescents and Youth Learning Centres established this year in some refugee camps, NRC intends to expand its reach to other camps while working with the same approach with refugee adolescents and youth. Additionally, NRC will directly work with host community adolescents and youth. The education programmes will be offered to refugees through community-based learning, and in adolescents and youth facilities being built this year. For host community, the programme will run utilizing community spaces, such as youth clubs, which in most cases obtain funds from the UNO office. Activities will include life skills, Better Learning Programme (BLP), basic literacy and numeracy, and technical skills training. Foundational skills materials are taken from a basic curriculum specifically tailored by DCA in Cox´s Bazar. NRC will also monitor the political decisions on education provision in the camps, and will advocate for the use of the Myanmar curriculum, in line with the Education Sector’s efforts.The Youth Led Market Assessment conducted at the time of writing will result in more youth ownership and will inform the technical skills pathways on which NRC has planned to produce a curriculum in partnership with UNICEF that will be available to all partners.NRC will target at least 52% females in the host community and the refugee community. In this activity, NRC will strive to include female facilitators by investing in community sensitisation, through objective 3. Through the community-based learning and upgradation of shelters for education purposes considering the environment needs, NRC will strive to make the centres accessible, by including in its designs ramps, and by ensuring accessible pathways. Objective 2 - Quality: With consideration to the need emerging from the analysis, NRC wants to invest on teachers, facilitators and all personnel supporting education in the field, who are the backbone of the programme. The active participation of youth and the inclusion of marginalised groups into the programme will be ensured through the professional development of facilitators on a wide range of topics, such as conflict-sensitive education, teaching methodologies and approaches, inclusive education, technical skills materials, child safeguarding principles, PSEA and knowledge on child protection mainstreaming into education activities. NRC will train on these topics the learning facilitators and non-teaching education support personnel in the camps and will offer a comprehensive training programme for para-teachers working in government and non-formal schools and in host community youth clubs. In addition to these topics, two modules will be dedicated respectively to life skills and technical skills training, drawing from NRC life skills pack contextualised to the Rohingya response in Bangladesh and the technical skills manual, which will be developed in 2020 through a project in the pipeline. Objective 3 - Community Engagement: In line with NRC’s focus on adolescents and youth, the school/facility management committees will have a strong youth component, and will be established (where not already present) in consultation with youth and the community. The existing committees will be supported through advanced training on community participation and ownership, and DRR.Among the activities:• Establishment and training of School Management Committee • Engaging parents and community stakeholders in ‘Participatory Monitoring’ to improve teaching-learning environmentYouth Learning Centres committees will regularly consult youth for their feedback and will design relevant activities in cooperation with them – Once a new learning centres is well-established and running, the youth will be encouraged to take ownership of management and meet monthly to discuss the topics they feel to be relevant.
