Project Summary

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8 million South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced.
Mercy corps proposed project aims to ensure equitable access to quality education, improve school-based services (including protection, psychosocial support, and health and hygiene interventions), and strengthen the education’s systems response capacity to mitigate and respond to external factors (such as the COVID-19 pandemic, floods, and other stressors) in Panyijiar and Rubkona counties of Unity State. In order to achieve these objectives, Mercy Corps plans to implement strategic enrollment and back-to-school activities, construct and repair school buildings and classrooms, upgrade all WASH facilities to meet minimum standards (including the construction of gender-segregated latrines), conduct a series of pre- and in-service teacher training courses, and provide teaching and learning materials, among others. These activities align with the identified humanitarian needs and directly support the Education Cluster's Three Strategic Objectives.

1 - Needs

3.4 Million school-age children (age 3 to 17 years) in South Sudan have been directly affected by the ongoing emergencies and crises, exacerbated in 2020 by the spread of the unprecedented COVID-19 pandemic. In addition to the ongoing turmoil brought about by conflict and economic instability, South Sudan’s education system in 2020 was completely shut down in response to the pandemic, and the total number of children in need of education and other related services only increased. Most children have remained out of school for a prolonged period of time in due to these converging crises, negatively impacting what positive strides had been made in recent years. In Unity State, especially vulnerable children—internally displaced people (IDPs) and returnees—face these challenges as well as increased barriers to education with limited infrastructure to address these dire needs. Combined, Mercy Corps and other education actors have identified more than 125,000 children with education needs in the targeted areas, with girls making up 51% and children living with disabilities accounting for approximately 15%. In Rubkona County, another critical humanitarian consideration to consider is the transitioning of Bentiu Protection of Civilians (PoCs) to IDP site under jurisdiction of the government. Currently, eight schools in the PoC are expected to support 30,000 schoolchildren in safe, quality, and inclusive education. Many more schools in the surrounding area support schoolchildren from host community, refugee, and returnee backgrounds. Furthermore, the severe flooding impacted school infrastructure in Panyijiar Country which was hit by both flood damage and further strain as schools were repurposed as temporary shelters for those who were subsequently displaced. Nearly 33,000 school children were supported in 2019 across 25 schools supported by Mercy Corps, and that number is expected to increase in 2021. The infrastructure in place is insufficient, as both new classroom structures need to be established and old and aging classroom blocks are in urgent need of updating. Back-to-school awareness campaigns, enrollment, and ensuring improved attendance after the long school layoff will prove to be critical activities. Key lessons learned and community mobilization gained from the challenges faced in 2020 will help to inform enrollment strategies in a combination of awareness raising and advocacy, house-to-house campaigning, and school-based events in order to account for the enrollment deficit currently faced, particularly for girls and children living with disabilities. Teacher capacity is another need requiring urgent attention, particularly as teachers work without incentives which increases lack of motivation, absenteeism burnout, and turnover, raising the risk of losing a skilled workforce to wage earning jobs. Previous academic cycles have prioritized pre-service teacher training. Unfortunately, this is no longer adequate as new teachers arrive with fewer skills and less content knowledge. In-service teacher training along with additional layers of support such as teacher mentor schemes, and classroom observations must become the new benchmark to deliver on the promise of quality education. Gaps remain ahead of opening schools and keeping them open as a result of COVID-19. WASH facilities complete with handwashing stations and soap are required. Many schools lack functioning water points, resources, and materials required to implement mitigation measures to slow the spread of COVID-19. The threat of school closures in response to increased transmission rates remains, and schools need to be equipped, school personnel trained, and awareness campaigns supported to ensure that all necessary steps are taken to keep schoolchildren, teachers, and communities safe during the ongoing pandemic. Current education activities in Panyijiar County supported by ECW will not be duplicated, but complimented to address the severe need.

2 - Project activities

Key activities in 2021 in the targeted areas include: 1) ensuring equitable access to school; 2) improving school-based services; and, 3) strengthening education systems’ capacity to mitigate and respond to external factors. Enrollment of children in pre-primary, primary, and secondary school will continue, with a specific focus on safely returning to school after the prolonged closure due to COVID-19. Targeted activities and community mobilization to support enrollment and attendance is a key factor in ensuring equitable access to education and better promote the inclusion of vulnerable children, primarily girls and children living with disabilities. Activities will seek to specifically accelerate enrollment and attendance of girls while targeting an overall increase in both.Establishing new and rehabilitating existing TLS and classroom blocks will help to ensure that safe learning spaces and schools grow with increased enrollment and new health and safety guidelines requiring social distancing. New semi-permanent classroom structures will be constructed on existing school sites in accordance with local need and demand, based on pupil-to-classroom ratios (to be established upon school re-opening). For rehabilitation, classrooms will be brought up to Education Cluster and INEE minimum standards according to the needs assessment which has specifically identified outdated and aging structures no longer adequate to serve their purpose. In Panyijar County, schools damaged during the severe flooding will be prioritized. The upgrade in construction materials and practices will revitalize and extend the life of the classroom blocks to withstand further use and future rainy seasons. Installing and rehabilitating WASH facilities—water points and gender-segregated latrines—will address the significant gap facing most schools so that child health and wellbeing is prioritized and all children, including girls, can remain in school throughout the day, regardless of ability, age, menstrual cycle, or any other potential barrier that proper WASH facilities can address. Schools will also be equipped with handwashing stations, IEC material, and materials necessary to slow the spread of COVID-19 to keep children safe in school during the pandemic.Improving school services at the school level and the inclusion of low-level MHPSS and wellness inputs, health and hygiene promotion, among others, will further help to enhance safe and protective factors of school. Upgraded referral pathways (particularly for child protection concerns and GBV), help desks, reporting and referral mechanisms, child- and girl-friendly spaces, and increased teacher capacity across all aspects will help to strengthen education systems. To ensure that teachers and school personnel are prepared to deliver across all responsibilities, pre- and in-service training will support their professional development not only in their content area, but across all roles required of a teacher.Finally, strengthening the schools’ ability to mitigate and respond to external emergency factors as outlined previously to better ensure enhanced (and uninterrupted) support of children, as well as their academic standing and health and wellbeing. Creating a strong education ecosystem around schoolchildren will require further capacity building of school and community leadership to improve school resilience.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Underpinned by Mercy Corps’ internal Safeguarding policies and Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM), this project will fall under the oversight and supports which cover all programming and community-based activities. Specifically, the zero tolerance policy states: treating beneficiaries with dignity and compassionately is a matter of priority, no act or behavior exploitation and abuse shall be condoned. Trainings will be conducted with teachers, PTA and SMC members, and other parties engaged in the project, on all issues relating to protection. Close collaboration and coordination will be carried out with protection teams in issues relating to protection, and all partners, participants, and children (girls and boys) will be made aware of the various organizational channels through which they can report or otherwise claim their grievances. Moreover, a key component of this project will be to highlight referral services and pathways, including the capacity development of formal, semi-formal, and non-formal teaching and learning institutions and spaces, as well as developing the capacity within the individual roles of teachers and other school personnel. This project will work in collaboration, consultation, and coordination with other partners in the area to consolidate workable and effective referral pathways for the survivors to access services without hindrance, as they are needed.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Involvement of all stakeholders, especially those who are most vulnerable, will enable the definite achievement of goals in a timely manner. Community engagement meetings will draw upon participants from all groups of beneficiaries, regardless of age, ability, gender, and position in the community, including school children. Ensuring that a diverse range of perspectives, lived experiences, and voices are represented in these conversations, prioritizing all types of community members will continue be the prerequisite of accomplishing Mercy Corps’ project goals and aims. Messages will be tailored to suit age- and gender-specific groups. Information, education and communication materials will be in local languages (e.g. Nuer) for Rubkona and Panyijiar counties. School and community meetings will also be held where necessary to target specific groups separately, for example parents or teachers, (men and women), schoolchildren (girls and boys), children (and families of children) who are living with disabilities, and others as necessary.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Ranging from informal feedback such as conversation and communication in school locations to official report feedback mechanisms including post-distribution monitoring (PDM) and the Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM), and steps in between, Mercy Corps includes various feedback and complaint mechanisms throughout all stages of implementation to ensure integrity and joint ownership of projects, activities, and outcomes. Additionally, all team members share responsibility in guaranteeing the inclusion of these feedback systems, from program and project team members working in the field, to specifically-assigned CARM Officers, Monitoring, Evaluating, and Learning (MEL) team members, as well as country and global headquarters supports and quality assurances. The cultural shift away from using the term “beneficiary” in favor of “participant” is another organizational shift towards increasing the role, dignity, and ownership over the projects on the part of the affected and supported population—the participant. This proposed project will continue to utilize and improve upon tools, mindsets, and culture specifically designed to solicit and on-board regular feedback (including complaints) from members of the targeted community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will work with community, CCCM in Bentiu POC and other partners on ground and use various media to ensure that community members are aware of Mercy Corps’ project activities and those feedback channels available. Sensitization meetings will be done with different groups to ensure information reaches all communities in the project target areas. Specifically, parents and caregivers as well as teachers and other school personnel (including school management committees) will have opportunities to provide feedback for adaptation and implementation throughout, as was indicated in their inclusion in the needs assessment for the completion of the current project proposal. Where appropriate, schoolchildren (boys and girls, as well as children living with disabilities) will be asked for their feedback to onboard and update within the current project cycle. Schoolchildren, as co-creators of their own educational experience, will enjoy opportunities for feedback as a component of the education project to ensure that their voices, concerns, and rights are acknowledged throughout. Furthermore, child-centered learning is a key component in quality and inclusive Education in Emergencies. Pre- and in-service teaching training will introduce these education principles and pedagogies and they will be further fortified through activities such as classroom observations and teacher mentoring schemes. Despite some limitations, priority is given to children where appropriate and feasible. Community engagement as a component of Monitoring, Evaluating, and Learning (MEL) includes measures such as interviews, focus group discussions, and post-distribution monitoring surveys to track efficacy of activities and interventions. A component of capacity building activities, such as teacher training and PTA and SMC training include pre-and post-training tests and surveys. Furthermore, participatory meetings are continuously featured during implementation of project activities and will also be use as monitoring and evaluation mechanisms.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Panyijiar and Rubkona counties have enjoyed relative calm, yet some risks still remain, particularly during specific times and in specific locations. Protection cluster partners and advisory groups will be consulted in the lead up to registrations and distributions to ensure that recipients of material goods and non-food items—children (both boys and girls), parents and caregivers, and people living with disabilities—can receive education-related goods and services and support with safety and dignity. Mitigation measures and risk assessments will continue to be a component of distribution and registration activities conducted by Mercy Corps and partners. Prioritizing participant perspectives, including members of marginalized communities and minority groups, this project will have an inclusive implementation of all activities, comprised of all stakeholders’ participation: regardless of sex, race, religion, language (mother tongue), and ability by a way of conducting community engagement meetings for collective decision making, school meetings, and other wide ranging consultation mechanisms. Embodied in this project is the issue of equity and equality as stipulated in the SDG4. Women, girls, and youth have been and will continue to be highly involved in consultations and decision-making processes. Multiple, simultaneous community-based approaches will be leveraged to break down socio- and cultural-normative practices which exclude or otherwise limit the participation of women and girls. These approaches will seek to address multiple community perspectives, especially those of women and girls, and include those of power-holders, to ensure safe and sustainable progress supported by the community. A key component of this project will be to update and highlight referral services and pathways, including the capacity development of school institutions as well as the individual roles of teachers and other school personnel. This project will work in collaboration, consultation, and coordination with other partners in the area to consolidate workable referral pathways for the survivors to access services without hindrance when needed.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Mercy Corps, in collaboration with GBV and Child Protection partners, will coordinate efforts to sensitize schoolchildren—including boys and girls—and engage community members and local leaders to ensure safety in and out of schools to mitigate GBV risks. This project will proactively work with various learners in primary and secondary schools as well as community members, PTAs, SMCs, community leaders, and women’s groups to identify potential risks to provide context-based solutions. Rubkona and Panyijiar County Education Departments and Mercy Corps will collaborate to give support and guide them through to raise and ensure school safety planning is given acceptable attention to account for the needs of children with disabilities, girls, and learners trekking further distances from home to school on a daily basis, as well as other vulnerable children. Sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and GBV risk mitigation will also be given additional emphasis through safety audits and protection concerns in schools. The project will address several issues by mapping out key actors within project target locations to involve them throughout all phases via community sensitization on adverse effects of concealing GBV cases, inaction, and the tendency of impunity where individual perpetrators responsible for alleged GBV-related crimes. The local authorities and traditional leaders will be involved in this project to better understand and exercise their role(s) and enforce local laws where applicable.
