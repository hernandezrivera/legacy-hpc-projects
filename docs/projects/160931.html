Project Summary

Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) works with vulnerable communities to alleviate suffering caused by both man made and natural disasters such as displacement by war or flood, destruction of shelter and property, disease, and hunger, among others. HACO is intending to undertake this project for the First time in Wau and Jur river counties in Western Bahr el Ghazal State. The project is proposed to provide lifesaving non food items (NFIs) to the returnees, IDPs and host communities in vulnerable situations in Wau and Jur river counties as affected by the crisis in South Sudan. Comprehensive feedback from internally displaced persons as collected by UNMISS and IOM in Wau in October 2018 indicated that functioning basic services and adequate housing are critical to enable people to return to their homes however the number of returnees from IDP situation, the returning refugees and those from military cantonment sites is expected to rise because of the on going peace reconciliation at the country level. The project will target 1,667 vulnerable households with 10,000 individuals on average affected by the conflict. Rapid Needs Assessment will be conducted in a multi-sectoral approach to determine the lifesaving NFIs needs of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities. The assessment will facilitate the identification, verification and registration of the extremely vulnerable individuals (women -lactating and pregnant, households with malnourished children, unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with disability) among the IDPs, returnees and host communities. Participatory approach with Community and their structures by dialogue and focused group discussion will be used during the rapid needs assessment, verification and distribution to ensure there is inclusion of all by gender and age, no discrimination of the minority and marginalized groups. The process will ensure commitment to communicate and engage with the affected communities at all phases of the program cycle: assessment, registration, verification, distribution and post distribution monitoring exercise. Interventions will be decided and made based on consultations, dialogue and FGD with the affected population. Staff will be responsible and accountable to the affected population as such needs, views and feed back from the beneficiaries are part of the decisions in the implementation process to be addressed timely to guide the direction of the program. After verification and registration distribution will be timely conducted. HACO in collaboration with other actors on ground will carry out post distribution Monitoring exercise to determine the level of satisfaction among the beneficiaries and the usage of the NFIs distributed. The project activities will be designed taking into account the gaps identified through the multi-sector assessments as such the implementation will also be in a cost effective method by close collaboration with other clusters, sub clusters and partners at site level, government departments to share resources, ideas, expertise and experience.

1 - Needs

The escalation of the conflict in Wau in mid-2016 and the subsequent episodes of armed violence in March 2017 displaced 35,000 individuals while the clashes between armed groups in June 2018 in areas south of Wau, including Baggari County and Jur river prompted another displacement of 2,000 individuals more to collective centres in Hai Masna and Agok. Looting and burning of houses caused the destruction of property. International Organisation for Migration (IOM) has been tracking the movement trend of the displaced people in Wau. In April 2019, IOM published that "despite continued insecurity, starting from early 2018 the number of displaced persons staying at the Wau PoC site has drastically reduced. A similar decrease of shelter-seeking people has been observed in other protection sites around town. While many residential areas remain relatively abandoned, parts of Wau are gradually seeing communities return. To respond to this trend, humanitarian partners, together with the local government and UNMISS have been implementing an action plan to create the conditions for the safe and voluntary return of displaced people since the beginning of 2018". From 2020 HNO and HRP, the S/NFI cluster estimates in South Sudan 1,998,228 individuals are in need of S/NFI assistance, however the cluster will target 1,015,331 individuals (approximately 169,222 households) who can not support themselves with life saving S/NFIs. Families are sharing the limited and basic resources with vulnerable hosts/relatives who fled with nothing because their homes were destroyed and burned while properties abandoned were either looted or destroyed, during the time of conflict. People need to go back and start new life. The number of IDPs has been decreasing in the PoC and other IDP locations, there have been returns from even outside the State from Sudan, the neighboring states and repatriation from refugee situation but not seen increasing in the places resettling. This indicates that these movements and return have been for those who can afford to move and support their situation. People not being seen in their places of origin could be because of fear about the security situation, their protection, how to regain the lost properties and land to start new homes. HACO is undertaking this project of NFIs distribution for the first time, however the HNO and HRP for 2020 and IOM information give the background as to why NFIs assistance is needed for the displaced people to return and settle in their former places. The on going peace process at the country level is likely to result into an increase in the number of returnees going back to their former homes and rebuild new life. HACO will work with the other partners (e.g. IOM, the S/NFI cluster, other partners and the government lands department) to address the issue of Housing, Land and Property (HLP) to help the vulnerable have basic land and shelter rights so that when given shelter materials they will be going to legitimate places to live. This will mitigate the risk of future eviction that can take back the vulnerable to displacement and loss of property again. The vulnerable (women, men, children, elderly and the disabled) among the host communities, IDPs and returnees in Wau and Jur River counties in Western Bahr el Ghazal State need to be supported with NFIs, and access to other basic services like health, water, education for their children, and protection to go back and restart their life in a dignified way. The shelter material will be provided in kind and the volunteers and the community will be sensitized and engaged to support the vulnerable in putting up the shelter.Another need in the area to be discussed with the community is introduction of fuel efficient stoves to mitigate the risk of women and girls from GBV when going to collect firewood. If the community will raise it as a concern and accept it to be part of the project, training will be undertaken with 500 women.

2 - Activities

HACO has an operation office in WAU however will be doing NFIs distribution to affected communities for the First time. HACO with support from the S/NFIs cluster, other sectors/actors integrated with other clusters, government departments, existing community structures and leaders on ground will coordinate to undertake a rapid needs assessment among the IDPs, host communities and returnees in Wau and Jur river counties. This will help to understand the actual needs of the people to return and for those who have returned to settle and cope with the situation, and what are the protection issues in the area to be addressed for people to settle. Community will help and account for the identification, verification and registration of the most vulnerable people: women (pregnant and lactating), households with malnourished children, survivors of GBV, men, unaccompanied children and elderly and the disabled to be targeted for the distribution of NFIs.This project plans to conduct the following activities in Wau and Jur river Counties. Activity 1: Conduct rapid needs assessments to identify lifesaving NFI needs of IDPs, returnees and host community beneficiaries (focusing on particular needs of pregnant and lactating women, men, households with malnourished children, girls and boys, unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and the disabled). Activity 2: Awareness raising with the community on access to the nearby available water points, GBV services, the referral pathways, psycho-social support to GBV survivors, support to malnourished children at the health facility and mental health services among others. Activity 3: Conduct awareness with the community on complaints and feedback mechanism to have issues raised and addressed before the project takes off, if there are any protection concerns like sexual exploitation and discrimination during the assessment; this CFM to continue throughout the implementation of the project to keep the project informed of the progress and new issues from the beneficiaries and how to address them timely. Activity 4: Train project staff on the accountability to affected people (APP) approach, guide and make staff committed to the project for the people; inform the community leaders of their role and accountability as well to ensure the commitments to their community are met. Activity 5: Order NFI and shelter items from Humanitarian hub through cluster pipeline for 1,667 HHs (10,000 individuals). Activity 6: Supported by Logistic cluster, transport NFIs from secondary warehouses to field locations or join the emergency needs response missions 'survival kit missions' in case the area becomes hard to reach. Activity 7: Mobilize and train community volunteers on NFI distribution, if there are those trained by other partners do refresh training on NFI distribution to support the exercise. Activity 8: In an integrated approach supported by other sectors, verify, register and carry out distributions of NFIs to verified beneficiaries. Activity 9: Conduct post distribution monitoring (PDM) to get the numbers that have benefited from the service, get feed back on implementation modality from both the beneficiaries and non beneficiaries, and follow up to know how useful the service has been and will be. Activity 10: The shelter material provided in kind will help in roofing the shelter structure; the community volunteers and the community members will be sensitized to support the vulnerable in putting up the shelter. Activity 11: Monitoring and evaluation on the use of the distributed items by the households that benefited from the service also collect feedback from the community. The assistance will support return, recovery and reintegration as well as help people build resilience and coping mechanism in the process. Activity 12: Reporting. 13: Training 500 women on making and using fuel efficient stoves to mitigate GBV risk among women and girls going to collect firewood from distant places

Yes. The project is aligned with National objectives because it is for provision of basic services to people. The national objective is to ensure a durable solution to the people who have been displaced make sure people return to their places and coexist in peace, harmony and in a dignified manner with the host communities, IDPs and the returnees. Take care of the most vulnerable members of the community without discriminating because of gender, age and disability and whether from minority groups in the community.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

With the on going peace implementation process the environment for return is still relatively calm, it is expected to get better to allow more people to return to their places of origin. HACO will work closely will the community security committees and the local government authorities for security updates all the time and ensure the vulnerable are accessing available basic services in the area without risking their lives. Any sign of deteriorating security situation will be communicated to ensure safe return and safe stay.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. The community will be part of the project design after supporting the identification of needs, response and beneficiaries. The vulnerable from the IDPs, host communities and returnees are members of the community, the NFIs support/assistance given is to support partly on what they lost, provide base for resilience and coping with the new situation so that they can continue with their life in a dignified way. The identified vulnerable are part of the community, records will be shared during the hand over of the project for the community and other sectors to know the level of assistance given and how it has helped the household/individual improve in recovery and coping with the situation. Individuals have to be temporarily supported by the project that they can be on their own or be supported by the community as a sustainable solution to their problem.
