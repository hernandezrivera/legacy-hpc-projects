Project Summary

Based on community participation, the project is designed based on the following: reduce hunger by providing access to food to the population affected by the crisis especially people living inside camps to have access to life-saving food to meet their basic food needs, all year round in Idlib and Aleppo Governorates.
Key interventions will include:
- 50,000 food baskets in Harim, Jebel Samaan, and AlBab districts for 12 months.
- 2500 food vouchers in Dana, Al-Bab, and Atareb districts for 11 months in line with FSL cluster recommendation.
- Moreover, there will be 28000 RTEs that will be distributed in emergency situations.
ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the vulnerability selection criteria:
• Women-headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
• Large families (more than 5).
• Families with disabled members.
• Families with low income (less than 50 USD)
• Families with elderly (men-women).
The total number of beneficiaries will be 402,500.
ATAA will remain AAP through open dialogue, that accurate and timely information (including goals, outcomes, and any vulnerability criteria to participate) will share with them. We have now $5,238,712 as a secure fund and ATAA will make efforts to secure funds to carry out the project.

3 - Needs

According to FSA_HNO 2023: The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population, the result shows a 7% increase in the PiN from last year. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of sliding into food insecurity, The alarming increase of people at risk of shifting to become food insecure during 2023, if the crisis continues at same worsening levels, is of major concern to the sector. As many as eight per cent of households had to sell assets due to lack of food or money to buy food in addition to more than 80 per cent who reported not having any assets to sell should they have needed to, indicating high levels of depleted means at households' level. As many as 90 per cent of households reported relying on markets as main sources of consumed foods.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will get orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Relative Stability in AlBab, Jebel Samaan and Harim districts and access to people in need. No disruption in the supply chain, transport and distribution of commodities. Adequate humanitarian access is secured. There is social acceptance for the project and the people are prepared to interact with it. An analysis of all risks related to the project was conducted where risk has been classified into protection, environmental and security risks as well as COVID-19 risk. Protection and GBV risk: When selecting the beneficiaries, some people and communities or dominant groups in the project area do not accept the idea of participation of women in project activities. This risk will be mitigated by external awareness-raising sessions in the community on women's rights and the importance of their participation in the project and signing MOUs with local councils which include the participation of women in the project. The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in. This risk will be mitigated by transparency with local authorities and the community and by informing them very clearly of the selection criteria regarding women. Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse.
