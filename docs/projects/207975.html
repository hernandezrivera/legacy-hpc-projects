Project Summary

MedGlobal will provide essential primary healthcare services at the Health Post operated by MedGlobal in Camp 24 as per Minimum Essential Service package (Health Post level) following clinical guidelines of Bangladesh and Sphere Standards. Besides, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable disease will be prioritized. Special health camps and telemedicine service sessions will be organized for catering special health needs. MedGlobal will also engage CHWs in raising community level awareness, promotion and increasing preparedness on health issues and emergency response. They will be equipped with an electronic health system as developed by MedGlobal to strengthen community to facility referral systems.
From the MedGlobal Health Post in Camp 24, Leda, Teknaf -minimum essential primary healthcare services required as per health sector guideline will be provided that includes outpatient curative care, maternal and child health, adolescent health, family planning, gender-based violence, diseases, mental health and psychosocial services, capacity building for local health care professionals, and community health worker such as case-follow up and public health education keeping gender, disability and diversity inclusion considerations in programming. The activities and outputs will be monitored using KOBO toolbox and internal clinic census, and reports will be made available to the Health Sector. The project also intends to provide crucial medical supplies and equipment to other camp facilities. All activities will be prepared in accordance with strategies of the Health Sector to ensure that they are consistent with the Health Sector's priorities and strategies in Camp 24. MedGlobal will prioritize global advocacy through showcasing outcomes and lessons learned from the activities. To ensure accountability to affected population the following steps will be taken:
(1) A continuing logic model planning process based on needs assessments and baseline surveys with the goal of involving the community.
(2) Rigorous audits and budgets to ensure financial transparency; and
(3) Program monitoring and evaluation to assure program quality management.
(4) Protection, Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD), disability inclusion, accountability to affected populations, PSEA, disaster risk management, and environment and ecosystem rehabilitation for addressing the impacts of climate change to be mainstreamed in the MedGlobal 2022 sustainable operation strategy. At the health post, MedGlobal's work concentrates on 51 percent women and includes maternity care for young mothers, as well as family planning. Gender, sex, disability, diversity, and age are reported in the statistics. MedGlobal is already performing needs/baseline evaluations for 2023, with women, girls, disability, diversity, and other age groups in mind, and we will aim to incorporate the results into our next logical framework model and project management cycle. We already conducted multiple COVID 19 capacity projects in 2020 and 2021 including supporting to Bangladesh government health facility e.g., the Upazila Health Complex with Central Oxygen Systems and other medical equipment and supplies to health facilities in the FDMN camps, and we spent roughly $505,000 on the projects. MedGlobal is committed to building the capacity of the health sector partners. In this JRP MedGlobal with collaborate with health sector and SRH working group in building local capacity for reducing maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

962,416 Rohingyas are currently sheltered in in 34 camps in Cox’s Bazar, as of 31 July 2023. Ensuring a minimum essential health service package remains a top priority for 2024. However, the periodic onset of communicable diseases in the camp setting continues to create additional challenges to health and other service provision. Managing vector-borne disease control, communicable diseases, and non-communicable diseases are some of the key priorities in the current response. As the crisis has reached its sixth year, people with vulnerability have faced the crisis differently. As per the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) Technical Working Group (TWG) assessments, of those individuals with unmet healthcare needs (12% were host and 11% were refugee individuals who needed healthcare but were unable to receive it), the reported unmet healthcare needs were: consultation or drugs for acute illness (fever, diarrhea, cough, etc.) and consultation or drugs for chronic illness (diabetes, hypertension, etc.) from the host community 12% and from the Rohingya community 18%. Still number of children under the age of 2 delivered at home is almost 43% of all the reported births in the camps setting according to a report of the 2023 Joint-Multi Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA). Almost 59% reported that they needed health care in the prior 3 months of reporting. The general morbidity/disease distribution pattern has remained consistent where skin diseases accounted for most (21%) of the top 10 morbidities followed by Acute Respiratory Infection: 18%, other acute illnesses, etc. Of the 5% of refugees and 2% of host community individuals with disabilities, 31% of those from the Rohingya community reportedly experienced challenges in accessing education, healthcare, food, shelter support, humanitarian assistance, employment, and social interactions due to their impairment. Most households reported barriers to accessing healthcare, by households with at least one member with a disability were long waiting times, unavailability of specific medicine, treatment, or service needed, not receiving correct medications, and health facilities being too far away. Thus, health service delivery is not reaching certain geographic areas within the camp and certain populations are not accessing needed health services when they need them.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection has been mainstreamed in MedGlobal Bangladesh operations. Any case of GBV and PSEA is dealt with proper confidentiality and privacy by the trained healthcare staff at the facility and community level. Any violence against children are to be notified to the proper authority and referral network for ensuring proper child protection. To prevent harassmment and abuse both at the workplace and at community, MedGlobal has ensured proper measures and functional mechanism such as an high-powered committee overseeing the entire process ensuring proper investigation and corrective measures are taken.

3.3 - Age & Disability

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be ensured during the different phases of the project. At the design and planning phase, the need assessment reports such as periodic situation reports published by ISCG, will be utilized in addressing the community need for support as the documents are readily available to save scarce resources. At the implementation level, consultations will be conducted with the beneficiaries. Special hot line will be set up for providing any feedback at the program implementation level. Besides, written feedback mechanisms will be installed such as complaint registers or complaint boxes will be maintained for any type of feedback. Special consideration will be given to maintaining confidentiality and neutrality in addressing the community feedback and complaints. MedGlobal will work with other organizations, advocacy groups for disability groups, and/or specific age groups, in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the responses as well as designing ways they may meaningfully participate in influencing or creating this response. Periodic coordination meetings will be held among various groups regarding the services available and mechanism of accessing the services. These feedbacks will be the basis for input from the community in terms of influencing the programming. The project will adopt all the structural and behavioral orientation required to tailor the needs of various age and gender groups. The results of these steps will be measured based on the data that will be generated.
