Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and longstanding emergencies. The protective environment for children in Somalia has been weakened due to the conflict that has stretched over two decades resulting in the collapse of institutions at national and local levels, and leading to child rights violations. The recruitment and use of children by armed forces and groups remains a major concern in Somalia.
The security situation remained highly volatile, with continued attacks by Al-Shabaab on the Somali security forces, government officials and the African Union Mission in Somalia (AMISOM), as well as inter-clan clashes. The Somali National Army and AMISOM continued joint offensives against Al-Shabaab . Military forces of Ethiopia, Kenya and the United States of America also conducted operations against the group
In May, 2019 the National Intelligence and Security Agency (NISA) captured 25 children after they escaped from Al Shabaab armed group military training Centre in areas around Jowhar. As per SOP for handling, reception and handover proceedures, UNICEF were alerted about the potential children who are formerly associated with armed groups/forces. INTERSOM social workers were immediately deployed to conduct rapid assessment on the children and quick-start the process of negotiating the release, handover and reception of the children. During the assessment, it was reported that more children could be potentially released as the fighting to liberate key towns and villages get underway in Middle Shabelle region.

Project Activities

Output 1: Children associated with armed forces/groups and other vulnerable children are provided with case management services (family reunification, psychosocial support, interim care and referral services) following their release or enrolment into the programme.Output 2: 180 children formerly associated with armed forces and groups and other vulnerable children, are provided with alternatives to recruitment through inclusive reintegration package of services (vocational skills, literacy, entrepreneurial skills training and opportunity) Output 3: 20 former CAAFAG and other vulnerable children below age of 15 years supported to access formal education Output 4: The capacity of local authorities and community organizations (child/youth clubs, village child protection committees, women groups, etc), is strengthened to protect, prevent and respond to the core rights violations of children especially those affected by the conflict and support their participation in peace building activities.

Mitigation Measures

INTERSOM will take an inclusive community-based approach in assisting the social and economic reintegration of children formerly associated with armed forces and armed groups, including other vulnerable children in conflict affect communities. The reintegrationprogramme will ensure age-appropriate and will be based on the individual’s developmental stage, culturally and gender sensitive, considering a range of socio-economic assistance options to serve the diverse needs, abilities and interest of children and their care givers. INTERSOM partnerships with communities - including children, their caregivers and community-based groups that are central to this initiative will be strengthened to prevent recruitment and protect children at risk.• Lack of government and state level awareness on SOPs on handling, receptions and handover of children associated with armed forces/groups can sometimes lead to children being detained for longer periods. The authorities need to uphold the commitments signed under the SOPs to ensure that children are transferred within agreed timelines.• Coordination between local authorities and ministries needs to be supported at the state-level. Ad hoc referrals by different agencies on the ground has lead to confusion and requires planning and contingency.• There is need to implement reintegration programme in Gedo region, since larger number of children supported by the programme come from those areas. Reunification and family support has been a challenge and authorities are not willing to transfer children to Gedo.

Please provide further explanation

Gender:INTERSOM is committed to adopting gender equity in all aspects of our programs and operations. Because of the long-standing tradition of gender inequality within the Somali context, we often give specific attention and resources on ensuring that more than 20% girls are engaged in the program activities and benefits, which is in line with the National Strategy for the reintegration of children affected by conflict. Gender approaches and tools allow us to bring an awareness of gender issues to our program design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation activities. Despite the context and the project nature favour 80% boys, yet girls will up 20% of the project. The needs of boys and girls have been identified, analyzed and appropriate intervention strategies reflected in the outcomes, outputs and project activities. Equity: INTERSOM is committed to adopting gender equity in all aspects of our programme operations. The project will give specific attention and resources to cater and promote the needs of CAFAAG children & UASC, particularly vulnerable boys/girls will be identified, analysed and appropriate intervention strategies reflected in the outcomes, outputs and project activities. Sustainability: The programme is designed to prevent the relapse of children back into the armed groups or armed forces, including the possible recruitment of those who may find themselves idle. The programme therefore provides alternative skills and education to former CAAFAG and other vulnerable children as a matter of priority in order to ensure their sustainable reintegration into society and to prevent them from being re-recruited by armed forces or groups or from engaging in exploitative work.
