Project Summary

The Education Cluster estimates at least 2,808,000 girls and boys between 3-18 years of age in conflict and crisis affected areas are estimated to not have access to pre-school, primary and secondary education in 2019; and at least 42,902 teachers/ volunteers and members of school management committees are in need of humanitarian assistance as they are crucial to deliver education services during emergencies.
Peace Corps Organization (PCO) proposes to implement this project in Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal States. The project will be aimed at increasing access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies, improving the quality of education to ensure continuity of relevant education services for children and youth in emergencies and strengthening the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth (2019 Education Cluster Strategy). This will be achieved through establishment and rehabilitation temporary learning spaces and classrooms, establishment and rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, provision of school-aged children with fortified school meals/take home ration, provision of emergency-affected children with quality education and recreational materials, equipping of teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience, training and capacity building of Educators, community members and authorities on psychosocial support and equipping SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance (2019 Education Cluster Strategy).
The project will support to children and communities facing unprecedented levels of malnutrition, protection, water-borne/ related diseases as a result of cyclic waves of conflict and seasonal flooding by targeting 3,600 beneficiaries comprised of 1,500 girls, 1,500 boys, 300 men and 300 women – includes people with special needs and youth amongst host and IDP communities in areas whilst coordinating with counterpart agencies working in Nutrition/health/FSL/protection sectors. Another key objective is to put in place actions to reduce/prevent incidences of GBV, sexual violence and other protection matters facing thousands of girls, boys, women and men. Cross cutting issues including protection, GBV, HIV/AIDs, Accountability to Affected Populations, protection, gender equity, environmental conservation and youth engagement will be mainstreamed at all stages of the project.
Peace Corps Organization, which has been operational in the proposed areas since 2006 has competent and experienced technical teams and relevant office space in Wau, Pibor, Rumbek and logistics, warehousing facilities; and strong grass root networks that can be easily deployed, including staff redeployment to proposed areas for scale up interventions.

Needs

Internally displaced and host children (boys and girls) and youth including women and men haveparticularly been vulnerable to an array of shocks which include unprecedented levels of food insecurity. The key drivers of these are conflict, the practice of child and women abductions and cross-border cattle raids, worsened by seasonal flooding. This is in addition to protection risks including attempted recruitment by armed groups (boys); sexual exploitation, abuse (girls) and physical violence girls, boys, young women and men. Despite majority of schools having received support from external actors in 2018, unprecedented levels of food insecurity (5.2 million projected to face extreme food insecurity early 2019 – IPC September, 2018), malnutrition-GAM rates of 15.0-29% are projected to significantly affect learning and school retention in states such as Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal. The 2017 Education Cluster estimates at least 2,808,000 girls and boys between 3-18 years of age in conflict and crisis affected areas are estimated to not have access to pre-school, primary and secondary education in 2019; and at least 42,902 teachers/ volunteers and members of school management committees are in need of humanitarian assistance as they are crucial to deliver education services during emergencies. Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal are among other states with changes in education needs attributed to lack of scholastic materials and supplies, inconsistencies in provision of teacher incentives, non-availability of school feeding programmes, poor infrastructure and insecurity in the region. Girls and boys have been particularly vulnerable to drop out rates due to deteriorating food insecurity in search of food while occasionally girls get married off at an early age or undertake transaction sex(when their husbands/male relatives are off to cattle camps) that exposes them to negative reproductive health consequences (IPC, 2018; Protection Trends January-May 2018; Education Cluster National Assessment of the Status of Education in South Sudan, 2018). A recent Education Cluster assessment indicated 21 per cent of assessed schools were non-functional, with insecurity being the major cause of school closure especially in Western Bahr el Ghazal, with reported attacks on schools, teachers or pupils and theft or looting by armed forces and groups. Peace Corps Organization (PCO) proposes to implement this project in Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal States. The project will be aimed at increasing access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies, improving the quality of education to ensure continuity of relevant education services for children and youth in emergencies and strengthening the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth (2019 Education Cluster Strategy). Cross cutting issues including protection, GBV, HIV/AIDs, Accountability to Affected Populations, protection, gender equity, environmental conservation and youth engagement will be mainstreamed at all stages of the project. Peace Corps Organization, which has been operational in the proposed areas since 2006 has competent and experienced technical teams and relevant office space in Wau, Pibor, Rumbek and logistics, warehousing facilities; and strong grass root networks that can be easily deployed, including staff redeployment to proposed areas for scale up interventions.

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will prioritize and focus on communication and information dissemination among targeted beneficiaries through public address campaigns, community meetings, radio talk shows, FGDs, interviews and feedback sessions. Active participation of beneficiaries will be encouraged as well.

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be context specific and tailored to target different groups and categories - men, women, girls, boys, people with disabilities, elderly, youth etc. This will be done through community meetings, participatory sessions, FGDs, interviews etc.
