Project Summary

The proposed project will respond to the protracted Rohingya crises in terms of supporting the vulnerable Rohingya community and host communities through ensuring their food security and sustainable livelihood. The project will develop a livelihood option of Rohingya refugees and marginalized host communities to help them ensure their food needs and access to sustainable income sources. Considering the multi-vulnerabilities factors like economic crisis, price-hike, Covid-19 and pre-existing natural hazards in the rohingya context, Oxfam will provide support through food and cash-based interventions to sustain their livelihoods and reduce vulnerability and upliftment of the quality of living. Hence, the activities will be inter-linked with environment and climate change issues where relevant. The project will target the most vulnerable women and girls, children, older age, men and boys and at-risk individuals living in Rohingya and host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilla by long term- lifesaving services. Project participants will be empowered through enabling food and cash interventions in previously inaccessible sites as well as raising and amplifying the voices of undermined community to influence over the decision impacting their lives. To improve access to adequate diversified food access and livelihood opportunity, livelihood support services like Climate adaptative agricultural initiatives like Homestead Gardening & Pit composting, Cash for Work (CfW) intervention, Unconditional Cash Grants (UCG); Enhancing food security and socio-economic empowerment through linkages to DRR and climate reilience activity, provisioning poultry and fisheries support for sustainable livelihood opportunity etc. .
By identifying and engaging the Rohingya community, local farmers, small-business holders (small shop owners etc.) and providing them inputs support such as resilient rice varieties or seedlings, organic agricultural inputs, cash incentives etc to uplift their production and supply. Connecting their production initiatives (with those small farmers/shop-holders) of complementary food items production or supply, to school feeding activity the sustainability and selling of their production will be ensured. The students of learning centres of Rohingya camps and primary school of host communities will be provided with the food rations/ package that are distributed at the end of month if students attend school regularly.
Thorough Climate resilient HG and pit composting support the community will increase their adaptive capacity to climate shocks, continue food availability during any emergency period, meet basic and nutritional needs of family members in additional to access the fresh vegetables and earning opportunity. During Programme desining phase oxfam will do practice "do no harm" and reduce social cohesion within Rohingya's and host communities by implementing different activites and technique 's and approaches.
To enable host and Rohingya refugees to access temporary income where they can meet their basic needs (e.g. food, cloth, shelter, water etc.) and rehabilitate their community assets & infrastructure (e.g. green action, post-disaster clean up; constructing new roads and pathways; and rehabilitation of pathways, streets, bamboo bridges, water culverts, etc.)
Oxfam will conduct the awareness sessions on DRR related topics (e.g. COVID-19, disaster, fire response etc.) at the local markets and communal places to enhance community’s knowledge and skills for DRR resilience. Oxfam will introduce waste management and Tree plantation initiative for the environmental protection at the community level integrated with the Cash for Work intervention. Thus, the project will improve the food security, livelihood status & environment of the community. Oxfam will well adressed Intersectionality (Persons with disabilities,Transgender and other's) during beneficiaries selection and project activities implementation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Access to fresh and nutritious food continues to be an obstacle for the refugee population residing in Cox’s Bazar camps and host community areas due to the ongoing Rohingya crises and in addition to the COVID-19 pandemic. In April 2021, The World Food Programme’s (WFP) Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 4) found that “Overall vulnerability levels have reached an all-time high in the refugee camps since the influx, with latest findings showing that 96 percent of all refugees are moderate to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance”. Food insecurity for both Rohingya and surrounding host communities has increased since 2019, primarily due to the COVID-19 pandemic. A previous assessment conducted by WFP found that by the end of 2020, 86 percent of Rohingya refugees were highly vulnerable to poverty and hunger, up from 70 percent in 2019. While within host communities, members considered moderately or highly vulnerable in terms of food insecurity increased to 51 percent in 2020 compared to 41 percent in 2019. This increase is reflected where poverty rates were found to have increased in 2020 and onwards (REVA-4, summary report-April 2021) due to the CV-19 impacts. Due to the food insecurity, at the household level vulnerable groups adopted mainly livelihood coping strategies to access food support. Compared to 2019 the livelihood coping strategies increased in credit-dependency of Rohingya households (62 percent). Respectively host communities dependency changed from 41 to 53 percent. Overall, two third of Rohingya and one third of host community households are involved in crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies to cover food and / or basic needs compromising their resilience and future productivity. Both groups have increased the selling of their assets to access food and other basic needs. One out of three income sources in the camps come from negative coping methods such as selling assistance, borrowing money, or relying on help from relatives and friends.Lack of adequate food and food diversity has caused malnutrition, especially among pregnant, lactating mothers, and children. UNHCR, CARE and ActionAid (2020). For Rohingya households with an income source, monthly earnings are on average 75 percent lower than host community households and are equivalent to 37 percent of the Minimum Expenditure Basket, barely covering for basic needs (REVA 04 April 2021). (Source: WFP REVA April 2021): Overall household vulnerability was significantly correlated with household income and the nature of the main income activity. Eighty-six percent of Rohingya households who were highly vulnerable had the lowest average household income levels (BDT 2,736), while the same for highly vulnerable host community households (11 percent) is BDT 6,822.As Covid-19 measures were in place in Cox’s Bazar, food access reduced and became more insufficient for refugees, and this trend was more for single mothers, households headed by persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly. Disproportionate realities of women and girls were exacerbated during the Covid-19 pandemic which impacted on their mobility thereby reducing access to basic needs, further reducing their limited access to income, and increased the burden care work due to stay at home protocols. Women who are mostly in the informal economy and rely on daily wage and minimum have very few safety nets especially in host communities. Across host and Rohingya communities, experience of GBV also increased including IPV and early marriage. (Rapid Gender Analysis on Covid-19, October 2020.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

(a) Inter-sectoral camp-based coordination: OXFAM will support the Food Security along with all activities in the same camp and regions. Oxfam will provide these multiple services in the same camp to allow an integrated approach between these sectors and to ensure smooth coordination with different tier of stakeholder. Oxfam programme teams actively participate in inter-agency clusters under the coordination of the United Nations (UN) and government to identify gaps and avoid duplication of assistance. Through clusters, Oxfam sends regular updates on its activities to feed into the “Who Does What, Where and When” database. (b) service delivery to refugees and host communities: Oxfam will support both the Rohingya and Host communities in all activities and do necessary coordination and cohesion between two communities (Rohingya and Host) by supporting both communities. Assisting participants to access to basic services, live with good health, dignity and better safety. (c) relations with the Government (in particular, with CiCs): Oxfam works closely with the Government of Bangladesh at the national and Cox’s Bazar level and has good relationships with line ministries including the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA), Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR), NGO Affairs Bureau and local authorities, Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) and the Deputy Commissioner (DC). At a local level, Oxfam attends regular meetings with, and provides programme activity reports to, the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) and the Deputy Commissioner (DC), Upazilla Nirbahi Officer (UNO), Camp Coordination, Camp in Charge (CiC). Oxfam ensures programme plans are shared with local government departments and participates in bi-weekly coordination meetings called by the respective CiC, UNO who is appointed by the government. Approved government forms are kept ensuring that activities are approved by government and field operations are implemented smoothly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will ensure SAFE programming, which is focused on identifying potential risks posed by the project and mitigation measures to prevent or mitigate those risks. The project will also ensure arised protection issues through integration with Protection sector where necessary. To ensure that PSEA and safeguarding are thoroughly understood by Oxfam staff, all new staff receive induction on protection and safe programming while long term staff attend mandatory refresher training periodically. Oxfam protection has its own resource pool within Oxfam Affiliates to support on safe programming.

3.2 - Gender

Project activities are designed to be gender-responsive for the affected population to ensure equitable access for women and other vulnerable groups. Specific attention will go to including women in consultations during the design, implementation and monitoring phases. Food security and livelihood activities will also consider the specific needs and issues for women, girls and other vulnerable groups). Gender issues will also be addressed alongside livelihood interventions, as women and men will be engaged in producers’ group to allow the transfer of knowledge and skills while also improving social cohesion.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Oxfam commits to creating and maintaining a safe organisational culture where all those whom Oxfam serves and who work for Oxfam feel empowered to insist on non- discriminatory and respectful behaviour from each other, where poor behaviour is not accepted, and where power is not abused. The safeguarding of children within Oxfam’s work is all our responsibility and must be considered in every aspect of our work. A commitment to good practice is expected of all Oxfam’s employees. Oxfam is open to feedback, continual learning, and improvement to ensure that safeguarding children is at the project location of our work.

3.4 - Diversity

During the participant selection process project team will involve and ensure diverse group of people as for example, Persons with disabilities by using prescribe template and Washington Group short set Questions, different ethnic people, gender, races, religions and intersectionality with also incorporated different types of diversified livelihoods and skill development income generation activities considering the beneficiaries, context and market demandable.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Population is at the core of Oxfam’s interventions and therefore accountability to project participants is mainstreamed into the design of this intervention, supporting our commitment to safeguarding and community participation. In line with the Core Humanitarian Standard and approach, Oxfam is adopting a community participation centered approach in the whole project cycle to ensure that participants are consulted during assessments, design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of activities. Oxfam will also closely involve the community in all monitoring and evaluation activities with regular feedback sessions through community consultation meetings as well as feedback opportunities during and after each distribution. Communities will also be able to provide feedback through the hotline, helpdesk and complaints mechanisms. The CPT is an additional tool to enable communities to express their views while allowing staff to capture, analyse and understand.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Through diverse environmental and climate resilient intervention at the targeted areas, the project will enhance environmental protection, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) along with improved communication facilities for the people in the climate vulnerable and disaster-prone areas of refugee and host community. For instance, climate resilient and adaptive Homestead gardening, Green Cash for Work (e.g; Waste management, Tree plantation) will contribute to environmental sustainability building in the targeted location and reduce future climate risks.
