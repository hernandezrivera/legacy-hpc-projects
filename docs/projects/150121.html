Project Summary

The two specific objectives of the proposed intervention are as follows
Specific Objective 1: Enable farmers to resume and maintain their livelihood sources through rehabilitation and support of livelihood productive assets
Specific Objective 2: Improve the coping ability of vulnerable farmers to withstand
protracted threats and intimidation.
The project is in line with the country strategic objective three (SOP3):
The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
The project aims to address the Specific Food Security Sector (FSS) no 1. The
resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected.
The Overall Objective is to improve coping mechanism of vulnerable Palestinians farmers in area C and mitigate the impact of settler and occupation violence and intimidation to sustain their livelihoods.

Needs

Agriculture is a traditional shock absorber for communities when other social safety nets fail; playing a critical role in protecting and promoting livelihoods and absorbing a significant percentage of the workforce. According to the PCBS (2018), 19.1% of the West Bank (WB) population is currently unemployed (20.6% of females participating in the workforce). Furthermore, 4.9% of the workforce is accounted for unpaid family members, a common trait among agricultural workers, especially females. According to SEF Sec Survey 2014, food insecurity in the WB decreased by 6% (15% male headed households and 25% female headed households). Overall, the survey indicates that 29.6% of the WB population has some degree of food insecurity. The vulnerability of women is further exacerbated by their limited possession of assets, hindering their ability to support their families including their basic needs. In Oxfam’s March 2018 gender analysis, gender inequalities were evident amongst the entire food production chain, including access to productive resources (land, labour, fertilizers, credit, technology, extension and markets) especially in female headed households. There is visible gender discrimination in the allocation of household resources, including nutrition. Nearly 63% of the WB farming land is classified as area C (UNCTAD, 2014) whereby Palestinian communities are considered among the most vulnerable. With the continued Israeli restrictions on land, water, market constraints and increased settler attacks, farmers are at risk of displacement; and their land and livelihood assets are at a increased risk of confiscation. Settler violence is one of the tools of forcible transfer creating an environment of fear and intimidation. The Ministry of Agriculture’s (MoA) Damage Documentation Department, reported that 400 agricultural attacks on vulnerable populations and their livelihood assets occurred between 2017-present. In 2018, 180 cases have been officially registered with pending verification and entry into the Ministry’s database. The value of destroyed assets exceeded 3 million USD in 2017-present. According to OCHA, an increase in settler violence was reported during the first half of 2018 (144); 43 incidents attributed to Israeli settlers, resulting in Palestinian casualties. During 2017-2018, LRC documented 12,531 demolition cases and demolition threats to agricultural assets resulting in vulnerable farmers living in constant anxiety and fear of their unknown future. In 2018, assaults are expected to dramatically increase during the olive picking season, especially in the Nablus Governorate. Farmers constantly feel threatened when accessing their lands due to limited agriculture roads and settler attacks; resulting in minimal farmer tracks and isolation rendering the cultivation of farming lands as easy targets for settler violence and vandalism under the protection of the Israeli army. Farmers in the area have expressed the necessity for support to protect their livelihoods and agricultural lands and roads, to ease access and rehabilitate their agriculture lands which have been destroyed or are inaccessible. Forced displacement has devastating short and long term socio economic consequences, resulting in increased poverty, reduced living standards and increased dependence on humanitarian assistance, especially amongst women. The humanitarian situation in Area C has been declining over the years and farmers are struggling to maintain their livelihoods. Farmers and their families use negative coping strategies (selling off assets, compromising quantity and quality of food, early marriage and debt) to maintain their livelihood and afford basic commodities (food and health care). This burden is often passed on to women exacerbating their care responsibilities and household chores. Agricultural related livelihoods in Area C are highly important and essential as one the main sources of income.

Activities

The project will address the above-mentioned needs by providing women and men farmers in Area C with agriculture assets to maintain their livelihoods and enhance their coping mechanisms. In close coordination with MoA, FSS Cluster, OCHA and other humanitarian actors in area C, Oxfam will address needs resulting from settler attacks and intimidation through gender sensitive interventions. The full project design as well as project implementation including the selection of localities and beneficiaries will be conducted in coordination with the MoA and other line Ministries, and other relevant organizations. Oxfam will premise interventions on “do no harm” principles to protect men and women from risks. This would entail examining possible risks pertaining to women’s protection when rehabilitating roads to enhance their safety, security and accessibility, taking into consideration women's needs and household chores. Oxfam will ensure livelihood resumption, sustainability and improvement of access to agricultural assets to support farmers in rehabilitating agricultural roads/lands, land preparation, seedlings, fertilizers, greenhouses, water carriers, fencing, other relevant gender sensitive livelihood and conditional cash assistance and provision of seeds, which will be coordinated with MoA in advance. The aforementioned activities will be implemented taking into consideration MoA procedures, guidelines and available resources to maximize benefits and to avoid any harm or duplication of efforts. When identifying agriculture livelihood needs, decision makers are represented by family members (HH-male and female farmers) taking into consideration that members participating in those activities, below 18 years, is considered as child labor. The project will adopt a community based approach to design and implement activities on coping mechanisms, prioritizing women headed farming families, and engagement of female engineers. Beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms will be ensured, including reporting pending cases to the MoA and FSS. The project will integrate these elements during baseline/end line surveys and throughout monitoring. Beneficiary lists will be provided by MoA, based on the HRF criteria. OCHA’s FSS tracker and referrals will be adhered to. With Oxfam leading the overall implementation, partners will implement activities in different geographical locations in Area C. Indicators

Oxfam is committed to genders-sensitive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) across all its programs and projects which ensure equal access to Oxfam project services for males and females from all age groups. Oxfam also developed a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability (CAMSA) which forms the basis of M&E systems for all Oxfam projects. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies will be applied. CAMSA approach guides on how gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues are monitored during implementation and how project reviews and evaluations consider gender sensitive issues. However, all tools, data gathering, data analysis, reporting and recommendations and action points are gender and age disaggregated were required. Throughout the project MEAL will be undertaken in accordance with a comprehensive M&E plan which will track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe, and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by age and sex for both qualitative and quantitative in nature using different tools such as questionnaires, Key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, and Focus group discussions (FGDs) Prior to the projects inception, OXFAM’s MEAL team will develop an M&E framework to ensure that all indicators are tracked properly and in a timely manner. Oxfam’s MEAL team together with partners will ensure carrying out regular monitoring visits, reporting through focus groups and individual meetings with women, men and beneficiaries at risk of settler and IF violence. Oxfam will work in close coordination with humanitarian assistance providers to communities at risk of losing their livelihood source and forcible transfer in Area C address gaps efficiently. It will also adopt a beneficiary feedback mechanism to inform MoA, FSS and OCHA about referred cases. A complaint mechanism will be put in place in collaboration with a representative of the MoA. Finally, lessons learnt workshops will be organized at the end of the project to document lessons learned, case studies, successes and challenges. Targets: 7,075 (Female: 3,467, Male: 3,608) farmers and herders’ beneficiaries receiving inputs and services, 575 dunums to be made productive or with improved production capacity,20 greenhouses will be rehabilitated,4 km of water carriers will be provided, 30 km of agricultural roads to be rehabilitated and 30 (25 men, 5 women) cases referred to relevant clusters.
