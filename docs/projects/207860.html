Project Summary

The aim of this project is to ensure gender responsive, inclusive and safe and quality education, Psycho-Social Support (PSS) along with Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) strategies for children, adolescent girls and boys, emphasizing on drop outs. FIVDB will adopt the gender transformative integrated ECD and Myanmar Curriculum (MC) and primers for targeted learners in Rohingya community and FIVDB’s adolescent primers (aligned with the NCTB curriculum) for the host community. 4118 Rohingya and host community girls and boys will be enrolled in the learning centers (LCs) in 3 targeted camps (Camp- 8E, 14 & 24) and 4 Unions of host community (Palongkhali of Ukhiya, Teknaf Sadar, Baharchara & Teknaf Municipality of Teknaf Upazilla), while 20 government or non-formal schools will be selected for rehabilitation from 4 targeted Union in the host communities. FIVDB is successfully implementing education programs in 8E, 14 and 24. Overall 60% female learners will be targeted (As enrollment is low for girls in camps) who are of age 3-18 years. The project will target 3% learners with disabilities following sector’s target. The project will ensure gender segregated WASH facilities in LCs with hand washing points and allocation of MHM kits for female learners and incorporate contingency support plan for emergency preparedness and response. 106 (60% female) Rohingya teachers will be recruited from the communities and accommodated by capacity building approach for all teachers including pedagogy, PSS, life skill, subject based instruction, DRR, first aid box maintenance, PSEA, gender sensitivity and disability inclusion training. The data will be collected maintaining SAAD criteria. All the activities will be monitored closely and periodical lesson learnt workshops will be conducted ensuring community engagement. Location of CBLFs will be chosen prioritizing safety of the learners. All the staff will be oriented on waste disposal and environment pollution. 115 Community education support groups (CESG) (50% women) will be formed consisting of 958 members (728 excluding learners) per four CBLFs including 8-10 members following education sector guideline. 5121 community people including parents will receive awareness and sensitization session on child marriage, child labor, trafficking through home visits and regularly attend in parents support group meetings. The CESG will be engaged in monitoring student enrolment, retention, and attendance, as well as ensuring participation and promoting parental involvement in children’s education. Education interventions will be designed based on the international standards, such as the minimum standards set by the International Network on education in Emergencies (INNE) and their contextualized version for Bangladesh. The retention and capacity building of 113 local teachers, as well as learning facilitators, will be supported through supervision and professional development opportunities in the areas of pedagogy, PSS, DRR, subject based instruction and life skills. The project will regularly coordinate with Child protection, WASH, Health, Food security, Nutrition sectors, where the focuses areas are engagement of parents, caregiver, boys and men, referral pathways, child friendly feedback mechanism, collaboration with duty bearers and relevant stakeholders to reduce the child protection risk, ensure safeguarding, accountability, meaningful access, participation and girls’ empowerment and leadership. The project will prioritize protection principles, safeguarding principles, humanitarian principles and do no harm approach. Right based and effective aid will be ensured prioritizing seven pillars of accountability to affected population. All the facilities will have a disaster risk management committee oriented on disaster risk management and referral pathway. All the staff will receive protection gears and FIVDB’s gender, PSEA and safeguarding policy will be active throughout the project.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"At present, approximately 0.96 million FDMNs are residing in 33 Refugee camps and Bhasan Char of Bangladesh (UNHCR, August 2023). According to REVA-6 2023 95% Rohingya HHs were found to be moderately vulnerable due to lack of access to food, livelihood opportunities, lack of education, non-food items, etc. The vulnerability of Rohingya HHs is reported to be higher than host community HHs. The reasons behind these increasing vulnerabilities are global food crisis due to price hike, slow recovery from COVID 19 and lack of employment opportunities. According to CPSS and CPIMS factsheet 2022 54% children are subjected to Neglect, 15% children are subjected to child labor which indicates that a number of children are currently dropped out of school to financially support their family. A number of adolescents and young adults are reported to be engaged in negative forms of employment to support their family due to lack of capacity and formal education. 8% adolescents especially girls are at risk or victim of child marriage. Social belief, culture and superstitions are the major barriers for adolescent girls to pursue education. The major reasons behind drop out and not attending learning facilities in host communities are the inability to afford education cost (41%), the inability to register children in school (26%), education is under prioritized (11%), child supporting home (7%), children having disability (4%), early marriage or pregnancy (4%). The major reasons behind drop out and not attending learning facilities in Rohingya communities are attending madrassa (20%), education under prioritized (17%), inability register or enroll children in school (15%), child supporting home (13%),learning facilities not separated for males and females (7%), Protection risks while commuting to school (6%), child having disability (4%), puberty or too old to go to school (4%), early marriage or pregnancy (4%). According to the assessment, 46% of Rohingya children and 78% of Bangladeshi community adolescents of school going age are currently out of education. Another major concern is lack of awareness and knowledge among parents, community leaders, religious leaders and community people. Children with disabilities, adolescent girls, children of female headed households face most barriers to pursue education. Lack of security in communities is another barrier for children and adolescents to pursue education. According to JMSNA 2023, 12% of population in host community and 11% of population in Rohingya community reported security concerns of child travelling to school (fear of physical threat, abduction, eve-teasing etc.). 59% of the Rohingya population reported lack of qualified teaching staff, 44% reported curriculum not adapted, 42% reported lack of teaching learning materials, 18% reported lack of teachers. Overall several numbers of deterrents are causing hindrance for children and adolescents in Rohingya and host communities to pursuing education,Joint Multi sector need assessment findings 2023, CPSS and CPIMS factsheet 2022

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project adopting an integrated approach-with focusing on education, it will address Disaster management, psychosocial support and other serious protection concerns. The project aims to encountering early marriage by engaging adolescent and youth girls, and protection mainstreaming, has been proposed under this project based on the previous learning of the organization since 2017 from its own assessment and from the sectors, camp administration, local government and community consultation. For every project, FIVDB always prioritizes coordination with and reporting to the sector, camp level coordination, coordination with the CiC, local government and administrations. This project will also address the rationalization principles of continuity by engaging communities in all activities of Education through regular community education support group meeting and by thus it will make communities understand the necessity of Education and even after a sudden closure of project, hope the major activities will be continued through these LCMC. The interventions proposed under this project are fully aligned with the sector priorities and Bangladesh Government priorities and strategies. FIVDB believes that the impact and sustainability will be remarkable as the approach and modalities that FIVDB proposed are already tested in development projects and in humanitarian settings with changes in Cox’s Bazar. Over the last five years, FIVDB implemented blended approaches based on need and that was useful to bring expected results. Lastly, from the beginning of FIVDB’s Humanitarian Response program, multi-dimensional community engagement approaches - community consultation, door-to-door visit, help desk, and interactive popular theater were undertaken for trust and relationship building with the communities which played the most vital role in successful implementation of every project and it will also be followed for this proposed project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will prioritize safety and security of the persons of concerns and abide by do no harm principles and understand protection context properly. During beneficiary selection priority will be given to most vulnerable households irrespective of gender, religion, age, and ethnicity, and disability, sexual orientation maintaining SAAD criteria and prioritizing humanitarian principles. Needs of most vulnerable individuals including pregnant woman, single woman, older persons, children persons with disabilities and LGBTQI+ will be identified in order to tailor assistance accordingly. The project will undertake capacity building of most underprivileged and disadvantaged individual and provide support for accessing existing services to ensure meaningful access. To foster accountability by establishing safe accessible and confidential complaint and feedback mechanism with timely response, proper information sharing regarding beneficiary rights, providing knowledge, training, and regular monitoring of protection mainstreaming is taking place and what impact this has had on affected populations in terms of safety, dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation and empowerment. The project will promote participation and empowerment by community engagement from design to implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project will also ensure collaborating with relevant actors to enhance protection, avoid duplication and access risks. As per FIVDB’s data protection guideline confidentiality will be maintained of all the data collected during baseline survey.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will target overall 3% children with disabilities of age 3-18. All the staff and volunteer will receive sector specific inclusive programming training for staff and volunteers. FIVDB will ensure conducting accessibility audit in all the facilities and service points of the project to ensure meaningful access for all individuals including disabilities. Throughout the project FIVDB will ensure use of social behavior change communication materials which is accessible for all. For women and girls with disabilities project will take initiatives to provide door to door services. The project will advocate and ensure participation of women including women with disabilities in forming community groups and leadership building approaches. The capacity building approaches will ensure life skill training for all including persons with disabilities following sector's standard. The project will also take initiatives to provide support to provide knowledge and support accessing existing services. The project will ensure conducting individual education planning and sessions for learners with disabilities and Purchase/develop accessible teaching and learning materials. The project will also take initiatives to provide home-based education support as per the needs of the child.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The needs of the project are identified based on JMSNA, 2023 and joint assessment analysis findings. The project aims to reduce vulnerability through capacity building of the most disadvantaged children, adolescents, men and women, LGBTQI+ including persons with disabilities. Community consultation will be ensured in beneficiary selection, project planning, and implementation and monitoring. Periodically lesson learnt workshops will be conducted involving the community and the findings will be incorporated while implementing future projects of the organization. The evaluations will be conducted based on OECD criteria and community engagement will be ensured in evaluation, post distribution monitoring. The findings will be shared with all relevant stakeholders. The joint response plan shared by inter sector coordination group will be translated and the messages will be shared with the persons of concern and respective community people through periodical focused group discussions with communities, community meetings and other sessions. All the facilities and project office will be accommodated with PSEA and safeguarding IEC materials, awareness posters regarding free humanitarian assistance for all, FIVDB’s complaint hotline number, complaint and feedback box with timely response and follow up ensuring community engagement. The project will ensure accessible mediums to disseminate any messages regarding services, assistance and other project related issues. A bi yearly meeting will be planned in presence of all relevant stakeholders to share the assistance delivered for persons of concerns, upcoming plans and good practices regarding Complaint and feedback response mechanism (CFRM). To update and improve complaint and feedback mechanism recommendations and suggestion from the community will be incorporate to strengthen organization’s CFM. The project will ensure accessible mediums to share about projects’ services, assistance and other project related issues.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

FIVDB is an active member of PSEA network and maintains regular coordination with the network. FIVDB’s PSEA policy is developed incorporating core principles adapted by the IASC on PSEA and Convention on the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women (CEDAW). FIVDB has participated in a PSEA assessment conducted by UNICEF in 2023 and has been declared as high/full PSEA capacity organization. FIVDB ensures PSEA reference check of all staff during recruitment and incorporates knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) questions regarding PSEA in staff recruitment interviews and written tests. All staff receives a mandatory training on prevention of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, gender sensitivity and orientation on all the policies of FIVDB. FIVDB is currently planning to conduct a KAP survey every year with the staff, volunteers and project participants to mainstream PSEA at the beginning of every year. All the facilities of the project will have PSEA and safeguarding IEC materials including awareness posters for all beneficiaries regarding their rights and FIVDB’s complaint hotline number. All the facilities will be installed with visible and accessible complaint and feedback box. The complaints and feedback will be responded and followed up regularly ensuring community engagement. FIVDB will take necessary actions regarding SEA complaints that are in line with the SOP for the SEA complaint referral. FIVDB has a dedicated gender cell to deal with all SEA complaints and take necessary actions. FIVDB ensures gender balance in gender cell to ensure accessibility of persons of concerns to place their complaints and issues. Regular risk assessments will be conducted by the organization to address and prepare a mitigation plan for the identified risks for the project and community participation will be ensured in the risk assessment. Regular monitoring of PSEA practices will be ensured throughout the project based on developed checklist.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. An emergency preparedness and response plan will be adapted for the project aligned with ISCG’s multi hazard response plan 2022 and the national plan for disaster management (2021-2025).The project will maintain a minimum contingency stocks and provide support for overall response efforts for host and Rohingya communities through relevant sectors and ISCG. FIVDB will ensure effective coordination among relevant government entities (DRRO, RRRC, CPP) ISCG’s operational partners, and Sectors, and the Energy & Environment Network (EEN). The project will undertake no harmful constructions that may cause hamper to the environment. Plastic usage will be discouraged by the project. All the project staff will be oriented on proper waste disposal and waste management. The CBLFs will have a DRR plan and a list of Vulnerabilities and Capacities for identiﬁed Risks of every Hazard. The hazard mapping list will be prepared through children’s participation. The CBLFs will be accommodated with DRM kits with a trained facilitator on the kit and its contents.All the CBLFs will have a disaster risk management committee trained on disaster risk management and referral pathway. All the CBLFs will be ensured with an emergency communication tree. Alongside all the CBLFs will have a Standard Operating procedure (SOP) for all identiﬁed Natural /Man made Hazards(Preparedness, Response, Recovery, Mitigation and prevention, Impact assessment, Remarks (responsible person, any speciﬁc timeline etc.)
