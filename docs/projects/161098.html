Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes the Provision of emergency PHCU and Mobile Unit health assistance to IDPs and host communities affected conflict and floods in Terekeka County, Central Equatoria, Aweil East and Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal South Sudan in the year 2020. This project will deliver a package of Integrated Health services activities in an effort to ensure continued access to minimum, and responsive integrated essential healthcare service that meet the needs of the vulnerable populations (IDPs, host community and returnees) in Terekeka County, Central Equatoria, Aweil East and Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal South Sudan by focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality. IHO conducted a needs assessment in Terekeka County, Central Equatoria, Aweil East and Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal South Sudan, and concluded that the health systems in the three locations were not only challenged with limited access to human resource medicine and other supplies but also limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services but also limited access to WASH services but the referral system is hampered by poor roads. The community has limited access to health education, Immunization coverage is still low. The leading causes of morbidity include Malaria, Pneumonia, Watery Diarrhoea among children, HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis. The IPC analysis September-December 2019 classified Terekeka, Aweil East and Aweil South malnutrition level in crisis stage (IPC 3 classification).
In this project, IHO will provide lifesaving quality health service to 2500 individuals (490 Male, 510 Female 735 Boys and 765 Girls)Terekeka 625 (123 Male, 128 Female, 184 Boys and 191 Girls, Aweil East 1250 (245 Male, 255 Female, 368 Boys and 383 Girls, Aweil South 625 (123 Male, 128 Female, 184 Boys and 191 Girls)) as part of health cluster target in Aweil East, Aweil South and Terekeka Counties by undertaking 3 mobile team, 3 PHCU Activities. IHO will work closely with communities to identify the problem affecting them and the community will greatly participate development and implementation of activities and community shall provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build capacities of the communities through trainings and awareness campaigns designed to halt the spread of communicable disease. IHO will address unique health needs of women and girls, particularly respond to GBV, including clinical management of rape. Finally, IHO will work closely with WHO, health cluster and MoH in ensuring provision of timely live saving health care services.

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of conflict the December 2013, the Republic of South Sudan faces considerable humanitarian problems, which have increased substantially. Among the counties affected include Aweil East, Aweil South and Terekeka Counties. In addition, a major crisis erupted in July 2016 further aggravated the humanitarian situation, producing secondary or third displacement with populations under stress lacking access to basic services and constraining humanitarian access. The conflict situation and floods in the targeted counties has impact greatly on the social services and as well the health care systems is challenged with inadequate human resource, limited access to safe water, poor waste management and infection prevention and control measures, poor infrastructure, potential risks of disease outbreak ie cholera, Ebola etc. The IPC analysis September-December 2019 classified Terekeka, Aweil East and Aweil South malnutrition level in crisis stage (IPC 3 classification) and is expected to raise due to worsening food insecurity is primarily driven by influx of conflict and destruction of crops due to floods hence insufficient crop production and disruptions to livelihoods and persistent macroeconomic deterioration. The County has been affected disease outbreak as WHO confirmed measles outbreaks in Aweil South (WHO Humanitarian Situation Report Issue # 12 18 – 24 MARCH 2019). IHO Assessments in Aweil East, Aweil South and Terekeka Counties showed that the main cause of morbidity included Malaria, Pneumonia and Watery Diarrhea among children. The high prevalence is attribute to limited access to primary health care services as the available health facilities are far and faced with limited human resource and shortage of drugs. As a result, there is limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services, as well as treatment of SAM cases with medical complications. The referral system is hampered by poor roads and lack of transport means. The community has limited access to health education and Immunization coverage is still low. There is a need to provide emergency health care services through static primary health care unit, alongside a Medical Mobile Unit to support other health partners in management of common illnesses to reduce excess mortality and morbidity. In this regards, IHO plans to reach 2500 individuals (490 Male, 510 Female 735 Boys and 765 Girls)Terekeka 625 (123 Male, 128 Female, 184 Boys and 191 Girls, Aweil East 1250 (245 Male, 255 Female, 368 Boys and 383 Girls, Aweil South 625 (123 Male, 128 Female, 184 Boys and 191 Girls) with health service in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations and Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. This project will provide lifesaving quality health service through the 3 mobile unit and 3 PHCU by working closely with the Nutrition, WASH and Protection clusters partners. In the same regard the project will monitor disease outbreaks in the intervention locations to inform the health cluster and undertake immediate response including emergency immunization in high-risk areas with lowest coverage. IHO will work closely with communities to identify the problem affecting them and the community will greatly participate development and implementation of activities and community shall provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build capacities of the communities through training and awareness campaigns designed to halt the spread of communicable disease. IHO will address unique health needs of women and girls, particularly responding to GBV, including basic management of SGBV survivors. Finally, IHO will work closely with partners, health cluster and MoH.

IHO will implement PHCU and Mobile Clinic Activities: IHO will improve access and scale-up responsiveness by establishing 3 Mobile clinics and support 3 PHCU. 1. Undertake Screening/detection for: • all under 5 and PLW for malnutrition • malaria for under 5 and pregnant women • Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy • vaccination needs2. Provision of essential Health Care Package through: • Outpatient consultation for common diseases • Vaccination against Measles & Polio for all under 5 • Vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women • Undertake routine EPI and Mass vaccination • Mass vaccination and case management for Measles and OCV • Provision of psycho-social first aid • Basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care ) • Provision of MCH services including Ante- and postnatal care services /check-ups and delivery 3. Undertake Management of common illnesses/Treatment through: • Management of uncomplicated cases of diarhoea: • Management uncomplicated cases of respiratory infection (Pneumonia) • Management uncomplicated cases of malaria4. Improve WASH services in the targeted PHCU through: • Water quality control at health facility • Environmental health (vector control and medical waste management)5. Undertake Referral of complicated cases to PHCC, Upgraded PHHCC or Country Hospital6. Provide primitive and preventive health services including • Folic Acid distribution for pregnant women• Vit A supplemention for under 5 every 6 months• Provision of Malaria RDT and prophylaxis for pregnant women• Deworming treatment for all under 5 every 6 months • Condom distribution• Ante- and postnatal check up• Family planning7. Undertake Health promotion on: • Education/ Promotion of breastfeeding• Health seeking behavior• Malaria prevention voiding stagnant water pool, Use of bednets• Use of clean water and sanitary facilities• Awareness raising of the availability of services for SGBV survivors• Mobilization for vaccination campaigns"8.
