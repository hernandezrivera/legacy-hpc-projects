Project Summary

Overall objective: To prevent and reduce impact of disability on individuals, families and communities, by addressing emerging, health concerns in Somalia.

SO2: Evidence and Documentation of rehabilitation needs, capacities of the system rehabilitation, to respond to current and emerging health needs is in place.
ER 1: Persons/Children with disabilities or at risk of developing disabilities, have better health outcomes due to access to timely and appropriate health and rehabilitation services.
ER 2. Health service providers have increased capacity to provide quality inclusive services and connected care (prevention, detection, management and rehabilitation) for people with or at risk of impairments

Needs

In recent years, rehabilitation services in Somalia have been neglected and received little support, most public centers are currently inoperative, the buildings have often fallen into disrepair, physiotherapy equipment are lacking. Lack of adequate staff, skills among staff on advanced rehabilitation care techniques, acute shortages of medical consumables and assistive devices due to disrupted financial constraints and poor monitoring, follow-up and patient referral mechanisms for patients requiring rehabilitation among some of the needs Handicap International have identified. Somalia has 4 rehabilitation centers, (2 Somaliland, 1 Mogadishu and Galkayo), The facilities are not sufficient to meet the growing number of PWDs, persons with injuries and elderly in need of their service, the existing limited rehabilitation centers have also reported underfunding (very little support from both humanitarian and government actors), hence experiencing limitation in production and procurement of assistive devices and mobility aids. The equipment in the rehabilitation centres were observed to be either outdated, malfunctioning, missing and/or destroyed.

Activities

ER 1Activity 1.1: Assess and respond to barriers (physical, financial, attitudinal and social) to seeking long term rehabilitation services.Activity 1.2: Support early identification of impairments, multi-disciplinary assessment and delivery of quality rehabilitation services by relevant service providers: (physiotherapy, occupational therapy, prosthetic and orthotic services, assistive devices, etc).Activity 1.3: Support development of patient information material to ensure compliance with rehabilitation plan developed by the multi-disciplinary team.Activity 1.4: Put in place a follow-up mechanism (such as, through the hospital social worker) to ensure better compliance with rehab plan, monitor progress and prevent drop-out.Activity 1.5: Facilitate formation of peer –groups (face to face) to support clients and help follow-up on the rehabilitation plans.ER 2Activity 2.0: Map rehabilitation services to support referral to relevant services.Activity 2.1: Train and sensitize primary health care staff, nurses and medical doctors on scope and benefits of rehabilitation ( in particular, for children with club feet, malnutrition) to enhance referral and reduce drop-out towards PT units/P&O units in health facilities and other specialized centres .Activity 2.2: Strengthen skills and knowledge of professional through training and coaching on managing various conditions and on improved inter-disciplinary, practice and person centred approaches.Activity 2.3: Support targeted hospitals and centres with necessary equipment including assistive devices as per need.Activity 2.4: Support preparation and implementation of rehabilitation protocols, ensuring that common standards of quality in rehabilitation services are implemented and monitored. ER 3:- Undertake research activities /studies to gather evidences on rehabilitation needs

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The action is based on the premise that if barriers are identified and addressed, the functional and health outcomes of the persons with disabilities will greatly improve, facilitating social, economic participation. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

People with disabilities face many additional difficulties before, during and after displacement. It is not common practice to include people with disabilities among those who are considered as particularly vulnerable in disasters and displacement and who therefore require targeted response – yet statistics tell us that up to 10% of all displaced people will have a disability. Solutions, 1) in areas with recurrent displacement, collect data on the number of persons with disabilities, and type of disability, alongside age and gender data plus any special needs they may have, 2) prioritize persons with disabilities in reunification efforts, particularly those dependent on caregivers, 3) Ensure disability questions are included in rapid assessments and target households with disabled people during interviews, focus group discussions or other assessment activities

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Persons with disabilities are extremely vulnerable to protection violations ranging from physical, sexual and emotional abuse to lack of access to justice systems and documentation. Children with disabilities are three to four times more likely to be physically or emotionally abused. Solutions include, 1) Target male and female persons with disabilities of all ages in all protection monitoring initiatives, 2) Assist persons with disabilities with communication difficulties to complete relevant forms and help all persons with disabilities to replace lost documentation and access justice and other systems, 3) Provide all information in simple language, using at least two means of communication (oral and written) and ensuring it reaches people who cannot leave their shelters/temporary homes. 4) Include persons with disabilities, persons with injuries and caregivers in psychosocial initiatives.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Death, physical (GBV, separations, disability, etc) and psychological trauma. People with physical, sensory as well as intellectual and psycho-social disabilities also face barriers in escaping and surviving armed conflict. Women with disabilities, already at a disproportionate risk for sexual violence during peace, are at an even greater risk of violence due to the insecurity of conflict.
