Project Summary

The proposed Activities targeting children under 5 and pregnant lactating women and communities. Health intervention will include PHC services (treatment of childhood illness, antenatal care, assisted deliveries, vaccination, provision of essential drugs and supplies, emergency preparedness and response in addition to building capacity of health staff in service delivery and monitoring implementations. Nutrition includes treatment of Acute Malnutrition and preventive services of IYCF, provision of nutrition supplies, training of volunteers, case identification and follow up, strengthen the community awareness of causes, danger signs and treatment of acute malnutrition, promoting positive health and nutrition behavior change. Under WASH, will rehabilitate facility latrines, water sources, provide cleaning materials and conduct home visits for hygiene promotion, provide water dispensers and hand washing facilities at health facilities in addition to improving medical waste management and infection prevention. SC integrated services delivery based on need and is inclusive and non-discriminatory. SC will ensure assistance is available to those who are most. The services provided will be respectful of cultural and religious backgrounds and will consider environment aspects. Whenever possible, health staff composition will reflect the gender, ethnic, and linguistic balance of the beneficiary population. Enforced policies prevent and respond to any breach of SC's Child Safeguarding Policy

Needs

According to HNO 2019 approximately 1.8 million IDPs and 324328 returnees are estimated target for among them 3.5 million People in need for nutrition support (2516 children under 5 and 1049 PLW). One in every three infants are not feeding with breastmilk exclusively. Children not receiving the minimum dietary diversity (72%). More than 59.3% do not receive age appropriate minimum meal frequency and almost 85 % of children 6-23 months do not receive recommended minimum acceptable diet. HNO 2018 estimated 5.2 million people with urgent humanitarian health needs, among them 820000 (401800 boys & 418200 girls) children U5 in to access to healthcare services and about 4million of the population are in need of WASH assistance. The results of MICS survey conducted in 2014 showed 38.2% of children under 5 years with acute malnutrition. Global Acute Malnutrition rate is above 15% in 11 states out of 18.Chronic malnutrition rate increased by 3% compare to 2010 MICS result. The conflict, economic crisis, inflation, and lack of access to markets has made many urban and rural households vulnerable to malnutrition especially IDPs. Poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices remain a major concern. During 2018 medical supplies’ imports has dropped by 36%, with consequent of increases in the price of lifesaving medicines, which affects the most vulnerable 5.5 million people in need of lifesaving assistance. The protracted crisis in Sudan and inadequate funding have had a major impact on healthcare. As per 2018 (HNO) about 36 per cent of the PHC facilities across Sudan are not fully functional. The chikungunya outbreak reported from Kassala State in August 2018 and in May seven States have been affected with a total of 13 978 cases. No death reported. Approximately 7% of the reported cases were children under 5 years of age and 60% were females. 13% of PiN across Sudan are in need of WASH emergency assistance, 87% of them are protracted IDPs, refugees and host communities. It is estimate that 4.9 million people do not have access to adequate drinking water and safe means of defecation and their hygiene practices do not improve health status. Poor access to safe water and sanitation services contributed to the outbreak of acute watery diarrhea in 2016 - 2018. The demand for water grows each year as rainfall fluctuates, water sources gradually declines especially in high-density areas of IDPs.SC will work through existing community structures, ensuring representation of marginalized and vulnerable groups emphasis on women's participation to minimize power dynamics and control for project activities. Feedback from beneficiaries (men, women) on project implementation will be consider to inform any adaptations required for future implementation. SCI will ensure men, women, boys and girls of the affected communities are motivated to participate in project activities related to Gender discrimination in their advocacy initiatives with the Government.

Activities

The project will respond to the basic needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities while increasing their self-reliance and improving their health, WASH and nutrition status and resilience. •Primary health care services (consultation, essential medicine, vaccination, antenatal care, postnatal care, assisted delivery, family planning and referral for emergency obstetric in addition to strengthening surveillance system) • Provision of lifesaving nutrition service for acute malnourished children (boys and girls 6-59 months of age) and PLWs .focusing on CMAM approach and promote infant and young child feeding in emergencies •Increase community access and knowledge to use safe water sanitation and hygiene promotion services . will use appropriate modality to meet the needs through in-kind and cash modality to purchase water from venders and to build their own latrines; provide incentives for volunteers that can be in cash /or In kind to support screening of U5 and referral to nutrition services. Will support referral from OTP to stabilization Centre through providing transportation fees for beneficiaries. SCI will undertake water quality monitoring, in addition to train community volunteers to promote hygiene •Will address the gender and age speciﬁc concerns as a crosscutting approach throughout the design ,implementation and capacity building and resilience mechanisms -SC will provide training for staff on scope of gender analysis, gender aspects and how women, girls, boys, and men are affected differently in different situations. Awareness sessions on gender aspects and increase the capacity to prevent abuse. The environmental issues will be considered through monitoring of water sources, waste management and sanitation as the main environmental issues affecting health and nutrition status of target communities.SC will coordinate and work closely with MOH, UN agencies, and partners.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project , and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities , prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The plan mitigation will through effectiveness of existing disposal mechanism as preventive measure for disease caused be insufficient or unsafe water supplies, poor drainage and uncontrolled diseases , Plan the location of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors. Healthcare and other forms of hazardous waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods including Sachet and cartons of RUTF/RUSF, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases. Water source will be project focused to determine sustainable levels of water use, an early assessment of the presence, quality, and quantity and recharge rate of groundwater sources will be done. Take care to locate latrines downstream of wells, at least 30m from groundwater sources and at least 1.5m above the water table as sanitation measures. Carefully will consider location of shelter and new building to minimize pollution and damage to sensitive natural environment. Appropriate storage of food supplies to Protect health of workers and food consumer (e.g. tablets used for fumigation) and food provision will have appropriate soilid waste management plan. Availability of Standards tools and guidelines, Standards, tools and guidance documents will be available to assist humanitarian responders in managing environmental impacts and risks.
