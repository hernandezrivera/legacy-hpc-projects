Project Summary

The project is going to tackle the livestock breeders’ hardships in having access to fodder in addition to quality veterinary services.
SSSD was able to identify the following needs through: Supported small-scale breeders across Syria during 2021, 2022 and 2023 through the distribution of more than 4,500 livestock (sheep/cow) with fodder, therefore, SSSD team has strong communications throughout the year with breeders and was able to understand their challenges and stresses Meetings with MAAR and its branches in Syrian governorates, SSSD team conducted field assessments in different governorates to verify and confirm FSA and ER&L needs and risks, through KII and FGD. Sector meetings and communication with FAO staff •Assessment findings were as the following: Lack of fodder because of high fodder prices in the market that are already under stress and supply chains bottlenecks The shortage of fuel and high prices for cattle, and sheep fodder, remain a challenge for livestock breeders Due to recent climate change and drought, having access to fodder is more difficult since less land is planted, less field residues exist and less grazing land is available Veterinary services are weak, and costly, resulting in extensive destocking Many breeders are forced to sell/slaughter their animals in order to buy fodder for the rest of the herd The government agriculture sector subsidized support is very limited and does not cover the needs of fodder The above-mentioned constraints have resulted in sharp increases in the prices of livestock products. SSSD had prioritized certain sub-districts in different Syrian governorates which are located in relatively dry zones and are in severe need (level 4 or 3) according to FSA sector severity score. The project will target small-scale breeders (sheep, goat and cows) in the following governorates: Idleb(Sanjar), Aleppo(Dayr Hafir), Hama(As suqaylbiyah,Shat-ha),Dar’a (Sheikh Misken) and Rural Damascus(Duma, Nashabiya).
Implementation of activities Obtaining the project approvals from governmental administrations. (SSSD already has an active approval to implement agriculture activities from MAAR, this approval is active till end of 2023 and majority of the targeted governorates are already included. SSSD can extend this approval in a meaningful time after communication with MAAR) Recruitment of project staff according to SSSD recruitment policy. Identification and assessment of potential beneficiaries in coordination with the local agriculture committees and branches of MAAR. Selection of beneficiaries based on the set criteria. The veterinarian will visit each beneficiary once every three weeks for six months once the final list of approved beneficiaries is set The vet services will be provided to all beneficiaries in the 5 targeted governorates; one vet will be recruited in each governorate. Procurement of kit’s items through tendering and according to SSSD procurement policy. Conducting an applied training (12 hours) for each targeted area in participation of selected livestock breeders to introduce hydroponic barely fodder growing. Distribution of inputs to the selected beneficiaries. Conducting PDM visits by the volunteers in coordination with M&E Officer. PDM impact assessment forms will be filled and recorded. Project final report will be submitted one month after the end of the project.
