Project Summary

Under the overall framework of support to basic primary care services, this project aims to provide pediatric and maternal health consultations for children and women in the high-risk areas and IDP camps inside Syria through fixed health points and medical mobile teams run by NGOs, Implementing Partners (IPs) and government health directorates, and provide them with the essential health supplies (emergency health kits, midwifery kits, paediatric kits, diarrhea kits, first aid kits, medicines and equipment) to treat the common maternal and childhood diseases and prevent deaths among the vulnerable children and women. It will also focus on raising awareness among vulnerable mothers and care-givers on prevention of maternal new-born and child diseases, the importance of timely and complete vaccinations, hygiene promotion, danger signs for ill child and ill new-born, high-risk pregnancies and the importance of antenatal care and facility delivery.
The project will support routine immunization program for children under the age of one year, and supplementary immunization activities for children under the age of five years.
The project will also provide capacity building for health staff and community volunteers to ensure quality service and compensate for the heavy loss of health workers during the 8 years of the conflict.

Needs

The crisis has significantly compromised the health system through destruction of healthcare infrastructure and loss of skilled healthcare professionals. The most recent Health Resource Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) estimates of Sep. 2018 indicates that 46% of hospitals and primary health facilities in Syria are partially functional or not functional. Access is further limited by various administrative and procedural requirements by approving local authorities in many other areas.Number of people in need of health assistance in Syria is estimated at 13.2 million, with females constituting 72% of the PiN. Vulnerable groups include 5.2 million women of reproductive age and 2.2 million children under 5 years old. The Health sector recognizes other population groups as the most vulnerable, including people living with disabilities, IDPs & returnees, and Palestinian refugees. People living in UN-declared hard to reach areas (8%) and highest severity areas (severity scale 4-6) constitute a particularly vulnerable group. It is estimated that close to 5.7 million people are living in severity areas of 4 and above.Populations and returnees, particularly those in northeast and northwest Syria, are vulnerable to outbreaks of infectious diseases due to unsanitary living conditions, overstretched health services, and low coverage of routine immunization. Thirty-seven per cent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services.The HNO further gives more details of the magnitude of the access challenges across the country. The national ratio of fully functional Primary Healthcare Centers of 2.28 per 50,000, while above the Sphere Standards, is highly unequal, with 101 of 270 surveyed sub-districts reporting having no fully functional PHC. In terms of geographical access challenges, the average reported travel time by car to the nearest PHC facility was reported to be 25 minutes, with a range of 5 minutes (Damascus) to over 120 minutes (32 communities in Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, and Deir-ez-Zor). Similarly, the availability of doctors, nurses and midwives in the public sector is below international standards, with only 2.44 health staff per 1,000 population available, against the Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) minimum standard of 4.45 per 1,000 population.Thirty-seven per cent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services. Regarding specific services for children, about 577,219 newborns will require routine immunization across Syria, with an estimated 320,000 children between 13 and 59 months of age not fully vaccinated during their first years of life according to WHO/UNICEF estimates. Routine Expanded Programme of Immunization (EPI) coverage rates of Penta 3 vaccination in children under 1 have only reached about 70% of children in 2019, with very low achievements in Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Aleppo governorates essentially due to access challenges. The risk of other vaccine preventable diseases such as measles remain high, with Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb alone reporting over three-quarters of the 22,656-suspected measles and rubella cases between Jan-Sept 2018.WHO Surveillance reports in 2018 (EWARS, EWARN) have further illustrated the fragile situation facing vulnerable populations with rampant outbreaks of infectious diseases such as leishmaniosis, typhoid fever, diarrheal diseases etc during 2018. This risk of outbreaks of communicable diseases is expected to remain high in 2019.Other specialized services for vulnerable women and children, including pediatrics, skilled delivery and newborn care, non-communicable diseases care and laboratory services remain in short supply across the board and will need to be strengthened and supported. Continuous professional development of health professionals was limited over the past seven years which affected the quality of health services provided.

Protection Risk Analysis

1.Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: -The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers persists. UNICEF is committed to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.Mitigation measures:- Explain and enforce UNICEF PSEA policy for all implementing partners, with zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse and explain accountability measures. - Establishment of community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - In built and regular awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training and monitoring on the topic. 2.Gender Based Violence: - The risk of GBV is heightened in many of the humanitarian situations across the country. UNICEF health interventions support the in-country response through: Mitigation measures:- UNICEF will work closely with other partners such as UNFPA to ensure as much as possible the availability of the full complement of training, skills and services for health workers in facilities benefitting from UNICEF support.- UNICEF has an active policy of promoting the recruitment of female health workers through its implementing partners at the facility and community levels, crucial to improve access and utilization of services for vulnerable women. 3.The relegation of Community Engagement and Participation: - Despite access challenges in most hard to reach areas, UNICEF promotes community engagement and participation to the extent possible. This potential risk shall be minimized throughout the cycle of programming. Mitigation measures:- The presence of 4 Field Offices and Cross border team and operations covering Idleb and inaccessible areas within Syria ensures a closer presence in the field. In areas where UNICEF staff are unable to access, remote programming options through Third Party Field monitors help to promote and verify community engagement and participation.- On a more limited scale, UNICEF programs also receive feedback by working with and through adolescents in a number of communities and engage with this age group through innovative information technology approaches.- The Communication for Development section (C4D) of UNICEF is involved in the production and dissemination of key messages to ensure affected communities have access to information needed to receive humanitarian assistance and provide feedback. 4.Exclusion of Vulnerable Groups and inequitable Access to Health Services: - The principle of equitable access to health care, targeting vulnerable groups is at the core of UNICEF programming principles. UNICEF programs will be concentrated in high severity (4-6) areas, and target women and children with the core interventions of PHC defined by local authorities and the UNICEF Global strategic plan. The risk of exclusion of vulnerable groups will be minimized by:Mitigation measures: - Utilization of sector approved vulnerability assessments for planning.- Delivering the full scope of integrated preventive and curative interventions.- Collection of dis-aggregated data for monitoring by gender, age and location (IDP camps, host communities, Palestinian communities etc) to guard against exclusion.5. Duty of care for health workers: - The rapidly changing conflict lines during 2018 have highlighted the need for measures to ensure implementing procedures.Mitigation measures: -UNICEF has established policies and administrative procedures to ensure duty of care for workers in incidents of rapidly changing lines of control. These measures were implemented in 2018 in the south of Syria.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Violence against health facilities and health care providers: - This is a continuing risk in some areas of operation as available data at the sector level shows.Mitigating measures: - UNICEF maintains internal mechanisms to minimize exposure of partners receiving programming support in high-risk zones. - UNICEF will support sector-wide measures such as reporting on attacks against healthcare and advocacy measures to support the protections issues facing health workers across Syria.
