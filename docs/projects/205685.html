1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face severe health crisis due to multiple factors. About 8.9 million people in the country need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in the counties most affected by floods, conflicts, IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons), returnees and disease outbreaks. The health system in South Sudan is unable to cope with the emergencies and provide life-saving services. It relies heavily on international aid and faces many challenges, such as lack of funding, staff, medicines, and equipment. The people suffer from hunger, violence, and poor health. Women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities are the most vulnerable groups. They have limited access to health care and face high risks of death and illness. They also need support from other sectors, such as nutrition, water, sanitation, hygiene, and food security. They also need community health services and engagement to bridge the gaps in accessing health care. According to the data collected by WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA World Bank Group and UNDESA/Population Division, South Sudan has a maternal mortality ratio of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births – one of the world's highest maternal mortality ratios (MMR) (MMEIG, 2022) . The MMR translated into 3,790 maternal deaths in 2020, about 10 deaths daily. Available projections indicate that the leading direct causes of maternal mortality were postpartum haemorrhage (16 per cent of cases), obstructed/prolonged labour (13.3 per cent) and antepartum haemorrhage (12.9 per cent) (Ministry of Health, 2013). A shortage of human resources and skills, referral constraints, financial constraints, challenges with availability and last-mile distribution of supplies, floods, and insecurity characterizes the weak health system at both facility and community levels.As of 5 November 2023, 362,355 people were recorded crossing the border from Sudan to South Sudan since the start of the conflict in April. Of these, 83 per cent are South Sudanese, whereas 15 per cent are Sudanese. South Sudan’s health system is unable to cope with, respond to and provide essential life-saving health services in humanitarian emergencies, as it relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance. Limited functionality of health facilities, capacity and poor coverage, including recent closures of health facilities due to reduced funding, have made it difficult to meet increasing health needs worsened by floods, food insecurity and conflict. Women, children, the elderly and PWD are particularly vulnerable to the limited access to health care. The lack of medicine and staff as well as equipment transportation cost, insecurity, floods, long distance to health facility and high cost of health care are the main barriers to accessing health services. Inadequate health workers, limited supplies, long wait time, water, maternal health services and lack of follow up care were most reported.The above challenges are are prominent in Twic, Renk, Ulang and Kajo keji counties, with the worst health indicators and frequent disease outbreaks. GOAL is proposing through this project to provide critical essential life saving interventions to address the gaps highlighted above in the four Counties. With special focus on the weak capacity of frontline healthcare providers at the primary health care level but also supporting the secondary facilities in these counties to be able to provide services that are referred to them from the primary. GOAL will work closely with CHDs in all four Counties and integrate these services with other sectors. Through this effort GOAL is seeking to reach 229,174 people most of them people by crisis with critical health services. This includes people who are displaced within the country, refugees from neighboring countries, and recent returnees from Sudan. They need interventions to prevent and respond to disease outbreaks and other public health emergencies.

2. Project activities

GOAL is proposing the following activities which will contribute towards achieving the Health Cluster objectives and MOH priorities: Support to EPI County departments to expand vaccination including cold chain maintenance, and outreach team to reach hard to reach communities. Support the improvement of waste management, including the construction of incinerators in PHCCs, PHUs and Hospitals. Support infection prevention and control through the provision of supplies, capacity building of staff, improving the WASH in health facilities.Support last mile delivery of Vaccines and other medical commodities and supplies. Capacity building of health care staff and in critical skills to be able to provide RMNCAH services. Support RCCE at the County level to conduct community engagement as part of emergency preparedness, during crisis and or disease outbreaks and during the recovery period to prevent relapse. Strengthen community based event surveillance and the timely investigation of any alerts of public health importance Procure and distribute emergency drug supplies to PHCCs and PHCUs as to provide life saving treatment to conflict affected population. Support CHD to develop EP&RPs and also to set up early warning systems. Support County level coordination efforts in the 4 Counties. Conduct joint monitoring and supervision to affected communities and service delivery points Conduct outreaches/ support mobile clinics to reach vulnerable population were they are.
