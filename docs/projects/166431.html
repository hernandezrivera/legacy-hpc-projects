Project Summary

On the 30th of January 2020, the Director General of the World Health Organization (WHO) declared the outbreak of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) a Public Health Emergency of International Concern; on the 11th March 2020 COVID-19 was declared as a global pandemic. Similarly, on the 27th February 2020 the Federal Ministry of Health of Nigeria announced the confirmation of the first case of COVID-19 in Lagos State, Nigeria. In the same communication the Minister of Health announced the immediate activation of the National Emergency operations Center by the Multi-sectoral Coronavirus Preparedness Group led by the Nigeria Center for Disease Control (NCDC). Since then, in a period of 2 months, Nigeria has recorded more than 2,800 cases across the country, with close to 100 cases recorded in Northeast Nigeria. The cases have been on the upward trend in Northeast Nigeria since the index case was identified in the month of March. Borno State has recorded the highest number of cases in the Northeast Nigeria States. NCDC updates COVID 19 incidences, morbidity and case fatality trends on a daily basis.
In response to the COVID 19 pandemic, UNICEF has worked closely with nutrition sector partners to develop a nutrition sector COVID-19 response strategy that seeks to;
• Reduce morbidity and mortality due to COVID-19 among children aged below 5 years, pregnant and lactating women and nutrition service providers in North East Nigeria.
• Mitigate the negative impact of COVID 19 on the nutritional status of children, pregnant and lactating women and nutrition service providers well-being
• Ensure the continuation of safe and high quality delivery of nutrition interventions.
To address the high burden of malnutrition and to prevent further malnutrition incidence among children, UNICEF, in line with nutrition sector COVID-19 response strategy; will support the design and implementation of COVID 19 sensitive nutrition programming in Northeast Nigeria for the collective nutrition response. In line with the governing global and national normative guidance, UNICEF will provide technical leadership and support to in-cooperate COVID-19 Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures in the community mobilization strategy of the Community based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) programme. This is in a bid to ensure active case finding and referral for children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) continues to be done at scale; in an effective, timely and safe manner. UNICEF Nutrition section in collaboration with the Communication for Development section will support the designing and appropriate use of COVID-19 specific Behavior Change Communication (BCC) Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials; which will strengthen community mobilization and sensitization activities for delivery of skilled COVID-19 specific optimum Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) promotional services. Technical support will be provided to COVID proof the SAM treatment protocol in both Out-patient Therapeutic Care (OTP) and the Stabilization Centers (SCs), and for effective treatment of SAM cases with confirmed COVID 19 disease in COVID-19 case management centers. UNICEF will support equipping SCs with necessary IPC materials including supporting the availability of dedicated COVID-19 isolation centers in all SCs. UNICEF will provide technical support through physical/ virtual//on-the-job trainings to nutrition sector implementing agencies, Borno State Primary Health Care Development Agency, Yobe State Primary Health Care Management Board and the Hospital Management Board .To ensure optimum nutrition service coverage, enhance quality and timely nutrition programme reporting and adherence to normative guidance for implementing COVID-19 nutrition responsive programme, UNICEF in collaboration with relevant State authorities will support the overall coordination and information management for the collective nutrition response.

1 - Needs

From the 8th round of Emergency Food Security and Nutrition Surveillance (EFSNS) conducted in October 2019, approximately 255,619 children aged below 5 years will suffer from with SAM in 2020, a condition associated with a 12-fold increased risk of death if not timely treated. This situation has been further aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic; whose incidence and case fatality rates have been on the increase in Northeast Nigeria since confirmation of the index case in the month of March. The nutrition sector estimates that an additional 77,000 children aged below 5 years, will suffer from SAM as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. To adhere to COVID-19 IPC measures, household visits undertaken by Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs) have been restricted as part of COVID 19 Infection Control and Prevention (IPC) measures. The nutrition sector identified the ‘mother MUAC approach’ as an alternative approach. The sector has projected that a total of 250,000 mothers and care-takers require trainings to be able to correctly and timely identify children suffering from SAM in their own households and accompany them to the nearest SAM treatment facility for treatment. As per the sector COVID-19 response the SCs will cater for SAM cases with medical complications not related to COVID-19 and/or SAM cases that have not been in contact with confirmed or suspected COVID 19 cases. Children having SAM with suspected COVID-19 disease will first be isolated in the isolation units of SCs and referred for testing and treatment in COVID -9 case management Centers where they will be treated until full recovery. All SCs require dedicated COVID- 19 isolation center to avoid cross-infection, since children having SAM are immunocompromised; and therefore, at high risk of contracting communicable diseases and other infections. Since not all COVID-19 cases are symptomatic, all OTPs and SCs will be supported and equipped to implement standard IPC protocols outlined in the COVID-19 response strategy. Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) is an integral component of SAM treatment, and accounts for approximately 70% of the total costs incurred to treat a SAM case until full recovery. An additional 77,000 cartons of RUTF are required to cater for the additional projected caseload. This will be procured and at least 2 months’ supply pre-positioned at Local Government Area (LGA) level to avoid pipeline break due to on-going movement restrictions as part of IPC measures. Additionally, approximately 5-10% of the total SAM cases are expected to present with medical complications, therefore therapeutic milks (F75 and F100) will also be procured and pre-positioned at LGA level. Exclusive Breastfeeding (EB) infants less than 6 months of age has been proven to protect them against diarrhea and common childhood illnesses such as pneumonia and may also have longer-term health benefits for the mother and child, such as reducing the risk of overweight and obesity in childhood and adolescence. The presence of severe COVID-19 disease among infants below 6 months or lactating women is expected to disrupt EB practices, in such cases replacement feeding will be explored to save the life of the infant. The use of Breast Milk Substitute, preferably Ready to Use Infant Formula (RUIF) will be used to provide skilled IYCF support to infants and mothers affected by COVID-19; in compliance with the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes. UNICEF as the Sector technical lead for nutrition response will strengthen the technical capacities of implementing partners and relevant State nutrition authorities for delivery of timely, comprehensive and high quality services at scale. UNICEF will leverage on her position as a major stakeholder to ensure that coordination meetings continue to happen and participate actively in the decision making processes ensuring that all decisions arrived at are of high technical value and evidence based.

2 - Activities

To adequately respond to the needs mentioned above, UNICEF in collaboration with the relevant State authorities and implementing partners will undertake the below activities • Provide physical (life-saving and in adherence to COVID-19 IPC measures) training, virtual trainings and technical support to; Community nutrition mobilizers, nutrition service providers and LGA facilitators on self-protection, Infection Prevention and Control, SAM referrals and COVID 19 messaging. The CNMs working closely with the Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWS) will be trained to identify COVID-19 basic signs and symptoms and refer suspected cases with COVID to nearest testing center for confirmation. For confirmed cases, the CNMS will support contact tracing at community level as will be required. Nutrition sector Implementing Partners and health workers and including local personnel in COVID 19 Nutrition in Emergencies programming.  Mother and caregivers of children aged below 5 months on “Mother MUAC”, an alternative approach to active case finding to promote self-household malnutrition screening and referral of identified SAM cases. Health workers on the provision of replacement feeding including provision of Breast Milk substitutes as a last resort and its monitoring to prevent infant morbidity and mortality as a result of compromised feeding; for cases of COVID 19 infant/maternal deaths and/or severe sickness. Health workers on SAM treatment protocol in COVID 19 case management centers, including remote technical support in locations with restricted movement to ensure delivery of high quality nutrition services. • In collaboration with Communication for Development (C4D) section, develop and disseminate COVID 19 Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials at all nutrition service sites and at community level to strengthen IYCF promotion and encourage safe hygiene practices. Close collaboration with WASH will be done to increase safe hand-washing practices and toilet facilities at nutrition sites. More awareness on hygiene promotion through Community Nutrition mobilizers (CNMs), local radio/FM and nutrition staffs. UNICEF will support procurement and prepositioning of essential hygiene related supplies alongside the IEC materials.• Provide skilled breastfeeding support to infants below 6 months who have been infected by COVID 19 or whose mother have been infected or died from COVID 19. Breast Milk Substitutes will be procured and issued to non-breastfed infants in compliance with the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes when re-lactation and wet nursing options are completely not feasible.• To effectively address the additional needs for children suffering from SAM as a result of infant or maternal COVID 19 disease/deaths, Ready to Use Therapeutic Food and Therapeutic Milks will be procured and prepositioned at strategic locations. • Facility-based screening of all persons visiting nutrition service sites will be intensified by doing temperature checks at entry points and administering questionnaire to check for COVID-19 contact tracing and symptoms.• UNICEF will leverage on existing coordination structure to support the coordination and information management function for COVID 19 response through intensifying virtual meetings at State and LGA levels.
