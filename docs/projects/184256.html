Project Summary

Education sector continues to face challenges despite the efforts made by the MoE and other actors in the country. Only an estimated 1.8M children accounting for 45% of the total school aged children in the country are enrolled in schools, only 17% of Internally Displaced Children access education. It is estimated that 3M (45% girls) crisis- affected children will be in need of education come 2022. of these children, 2,419,059 are from non-displaced families and 667,891 are from displaced families while children with disabilities account for 463,042. The high number of children in need is attributed to rise in population, triple shocks of droughts, floods and COVID-19 pandemic.
In 2021, 214,000 (40%) were displaced as a result of conflict. Estimates suggest that 408,000 children (45% girls) were displaced as result of the ongoing droughts. It is expected that the drought conditions will persist through to March 2022. In Middle Shabelle, over 12 schools were closed disrupting schooling for more than 6,000 learners. The impact of COVID-19 on household financial abilities has led to drop in children’s attendance and retention in schools. The trend is likely to persist in 2022 as household resources continue to dwindle. Further, schools are expected continue experiencing challenges of meeting requirements for a COVID-19-preventive environment i.e. access to WASH, disinfection protocols and safe distancing.
These crises will likely expose children to various protection threats and risks which include recruitment into armed groups, sexual violence, abduction, child labor and early marriage. According to the UN SG Report on Children and Armed Conflicts 2021, in 2020 1,716 children (61 girls) in Somalia were recruited and/ or used by non-state armed groups, 406 children (400 girls) reported sexual violence and 1,430 children (82 girls) were abducted. In addition, 825 boys and 262 girls were killed and maimed with most child casualties resulting from crossfire, targeted killings, aerial bombardments, IEDs and suicide attacks. Of this, Hirshabelle one of the target states of this project recorded 296(66 girls) grave violation against children.
According to DSA 2021, low education services in IDPS can be attributed to lack of government support, and inadequate and short-term funding to support affected IDP school-aged children. The situation is further exacerbated for CWDs by prevalent negative attitudes towards them by teachers, parents, and structural challenges that hinder these children to access an inclusive learning environment.
To improve retention there is need to: provide safe water, food, teacher incentives, deployment of low-cost technology to address challenges of temporary and long term closure of schools. To ensure more girls are not only enrolled in schools but also retained, there is need to address barriers that hinder their access to education. To this end, protective environment will be provided at schools for example presence of female teachers as role models, provision of gender segregated latrines and provision menstrual hygiene kits.
Further there is dire need to improve child protection services in schools. Safety Audit carried out in 2020 revealed 85% of schools assessed lacked disability friendly learning facilities, 77% of CP Focal points had no training on PSS and GBV while 41% of KI interviewed said routes to schools were unsafe and only 15% of girls said they had access to Menstrual Hygiene facilities.
Central to the proposed project will be protection of 6,058 (2,787 girls and 3,271 boys) learners enrolled in targeted schools of whom 909 (418 girls) are CDWs, particularly IDP children who are vulnerable to risks such as child labor, SGBV, early marriages etc. Robust measures be put in place to ensure services provided are non-discriminative and inclusive those in need particularly CWDs and girls. To achieve this, gender parity will be ensured, structures built will be disability and girl child friendly.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

At risk of exclusion are children with disability. Aid Vision will strongly advocate for for and enforce the equal access to education and protection services, regardless of gender, social status, disability, or economic situation of a child. Also at risk of exclusion are children from minority who may be locked in decision making by predominant communities. Aid Vision pay attention to this ensure such communities cushioned from exclusion and are present at decision making table. Aid Vision will negotiate with the local authorities and community leaders to ensure children’s equitable access to education, especially girls and those living with disabilities. Where practicable, the project will advocate for 50-50 gender representation in all positions of leadership in the project, for example: teachers, CECs, Protection Focal Points etc. The other strata of the community like representation of the youth and elderly will also be considered for community capacity building and awareness sessions.
