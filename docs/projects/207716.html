Project Summary

According to OCHA’s “Situational Baseline, 31 November 2023” there are more than 1, 306, 768 IDPs, 658, 544 returnees a as well as an increasing number of victims on the military side, non-state actors and civil level as well. The current context in the NWSW regions remains dynamic and is characterized by continued displacement of families leading to food and nutrition insecurity.
About 508, 480 food insecure people have lost their access to farmlands, assets and livelihoods in the NWSW regions. The result of this is presented in the October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé analysis that shows an average of about 22% of the population in NW/SW applying negative livelihood coping mechanisms. Food insecurity is worsening because of the war between Ukraine and Russia Crisis that has led to drastically increased input prices especially seeds and fertilizer. For the first time ever all 13 divisions are classified as under “pressior” phase”.
According to the recent Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) of Septembre 2023, food security represents represented the highest need. Due to lockdowns and ghost towns access to markets and services become more and more challenging for the population and for humanitarian partners. About 80 % of farms in remote locations remain inaccessible and sometimes crops are abandoned. Due to insecurity, most people have moved from rural areas (where there is land) to cities where even space for home gardens is very limited. There are some functioning local markets with partial access to regional markets
According to the IOM Labor Market Survey 2022 , there is therefore a need to scale up agricultural production and further build up the local capacity to transform and process raw material that could eventually contribute to local economic development.
The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic restricted usual execution of agricultural extension services and feeding the markets with agricultural products. Direct access to extension services for farmers was limited due to travel restrictions and banning of public gatherings. Both lead to an increase in food prices and agricultural resource prices. The war between Ukraine and Russia has worsen food supply chain like the supply with fertilizer. This in turn has a major impact on food demand and consequently leads to food insecurity with a great effect on the most vulnerable people. The combination of the war in Ukraine, the COVID 19 pandemic and the crisis in the North-West and South-West regions has a tremendous economic impact on agricultural activities on IDPs, Persons living with Disabilities (PwD) and host communities.
The proposed one-year intervention will contribute to strengthening livelihoods and reducing the vulnerability of crisis-affected IDPs and host communities in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, especially women, men, children and PwD, through the provision of agricultural support. Specifically, the intervention aims (i) to increase access and utilization of diverse and nutritious vegetables, (ii) to increase access and consumption of eggs, (iii) to increase access and utilization of mushrooms, (iv) to increase access and utilization of snails. Furthermore, 75 % of the beneficiaries selected will be reached by FAO and WFP. Implementing partners organizations will take care of the remaining 25% of the beneficiaries.
