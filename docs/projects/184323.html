Project Summary

In the context of the current multi-faceted emergencies (increased risks of flooding, protracted conflict, droughts, Covid 19, Cholera/AWD Outbreaks, Alight proposes this emergency WASH project to target IDPs and most vulnerable host communities to save lives, restoring access to basic wash services, and reducing morbidities associated with WASH-related diseases. The project will target worst – affected districts such as Kismayo, Benadir, Afmadow, lascanod, Telex, Badade , and Xudun.
Alight understands that droughts and protracted conflicts are the key drivers of internal displacements and also the humanitarian needs in Somalia. Many IDPs and Poor vulnerable Host communities are currently residing in Urban and pre-urban informal crowded areas with insufficient access to water and Sanitation. According to JMCNA assessment done in August by REACH, 53% of the IDPs face challenges in terms of access to WASH services in Somalia. The situation of IDPs and non- IDPs vulnerable host community members are complicated by frequently forced evictions meted on them leading to disruptions of access to critical WASH services and loss of gains made on access to these essential services. According to JMCNA 2021, 39 per cent of vulnerable people in Somalia do not have access to sustained, improved and safe water sources for drinking and other domestic uses, while 84 per cent still walk for more than 15 minutes to the nearest water source. Due to these constraints, one in five Somalis does not have enough water per day to cover their basic needs for both drinking and domestic use.
Cognizant of these contexts, Alight will provide emergency water trucking to 12000 (2000HH) drought-affected IDPs and vulnerable pastoral communities living in drought-prone areas of Lascanod, Xudun, Telex and Afmadow districts for two months as the agency will find and rehabilitate alternative water sources for the affected people. 12 critical shallow wells will be rehabilitated and protected targeting IDPs and Non- IDPs vulnerable communities and settlements from Kismayo, Kaxda & wartanabada (Banadir), Lascanod, Telex. Alight will also conduct Disinfections and chlorination of floods affected wells.
Alight will Provide 10 Strategic boreholes in drought-prone locations in Lascanood, Telex, and Xudun and Afmadow district with generators, submersible pumps, cable wires and control panels to ensure drought affected peoples have access to safe, adequate and reliable water.
ARC will establish 6 Pipeline water extensions to identified IDPs Camps and non- IDPs camps including the construction of elevated water storage tanks, installation of solar-powered systems and water kiosk with a minimum of 6 taps to ensure increased access to adequate water by the IDPs and Non-IDPs. Alight will construct/rehabilitate 600 gender-segregated, disability-friendly communal latrines for IDPs and vulnerable host community members who have no access to any sanitation facilities. Alight will construct sex-Disaggregated, Disability Inclusive 40 Institutional latrines with water and Handwashing facilities for 24 target schools and 15 health centres.
Hygiene promotion will remain a key component of the intervention to prevent the spread of Covid-19 and other WASH-related diseases through risk communications and community engagement promoting behaviour changes. 2000 Hygiene kits and basic MHM kits will be provided to adolescent girls in schools and villages/camps. Alight will target approximately 48, 000 beneficiaries from North and south-central Somalia (7488 women, 5823 men, 12165 boys, 12165 girls, 9201 disabled, and 1158 elderly people). Alight will consult men, boys, girls, elderly, and people with disabilities on their needs and preference regarding the type, Design and location of the water and sanitation facilities to address barriers relating to access. Alight will involve women, boys, girls and disabled people in the project monitoring and their feedback will be sought.

1. - Projects activities

Alight will provide emergency water trucking support to 12000(2000HH) drought- affected IDPs and vulnerable pastoral communities living in drought-prone areas of lascanod, Xudun, Telex and Afmadow districts for a period of two months. The support will be delivered through water vouchers and each beneficiary will be provided with a minimum of 7.5 liters p/person /day. Alight will rehabilitate 10 Strategic boreholes in drought-prone locations with provisions of Generators, submersible pumps, control panels, storage tanks and animal troughs to improve access to safe, adequate, and reliable water for the affected populations. In additions for IDPs and urban poor host communities living in major IDP camps like in Benadir ( Kaxda, Deynile) and Dalxiska and Galbeet IDP camps in Kismayo, Alight will rehabilitate /establish piped water extensions from 6 strategic water sources to bring water closer and to provide reliable and sustained water access . This includes construction of elevated storage tanks, piped water installations and constructions/ rehabilitations of water kiosks.Alight will also conduct protection and shock chlorination of 12 floods-affected shallow wells in Lower juba region. Point of use water treatment and distribution of Aquatabs and Water Makers will be distributed to targeted households to secure their drinking water and prevent AWDs outbreaks. Alight will establish Gender balanced water management committees for the targeted water points to ensure continuity of services, appropriate operation, and maintenance as well as strengthening women participation in the WASH projectOn Emergency sanitation, Alight will construct 600 gender segregated and disability inclusive communal latrines with hand washing facilities and other protective and privacy features such as lockable doors from inside, solid walls etc. targeting those with no access to any latrines e.g. new IDP arrivals and evicted IDPs. Alight will also construct/rehabilitate 40 gender segregated and institutional sanitation facilities for 24 targeted schools and 15 health facilities. Alight will consult men, boys, girls, elderly and people with disabilities on their needs and preference regarding the type, Design and location of the water and sanitation facilities to address barriers relating to access e.g. providing access ramps, wide doors and hand rails in latrines for households with disability members. Alight will involve women, boys, girls and disabled in the monitoring activities and their feedback sought to improve future response.Alight will distribute 2000 hygiene kits and basic MHM kits to most vulnerable community members ( 12,000 people) including adolescent girls in schools and villages/ camps and will promote positive behavior changes in affected communities through conducting hygiene campaigns, household visits, conducting dramas and community trainings. Where market conditions allows, Alight will deliver some of the hygiene kits interventions and the latrines constructions works through MBP approaches e.g. use of commodity vouchers in the delivery of key hygiene items and sanitation interventions in areas like Benadir and Kismayo. The Hygiene kits will be provided on quarterly basis for one year.Alight will be coordinating very closely with line ministries and WASH and other relevant actors in the project implementation.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project has proposed use of MBP Programming approach particularly use of Commodity vouchers in the delivery of hygiene kits and sanitations materials to targeted beneficiaries. Depending on the activity, Each beneficiary will be given a voucher containing a list of essential hygiene items like soap, jerycan, basins, pads or latrine construction materials like cement, sand, iron sheets, timber, nails etc. and information on where to redeem the vouchers and the frequency of access etc., Alight will identify suitable local vendors from the local markets, accessible to the beneficiaries where they can redeem their vouchers.The voucher for latrine constructions will be one – off support while those of the hygiene kit provisions will be done on quarterly basis for a period of 12 months. Alight will do post distribution monitoring after every cycle to seek feedback on the process, quality of the material accessed, and the satisfaction level of the beneficiaries.The justication for the use of the vouchers over in-kind is that vouchers will help in stimulating demand for hygiene and sanitation materials by the targeted beneficiaries. By giving vouchers to the beneficiaries to solely access the specified items, beneficiaries will get the chance to link directly with the local traders, and encouraging the local traders to stock more of the demanded supplies. Furthermore, the use of voucher directly delivered to the beneficiaries is faster and logistically easier as it removes times for procurements, less prone to diversion than in Kind distributions. On security aspects, vouchers has relatively minimal security risks compared to in- kind distributions on the part of the beneficiaries as well as the agency staffs. Alight will be working in big towns (Banadir, Kismayo, lascanod, telex) and other smaller locations outside the big towns, where availability of the essential hygiene items, sanitation materials and market functionality, inter-connectedness, and inter- dependency of the markets are guaranteed as informed by recent REACH Market needs assessment in the country. Alight will work closely with other actors on the ground and government agencies involved in the IDPS affairs supports especially on targeting and registration of beneficiaries to minimize risks of exclusions errors. Alight also has the capacity and the experience to do the voucher based response since it has implemented similar project in Afmadow in 2020-2021 SHF funded WASH Project where it successfully used community vouchers in the delivery of latrines construction intervention to IDP families. The proposed approach is therefore to replicate the same approach in other districts to build more evidences for the approach.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Alight has conducted an initial market assessment (conducted interviews of key traders and beneficiaries) at the proposed sites before deciding on the use of market-based programming. Alight has also reviewed the recent joint market assessment report done by REACH in Somalia and has found out that essential hygiene items and construction materials are locally available in major markets and beneciaries can access them if the given support in terms of the vouchers supports . Alight has also established that markets are integrated, inter-connected, and functional in the proposed target areas (Mogadishu, Kismayo, Sool, and Sanaag). In the event that, certain locations will have no functional markets and therefore not feasible to do the voucher based support, Alight will be flexible to switch to in- kind based distribution. Alight will conduct continuous monitoring of the local markets and collect information related to pricing change, effects of seasonality, inflation etc. which might affect the suitability of the preferred mode of delivery.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

ARC has conducted an analysis of the protection risks that potentially threaten ARC’s ability to provide assistance that is impartial, does not endanger or harm beneciaries, allows for feedback to be incorporated into the program monitoring, plan and promoteRequest blocked due to invalid security credentials Client or key not accepted hidAuthUser×community empowerment. The rst protection risk facing the target population is the risk of being targeted for attacks by armed groups or explosions due to association with organizations receiving a grant from donors or perceived their enemies. All beneciaries are vulnerable to this risk. In order to minimize this risk, ARC will operate in a low prole with minimal or no visibility in such a context where such risk is imminent. ARC will ensure that the aid delivered is seen as ‘Somalis helping Somalia’ by supporting teams from the local community. The second main protection risk is the risk of beneciaries being asked to provide sex in exchange for goods and services. The most vulnerable group to this risk is women, especially girls and young women. ARC believes the risk is low but recognizes the problem of sexual exploitation and abuse exists within humanitarian spaces, and must therefore take all measures to prevent its occurrence on the part of its staff, partners, vendors, and other associates. ARC has a fully adapted Policy on Sexual Harassment, Exploitation, and Abuse (PSEA) and has operationalized PSEA throughout its recruitment and procurement practices. ARC requires all partners and contractors, to sign its Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff and consultants at the time of offer letter so that the contracts are not valid without a signed code of conduct in place. The third main protection risk is exclusion, denial of assistance or services, or other factors that render accessing the services dangerous or impossible. Those who are most vulnerable to this threat are older persons, persons with disabilities, children, women and unaccompanied youth. ARC will ensure that the WASH assistance provided across all the project supported water facilities are accessible to these vulnerable populations by targeting these groups within needs assessments, monitoring patient data to ensure that all age and gender groups are represented and persons with disabilities are being served, providing the aforementioned complaints mechanism, and assessing the water points and their locations to ensure that physical and security challenges do not prevent access for any population group. The 4th risk is the exclusion of service due to gender affecting particularly the women. ARC is incorporating gender mainstreaming throughout. With previous institutional experience implementing WASH programming, ARC is acutely aware of how women and girls face different challenges than men and boys in the current context. To this end, ARC will worked tirelessly with project staff in all program locations to ensure the inclusion of female hygiene promoters in the assessment and in the implementation of WASH projects so that special interests and views of women are given special attention throughout the response period. 5th risk is a lack of feedback Mechanism or inability to report concerns. ARC uses the real-time beneciary feedback system, Kuja Kuja. Kuja Kuja is a tool to allow ARC capture the ‘pulse’ of customer satisfaction with services and to allow ARC service teams, technical teams, and support functions both at the local and global levels to understand real-time issues being faced, see opportunities for immediate and strategic improvement to service deliver
