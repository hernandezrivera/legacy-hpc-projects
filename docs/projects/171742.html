Project Summary

Conflict, natural disasters and COVID19 continue to deter Somalia from making significant development strides to benefit the most vulnerable population. The humanitarian context remains fragile for millions of Somalis and protection issues affecting children have increased. According to the protection cluster 5.9 million people need humanitarian assistance. At least 1.8million are in need of critical protection services. Children are the most affected due to exposure to extreme violence which includes recruitment and use by parties to the conflict and killing and maiming. A trend analysis by the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting shows that sexual violence increased by 35% when compared to the same period last year. Killing and maiming increased by 77% during the first 6 months of 2020 when compared to the same period in 2019. Displacement due to conflict, droughts and famine have triggered separation of children from their families and exposed them to exploitation, violence and abuse. Most families and children have resorted to negative copying mechanism. Boys and girls continue to endure multiple protection risk such as family separation, sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), child recruitment, mental and psychosocial distress. IDPs and minorities including children with disabilities remain particularly vulnerable. Integrated community-based prevention and response services that address the needs of vulnerable families and survivors of violence remain a key priority for child protection agencies in Somalia throughout 2021

Mitigation Measures

Protection Risks and Mitigation Measures1.Risk of women and girls being exposed to Sexual Violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse and partners not having sufficient internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safe guarding and PSEA. Mitigating measures: The risk to survivors of further stigmatization, abuse or isolation is high even when they access professional support services. In order to mitigate against this risk all partners will undergo mandatory PSEA and Child Safeguarding training and sign a code of conduct as testimony to protect beneficiaries from abuse. PSEA and child safeguarding focal points will be identified and their roles institutionalized. UNICEF will ensure partners apply fundamental principles in the delivery of services such as absolute confidentiality (in both consultation as well as record keeping); providing survivors with the opportunity to decide what is in their best interest, and involving them in the management and care process through the application of informed consent procedures. 2. Exclusion and Marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations anddecision-making processes Mitigating measures: * Conduct training on gender equality in the targeted communities focusing on opinion leaders, Imams, women and girls. *Recruit women in decision making roles * Conduct community action research /assessment to understand causes of marginalization and community solutions to address them. * All beneficiary lists include a category of marginalized groups, people with disabilities, the elderly, women and girls. * Use focused radio programmes and community meetings to mobilise women and girls to take on leadership roles and participate in activities affecting their lifes.3. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area: Mitigating measures: *Develop mine risk awareness messages will be developed and disseminated during public meetings, on social media and radio outlets for wide dissemination * Imams, teachers, women groups, children's clubs will be mobilized to conduct grassroot awareness campaigns to mitigate accidents. * Develop mine risk and UXO referral pathways with clear guidelines on who and where to report in case a mine or UXO is sighted or an area is suspected to be mined * Conduct high level advocacy against the manufacture and use of anti-personnel mines * Conduct training on humanitarian lawtargeting the military. * Conduct Mine risk awareness in local schools, and negotiate for mine risk awareness to be included in school curriculum 4. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. * Conduct riskand safety analysis prior to implementation of any protection activities. All humanitarian workers in IDPs and host communities will receive PSEA training and GBViE to increase knowledge and understanding of mitigating measures. Staff screening and reference checks will include questions on history of any GBV and child abuse related crimes5.The risk of unsafe and unethical management of data will be mitigated by enforcing the data management SOPs endorsed by the AoRs. Sensitive electronic data will be encrypted and shared with not more than three individuals
