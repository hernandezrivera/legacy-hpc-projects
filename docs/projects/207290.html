Project Summary


The situation in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions remains concerning. Armed clashes, targeted attacks resulting in killings, armed extortion, and arbitrary arrests, remain frequently reported even till October 2023. The population is caught in between and exposed to violence, leading to displacement. In October of 2023 in the Akwaya subdivision (Manyu division, SW), over 875 persons were forced to flee Kajifu 1, Kekeukessim 1, and Kekeukessim 2 villages due to targeted attacks by non-State armed groups (NSAGs). During those attacks, NSAGs killed 4 persons, injured 25 others, and burned 33 houses and 7 motorbikes. The population in these villages and most of the villages in Akwaya subdivision have fled either to villages in neighboring Nigeria, to Mamfe, or nearby bushes due to the attack and continuous threats. Also, On Monday 06 November around 03:30 AM, suspected NSAG members attacked members of the “Boki” community living in Egbekaw village in Mamfe. Over 15 houses were burnt during the attack, leaving around 20 people injured and at least 50 deaths.
The conflict which has been christened by the United Nations as a neglected crisis is characterized by the destruction of the already fragile economy, massive displacements due to continuous armed battles coupled with the effects of COVID-19 is having disastrous consequences on the populations of the embattled regions with women and children suffering the most. Aadditionally, people affected by crises are more susceptible to illness and death from disease, particularly diarrhoeal and infectious diseases, especially vaccine-preventable diseases. This is so because such diseases are strongly related to inadequate sanitation, water supply, and poor hygiene. The health system in the Northwest and Southwest regions have been hard hit by the crisis with many health facilities in remote hard-to-reach areas destroyed and most healthcare personnel have vacated their areas of work due to insecurity. In most localities now, the few surviving facilities are mostly run by Community Health Workers (CHWs), who for the most part are unskilled, thus unable to adequately respond to health needs in an emergency and humanitarian context, especially vaccinations and deliveries. In addition, as is the case with massive displacements in crisis times, IDPs live in crowded, unhygienic conditions in host communities, bushes, or hard-to-reach areas in make-shift settlements that is a breeding ground for diarrheal and common childhood diseases like malaria and acute respiratory infections and vaccine-preventable diseases like measles. Because access to safe drinking water remains problematic in such settings, an outbreak of any of the WASH-related diarrhoeal and infectious diseases in such a fragile health infrastructure could be more difficult to contain thus, necessitating prevention. Some health districts in the southwest region have recorded repeated episodes of cholera outbreaks, in Ndian, Meme, and Fako divisions. In the northwest region due to inadequate vaccination, the region has suffered pockets of measles outbreaks in the Ako and Nwa Health districts. The displacements and COVID-19 have equally led to many people losing their means of livelihood, and reduced farming which constituted the main means of survival for most people, explaining the observed increase of GBV especially towards women and the girl child. In such situations, PwDs especially women and children face a triple jeopardy resulting from their gender, economic situation, and disability.
To respond, the project will improve lay emphasis on the following domdains
- Improve access to basic health services especially for women and children in crsis affected communities of the Northwest and southwest regions especially in hard-to-reach communities.
- Improve access to trauma care mental health and psychosocial support.
- Support preparedness to and response to epidemics.
- Enhance access to emergency health care

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Following IOM MSNS result round 8 of 2023, the burning health needs of the people can be summarized as thus:- Basic health care services:There is great need for mobile clinics and caravans to reach the needs of many who cannot afford good care in the communities given the present state of affairs. Remote hard to reach communities especially are at the mercy of vendors and untrained health providers who in some circumstances aggravate medical conditions with poor prescriptions. These organized mobile clinics will strategically target specific communities where the need is highest. Also, outreach activities and organized caravans could also be made to be comprehensive in nature. A comprehensive outreach team will address Health, Nutrition, WASH, and protection and health needs. - Mental Health and Psychological support needs:The trauma caused by frequent killings, burning of homes and the destruction of property and the associated effects of the COVID-19 pandemic has driven many inhabitants of these regions mental. The glaring example has been the number of person being diagnosed with high blood pressure while thousands are not in their right minds. This has posed a huge need for Mental health services to assist these people cope with the trauma they are living with. - Basic obstetrics and neonatal care needs:Most trained health professionals have vacated their stations for fear of death. This has left pregnant and lactating mothers at the mercy of untrained birth attendants and CHWs whose skills are inadequate to handle especially complicated pregnancy presentations. There are not good centers that can offer emergency obstetrics in these communities that is leading to high rate of maternal mortality and preterm deaths post deliveries. - Early detection and referral of obstetrical emergency:Early antenatal care is the key to a better pregnancy outcome in all cases. In the situation where antenatal care is absent or poorly conducted, there is the tendency for complications as result of late detection. Improvement of mother and child services and the established of a good referral system will further assist in improved pregnancy outcomes as this will enhance early detection and referral of cases for emergency management.- Trauma Care: Most health facilities has either less functional of nor functional theaters. The provision of trauma centers at strategic locations will improve the outcome of most trauma cases arising from the armed conflict. - Epidemic and disaster response Continuously in 2023, the NWSW have recorded episodes of outbreaks of cholera and measles. The detection process and response is said to have had lapses due to poor coordination and supplies. Readiness to response to epidemics and disaster management is needed: the training of CHW, HCW, and awareness to communities etc.
