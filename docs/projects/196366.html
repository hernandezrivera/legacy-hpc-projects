Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to support CFS and Schools to enhance the protective environment for children, by increasing capacity to deal with these cases alleviate them and identify them on a sound basis to deal with the psychological conditions of the family.The project also aims at empowering families with rights and duties, dealing with children, and Secure child-friendly spaces for children for "Boys and girls affected by the crisis and exposed to neglect, abuse and violence are protected through the access for them and their caregivers to groups and individual psychosocial support,Strengthen the capacity of teachers to address child protection risks and needs and to enhance access to quality specialized child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action.The project will also support children with special needs through social support sessions for them and their families, and train teachers and parents , This project focuses on the Children and Caregivers, SCA provides integrated protection activities through prevention and risk mitigation activities like awareness-raising sessions, and response activities like case management approaches and consolidated referral channels. Psychological and Social Assistance Services (PSS) are provided in a safe place that is functioning and builds resilience of survivors and vulnerable boys and girls with disabilities. All activities are carried out in consideration of protection Task Force guidelines. SCA works with community-based support services in the NWS provinces through Child Friendly spaces (CFS), Outreach activities or Schools.SCA through this Project will be provide integrated services for CP, psychosocial support, and early childhood development, Parenting Skills within Schools, Outreach services and CFS . through field activities.Conduct PSS recreational activities and events for children and parents including games, drawing, movies, and theatre-plays, By integration and mainstreaming approaches, accessibility is improved and barriers to vulnerable groups are broken. psychosocial workers will be present in the CFS and PSS rooms to manage the activities and provide private sessions,referred cases, or other individuals seeking focused specialized PSS services. Work includes integrated case management,communication skills, community protection practices, behavioral change communication, maternal and child health, hygiene promotion, nutrition,Includes guidance on referral routes. Protection awareness messages are delivered to beneficiaries through daily home visits, prioritizing messages on civil documents, HLP, child labor, child marriage and gender-based violence. Individuals in need of protection or other forms of support are referred to facilities and service providers within the referral channel to minimize the risk of breach.All activities will design based on gender sensitivity, diversity and group ages, and provide the sessions by female staff. CwDs will be targeted in the opened and safe areas in the camps or schools, Design the activities appropriate to PwDs. All information from all sources prior to decision making. Established and trained community-based child protection committees/Networks to enhance community-based protection referrals and reporting mechanisms.MEAL team, which has the responsibility to document the reached results and to assess the standard of work delivered by; Conducting regular observation visits to the project site. A MEAL team member will follow a checklist to assess the interaction of staff with the target group, the safety and security, the implementation of activities and upholding procedures and policies such as child protection and safeguarding. Conducting regular satisfaction surveys and regular FGDs. SCA will report on 4W tool. Needs assessment/community consultations(FGDs & KII, PDM) are regularly conducted to foster communities active participation

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023, Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. Deepening poverty continues to fuel harmful coping mechanisms and strain the capacities of families and communities to protect children, particularly adolescent girls and boys: child labor, including its worst forms, is reported in all NWS and The continuous deterioration of the protection situation will result in further neglect of persons with disabilities and the elderly. In NW Syria, 1.8 million are already displaced. 1,300 IDP sites are unplanned with high population densities. More than 6 M children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services. Grave violations against children remain a major concern. 85% of people live in communities with child labour. 84% of people live in communities where child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls, and 36% of people live in communities with family separation. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 28% of households, demonstrating the increasing cumulative toll on mental well-being.PSS needs rank the highest with women and girls representing the main part of those in need of psychological support and a significant number of boys in need of PSS

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Unsafe CFS spaces Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation measures: Each of the project locations will be identified and set upusing SCA CFS guidelines, Protection risk: No inclusion and/orparticipation of disadvantaged groups Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: To increase equitable access to the facilities, aninclusion checklist will be applied, so that the needs of disadvantaged groups are considered into program design (IDPs, CwDs, adolescents, girls).The checklist will assess accessibility and meaningful participation in the needs assessment; outreach activities and inclusive development ofactivities; engagement of marginalised groups; and representation in monitoring and evaluation activities. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SCA is invested in the Centrality of Protection and is developing an action plan that focuses on capacity building of staff, the embedding ofconflict sensitivity and protection analysis, strengthening safe programming and child safeguarding approaches, improving risk management, andintegrating protection into advocacy work. In addition, SCA integrates protection mainstreaming across its programs, SCA has developed child-friendly PSEA materials that can be displayed in classrooms and CFS, as wellas child-friendly identification and reporting mechanisms, to ensure the safety of children. These are constantly monitored through the projectlifecycle by the Child Protection team at SCA, and regular training and updates are conducted with partner field staff and management teams. Toavoid inefficient referrals that do not result in service provision, referral pathways will be mapped for all project locations and regularly updatedthroughout the lifecycle of the project.ensure all staff are aware of the PSEA and CoC;review the policies on regular basis and updates them accordingly; review the complaint mechanism and ensure all staff are aware of them byrunning refreshing training on regular basis.getting training on PSEA and Safeguarding for all staff. - PSEA awarenessthrough brochures, videos. - Assigning complaint mechanism for SEA issues. - policies of Code of conduct, PSEA, child safeguarding is mandatoryfor all staff and vendors working with SCA.
