Project Summary

In line with the UNHCR GBV policy, the Inter-Agency minimum standards for GBV in emergency, the IASC guidelines, the GBV strategic objectives of the HRP as well as the “Call to Action” road map 2021-2025, the project is designed with the three areas of Prevention, Risk Mitigation and Response to address gender-based violence (GBV). The project aims at strengthening protection of women, men, boys and girls internally displaced and returnees including those with specific needs against exposure to risks of GBV and ensuring that all survivors have adequate and timely access to quality life savings services in Borno and Adamawa states. Importantly, through capacity building and sensitization, the project will address specific issues related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) involving humanitarian workers, security actors and community leaders in camps and host communities. In its GBV programming, UNHCR is guided by the human rights based, survivor-centered, Age Gender and Diversity (AGD), child friendly, and community based approaches. As such, UNHCR ensures that affected communities, in particular women and girls from all ages, backgrounds and abilities, who are most affected by GBV and the best sources of information about risk factors, are consulted in programming. Year-round focus group discussions, key informant interviews, safety audits, participatory assessments, and reporting/feedback mechanisms are key means through which UNHCR ensures consultative and adapted GBV programming.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a major protection concern in Northeastern Nigeria. Widespread insecurity and ongoing conflict occasioned by incessant insurgencies, combined with lack of livelihood opportunities and basic necessities continue to increase the risk of GBV. Not only deep-rooted gender disparity but also changes of gender roles in family and community due to displacement further frustrates and challenges traditional gender norms that lead to GBV against women, men, girls and boys. Vulnerability of women and girls has also increased due to the social status of women since most of them are widows or single due to the high number of male fatalities during the conflict. Extremely limited livelihood opportunity and socio-economic support exposes women and girls to the sale and exchange of sex for money or goods. Women and girls continue to suffer from the humanitarian crisis being at risks of kidnapping, sexual violence and forced marriage by non-state armed group, and face stigmatization, rejection and secondary violence upon return to their family and community. An estimated 1.3 million individuals need GBV prevention and response services across the BAY states in 2022 (of whom 46% are IDPs, 23% returnees, and 31% host communities; 82% are women or girls). The majority (63%) of those in need are in Borno State, 22% in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. The GBV sub-sector uses the Protection Sector severity rankings and the severity analysis for the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR) to calculate the number of people targeted in each state and LGA. Based on these analyses, the GBV sub-sector and its partners plan to target 1,030,000 people (46% IDPs, 23% returnees, 31% host communities; overall 82% are women or girls) with GBV prevention and response interventions in 51 LGAs (25 in Borno, 15 in Yobe and 12 in Adamawa) with the severest needs, as informed by the JIAF and other analyses from the GBV Information Management System. The majority (63%) of those to be targeted are in Borno State, 22% are in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. As highlighted by the GBV Information Management System data, GBV continues to be a major protection concern for women and girls in north-east Nigeria and thus the reason why they remain the main target. Men and boys and other minority groups will also be targeted.In 2022, UNHCR’s through CCCM implementation partner INTERSOS carried out GBV safety audits in Michika and Mubi in Adamawa and in Monguno and Ngala in Borno. As at this date, the GBV safety audit locations for 2023 have not yet been determined, but tentative plan is for Bama, Banki, Pulka in Borno and Yola in Adamawa. Also, UNHCR carried out a participatory assessment on GBV (an assessment of needs, coping mechanisms and solutions for UNHCR to consider in programming, in close consultation with communities) in Nov-Dec 2022 covering the locations Bama, Banki (Borno) and Mubi N, Yola N and Fufore (Adamawa). Additionally, UNHCR partner BOWDI conducts regular FGD and KIIs (frequency of the same has been identified in the 2023 partnership agreement).

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Training/capacity building of GBV community-based protection structures - Primary to UNHCR’s GBV prevention programming is the focus on the empowerment of women and girls and engagement of men and boys, and community members more broadly, in a comprehensive and phased approach to changing social norms and promoting behavioral change. Community based structures will be established and capacitated on GBV, PSEA, and reporting mechanisms, targeting 2,500 community members. - GBV structures include Women Engagement Network, Male Engagement Network, Advisory Group Members, GBV clubs, Zero Tolerance Village Alliance Members and GBV Community Volunteers. Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP), a globally recognized methodology on the prevention of GBV and addressing social norms to tackle gender inequality as the root cause of GBV through engaging men in transformative individual behavior change guided by the voices of women, will be implemented. - Trainings of community structures will include the impact of climate change as a threat multiplier in compounding GBV risks and threats. - Community-based complaint mechanisms (including on PSEA) to be strengthened Activity 2: Community sensitization and awareness raising - Awareness raising and sensitization sessions on GBV, PSEA and community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms will be conducted reaching 100,000 individuals. IEC materials and radio call in programme will be used, and special attention paid to international commemorative events such as 16 DoA to bolster awareness raising. Activity 3: Relevant GBV actors/stakeholders targeted with training and capacity building - 500 partner staff, security personnel, govt authorities, religious leaders, and other GBV stakeholders provided with training on GBV prevention, mainstreaming, PSEA. Training includes identification and documentation of GBV risks and incidents. Activity 4: Provision of case management and PSS - WGSS, equipped with counselling room, emergency safe shelter and offering a range of activities, will be maintained in Bama, Gwoza, Ngala, Dikwa, Jere, Maiduguri, Fufore, Yola N, Yola S, Mubi N, Mubi S. GBV response interventions offered at the WGSSs include case management to 4000 and PSS (individual and group) to 4500 GBV survivors/persons at risk. Additionally, a range of informational, recreational and income generating activities will be offered at the WGSS. - Legal, medical, livelihood and other referrals will be made as part of case management support, as required. Legal support will be provided by UNHCR through partner NBA, targeting some 1000 GBV survivors/at risk persons. - Approximately 350 men and boy survivors of GBV will also be supported with mobile case management and PSS. Activity 5: Skills building/socio-economic opportunities - To mitigate and respond to GBV risk, through the WGSS (managed through UNHCR implementation partner BOWDI for women and girls) and through UNHCR’s livelihood partner (AUN), GBV survivors/persons at risk (including women, men and adolescent girls and boys) will receive skills training and material start-up material support. Approximately 3000 women and girl GBV survivors/at risk persons will benefit from this skills training at the WGSS, while 1600 vulnerable women, men, youth (girls and boys) will receive entrepreneurship skills, micro economic fundamentals and start-up materials) from UNHCR partner AUN. Activity 6: Distribution of Dignity Kits and other GBV related items (fuel efficient cooking stoves) - To mitigate and respond to GBV risk, 6,000 Dignity kits and 3,000 fuel-efficient stoves will be distributed to women and girls to mitigate GBV risk.In 2023, our activities will be prioritized for locations where we have an existing WGSS. UNHCR and its GBV partner BOWDI are both participating to the GBV sub-sector, reporting activities/funding information, and coordinating the delivery of assistance and identifying gaps/challenges.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures.As part of its Community based Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CBCFM), UNHCR has established several reporting channels led by agencies as well as those led by the community. The community based GBV structures include peer to community volunteers, peer groups, advisory groups, women and men’s engagement groups, which in 2023 are undergoing reconfiguration. In 2023, the locations of these structures will be reduced to Banki, Bami, Ngalla and Pulka (Borno) and Yola N and Mubi N (Adamawa). The community-based general protection structures are referred to as the Protection Action Groups, present in numerous LGAs across Yobe, Borno and Adamawa. At the agency level, UNHCR has established PSEA focal points at the Sub-Office and field unit levels, Protection desks and Women and Girls Safe Spaces in field locations, confidential toll-free lines, email address and complaint boxes where PSEA related complaints can be made. Partners’ training on PSEA by UNHCR will take place in Q1 2023.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In adherence to UNHCR’s Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Age Gender Diversity approach, UNHCR will strengthen community-based feedback mechanisms enabling men, women, boys and girls to express feedback/complaint. Beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of child protection-based materials. UNHCR and child protection partner will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, child safeguarding and child protection policy. UNHCR’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries through Participatory Approach, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict and insurgency.
