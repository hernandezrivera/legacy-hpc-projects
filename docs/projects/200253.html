Project Summary

This project aims to improve availability, access, quality and demand of maternal and child health services at primary health care level of vulnerable Lebanese and Migrants, especially in Baalbek-Hermel, Bekaa, South and Nabatiyeh. The planned response of Action Against Hunger (AAH) under the Health Sector Response is to improve primary health care services for women and children and complement the nutrition-specific interventions under the Nutrition Sector Response within this health response.
AAH will build on its multiple years of experience with community mobilization in WASH and nutrition and expand to improve knowledge on routine immunization, health seeking behavior for sexual and reproductive health, maternal and child health, and mental health. In addition to this, AAH plans to incorporate targeted awareness during public health outbreaks, to limit the spread of the outbreak and timely tackle misinformation. Through this community mobilization women and adolescent girls attending awareness sessions will receive a dignity kit to improve access to menstrual hygiene and so minimize menstrual poverty. To complement this community outreach AAH will also hire community midwives to complement the ANC received through primary health care facilities with a community outreach for women with high-risk pregnancies. This could be women that are malnourished or have COVID-19 or other risks.
To support primary health care facilities to be able to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining health services, AAH will provide costs for primary health care facilities to be able to extend their opening hours for essential primary health care, such as maternal and child health care, to ensure more people are able to access the services.
To ensure health care staff are equipped to respond to outbreaks; health facilities are able to mainstream protection; and provision of ANC and PNC in line with the new package, health care workers will be provided with specific training to improve quality of services and so able to responds to the needs of vulnerable communities. To respond to outbreaks health facilities will also be provided with basic medical supplies, such as PPE.
Overall, this project will aim to ensure primary health care, including a community outreach component, is able to respond to the current needs among vulnerable Lebanese, migrants and Palestinian refugees, but will indirectly also support vulnerable Syrians, since AAH will also ensure that the supported primary health care facilities are accessible to all, irrespective of their nationality.
This project will target 3 primary health care facilities that will reach approximately 15,000 patients during extended hours and 8,000 through community outreach activities.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

- Mobilization, capacity building and supervision of community volunteers to conduct awareness sessions on routine immunization, SRH, mental health, health seeking behaviour, outbreak RCCE (i.e. COVID-19, cholera etc) and appropriate IYCF practices.- Community outreach on preventive maternal care by midwives to women with high-risk pregnancies, such as nutrition specific risk and COVID-19 infection- Provision of training and follow-up to health care workers at primary health care level on the new antenatal and postnatal care package.- Cover costs of primary health care facilities to be able to extend opening hours for essential primary health care such as maternal and child health services. - Ensure facilities are equipped with basic medical supplies to respond timely and in line with WHO and MoPH guidance to public health outbreaks.- Training of health care workers on infection prevention and control in line with health sector guidance- Provide training to health care workers on GBV safe identification and referral and on PSEA- Through the community outreach as described above dignity kits will be provided to households with adolescent girls and women aged 15 through 45 years.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ACF works to ensure gender and protection mainstreaming at every stage of its project through a compliance with ACF’s Gender Policy and Do No Harm approach. Needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls have been assessed through a participatory approach to develop meaningful activities in line with the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. Additionally, ACF also ensures two-way communication with beneficiaries through its dedicated feedback and complaints mechanism, allowing complaints to be directly collected and addressed. ACF shall ensure that all its employees and personnel comply with the provisions of ST/SGB/2003/13 entitled “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse”. ACF shall further ensure that none of its employees and personnel exposes any intended beneficiary, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ACF field staff engages with affected populations to clearly introduce them to program activities, expected impacts, and selection criteria in inception meetings planned in advance with local stakeholders and engagement and coaching of Community Mobilizers and Focal Points in diverse settings. The involved stakeholders, level of detail and level of engagement is project-specific, based on contextual factors such as access and acceptance in targeted locations. This engagement supports stakeholder trust and acceptance of project activities as well as providing a venue for stakeholder feedback on their relevance. ACF Lebanon is accountable to its beneficiaries, meaning it will acknowledge and act on all feedback and complaints from communities, local authorities, beneficiaries, contractors, service providers, and all other stakeholders throughout project implementation, especially through amplifying its Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM).
