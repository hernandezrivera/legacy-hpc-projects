Project Summary

The proposed project will provide critical humanitarian services through an evidence-based, integrated strategy that will adapt service delivery within the complex Syrian context, combining direct implementation and partnerships with Shafak to address humanitarian needs and utilizing robust monitoring. IRC and Shafak will meet the immediate needs of the most at-risk IDPs and Host community populations through the provision of distinct and integrated protection services in the targeted locations through 1) protection monitoring and inter-agency coordination that promotes cluster-level advocacy and informs humanitarian actors’ 2) protection mainstreaming; and 3) Provision of critical information that enables displaced populations to obtain referrals for services, 4) legal services and individualized consultations, 5) IPA with linkages to Cash for Protection services.
IRC proposes to reduce barriers to accessing essential services in hard-to-reach locations and for highly affected populations and to focus on quality, impact, and provision of holistic support through increased collaboration across sectors and programs in Syria. IRC’s direct rapid response services in Idleb will be available through mobile teams with dedicated staff for specific client groups and specialization in supporting women, children and men for integrated service provision and effective intersectoral referral pathways and provision of critical and timely information.
The goal of this project is to ensure conflict-affected people across Northwest Syria are safe in their homes and communities and receive support when they experience harm. In line with the strategic and specific protection objectives outlined in the 2021 Syrian Humanitarian Response Plan, IRC will deliver integrated protection services targeting women, girls, boys, and persons with specific needs with specialized GBV, CP and PSS interventions. The protective environment in Syria will be enhanced through the systematic collection and dissemination of data on protection risks and violations of rights, which will in turn inform the broader humanitarian response. This 12-month integrated protection project will (1) meet immediate, lifesaving GBV, CP, and MHPSS needs through comprehensive protection services, and community-based protection mechanisms, and (2) improve the resilience of IDPs and conflict-affected communities by increasing their ability to prevent, mitigate and respond to ongoing protection risks.
Further, IRC and partners will work to provide life-saving, reliable and responsive information services and raise awareness on protection risks and mitigation strategies and available services and how to safely access them.
Throughout this project, IRC will regularly monitor the violation of rights and the protection situation in NWS to understand the changes overtime, improve access to primary and specialized services for those that wouldn’t otherwise seek services, inform response planning and enhance the protective environment across Syria. Explicitly targeted in this project are vulnerable women, girls, and persons with specific needs (PWSN). Interventions are chosen to reflect their needs and specific indicators to measures results. The proposed project leverages multiple points of integration so that the affected populations are supported to claim their rights. This is facilitated through IRC’s internal referral pathways and integrated points of entry.

4 - Needs

As the Syrian conflict persists into its tenth year, an estimated 13.4 million people remain in need of humanitarian assistance, 5.9 of whom are in acute need. According to the 2021 Syrian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), in addition to the physical destruction, the protracted and multiple cycles of displacement have contributed to a steady erosion of individual and community protection mechanisms, generated high levels of psychological distress, disrupted the availability of support services to the most vulnerable populations, and strained existing community support structures. The Syrian crisis remains at its heart a protection crisis. Protracted and multiple cycles of displacement, persistent instability, COVID-19 and an economic crisis have contributed to the rapid deterioration of the protection environment in Syria. Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail across the country, resulting from a variety of situations ranging from direct exposure to hostilities, multiple cycles of displacement, targeted and arbitrary detention, lack/loss of civil documentation, constraints on freedom of movement. HLP rights violations are prevalent, including lands and property grabbing, illegal occupation of displaced or absent owners’ properties, restitution, and disinheritance, particularly of women, IDPs returnees and children. Against this backdrop, the 2021 Syria Humanitarian HNO estimates that the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is over 13.4 million, including 6.7 million IDPs. Of that number, approximately 5.9 million people are in acute need. This represents a 20% increase from 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRC and Shafak will continue protection monitoring (PM) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates and IRC will continue to co-lead the protection cluster through 2021. Routine monitoring will be conducted by protection monitors using a combination of methodologies including household and community level interviews, focus group discussions, and observation. Once collected, data will be analyzed and developed into information management products, to be shared through coordination platforms and with other relevant humanitarian stakeholders. Reports will cover key protection topics such as undocumented children, HLP issues, and access to specific rights. When an emerging protection concern or situation requiring particular attention (such as a new displacement; spontaneous return) is identified, an integrated rapid protection assessment is conducted and a specific protection team is deployed to respond. The Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team, supported by TPMs will conduct spot-checks using observation monitoring tools during their field visits to project sites, including at Cash for Protection registrations and distributions. In addition, the MEAL team will conduct focus group discussions (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) to understand community’s perceptions of IRC’s services. A data quality audit will be conducted on a quarterly basis to ensure that data is complete, correct, and consistent. The audit will also verify source data against what was reported.The MEAL team and TPMs will conduct exit/satisfaction surveys with a statistically determined sample size ofbeneficiaries to measure the beneficiaries’ overall satisfaction level with services provided by IRC .

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The IRC’s approach to integrated protection programming in Syria seeks to provide the most vulnerable clients with access to comprehensive, tailored services that adhere to the principle of Do No Harm. To do so, the IRC depends on (1) regular context analysis through stakeholder analyses, updated security assessments, monthly protection monitoring, regular communication with camp and local authorities, and information sharing across partners and humanitarian networks; and (2) meaningful participation of affected populations throughout the project cycle. Service interruption of any kind can have negative impacts on clients, but the interruption of protection services can be particularly harmful and destabilizing. The IRC’s mixed mobile and static approach and timely, safe referrals (including internal IRC services) mitigate the risk of an abrupt end to service provision. Protection monitoring allows the IRC to track whether clients are unable to access protection services, as well as their degree of satisfaction or any concerns associated with the services received. With data gathered on a monthly basis, insights from respondents can be translated into course corrections and program adaptations, and inform IRC’s understanding of unintended impacts of humanitarian service provision more broadly. 7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

IRC and IRC Staff recognize the unequal power dynamic and the resulting potential for exploitation inherent in our work with local beneficiaries. IRC and IRC Staff further recognize that exploitation undermines the credibility of our work and severely damages victims of these exploitive acts and their families and communities. Accordingly, IRC maintains and upholds the present policy which explicitlyprohibits all forms of beneficiary exploitation and abuse by IRC staff, including but not limited to sexual exploitation and abuse as a recurrent and especially abhorrent form of beneficiary exploitation and abuse.IRC expects its Staff to uphold the highest ethical standard of integrity, accountability, and transparency in the delivery of goods and services while executing the responsibilities of their position. Accordingly, the following requirements apply to IRC Workers: IRC Staff are prohibited from engaging in sexual relationships with beneficiaries. IRC Staff are also prohibited from organizing, facilitating, or participating in sexual relationships or activities with beneficiaries. IRC employees are prohibited from engaging in any forms of intimidating, humiliating, degrading, or aggressive behavior towards beneficiaries. Sexual activity with children (defined according to the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) as all persons under the age of eighteen) is strictly prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally.
