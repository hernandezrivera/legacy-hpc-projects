Project Summary

In 2021 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including WASH, reaching a total of 118,130 beneficiaries. In 2021 ZOA was able to secure $4,732,378 in funding, of which $1,200,000 was SHF funding. ZOA was able to allocate $1,635,000 for activities under the WASH sector, reaching out to 55,000 beneficiaries. In 2022 ZOA has to date secured $2,122,655 with around 1 million USD secured for WASH interventions.
Through the proposed project for 2022-23 ZOA is estimating to target 156,000 beneficiaries, the cost per beneficiary is estimated $44. In 2022 ZOA is targeting 114,00 beneficiaries (58,140 female, 55,860 male, children 05 - 14,770, 5-17 - 34680, adults 18-59 - 58,630 & 59 older - 5,920). Through this proposal ZOA is aiming at expanding its interventions to reach communities most in need of WASH in underserved conflict affected areas in Aleppo, Al-Hasakeh, Dar’a, Deir-ez-Zor, Idlib, Hama, Quneitra and Rural Damascus. All selected sub-districts fall under scores 3-5 on the severity scale, according to the WASH PiN assessment document shared by the sector, also taking into consideration the activity needs in the selected sub-districts. ZOA’s activities focus on the maintenance and/or light rehabilitation of existing WASH systems, specifically prioritizing activities supporting existing water and sanitation systems, including water quality assurance and rehabilitation of infrastructure. Interventions also include solid waste management activities. The overall objective is to provide support to water and sanitation/sewage systems to ensure regular services for vulnerable households in areas with high severity of needs.
ZOA is proposing the following broad activities:
Support to water systems and services: Rehabilitation of water wells will ensure sufficient quantity and quality water for target beneficiaries. Since electricity is a major hurdle ZOA has taken into consideration the provision of solar energy systems wherever possible. The rehabilitation of Bab Al Nayrab sand filtration system is also taken into consideration upon the recommendation of Aleppo WASH sector and Aleppo Water Establishment.
Support to sanitation/sewage networks: Rehabilitation of damaged sewage pipes to prevent groundwater infiltration as well as sewage leaks which can contaminate the environment.
Support to solid waste management systems:
- Community debris and garbage clean-up in target communities - The solid waste management activities are especially important in light of the COVID-19 pandemic as breathing difficulties can arise from the fungi that develop from garbage that has not been safely disposed of. Solid Waste Management helps to prevent the transmission of bacterial infections at a community level.
- Provision of communal garbage bins so target communities can safely dispose of their household waste.
- Cash for work activities.
- Capacity building of municipalities for operation and maintenance of infrastructure.
The project activities are aligned with the WASH sector: Objective 1: Support water, sanitation / sewerage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular service for affected people in Syria.
The selected activities are either based on the needs that ZOA team came across during the (evaluation of) activities implemented during 2021, or recommendations by local population, WASH sector and authorities, which ZOA all takes very seriously. Therefore, before the final design of any project ZOA will consult the concerned ministry, local authorities (governor, mayor, moukhtars) and organizes FGDs with different age groups of the community, so that the activities really serve their needs. In parallel, ZOA carries out a quick market assessment or search for a recent assessment done by partners, to define the proper modality that works best in the targeted area. ZOA is also in process of signing an MoU with the Ministry of Water Resources.

4 - Needs

In Syria, basic WASH systems have suffered from damage owing to years of functioning at high capacity, with limited or no maintenance & destruction. ZOA’s 2022-23 project is aimed at supporting water & sanitation/sewage systems to ensure regular services. HNO shows an increase in the WASH needs; the number of PiN of WASH assistance has raised from 12.2 to 13.2M; 65% of the whole population in Syria. Access to sufficient quantities of safe water remains limited, with increased water quality assurances & support to water systems rehabilitation, operation & maintenance unconditionally needed. As a result of the crisis at least 70% of sewage is untreated & at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, exposing communities to significant health risks. There is a high need for WASH infrastructure repairs & capacity building. With 47% of the general population relying on alternatives to piped water & often unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs, with 70% of sewage being discharged untreated, half of the sewerage systems not functional, 30% of garbage inappropriately disposed, the WASH infrastructure & service provision in many parts of Syria requires significant repair & operational support (HNO 2022-23 WASH). The COVID-19, coupled with political situation in general & particularly in Al-Hasakeh, economic situation & power shortage in damaged WASH infrastructure are all whistleblowers to be prepared & to plan well for WASH interventions.

5 - Implementation of activities

For all activities: 1 Obtaining access & implementation authorizations from relevant authorities. 2 Carry out needs assessments, consult with the concerned ministries, WASH sector, local authorities & communities. 3 Generation of BoQs to ensure that the implemented work will result in the desired outcomes for PWDs, women, men, girls & boys. 4 Tender – the bids (technical & financial) will be reviewed by a committee & the most suitable contractors will be selected based on evaluation of cost, quality, experience, & timing. 5 COVID-19 awareness raising. Wells & sewage pipes: 1 Suppliers will be checked for capacity, experience, & compliance to sanctions. ZOA’s WASH engineers will be responsible for monitoring the quality of work & efficiency. The wells will be diagnosed for technical feasibility taking into account the history of the well, prior rehabilitation procedures/result & capacity. A scale & water analysis, checking for potential chemical & biological well plugging problems will also be carried out. Work includes excavation, concrete, mechanical & electrical & solar work. 2 Upon completion, ZOA will hand over the completed verified work. Manager of Program Quality supervises MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators & activities with sex, age & disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability & for demonstrating impact.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Tension in community - for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1 Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the WASH sector, such as UNICEF beneficiary targeting tool; 2 Consult with actors on the ground to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible; 3 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/changes.R2: Risk of female and children hygiene negligence within the Covid-19 context. Likelihood: high. Impact: medium. Mitigation measures: 1. Referrals to actors who distribute female and children hygiene kits in the area; 2. General awareness raising sessions; 3. Focused awareness sessions; 4. Training local community representatives on issues related to hygiene within the context of Covid. R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1. Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2. Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3. Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4. Regular staff training; 5. Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1 Government entities require agencies to adhere to new regulations or additional approval levels are required in order to be able to implement activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Coordinate well within the sectorial coordination spaces, to be aware of how other actors deal with the challenges; 2 Continue to engage closely with ministerial entities; 3 Continue to build capacities of local actors & continue to implement through them. R2 Market & exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2 Transfer funds into Syrian bank according to the immediate needs. 3 Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency. R3 Security related issues during interventions in the field. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous assessment of the intervention areas; 2 Coordination with local authorities & community leaders. R4 Fraud & corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2 ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its code of conduct; 3 Operational policies in place for the mitigation of fraud & corruption risks; 4 All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption & abuse; 5 All financial transactions are documented & filed; 6 Regular finance spot-checks & unannounced audits are carried out.
