Project Summary

This project proposed by Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) will be implemented in Ibba, Mundri West, Mundri East, Maridi, and Wau Counties of South Sudan in response to child protection needs by addressing multiple protection risks faced by girls and boys. This project will reach 23,520 (6,400 boys, 6,400 girls, 5,360 males, 5,360 female) beneficiaries.
We have tailored the proposed activities in line with Child Protection cluster objective PC1 – to ensure the mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability, presence/missions to field and deep field locations, and PC 3 - to promote nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building, and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and children with disabilities. We have considered that children with disabilities face multiple barriers to inclusion including inaccessible physical environment, communication, negative attitudes, poverty, rigid education systems, and poverty. Additionally, girls with disabilities and returnees are particularly vulnerable, as boys tend to get the few opportunities available. We have proposed an activity that will assess their individual needs to facilitate comprehensive case management services and referrals of children with disabilities to available services.
With limited access to services such as health, shelter, psychosocial support, education, and livelihoods coupled with a weak social support system, returnees from Uganda and Sudan are living under undignified and distressed conditions. Such factors intensify their vulnerability and expose them to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as forced marriages, child marriage, and child labor. Conducting mapping of available child protection services to update the referral pathways will improve access to multisectoral services among children. Lack of awareness of the right to assistance and limited coverage of response services, complicated by deep-rooted social norms related to gender make accessing services ever more challenging. We shall conduct awareness and prevention messaging through our caseworkers and existing community-based protection networks targeting men, women, boys, and girls with messages on the rights of children and the inclusion of children with disabilities.

1. Needs

Key findings of an intersectoral needs assessment published in October 2023 in Wau indicated that The main protection concern for girls (83.4%) was early marriage, and the main protection concern for boys (36.1%) was child recruitment by armed forces (40.2%). 38.7% reported that all primary school-aged children in their household attend school and 46.9 percent reported having children in their household who dropped out of school. Similarly, an inter-agency report on floods assessment in Mundri East and Mundri West Counties published in September 2022 highlighted the plight of children who were affected by floods and the challenges they faced like sleeping in an open and wet place, and the dangers they faced in moving like the risk of diseases and being separated from their families.We shall address these above-mentioned needs through conducting community awareness-raising on child protection. The awareness plays a crucial role in child protection by empowering children and families to recognize and prevent child abuse and neglect like the cases of early marriage and recruitment by armed forces. Families and children will be better prepared to identify and report these issues after the awareness.We shall also address the above needs by conducting a mapping of available child protection services to update the referral pathways in Ibba, Mundri West, Mundri East, Maridi, and Wau Counties. Updating and documenting the multisectoral referral pathways and training service providers on how to safely refer children with physical or mental health or other protection needs will increase access to services among children from the host, IDP, and returnee communities who are in need. The multi-sectoral services will include schools that will provide safe spaces for the children, spaces for healing, and integration.Conducting training for men and women on CPiE to build the capacity of men and women as well as those who are part of community-based child protection networks on CPiE will empower them to be able to identify and refer children at risk to support services. Additionally, it will promote a safe, dignified, and protective environment and improve the impact of all humanitarian actors by applying the do no harm principle and proactively reducing risks and harm.According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting verified 302 grave violations affecting 246 children (141 boys, 105 girls). Boys between 13 and 17 years old are significantly at risk of conflict-related violence including abduction, killing, and recruitment. A total of 66 children (64 boys and 2 girls) were verified as victims of recruitment. Some 65 children (55 boys, 10 girls) were killed/maimed, 45 children (22 boys, 20 girls) were abducted, and 73 girls were victims of rape/sexual violence. This project will provide community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities to promote their recovery. The PSS activities will speed up the process of recovery and prevent problems that children may encounter in the future to increase the resilience and coping of children. The efforts by this project to provide comprehensive PSS services will be crucial as the 2023 HNO explains that the existing PSS services being provided in accordance with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) pyramid only reflect the first two layers, including the provision of a safe space for a few hours a day in a child or women-friendly space and promotion of non-specialized services. Psychological support is not available and risk prevention and mitigation activities are limited.

2. Project activities

We shall achieve Protection Cluster Objective PC1 – in ensuring mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs by carrying out the following activities: 1) Provision of comprehensive case management services and referrals to children (including children with disabilities) to 1200 children (600 boys and 600 girls). The case management process will start with the identification of children with disabilities, assessment of their needs, development of a care plan, and referrals for other health needs/inter-agency referrals for other unmet needs (# of children who have received case management services). 2) Provision of community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with disabilities) to reach 3600 children (1800 boys and 1800 girls) and 2400 adults (1200 men and 1200 women). We shall provide psychosocial support services to boys, girls, and their parents/ caregivers of children with disabilities. The PSS will be conducted by trained caseworkers and will include recreational sports, as well as training on artwork, beadwork, leatherwork, and other skills (# children, adolescents, and caregivers accessing community-based mental health and psychosocial support). 3) Conducting community awareness-raising on child protection targeting 14,000 beneficiaries (4000 boys, 4000 girls, 3000 men, and 3000 women). We shall conduct awareness and prevention messaging through our caseworkers and existing community-based protection networks targeting men, women, boys, and girls with messages on the rights of children and the inclusion of children with disabilities (# of awareness sessions conducted with children and caregivers).We shall achieve Protection Cluster Objective PC 3 – by promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building, and the provision of long-term development through the following activities: 4) Conducting mapping of available child protection services to update the referral pathways in Ibba, Mundri West, Mundri East, Maridi, and Wau Counties. The outcome will be shared among all stakeholders operating in the project area (# of Child Protection referral pathways updated). 5) Conducting training to men and women on CPiE to build the capacity of men and women as well as those who are part of community-based child protection networks on CPiE, reaching 1,500 (750 males, 750 female) participants (# of women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff) trained on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE). 6) Conducting training for non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response with the aim of weaving ethical, effective actions and approaches that address GBV throughout a sectoral project by recognizing that GBV risk and protective factors intersect with the development challenge being addressed by the other broader projects reaching 500 (250 males, 250 female) participants (# of non-GBV humanitarian workers trained on GBV integration in their sectoral response). 7) Conducting training to child protection networks on the inclusion of children with disabilities in education and other inter-sectoral services within their communities reaching 320 (160 male, 160 female) participants (# of community-based protection activities implemented including resilience and PSS activities, PSEA, peace-building activities and training through AGD approaches)
