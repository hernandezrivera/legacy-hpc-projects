Project Summary

Access to education for girls and boys of school-age (3-17) has been obstructed by a range of complex challenges, including poor and unsafe school infrastructure, lack of proper water and sanitation facilities, insecurity, attacks on educational facilities, shortage of teachers, and lack of premises adapted for children with disabilities and other protection concerns. These challenges have varying effects on different population subsets, for instance, girls are likely face the risk of early marriage, while boys may force to recruitment by armed groups. The crisis has exacerbated the situation for children with disabilities, who constitute 15% of the population (8% of whom are girls), depriving them of essential, needs-based educational services. Nkafamiya Rescue Mission (NRM) through this project aims to:
• Ensure increased access to inclusive essential quality basic education and vocational skills opportunities within a safe learning environment.
• Ensure that boys and girls receive quality and conflict-sensitive educational services to improve their learning progress with resilience.
These objectives will be achieved through tailored activities focused towards boys and girls of school age. These children (both boys and girls) will be provided with alternative learning opportunities, which will include essential services such as maintaining temporary learning arrangements, with the potential to transition them into longer-term solutions. Addressing urgent needs involves establishing temporary learning centers, renovating classrooms and WASH facilities, providing teacher training on cross-cutting issues, and distributing essential learning materials.
The project will be implemented in MMC and Mobbar LGAs of Borno state while Yola North, Michika & Madagali in Adamawa States. NRM is requesting the total sum of $450,000 to implement this project.

1 - Needs

According to the education sector there are 1.55 million children aged 3-17, affected by crises are in need of critical educational assistance. The projected decrease in funding in 2024 can lead to huge education response gap and potentially exacerbate child protection issues such as child labor, child marriage, early pregnancy, GBV and recruitment into armed groups. Furthermore, in the BAY states, an increasing number of children persistently encounter gender-based violence, including instances of sexual exploitation and abuse, particularly when they are denied access to education services. An estimated 24 per cent of the GBV survivors who sought assistance from GBV service providers were children, adolescents, young girls and boys, ranging from 1- 18 years of age.The gender need include ensuring a safe and secure learning environment for girls, materials and resources for menstrual health management, implementing a gender-responsive curriculum, offering targeted interventions for out-of-school girls, supporting female teachers as well as adolescent girls' specific needs related to reproductive health, life skills and vocational training. There is a need to continue promoting gender equality while also empowering both boys and girls to reach their full potential. In addition the response should ensure inclusivity and provide assistance to children with disability. The need for essential education support is projected to remain considerably high in 2024, primarily due to the protracted security challenges, climate crises, gaps in services and adverse economic impact on IDPs, returnees, and host communities. Furthermore, the extended period during which displaced children were denied access to education services has resulted in noticeable learning gaps. Children living in the extremely remote and hard to reach locations have the pronounced needs of education opportunities. The escalating levels of insecurity and food scarcity are expected to drive further displacement in 2024, necessitating alternative learning opportunities for affected school-age and over-aged children, especially for those involved in livelihood activities. The sudden closure of camps and camp-like settings potentially lead to a significant relocation of IDP and returnee children, potentially to areas that are inaccessible to humanitarian organizations.

2 - Activities

NRM through this project aims to implement the following activities to achieve the project objectives:a. In partnership with the CP sector, teachers will receive training to equip them with essential child protection and psychosocial support skills, scaling up flexible learning approaches using technology and implementing second shift classes, b. Establishment of safe and inclusive learning environments: Create safe spaces in conflict-affected areas where boys and girls can access quality education without fear of violence or discrimination.c. Provision of gender-sensitive teacher training: Train teachers to be more aware of and responsive to the specific educational needs and rights of both boys and girls, ensuring equal opportunities and a gender-inclusive learning environment.d. Development of gender-focused curriculum: Adapt existing curricula to address gender disparities and promote gender equality in education, challenging gender stereotypes and promoting positive gender roles.e. Distribution of education materials: Provide boys and girls with essential educational materials like textbooks, stationery, and digital resources, ensuring equal access for all, regardless of gender.f. Promotion of girls' education and empowerment: Implement awareness campaigns and community engagements to encourage families and communities to value and support girls' education. Provide scholarships and incentives to girls, encouraging them to stay in school and complete their education.g. Support for boys' education and masculinity programs: Recognize the specific challenges faced by boys in conflict-affected areas, such as recruitment into armed groups, child labor, and lack of positive role models. Offer programs that address these challenges and promote positive masculinity, allowing boys to develop into responsible and educated young men.h. Establishment of alternative education programs: Create non-formal education programs, such as catch-up classes or accelerated learning programs, to bridge the education gap for conflict-affected boys and girls who have missed out on formal education due to the crisis.i. Provision of psychosocial support: Recognize and address the psychological impact of conflict on boys and girls, offering counseling services and trauma-informed teaching methodologies to support their emotional well-being and enhance their learning outcomes.j. Integration of technology in education: Provide access to technology and digital tools for both boys and girls, allowing them to access online resources, educational platforms, and distance learning programs, especially in areas with limited educational infrastructure.k. Collaboration and partnerships: Foster partnerships with other organizations, communities, and local authorities to strengthen the inclusive education system, ensuring sustainable solutions and ongoing support for conflict-affected boys and girls

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRM will adhere to the core guiding protection mainstreaming standards throughout the project. NRM will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations, as well as avoids causing harm. NRM has a dedicated protection team that works together along with the programme teams to map out potential protection risks and advise the project staff on how to mitigate them. NRM has already mapped out protection risks in across the different locations and will continue to do so during the project period. The protection risks already identified include restriction of movement of IDPs in camps, sexual harassment and violence, exploitation, discrimination of returning refugees in Adamawa, abduction of IDPS when fetching firewood, perceptions of young women by their own communities and others, harassment as a result of perception of former affiliation with AOG in the family and gender based. NRM will ensure that an effective complaints and feedback mechanism is in place to receive opinions from beneficiaries and local authorities. A stronger presence in these hard-to-reach areas will ensure NRM’s ability to identify quickly, document, and refer (to other partners) protection concerns by NRM’s field staff. NRM will also ensure that all demographic groups can be part of the community engagement and governance process, to ensure a better representation. Hence, woman participation but also youth, elderly of persons with disabilities’ engagement will be highly encouraged and supported. NRM will thereafter work in close coordination with these community representatives to identify needs and conduct referrals to appropriate services. NRM will also mainstream protection and GBV in the site improvement and maintenance activities, by ensuring that specific groups of the population have an appropriate access to communal services. Infrastructures and services will be improved to integrate accessibility and inclusion facilities. NRM has developed detailed protection guidelines and will deliver training to project staff, to increase its staff capacity to recognise and deal with sensitive protection issues. NRM will ensure that the Community-Based Workers are trained on basic protection principles, as part of the induction process.
