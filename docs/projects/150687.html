Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the education sub-cluster framework objectives 1 and 2 and aims to ensure that boys and girls have access to a protective and enabling educational experience in vulnerable MoEHE schools in the Gaza Strip. This project - fitting under a broad multi-year NRC education intervention applying psycho-social support in formal schools- is intended to focus specifically on Education in Emergency (EiE) based on the following three-fold approach: i) provision of technical support through the constitution of an Emergency Task Force under the Education sub-cluster to steer the EiE priorities; the mapping of teaching and non-teaching staff around the Designated Emergency Shelters (DES); the provision of tailored EiE training; and the delivery of pre-positioned educational kits to 20 school assigned Designated Emergency Shelter (DES); ii) implementation of the psycho-educational ‘Better Learning Program’ (BLP) reinforcing the system through the training of new cohorts of BLP Master Trainers at General and Educational Directors, Subject Supervisors and Head of Departments levels; contributing to the evidence based improved well-being and learning outcomes by finalizing the development of the M&E BLP toolkit; capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff in 30 target MoEHE schools; provision of emergency kits to the schools, awareness sessions for parents; and provision of a ‘Supporting the Supporters’ package for teaching and non-teaching in need; and iii) Advocacy pursuing enhanced protection from violations of the right to education.

Needs

Since 30 March 2018 Gaza Strip has witnessed a significant increase in casualties in the mass demonstration that took place along Israel perimeter’s fence resulting in 18,739 persons injured including 1,642 children (OCHA Aug 2018). The increase of needs take place against a backdrop of an overall precarious humanitarian situation. 56% of children surveyed by NRC in March 2018 were suffering from traumatic nightmares, and when repeating the survey in April findings indicated an increase to 60%. Recent research (Safwat Diab, Jon-Håkon S., 2018: Factors Contributing to Students’ Academic Underachievement in War and Conflict: A Multilevel Qualitative Study-Gaza) found that the number of underachievers estimated by teachers/counsellors in sixth-grade classes was between 18–22%. The findings also showed that parents and their children faced high levels of stress mainly due to their low economic and living conditions, as a result of the repetitive wars and years of economic siege of Gaza. Consequently, the family priorities changed and became more focused on facilitating basic needs for their children, while academic achievement became less important. The research findings have demonstrated how factors from students’ society, home, school, and personal psychology are interrelated and contribute to psychosocial difficulties, leading to their academic underachievement.Teachers and counsellors often lack the skills and response methodology to identify these children and respond in an appropriate manner. In the NRC ‘Better Learning evaluation’ (“Improving Children Wellbeing: An evaluation of NRC’s BLP in Palestine”, 2017), it was reported that 36% of counselors suffered from sleep disturbances, over 70% of them suffered from consistent feelings of sadness and 51% felt helpless. The above data is reinforced in the October NRC 2018 rapid- assessment carried out in East Khanyunis where it was found that 50% of the teachers interviewed were planning for early retirement due to the economic crisis burden (presently teachers on the PA payroll receive only 50% of their salaries while teachers on the MoEHE Gaza payroll are paid 40%). Students interviewed showed significant symptoms of frustration where 49% had lost hope in the future and 58% had problem in sleeping and nightmares. The assessment also showed an alarming trend of 18% male and 10% female students (in higher grades) thinking of dropping out. The latter was confirmed in the Sept 2018 NRC ‘Eviction Assessment’ where it was found that 20% of FGD participants in Gaza had stopped sending their children to school because they were not able any longer to pay for basic school-related expenses (such as stationary, technical aids, daily expenses etc.) and 10% out of them sent their children to work in order to help cover house expenses and meet basic needs. Teaching methods continue to rely on memorization and note learning, 60% remain unqualified (EDPS 2014-2019). 60% of children (“NRC Improved Education evaluation”, Sue Nicholson, 2018) reported that their teachers were sometimes angry and punished them, with 15% saying this happened a lot. In the September 2018 consultations carried out by NRC -in close collaboration with the education sub-cluster- with education, protection main key stakeholders in Gaza, it was reported that the EiE file consisting in i) the mapping of teaching and non-teaching staff living near the Designated Emergency Shelters (DES), ii) their training on the EiE packages, iii) the revision of an accelerated learning curricula for catch up classes and iv) the update of the contingency plan, had not progressed due to the lack of resources. It was also highlighted that the only pre-positioned educational material stock available for emergencies consisted of very low numbers of school bags, recreational kits, ECD, stationery, drawing notebooks, remedial folders and child protection kits not sufficient to cover the basic educational needs in case of emergency.

Activities

NRC has in place a MoU with the MoEHE for collaboration and engagement across the WB and Gaza. A Framework Agreement has been drafted between NRC and MoEHE outlining an overall commitment to institutionalizing BLP, as well as specifying the roles and responsibilities in a multi-year work plan describing how this will occur.
