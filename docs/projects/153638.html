Project Summary

Through the proposed project, RI will provide timely and quality treatment of acute malnutrition to children 6 to 59 months and Pregnant Lactating Women (PLW) reaching at least 85% of SAM with the expectation of 10% of which will be referred and managed at the stabilization center (SC) for medical complications, 80% of MAM children 6-59 months and 55% of PLWs. Additionally, RI will provide nutrition preventive services through early screening at the community level and referrals, increasing access to MIYCN services, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation as well as improving the access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH and FSL services.
Blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) for children 6 to 59 months, PLW and people living with HIV/AIDS and tuberculosis patient will be implemented.
RI will strengthen nutrition surveillance mechanisms through accurate and timely reporting, monitoring and analysis of data for early and objective decision making.

Needs

Malnutrition in Upper Nile remains critical and the causes are multifaceted. The continued conflict led to displacement of thousands to the deterioration of economy affecting the food security. Following the peace agreement signature, local authorities are already witnessing movement of returnees from surrounding countries, including Ethiopia. Coverage and access to regular health and nutrition services, remains challenging due to long distances, remoteness, and floods in the rainy season. In Maiwut, the most recent SMART survey conducted early August 2018 by Relief International (RI) has revealed a GAM rate of 10% (classified as serious) with the poor health and food security an aggravating factor in the county. The SAM rate in Maiwut is 2.4%. The same SMART survey has shown a coverage of measles vaccination among children 9 to 59 months of age very poor at only 7.2%, vitamin A supplementation coverage was 5.7%, and deworming tablet supplementation was only 3.4 percent – resulting in high morbidity rate in a county where malaria is endemic. The GAM rate is expected to increase if these contributing factors are not addressed.In Nasir the GAM rate is 17.2% and SAM rate is 3.3% - also classified as emergency according WHO. The prevalence of childhood diseases in the county (is also known as one of the county with high prevalence of acute bloody diarrhoea in the county) is directly linked to the malnutrition situation. According to the latest IPC analysis in September 2018, this situation is compounded by high food prices in the rare markets and very limited access to land for cultivation. The presence of Fall Army Worm (FAW) in the county and a rainfall shortage reported in 2018 will likely result in a poor harvest. In Ulang the GAM rate is 14.5% and SAM is at 6.1% - highly above the WHO threshold of 2.5%. This high SAM rate will likely lead to high morbidity in the county with an expectation of a good portion of these cases requiring admission to the SC. The IPC classification is projecting 30% of the population of Nasir to be in Phase 4, and access to markets may remain limited due to unpredictable insecurity.In Longechuk the GAM rate is 15.8% and SAM rate is 3.2%, both above WHO emergency threshold. The IPC analysis released in September 2018 is projecting 30% of population in Longechuk to fall under Phase 4. The analysis has identified key shocks including the dry spell, increasing food prices, and the fact that 57% of households do not own livestock. Food insecurity remains one of the immediate causes of malnutrition in the county. In Maban the nutrition situation remains also serious with a GAM rate of 10.2% as per the SMART survey conducted in June 2018 and SAM at 2.8% (above WHO emergency threshold). The coverage of measles vaccination (52%), vitamin A supplementation (57%), and deworming (42%) remain low compared to WHO standards. The SMART survey has also revealed an exclusive breastfeeding rate of only 31.9% in Maban host community - a direct link to malnutrition. Insecurity including inter-communal conflict in UNS remain unpredictable and drive the same causes of malnutrition in these four counties. As the nutrition needs remain acute in UNS, all assessments are indicating an urgent nutrition intervention is required. Relief International has maintained presence in Upper Nile since 2006 and is well-placed to respond to the multifaceted needs leading to high rates of malnutrition.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Messages will be provided in both verbal form as well as through visual aids. Mass messaging for all groups will be provided in a generic format to blanket reach beneficiaries. To reach the most vulnerable including women, girls, the elderly, and disabled, specific messaging including house-to-house visits and messaging by female staff and CHWs will be provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Separate discussions and consultations will be held through individual meetings and FGDs with specific age groups such adolescent girls and young mothers (below 18) for specific topics or issues related to their age in relation to MIYCN, prevention of malnutrition. Individual meetings and FGDs will also be conducted with men to actively engage them in the program supporting women on nutrition-related issues (including breastfeeding and food diversity). PLWs and OTP/TSFP caregivers will meet and be consulted with separately as well to discuss their specific issues and support needed or expected from their family and the community at large.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

At field level all Relief International new staff attend mandatory training on sexual exploitation and abuse; refresher sessions will be conducted for other staff on a bi-annual basis. The community and beneficiaries will also be oriented on RI PSEA policies. In collaboration with the community, a feedback and complain mechanism will be established for cases of SEA. This platformwill serve as a bridge between RI and the beneficiaries and will facilitate sensitization for prevention of SEA. Moreover, an RI focal person for SEA has been identified in each site to collect at the field level any case and treat it as per RI policy through thorough reporting including to the Country Director and headquarters.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Local leaders - informal and informal have been consulted on a regular basis throughout 2018 for feedback on 2019 programming through formal and informal meetings. Consultation with beneficiaries also occurred through meetings with patients at the time of consultation as well as through feedback meetings with CNVs and MSGs. Beneficiaries of RI's 2018 programming areconsulted on a regular basis for feedback on programming and quality of service to improve and enhance programming in 2019. Additionally, coordination meetings with national and international NGOs take place in Juba and in the field to review ongoing programming and plan for 2019.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Feedback and complaint mechanisms are established through multiplemechanisms including: - compliant boxes at nutrition facilities (mobile and static) sites - anonymous and non-anonymous reporting mechanisms to a reportingfocal point in each of RI's field offices and health care facilities. - Supervisory and beneficiary feedback meetings conducted by senior monitoring staff.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring and evaluation includes patient follow-up, site/household visits, as well as regular supervision by field and Juba staff. The community participates in monitoring through regular feedback received at the facility and through CNVs. the community, together with local authorities assist RI in resolving disputes and any potential problems which may arise. Additionally,the community participates in monitoring through established complaints mechanisms outside of the facility, wherein they can immediately report to RI and local authorities any violations. Moreover, the community is consulted through one-on-one interviews and group discussions on improvements in programming for 2019.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection risks include the following: - HIV/TB patients not accessing services due to stigmatization. - Mental health and psycho social patients not accessing services due to stigmatization. - Insecurity for women in most of the counties where the facilities are at 2 or 3 walking days. - Communal differences at the payam level can reduce access to services - Some communities may not access the services during the rainy season due to flooding. RI is addressing these specific stigmatization and protection needs through targeted messaging,communal meetings to resolve disputes, consultations with local authorities and partner NGOs, as well as through consultations with the CNVs and MSGs. Additionally, gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project period through prioritization of female beneficiaries (both women and girls as well as PLWs). Staff capacity building will be supported through a gender lens, including prioritization of a female staff in hiring to promote gender balance in the workplace. Women and girls’ rights and needs will be supported through provision of complementary gender-sensitive health and WASH programming including referrals of GBV survivors and female-specific hygiene promotion.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

At the start of the project and during implementation, consultation at the community level will be conducted to identify the root causes of stigmatization of mental health patients as well as TB and HIV patients. Feedback, conceptions of the community will be analysed and a context-based strategywill be designed to address identified barriers to access the services. Regular awareness campaigns on HIV, TB, and mental health will be conducted. For the communal differences that may arise and which prevent some communities to attend nutrition services as well as inaccessibility due to rains and/or conflict, mobile nutrition services will be utilized and outreach activities will be applied whenever possible. Consultation will also be conducted between the program team, localauthorities, beneficiaries and the community at large to explore other options to serve better people in need.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Inequalities are addressed mainly through the prioritization of women in beneficiary selection as well as in decision-making processes. Women are prioritized for hiring, and are consulted on all decision-making procedures to ensure their voice is heard and considered. The threat of coercion and community tension is mitigated through involvement in programming by local leaders and beneficiaries, to ensure transparent and accountable mechanisms.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The access and coverage of service will be monitored through the attendance rate to the facilities, through the weekly and monthly data report, analyzing the trend of admission as compared to the previous project. The report on the MUAC screening done at community level in comparison with admission trends will also an indicator of the access and coverage of the program.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

To ensure safety of beneficiaries, the local authorities and the community at large will be informed on the project and discussions will take place to agree on the expectation from each stakeholder to support safety and security of beneficiaries. Through the mobile clinics the nutrition services will be closer to the community. The OTP and TSFP services will be provided on fixed day to allow movement of beneficiaries in a group and clear messaging. Quarterly FGD are planned with beneficiaries and the communities to get their feedback on the quality of services as well as on any safety and protection concerns. In addition, RI has an area manager who is in charge of daily operations, safety and security assessment with local authority to mitigate any safety issue.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Serving beneficiaries without harming their dignity is part of RI fundamental values and as such all staff will be oriented on how to preserve the dignity of beneficiaries in each stage of treatment, from their reception to their release. This orientation will provide the knowledge and skill to the staff to ensure that all verbal and non-verbal communication with the beneficiaries will ensure respect to beneficiaries, respect privacy of beneficiaries, confidentiality and right to listen from and to the beneficiaries. This dignity will be measured during a midyear FGD to gain feedback of beneficiaries on their feelings and their impression on the relationship with the nutrition staff. As an integrated intervention, RI's WASH program will provide a dignity kit for IDPs.
