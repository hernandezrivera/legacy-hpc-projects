Project Summary

Nile Hope anticipates to increase access to emergency essential primary health care services through an integrated service delivery package using both the developmental and emergency service with a focus on the major causes of Morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable community (Mothers and under-five children).
So as to contribute to an improved resilience of crisis affected populations, Nile Hope believes that an enhanced coordination between humanitarian and development approaches is essential. As a result, the development support has increasingly been forced to move away from the ambitions of building sustainable health structures and come to focus more on service delivery and resilience-oriented activities, in that way, the gap between humanitarian and development interventions has shrunk.
Hence, Nile Hope will work on enhancing and synergizing the emergency humanitarian response to focus on lifesaving activities while striving at building more durable solutions from the start. With the urgent life-saving needs that exist in South Sudan at present this is a considerable challenge which requires a joint effort of intertwining and synergizing both the development and humanitarian responses together.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is one of the counties with weak health systems, thourgh there is a slow improvement still the nation is among counties with poor health indicators in the world, maternal mortality and mortality rates of children under five years are severely high at 789 per 100,000 and 99 per 1,000 live births, respectively. Limited funding in Upper Nile State, Stock out of drugs and lack of qualified health workers has affected surveillance for reportable diseases, preventative and curative healthcare. Currently all the counties in uppernile has suffered chronic shortage and stockouts of essential drugs and community is forced to buy drugs from private vendor and pharmacies which is not affordable by most of the general public.Weak health systems and a high attrition of qualified health workforce coupled with fluid security conditions in Nassir had increased the disease burden and morbidity of mothers and children in the County. According to the Ministry of Health, the average BPHNS threshold is 4.5 facilities per 50,000 persons and Nassir is among one of the counties that account for the lowest service availability in record. Currently, Nassir is classified as phase 4 in the recently released IPC Oct 2022 and the situation is likely to get worse in the lean season of 2023.In Nassir, there is continued heavy rain and flooding from tributaries of Nile River. The floods usually the community is displaced to higher grounds to safeguard the families and livestock from the flood’s destruction. this adversely affects access to and availability of health and wash facilities. This hampers service delivery and access to basic services through the routine mechanism.Targeted counties are among the most deprives with high morbidity and mortality rates associated to common health aliments. Neglected Tropical disease/NTDs such as Kala azar and Guinea worm are among the prevalent health in the county . The number of functional facilities in the county is minimal and has ensured low EPI and essential health care because the county is populous and vast in geography where community has to walk for hours to reach to the nearest facility. Potential access to essential health care remains to be far less that 50% which is lower that the national average 56%.

2 - Project activities

Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response in Nassir county, IPC in 8 health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities,Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using the Boma Health Initiatives,Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services to 7,000 beneficiaries including management of non-communicable diseases through static at PHCC, PHCU and mobile healthcare services,Conducting community consultative meetings in five counties within the community on monthly basis and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance,Provide technical and operational assistance to 8 heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population,Promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources in five counties and two states.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We work to improve humanitarian standards, as partners in responding emergency and in response to disasters. Nile Hope will act at all times in the interests of vulnerable people. A mechanism of engaging the vulnerable community will be sought such that the time of community meetings and engagement will fit in to the time of the group to maximize their inputs. For participants who are less literate the organizer will read out loud and put clarifications so that thy fully understand and participate in decision making process.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At county level focus group discussions and community meetings will be held to on a periodic basis where community express their views and complaints. while at facility a suggestion box is placed for beneficiaries to express their views, satisfaction reflections, In addition a community conversation is held. The technical team has a planned supervisory visit where one of the major areas is creating a platform where the CHD, community representatives and stakeholders are brought for consultation and their concerns and an appropriate action is taken to the raised concerns and is communicates the response/action taken where relevant back to the beneficiary.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will seek to ensure health team and are trained on Protection of Sexual Exploitation and abuse and community awareness on sexual exploitation and sexual abuse will be enhanced through community conversation and engagement. Nile Hope will create awareness of the affected community about PSEA through sessions on 1. What PSEA is, 2. Who can commit SEA? 3. What are the rights of the beneficiary or affected community, 4. How and who to report to? and 5. The right of accessing the services.Nile Hope also will Distribute BCC flyers and posters that include the reporting channels of the organization. Nile Hope has a code of conduct that solely its to respect to humanity and protection of community and beneficiaries from sexual abuse and exploitation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Community conversation is held on a periodic basis where community express their views and complaints. The department also has a planned supervision visit where one of the major areas is creating a platform where the community representatives and local influential members are brought to reflect this will be learning for projects and stakeholders supporting the project and will be used to help the team to adapt project activities and improve in subsequent decision-making. The project will continuously be a learning platform and is flexible in programming and budgeting. This will build trust and strengthen relationships that will improve the project implementation through necessary consulted adjustment.
