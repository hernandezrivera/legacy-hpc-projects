Project Summary

Through this project, ADRA aims to assist conflict affected communities in Eastern Ukraine to meet their basic needs & improve their basic economic security & self-sustenance.
This will be achieved by:
• Providing access to food to the vulnerable residents living in isolated settlements (<5 km zone) through the distribution of food electronic vouchers , by using ADRA’s free transportation services (funded by GFFO) to have access to basic needs and social services in larger cities . This will increase the resilience of elderly people in isolated settlements to poverty.
• Providing access to basic items & services to meet the basic needs & improve social protection outcomes for the most vulnerable & marginalized population through well coordinated MPCG programming (<5 km zone) . MPCGs are an ideal aid tool designed & tested to offer crisis-affected people a quick, effective, & flexible support while maintaining their dignity in areas where most markets are still functioning.
• Implement early recovery intervention to provide financial literacy, entrepreneurship & technical skills trainings & conditional agricultural micro-grants to vulnerable families in both urban & rural localities to set-up micro-businesses (0-20+ km. zone). This will increase resilience to poverty strengthen the recovery of the beneficiaries by providing a longer-term impact through stable income for several years for the sustainable businesses. This approach allows beneficiaries to use & build on their existing skills & knowledge to generate income for their families.
• Supporting the conflict-affected communities (<5 km zone) to recover through participatory light rehabilitation projects from which the most vulnerable community members will generate income. This component is designed to create temporary work opportunities to vulnerable segments of the population through small-scale projects & it serves as an initial step for income stabilization for crisis-affected population. It also helps to restore their dignity feeling themselves able to contribute to improving their own community structures. Also, this component will support facilities & social institutions with small repairs & renovations which will benefit the whole community on the long run. These facilities will be used also in the nearest future as the first step of rehabilitation & development of conflict-affected communities. The facilities will be identified in close cooperation with local authorities to ensure ownership by the whole community & sustainability of results.
The project will be implemented in GCA Donetsk & Luhansk Oblasts with main focus on settlements near the contact line (0-20 km), & will be targeting vulnerable groups with focus on elderly people, persons with disabilities or injury, persons with chronic illness, & women & girls. Activities will be coordinated with local authorities, local & international organizations operating in the same areas & sectors, & the relevant clusters (FSL & CWG) plans & strategies.
ADRA will ensure that gender considerations are integrated in all phases of the project cycle .Beneficiaries will be consulted throughout the project about their concerns, specific needs , opinions and feedback . All data collected will be disaggregated by gender and age . Parity in recruitment and job opportunities will be carefully considered .ADRA will also apply a range of mechanisms to ensure accountability to the affected population . Information about the planned activities will be made available to target communities . A feedback hotline is already in operation .Specific complaints and response mechanism provision will also be made .
The project proposal is prepared based on Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 (HRP). The project will contribute to the implementation of HRP Strategic collective outcomes 1 & 2 . It will also contribute to the FSL Cluster Objectives 1,2,3 and will contribute to MPCs objectives 1 & 2.

1 - Needs

Six years of active fighting have had profound consequences on the lives of more than five million people in conflict-affected Donetska and Luhanska oblasts of eastern Ukraine. Some 3.4 million of these people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection.With the spread of the COVID-19 in Ukraine, there is a growing concern that eastern Ukraine – ravaged by six years of armed conflict & with weakened health systems and an ageing population–may face a virus outbreak of considerable scale. Since the first confirmed case of COVID-19 was reported on 19 March in the conflict-affected region, the number of new confirmed cases has steadily increased. As of 30 September, 2020, there are 4526 confirmed cases of COVID-19 in the government-controlled area (GCA) of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts . The high proportion of elderly people (36% of the population–where of 56% are women) in the conflict-affected region increases their vulnerability. Almost all older persons in the conflict-affected region (97%) have at least one chronic illness , making them highly susceptible to complications if infected with COVID-19. Elderly with disabilities in conﬂict-affected areas are often excluded from humanitarian assistance . Their main concerns are access to healthcare due to limited resources. They also often face insecurity & are unable or unwilling to move away from their homes. To survive, they often rely on their meagre income & on families or neighbours to access basic services & food. IDPs are also among the most vulnerable groups, as they have more special needs & challenges. A total of 89 % of IDPs,who were actively seeking employment, reported facing difficulties,80 % of them women.According to IOM survey covering both GCA and NGCA revealed that over half of respondents reported having to switch to cheaper foods to cope with the growing financial burden caused by the impact of COVID-19 measures.According to HNO 2020,more than 530,000 people, including IDPs outside Donetska and Luhanska oblasts,are food insecure and about 480,000 require livelihood support. The groups most at risk and in need of humanitarian assistance include the elderly,individuals and HHs with disabled members, unemployed members, especially those headed by people aged 40-60,SHHs with children, FHHs and people with chronic illnesses, such as HIV/AIDS,TB,diabetes,IDPs in host communities (HNO 2020).FHHs face particular challenges with 39% currently unemployed compared to 25% of MHHs, as many businesses have closed & adults in GCA previously went to NGCA for work. The REACH winter assessment data confirms the trend identified in 2017, directly linking food security & unemployment. Minimum subsistence levels, minimum salary levels,utility prices,social protection services, & economic factors have a direct impact on a household’s food security status. All state social payments (such as assistance for a household when a child is born, support to households with members with a disability, support for single parents etc.) are based on the ‘legal stationary minimum subsistence level’. However, this level does not always match the level of inflation & vulnerable groups, as people receive far less than they did in 2013 prior to the conflict . The conflict has had a significant impact on the agricultural sector, traditionally a major sector for livelihoods in Ukraine: 21,000 rural HHs in GCA require agricultural support (HNO 2019).

2 - Activities & Outcomes

• Outcome 1: Most vulnerable conflict-affected groups have immediate access to food Activity 1.1: 8,264 of most vulnerable beneficiaries in isolated settlements are supported by food electronic vouchers. The amount of one electronic voucher per beneficiary is 2475 UAH covering 3 months assistance (825 UAH/month ). Beneficiaries will redeem their vouchers ,by using ADRA’s social transportation component (funded by GFFO) for free , to larger cities where they can have access to basic and social services .The activity will be implemented as follows :- Locations identifications through FSL cluster. - Signing agreements with large supermarkets (ATB, Silpo, etc.).- Beneficiary registration;- Beneficiary selection based on transparent vulnerability criteria following community mobilization.- Crosschecking of beneficiaries with other NGO/INGO actors to avoid overlapping.- Electronic voucher distribution;- PDM • Outcome 2: Increasing the purchasing power of targeted population to cover their immediate basic needs  Activity 2.1: 3,376 targeted conflict affected boys, girls, men and women have increased capacity to cover their essential basic needs strategies through provision of MPCGs. Beneficiaries will receive the cash grants through national post offices and/or banks . They also can benefit from ADRA’s social transportation component (funded by GFFO) for free , through which they can have access to basic needs and social services in larger cities . In case of beneficiaries who cannot be covered by the previous options , then the grants can be transferred to their bank accounts directly . The amount of grant per beneficiary is 3450 UAH (1150 UAH/month ) covering 3 months. The activity will be implemented as follows :- Locations’ selection through CWG cluster. - Beneficiaries’ registration.- Beneficiaries’ selection based on transparent vulnerability criteria and scoring matrix.- Crosschecking of beneficiaries with other NGO/INGO - Disbursement of cash through national post office and/or banks - PDM.• Outcome 3: Affected rural and Urban households improve their resilience through rehabilitated agriculture and/or increased agricultural production for sustainable livelihoods. Activity 3.1: 190 HHs (75 Rural & 115 Urban)improve their resilience through receiving provision of livelihood agricultural grants up to 1000 USD. The activity will be implemented as follows :- Locations’ identifications through FSL cluster. - Beneficiaries’ selection - Crosschecking of beneficiaries with other NGO/INGO - Development of business plans by selected beneficiaries.- Selection of business plans for micro-grants based on scoring matrix. - Disbursement of the 1st tranche of cash through national post office and/or banks (75%).- Conditionality assessment.- Disbursement of the 2ndtranche of cash (25%) - Provision of mentoring and consultation over several months • Outcome 4: Affected urban populations gained access to employment and income opportunities through the restoration / creation of sustainable livelihoods & rehabilitation/building of sustainable livelihood assets  Activity 4.1.: 440 people participate in light rehabilitation projects (minor repairs in schools and kindergartens) from which the most vulnerable community members. The activity will be implemented as follows :- Locations’ identifications. - Beneficiaries’ selection - Engagement with authorities and beneficiaries to define the scope of work for facility rehabilitation, taking into account local skills, community needs and capacities.- Establishment of teams and trainings (basic rehabilitation, gender equality).- Procurement of materials after agreement with local authorities and communities.- Cash for work activities (minor repairs, cleaning of territories)- Transfer of income to beneficiaries.
